# Building Empathy

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 12 claims (8 for, 4 mixed) · 24 studies (8 causal, 7 quant-synthesis, 4 review, 2 qualitative, 1 associational, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q4` · 6 of 24 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Conditional relationship

A learner who forms an interpretation of another person or group from limited information may come to represent that person's perspective more accurately, and may hold more positive attitudes towards the group, after a structured activity that makes the other's point of view concrete: an account to listen to or read, a prompt to take that perspective, and a chance to compare one's own reading with what the other person says. The relationship is conditional on the outcome named (empathic concern, mental-state inference, attitude towards a group and intergroup behaviour are different outcomes), on the dose (a single passage or exercise against a sustained programme), on the target (one person against a group, and how stigmatized that group is for these learners) and on the horizon (immediately after the activity, weeks later, or in later conduct). No matching pattern page holds a conditional model of it yet.

Learners: any age (`child` to `adult`); the wiki's evidence comes from adults in laboratories, students in grades 4–6 in classrooms, and undergraduates in experiential programmes. Knowledge type: `attitude-motivation` and social inference, not a procedure with a correct answer. The wiki has no claim testing empathy-building as the general instructional relationship stated here; the claims below test parts of it.

## Observation, state and explanation

Empathy is an inferred state. What a design can observe is a response under stated conditions: what a learner says another person feels or wants, how they summarise someone else's view, a rating on a feeling thermometer, a choice to take part in contact. "Empathic" is a reading of those responses, and agreement with a view is not the same response as understanding it. Self-reported empathy after an exercise designed to produce it is especially open to demand: learners know what the activity is for.

| Response | Plausible explanations | Observation that could change the interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Learner summarises another person's view accurately but rejects it with hostility | Understanding without concern; identity threat raised by the activity; disagreement on substance that accurate perspective-taking does not remove | Ask the learner to say what the other person would object to in their summary, and separately what they themselves disagree with; repeat with a less threatening topic. |
| Learner reports more warmth towards a group right after a narrative, with no later change in conduct | Short-lived state change; demand from an activity whose aim is obvious; attitude change that does not reach behaviour | Re-measure at a delay with a measure not tied to the activity, and record an optional behavioural choice (as Weiss et al. 2023 did with contact registration). |
| Learner attributes feelings to a character or interviewee that the account does not support | Projection of own perspective; stereotype filling the gaps; too little information in the account | Ask what in the account supports each attribution; give a second account from the same person and see whether the learner revises. |
| Learner says little in a structured listening round | Low engagement; discomfort with disclosure or the topic; language demand of the task; reading the norms as unsafe | Offer a written or paired response mode and a less personal prompt; ask privately what made the round hard. |
| Learner's interpretation changes after dialogue | Perspective-taking; conformity to the group or facilitator; new factual information alone | Ask the learner to explain what changed and why, and check the change against a private response made before the group discussion ends. |

These rows are proposals from the claims below and from the old page's cautions, untested with learners. A probe can itself change the state, and none of them identifies a cause on its own; record what each one leaves unresolved.

## Evidence and qualifications

- [Building empathy improves intergroup attitudes](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [~M]: three laboratory experiments in which inducing empathy for one member of a stigmatized group led to more positive attitudes towards the group, with the effect for the most stigmatized group weak at once and appearing 1–2 weeks later; a cluster-randomized classroom programme for grades 4–6 that combined outgroup exposure with perspective-taking discussions and raised positive affect towards outgroups by about a third of a SD up to 8–13 weeks later, without changing registration for an actual contact event; and a review of 418 prejudice-reduction experiments that finds perspective-taking the modal intervention, signs of publication bias, and that 76% of studies test light-touch interventions whose long-term impact is unclear. The classroom programme combined three mechanisms, so its effect is not attributable to empathy alone. Marked mixed: it supports an attitude change and limits it for one-off exercises and for behaviour. Sources read from abstracts; not settled by the judge.
- [Fiction reading improves empathy](../claims/reading-literary-fiction-improves-theory-of-mind.md) [~M]: a meta-analysis of 14 experiments finds reading fiction gives a small improvement on social-cognitive tasks against nonfiction or no reading (g = .15–.16, below the small-effect threshold), and a three-lab randomized replication with 792 adults found no advantage for a single literary passage on the eyes test, while lifetime fiction exposure predicted scores. The outcome is social-cognitive task performance, not empathy as the title says. A limiting claim: brief narrative exposure alone predicts little. [Reading literary fiction improves theory of mind](../claims/reading-literary-fiction-improves-theory-of-mind.md) [~M] carries the same three entries and is a merge candidate.
- [Students perceive that interacting with others in experiential learning fosters empathy by enhancing perspective-taking](../claims/students-interaction-empathy-perspective-taking.md) [+W]: a thematic analysis of twelve undergraduate interviews in which students described collaboration, practicums and study abroad as enhancing perspective and fostering empathy. It records what learners reported valuing, not a measured change against a comparison. Checked by the judge on the full text.
- [SEL programs improve social emotional skills](../claims/sel-programs-improve-social-emotional-skills.md) [~M]: meta-analyses of universal school SEL programmes (K–12, and ages 2–6) find gains in social-emotional skills, larger for sequenced, active, focused and explicit programmes. The skill composite includes social awareness but the claim page does not isolate empathy or perspective-taking, so it bears on structured, sustained programmes in general rather than on this relationship. Partly checked.

Keep learners, the exact activity and comparator, the outcome instrument and the horizon with each of these when transporting them. A null on one measure (contact registration, the eyes test) is not evidence that nothing changed, nor that the measures are equivalent.

## Objective and learner-valued goal

The designer's objective and what the learner wants from the activity can differ, and the difference is an observation to record rather than resolve by assumption. Ask what the learner hopes to get from the work, separately from the stated objective, and do not read participation as agreement.

For example, in a nursing course a designer may aim for students to represent a patient's experience of a diagnosis accurately in an empathy interview, while a student may value getting through difficult conversations without freezing. A feeling-thermometer rating would establish neither aim. A summary the patient (or a standardized patient) confirms tests the designer's objective; the student's own account of a later conversation is evidence about theirs. Agreement between the two is something to check, not assume.

## What would revise this model?

The expectation that structured perspective-taking improves attitudes towards a group should weaken if sustained, well-implemented programmes compared with defensible controls fail to show it at a delay, or if their effects disappear once the publication bias the review flags is taken into account. If the attitude change appears without any change in measured perspective-taking or empathic concern, the mechanism this page assumes is wrong, and another explanation (exposure, information, demand) fits better. If gains on ratings never reach conduct, keep the two outcomes apart rather than calling both empathy. Do not protect the model by relabelling every null as poor facilitation.

An immediate change in a rating, a delayed change in attitude, more accurate inference of what another person thinks, and changed behaviour towards others are separate claims. The present evidence does not establish which activity type, dose or facilitation produces durable change, does not test interviews, journaling or listening rounds as such, and does not say how the relationship differs by age.

## Examples

- **Empathy interviews**: Learners interview a peer, client, or community member using prompts focused on needs, frustrations, and lived experience.
- **Perspective journaling**: After discussion or field observation, learners write from another stakeholder's viewpoint and then reflect on what they still do not know.
- **Narrative comparison**: Learners compare multiple first-person or community narratives about the same issue before discussion.
- **Structured listening rounds**: Small groups take turns summarizing another person's view before offering their own response.

## Description
Building empathy is the instructional principle of helping learners understand how other people perceive, feel, and interpret a situation. In learning environments, empathy matters because it improves listening, collaboration, conflict navigation, and the ability to respond thoughtfully across differences in experience or identity. It is usually cultivated through structured perspective-taking, dialogue, narrative exposure, reflection, and authentic interaction rather than through abstract exhortation alone.

## Implications
Building empathy requires more than telling learners to care about other people. Perspective-taking strengthens when learners encounter concrete accounts, listen carefully, reflect on what they assumed, and compare their interpretation with what another person actually says or experiences; that reflective explanation work is one reason these activities often depend on learners making sense of perspectives in language [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding and problem-solving performance.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [~M]. Empathy-building is also more likely to transfer when it is tied to authentic interaction, dialogue, or community participation rather than abstract exhortation alone [Whole-task performance improves transfer of complex skills to real-world settings.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [~M]. That means empathy-building tasks have to be structured and carefully facilitated: without safety, specificity, and reflection, they can collapse into stereotype performance, shallow agreement, or defensive conflict rather than deeper understanding.

### Context
#### Requirements
- **Structured perspective-taking**: Learners need prompts, scenarios, or narratives that make another person's point of view concrete.
- **Psychological safety**: Empathy-building work requires norms that reduce ridicule, defensiveness, and performative disclosure.
- **Skilled facilitation**: Instructors need to help learners distinguish understanding another perspective from simply agreeing with it.
- **Reflection opportunities**: Learners need time to compare their assumptions with what they heard or observed.
#### Constraints
- **Forced disclosure**: Activities that pressure learners to expose trauma, identity, or private experience can backfire.
- **Superficial role play**: Empathy does not grow from caricature or thin simulations that flatten other people's lived reality.
- **Identity threat**: Poorly facilitated dialogue can heighten defensiveness rather than perspective-taking.
- **Indirect evidence base in this repo**: Current linked claims here mainly support reflection, explanation, and transfer mechanisms rather than empathy-specific outcomes directly.

### Target Learners
- **Learners working in groups or communities**: Particularly relevant when collaboration, discussion, or service is central to the learning design.
- **Learners navigating difference**: Useful in multicultural, interdisciplinary, and civic learning contexts where understanding other perspectives matters.
- **Learners developing communication and relationship skills**: Strong fit for oral communication, leadership, mentoring, and conflict resolution goals.
- **Learners with rigid initial assumptions**: Structured empathy work can help unsettle oversimplified views and expand interpretation.

### Target Learning Objectives
- **Perspective-taking**: Recognizing how a situation can look different from another position or experience.
- **Improved listening and discussion**: Responding to others with greater care, curiosity, and specificity.
- **Inclusive collaboration**: Working more productively across different backgrounds, roles, and priorities.
- **Reflective judgment**: Noticing one's own assumptions and revising them after dialogue or observation.

### Theory
#### Supporting
- Social learning theory — learners pick up empathic discourse and listening practices by observing modeled interaction.
- Transformative learning theory — empathy can deepen when learners critically examine assumptions and encounter disorienting alternative perspectives.
- Social constructivist views of learning — understanding is built through dialogue with other people, not in isolation.
- [Person-Centered Psychology](../theories/person-centered-psychology.md) — gives a structured relational methodology (unconditional positive regard, empathic listening, facilitator congruence) for the specific conditions under which perspective-taking can go beyond surface performance
#### Contradicting / Qualifying
- Empathy-building is not value-neutral; facilitation choices shape whose experiences are centered and whose are simplified.
- Perspective-taking activities can increase confidence in misunderstanding if learners infer too much from too little evidence.

- [Whole-task performance improves transfer of complex skills to real-world settings.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [~M] — authentic interaction and perspective-taking tasks are more likely to transfer than decontextualized discussion about empathy
