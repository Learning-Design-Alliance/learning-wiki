Treat talking and writing as means to learning through dialogue-based, student-centered instruction¶

Principle · All principles Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q1–q4 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article presents the Bullock Report's basic tenet, adopted by Cummins for minority students, that "talking and writing are a means to learning" (p.50). It argues for an instructional model based on dialogue between student and teacher using speech and writing as instruments for learning, encouraging a collaborative learning environment, constructing a student-centered environment guided and facilitated by the teacher, and emphasizing higher level cognitive skills rather than correction of surface forms.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A collaborative, student-centered environment guided and facilitated by the teacher
  • Pedagogies promoting intrinsic motivation to use language actively to generate students' own knowledge
  • Careful planning to decrease teacher talk time in a time-limited classroom
  • Teacher talk that is accurate, logical, standard, succinct and rhythmical
  • A student-centered approach

Constraints¶

  • Cummins frames this as breaking a cycle of failure rooted in power-laden relationships between educators and minority students and communities
  • Classroom time is limited, so reduced talk time raises the demand for talk quality

Target Learners¶

  • minority students
  • first and second language students
  • English majors in English Reading classes

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • higher level cognitive skills rather than just factual recall
  • meaningful language use
  • knowledge generation
  • Expressing themselves deeply and fully in the target language
  • Discussion and exchange of ideas

Claims¶

  • Some classroom discussion approaches improve text comprehension, but gains are not uniform across approaches and more student talk alone does not produce them [+M]
  • Some classroom discussion approaches improve text comprehension, but gains are not uniform across approaches and more student talk alone does not produce them [+M]
  • Student-centered writing environments are argued to lower anxiety, increase confidence, and provide natural language contexts [+W] — attached 2026-10-07 from Kimberly Miller Linnell (2010), which proposed "Establish meaningful communication first, then layer in focus on form gradually".

Related Principles¶

  • Adjust delivery to students' proficiency so language input is comprehensible during communicative reading activities
  • Create a relaxed, non-threatening atmosphere before engaging students in communicative activities

Examples¶

  • Use process writing techniques in natural language settings to develop the push to communicate and facilitate acquisition
  • Post-reading group work in small groups where individual efforts are pooled to interpret the text
  • Evaluate student contributions through praising, encouraging, quoting students' words, and gestures
  • Use role-play and drama techniques after reading to deepen understanding through lifelike language use
  • Apply six questioning techniques in whole-class discussion: simplify, moderate, provoke thought, challenge, follow up, and relate to students
  • Text Based Discussion Protocols
  • Discussion Based Learning
  • Class Discussion Facilitation
  • Class Discussion Norms
  • Letting students know what order they will be called on
  • Read Alouds With Accountable Talk
  • Debate Format
  • Establish Talk Partners
