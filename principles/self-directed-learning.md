# Self-Directed Learning

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 32 claims (12 for, 18 mixed, 2 against) · 41 studies (14 review, 8 theoretical, 7 causal, 5 quant-synthesis, 4 associational, 2 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q4` · 6 of 41 report an effect size · 23 claims rest on one study

## Conditional relationship

This page owns **handing decisions about the learning process to the learner**: which goal to pursue, which resources and tasks to use, how fast to go, which study strategy to apply, and how to judge whether it worked. For a learner who is expected to take over some of those decisions (usually an `adolescent` or `adult`, in project work, `online-self-paced` study, a `workplace-clinical` setting or independent study over `days-weeks` or a `term-plus`), handing them over is expected to produce sustained engagement, completed work and growing independence, **and** learning comparable to a teacher-directed course, only when three conditions hold: the learner already knows enough about the content to choose well (task-specific expertise not `novice`), the regulation routine (plan, monitor against a stated criterion, evaluate, adjust) is taught and practised in the subject itself, and the hand-over is gradual, with scheduled checkpoints at which someone or something checks the learner's judgement. Where these fail, learner-directed study is expected to drift: learners choose strategies that feel fluent rather than ones that work, self-paced learners finish less often, and novices given little guidance learn less than those taught explicitly.

**No claim in the wiki compares learner-directed with teacher-directed study of the same content for matched learners**, so the relationship as a whole is untested here. What the claims test are its parts: teaching regulation strategies against ordinary instruction (school students, achievement, strategy use and motivation together), instructing learners in one effective strategy and watching what they then choose on their own (undergraduates, retrieval practice, one week later), self-pacing against group pacing (college mastery courses, completion), minimal against explicit guidance (novices), what learners choose when left to choose (surveys and experiments on study strategies), and one cluster-randomised trial of a student-directed goal cycle (students with disabilities, self-determination), which found no significant difference from a delayed-treatment control and so qualifies rather than supports the independence half of the expectation. Outcomes are mostly `immediate-performance`, `general-achievement`, `persistence` and self-reported `motivation-affect`; none measures independence itself at a delay.

How the converted siblings sit beside this: [Self-Regulated Learning](self-regulated-learning.md) owns the plan–monitor–act cycle and whether prompts and training make learners regulate better; this page uses that cycle as the routine it hands over and does not repeat its model. [Goal Setting & Monitoring](goal-setting-monitoring.md) owns what a goal should be (specific, challenging, process or outcome); [Self-Monitoring](self-monitoring.md) owns checking one's work during a task; [Learner Choice](autonomy.md) owns the motivational side, autonomy support against control. This page owns the **transfer of control across sessions and weeks**: which decisions move to the learner, when, with what supports, and how the designer knows it is working. The [Self-Directed Learning pattern](../patterns/self-directed-learning.md) carries the reusable course sequence; its branches are proposals.

## Default design, while the relationship is untested

The earlier page's guidance (real decisions for the learner, visible planning and monitoring, support for evaluation, gradual release), made concrete. Each step is a design proposal; its evidence status is stated beside it.

1. **Name the decisions you are handing over, and start with one or two.** List them: goal, resources, sequence and pace, strategy, product, evaluation criteria. In the first sessions hand over pace and choice of product or practice task; keep goals and criteria with the teacher. Untested proposal; from the earlier page ("real decisions to make about goals, resources, strategies, sequencing, or products").
2. **Check readiness on the content before handing over the content decisions.** A short task-specific baseline (two or three items of the kind the course targets) and a sample plan ("what will you do in the next week, with what, and how will you know it worked?"). A learner who cannot begin the items keeps instruction-led content with choice only of pace and product; a learner who succeeds and writes a workable plan can take over resources and sequence sooner. Basis: [Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than explicit instruction](../claims/minimal-guidance-less-effective-for-novices.md) [~S] (novices; unassisted discovery against explicit instruction) and [Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]; the two-part readiness check itself is untested.
3. **Teach the regulation routine inside the subject, then require it.** In each of the first three sessions, spend 10–15 minutes modelling a plan for the coming work, a check against the criteria, and a decision about what to change; then have learners do the same on their own work. Basis: [Self-regulated learning improves achievement](../claims/self-regulated-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S] (school students; training against ordinary instruction; its table gathers 11 distinct studies). The minutes and the three-session dose are untested proposals.
4. **Tell learners which strategies work and build them into the plan template, rather than leaving strategy wholly to choice.** The weekly plan has fixed slots for self-testing and for returning to earlier material on a later day; the learner chooses what goes in them. Basis: learners left to choose favour rereading and massing ([Learners misjudge which learning strategies are effective](../claims/learners-misjudge-effective-learning-strategies.md) [~M], [Learners Misjudge Spacing Benefits](../claims/learners-misjudge-spacing-benefits.md) [~S]); an instruction about retrieval practice raised undergraduates' self-testing ([Instructions increased self-testing and cumulative learning but not study choices or feedback seeking in Experiment 1](../claims/instructions-increase-self-testing-cumulative-learning.md) [+M]) and carried to new material a week later without reminders ([Students given retrieval practice instructions spontaneously used a repeated retrieval strategy on new materials one week later without further instructions](../claims/spontaneous-strategy-transfer-one-week-delay.md) [+M]). Say why the strategy works as well as what it is, since a review names false beliefs, lack of awareness and the strategies' counter-intuitive feel as possible causes of underuse ([Underuse of effective strategies may stem from false beliefs, lack of awareness, and the counter-intuitive nature of the strategies](../claims/underuse-stems-from-false-beliefs-and-counterintuition.md) [+W], a hedged list, not a tested result). The template is an untested proposal.
5. **Process goals first, outcome goals later.** A learner new to the skill sets goals about the steps to carry out ("use the three revision moves on each paragraph"); once those are reliable, they add a result to reach. Goals are specific, not "do my best". Basis: [Process goals lead to better skill acquisition for novices than outcome goals.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [~M] (high-school girls, a writing revision skill) and [Specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance than easy or vague "do your best" goals.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md) [~M] (a narrative review, mostly work tasks). How to write the goal is owned by [Goal Setting & Monitoring](goal-setting-monitoring.md).
6. **Make progress visible against criteria, and check the learner's judgement.** A tracker or log the learner fills in at each session (planned, done, evidence, next step), and a checkpoint at least every week: plan, progress, a self-assessment against the stated criteria, and a reply from a teacher, tutor, peer or automatic check within the week. Basis: [Self-regulated learning models treat self-monitoring as a core process through which learners notice gaps and adjust strategy](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M] (two syntheses; no effect size). The weekly interval and reply window are untested proposals.
7. **Set intermediate deadlines when pace is the learner's.** In self-paced study, fix a date for each checkpoint and a short contact when one is missed. Basis: [Self-paced mastery programs (PSI) reduce college course completion, though not by enough to explain their examination advantage](../claims/self-paced-mastery-programs-reduce-college-course-completion.md) [-M] (college courses; completion, not achievement); the deadline remedy is untested.
8. **Hand over more decisions across the weeks, on evidence.** A default arc for a course of six weeks or more: **weeks 1–2**, the teacher sets goals, resources and criteria; the learner chooses pace and practice tasks and keeps the log. **Weeks 3–4**, the learner chooses among three or four vetted resources or tasks (a [choice board](../strategies/choice_boards.md)) and sets process goals the teacher approves. **Week 5 on**, the learner proposes a goal, resources and how the result will be judged, with checkpoints every one to two weeks. Move a learner to the next stage when their last two plans were carried out and their last two self-assessments agreed with the teacher's or an external check within one level of the rubric; move them back a stage after two missed checkpoints. The arc and thresholds are untested proposals.
9. **Let learners pose the questions, and teach the questioning.** In project or inquiry work, have learners generate the questions they will pursue, then teach how to sharpen a question and find sources, because both are skills they may lack. Basis: [Teachers reported that trusting students to generate and pursue their own questions worked better than expected and revealed new student capabilities and gaps](../claims/teachers-surprised-by-student-questioning-capability.md) [+W] (teacher interviews, one programme).
10. **Where generative AI is available, make its use part of the log.** Learners record what they asked a tool, what it gave, and what they did themselves with it, and the checkpoint looks at that record. Basis: [Thoughtless use of generative AI has a significant negative direct effect on undergraduates' self-directed learning](../claims/tuga-negative-direct-effect-sdl.md) [~W] (a survey path model; associational, self-report) and [Without explicit guidance and scaffolding, unguided GenAI use may disproportionately benefit already advantaged students and widen performance gaps](../claims/unguided-genai-benefits-advantaged-students.md) [~W] (an argument, no data); the logging move is untested.

When a condition is not met: if no one can read checkpoints, use an automatic check of the content (a short quiz scored against the criteria) and compare it with the learner's self-assessment; if a learner's baseline shows they cannot begin the content, teach it first and hand over only pace and product.

## Fitting the design to a situation

The three facts that most change the decision:

- **What the learner can already do with this content, and how well they plan.** A `novice` on the content needs instruction and a narrow hand-over (pace, product); a learner who succeeds on the baseline and writes a workable plan can take over resources and sequence sooner. If unstated, ask: can they do two or three items of the target kind now, and can they say what they will do next week and how they will know it worked?
- **Who will read the learner's plans and self-assessments, and how often.** The hand-over is safe only if someone or something checks the learner's judgement against the criteria. If unstated, ask: who looks at a learner's log or checkpoint, how long after it is submitted, and what can they do about a learner who is drifting?
- **Which decisions the brief wants the learner to own, and what counts as success.** Ownership of the topic, of pace, of strategy or of evaluation are different hand-overs, and a course judged on completion, on achievement or on later independence needs different checks. If unstated, ask: which choices are the learner's, which are fixed, and what must the learner be able to do, alone, at the end?

| If the brief says… | Then change… | Basis |
|---|---|---|

| Learners are `advanced` or experienced in the domain | Skip the first stage of the arc; let them propose goals and resources from the start, with checkpoints every two weeks and a check of their criteria rather than their plan | [Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M] (cognitive-load review; gives no crossover point) |

| Mixed modes: some learners remote or phone-only while others are in the room | Keep the log and checkpoint in one shared place that works on a phone (a short form, a message thread); run the weekly checkpoint the same way for everyone, so remote learners' plans are read on the same day as those in the room | untested proposal |
| One teacher for many learners, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a busy shift, a tutor who checks in briefly) | Read checkpoints by exception: an automatic content check plus a one-line self-assessment from everyone; the facilitator reads only those where the two disagree or a checkpoint was missed, for two to three minutes each; peers review one another's plans against a checklist | untested proposal; the risk it guards against is in [Learners misjudge which learning strategies are effective](../claims/learners-misjudge-effective-learning-strategies.md) [~M] |

| Learners with low confidence or low literacy | Hand over one decision at a time; use a picture- or tick-based log; make the first goals small enough to be met in the session; the teacher states the next choice aloud and checks it in person | untested proposal; [Self-efficacy and motivation positively predict self-directed learning, with motivation the strongest direct predictor](../claims/se-mov-positive-predict-sdl.md) [~W] (an associational survey of undergraduates, carried here as a reason to protect early success) |
| Students with disabilities, or transition planning | Use a structured student-directed cycle (set a goal, make an action plan, self-evaluate) in which the student answers each question; train and check that the teacher does not answer for them | [Students taught with the Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction gain in self-determination, but not significantly more than a delayed-treatment control group](../claims/self-determined-learning-model-of-instruction-improves-self-determination.md) [~M] (cluster-randomised, 312 high-school students with intellectual or learning disabilities; within-group gains, between-group difference small, d = 0.23, and not significant) |

| The brief gives its own categories, criteria or rubric | Learners self-assess against those, in those words; do not add a second rubric | untested proposal |
| Goals or journals are private or sensitive (health, career change, personal history) | The learner decides who sees the plan and log; the checkpoint asks only whether the planned step was done and what the learner will do next | untested proposal |

| Generative AI or automated writing feedback is available | Allow it inside the log (step 10); ask learners to revise once from tool feedback and once without it, and compare | [A review of 50 studies reports that AWE tools can improve the organization and overall quality of writing and encourage self-directed learning](../claims/awe-enhance-organization-quality-self-directed-learning.md) [+W] (a review's summary statement) and [Thoughtless use of generative AI has a significant negative direct effect on undergraduates' self-directed learning](../claims/tuga-negative-direct-effect-sdl.md) [~W] (associational) |
| Stakes are high (certification, a licensing exam) | Keep goals, criteria and the final assessment fixed; hand over strategy and pace only, with fixed checkpoints scored on unaided items | untested proposal |

Seven of the seventeen rows rest on a claim for their main move, and most of those carry a neighbouring finding (discovery against explicit instruction, SRL training, self-paced mastery courses, interviews about expectations) to learner-directed study and say so. The rest are untested proposals; three of them cite a claim only for the risk they guard against.

## Observation, state and explanation

What can be observed is what the learner planned, what they did, what they chose (resources, tasks, strategy, time), what their self-assessment said, and what an external check of the same work showed, each with a date. "Is self-directed", "has drifted" and "is not motivated" are inferred states. A completed log shows that the learner kept a log; it does not show that the choices in it were good.

| Response | Plausible explanations | Observation that could change the interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Plans are written but not carried out | The plan was too large; competing demands (work, family); the learner wrote what they thought the teacher wanted; low confidence in the task | Ask for one step for the next three days only and check it; ask what got in the way; compare with a week in which the plan was set by the teacher. |
| Chooses rereading, easy tasks or familiar material | Fluency feels like learning; the effective option was not known; the learner values finishing over learning; the harder task is too far beyond them | Give a short self-test on the material and show the result beside the learner's confidence rating; offer the harder task with a worked first step. |
| Self-assessment is consistently higher than the external check | Criteria not understood; judging by effort or time spent; reluctance to admit difficulty | Ask the learner to mark one anonymous example against the criteria and compare with the teacher's marking; check again after the criteria are discussed. |
| Falls behind pace or misses checkpoints | Procrastination; the work is too hard; life events; the course gives no reason to keep a date | Ask; look at whether content checks were failed before the missed date; offer a fixed date with a short contact and see whether pace recovers. |
| Asks for direction on every choice, or never asks for help | Uncertainty about what a good choice is; fear of being wrong; a belief that asking is weakness or that help is not available | Offer two vetted options instead of an open choice; make help requests private and note whether they rise. |

These rows are proposals, untested with learners as diagnostics. A checkpoint is itself an intervention: being asked to plan changes what the learner does, so record each probe, and keep the explanations that remain open rather than choosing the one whose name fits.

## Evidence and qualifications

No claim in the wiki compares learner-directed with teacher-directed study of the same content, or varies how many decisions are handed over. The claims below test neighbouring parts of the relationship.

- [Self-regulated learning improves achievement](../claims/self-regulated-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]: a meta-analysis of 84 training studies in primary and secondary school reports an average effect of 0.69 across academic performance, strategy use and motivation together, larger when researchers rather than regular teachers delivered the training; a correlational meta-analysis finds measured SRL only weakly related to achievement (r = 0.20 and 0.11); a review of 35 online studies finds most supports helped performance, feedback alone did not, and integrated supports helped only learners who used them. Its table gathers 11 distinct studies. It supports teaching the routine before handing over; it does not test handing over decisions, and the 0.69 is not an achievement figure alone. Read as abstracts, except the online review.
- [Instructions increased self-testing and cumulative learning but not study choices or feedback seeking in Experiment 1](../claims/instructions-increase-self-testing-cumulative-learning.md) [+M] and [Students given retrieval practice instructions spontaneously used a repeated retrieval strategy on new materials one week later without further instructions](../claims/spontaneous-strategy-transfer-one-week-delay.md) [+M]: in one randomised study with undergraduates learning foreign-language translations, an instruction to use retrieval practice raised the self-testing learners chose (d = 0.83) and the items recalled during learning (90% against 70%), and a week later, with new materials and no reminder, instructed learners still recalled each item more often and recalled more at the final test. Study choices and feedback seeking did not change. It is the nearest test of the page's claim that teaching learners what works changes what they do when left to themselves; it is one study, one strategy and simple verbal material.
- [Effects of instructions on some measures were inconsistent across experiments, and instructed students did not recall all items to criterion](../claims/mixed-effects-across-experiments-criterion-failures.md) [~M]: the same study qualifies itself: some effects did not replicate across experiments, and instructed learners did not apply the strategy to every item. A taught strategy is used, but not fully.
- [Learners misjudge which learning strategies are effective](../claims/learners-misjudge-effective-learning-strategies.md) [~M] and [Learners Misjudge Spacing Benefits](../claims/learners-misjudge-spacing-benefits.md) [~S]: a survey of 177 undergraduates (most reread; about one in ten reported self-testing), a narrative review, and experiments in which most learners judged massed study better although spacing worked better for most of them. They are why the default design builds effective strategies into the plan rather than leaving strategy to choice. Neither measures what learners do in a self-directed course.
- [Self-paced mastery programs (PSI) reduce college course completion, though not by enough to explain their examination advantage](../claims/self-paced-mastery-programs-reduce-college-course-completion.md) [-M]: across 32 evaluations of self-paced mastery college courses, 23 found higher completion in the conventional class (a small effect on attrition), while the examination advantage held either way. It is a cost of handing pace to the learner; it does not say which supports remove it.
- [Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than explicit instruction](../claims/minimal-guidance-less-effective-for-novices.md) [~S]: a meta-analysis of 580 comparisons favours explicit instruction over unassisted discovery (d = 0.38) and enhanced discovery over other instruction; a classroom experiment with 112 third- and fourth-graders and a narrative review agree. It limits this page for novices on the content: handing them content decisions with little guidance is expected to cost learning. It is about discovery of content, not about choosing pace or product.
- [Process goals lead to better skill acquisition for novices than outcome goals.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [~M]: 84 high-school girls learning a writing revision skill did better with goals about the strategy than about the score, with a suggestion that shifting to outcome goals helps once the skill is mastered; a theoretical chapter agrees. No effect size is recorded.
- [Specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance than easy or vague "do your best" goals.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md) [~M]: one narrative review of goal-setting research, mostly work tasks, no effect size recorded. (The earlier page marked it `[~S]`; one study at `q2` caps it at M.)
- [Self-regulated learning models treat self-monitoring as a core process through which learners notice gaps and adjust strategy](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]: two syntheses, one theoretical, arguing that monitoring against a goal is how learners notice gaps and change strategy; no effect size.
- [Students taught with the Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction gain in self-determination, but not significantly more than a delayed-treatment control group](../claims/self-determined-learning-model-of-instruction-improves-self-determination.md) [~M]: the only trial in the wiki that hands goal-setting, planning and self-evaluation to students under a taught routine and compares them with others. 312 high-school students with intellectual or learning disabilities in 20 districts, cluster-randomised to the model or a delayed-treatment control: the treated group gained on two self-report scales of self-determination, but the between-group differences at the end (d = 0.14 and 0.23) were small and not significant. It is the nearest measure of the "growing independence" outcome and does not establish it; it measures self-report, not learning or later independent study. Used for the disabilities row.
- [Self-determination theory holds that autonomy support raises intrinsic motivation, and a meta-analysis finds expected tangible rewards undermine it](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]: the motivational half of the expectation (sustained engagement). It rests on a narrative review of self-determination theory (autonomy-supportive against controlling instruction, no effect size), a meta-analysis of 128 experiments in which expected tangible rewards undermined intrinsic motivation for interesting tasks (d = -0.4), and a conceptual paper proposing that cognitive autonomy support (evaluating one's work against one's own standard) matters most; its table gathers 8 distinct studies. It supports the engagement claim, not learning comparable to teacher-directed study, and its learners were not choosing goals or resources in a course. Owned by [Learner Choice](autonomy.md).
- The generative-AI claims ([Thoughtless use of generative AI has a significant negative direct effect on undergraduates' self-directed learning](../claims/tuga-negative-direct-effect-sdl.md) [~W], [Self-efficacy and motivation positively predict self-directed learning, with motivation the strongest direct predictor](../claims/se-mov-positive-predict-sdl.md) [~W], [Without explicit guidance and scaffolding, unguided GenAI use may disproportionately benefit already advantaged students and widen performance gaps](../claims/unguided-genai-benefits-advantaged-students.md) [~W], [A review of 50 studies reports that AWE tools can improve the organization and overall quality of writing and encourage self-directed learning](../claims/awe-enhance-organization-quality-self-directed-learning.md) [+W]): one survey path model of 487 undergraduates in which self-reported thoughtless AI use went with lower self-reported self-directed learning (β = −0.42) and motivation was the strongest positive predictor (β = 0.68); an argument without data; and a review's summary sentence with no measure of self-directed learning. Associational or untested; they justify logging AI use (step 10) as a precaution, not as a tested move.
- The qualitative claims ([Adult learners expect AI to enhance autonomy through choices, flexible learning spaces, metacognitive awareness, and structured self-assessment](../claims/ai-enhances-adult-learner-autonomy.md) [+W], [Adult learners expect AI to enhance relevance through real-time goal monitoring, goal breakdown, career companionship, and networking recommendations](../claims/ai-enhances-learning-relevance-goal-career.md) [~W], [Teachers reported that trusting students to generate and pursue their own questions worked better than expected and revealed new student capabilities and gaps](../claims/teachers-surprised-by-student-questioning-capability.md) [+W], [In one extreme case, two sixth-graders with a choice-rich, ungraded STEAM infrastructure extended a game-design challenge into a year-long self-directed project well beyond its designed scope](../claims/choice-rich-infrastructure-supports-productive-deviation-and-learning.md) [+W]): one interview study of what adults hope AI will do (expectations, not outcomes), teacher interviews in one challenge-based science programme that also found students lacked questioning and research skills, and an ethnographic extreme case of two sixth-graders who extended one challenge across a school year. They show what a hand-over can look like and what learners say they want; none compares outcomes. Used in step 9 and the adults, project and AI rows.

Learners, tasks, settings and outcomes differ across these claims (school students in training studies, undergraduates on word lists, college courses, children in science), so do not rank them by effect label. None measures later independence (whether learners go on directing their own learning after the course) at any delay.

## The evidence by question

- *When learners choose their own study strategies, do they choose the ones that work?* [Learners underuse effective learning strategies such as spacing and retrieval practice](../claims/learners-underuse-effective-learning-strategies.md) [~M] — its table gathers the studies behind it and the narrower claims on misjudging strategies and on teaching retrieval practice.

## Further evidence, not yet read against this model

Read against the model on 2026-10-15; the one claim below does not bear on a step, a row or the relationship.

- [Tough's 1971 study found nearly all adults had undertaken a learning project in the past year, 68% self-planned and under 1% for credit, with women's categories logging fewer hours](../claims/tough-1971-learning-projects-prevalence-self-planned.md) [+W]: background only. A 1994 review's report of an interview study of 66 adults; it says most adult learning projects are self-planned, which is context for designing for adults, not evidence about how a hand-over should be run or whether it works.
- [Evidence that PBL fosters SDL is mixed across studies](../claims/pbl-fosters-sdl-mixed-evidence.md) [~W] — attached 2026-10-19 from Loyens et al. (2008), which proposed "Provide conceptual clarity and guidance to teachers and students so PBL can produce self-directed learners".

## Objective and learner-valued goal

The designer's objective is usually two things at once: the content capability at the end of the course, and the learner's ability to plan, monitor and evaluate their own learning afterwards. Elicit separately what the learner wants: a qualification, a skill for their job, to follow an interest, to keep up with peers, or simply to be left alone to work at their own pace. Agreement and divergence are both observations. A learner who values finishing quickly will choose the shortest route and may welcome deadlines; one who values control may resist checkpoints that feel like surveillance, and needs to see what the checkpoint gives back.

For example, an adult in a workplace course may want to be able to handle one difficult kind of customer call, while the designer's objective is the full module on complaint handling plus a habit of planning their own development. Handing over the choice of which scenario to practise first serves the learner's goal; keeping the module's criteria fixed serves the designer's. Record both, so that a completed module is not read as the capability the learner came for, and a learner's narrow focus is not read as failure to self-direct.

## What would revise this model?

The expectation should weaken if comparisons of learner-directed against teacher-directed study, with comparable learners and aligned outcomes, show no difference once the regulation routine is taught, or show that the learners who directed their own study did not go on directing it after the course. If learners who are taught which strategies work still choose poorly once the plan template is removed, building strategies into the template is the active ingredient, not the hand-over. If deadlines and checkpoints in self-paced study do not restore completion, the pace hand-over has a cost the design cannot pay. If novices given content choices with strong checkpoints learn as much as those taught directly, the readiness check is too strict. Do not protect the model by calling every failure "not enough support" or "handed over too early" after the fact.

Completion, achievement at the end of the course, self-reported autonomy or motivation, and later independent learning are separate claims. The present evidence establishes no number of sessions for the hand-over, no readiness threshold and no checkpoint interval.

## Description
Self-directed learning is the principle of helping learners plan, monitor, and manage important parts of their own learning process.

## Implications

Self-directed learning is most valuable when learners are expected to take meaningful responsibility for goals, resources, pacing, or evaluation rather than simply comply with a fixed sequence. The principle does not mean abandoning structure. It means designing supports that let learners make consequential decisions while still receiving enough guidance to avoid drift, confusion, or false confidence. [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation and supports better learning decisions.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) supports making progress visible through check-ins, trackers, or reflection prompts rather than assuming learners will monitor themselves reliably. For many learners, the challenge is not willingness but capability: planning, monitoring, and revising are themselves learned behaviors, and [Process goals lead to better skill acquisition for novices than outcome goals.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) suggests that novices often need process-level guidance before autonomy becomes productive. Self-direction also works better when goals are concrete and demanding enough to organize effort, which is consistent with [Specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance than easy or vague "do your best" goals.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md).

### Context
#### Requirements
- **Learner agency over some part of the learning process** — learners need real decisions to make about goals, resources, strategies, sequencing, or products
- **Structures for planning and monitoring** — self-directed learning depends on visible goals, progress indicators, reflection prompts, or check-ins rather than freedom alone
- **Support for evaluation and adjustment** — learners need ways to judge whether their chosen path is working and what to change next
#### Constraints
- **Many learners need scaffolds before they can direct learning effectively** — weak planning, monitoring, or help-seeking can turn freedom into frustration
- **Choice without criteria can create drift** — learners may select easy, familiar, or low-value paths unless quality expectations are explicit
- **Uneven prior knowledge changes what autonomy is productive** — some learners can self-direct immediately; others need a gradual release

### Target Learners
- Adolescents, adults, and advanced learners expected to manage parts of their own learning process
- Learners in project-based, inquiry-based, or asynchronous environments where planning and persistence matter
- Learners building autonomy after a period of heavier instructional support

- Support transfer from externally structured learning to increasingly independent learning
