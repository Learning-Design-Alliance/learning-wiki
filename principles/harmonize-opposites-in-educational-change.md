# Harmonize opposites: strike a balance between competing educational ideas rather than embracing one method wholesale

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 26 claims (15 for, 11 mixed) · 20 studies (6 review, 5 theoretical, 4 causal, 3 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q4` · 4 of 20 report an effect size · 24 claims rest on one study

## Conditional relationship

This page owns the decision a designer, teacher or school faces when two methods compete for the same time: **adopt one wholesale, or combine them.** When the target is made of several components that the competing methods serve unequally (content knowledge and its application; accurate language form and communicative use; precise and expressive language; individual and group work), assigning each method to the component its evidence favours, and checking each component separately, is expected to cover more of the target than adopting either method for everything. The learner's observed state is a *profile* across those components (`verbal-association` or `concept` knowledge against `skill-performance` or `near-transfer`), not one score; a method adopted wholesale is expected to leave one component behind, and that is only visible when the components are measured apart.

The relationship is conditional on three things: that the components can be named and measured separately; that each competing method has some evidence of serving a component the other does not (a method with strong evidence for the whole target is not "balanced" away); and that the people running each method stay engaged in it. **No claim in the wiki tests "balance competing methods" as a general rule against wholesale adoption.** What the wiki holds is narrower: one randomized trial with middle-school students in which each of two methods won on a different outcome (a trade-off, not a test of combining them), a vocabulary meta-analysis and a review that report combined instruction ahead of either component alone, a school-studies review on combining group rewards with individual accountability, and essays (Ediger 1995, Yang Xu 2010, Jenkins 1962, Monroe 1992 on Mead) that argue for balance without data. The harmful approaches the essay names, such as physical punishment, are outside the balance altogether.

How the converted siblings sit beside this: each owns one balance this page only points to. [Problem-Based Learning](problem-based-learning.md) owns the problem-based versus direct-guidance comparison for application; [Blended Learning](../patterns/blended-learning.md) owns how to split a course between online and in-person work; [Flexible Grouping](flexible-grouping.md) owns when to group by similar need and when to mix; [Phonics](phonics.md) owns early decoding; [Explicit Vocabulary Instruction](explicit-instruction-vocabulary.md) owns taught words followed by encounters in context; [Cooperative Learning](cooperative-learning.md) owns group work with individual accountability. This page is the general rule they share: name the components, give each to the method that serves it, and do not let a reform remove what the old method did well.

## Default design, while the relationship is untested

The earlier page's guidance (and the four pages folded into it on 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-14), kept as a concrete default a designer can act on and revise. Each step says its evidence status.

1. **Split the target into components before choosing a method.** Write the outcome as two to four parts that a single method could serve unequally, using the brief's own terms: for an information-literacy unit, content knowledge and application; for a foreign-language course, form, use and content; for language arts, precise and expressive writing. Untested proposal; from the earlier pages ("no single method covers form, use and content").
2. **Check each component at the start, separately.** A 10-minute pre-check with a few items per component (untested proposal for the length). The middle-school students in the information-literacy trial began weak on both, and their application and knowledge gains then went different ways under the two methods ([knowledge](../claims/problem-based-larger-knowledge-gains.md) [+M]; [application](../claims/simulation-larger-application-gains.md) [+M]).
3. **List each candidate method's pros and the reaction it is likely to provoke.** For a reform, write what it adds, what it may remove from the current method, and who will resist it, before adopting it. Untested; from the earlier page (Ediger's "every action has an opposite reaction"), with team teaching as its worked case: planning with colleagues on one side, friction and domination by one dogmatic member on the other ([Team Teaching Pros And Cons](../claims/team-teaching-pros-and-cons.md) [~W], an essay).
4. **Give each component to the method that serves it.** Direct, dynamic instruction for content knowledge and a scaffolded simulation or practice community for applying it ([knowledge](../claims/problem-based-larger-knowledge-gains.md) [+M]; [application](../claims/simulation-larger-application-gains.md) [+M]); taught word meanings plus encounters in context for vocabulary ([Pairing Contextual Encounters With Explicit Instruction Produces Stronger Vocabulary Outcomes Than Either Alone](../claims/pairing-contextual-encounters-with-explicit-instruction-produces-stronger-vocabulary-outcomes-than-either-alone.md) [+M]); traditional practice of form beside communicative tasks in a foreign language (untested; [Yang Xu's history](../claims/clt-china-1990s-initial-weak-adoption.md) [~W]); the method of fact beside the method of imagination in language arts ([Language Subjective Emotional Aesthetic](../claims/language-subjective-emotional-aesthetic.md) [+W], an essay).
5. **Dose and progression across a unit (untested proposal).** For a unit of about four weeks (the trial's length) with three or four 45-minute sessions a week: in week 1, about 20 minutes of the knowledge-serving method and 25 of the application-serving one per session; by weeks 3–4, about 10 and 35. Early sessions draw examples from the teacher's prepared material; later ones from the learners' own problems or texts. The order (knowledge first) is a proposal; no claim here tests which order works better.
6. **Re-check each component weekly and move time toward the one that lags.** A 5–10 minute check with items for each component at the end of each week; if one component falls behind, give its method more of the next week. A learner moves on from the unit when both components meet the criterion set locally (an untested default: 80% on each check, two weeks running). Untested proposal.
7. **Keep the people running each method engaged in it.** In the trial, the simulation group's knowledge outcomes suffered when live educators disengaged (from the earlier page's Requirements). A combination fails if one half is left to run itself. Untested as a rule.
8. **Within group work, keep an individual part rather than alternating whole days.** Have groups work together but check each member individually, and recognise the group on members' learning; plan some individual work each day as well. Ediger's case for both cooperative and individual work ([Cooperative Learning Versus Individual Endeavors](../claims/cooperative-learning-versus-individual-endeavors.md) [+W], an essay) is supported more directly by school studies of group rewards based on each member's learning ([In school studies, cooperative and peer-learning methods that reward groups on every member's individual learning raised achievement more than methods without such rewards](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+M], carried from achievement in structured school methods to this design).
9. **Use both grouping plans, each for its own job.** Similar-attainment groups for reading instruction, mixed groups for discussion, where slower readers can lean on listening vocabulary ([Mixed Homogeneous Heterogeneous Grouping](../claims/mixed-homogeneous-heterogeneous-grouping.md) [+W], an essay; how to form and re-form groups is modelled in [Flexible Grouping](flexible-grouping.md)).
10. **Pilot a reform alongside the old method before replacing it.** Run the new method on one component or one class for a term while the old method keeps the rest, and compare component checks. A school that insisted on one method as the only correct one is described as having stifled new ideas ([Washington's insistence on the correctness of his Tuskegee method stifled the emergence of new ideas and narrowed his system's application](../claims/washington-tuskegee-method-stifled-new-ideas.md) [+W], a historian's interpretation); untested as a design rule.
11. **For adults and teachers, add lateral learning beside vertical teaching.** Alongside instruction from experienced teachers, schedule regular in-service or continuing-education sessions in which colleagues pass on what is new (proposal: one session a month). Mead's argument, as Monroe reports it, is that rapid change makes yesterday's knowledge useless ([Mead Rapid Change Obsoletes Knowledge](../claims/mead-rapid-change-obsoletes-knowledge.md) [+W]); untested as a design.
12. **Do not balance away a method with strong evidence for the whole target, or put a harmful one in the mix.** If one method has strong comparative evidence on the outcome that matters (systematic phonics for beginning readers' decoding), keep it whole and add the other method around it, not instead of part of it ([phonics](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [~S]; see below). Physical punishment and other harmful approaches are excluded from balancing (from the earlier page).

When a condition is not met: if the components cannot be measured separately, do not claim a balance has worked; record which method ran for how long and check the one outcome you can. If only one method can be staffed well, run that one well rather than both badly (step 7).

## Fitting the design to a situation

The three facts that most change the decision:

- **Which components the target has, and which the brief will judge.** A unit judged only on content knowledge needs less of the application-serving method than one judged on applying it. If unstated, ask: what will learners be asked to do at the end, and which part (knowing, applying, speaking, writing) counts most?
- **What evidence each competing method has, and for which component.** A method with strong evidence for the whole target is kept whole; one whose evidence is for one component is given that component. If unstated, ask: what has each method been shown to improve, for learners like these, and is either already strongly supported on the outcome that matters?
- **Who will run each half, and with how much time.** In the one trial, a method whose live facilitation lapsed lost its knowledge outcome. If unstated, ask: who will lead each method, how many minutes a week does each get, and can someone see the learners' work in both?

## Observation, state and explanation

What can be observed is a component check under stated conditions: which items, which method preceded it and for how long, who facilitated, and each learner's score on each component. "Balanced", "over-committed to one method" and "the reform is working" are inferred states. A single overall score can hide a trade-off: in the trial, the simulation group's overall gain was larger while its knowledge gain was nil ([The participatory simulation group showed significantly larger overall (combined knowledge and application) gains than the problem-based group after four weeks](../claims/simulation-larger-overall-il-gains.md) [~M]).

| Response | Plausible explanations | Observation that could change the interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Application improves, knowledge does not | The application method carries no content teaching; its facilitator disengaged; knowledge items test terms the method never used | Check whether the knowledge items were taught at all under that method; log facilitator time; give one short direct segment and re-check. |
| Knowledge improves, application does not | Too little application time; application tasks too far from what was taught; learners lack a process framework | Give a scaffolded application item close to the taught content, then a further one; compare. |
| Overall score rises, one component flat | The rise comes from one component only | Report components separately before calling the balance a success. |
| Teachers revert to the old method after adopting a reform | The reform removed something the old method did well; exam or class size favour the old method; training was thin | Ask what the old method gave that the new one does not; check what the exam tests. |
| Weaker learners passive in group work | They rely on stronger members; groups lack individual checks; discussion uses vocabulary beyond them | Test each member individually; observe who speaks; try a mixed discussion group with a listening role. |

These rows are proposals, untested as diagnostics. A component check is itself practice for that component, so record each one.

## Evidence and qualifications

No claim tests the general rule. The claims below test parts of it.

- [The problem-based group achieved significantly larger content-knowledge gains, while the simulation group made no knowledge gains](../claims/problem-based-larger-knowledge-gains.md) [+M], [The participatory simulation group achieved significantly larger application gains than the problem-based group](../claims/simulation-larger-application-gains.md) [+M] and [The participatory simulation group showed significantly larger overall (combined knowledge and application) gains than the problem-based group after four weeks](../claims/simulation-larger-overall-il-gains.md) [~M]: one randomized two-group pretest–posttest trial (Newell 2008) with 54 seventh and eighth graders (27 per group) over four weeks, problem-based instruction with direct, dynamic teaching against a computer-mediated participatory simulation. The problem-based group gained more knowledge (the simulation group's knowledge means were essentially unchanged); the simulation group gained more on application and overall (one-tailed p = .04). No effect size is printed. It shows a trade-off between two methods by outcome; it does not test combining them. All three are coded `q3`, `causal · r2`.
- [Problem-based learning shows null or negative effects on basic knowledge acquisition but positive effects on conceptual understanding and transfer relative to direct instruction](../claims/pbl-null-basic-knowledge-positive-transfer.md) [~M]: Kapur's (2016) reading of meta-analyses of problem-based learning against direct instruction; no pooled effect is printed. Its table counts **3 distinct studies** behind it and its narrower claims (Kapur, Newell, one review of principal preparation). It is the general form of the trade-off: which method wins depends on which outcome is measured.
- [Pairing Contextual Encounters With Explicit Instruction Produces Stronger Vocabulary Outcomes Than Either Alone](../claims/pairing-contextual-encounters-with-explicit-instruction-produces-stronger-vocabulary-outcomes-than-either-alone.md) [+M]: Stahl and Fairbanks's (1986) model-based meta-analysis, read as an abstract, concluded that the most effective vocabulary methods combined definitional and contextual information, gave more than one or two exposures and involved deeper processing. No effect size is printed for combined against single methods. This is the closest the wiki has to a test of "combine, not choose", and it is in one domain.
- [Combining intentional and incidental vocabulary learning produces greater gains and better retention than either mode alone](../claims/combined-intentional-incidental-greater-gains.md) [+M]: a review (Zou & Yan 2019) reporting Guo's (2010) study of 93 Chinese university students; both modes gave gains and the combination more, with better retention. Second-hand; no effect size. The same review also reports a direct comparison in which intentional learning beat incidental learning ([Intentional vocabulary learning yields higher, faster gains and better retention than incidental learning through reading](../claims/intentional-beats-incidental-vocabulary-gains.md) [~M]), which qualifies the balance: the parts need not be equal.
- [In school studies, cooperative and peer-learning methods that reward groups on every member's individual learning raised achievement more than methods without such rewards](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+M]: a review of 99 school studies, an elementary meta-analysis and a randomized component analysis; group rewards with individual accountability had a median effect of +.32 against +.07 without (Slavin 1996); its table counts **7 distinct studies**. It supports combining group and individual elements inside cooperative work; it does not test splitting the day between cooperative and individual work, which is what Ediger's essay proposed.
- **Limit:** [Systematic phonics outperforms unsystematic or no-phonics instruction on children's reading overall, but its advantage over whole-word teaching on novel words is untested in children and contested](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [~S]: the National Reading Panel meta-analysis (66 comparisons from 38 experiments, d = 0.41) found systematic phonics ahead of every control, whole language included; a systematic review of 12 meta-analyses argues the evidence does not show it beating whole language. Where one method has strong comparative evidence on the outcome that matters, "balance" can mean diluting it; this is the case the default keeps whole (step 12).
- The essays and accounts behind the earlier page, each one source, coded `q1`–`q2`, no data on the balance itself: [Cooperative Learning Versus Individual Endeavors](../claims/cooperative-learning-versus-individual-endeavors.md) [+W], [Balance Whole Language And Phonics](../claims/balance-whole-language-and-phonics.md) [~W], [Mixed Homogeneous Heterogeneous Grouping](../claims/mixed-homogeneous-heterogeneous-grouping.md) [+W] and [Team Teaching Pros And Cons](../claims/team-teaching-pros-and-cons.md) [~W] (Ediger 1995); [Communicative Approach introduced to China in the 1990s was initially not very communicative](../claims/clt-china-1990s-initial-weak-adoption.md) [~W] (Yang Xu 2010, a historical account: a new method introduced by syllabus and textbook was at first taught in the old way); [Language Subjective Emotional Aesthetic](../claims/language-subjective-emotional-aesthetic.md) [+W] (Jenkins 1962); [Mead Rapid Change Obsoletes Knowledge](../claims/mead-rapid-change-obsoletes-knowledge.md) [+W] (Monroe 1992 on Mead); [Washington's insistence on the correctness of his Tuskegee method stifled the emergence of new ideas…](../claims/washington-tuskegee-method-stifled-new-ideas.md) [+W] (Chennault 2013, interpretive).
- [Some blended learning interventions improve student outcomes, but whether a positive effect exists varies across interventions and domains of achievement](../claims/blended-learning-effects-vary-by-intervention-and-domain.md) [~M] and [Gateway math students benefit from a balance of social, active learning and independent practice, but faculty constraints limit active learning](../claims/balance-active-learning-independent-practice-gateway-math.md) [~M]: a regional laboratory review and a qualitative report, no effect sizes; a combination's effect varies by intervention and outcome, and staff constraints limit the more demanding half.

Learners, domains and outcomes differ across these (middle-school information literacy, vocabulary, school cooperative learning, early reading), so do not rank them by label. None measures a combination's effect at a delay against wholesale adoption of either method.

## Objective and learner-valued goal

The designer's objective is usually stated per component (content knowledge on a unit test, application on a performance task, communicative use in a speaking task). Elicit separately what the learners value: to pass an exam that tests grammar, to be able to talk to people, to enjoy reading, to keep up with changes in their work. Agreement and divergence are both observations. Learners who value the exam may resist the communicative half; those who value talking may skip the form practice. Record which components each learner values, so a gain on the designer's component is not read as the one the learner came for.

For example, teachers in a school adopting a new method may value keeping the routines that work in large classes, while the designer's objective is the new method's outcome. Piloting the new method on one component, with the old method kept for the rest, serves both and shows which component each one moves.

## What would revise this model?

The expectation should weaken if trials comparing a planned combination with wholesale adoption of either method, on outcomes measured by component, show no advantage for the combination, or show that the combination does worse than the stronger method alone because each half gets too little time. It should narrow if the vocabulary finding (combined beats either alone) does not appear in other domains. If, in a domain, one method turns out to serve every component as well as the other, the case for balancing there disappears. Do not protect the model by calling every failure "the wrong balance" after the fact.

Combining two methods, the share of time each gets, the order of the two, and the criterion for moving on are separate questions; the present evidence settles none of them in general.
