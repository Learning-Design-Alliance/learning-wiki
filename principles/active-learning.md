# Active Learning

## Description
Active learning is the principle that learners should do meaningful cognitive work with content rather than only receive it. The core move is not activity for its own sake, but requiring learners to explain, apply, compare, predict, generate, or revise so that understanding is constructed through use.

## Implications
Active learning improves learning when the activity requires learners to think with the content rather than merely touch it. Explanation, prediction, retrieval, and comparison are especially valuable because they force learners to surface assumptions, test understanding, and revise mental models [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding and problem-solving performance.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. That is why active learning typically works best when tasks are focused, accountable, and supported with feedback: poorly structured activity can still become busywork, while well-designed activity can even turn confidently expressed mistakes into opportunities for stronger retention when those errors are corrected and discussed [High-confidence errors lead to better retention after correction than low-confidence errors.](../claims/high-confidence-errors-improve-retention.md) [~S].

The effect size for active learning in STEM is unusually large and consistent for an education intervention: Freeman et al.'s (2014) meta-analysis of 225 studies comparing constructivist/active class designs against traditional lecturing found students in straight lecture courses were 1.5 times more likely to fail (odds ratio 1.95) than students in courses with some active learning, and that assessment performance (exams, concept inventories) improved by about half a standard deviation on average (d = 0.47) — with no significant difference in this effect across biology, chemistry, computer science, engineering, geology, math, physics, or psychology. A publication-bias check found the result would require a large number of unpublished null-result studies (114 for exam/concept-inventory performance, 438 for failure rate) to overturn — leading the authors to compare the strength of evidence to a medical trial that would ethically need to be stopped early for benefit. Beyond raising performance broadly, active learning has also been shown to disproportionately help already-disadvantaged students specifically [Active learning narrows achievement gaps for disadvantaged and underrepresented students](../claims/active-learning-narrows-achievement-gaps.md) [+S], functioning as an equity intervention in gateway STEM courses, not merely an average-performance one.

### Context
#### Requirements
- **A task that requires thinking**: Learners need something to decide, produce, explain, or test.
- **Alignment to the objective**: The activity needs to exercise the target understanding or skill.
- **Processing time**: Learners need enough time to articulate and revise ideas.
#### Constraints
- **Busywork risk**: Activity without cognitive demand does little.
- **Weak guidance**: Novices can flounder if active tasks are under-supported.
- **Time tradeoff**: Active learning often covers fewer topics but with better processing.

### Target Learners
- Learners who benefit from explanation, application, and retrieval rather than passive exposure alone.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Improve conceptual understanding, transfer, and durable retention through use.

### Theory
#### Supporting
- [Constructivism](constructivism.md)
- [Information Processing Theory](../theories/information-processing-theory.md)
#### Contradicting / Qualifying
- Active learning still needs structure, modeling, and feedback to be instructionally effective.

### Claims
- [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding and problem-solving performance.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S] — active tasks are strongest when learners explain, compare, or justify rather than only complete an action
- [High-confidence errors lead to better retention after correction than low-confidence errors.](../claims/high-confidence-errors-improve-retention.md) [~S] — prediction and discussion tasks can make errors instructionally valuable when they are surfaced and corrected
- [Active learning narrows achievement gaps for disadvantaged and underrepresented students](../claims/active-learning-narrows-achievement-gaps.md) [+S] — in STEM gateway courses, a uniformly applied active-learning redesign has been shown to disproportionately benefit already-disadvantaged students, narrowing rather than just shifting the overall achievement distribution
- [Discussion promotes more active thinking than lecture, though lecture is no worse for transmitting information](../claims/discussion-promotes-more-active-thought-than-lecture.md) [+M] — direct observational evidence for why moving from passive lecture toward active formats changes the kind of thinking students actually do in class
