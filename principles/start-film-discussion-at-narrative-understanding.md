# Begin film discussion at the level of narrative understanding before moving to analysis

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because spontaneous, intuitive responses to a film constitute the level of narrative understanding, the article recommends that post-screening discussion start there — registering impressions and emotions with questions such as 'What triggered your attention?' — and explicitly warns educators 'to prevent oneself or one's students from arriving at the level of narratological rationality too soon'. Only after this amplification phase should contextual information, critical perspectives and structural analysis be introduced, followed by appropriation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Opening questions that register spontaneous impressions and emotions aroused during watching
- Associations that keep returning to the core of the film experience rather than drifting away from its images and scenes
#### Constraints
- The film experience itself, not existential questions in the abstract, must remain the main object of analysis during the amplification phase

### Target Learners
- University students discussing films in working groups

### Target Learning Objectives
- Reconstructing and deepening subjective film experience
- Explicitly relating film meaning to one's own life
