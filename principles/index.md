#### A {: #letter-a }

* [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md) - For a learner whose failure on a content task may be linguistic rather than conceptual, removing avoidable wording barriers while explicitly teaching the few essential terms is expected to improve comprehension of the taught material, with weak evidence for transfer to authentic texts or delayed retention.
* [Activation](activation.md) - For learners whose relevant prior knowledge is accurate and is then used by the instruction that follows, an activity that makes it visible before instruction may improve comprehension or retention, while activating topic knowledge alone has often shown little effect and activated misconceptions need confronting.
* [Active Learning](active-learning.md) - For learners meeting new material in a course, class time spent generating, explaining or applying the content with feedback, rather than only listening, is expected to raise course-end exam and concept-inventory scores, qualified by guidance for novices, the kind of engagement and what else changed with the format.
* [Adaptive Learning](adaptive-learning.md) - When learners on a cumulative, decomposable task start from different observed responses, choosing each next task, hint or check from the learner's own recent responses (rather than one fixed path) may improve aligned achievement, conditional on how well the responses diagnose the learner, what the adaptation changes, and whether support is withdrawn as performance grows.
* [Administer universal early literacy screening three times per year plus dyslexia-characteristics screening in K–2](universal-early-literacy-screening-thrice-yearly.md) - The toolkit prescribes universal early literacy screeners for all K–3 students \"administered three times per year with progress monitoring along the way,\" serving as a \"first alert\" that a student may need extra help...
* [Adopt humanistic and radical approaches so adult learners develop as individuals and can question social injustice](humanistic-radical-adult-education.md) - For adult learners, a course in which the educator hears and answers views unlike their own, brings social events in as material to question and leaves action to the learner is expected to strengthen learners' sense of voice, systemic accounts of social problems and readiness to act, but no claim in the wiki tests this, and the one controlled survey found more frequent discussion did not predict more critical reflection.
* [Always report and interpret effect sizes alongside p values for primary outcomes](always-report-interpret-effect-sizes.md) - For anyone writing or reading an evaluation of an instructional intervention, reporting a standardized effect size with its interval, comparison and measure beside every p value, and reading it against effects for the same kind of outcome rather than a universal label, is expected to lead to better adopt-or-revise decisions than significance alone; no claim in the wiki tests that expectation, though claims show significance and size diverging, significance filtering inflating sizes, and sizes depending on the measure.
* [Analogical Reasoning](analogical-reasoning.md) - Comparing a new situation with a provided analogous case, prompted to find the shared relation and followed by the principle, is expected to help learners who do not yet see that structure transfer it to a near new case at a short horizon; analogies from a familiar source domain, far transfer and delayed effects are untested here.
* [Annotating](annotating.md) - For readers of a demanding text, marking it may improve later performance on what was marked, while gains in comprehension are expected only from annotations that make the reader select, explain or question, and remain largely untested.
* [Ask Experts](ask-experts.md) - For a novice or intermediate learner who brings a specific question or attempt to someone with more domain expertise, a consultation in which the expert has the learner explain first, responds with the least help that works, and does less across a series, followed by applying the advice, is expected to improve unaided performance on that task, though no claim here tests expert access as such.
* [Assessment for Learning](assessment-for-learning.md) - Assessment evidence gathered during instruction is expected to support learning when teacher and learner read it against shared criteria, under stakes that let partial understanding show, with a revision step before the outcome is judged.
* [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](authentic-audiences-purposes.md) - For writing and project work, addressing a genuine reader beyond the teacher-as-grader is associated with higher-rated products in small comparisons, conditional on the audience being credible, the criteria explicit and support for revision present.
* [Autonomy](autonomy.md) - For a learner whose engagement is controlled by pressure or reward, meaningful choice within structure, a rationale for required work and non-controlling language are expected to sustain motivation and voluntary engagement; their effect on learning outcomes is small, inconsistent and depends on what the learner controls and when.

#### B {: #letter-b }

* [Build productive instructor-learner relationships so adults feel connected and take an active role in learning](productive-instructor-learner-relationships.md) - For adult learners, deliberate early relationship work by the instructor (learning about each learner, greeting by name, stated working terms, a brief one-to-one, low-exposure participation before public, non-punitive responses to conflict) is expected to raise willingness to participate, raise needs and engage opposing arguments, but no claim in the wiki tests this for adults: the evidence is correlational syntheses and randomised tests of single moves in middle schools.
* [Building Empathy](building-empathy.md) - For a learner forming an interpretation of another person or group, structured perspective-taking with concrete accounts and comparison of interpretations may improve attitudes towards that group, a relationship qualified by outcome, dose, target and horizon.
* [Bullying Prevention and Intervention](bullying-prevention-and-intervention.md) - For school-age learners, a schoolwide plan kept for two or more years (one definition shared by all adults, supervised and organised unstructured times, rehearsed bystander and defender moves, several reporting routes including anonymous ones, and an incident response that protects the target, with separate steps for cyberbullying) is expected to reduce bullying and fear of it more than a response aimed only at the bully and target after incidents; trials of schoolwide behaviour, recess and restorative programmes test schoolwide action against comparison schools, but no claim tests the role-focused design or a cyberbullying response.

#### C {: #letter-c }

* [Character Education](character-education.md) - For school-age and university learners, making ethical understanding, caring response and moral action part of everyday school life (shared conflict-resolution routines, class meetings, service tied to reflection, moral problems in specific situations across subjects) is expected to change behaviour and civic commitment more than a separate civic or moral course or rule recitation; WWC reviews test three branded programmes against usual practice, finding behaviour effects more often than attitude or achievement effects, but no claim tests the integrated form against a separate course.
* [Check-ins](check-ins.md) - A brief, recurring prompt for learners to report their state can guide the next instructional move, but only when the report is read as uncertain evidence, checked against performance where accuracy matters, and acted on.
* [Chunking](chunking.md) - For a learner who cannot yet treat a task's elements as familiar units, presenting the material in meaningful units with boundaries at conceptual breaks, then recombining them, is expected to ease processing and may improve immediate performance; what counts as a unit depends on that learner's prior knowledge.
* [Classroom Space and Procedural Design](classroom-space-and-procedural-design.md) - For a group sharing a classroom, a room arranged for visibility with moderate displays, taught procedures kept separate from a few positively stated rules, tasks pitched at moderate difficulty with structure that fades, and prepared transitions are expected to reduce disruption and lost time compared with improvising them; no claim tests these moves directly, and the nearest test is a meta-analysis of universal classroom management programmes against usual practice.
* [Clear Structure](clear-structure.md) - For learners without an organization of their own for unfamiliar material, making its organization explicit (signals, lean organizers, graphics that match the content and task) may improve what they select and recall on immediate tests, an effect qualified by prior knowledge, structural match and outcome.
* [Cognitive Activation](cognitive-activation.md) - For learners working toward a concept or principle, tasks that require them to explain, compare, predict or justify beyond what was presented, with enough guidance to produce something correct, are expected to improve immediate understanding and near transfer more than tasks completed by recall or copying, with weak evidence for classroom and delayed outcomes and no claim testing cognitive activation as a dimension of teaching quality.
* [Cognitive Disequilibrium](cognitive-disequilibrium.md) - For a learner who holds a confident but inaccurate explanation, meeting evidence it cannot account for and then being helped to resolve the mismatch is expected to improve conceptual understanding more than the correct account alone, with weak evidence that the revision lasts or that conflict without support helps.
* [Cognitive Flexibility](cognitive-flexibility.md) - Revisiting the same concepts across several cases, perspectives or representations that differ in how the concept applies, with explicit prompts to compare them, is expected to help learners with some but rigid knowledge of an ill-structured domain transfer it to a new case at a short horizon; it may cost factual recall, novices may need a simpler start, and delayed effects are untested here.
* [Cognitive Load Management](cognitive-load-management.md) - Across a sequence, matching how presentation, task order and support distribute processing demand to a learner's current task-specific capacity is expected to improve learning, while keeping effortful processing that the intended outcome and horizon need; in the wiki, single levers and two whole load-reducing designs are tested, the withdraw-and-restore policy is not.
* [Cognitive Load Theory](cognitive-load-theory.md) - For a task-specific novice on high-element-interactivity material, reducing avoidable processing is expected to improve performance or efficiency, a relationship that weakens or reverses as task-specific expertise grows.
* [Cognitive Styles](cognitive-styles.md) - For a learner whose attempts stall in a way that looks like a habit of approach (holistic or analytic, quick or deliberate), teaching the approach the task requires to everyone who needs it, faded across sessions, is expected to help more than matching tasks or formats to a measured style; no claim in the wiki tests either use of cognitive styles, and the matching evidence, from learning styles, runs against it.
* [Coherence Principle](coherence.md) - When learners study an explanation of something they do not yet understand, leaving out interesting but irrelevant words, pictures, sounds and music (seductive details) is expected to improve comprehension and transfer over the same explanation with them added; the wiki's claims test this, in a meta-analysis and many experiments, and record effects that range from large in one research programme to small or absent elsewhere, depending on the material, its pacing and complexity, and the learner's prior knowledge, working memory and attentional control.
* [Collaborative Learning](collaborative-learning.md) - For learners who can take part in the task, working jointly so that each member must engage with the others' reasoning is expected to raise individually measured understanding over working alone on the same task, for goals that gain from explaining and reconciling ideas; co-presence, equal participation or group satisfaction alone are not expected to show it.
* [Combine intentional and incidental vocabulary learning rather than relying on incidental learning alone](combine-intentional-and-incidental-vocabulary-learning.md) - For second-language learners building general vocabulary, deliberate study of selected words combined with reading and listening for meaning (glossed, with recurring words) is expected to give more words and better retention than either mode alone, with the deliberate share largest while text coverage is below about 95%; two thin claims test the combination, none tests its order, ratio or dose.
* [Communities of Practice](communities-of-practice.md) - For adults and professionals entering a shared practice, sustained and structured participation with more experienced members, under norms of trust and critical inquiry, is proposed to change their practice and understanding over a term or more, though the wiki holds only descriptive, qualitative and argued evidence for it.
* [Community of Inquiry](community-of-inquiry.md) - For learners in a discussion-based course whose shared inquiry stalls at sharing ideas, the CoI framework proposes that designed social, cognitive and teaching presence together move discourse towards resolution, but the wiki holds only survey associations and descriptive profiles of the presences, no test of that effect on learning.
* [Community-Based Learning](community-based-learning.md) - For a learner whose coursework stays school-internal, giving the work a real role in a community is associated with civic attitudes, self-reported growth and deeper reflection when the work is tied to course concepts, reflection is structured and relationships are supported; no claim here tests its effect on content learning.
* [Compensate for age-related sensory impairments by combining audio input with visual presentation](combine-audio-visual-compensate-sensory-loss.md) - For an adult, often older, learner whose hearing or eyesight has declined, presenting each new item both spoken and written or shown, in a well-lit room with little outside noise and with control of pace, is expected to let them take in more of the new material than speech alone; no claim in the wiki tests this with such learners, only spoken-plus-written words, captions and pacing with other learners.
* [Competency-Based Assessment](competency-based-assessment.md) - Scoring a learner's response on a task that represents the intended capability against explicit criteria and a stated threshold, by consistent assessors, is expected to support better teaching, progression and certification decisions than a rank or seat time, though no claim here tests that comparison and assessor agreement is not guaranteed.
* [Concreteness Fading](concreteness-fading.md) - When learners must come to understand an abstract symbol system or formal structure (an equation, a group property, a permutation formula), starting with a concrete model of it, moving through an iconic picture of the same model, and ending with the abstract notation, each step explicitly linked to the last, is expected to produce better learning and transfer than starting abstract or staying concrete; one experiment, reported second-hand, tests the full sequence here, a meta-analysis supports concrete materials over symbols alone, and the rest is a review's argument and one teaching experiment.
* [Considering The Opposite](considering-the-opposite.md) - For a learner who has committed to a judgment where the evidence could point either way, a prompt to generate specific reasons the opposite could be right, then to decide again, is expected to reduce confirmation, anchoring and overconfidence biases more than a general warning to be objective; no claim in this wiki tests it directly, and the one claim on it rests on a review that names the method without testing it.
* [Contiguity Principle (Spatial and Temporal)](contiguity.md) - When words and a picture explain one thing and neither makes sense without the other, placing each word next to the part of the picture it describes, and presenting narration at the same time as the animation it describes, is expected to improve learning over separated or successive presentation for learners new to the material; meta-analyses test this (spatial and temporal effects of similar size), with larger effects for complex material and system-paced media and a reversal reported for experienced learners.
* [Cooperative Learning](cooperative-learning.md) - For learners who can take part in the task, structured small-group work in which each member's learning is individually checked and counts toward the group's success is expected to raise individually measured achievement over several weeks, more than individualistic or competitive work; grouping alone is not expected to.
* [Criterion- and Norm-Referenced Testing](criterion-and-norm-referenced-testing.md) - A decision about a learner is expected to be more accurate when the score matches the question asked: a criterion-referenced report against skills defined in advance for what a learner can do and should be taught next, a norm-referenced score read against a fitting norm group (and never alone) for relative standing and selection, and a diagnostic profile for why a learner struggles; no claim here tests the relationship.
* [Cultural & Life Experiences Connections](cultural-life-experiences-connections.md) - For learners whose instruction draws on contexts they do not recognise or see no stake in, building some tasks, examples and assessments from their own cultural, community, family and work experience, tied to the concept taught, is expected to raise engagement, open sharing of developing thinking and the competence an assessment can see; no claim here compares it with the same content taught without that connection.
* [Culturally Responsive Classroom Norms](culturally-responsive-classroom-norms.md) - For learners whose home or community norms of talk, questioning, competition, time and conduct differ from the classroom's, widening the classroom's norms (longer wait time, private or all-student response, cooperative options, conventions taught explicitly) and reading a departure from a norm as a possible difference of convention before judging ability or defiance is expected to make questioning show more of what learners know and to raise participation and reduce disciplinary exclusion; no claim here tests this, though claims test wait time, cooperative structures and empathic discipline in general populations.

#### D {: #letter-d }

* [Debriefing](debriefing.md) - For learners who have just shared an experience (a simulation, case, team exercise or real event), a facilitated debrief that establishes what happened, asks for their reasoning at a few decision points against stated criteria, gives task- and process-level feedback and ends with one change to try is expected to improve the next comparable performance more than the experience alone or an unstructured recap, though no claim here compares an experience with and without a debrief.
* [Deliberate Practice](deliberate-practice.md) - For a learner who can already perform a task but has stopped improving on part of it, repeated attempts on that one diagnosed component, set just beyond reliable performance, judged against an explicit standard with feedback and a deliberate change on each next attempt, are expected to improve whole-task performance more than repeating the whole task; no claim tests this in schooling, and the nearest causal evidence is simulation training with deliberate practice in the health professions.
* [Design tools so productive usage is guaranteed by affordances and constraints rather than instructions](productive-usage-guaranteed-by-design.md) - For learners using a simulation, practice tool or AI assistant, building the productive use into the tool (obvious productive actions, controls that invite comparison, limits on shortcuts such as answer-giving) is expected to yield more of the intended activity and better unaided performance than the same tool governed by written directions; no claim in the wiki compares the two directly, and one randomised trial shows a hints-only limit preventing harm without producing a gain.
* [Developing Your Cultural Awareness](developing-your-cultural-awareness.md) - A recurring cycle in which an educator gathers outside evidence about their own practice, names the assumption it shows and changes one decision is expected to make their reading of learners more accurate and less excluding; no claim tests this whole, and one brief empathic-discipline exercise tests a narrow version.
* [Digital Learning](digital-learning.md) - Putting part of a course on a digital tool is expected to help only through the method it makes affordable (more practice with feedback, adaptive hints, prompts to explain, visible progress) and not through the medium itself; no claim here tests a digital against a non-digital version of the same method.
* [Direct Instruction](direct-instruction.md) - For a learner with no working method for a structured target, explicit explanation, modelling and guided practice with checks is expected to beat minimally guided discovery on near-term performance, qualified by task-specific expertise, age, outcome type and horizon.
* [Disciplinary Literacy](disciplinary-literacy.md) - For adolescents who read adequately in general but meet a discipline's texts as novices, a semester or more of instruction that names, models and has them practise the discipline's own reading and reasoning moves on its own texts is expected to improve comprehension of new texts in that discipline and the practised kind of disciplinary thinking; two studies test this against usual or textbook instruction, none against generic strategy instruction, and the one writing measure showed no gain.
* [Discussing Race](discussing-race.md) - For learners studying a topic in which race shapes outcomes or experience, naming race explicitly around a shared text, case or data set, under agreed norms with optional disclosure and a closing debrief, is expected to yield more specific, better-evidenced analysis than neutral treatment or unstructured talk without raising risk for learners in the minority; no claim in the wiki tests this.
* [Distributed Cognition](distributed-cognition.md) - For a task too large for one learner to hold or do alone, putting part of the work into shared representations, tools or partners is expected to raise performance with those aids present, and to build what the learner can do alone only when the parts the target requires stay with the learner and the learner's decisions to rely on aids are calibrated; no claim recorded in the wiki tests distributed against individual work at first hand, while single studies test how much to hand to an AI tool, feedback that calibrates offloading, and the form of a group's shared representation.
* [Dual Coding](dual-coding.md) - For a learner without a working model of a structure or process, pairing the words that explain it with a visual of the same idea, supplied or learner-made, is expected to improve comprehension over words alone; claims test each half against words alone, none tests the sequence, direct tests favour provided over learner-built organizers for transfer, and the drawing supports tested did not help.

#### E {: #letter-e }

* [Educational Readiness](educational-readiness.md) - For learners starting a new stage, course or unit whose tasks assume earlier capabilities, checking each learner's starting response on those capabilities and adjusting the entry point, support and setting to it is expected to let more of them reach the stage's goals than holding them back until judged ready or teaching all from one assumed starting point; no claim in the wiki tests this directly, though claims on delayed entry, repetition and preschool attendance bear on it.
* [Educators should act as mediators and facilitators of learning rather than content disseminators](teacher-as-mediator-not-content-disseminator.md) - For learners working toward understanding they can use, a teacher who states the goal, organizes a task learners act on, questions and helps contingently while they work, and has them explain and transfer what they understood is expected to yield more engagement and usable understanding than presenting the content, provided novices still get explicit guidance; no claim in the wiki tests the teacher's role directly, only neighbouring comparisons (active learning against lecture, teacher-led against student-led inquiry).
* [Effective Classroom Management Plan Criteria](effective-classroom-management-plan.md) - For a group of learners in a shared space, a classroom-wide plan put in place before problems arise and kept consistently (relationships, taught rules and routines, unobtrusive redirection, a short consistent response to persistent misbehavior, self-control taught with external controls planned to fade, and corrections checked for cultural meaning) is expected to reduce disruption and raise engaged time more than improvised practice; one meta-analysis tests planned universal management against usual practice and a K–5 practice guide rates taught behavior expectations as strongly supported, but no claim tests the six-part plan or any of its traditions.
* [Elaborative Reasoning](elaborative-reasoning.md) - For a learner with relevant prior knowledge studying facts or prose, answering why-questions about each statement or generating their own connections is expected to improve recall and recognition over reading it, at a cost in time; randomised experiments with school students test this for science facts, and one experiment finds the reverse for novice undergraduates learning a probability calculation.
* [Employ flexible error treatment strategies matched to objectives, competence, affect, and correction effectiveness](flexible-error-treatment-strategies.md) - For a second-language learner, treating errors according to the activity's objective (accuracy or meaning), the learner's competence with the form, their affect and whether earlier corrections have taken hold is expected to improve accuracy at immediate and delayed tests better than one uniform correction policy; no claim in the wiki tests that matching, though corrective feedback as a whole has a durable effect and correction forms differ.
* [Engagement](engagement.md) - For a learner whose participation in a task is low, a personal contact, a visible reason for the task and visible, reachable progress are expected to raise observed on-task time, completion and persistence, and that engagement is expected to raise learning only when the task requires thinking with the target; claims test single conditions on single outcomes, and none tests engagement as a whole or shows that it causes learning.
* [Epistemic Cognition](epistemic-cognition.md) - When learners must judge claims, explicit teaching and practice of the standards for a particular kind of evidence (checking who is behind a web source, judging a study's methods, justifying an argument with evidence) is associated with better judgments of that kind, measured up to five weeks later; the gains have not been shown to carry over to other kinds of evidence.
* [Error Analysis](error-analysis.md) - For a learner who can already follow a correct solution, comparing an incorrect solution with a correct one, explaining the error and seeing the correction is expected to improve conceptual understanding, and correcting errors (confident ones first) to improve later recall, but the evidence is a few short studies, mostly children in mathematics and adults on recall items.
* [Evaluating Sources](evaluating-sources.md) - For adolescent and adult novices who judge unfamiliar web sources by their own features, several lessons teaching and practising a move off the page (searching what independent sources say about who is behind it) are expected to improve credibility judgments of new sites up to five weeks later; one claim tests this package against untaught classes, though not against a checklist taught over the same number of sessions, nor for research articles or primary documents.
* [Evidence-Based Teaching and Scientific Reasoning](evidence-based-teaching-and-scientific-reasoning.md) - For a teacher, designer or purchaser choosing an approach for other people's learners, writing the belief as a claim some result could refute, seeking a comparison that could have come out otherwise and the case against it, and adopting as a recorded local trial where no comparison exists, is expected to select approaches that do more for learners than choosing by impression, popularity or philosophy; no claim in the wiki tests this for practitioners' own decisions, and one school-reform evaluation tests a school-level version with mixed results.
* [Expanding Social Networks](expanding-social-networks.md) - For adults in transition who can name few people to ask about their goal, repeated structured contact with the same peers, outreach by staff or mentors, and brokered introductions with a follow-up step are expected to yield usable new ties, less isolation and, more slowly, persistence and access to opportunity, though no claim in the wiki tests this against leaving networking to the learner.
* [Experiential Learning](experiential-learning.md) - For learners building applied, professional or interpersonal capability, an experience followed by structured reflection and a further attempt with feedback is expected to improve performance and transfer, while unguided experience for novices learns less than guided instruction.
* [Explicit Instruction: Computer Basics](explicit-instruction-computer-basics.md) - For adult novices with a device, especially older or low-confidence adults, demonstrating one short routine at a time on their own device, having them do it at once with help that responds to each attempt, and withdrawing that help as they succeed is expected to produce more accurate unaided use of those routines than exploration or explanation alone, though no claim in the wiki tests this for computer basics.
* [Explicit Instruction: Internet Search](explicit-instruction-internet-search.md) - For a novice searcher who types whole questions and stops at the first results, explicit modelling of a routine for turning a question into terms, building and revising queries and checking results, practised on real inquiry questions with feedback on the queries, is expected to find more relevant sources with less abandonment than unguided exploration, though no claim in the wiki tests search instruction directly.
* [Explicit Instruction: Math Strategies](explicit-instruction-math-strategies.md) - For a learner who can compute but not yet choose a method for word or non-routine problems, explicitly modelling when and why a strategy fits, comparing methods side by side and guiding justified choice are expected to build flexible, accurate problem solving, though no claim in the wiki tests strategy instruction against its absence.
* [Explicit Instruction: Online Reading Strategies](explicit-instruction-online-reading-strategies.md) - For a learner who reads print adequately but navigates linked online texts without a goal, explicit modelled instruction in a few online reading moves (goal and questions before opening links, choosing links against the goal, a running record of pages, returning from drift, combining pages), practised on curated then open sets of pages with prompts that fade, is expected to improve comprehension and integration across pages; no claim tests this, and the nearest evidence is print strategy instruction and computer-based metacognitive prompts.
* [Explicit Instruction: Vocabulary](explicit-instruction-vocabulary.md) - For a learner whose comprehension or use of language is limited by particular unknown words, explicitly teaching a small selected set and then arranging repeated encounters and use across contexts is expected to improve knowledge of those words and comprehension of texts that use them more than exposure alone, which claims test for school-age and second-language learners, but not for general comprehension, dose or schedule.

#### F {: #letter-f }

* [Feedback Loops](feedback-loops.md) - For a learner whose performance can be observed, information about it is expected to improve later performance only when the loop closes (the information says what to change, a next attempt uses it, and that attempt is checked again on a new item); corrective feedback and correct-and-recheck programmes are supported on average, but no claim here tests a closed loop against the same feedback without a next attempt.
* [Flexible Grouping](flexible-grouping.md) - In a class whose learners differ on the current task, forming temporary similar-need and mixed groups for a stated purpose from recent task evidence, and re-forming them as the evidence changes, is expected to match support and challenge to need better than one whole-class format, and to keep what targeted support gains without the costs fixed attainment sorting shows on grades, pass rates and the lowest group's classroom climate; no claim here tests flexible grouping itself, and the fixed-sorting evidence (one district's algebra policy) shows test-score gains for high attainers as well as those costs.
* [Flipped Learning](flipped-learning.md) - For learners who can follow a topic's first explanation on their own, moving that first exposure before the live session and spending the freed time on application with feedback is expected to give better course outcomes than spending live time on the same exposition, provided learners prepare and the home time is not simply added; one meta-analysis of undergraduate health-professions courses found flipped classes ahead of lecture-based ones on exam performance (SMD 0.57), at low certainty with very high heterogeneity, and no claim here tests it with home time held equal.
* [Formative Assessment](formative-assessment.md) - A conditional model relating learner state, activity and subsequent observation; the linked pattern specifies a reusable design policy.
* [Fostering Creative Thinking](fostering-creative-thinking.md) - For learners producing ideas or products in a domain they partly know, open prompts with many answers, generating kept apart in time from judging, grading and reward, and own ideas before AI are expected to yield more varied ideas, sustained interest and better unaided generation later; no claim in the wiki tests the relationship as a whole, though neighbouring claims test rewards, praise, AI sequencing, explicit strategy instruction and a pause before choosing.
* [Free-Choice Learning Environment Design](free-choice-learning-environment-design.md) - In settings where learners choose whether, what and how long to engage, several entry points, a start from their own interests, no grades or expected rewards for taking part, and an activity that responds to each attempt are expected to lengthen voluntary engagement and, with feedback on the target idea, to improve understanding; no claim tests a free-choice environment as a whole, and one randomised exhibit study tests the feedback part.
* [Functional Behavior Assessment](functional-behavior-assessment.md) - For a learner whose disruptive behavior recurs despite sound classroom management, identifying what triggers it and what it gets them, then changing the trigger and teaching a replacement that meets the same need, with consistent documented responses, is expected to reduce it more than generic or escalating discipline, though no claim here tests a function-based plan against a generic one.

#### G {: #letter-g }

* [Game-based Learning](game-based-learning.md) - For a learner who has not yet reached a stated capability, a game whose winning move is the target reasoning, played over several sessions inside a course of instruction, with modelling for novices and a debrief that asks for the reasoning outside the game, is expected to improve aligned outside-game performance over passive or no instruction; meta-analyses find small-to-moderate average gains that vary widely and disappear against active non-game instruction, and enjoyment and game score are separate outcomes.
* [Gamification](gamification.md) - For learners whose bottleneck is keeping going through repeated practice, a game layer (points, levels, badges, team scores, a theme) added to sound instruction is expected to give a small gain in later performance when it rewards accuracy and improvement rather than activity, leaves meaningful choice and keeps decoration tied to content; one meta-analysis tests gamification on average, and no claim tests these conditions.
* [Goal Setting & Monitoring](goal-setting-monitoring.md) - When a learner holds a specific, challenging goal for a task, with a plan for reaching it and feedback to check progress against it, effort and task performance may rise, qualified by task complexity and the learner's expertise (process goals before outcome goals for novices), commitment and self-efficacy, and whether the outcome measured is behaviour, regulation or learning.
* [Graphic Organizers](graphic-organizers.md) - For learners who do not yet hold a material's structure, a diagram whose layout matches that structure, provided first and later built and explained by the learner, is expected to improve comprehension and retention over text alone; concept-map meta-analyses test this for node-link maps, and no claim tests other organizer forms, partial organizers or fading.
* [Guide change efforts with systemic understanding, focusing interventions on one framework component at a time as needed](systemic-understanding-guides-change-efforts.md) - For a team introducing an innovation that others must adopt, mapping the system it enters, planning the other changes it needs as one coordinated bundle and working on whichever part blocks now is expected to produce more lasting use than introducing the innovation alone; no claim in the wiki tests this, and the evidence is mostly the change literature's own argument.

* [Teacher professional development should include ongoing activities like study teams and peer coaching rather than only occasional large-group sessions](ongoing-study-teams-peer-coaching-in-service.md) - For in-service teachers whose practice does not change after occasional large-group sessions, adding ongoing school-based structures (study teams, peer coaching, a school-based coach) with protected time, an instructional focus and non-evaluative participation is expected to change observed classroom practice more than the sessions alone; no claim in this wiki tests that comparison, and the nearest evidence (a coaching meta-analysis) favours pairing coaching with group training rather than replacing it.
* [Technology should augment, not supplant, learning processes in student-centered environments](technology-augments-not-supplants-processes.md) - When a tool could do part of a task the learner must learn to do unaided, using it so the learner still does that part (attempt first; hints, questions and feedback rather than solutions; simulation or VR beside hands-on practice, not instead of it) is expected to give better performance once the tool is removed than letting it take the part over; single studies of generative AI with adults test this, three experiments limit it, and no claim tests complement against replacement for simulation or VR.
* [Text Chats](text-chats.md) - For learners who need practice writing for a live audience or a lower-threshold way to ask and respond, a focused text chat on a closed task, with content prepared beforehand and someone keeping the pace legible, is expected to produce negotiation of meaning, self-correction and wider participation, but no claim in the wiki tests chat against the same task without it on a learning outcome.
* [Text-to-Speech](text-to-speech.md) - For a learner whose barrier to a text is decoding, vision or fatigue rather than its language, and when the goal is its meaning rather than reading the words, hearing the text read aloud while following the print with control of pace is expected to raise comprehension over print alone; no claim in the wiki tests text-to-speech itself.
* [Transfer of Learning](transfer-of-learning.md) - Showing the same structure in several surface forms, prompting learners to compare them and name what they share, and then having them retrieve and apply it on changed items is expected to raise near transfer to new cases once the original learning is secure; no claim tests varied against single-context instruction directly, and far transfer stayed at floor where it was measured.
* [Treat talking and writing as means to learning through dialogue-based, student-centered instruction](talking-and-writing-means-to-learning.md) - For learners meeting content they must understand, especially through a second language, moving lesson time from teacher talk to learners' own purposeful talk and short writing about the content, in a class where mistakes are safe, is expected to deepen understanding and willingness to speak; no claim tests this whole relationship, writing-to-learn and discussion-versus-lecture claims test its halves, and more talk alone does not raise comprehension.

#### U {: #letter-u }

* [Understanding must be cultivated through exploration, not described or told](understanding-cultivated-not-described.md) - For a learner who can make attempts at a new concept or principle, exploration guided by clues, questions on the learner's own ideas and critique of everyone's work, with the principle then named from that work, is expected to deepen understanding more than being told first or exploring unguided, though no claim tests indirect against direct guidance.
* [Universal Design For Learning](universal-design-for-learning.md) - For a class whose learners vary in access, language, prior knowledge and motivation, planning several routes into content, engagement and expression while the goal and criteria stay fixed is expected to let more learners reach the goal than a single-route design; no claim here tests UDL as a framework, only its parts.
* [Use computer tools that let students explore hypotheses and solutions faster so they can test many ideas without frustration](exploration-tools-fast-hypothesis-testing.md) - For learners exploring how a system behaves, a computer tool that returns the result of each idea in seconds is expected to let them test more hypotheses with less frustration and spend the saved time interpreting, improving conceptual understanding and inquiry skill when the exploration is guided; no claim in the wiki tests the speed of the cycle itself, though guidance during inquiry is supported.
* [User-Centered Design for Learning](user-centered-design-for-learning.md) - For learners who reach instruction through a digital interface, revising that interface against what representative users actually do on the real learning tasks (finding, reading, submitting, interpreting) is expected to remove avoidable effort and failed attempts that compete with the content, though no claim in the wiki compares a user-tested interface with an untested one on a learning outcome.

#### V {: #letter-v }

* [Validity, Reliability, and Bias in Classroom Assessment](validity-reliability-and-bias-in-classroom-assessment.md) - A classroom assessment score is expected to support a decision about a learner only as far as the tasks sample the intended capability without demands unrelated to it, the score is consistent across items, occasions and raters, and no group is disadvantaged by content or administration; no claim here tests that relationship in classrooms, and the claims that bear on it come mostly from large-scale testing and simulations.
* [Video Replay Analysis](video-replay-analysis.md) - For a learner building a real-time performance skill who cannot yet say accurately what they did at the moments that mattered, reviewing a recording against one stated focus, explaining paused moments, debriefing with someone who asks before telling and carrying one change into a recorded next attempt is expected to improve what they notice and their next performance, though no claim tests reviewing one's own recording against an alternative; the evidence is coaching reports, one study of teacher candidates analysing others' video, and findings carried from self-explanation and contingent support.

#### W {: #letter-w }

* [Wise Feedback Across Racial and Ethnic Difference](wise-feedback-across-difference.md) - When critical feedback crosses a racial or ethnic difference, or reaches a learner from a group stereotyped in the domain, specific task-level critique stated together with the high standard it is held to and an explicit assurance that the learner can reach it is expected to be trusted and acted on more than the same critique without that framing; no claim in the wiki tests this, and neighbouring brief identity-targeted interventions are fragile.
