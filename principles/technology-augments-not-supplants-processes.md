# Technology should augment, not supplant, learning processes in student-centered environments

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 14 claims (13 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (2 qualitative, 1 causal, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 1 of 5 report an effect size · 14 claims rest on one study

## Description
Among the assumptions in Table 1, the paper holds that understanding is best supported when processes are augmented rather than supplanted by technology. Stated functions include allowing "novices to become familiar with complex notions without excessive cognitive load" and engaging learners in complex ideas and problems encountered by experts, leading to understanding surpassing what could be achieved without support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Technology support must leave core cognitive processes in the hands of learners
- access to real collision carts equipment and optionally video-analysis tools such as Tracker for analyzing real-world video
- Learners should already have acquired fundamental procedural skills through conventional practical training
#### Constraints
- The paper offers this as an assumption with proposed functions, not a tested effect
- random measurement errors and tacit 3D experience are absent from the simulation; the significance of just-before/just-after velocities is "currently not designed in our simulation"
- Findings come from a single institution, a small sample of 20 undergraduate students, self-reported measures only, a fixed training order, and immediate posttraining assessment only

### Target Learners
- novices encountering complex domains
- physics students in laboratory lessons
- undergraduate radiological technology students with prior procedural training

### Target Learning Objectives
- understanding complex ideas and problems
- understanding measurement error in scientific measurement
- applying conservation of momentum to just-before and just-after collision velocities
- conceptual understanding of radiation
- radiation protection awareness
