# Direct Instruction

## Description
Direct instruction is the principle of teaching explicitly through clear explanation, modeling, guided practice, and feedback.

## Implications

Direct instruction is strongest when learners need a clear model of what success looks like, what steps matter, and how to avoid unproductive confusion early in learning. The principle is often misunderstood as “lecture,” but its instructional core is explicitness: the teacher names the goal, demonstrates the process, checks understanding, and supports early performance before fading responsibility. That matters because novice learners benefit when instruction reduces unguided search and makes correct structure visible [[claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search]] [+M], and it works best when teacher support adapts to learner performance rather than following a rigid script alone [[claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning]] [~M]. The main design challenge is calibration. Too little guidance leaves novices searching blindly; too much guidance, held for too long, becomes redundant as expertise grows [[claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise]] [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- **A clearly specified target performance or concept** — direct instruction works best when the instructor can say what learners should do or understand and what correct performance looks like
- **Modeling plus guided practice** — explanation alone is weaker than explicit demonstration followed by coached attempts
- **Frequent checks for understanding** — the teacher needs evidence that learners are actually tracking the explanation before moving on
#### Constraints
- **Can become passive if it stops at telling** — the principle depends on active checking, practice, and feedback, not presentation alone
- **Less suitable for open-ended exploration as a first move** — where the learning goal is broad generation or divergent inquiry, heavy front-loaded direction may narrow thinking too early
- **Guidance should fade with competence** — the same structure that supports novices can create redundancy for advanced learners

### Target Learners
- Novices who need explicit models, worked steps, and reduced ambiguity
- Learners with weak prior knowledge, fragile schemas, or high risk of cognitive overload during unguided discovery
- Learners who benefit from a clear lesson structure and visible success criteria

### Target Learning Objectives
- Build accurate initial understanding of a concept, procedure, or task
- Reduce confusion and inefficient search during early learning
- Move learners from explanation to supported performance and then to independence

### Theory
#### Supporting
- [[theories/cognitive-load-theory|Cognitive Load Theory]] — explicit demonstration and sequencing reduce unnecessary search and preserve working-memory resources for schema formation
- [[principles/explicit-instruction|Explicit Instruction]] — direct instruction is one of the clearest operational forms of explicit teaching
- [[principles/guided-practice|Guided Practice]] — the principle only works well when explanation is followed by supported learner performance

#### Contradicting / Qualifying
- [[theories/constructivism|Constructivism]] — qualifies that explanation alone is insufficient for deep understanding and that learners still need to actively process, explain, and apply what they are taught

### Claims
- [[claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search]] [+M] — novice learners benefit when instruction reduces unguided search and makes correct structure visible
- [[claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning]] [~M] — direct instruction is stronger when teacher support adapts to learner performance rather than following a rigid script alone
- [[claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise]] [~M] — the usefulness of high-guidance instruction drops as learner expertise increases

## Examples

### Illustrative

**[[strategies/explicit_teacher_modeling|Explicit Teacher Modeling]]** — The teacher breaks a skill into steps, demonstrates each move, and explains why each step matters before students try it with support.

**[[strategies/teacher_modeling|Teacher Modeling]]** — In writing instruction, the teacher composes live, narrates choices, and then guides students through their first attempts so the invisible process becomes visible.

**[[patterns/direct-instruction|Direct Instruction]]** — A lesson sequence that starts with clear explanation and demonstration, checks for understanding, moves into guided practice, and then shifts toward independent work.
