# Cooperative Learning

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 13 studies (6 causal, 6 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q4` · 6 of 13 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Conditional relationship

For learners of any age who can already take part in the target task, and for goals that benefit from explaining, checking and reconciling ideas (concepts, principles and complex skills more than verbal associations), structured small-group work, in which the group's success depends on every member's learning and each member's learning is assessed separately, is expected to raise individually measured achievement relative to individualistic or competitive arrangements over several weeks of use. Grouping alone is not this relationship: the expectation attaches to the structure (interdependence plus individual accountability), and science-classroom comparisons show it can fail. Evidence is mostly from school and undergraduate classrooms, on general or course achievement, at the end of multi-week programmes; it does not forecast a single session, an adult workplace team or a particular learner. The [cooperative learning pattern](../patterns/cooperative-learning.md) specifies a reusable design policy; its local diagnostic branches remain proposals.

## Observation, state and explanation

Record what happens **under stated conditions**: the task, group size and composition, roles, how the reward or grade was computed, the talk the group produced, and each member's individual response afterwards. A group product is an observation about the group, not about any member's capability. "Contributes", "free-rides", "dominates" and "understands" are inferences from talk, work and individual checks; none is directly observed. A quiet member may be learning; a fluent one may be repeating a peer.

| Response | Plausible explanations | Observation that could change the interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Good group product, weak individual check for some members | Division of labour hid a member's gap; the member understood during talk but cannot yet perform alone; the individual check asks for something the group task did not | Compare each member's individual attempt on a task matched to the group task with one on a changed task; record who did which part of the product. |
| One member does most of the talking | Higher task-specific knowledge; higher status in the group; others are uncertain or excluded; the task is divisible so others are working silently | Ask each member privately to explain a step of the product; assign a rotating role and see whether participation and individual responses change. |
| Group talk is off task or socially focused | The task can be done by one person or needs no discussion; members lack the knowledge to discuss it; unclear purpose; ordinary relationship-building | Check whether an individual could complete the task alone; probe prerequisite knowledge individually; ask the group what they think the task is for. |
| Members reach a shared wrong answer | A shared misconception; deference to a confident peer; a reasonable reading of an ambiguous prompt | Ask members individually, before and after discussion, for their answer and reasoning; compare answers in a differently worded prompt. |
| Individual scores rise across a unit | Peer explanation; the individual quizzes and feedback themselves; extra time on task; the teacher's monitoring | Only a comparison that holds quizzes, time and teaching constant can attribute the gain to group structure. Record all of them. |

These rows are proposals, untested with learners. A single probe shifts confidence among explanations; it does not identify a cause, and the probe itself is a learning exposure.

## Evidence and qualifications

- [Structured cooperative goal structures against competitive and individualistic ones](../claims/cooperative-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]: a meta-analysis of 148 studies of early adolescents associates cooperative goal structures with higher achievement and better peer relationships (direction only; the abstract gave no magnitude), and an unpublished technical report on the Learning Together method reports weighted d = 0.91 against individualistic and d = 0.70 against competitive conditions. Checked by the judge against abstracts (all entries pass). It does not establish that any grouping works, gives no magnitude for cooperative learning in general, and does not separate the components.
- [Small structured groups in undergraduate STEM](../claims/small-group-learning-improves-stem-achievement.md) [+M]: 39 North American undergraduate studies, small-group against non-group instruction; achievement weighted d = 0.51, with persistence and attitudes also higher. Effects were larger when the investigator taught (0.73 against 0.41) and in two-group designs; two-year colleges showed a non-significant 0.21. Not settled against its source (abstract). It does not isolate interdependence or accountability: the included studies compared small-group with non-group instruction, and the restriction to *structured* groups is the claim page's own reading.
- [Group rewards tied to individual learning, against single-product or no group reward](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [~M]: a best-evidence review of 99 school studies of four weeks or more reports median effects of +.32 with group goals and individual accountability against +.07 without (vote counts and medians, not pooled estimates); a randomised 2×2 component study of 64 at-risk 4th and 5th graders found reward plus structured tutoring most accurate in computation. Not settled (0 of 3 entries confirmed from abstracts). The same review reports structured interaction that works without rewards, so it supports accountability as a moderator, not as strictly necessary, and it does not measure free-riding itself.
- [Collaborative writing](../claims/collaborative-writing-improves-text-quality.md) [+M]: seven comparisons in grades 4–12 found jointly planned, drafted or revised texts of higher quality than texts written alone (weighted ES 0.75); one small randomised study of 10–11-year-olds found larger gains in *individual* writing after structured pair writing. Partly checked (1 of 2 entries pass, abstract). It shows the distinction between group product and individual capability matters; it is about writing, not cooperative learning generally.
- [Mixed science-classroom comparisons](../claims/cooperative-learning-science-studies-mixed.md) [~W]: a clearinghouse digest reports a grades 3–5 temperature study with no advantage over pair work, a preservice-teacher chemistry course where control classes won all 60 comparisons, and a seven-week biology study with no difference. Not yet checked against its source, second-hand, no effect sizes. It predicts that the expectation can fail in a given implementation; it does not say why.

Keep each study's learners, comparator, duration and outcome when transporting these observations. A pooled d is a programme-level average, not a learner's probability of benefit, and synthesis effects from unlike comparators should not be ranked.

## Objective and learner-valued goal

Ask what each learner wants from the group work and why, separately from the designer's objective. A learner may value being heard, working with friends, finishing quickly or getting a good group grade; the designer may want every member to be able to explain the concept alone a week later. These can diverge sharply in group work, because a learner who values the group grade may rationally divide labour, which serves the product and undercuts the individual objective. Record agreement or divergence as an observation and negotiate the task, roles or grading when they conflict.

For example, a student in an undergraduate chemistry lab group may value a finished lab report and a fair share of the workload, while the designer wants each student to be able to predict and justify a reaction outcome on an individual test. A group report grade alone cannot show whether that objective was met; an individual prediction task, agreed in advance and scored on a stated rubric, can. The pattern shows how to keep both aims visible.

## What would revise this model?

The expectation should weaken if comparable learners in structured groups, with both interdependence and individual accountability in place, fail to outperform matched individual or competitive conditions on individually measured, independent outcomes. If gains turn out to be carried by the individual quizzes, feedback or time on task rather than by group interaction, the model should credit those components instead. If structure without rewards works as well as rewards plus structure, as the review cited above suggests it sometimes does, drop rewards from the required core. Do not protect the model by relabelling every failure as "not real cooperative learning" after the fact; record the structure in advance.

Immediate group performance, individual achievement at the end of a unit, delayed retention, collaboration skill and belonging are separate claims. The present evidence does not establish an optimal group size, composition or reward scheme, effects for adult workplace teams or online groups, or benefit for every learner in a group.

## Examples

### Validated
- [Cooperative learning improves achievement when groups have positive interdependence and individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S] — Johnson & Johnson's (2009) synthesis of 60+ years of research found cooperative methods consistently outperformed competitive and individualistic conditions when both defining conditions were present.

### Illustrative

**[Jigsaw](../patterns/cooperative-learning.md)** — Each group member masters a unique segment of material in an "expert" group, then teaches it to their home group. Interdependence is structural: no member can complete the task alone. Aronson's original desegregation-era design remains one of the most replicated cooperative structures.

**[Think-Pair-Share](../patterns/think-pair-share.md)** — Learners think individually, rehearse with a partner, then share publicly. The individual think phase provides accountability; the pair phase provides elaboration. Low-cost and applicable to nearly any [class discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md).

**[Peer Instruction (Eric Mazur, Harvard)](https://blog.mazur.harvard.edu)** — Students answer a concept question individually, discuss with neighbors to convince each other, then re-answer. ConcepTest discussion produces large conceptual gains in physics; the individual vote before and after discussion supplies accountability.

**[Complex Instruction (Stanford)](https://cste.stanford.edu)** — A status-treatment cooperative approach (Cohen & Lotan) using group-worthy tasks, assigned roles, and teacher interventions that publicly assign competence to low-status students, directly addressing free-riding and participation inequities.

**[PALS — Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies](https://my.vanderbilt.edu/pals/)** — A structured peer-tutoring program in reading and math in which dyads alternate tutor and tutee roles with scripted routines and point systems tied to individual improvement; validated in numerous randomized trials.

## Description
Cooperative learning organizes instruction so that small groups of learners pursue shared goals under two defining conditions: **positive interdependence** (one member's success depends on all members' learning) and **individual accountability** (each member's learning is separately assessed). Structured interaction — discussion, explanation, mutual checking — converts group activity into learning rather than mere task completion.

## Implications

Cooperative learning is among the most consistently supported instructional approaches, but its benefits depend on structure, not proximity: simply seating learners together produces little gain, while designs that enforce interdependence and accountability produce reliable achievement effects [Cooperative learning improves achievement when groups have positive interdependence and individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Meta-analytic evidence shows cooperative methods outperform both competitive and individualistic structures across ages and subjects, with the largest gains where group goals are tied to individual learning [Springer, Stanne & Donovan (1999) meta-analysis of cooperative learning in postsecondary settings.](../claims/cooperative-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. The mechanism is largely generative: explaining to peers, arguing for positions, and reconciling differing ideas force elaboration that solitary work does not [Collaborative writing improves text quality through mutual elaboration.](../claims/collaborative-writing-improves-text-quality.md) [+M]. Without individual accountability, groups can develop free-rider problems in which weaker members coast and stronger members do the work, eroding learning for everyone [Cooperative learning without individual accountability produces free-rider effects and reduced learning.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [-M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks genuinely requiring multiple perspectives or pooled effort ([Collaborative Learning](collaborative-learning.md)) — tasks one learner can complete alone invite parallel solo work
- Explicit teaching of collaborative skills ([Articulation](../elements/articulation.md), [Active Listening](../strategies/active-listening.md)) — turn-taking, explaining, and disagreeing productively do not emerge spontaneously
- Individual assessment of each member's learning ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)) — quizzes, individual write-ups, or random-report protocols
- Group processing time ([Check-ins](../elements/check-in.md)) — brief structured reflection on how the group worked sustains collaboration quality

#### Constraints
- Fails when accountability is group-only: high-ability members dominate and low-ability members disengage [Cooperative learning without individual accountability produces free-rider effects and reduced learning.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [-M]
- Less effective for tasks requiring sustained individual concentration or idiosyncratic pacing; forced grouping can add extraneous load [Cognitive overload degrades learning when coordination demands exceed task benefits.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [~M]
- Gains shrink when group composition is highly heterogeneous in prerequisite knowledge without scaffolds, because shared ground for discussion is absent [~W]
- Poorly specified tasks produce off-task socializing rather than academic interaction [-M]

### Target Learners
- Learners across K–12 and higher education; effects are robust across age bands [+S]
- Learners who benefit from verbalizing reasoning — explaining to peers is especially valuable for intermediate learners consolidating partial understanding
- Learners at risk of disengagement; well-structured groups increase belonging and persistence [Belonging interventions improve outcomes, particularly for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Caution with novices on high-element-interactivity tasks: discussion overhead can exceed its elaboration benefit until basic schemas form [~M]

### Target Learning Objectives
- Conceptual understanding that benefits from explanation, argument, and reconciling alternative ideas
- Problem-solving and application tasks with multiple solution paths
- Communication, perspective-taking, and teamwork as explicit outcomes
- Retention through generative processing (retrieval via peer quizzing, teaching roles)

### Theory
#### Supporting
- [Social Learning Theory](../theories/social-learning-theory.md) (Bandura) — peers model strategies and standards; observing and being observed shapes effort and self-efficacy
- [Situated Learning](../theories/situated-learning.md) — knowledge is built through participation in a community practice; groups approximate legitimate peripheral participation
- [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md) — cooperative structures satisfy relatedness and can support autonomy and competence when roles are meaningful
- [Constructivism](../theories/constructivism.md) — social negotiation of meaning drives conceptual change; disequilibrium between peers' ideas prompts restructuring

#### Contradicting / Qualifying
- [Cognitive Load Theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) — for novices on complex material, coordination and communication demands add extraneous load; cooperative formats should follow, not precede, initial schema building
