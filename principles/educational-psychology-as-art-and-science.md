# Educational Psychology as Both Art and Science

## Description
Art is "skill acquired by experience, study, or observation... an occupation requiring knowledge or skill" (Merriam-Webster, 2020a) — an expression of thoughts and ideas, a manifestation of experience and understanding, and a practice of what is already known. Science is "the state of knowing... knowledge or a system of knowledge covering general truths or the operation of general laws especially as obtained and tested through scientific method" (Merriam-Webster, 2020b) — a process of acquiring understanding and discovering truths through systematic study.

Educational psychology is both. Researchers use the scientific method and research designs to study phenomena that influence learning and education, and this research can be applied to the art of teaching. Practitioners, in turn, use this research to hone their craft and become effective teachers. The relationship is not one-directional: with expertise comes innovation, and with innovation comes more questions for systematic study. Teaching decisions — how to sequence a lesson, which technique fits a particular class — draw on craft knowledge that isn't fully reducible to a research finding, even where research constrains and informs the choice.

## Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Willingness to treat research findings as informing, rather than replacing, professional judgment about a specific class or student
- A practice of turning classroom observations and innovations back into questions that could be studied systematically
#### Constraints
- Treating teaching as pure craft (ignoring research evidence) risks repeating known ineffective practices
- Treating teaching as pure applied science (ignoring situated judgment) risks over-standardizing decisions that depend on a specific class, student, or moment

### Target Learners
- Preservice and practicing teachers forming a professional identity that has to hold both scientific evidence and situated craft judgment at once

### Target Learning Objectives
- Recognizing when a teaching decision is well-settled by research evidence versus when it depends on artful, situated judgment the evidence doesn't resolve

### Theory
#### Supporting
- [Reflective Practice](reflective-practice.md) — the practitioner side of this loop: using reflection on one's own experience (not only external research) to refine practice
