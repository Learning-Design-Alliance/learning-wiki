# Position VR-based radiation training as a complement to, not a substitute for, conventional hands-on training

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that VR-based training with radiation visualization supports conceptual understanding and radiation protection awareness, while conventional training retains advantages in experiential immersion and psychomotor realism. It states that "VR-based training should be strategically positioned as a complementary educational modality" supporting conceptual understanding and radiation protection awareness alongside conventional practical training. The findings are most applicable to students who have already acquired fundamental procedural skills through conventional practical training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners should already have acquired fundamental procedural skills through conventional practical training
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a single institution, a small sample of 20 undergraduate students, self-reported measures only, a fixed training order, and immediate posttraining assessment only

### Target Learners
- undergraduate radiological technology students with prior procedural training

### Target Learning Objectives
- conceptual understanding of radiation
- radiation protection awareness
