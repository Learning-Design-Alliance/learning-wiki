# Guided Practice

## Description
Guided practice is the instructional principle of giving learners repeated opportunities to apply a skill or concept while support is still available. It sits between initial explanation or modeling and independent performance. The purpose is not mere repetition: guided practice lets learners attempt the task, receive targeted feedback, and gradually assume more of the work as scaffolds are faded. It is especially important when a skill has multiple steps, hidden decision points, or a high likelihood of early error.

## Implications
Guided practice matters because early application is where misunderstanding becomes visible, but also where novices are most likely to rehearse errors if left alone too soon. Multiple supported attempts, contingent feedback, and gradual fading help learners stabilize a new routine without turning support into dependency [Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+S] [Fading support promotes the transfer of responsibility from instructor to learner.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+S]. The design challenge is calibration: too little guidance leaves learners floundering, while too much held for too long creates redundancy and weak transfer [Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]. Strong guided practice therefore stays close to the learner’s current need and points clearly toward independence, often by moving from modeled examples into near-transfer attempts rather than into independent performance immediately [Example–problem sequences reduce cognitive load and improve learning outcomes](../claims/worked-examples-example-problem-sequences.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- **A clear model or explanation first**: Guided practice works best after learners have seen or heard what good performance looks like.
- **Contingent support**: Instructors need to observe learner attempts closely enough to give help where it is actually needed [Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+S].
- **Multiple attempts**: Learners need enough practice rounds to adjust based on feedback rather than only try once.
- **A path toward independence**: Support should reduce as competence grows [Fading support promotes the transfer of responsibility from instructor to learner.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+S].
#### Constraints
- **Premature independence**: If support is removed too early, learners may rehearse errors or disengage.
- **Permanent support**: If guidance never fades, learners may complete tasks successfully without building independence [Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M].
- **Practice without diagnosis**: Repetition alone is weak when misconceptions are not noticed and corrected.
- **Over-fragmentation**: Excessive chunking can obscure the whole-task purpose if practice never reconnects to authentic performance.

### Target Learners
- **Novice learners**: Benefit most when a new skill still requires support to perform successfully.
- **Learners practicing multistep procedures**: Strong fit for literacy, numeracy, writing, digital tasks, and applied routines.
- **Learners needing confidence through success with support**: Guided practice can make difficulty manageable without hiding the challenge.
- **Learners transitioning from model to performance**: It bridges explanation and independent application.

### Target Learning Objectives
- **Procedural fluency**: Rehearsing a skill accurately enough that it becomes more stable and efficient.
- **Error correction during learning**: Catching misconceptions before they harden through repeated unguided practice.
- **Transfer of responsibility**: Moving learners toward independent performance as support fades.
- **Retention through structured practice**: Strengthening learning by applying ideas soon after they are introduced.

### Theory
#### Supporting
- [Cognitive Load Theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) — guided practice reduces unnecessary search and helps novices focus on meaningful steps rather than floundering.
- Self-regulated learning — learners develop monitoring and adjustment by attempting tasks with support still available.
- Instructional scaffolding traditions — guidance is calibrated to current performance and withdrawn as competence develops.
#### Contradicting / Qualifying
- Some advanced learners may need less guided practice and more independent or whole-task challenge.
- Guided practice should not replace authentic performance; it should prepare learners for it.

### Claims
- [Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+S] — guided practice works best when support responds to the learner’s actual error pattern and current need
- [Fading support promotes the transfer of responsibility from instructor to learner.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+S] — guidance should narrow as competence grows so practice leads toward independent performance
- [Example–problem sequences reduce cognitive load and improve learning outcomes](../claims/worked-examples-example-problem-sequences.md) [+S] — guided practice is especially effective when learners move from modeled examples into closely related attempts
- [Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M] — prolonged high guidance can become redundant once learners no longer need the same level of support

## Related Principles
- [Worked Examples](worked-examples.md) — guided practice often follows worked examples so learners can apply a model immediately.
- [Scaffolding and Fading](scaffolding-and-fading.md) — guided practice is one of the main places where contingent support and fading are enacted.
- [Immediate Feedback](immediate-feedback.md) — the quality of guided practice depends heavily on quick correction and coaching.
- [Chunking](chunking.md) — breaking complex tasks into manageable parts often makes guided practice more workable for novices.

## Examples
- **Teacher-led problem solving**: Learners solve a near-transfer item while the instructor prompts, checks, and intervenes only where needed.
- **Supported writing conferences**: Learners draft or revise with short targeted coaching on one or two moves at a time.
- **Guided reading or decoding practice**: Learners rehearse a specific reading move with immediate cueing and correction.
- **Software practice with hints**: A digital system provides step-level prompts that fade as accuracy improves.
