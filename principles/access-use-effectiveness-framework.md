# Access-Use-Effectiveness Framework

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)

## Description
Davies and West (2014) argue that technology-integration efforts should be evaluated as progressive stages rather than as a single access/no-access binary. Their three stages: (1) **access** — do teachers and students have working, adequate technology available; (2) **use** — do they actually use it for instructional purposes, as opposed to administrative tasks or passive consumption; and (3) **effectiveness** — is the technology used in pedagogically sound, student-centered ways that measurably facilitate learning.

The framework was developed as a corrective to access-only indicators (such as the earlier LoTi Framework) that treat widespread device availability, or even pervasive use, as itself sufficient evidence of quality integration. Davies and West's review of national survey data shows most schools plateau at stage 1 or 2: 97% of teachers report daily computer access, yet 29% of those with daily access rarely or never use it instructionally, and even when technology is used, it more often serves record-keeping or information-gathering than pedagogically-driven redesign of instruction. This is the crux of the framework's diagnostic value: a school reporting high device saturation and high classroom use can still be evaluated as failing at stage 3 if the use amounts to digitized worksheets rather than a pedagogical change technology specifically enables.

The framework operates at the institutional or programmatic level — evaluating a school's or district's overall technology-integration effort — which distinguishes it from [Technology Integration Levels (SAMR / RAT / PIC-RAT)](technology-integration-levels.md), which judge the effect of a single lesson's or task's specific technology choice, and from [TPACK](tpack.md), which assesses an individual teacher's knowledge rather than an institution's implementation.

## Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Data collection at all three stages — device/access counts alone are insufficient; usage logs or observation are needed to distinguish use from non-use, and pedagogical review is needed to distinguish effective from merely administrative use
#### Constraints
- Access is a necessary but nowhere near sufficient condition for effective integration — treating 1:1 device rollout as the endpoint of a technology-integration initiative, rather than its starting condition, is the framework's central warning
- Moving from stage 2 to stage 3 (use to effectiveness) typically requires professional development in pedagogy, not additional technology — throwing more devices or software at a use-without-effectiveness problem does not resolve it

### Target Learners
- Not learner-facing directly — an evaluative framework for school/district technology leaders and instructional designers assessing an institution's technology-integration program, in the same category as [Performance Technology](performance-technology.md)

### Target Learning Objectives
- Not a learning objective — an institutional diagnostic for whether a technology investment is translating into pedagogically meaningful classroom practice

## Examples
- A district reporting 97% daily device access, where survey data still show large shares of teachers rarely using the devices instructionally — stalled at stage 1–2
- A classroom using tablets daily only for digitized worksheets and drill exercises — stage 2 (use) without stage 3 (pedagogically transformative effectiveness)
