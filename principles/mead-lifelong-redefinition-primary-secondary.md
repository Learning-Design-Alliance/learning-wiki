# Redefine primary and secondary education as lifelong stages so adults retain the right to study throughout life

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)

## Description
The report describes Mead's redefinition of educational stages: primary education teaches all children what they need to be fully human, including basic skills of reading, writing, numbers, money, geography, and communication; secondary education builds on primary and "can be obtained in any amount and at any period during the individuals' whole lifetime." Mead argued the adult's need and right to study more is as great as the need to hold the same job until 65, and that keeping children in school longer cannot accomplish the essential educational task.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An educational system assuring all individuals the secondary and higher education they want and can use at any time throughout their lives
#### Constraints
- The report presents this as Mead's proposal in her 1959 NEA Journal essay, not an implemented or evaluated program

### Target Learners
- all children
- adults at any period of life

### Target Learning Objectives
- basic literacy, numeracy, and civic knowledge in primary education; flexible lifelong skill development in secondary education
