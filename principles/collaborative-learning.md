# Collaborative Learning

## Description
Collaborative learning is the principle of designing tasks so learners build understanding through shared work, dialogue, and interdependence rather than isolated effort alone.

## Implications

Collaborative learning is strongest when the task genuinely benefits from multiple perspectives, shared reasoning, or distributed effort. It is not synonymous with placing learners in groups. The principle works when collaboration changes the intellectual work itself: learners explain, challenge, compare, synthesize, and co-construct understanding in ways that would be less likely in isolation [[claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding]] [+S]. The main design problem is quality control. Without structure, group work often becomes uneven participation, social loafing, or parallel solo work under a collective label, which is why collaboration improves when facilitation and support respond to actual learner needs rather than assuming groups will self-organize productively [[claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning]] [~M]. Collaborative work is also stronger when groups monitor progress against visible goals and criteria instead of relying on vague goodwill [[claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation]] [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- **A task that benefits from joint work** — learners need a reason to rely on one another’s thinking, not just to divide labor
- **Structures for contribution, accountability, and synthesis** — roles, prompts, checkpoints, or shared products help make participation visible and meaningful
#### Constraints
- **Group work without design can become uneven or superficial** — collaboration needs norms, facilitation, and task architecture
- **Interdependence is easy to claim and hard to create** — many “group tasks” are really parallel individual tasks with minimal integration

### Target Learners
- Learners working on interpretive, design, problem-solving, or discussion-rich tasks
- Learners who benefit from exposure to alternative reasoning and explanation
- Learners in classrooms where social participation is part of the learning goal as well as the method

### Target Learning Objectives
- Improve collective reasoning, explanation, and participation
- Strengthen the ability to justify ideas, respond to alternatives, and build shared understanding
- Develop collaborative habits such as listening, coordination, and shared accountability

### Theory
#### Supporting
- [[principles/social-learning|Social Learning]] — learners often acquire concepts, strategies, and dispositions through interaction with others
- [[principles/community-of-inquiry|Community of Inquiry]] — collaboration can improve meaning-making when cognitive, social, and teaching presence are aligned
- [[principles/social-interdependence|Social Interdependence]] — positive interdependence helps explain when collaboration improves outcomes rather than merely redistributing work

#### Contradicting / Qualifying
- [[theories/cognitive-load-theory|Cognitive Load Theory]] — collaboration can increase processing demands if tasks, roles, or communication structures are poorly designed

### Claims
- [[claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding]] [+S] — explaining ideas to peers can deepen understanding and reveal gaps
- [[claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning]] [~M] — collaboration improves when support and facilitation respond to learner needs rather than assuming groups will self-organize productively
- [[claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation]] [~M] — collaborative work is stronger when learners and groups monitor progress against visible goals and criteria
