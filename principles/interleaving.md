# Interleaving

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 15 claims (7 for, 8 mixed) · 12 studies (6 causal, 2 quant-synthesis, 2 review, 1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q4` · 4 of 12 report an effect size · 12 claims rest on one study

## Conditional relationship

This page owns the decision of **whether practice mixes kinds or keeps them apart**. When a learner must later decide *which* category an example belongs to, or *which* procedure a problem calls for, and the candidate categories or problem types resemble one another, practising with examples or problems of different kinds mixed together (interleaved) is expected to produce better later classification and problem choice (`near-transfer`, `delayed-retention`) than practising one kind at a time (blocked), even though practice itself usually goes worse when mixed. Interleaving gives the learner repeated chances to compare kinds and to choose, which blocking removes: in a block, the kind of the next item is already known.

The relationship is conditional on four things, each from the evidence below:

- **How alike the categories are.** The benefit grows with similarity *between* categories and shrinks with similarity *within* a category. Where the kinds are easy to tell apart, there is little for mixing to teach.
- **The material.** Across 59 studies the benefit was largest for paintings and naturalistic photographs, smaller for mathematical tasks, absent for expository texts and reversed for word lists, where blocking did better.
- **The goal.** The evidence is about discriminating kinds and choosing among them. It is not evidence that mixing helps a learner master a single new procedure, learn one text, or memorise words.
- **What the learner sees during practice.** Mixed practice feels harder and looks worse while it happens; learners in one study judged blocking more effective after interleaving had served them better. A design judged on practice performance or on learners' preference will choose blocking.

The meta-analysis found no reliable moderation by retention interval, by retention against transfer test, or by whether examples were shown together or one after another; finding no moderation is not evidence that these never matter. It found larger effects in samples of university students than in other samples, so carrying the size of the effect to children or to adults outside education is untested.

How this sits beside its neighbours: [Spaced Learning](spaced-learning.md) holds *when* practice of the same thing recurs; interleaving changes *what sits next to what*, and in most mixed schedules it also spaces each kind, so the two are confounded unless a study separates them. [Sequencing](sequencing.md) holds the wider order of a unit and already carries the blocked-then-mixed choice as one row; [Retrieval Practice](retrieval-practice.md) holds whether practice asks for recall.

## Default design, while the dose is untested

The meta-analysis establishes the direction and its moderators, not a dose. This default is a **labelled proposal**: the order of steps follows the evidence, and the numbers (how many blocked items, how much mixing, how many sessions) are untested choices.

1. **Check the condition first.** List the categories or problem types the learner will have to tell apart. Ask: would a competent person confuse them? If the kinds are not confusable, keep practice blocked and stop here: there is little evidence that mixing dissimilar kinds helps.
2. **Introduce each kind on its own** with two or three worked examples or items, so each is recognisable before it has to be distinguished (the blocked start is a design choice, see Evidence and qualifications).
3. **Mix from the first practice set after each kind has been met**: in a set of 12 items covering three kinds, no two consecutive items of the same kind more than occasionally, in an order the learner cannot predict.
4. **Ask for the choice explicitly**: before solving or labelling, the learner names the kind or the procedure, and gets feedback on the choice as well as on the answer.
5. **Across sessions**, keep mixing earlier kinds into later practice (week 1 kinds A–C mixed; week 2 adds D and keeps A–C in the mix), so the mixed sets also space each kind. Plan at least one delayed, mixed check (a week or more later) before judging the design.
6. **Judge it on the delayed, mixed check**, not on practice accuracy and not on learners' ratings of which practice felt better.

## Fitting the design to a situation

Three facts change the decision most. Ask for them when the brief omits them: **are the kinds the learner must tell apart actually confusable?** (if not, interleaving has little to work with); **what is the material?** (visual categories, mathematical problem types, texts, word lists); and **what will the learner have to do later: choose among kinds, or reproduce one?**

| If the brief says… | Then change… | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| The goal is to recognise or classify visual categories (artists' styles, species, skin lesions, rock types) | Interleave from the first practice set after one or two examples of each; this is where the evidence is strongest | [Interleaving shows positive effects for all visual stimulus types, largest for paintings](../claims/interleaving-strongest-for-paintings-visual-materials.md) [+M] (g = 0.67 for paintings, 0.35 for naturalistic photographs) |
| The goal is choosing the right procedure among mathematics problem types (`procedure`, several types taught) | Mix types in practice and homework once each has been taught; ask the learner to name the type before solving; expect smaller gains than for visual material | [Interleaving benefits are small for mathematical tasks](../claims/interleaving-limited-for-math-texts-and-words.md) [~M] (g = 0.34 for mathematical tasks); [Interleaving improves discrimination](../claims/interleaving-improves-discrimination.md) [+S] (shuffled mathematics practice improved a delayed test) |
| The material is expository text or prose passages | Do not interleave passages to improve learning of the passages; keep each text whole | [Interleaving benefits are absent for expository texts](../claims/interleaving-limited-for-math-texts-and-words.md) [-M] (no significant effect) |
| The material is word lists or vocabulary categories | Block by category; interleaving reversed in this material | [For word materials the effect favoured blocking](../claims/interleaving-limited-for-math-texts-and-words.md) [-M] (g = −0.39) |
| The kinds are easy to tell apart (low similarity between categories), or each kind varies a lot internally | Interleaving has little to work on: keep blocks, or first teach what varies within a kind | [Between-category similarity raises and within-category similarity lowers the interleaving effect](../claims/similarity-and-complexity-moderate-interleaving.md) [~M] |
| Learners are `novice` on every kind and the kinds have not yet been met | Meet each kind on its own first (two or three items), then mix; do not open with mixed practice of unfamiliar kinds | untested proposal; the blocked-then-mixed order is a design choice the meta-analysis did not test; guidance that helps novices may be redundant later ([expertise reversal](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]) |
| Learners are `advanced` on the kinds | Skip the blocked introduction; use mixed sets from the start, harder to discriminate | untested proposal |
| Learners are children or adolescents | Use interleaving where the condition holds, but do not promise the effect sizes above: they come mostly from university samples | [Interleaving effects are larger in university student samples](../claims/interleaving-larger-in-university-student-samples.md) [~M] |
| The test is immediate, or the design is judged on practice scores | Expect mixed practice to look worse; judge on a delayed, mixed check instead | [Interleaving improves discrimination](../claims/interleaving-improves-discrimination.md) [+S] (worse during practice, better at the delayed test); the meta-analysis found no moderation by retention interval ([null moderators](../claims/interleaving-null-moderators-retention-test-presentation.md) [~M]) |
| Learners choose their own practice order (`online-self-paced`, homework, revision) | Build the mixing into the materials or the software; do not rely on learners to choose it, since learners in one study judged blocking more effective | [Interleaving improves discrimination](../claims/interleaving-improves-discrimination.md) [+S] (Kornell & Bjork: most learners believed blocking had served them better) |
| Homework or review sets in a school course | Mix earlier problem types into each later set, alongside new ones | [Spaced and interleaved homework practice improves learning and retention](../claims/spaced-interleaved-homework-practice.md) [+W] (a practice brief summarising research, not a new study) |
| A single session only | Introduce the kinds, then one mixed set and one mixed check at the end; do not claim retention | untested proposal |
| One teacher for many learners | Give everyone the same mixed set and the same "name the kind first" step; look at which pairs of kinds are confused across the class and re-teach those pairs | untested proposal |
| Stakes are high and the kinds are dangerous to confuse (look-alike drugs, similar alarms, similar fault patterns) | Interleave the confusable pairs specifically, and require correct discrimination on a delayed mixed check before sign-off | untested proposal; the condition (high between-category similarity) is where the evidence predicts the largest benefit |
| The brief names its own unit order or textbook sequence (chapter by chapter, one type per chapter) | Keep the brief's order for introducing kinds; change only the practice and review sets, which mix earlier types back in | untested proposal |

## Observation, state and explanation

These rows are proposals, untested with learners as diagnostics. A check is itself practice and changes what it measures, so record each one.

| Response | Plausible explanations | Observation that could change the interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Accurate on blocked practice, many errors on a mixed set | Has learned each procedure but not when it applies; the kinds were mixed before each was recognisable; the mixed items also differ in surface features | Ask the learner to name the kind of each mixed item before solving it; compare naming accuracy with solving accuracy. |
| Names the kind correctly but still solves wrongly | The choice is learned; the procedure itself is not | Give a blocked item of that kind; if it also fails, the problem is the procedure, not discrimination. |
| Confuses two particular kinds consistently | Those two share the features the learner attends to; the distinguishing feature was never pointed out | Show a pair side by side and ask what differs; see whether the confusion persists on new examples. |
| Prefers blocked practice and asks for it | Mixed practice feels slower and less successful; the learner reads difficulty as failure | Show the learner their own delayed mixed-check results under each kind of practice, if both were used. |
| Mixed practice helps on the practised examples but not on new ones | The learner memorised examples rather than the features of the kind | Test with new examples of each kind, not the practised ones. |

## Evidence and qualifications

- [Interleaving improves discrimination](../claims/interleaving-improves-discrimination.md) [+S]: three studies. Kornell & Bjork (2008) found learners classified *new* paintings better after interleaved study of artists, while most believed blocking had helped them more; Rohrer & Taylor (2007) found shuffled mathematics practice gave far better delayed performance despite worse practice; Brunmair & Richter (2019) found the overall benefit depends on category similarity.
- [Interleaved practice produces a moderate overall benefit for inductive category learning](../claims/interleaving-improves-inductive-learning.md) [+M]: g = 0.42 across 238 effect sizes in 158 samples (Brunmair & Richter 2019). The same finding is recorded on [Interleaving category examples improves inductive category learning](../claims/interleaving-improves-inductive-learning.md) [~M]; the two claim pages are candidates for a claim merge.
- [Between-category similarity raises and within-category similarity lowers the interleaving effect](../claims/similarity-and-complexity-moderate-interleaving.md) [~M]: the condition this page is built on.
- [Interleaving benefits are small for mathematical tasks and absent or reversed for expository texts and words](../claims/interleaving-limited-for-math-texts-and-words.md) [~M]: g = 0.34 for mathematical tasks, no significant effect for expository texts, g = −0.39 for words. The limiting claim: interleaving is not a general improvement to any practice.
- [Interleaving shows positive effects for all visual stimulus types](../claims/interleaving-strongest-for-paintings-visual-materials.md) [+M]: g = 0.67 for paintings, 0.35 for naturalistic photographs.
- [Several candidate moderators show no reliable effects](../claims/interleaving-null-moderators-retention-test-presentation.md) [~M]: no moderation by retention interval, retention against transfer test, simultaneous against successive presentation, or publication status. Not detecting a moderator does not show it has no effect.
- [Interleaving effects are larger in pure university student samples](../claims/interleaving-larger-in-university-student-samples.md) [~M]: the effect sizes above may overstate the benefit for other learners.
- [Spaced and interleaved homework practice improves learning and retention](../claims/spaced-interleaved-homework-practice.md) [+W]: a practice brief, not a new study; it carries the idea into homework design.

Not yet read against this page: Rohrer, Dedrick & Stershic (2015), a classroom experiment in mathematics, was fetched in batch 70 but its extraction did not pass validation, so it has no claim yet.

Taken together: mixing kinds in practice reliably helps learners tell confusable kinds apart, most for visual categories and less for mathematics; it does nothing or harm for texts and word lists. No claim in the wiki tests a dose (how much mixing, after how many blocked items), and no claim separates interleaving from the spacing it usually brings with it.

## Objective and learner-valued goal

The designer's objective is usually correct choice among kinds at a later time, without being told which kind it is. Ask separately what the learner wants: a learner preparing for an exam of mixed questions has a goal that interleaving serves directly, while one who wants to feel fluent today may resist mixed practice because it feels worse. Record that preference as an observation. Do not overrule it silently, and do not read it as evidence that blocking works better.

## What would revise this model?

The expectation should weaken if comparisons with comparable learners find no advantage for mixing on a delayed, mixed test where the kinds are confusable, or if the advantage disappears once spacing is held constant. If new syntheses find benefits for expository texts or words, the material rows above should change. If the blocked-then-mixed start loses to mixing from the first item for novices, step 2 of the default design should go.
