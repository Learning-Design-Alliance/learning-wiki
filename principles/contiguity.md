# Contiguity Principle (Spatial and Temporal)

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 30 claims (16 for, 14 mixed) · 22 studies (6 causal, 6 review, 5 quant-synthesis, 2 associational, 2 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q4` · 7 of 22 report an effect size · 25 claims rest on one study

## Conditional relationship

This page owns one decision inside [Multimedia Learning](multimedia-learning.md): **where and when the words go, once a picture has a job.** When an explanation's words and a picture refer to each other, so that neither can be understood alone (a diagram and its labels, a geometry figure and the proof steps, an animation and the narration describing it), and the learners are new to the material, placing each word or sentence next to the part of the picture it describes (**spatial contiguity**) and presenting the narration at the same moment as the matching animation (**temporal contiguity**) is expected to improve learning over designs that put them apart on the page or screen, or present one after the other. The **split-attention effect** is the same relationship from the learner's side: separated sources make the learner search, hold one source in mind and match it to the other, and learning suffers.

Claims in this wiki test the relationship directly, in meta-analyses, and report its boundaries:

- **Size and the two forms.** Across 50 randomised between-group effects (2,375 students), integrating separated information gave d = 0.85 ([Ginns 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2006.10.001)); a later meta-analysis of 58 comparisons (n = 2,426) found g = 0.63 for spatial integration ([Schroeder & Cenkci 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9435-9)). Spatial (d = 0.72) and temporal (d = 0.78) effects did not differ significantly, which is not a test that they are equal.
- **Complexity of the material.** The effect was d = 0.78 for high element-interactivity material and d = 0.28, with an interval crossing zero, for low ([Ginns 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2006.10.001)). Across design principles generally, effects were g = 0.70 in complex and 0.20 in simple media ([Noetel et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329)).
- **Pacing.** Across design principles, effects were larger in system-paced media such as lectures (g = 0.41) than in learner-paced media such as websites (g = 0.27).
- **Expertise.** Integrated text that novice electrical trainees needed became redundant for experienced trainees, who did better with the diagram alone (Kalyuga et al. 1998, as reported by two reviews). Noetel et al. found prior knowledge did *not* significantly moderate design-principle effects as a whole (p = 0.14). The two disagree in grain: one experiment programme on one principle, one moderator test across all principles. Plan for the reversal and check for it.
- **Medium and testing.** Static (print) and dynamic (animation) presentations did not differ reliably in effect. Effects were larger under one-to-one testing (d = .89) than group testing (d = .65), and the group-tested effect is still large enough to matter in a classroom.
- **Mechanism.** The usual explanation is extraneous load from search and matching. A systematic review of 41 comparisons found that cognitive-load measures mostly did *not* differ between integrated and separated designs ([Schroeder & Cenkci 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000372)): the learning benefit stands, the load explanation is not established.

How this sits inside its neighbours: [Multimedia Learning](multimedia-learning.md) decides whether a picture is added and holds the umbrella model; this page decides placement and timing. Speaking the words instead of printing them (the modality principle) is the other way to remove the search a separated layout causes, and it has its own pacing limit. Cutting words that repeat what the picture already shows (redundancy and coherence) is the opposite move to integration, and which one fits depends on whether the words are needed at all. [Cognitive Load Management](cognitive-load-management.md) and [Cognitive Load Theory](cognitive-load-theory.md) hold the wider model that integration is one lever of.

## Default design, while the dose is untested

The meta-analyses establish the direction, its size and the conditions where it is larger; they do not set a dose or a schedule for withdrawing integration. Integration is a change to layout and timing, not added content, so it should not lengthen a lesson. The progression across sessions below is a **labelled proposal**.

1. **Audit the materials (before session 1, about 30 minutes for a lesson's materials).** List every pair of sources that refer to each other: legends and figures, captions on another page, a worked example's text and its diagram, instructions in a manual for an interface on screen, narration that runs before or after the animation. For each, ask whether either source makes sense alone. Integrate only the pairs where neither does. *Basis: [contiguity across space and time](../claims/contiguity-spatial-temporal-large-learning-benefits.md) [+M]; the "neither alone" test is the condition the integration studies used, and the rule for pairs that do stand alone follows the redundancy reversal below.*
2. **Integrate in space.** Put each label on, or touching, the part it names; put each sentence beside the region of the figure it explains; keep figure and text on the same page, slide or screen; replace numbered legends with labels in place. *Basis: [split attention degrades learning](../claims/split-attention-effect-degrades-learning.md) [+M]; [Mayer's spatial tests, 5 of 5](../claims/contiguity-principles-mayer-2008.md) [+M].*
3. **Integrate in time.** In video, narrated animation or a live lecture with slides, say each sentence while its part of the picture is on screen and pointed to; never explain first and show later, or show first and explain later. *Basis: [Mayer's temporal tests, 8 of 8](../claims/contiguity-principles-mayer-2008.md) [+M]; [spatial and temporal effects of similar size](../claims/spatial-temporal-contiguity-equivalent-magnitudes.md) [~M].*
4. **Where integration is impossible** (lab equipment with a separate manual, two screens, a printed handout for a projected diagram), speak the explanation while learners look at the object, in system-paced settings; in self-paced settings keep the printed words but put them in the learner's line of sight beside the object. *Basis: [modality effect](../claims/modality-effect-narration-over-text.md) [+S] as the alternative, limited by [pacing reversing it](../claims/modality-effect-moderated-by-pacing.md) [~M].*
5. **Sessions 1–2 of a topic (learners new to it).** Use fully integrated materials. Judge on whether learners can explain how the parts relate, not on whether they found the page easy. *Basis: integration effects for novices (step 2); [load ratings and learning come apart](../claims/cognitive-load-reduction-improves-learning.md) [~S].*
6. **From session 3, or once a learner can explain a figure unaided, check for reversal.** Give the same kind of figure with no explanatory text and a changed-condition question ("what happens if this part fails?"). A learner who answers well moves to diagram-only or key-label versions of that topic's materials; a learner who does not stays with integrated versions. Re-check at each new topic, since expertise is topic by topic. *Basis: [integration reverses into redundancy for experienced trainees](../claims/expertise-reversal-split-attention-redundancy-review.md) [~M]; the session count and the check are an untested proposal.*
7. **Judge the change on a transfer question** about the content (explain, predict, troubleshoot), and on a delayed test where the course has one. The effects above come mostly from short lessons tested soon after; a delayed horizon is not shown by them.

## Fitting the design to a situation

Three facts change the decision most. Ask for them when the brief omits them: **do the words and the picture need each other** (would the diagram make sense without its text, and the text without the diagram)?; **how much do these learners already know about this content and this kind of figure?**; and **who controls the pace, in what medium** (print or learner-paced screens, system-paced video or a live lecture)?

| If the brief says… | Then change… | Basis |

| The material has many interacting parts (a circuit, a body system, a proof) | Integrate first here; this is where the effect is largest | [Integration helps most with complex material](../claims/element-interactivity-moderates-contiguity-effect.md) [+M] (d = 0.78 high vs 0.28 low) |
| The material is simple, with few interacting parts | Integration is low priority; fix it if cheap, spend design time elsewhere | [Integration helps most with complex material](../claims/element-interactivity-moderates-contiguity-effect.md) [~M]; [design principles matter less in simple media](../claims/design-principle-effects-moderated-by-pacing-and-complexity.md) [~M] |
| Learners are advanced on this content (`advanced`), or already read this kind of diagram fluently | Drop the explanatory text and give the diagram alone or with key labels; check with a changed-condition question | [Integration reverses into redundancy for experienced trainees](../claims/expertise-reversal-split-attention-redundancy-review.md) [-M]; [guidance that helps novices can become redundant](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~S]; qualified by [prior knowledge did not moderate design-principle effects overall](../claims/design-principle-effects-moderated-by-pacing-and-complexity.md) [~M] |

| Phone-only, small screen | Put labels on the figure, or reveal a label beside its part on tap; never put the explanation on a different screen from the figure | untested proposal |

| Children in primary school | Use the same integrated layouts; check understanding with a short "what happens if" question | [Education level did not consistently moderate design-principle effects](../claims/design-principle-effects-moderated-by-pacing-and-complexity.md) [~M]; the contiguity meta-analyses here are not broken down by age |

| Learners are blind or have low vision | Keep the description of each part with that part: in a tactile diagram, braille labels on the part; in audio description, describe each part as it is presented | untested proposal |
| A single session, or a fixed short time budget | Integrate the existing materials; it moves words rather than adding them, so it costs no learner time | untested proposal; the effects are on [immediate tests of short lessons](../claims/integration-of-related-elements-large-mean-effect.md) [+M] |

| The material is private (patients' images, learners' own work) | Annotate inside the same view, as a layer on the image, rather than in a separate document that must be shared alongside it | untested proposal |

## Observation, state and explanation

These rows are proposals, untested with learners as diagnostics. Where a learner looked, how long they took and how hard they say it was are observations; "split attention" and "extraneous load" are explanations, and the second is not established by the measures in this wiki ([Schroeder & Cenkci 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000372)).

| Response | Plausible explanations | Observation that could change the interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Looks back and forth between figure and text; slow; cannot say how the parts relate | Separated sources force search and matching; the figure itself does not show the relation; the words are too hard to read | Re-present one section integrated; if the explanation improves, integrate the rest. If not, check reading access and whether the figure shows the relation. |
| Explains well with integrated material but not from a plain diagram later | Integration did its job and the learner now depends on it; the target requires reading unlabelled diagrams | Decide whether the objective needs unaided reading; if so, fade labels across sessions and check with a plain diagram. |
| An experienced learner is slower or worse with integrated material | The integrated text is redundant for them and hard to ignore | Give the diagram alone with a changed-condition question; if they do as well or better, drop the text for them. |
| Misses the narration's point in a video | Narration runs before or after the relevant visual; pace too fast; language or hearing access | Check the timing of narration against the animation; offer pause control or captions placed by the figure. |

## Evidence and qualifications

- [Presenting related material contiguously in space and time yields large learning benefits](../claims/contiguity-spatial-temporal-large-learning-benefits.md) [+M]: Noetel et al.'s (2022) overview reports Ginns's pooled g = 0.74 (k = 46) for contiguity, spatial g = 0.63 (k = 58, Schroeder & Cenkci 2018) and temporal g = 0.78 (k = 13). These are re-reports of the two meta-analyses below, not further studies.
- [Integrating spatially or temporally separated but related elements produces a large average learning benefit for novices](../claims/integration-of-related-elements-large-mean-effect.md) [+M]: Ginns (2006), 50 effects, 2,375 students, all randomised between-group designs; d = 0.85 [0.68, 1.02], with significant heterogeneity.
- [Split Attention Effect Degrades Learning](../claims/split-attention-effect-degrades-learning.md) [+M]: Schroeder & Cenkci (2018), 58 comparisons, n = 2,426, g = 0.63, holding across the moderators examined. The page's second entry re-reports the same meta-analysis.
- [Contiguity principles: near placement and simultaneous presentation improve transfer](../claims/contiguity-principles-mayer-2008.md) [+M]: Mayer (2008) reviews his group's experiments: spatial, 5 of 5 tests (paper and computer lessons on brakes and lightning), effect size 1.12; temporal, 8 of 8 experiments (pumps, brakes, lungs, lightning), median d = 1.31. One research programme, transfer tests soon after short lessons.
- [Spatial and temporal contiguity effects do not differ significantly in mean magnitude](../claims/spatial-temporal-contiguity-equivalent-magnitudes.md) [~M]: d = 0.78 temporal (13 effects) vs 0.72 spatial (37); equivalence not tested.
- [The benefit of integration is larger for high element-interactivity material](../claims/element-interactivity-moderates-contiguity-effect.md) [~M]: d = 0.78 (43 effects) vs 0.28 (7 effects, interval crossing zero).
- [Design principle effects are larger in system-paced and in complex media, and learner prior knowledge was not a significant moderator (p = 0.14)](../claims/design-principle-effects-moderated-by-pacing-and-complexity.md) [~M]: across all multimedia design principles, not contiguity alone. The prior-knowledge result is a non-significant moderator (p = 0.14), not evidence of no moderation.
- [Contiguity effects show no reliable difference between static and dynamic presentation](../claims/static-dynamic-presentation-no-moderation.md) [~M].
- [Contiguity effects are larger under one-on-one testing than group testing](../claims/testing-format-moderates-contiguity-effect.md) [~M].
- [The split-attention benefit for novices reverses into a redundancy effect for more experienced learners](../claims/expertise-reversal-split-attention-redundancy-review.md) [~M]: Kalyuga et al. (2003) reporting Kalyuga et al. (1998), electrical trainees, and Yeung et al. (1998), integrated notes in text helping low language-competence learners and hindering more knowledgeable ones. No effect sizes printed.
- [Multimedia principles benefit novices](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]: the same two Kalyuga sources; the 1998 experiments are read from the abstract only.
- [Split-attention worked examples lose their advantage, while integrated formats restore it](../claims/split-attention-effect-in-worked-examples.md) [+M]: Tarmizi & Sweller's geometry experiments as reviewed by Atkinson et al. (2000).
- [Cognitive load reduction improves learning](../claims/cognitive-load-reduction-improves-learning.md) [~S]: carries Schroeder & Cenkci (2020), 41 comparisons in which integrated designs did not reliably change cognitive-load measures; the benefit is real, the load mechanism is unconfirmed.
- [Temporal contiguity is associated with facilitated understanding and lower perceived load](../claims/temporal-contiguity-lower-cognitive-load.md) [+W]: two studies as reported second-hand in a narrative review, without effect sizes.

Taken together: for learners new to complex material, words placed with and timed to the pictures they explain beat separated or successive presentation by a large average margin, in print and in animation, with spatial and temporal forms of similar size. The effect is smaller for simple material and for learner-paced media, and is reported to reverse for experienced learners, though one meta-meta-analytic moderator test did not find prior knowledge to matter across principles. No claim in this wiki tests a schedule for withdrawing integration, a delayed horizon, or learners with sensory impairments.

## The evidence by question

- Does placing words with the pictures they describe, in space and in time, improve learning, and by how much? [Presenting related material contiguously in space and time yields large learning benefits](../claims/contiguity-spatial-temporal-large-learning-benefits.md) [+M]
- For which material, media and testing conditions is integration worth most? [The learning benefit of increasing integration is substantially larger for high element interactivity material](../claims/element-interactivity-moderates-contiguity-effect.md) [~M]
- Does integration keep helping as learners gain expertise? [Multimedia principles benefit novices](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

## Objective and learner-valued goal

The designer's objective is usually an explanation of how a system works, or the ability to use a diagram on a new problem. Ask what the learner needs: a technician who must read unlabelled schematics on the job needs integration early and its withdrawal later, while a learner who only needs to recognise parts may need neither. Ask also whether the figure will be available when the capability is used.

## What would revise this model?

The expectation should weaken if new comparisons with novice learners on complex material, holding content and time equal, find no advantage for integrated or synchronised designs at a delayed test, or if the advantage disappears when separated designs give learners full control of pace. The expertise boundary needs a direct test: integrated against diagram-only versions with learners measured for prior knowledge of the specific content, on the same transfer test. If integration helps learners who report no difference in effort, that would be consistent with Schroeder & Cenkci (2020) and would revise the mechanism, not the design.
