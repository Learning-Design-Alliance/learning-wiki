# Pretesting (Prequestions Before Study)

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 25 claims (6 for, 16 mixed, 3 against) · 23 studies (10 causal, 5 quant-synthesis, 4 review, 3 theoretical, 1 associational), `q1`–`q4` · 11 of 23 report an effect size · 19 claims rest on one study

## Conditional relationship

This page owns the decision of **whether to test learners on material before they study it**. When learners are about to study material they do not yet know (a text, a video, a lecture, a set of facts or terms), asking them to attempt a few questions on its central content first, and then letting the study that follows supply the answers, is expected to improve later memory of the questioned content (`immediate-performance` and `delayed-retention` on items about the same content) compared with studying for the same time without the questions. The attempt does not have to succeed: in the recorded experiments almost every pretest item was answered wrongly or left blank, and the benefit was measured on exactly those items.

**Claims in the wiki test this relationship**, in its narrow form: two multi-experiment laboratory studies with undergraduates ([Pretesting enhances learning](../claims/pretesting-enhances-learning.md) [+S]; Experiment 1 of the text study, 75% against 56% correct, d = 1.1, counting only items failed at pretest), and a 2023 review whose accounts of text, video and classroom studies are recorded as separate claims. Both laboratory studies share an author, and the review's findings reach the wiki second-hand, one claim per study it describes.

The relationship is conditional on six things, each from the evidence below:

- **What the later test asks.** The gain is on the content the pretest asked about. For untested content that was in the same passage, a benefit appeared in one of five similar experiments ([Effects of pre-instruction testing on untested but studied materials are highly inconsistent](../claims/pretesting-untested-materials-inconsistent.md) [~M]), and factual pre-questions can depress learning of unquestioned material ([Question prompts improve learning](../claims/question-prompts-improve-learning.md) [~M]).
- **Whether, and when, the answer arrives.** Every recorded benefit came where the reading, video, lecture or feedback supplied the answer after the attempt. With arbitrary paired items (word pairs), only immediate correct-answer feedback produced the effect; with trivia facts, feedback delayed up to 24 hours still did ([Feedback timing moderates pretesting](../claims/feedback-timing-moderates-pretesting.md) [~M]).
- **What the material is.** For word pairs with no meaningful link between the two members, no advantage appeared on cued recall, though recognition tests showed one ([Semantic association moderates pretesting](../claims/semantic-association-moderates-pretesting.md) [~M]).
- **Attempting, not only reading the questions.** Reading the questions without attempting them helped less than attempting them (Experiment 5 on [Pretesting enhances learning](../claims/pretesting-enhances-learning.md) [+S]).
- **The delay.** In one experiment the effect grew from one minute to 30 minutes to one week ([The pretesting effect grows with retention interval](../claims/pretesting-effect-grows-with-retention-interval.md) [+M]).
- **Whether learners stay.** In a randomised MOOC experiment, pre-tests reduced persistence; across all learners post-test learning did not change, while among completers it improved ([Pre-tests reduced adult learners' persistence in a MOOC](../claims/pretesting-can-harm-motivation.md) [-M]). Learners also rank pretesting as the least effective technique even when it helped them most ([Learners fail to recognize the benefits of pre-instruction testing](../claims/metacognitive-illusion-pretesting-underrated.md) [~M]).

How this sits beside its neighbours. [Retrieval Practice](retrieval-practice.md) is testing *after* study, on material already met, where failure without feedback removes the benefit; here failure is the expected case and the study that follows is the feedback, so the two should not be assumed to share moderators. [Productive Failure](productive-failure.md) is an attempt before instruction too, but at a problem whose solution methods learners must generate, judged on conceptual understanding and transfer; a pretest is a short question attempt judged on memory of the answer. [Pre-reading Questioning](pre-reading-questioning.md) is this page narrowed to texts, with the attempt–read–check routine and a progression to learner-written questions; [Activation](activation.md) is the wider family of making prior knowledge visible before instruction, of which a pretest is the best-evidenced form; [Immediate Feedback](immediate-feedback.md) holds the general timing question. This page is not one of the [multimedia learning](multimedia-learning.md) principles: it is about the order of testing and study, whatever the medium.

## Default design, while the dose is untested

The direction and the conditions are from the evidence; no claim in the wiki tests a number of questions, a length of attempt or a schedule across a course, so those are **labelled proposals**.

1. **Pick the content the later assessment will ask about.** Write the pretest on the central ideas, terms or facts of the coming material that the course's own assessment covers, not on peripheral details. The gain is on questioned content ([Pretesting enhances learning](../claims/pretesting-enhances-learning.md) [+S]); untested content may not benefit ([Effects on untested materials are inconsistent](../claims/pretesting-untested-materials-inconsistent.md) [~M]).
2. **Three to six questions per segment of study, two to five minutes.** The recorded studies used five questions before a text passage ([Pre-instruction testing enhances memory for directly tested text content](../claims/pretesting-enhances-learning.md) [+M]) and six questions among two-minute segments of a seven-minute video ([Pre-instruction testing enhances memory for directly tested video content](../claims/pretesting-enhances-memory-tested-video.md) [+M]); the count for other material is an untested proposal.
3. **Ask for an answer or a best guess on every item, privately and unscored.** Short answer or multiple choice; multiple-choice pretests at the start of lectures worked in a ten-week course ([Classroom pretesting improves high-stakes exam performance](../claims/classroom-pretesting-exam-gains.md) [+M]). Say that most guesses will be wrong and that this is the point (framing: untested proposal, against the persistence cost on [Pre-tests reduced adult learners' persistence in a MOOC](../claims/pretesting-can-harm-motivation.md) [-M]).
4. **Make sure the study answers every question, soon.** The text, video segment or lecture must contain the answer. For vocabulary or other paired items, show the correct answer straight after each item ([Feedback timing moderates pretesting](../claims/feedback-timing-moderates-pretesting.md) [~M]); for richer material, the lesson can supply it.
5. **Study at once, with no extra time.** The benefit was against equal extra study time ([Pretesting enhances learning](../claims/pretesting-enhances-learning.md) [+S]), so the pretest minutes can come out of the study time.
6. **Across a course:** open each unit or lecture with a pretest on that unit's content (early sessions: teacher-written items, answers given within the session); in middle sessions, return the pretest items later as [retrieval practice](retrieval-practice.md) after study, which is a different principle with its own evidence; in late sessions, when learners already answer most pretest items correctly before study, the pretest has become a review, so drop it or replace it with harder items on the coming material. Moving on: when a learner gets most of a unit's pretest right, skip the pretest for that learner. The arc and the threshold are untested proposals; the ten-week classroom study is the only multi-week course recorded ([Classroom pretesting improves high-stakes exam performance](../claims/classroom-pretesting-exam-gains.md) [+M]).
7. **Judge it at a delay, on the questioned content.** The effect was largest at one week in the experiment that compared delays ([The pretesting effect grows with retention interval](../claims/pretesting-effect-grows-with-retention-interval.md) [+M]); judge it with the course's own end assessment rather than adding a test.

Dose, as a proposal: two to five minutes of pretest per study segment of 10–30 minutes (videos of 5 to 30 minutes are the range on the video claim), one pretest per unit, for as many units as the course has.

## Fitting the design to a situation

Three facts change the decision most. Ask for them when the brief omits them:

- **Will the study that follows answer the questions, and when?** If unstated, ask: which part of the material answers each question, and how soon after the attempt does the learner meet it?
- **What does the end assessment cover?** If unstated, ask: will the assessment ask about the same ideas the pretest can ask about, or about everything in the material?
- **Can learners leave, and how will they take failing?** If unstated, ask: is the course optional or self-paced, is anything scored, and how confident are these learners?

| VR or a simulation | Ask the questions before entering, not inside the scene, and make sure the scene answers them | untested proposal |

| Learners with a visual or hearing impairment | Make items screen-reader readable and captioned like the rest of the course; give the answer in the same accessible form | untested proposal |
| The brief brings its own materials, routine and assessment or rubric | Keep them as given; write pretest items on the content the brief's assessment covers, not copies of its items, and add no test the brief did not ask for | untested proposal; the classroom gain was on exam questions about pretested content ([Classroom pretesting improves high-stakes exam performance](../claims/classroom-pretesting-exam-gains.md) [+M]) |

Of these 22 rows, 15 cite a claim; the children, advanced, phone, VR, literacy, impairment and private-material rows are untested proposals, and two rows carry a neighbouring finding (retrieval after study; productive failure) and say so.

## Observation, state and explanation

What a designer can observe: each pretest answer, whether it was blank or a guess, how long the learner spent, whether they reached the study that followed, and later performance on questioned and unquestioned items. "Is curious about the answer" or "has a search set for it" are inferred states. A wrong or blank pretest answer is the expected case and shows nothing about effort. These rows are proposals, untested with learners as diagnostics.

| Response | Plausible explanations | Observation that could change the interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Most pretest items answered correctly | The material is not new to these learners; items are too easy or guessable | Ask how they knew; if they knew, drop the pretest for them and use retrieval practice after study. |
| Later test good on questioned items, poor on the rest | Attention narrowed to the questioned content; the questions sampled too little | Compare questioned and unquestioned items of similar difficulty; add a whole-material task and see whether the gap closes. |
| No gain on questioned items | The study did not answer them; the answer came too late for paired items; the test came at once and the effect shows later | Check that each item's answer is in the material and when it was met; re-test at a week. |
| Learners leave or skip at the pretest screen (online) | Discouraged by failing; the step looks like a graded test; time cost | Compare completion with and without the step, or with an unscored "guesses expected" version. |
| Learners copy answers forward or look them up | They read the pretest as a graded test | Restate that it is unscored; remove anything that looks like a score. |

## Evidence and qualifications

- [Pretesting enhances learning](../claims/pretesting-enhances-learning.md) [+S]: two multi-experiment laboratory studies with undergraduates. Text: five experiments with an essay on human vision; attempting questions before reading beat equal extra reading time on the same content in every experiment (Experiment 1: 75% against 56%, d = 1.1), analysing only items failed at pretest; emphasis by italics or bold did not explain it, and reading the questions without attempting helped less. Trivia and word pairs: six experiments, failed attempts followed by the answer beat reading question and answer together (Experiment 1: 41% against 31%, d = 0.58), and items first answered with a wrong guess were later recalled better than items left blank. Every trial ended with the answer, so the studies show the attempt enhancing the study that followed it, not learning on its own. The two studies share an author and use short delays.
- [Pre-instruction testing enhances memory for directly tested text content](../claims/pretesting-enhances-learning.md) [+M], [video content](../claims/pretesting-enhances-memory-tested-video.md) [+M] and [classroom exam performance](../claims/classroom-pretesting-exam-gains.md) [+M]: the 2023 review's accounts of Richland et al. (2009; immediate and one-week posttests), Carpenter and Toftness (2017; six questions among two-minute segments of a seven-minute video) and Soderstrom and Bjork (2023; ten-week research-methods course, pen-and-paper pretests without feedback, 8–9% better on final-exam questions about pretested content). No effect sizes are recorded on these claims.
- [The pretesting effect grows with retention interval](../claims/pretesting-effect-grows-with-retention-interval.md) [+M]: one experiment (Kliegl et al. 2022) with three delays, largest at one week.
- [Effects of pre-instruction testing on untested but studied materials are highly inconsistent](../claims/pretesting-untested-materials-inconsistent.md) [~M]: a benefit for untested content in one of five similar experiments. **This is the limiting claim**: the page promises nothing beyond the questioned content.
- [Question prompts improve learning](../claims/question-prompts-improve-learning.md) [~M]: a review of adjunct questions in prose (abstract only): questions help on the same content, higher-order questions generalise further, and factual pre-questions can hurt unquestioned material.
- [Feedback timing moderates pretesting](../claims/feedback-timing-moderates-pretesting.md) [~M] and [semantic association moderates pretesting](../claims/semantic-association-moderates-pretesting.md) [~M]: the review's synthesis of paired-associate studies; immediate correct-answer feedback was needed for word pairs, delayed feedback sufficed for trivia, and unrelated pairs showed no cued-recall benefit.
- [Pre-tests reduced adult learners' persistence in a MOOC](../claims/pretesting-can-harm-motivation.md) [-M]: 399 adults randomly assigned to pre-tests (with none, basic or elaborate feedback) or none; pre-tests reduced persistence, did not change post-test learning across everyone, and improved it among completers. Read from the abstract; motivation itself was not measured.
- [Learners fail to recognize the benefits of pre-instruction testing](../claims/metacognitive-illusion-pretesting-underrated.md) [~M]: one experiment in the review (Huelser and Metcalfe 2012): pretested related word pairs were remembered best and pretesting was ranked least effective.
- [Retrieval Failure Reduces Benefit](../claims/retrieval-failure-reduces-benefit.md) [-M]: a 61-study meta-analysis of testing after study, where mostly failed attempts without feedback gave no testing effect (g = 0.03). It is the boundary for a pretest whose answer never arrives, carried from post-study testing.

Taken together: for new material studied soon after, a short attempted pretest beats the same time spent studying, on the questioned content, from immediate tests to a week, in laboratory studies and one multi-week classroom course; for unquestioned content, for arbitrary pairs without immediate answers and for learners who can leave, it is not established or has a cost. Where the claims name the learners, they are adults, mostly undergraduates.

## The evidence by question

- Does attempting questions on material before studying it improve later memory of the questioned content, and under what conditions on the answer and the material? [Pretesting enhances learning](../claims/pretesting-enhances-learning.md) [+S]
- Does the gain reach content the pre-questions did not ask about? [Question prompts improve learning](../claims/question-prompts-improve-learning.md) [~M]
- What does failing first cost the learner: persistence, belief in the method, the effect of confusion? [Pre-tests reduced adult learners' persistence in a MOOC](../claims/pretesting-can-harm-motivation.md) [-M]

## Further evidence, not yet read against this model

- [High-confidence errors lead to better retention after correction than low-confidence errors](../claims/high-confidence-errors-improve-retention.md) [~M]: the hypercorrection effect (a review and one experiment); bears on why a confident wrong guess corrected by study is not harmful, carried from corrective feedback to pretests without a test.
- [Generation Effect Improves Retention](../claims/generation-effect-improves-retention.md) [~M]: generating an answer beats reading it, best attested for simple verbal material; a relative of the attempt, not a test of attempting before study.
- [Providing feedback after initial multiple-choice tests cut lure intrusions roughly in half](../claims/feedback-after-multiple-choice-tests-halves-lure-intrusions.md) [~M]: a reason to show answers after multiple-choice pretests, from testing after study.
- [Unsuccessful retrieval attempts confer a smaller benefit than successful retrievals](../claims/unsuccessful-retrieval-smaller-benefit-than-successful.md) [~M] and [Higher self-reported confusion predicts a larger negative effect of incorrect trials](../claims/confusion-amplifies-negative-effect-of-incorrect-trials.md) [~M]: one modelling study of practice data, where failed trials still helped but less, and confusion during mostly failed practice hurt; practice after study, not pretesting.
- [Errorless discrimination training avoids emotional responses and yields more effective transfer](../claims/errorless-discrimination-learning-transfer-advantage.md) [-W], [The usefulness of induction and errorful learning varies with the type of terminal task](../claims/task-type-moderates-induction-error-usefulness.md) [~W] and [Self-discovery through errorful exploration is basically incompatible with error minimization](../claims/self-discovery-incompatible-with-error-minimization.md) [~W]: Glaser's 1966 analysis of errorful learning (pigeon studies, theoretical argument); the older argument against designing for errors, which the pretesting studies, with the answer supplied, do not bear out for verbal material.
- [Adaptive spaced retrieval practice produced higher end-of-semester posttest performance than learner-directed AI study](../claims/adaptive-retrieval-posttest-retention-advantage.md) [~M] and [Post-baseline practice condition has a significant multivariate effect after adaptive AI-assisted pretesting](../claims/condition-multivariate-effect-posttest-effort.md) [~M]: one study of what follows an AI-assisted adaptive pretest; it compares practice conditions after the pretest, not pretesting against none.
- [Retrieval practice improves long-term retention](../claims/retrieval-practice-improves-retention.md) [+M]: the post-study counterpart this design hands over to in later sessions.

## Objective and learner-valued goal

The designer's objective is that learners remember and can use the questioned content on a later assessment the course already has, at a stated delay. The pretest answers are a means, not an outcome, and should never be read as a measure of the learner. Ask separately what the learner wants from the material: a learner who wants to finish quickly experiences the pretest as a delay, and one who reads failing as a verdict may stop, as some did in the MOOC. Record where those diverge.

## What would revise this model?

The expectation should weaken if classroom comparisons with comparable learners and equal time find no advantage on questioned content at a delay, or find that the gain on questioned content is matched by a loss on unquestioned content, so that learning of the whole material is unchanged. It should be narrowed if the effect holds only for fact-like content and not for concepts or application. If unscored, explained pretests still reduce persistence in courses learners can leave, the self-paced row should drop pretests. Studies with children or school-age learners would extend or limit a model that rests on adults. Do not protect the model by calling every null "the wrong questions" after the fact.
