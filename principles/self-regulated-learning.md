# Self-Regulated Learning

## Description
Self-regulated learning is the principle of designing instruction so learners can plan, monitor, adjust, and reflect on their own learning processes. This page serves as the principle-level target for references that emphasize learner control and strategic regulation.

## Implications
Self-regulated learning improves when instruction makes planning, monitoring, and adjustment visible and actionable rather than assuming learners will do these things on their own. [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation and supports better learning decisions.](/claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) supports building routines in which learners compare current performance with criteria, interpret feedback, and decide what to change next. Learners who regularly monitor progress and interpret feedback are more likely to adapt strategies effectively, but most learners need explicit prompts, models, and structures before these habits become reliable. The design move is to externalize regulation first, then gradually hand more of it to the learner.

### Context
#### Requirements
- **Clear goals or standards**
- **Feedback or evidence learners can interpret**
- **Opportunities to adjust strategy**
#### Constraints
- **Learners often need explicit support to regulate well**

### Target Learning Objectives
- Improve planning, persistence, monitoring, and revision.

## Examples
- A writing course uses planning checklists, mid-draft self-ratings, and revision memos so learners can monitor progress against clear criteria.
- An online math platform asks learners to predict difficulty, check performance after practice, and choose the next support option based on that reflection.
