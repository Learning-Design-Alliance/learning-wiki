# Base decisions about teaching grammar primarily on learner needs and variables (Celce-Murcia)

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The bibliography reports, citing Celce-Murcia (1985), that whether to teach grammar should be based primarily upon the need of the learner. Learner variables such as age, proficiency level and education, and instructional variables such as skill, register and need/use should be taken into consideration. The article is described as helpful for teachers making informed decisions about the place of grammar, and also suggests effective ways to focus on form and correct errors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must consider learner variables such as age, proficiency level and education, and instructional variables such as skill, register and need/use.
- Knowledge of learners' beliefs about grammar instruction, gathered for example through surveys of the four construct pairs the article uses.
#### Constraints
- The article notes learner beliefs differ by year of study and context, so preferences must be assessed in the local setting rather than assumed.

### Target Learners
- ESL/EFL learners of varying ages and proficiency levels
- EFL preparatory-year and undergraduate students at English-medium universities

### Target Learning Objectives
- Informed decisions on the role of grammar in language teaching
- English grammar learning and form-focused instruction preferences
