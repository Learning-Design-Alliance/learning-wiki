# Universal Design For Learning

> **Principle** · [All principles](index.md)

## Description
Universal Design for Learning (UDL) is a framework that treats learner variability as the norm and designs instruction proactively around it, rather than retrofitting accommodations after a barrier appears. It recommends providing multiple means of **engagement** (the "why" of learning), **representation** (the "what"), and **action and expression** (the "how"), so that learners with different needs, preferences, and backgrounds can reach the same rigorous goals. UDL borrows its logic from universal design in architecture: ramps and automatic doors built in from the start benefit everyone, not only those with disabilities.

## Implications

UDL shifts the design question from "how do we fix this learner?" to "how do we design the environment so fewer learners need fixing?" Its three principles map onto established learning science: multiple means of representation aligns with [Dual Coding Theory](../theories/dual-coding-theory.md) — presenting content in verbal and visual forms improves recall [Dual coding improves recall.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-recall.md) [+S] — and with load management, since offering chunked, structured materials reduces extraneous load [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]. Multiple means of engagement draws on [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md): offering meaningful choice and relevance supports autonomy and intrinsic motivation [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]. Multiple means of action and expression decouples the learning goal from a single performance format, which is especially important for learners whose barriers are expressive rather than conceptual. Evidence for the framework as a whole is promising but uneven: a meta-analysis of studies between 2013 and 2016 found positive effects on learning outcomes, though the author notes the evidence base is limited by few rigorous experimental studies and inconsistent implementation fidelity [Capp, 2017](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316685135) [+W]. UDL works best when designers first fix a clear, rigorous goal and then vary the pathways — offering options in *how* learners engage, access, or express, not in *what* they are expected to learn.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clearly articulated learning goal that stays constant while pathways vary — without a fixed goal, "multiple means" collapses into unfocused choice
- Multiple representations of core content ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md), [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md), visual and verbal formats) — a single modality guarantees barriers for some learners
- Structured options for expression ([Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md), varied [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) formats) — options must be equivalent in rigor, not just in preference
- Deliberate supports for engagement ([Check-ins](../elements/check-in.md), relevance framing, sustained challenge) — motivation is designed, not assumed

#### Constraints
- Offering choice without scaffolding can overload novices or let them avoid productive difficulty; unstructured choice benefits stronger learners more than novices [~M]
- Implementing UDL as surface-level variety (e.g., "add a video") without addressing barriers produces little measurable gain; studies with weak implementation show attenuated effects [Capp, 2017](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316685135) [-W]
- Overloading lessons with too many simultaneous options increases extraneous cognitive load and decision fatigue, particularly for novices [~M]
- UDL does not replace individualized accommodations: learners with specific disabilities may still need targeted supports beyond universal options [-W]

### Target Learners
- Learners with disabilities or language barriers who are underserved by single-modality, single-pathway instruction
- Learners with variable working memory or attentional capacity who benefit from chunked, multimodal presentation [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]
- Disengaged or low-motivation learners who respond to autonomy and relevance [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- All learners in heterogeneous settings — the framework assumes variability is universal, not exceptional

### Target Learning Objectives
- Equitable access to grade-level conceptual understanding across learner variability
- Development of self-regulated, strategic learners who can select and monitor their own supports [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md)
- Demonstrating the same competency through multiple valid performance formats
- Sustained engagement and persistence on challenging, long-horizon tasks

### Theory
#### Supporting
- [Cognitive Load Theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) — multiple representations and chunked materials manage intrinsic and extraneous load, but only when options are curated rather than unlimited
- [Dual Coding Theory](../theories/dual-coding-theory.md) — combining verbal and visual representation creates redundant memory codes and improves recall
- [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md) — choice, relevance, and appropriate challenge support the autonomy and competence needs that drive engagement
- [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) — UDL's emphasis on learner choice and self-monitoring of supports builds strategic control

#### Contradicting / Qualifying
- [Constructivism](../theories/constructivism.md) — UDL's emphasis on structured, teacher-designed options can sit uneasily with open-ended discovery; the framework works best when options scaffold rather than replace guidance
- Cognitive Load Theory also qualifies UDL: unlimited learner choice imposes navigation load that novices cannot spare [~M]

### Claims
- [Dual coding improves recall.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-recall.md) [+S] — multiple means of representation create redundant memory codes
- [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S] — structured, segmented presentation lowers extraneous load
- [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M] — meaningful choice in engagement and expression supports motivation
- [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S] — feedback must remain actionable across varied expression formats

## Related Principles
- [Cognitive Load Management](cognitive-load-management.md) — UDL's multiple options must be curated to avoid imposing navigation load on novices
- [Chunking](chunking.md) — segmented, structured representation is a core "multiple means of representation" tactic
- [Assessment For Learning](assessment-for-learning.md) — flexible expression formats work only when assessment criteria are transparent and goal-referenced
- [Active Learning](active-learning.md) — multiple means of action and expression typically manifest as varied, participatory performance formats

## Examples

### Illustrative

**[Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md)** — A grid of equivalent tasks through which learners demonstrate the same objective (e.g., write, record, or diagram an explanation). A well-designed board enforces equal rigor across options and requires all learners to hit the same success criteria.

**[CAST UDL Guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org)** — The canonical framework resource, maintained by CAST, organizing dozens of design checkpoints under the three principles. Widely used as a design-review rubric for curriculum and digital learning products.

**[Bookshare](https://www.bookshare.org)** — An accessible ebook library providing alternate formats (audio, braille, large print, dyslexia-friendly) of the same texts, exemplifying multiple means of representation at scale for learners with print disabilities.

**[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Offers the same content as video, transcript, worked examples, and practice with hints, letting learners choose their entry point and re-represent material on demand — a large-scale implementation of multiple means of representation.

**[Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md)** — Individualized supports (extended time, scribing, assistive technology) that remain necessary alongside universal design; UDL reduces but does not eliminate the need for targeted accommodations.

**[Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md)** — Pre-reading structures and graphic organizers that give learners with weaker background knowledge an access route into the same content as their peers.
