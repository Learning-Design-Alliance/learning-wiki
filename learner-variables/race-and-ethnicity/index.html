Race and Ethnicity¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 206 claims (167 show a difference, 29 mixed, 10 no difference) · 155 studies (88 associational, 28 review, 19 causal, 8 theoretical, 7 qualitative, 3 design, 2 quant-synthesis), q1–q4 · 2 of 155 report an effect size · 204 claims rest on one study

How to read this page¶

Race and ethnicity do not cause differences in learning. They are social categories, not learner abilities, and there is no evidence that race or ethnicity itself makes anyone learn more or less. They correlate with outcomes because they track the conditions learners meet: school funding and segregation, access to experienced teachers and advanced courses, tracking, discipline practices, family income and wealth, discrimination and stereotype threat, and tests and materials built around one group's language and culture.

So every gap listed below is a measure of those conditions, not of the learners. A claim that "Black and Hispanic students score lower" says where a system is serving learners unequally; a claim that a design "narrows the gap" says it changed some of those conditions. Read a group difference as a question about circumstances and treatment, never as a prediction about a learner. For design, the useful findings are the second kind: which instruction, support or school practice narrows a gap (see The evidence by question).

Description¶

Race and ethnicity are social categories, recorded in most education research from school and census records or from learners' own reports. In the research they appear as a predictor (gaps in achievement, attainment, discipline and access to advanced courses), as a moderator (a programme whose effect differs across groups) and as the population a programme serves. A gap by race or ethnicity describes unequal circumstances and treatment, such as school funding, teacher assignment, tracking and discrimination; it does not describe an ability of the learners. Categories also differ across countries and datasets, so findings rarely transfer unchanged.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Gaps by race or ethnicity are a baseline for reading an effect: narrowing a gap and raising everyone equally are different results.
  • Many of the findings are descriptive and come from administrative data; they say where gaps are, not what closes them.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners from racial and ethnic groups that schools and courses have historically served less well.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Achievement, attainment, course-taking and discipline outcomes, where gaps are most often measured.
