Access¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables Evidence · 51 claims (38 for, 9 mixed, 4 against) · 37 studies (22 associational, 5 review, 3 causal, 2 quant-synthesis, 2 qualitative, 2 theoretical, 1 design), q1–q4 · 2 of 37 report an effect size · 49 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Whether a learner can perceive and operate the material at all: device, bandwidth, screen reader, captions, motor demands. Distinct from digital literacy, which is whether they can drive it. Access is binary in a way the other dimensions are not — a design that cannot be perceived does not teach less, it teaches nothing — which is why it is checked rather than optimised.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Decides which media and interactions are usable at all, before any question of effectiveness arises.
  • The remedies mostly benefit everyone: captions, clear contrast and text alternatives are not a separate track.
  • 'Everyone is issued the same device' is a real finding, and rules out a class of hedging.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners with sensory, motor or processing differences.
  • Learners on constrained devices or bandwidth — a condition of circumstance rather than of the learner.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Gates every outcome. A learner who cannot perceive the material produces no evidence about anything else.
  • Makes assessment invalid rather than merely harder, which is a different category of problem.

Related Learner Variables¶

  • Digital literacy — whether they can operate it, as against whether they can perceive it.
  • Reading and language — audio routes around a decoding barrier as well as a visual one.
  • Working memory — accommodations often work by removing a load rather than by adding support.
