Executive Function¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 118 claims (71 show a difference, 23 mixed, 24 no difference) · 68 studies (28 associational, 15 causal, 11 review, 10 quant-synthesis, 3 theoretical, 1 design), q1–q4 · 20 of 68 report an effect size · 116 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Executive function covers inhibitory control, updating working memory, and shifting between tasks or rules. It is measured by performance tasks (such as the Stroop, Flanker and dimensional card-sort tasks) and by teacher or parent ratings. It develops through childhood and adolescence, predicts school readiness and achievement, moderates designs that load self-direction on learners, and is the target of training programmes, whose effects rarely transfer beyond the trained tasks. It underlies self-regulation and attention and overlaps with working memory.

How strongly it relates to achievement¶

Mathematics. These are correlations across meta-analyses (Breit et al. 2025, a systematic review of 30 meta-analyses; overview [+W]): how strongly learners who differ on this characteristic differ in mathematics achievement, not how much changing it would change achievement. Many of these characteristics are correlated with one another, and the review is about mathematics only.

  • Executive-function composites r = .40 in preschool and .36 in elementary school; cognitive flexibility .35, more closely related in younger children; inhibition .29–.30, shifting .28–.32. Claim [+W]

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Young children and learners with weaker executive function need more external structure: routines, reminders and fewer simultaneous demands.
  • Training the processes directly has shown little far transfer.

Target Learners¶

  • Young children, learners with ADHD, and others for whom self-directed tasks are hard to manage.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • School readiness, achievement, and classroom behaviour.

The evidence by question¶

  • Does a child's executive function predict achievement, and through what? Children's executive function at the beginning of pre-kindergarten predicts growth in mathematics achievement across the pre-kindergarten year [+M]
  • Does training the processes directly (computerised, working-memory or mindfulness training) improve executive function itself? CCT shows no significant effects on executive functions or attention in healthy older adults [-M]
  • Does attending a pre-kindergarten programme raise children's executive function? Pre-kindergarten attendance yields small positive impacts on social-emotional development, executive functioning, and emotion recognition [+M]
