# Mathematical Flexibility

> **Learner Variable** · [All learner variables](index.md)

## Description
Mathematical flexibility is the ability to keep several representations of a number in mind (a fraction as a shaded region, a decimal, a point on a line) and several ways of solving a problem, and to move among them adaptively, choosing the strategy that suits the problem rather than always using the same one. The Learner Variability Navigator says it helps learners understand concepts more deeply and find solutions more efficiently. It is measured with tasks that test knowledge of multiple strategies for solving equations and judgement of which is most efficient.

LVN follows it from preschool to grade 10. Its general counterpart is cognitive flexibility, part of [executive function](executive-function.md); mathematical flexibility is the domain-specific version. It works across [arithmetic fact retrieval](arithmetic-fact-retrieval.md) (knowing when to retrieve rather than calculate), [operations](operations-and-place-value.md) (estimation strategies), [algebraic thinking](algebraic-thinking.md) and [proportional reasoning](proportional-reasoning.md), and it grows when learners compare strategies in classroom discussion.

## Implications

### Context
- Comparing and discussing several solution strategies for the same problem is the usual way to build it.
- Using several representations of the same number, such as models, numerals and number lines, supports it from the early grades.

### Target Learners
- Learners from preschool to lower secondary who rely on one procedure for every problem.
- Learners moving into algebra and fractions, where strategy choice affects efficiency.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Adaptive choice of strategy and representation in problem solving.
- Conceptual understanding in arithmetic, fractions, algebra and geometry.
