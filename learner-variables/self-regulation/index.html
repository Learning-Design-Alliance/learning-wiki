Self-Regulation¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables Evidence · 77 claims (55 for, 16 mixed, 6 against) · 63 studies (16 associational, 13 review, 11 causal, 9 quant-synthesis, 6 theoretical, 5 qualitative, 3 design), q1–q4 · 14 of 63 report an effect size · 69 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Whether a learner can plan, monitor and adjust without the structure being supplied for them. It decides whether a multi-week deliverable survives without scaffolding and pacing support. The finding that matters most for design is that self-monitoring is unreliable by default: learners judge fluency rather than learning, and confident misjudgement is the normal case rather than the exception [-M]. So the design job is supplying the external signal, not exhorting reflection.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Decides whether an extended deliverable needs imposed checkpoints or can be learner-paced.
  • Self-assessment is inaccurate without training and without criteria — asking a learner how they are doing is not a measurement [-M].
  • The strategies are teachable, which is what separates this from a fixed trait.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners new to a domain, whose monitoring has no accurate standard to compare against.
  • Learners meeting an unfamiliar task genre — a first literature review, a first long project.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Predicts achievement across domains, and predicts completion of extended work far more strongly.
  • Is itself a legitimate learning objective, not only a moderator.

Related Learner Variables¶

  • Motivation — starts the effort that self-regulation then has to sustain.
  • Prior knowledge — accurate self-assessment needs a standard, which novices do not yet hold.
  • Time and continuity — external pacing substitutes for regulation a learner cannot yet supply.

Examples¶

  • Goal Setting — the element form of the most reliable lever
  • Cognitive Strategies — the teachable repertoire rather than the disposition
  • Goal Setting — specific, proximal goals in practice
  • Metacognitive Reflection Prompts — supplies the monitoring question rather than assuming it
  • Self-Regulated Learning — the pattern that sequences plan–monitor–adjust
  • Metacognition — the underlying account
  • Self-Regulated Learning — the cyclical model design draws on
  • Teach less-proficient EFL learners metacognitive strategies, effort regulation, and help-seeking, supported by goal-setting training
  • Design GenAI learning activities that position GenAI as a collaborator to support the full metacognitive chain
