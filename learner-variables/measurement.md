## Description
Measurement, as a mathematical skill, is identifying an attribute of something (its length, area, volume or duration) and comparing it with a unit that has the same attribute (an inch, a square centimetre, a minute), so that the attribute gets a number that can be reasoned with and calculated with. The Learner Variability Navigator describes it as the basis of scientific inquiry and a way into other mathematics, including estimation, fractions and [statistical reasoning](statistical-reasoning.md). It is assessed with instruments that separate spatial or conceptual reasoning about length, distance, perimeter, area and volume from the use of formulas.

LVN follows it from grade 3 to grade 10. It draws on [spatial ability](spatial-ability.md), because measuring involves forming and manipulating mental images and relating an object to its unit, and on [proportional reasoning](proportional-reasoning.md), because understanding a unit rests on the multiplicative relationship between quantities. This page is about learners' measurement skill, not about measuring learners.

## Implications

### Context
- Hands-on measuring, and connecting formulas to what they count, support conceptual as well as formula-based measurement.
- Measurement tasks in science and everyday settings give the skill a use beyond mathematics lessons.

### Target Learners
- Upper-primary and lower-secondary learners working with length, area and volume.
- Learners who can apply formulas but do not understand what a unit is.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Conceptual and formula-based measurement of length, perimeter, area and volume.
- Estimation and the use of measurement in fractions, data and science.
