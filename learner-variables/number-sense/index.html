Number Sense¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 128 claims (94 show a difference, 19 mixed, 15 no difference) · 57 studies (26 associational, 22 causal, 3 review, 2 qualitative, 2 design, 2 theoretical), q1–q4 · 25 of 57 report an effect size · 124 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Number sense is a learner's grasp of how large numbers are and how they relate to one another. It is measured with magnitude comparison (which of two numbers or dot arrays is larger), number line estimation (placing a number on a line with labelled ends) and similar tasks. These measures correlate with mathematics achievement and predict later mathematics learning, and they can be improved by instruction such as number board games and number line activities. It is a domain-specific part of prior knowledge in mathematics.

How strongly it relates to achievement¶

Mathematics. These are correlations across meta-analyses (Breit et al. 2025, a systematic review of 30 meta-analyses; overview [+W]): how strongly learners who differ on this characteristic differ in mathematics achievement, not how much changing it would change achievement. Many of these characteristics are correlated with one another, and the review is about mathematics only.

  • Number line estimation r = .44, larger after age 9 (.49) than before 6 (.30); symbolic magnitude comparison .30, nonsymbolic .24, smaller in older samples. Claim [+W]

Context¶

  • Early-years and primary mathematics, and diagnosing mathematics difficulties.

  • Young children starting mathematics; learners at risk of mathematics difficulties.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Arithmetic and later mathematics achievement, including fractions.
