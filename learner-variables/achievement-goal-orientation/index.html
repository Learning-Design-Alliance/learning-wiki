Achievement Goal Orientation¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 47 claims (26 show a difference, 15 mixed, 6 no difference) · 25 studies (10 associational, 6 review, 4 causal, 3 theoretical, 2 quant-synthesis), q2–q4 · 10 of 25 report an effect size · 45 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Achievement goal theory distinguishes mastery goals (developing competence and understanding) from performance goals (showing competence relative to others), each with an approach form (seeking success) and an avoidance form (avoiding failure or looking incompetent). Goals are measured by self-report and are shaped by classroom goal structures: how tasks, evaluation and recognition are organised. Mastery-approach goals generally go with persistence and deeper processing, performance-avoidance goals with anxiety and lower achievement. It is one part of motivation.

How strongly it relates to achievement¶

Mathematics. These are correlations across meta-analyses (Breit et al. 2025, a systematic review of 30 meta-analyses; overview [~W]): how strongly learners who differ on this characteristic differ in mathematics achievement, not how much changing it would change achievement. Many of these characteristics are correlated with one another, and the review is about mathematics only.

  • Mastery goals r = .17, performance-approach .07, performance-avoidance −.17. Claim [~W]

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Evaluation practices, public comparison and recognition signal which goals a classroom rewards.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners who avoid challenge to avoid looking incompetent.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Persistence, help-seeking, strategy use and achievement.
