## Description
Morphological awareness is recognising and using morphemes, the smallest meaningful parts of words, such as the root and prefix in *replay*. This page also covers morphological knowledge, the LVN's name for the same capacity in older children. Learners who understand morphemes can treat them as building blocks, recognising, understanding and producing longer, more complex words when reading and writing. It also includes knowing how stress and pronunciation change between related words (*sign* and *signature*) and how some morphemes carry grammatical meaning (adding *-ed* for the past tense). It is measured with tasks in which learners choose or complete the right word form, such as picking the suffix that fits a sentence.

The LVN describes it for early-elementary (PK-3) and upper-elementary (grades 4-6) learners. It supports [vocabulary](vocabulary.md) growth by letting learners infer the meanings of unfamiliar words, [decoding](decoding.md) of long words, and spelling. Because languages build words differently, a learner's first language can shape their morphological awareness in a new one.

It sits within the wiki's broader [reading and language](reading-and-language.md) page.

## Implications

### Context
- Matters when learners meet long, derived words, which become more common as texts become more academic.
- Being read to at home is an important predictor, likely because book language has more complex words.
- Even mild-to-moderate hearing loss can affect the processing of word endings.

### Target Learners
- Early- and upper-elementary learners (PK-6).
- Learners acquiring a language whose word-building differs from their first language.
- Learners with mild-to-moderate hearing loss.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Vocabulary growth and inferring word meanings.
- Decoding of morphologically complex words.
- Spelling.

## Measures
- **The Suffix Choice Test** (Nagy et al., 2003): recognising, understanding and using morphemes by choosing the right suffixed form.
- **Test of Language Development, Primary (TOLD-P): Morphological Completion subtest** (Newcomer & Hammill, 2019): recognising, understanding and using morphemes, ages 4 to 8.
