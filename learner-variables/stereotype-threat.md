# Stereotype Threat

> **Learner Variable** · [All learner variables](index.md)

## Description
Stereotype threat is the situation in which a negative cultural stereotype about a group a learner identifies with, for example by race, gender, disability, age, income or language, leads to lower performance than the learner is capable of. Learners who know the stereotype can come to fear confirming it, especially when the relevant part of their [identity](identity.md) is made salient, such as by asking for it before a test or describing a test as diagnostic of ability. The lower score then reflects the situation, not the learner's capability, and the group difference it produces is not evidence of a difference in ability. Proposed mechanisms in the LVN summaries include anxiety and preoccupation that take up [working memory](working-memory.md), weaker [belonging](belonging.md), and, over time, disengagement from and devaluing of the domain. To be affected, learners must already know the stereotype, which develops through childhood. It is studied experimentally, by comparing performance under threatening and non-threatening testing conditions, and through interviews about stereotype awareness; it is a property of situations rather than a trait a learner carries. The LVN covers it from pre-kindergarten to adulthood, including adults learning technology skills. It bears on the wiki's pages on [gender](gender.md), [race and ethnicity](race-and-ethnicity.md) and [socioeconomic status](socioeconomic-status.md), where gaps are read as reflecting circumstances and treatment rather than as caused by the group.

## Implications

### Context
- How a test or task is framed, and whether group identity is made salient, can change performance.
- Discussing stereotypes openly and why they are baseless, and framing ability as able to grow, are described as protective.
- Parents' and teachers' stereotyped beliefs, for example about girls and mathematics, can shape what learners expect of themselves.

### Target Learners
- Learners from groups targeted by academic stereotypes, once they are aware of those stereotypes.
- Adolescents and adults in high-stakes evaluations; older adults facing stereotypes about memory or technology.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Performance on tests and evaluative tasks, especially in mathematics, reading and STEM.
- Belonging, self-efficacy and persistence in a domain, and later course and career choices.

## Measures
- **Diagnostic versus non-diagnostic test comparisons**: compares learners vulnerable to the stereotype who are told a reading, writing or mathematics test is diagnostic of ability with those told it is not; lower scores under the diagnostic framing indicate threat.
- **Testing conditions** (e.g., Ambady et al., 2001): varies testing conditions, such as making group identity salient, and compares performance.
- **Stereotype-Consciousness Interview** (McKown & Strambler, 2009): measures children's knowledge of cultural stereotypes, a precondition for susceptibility.
