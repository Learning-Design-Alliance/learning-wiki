Learning Disabilities and Developmental Differences¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables Evidence · 58 claims (44 show a difference, 10 mixed, 4 no difference) · 39 studies (20 associational, 7 review, 5 causal, 3 quant-synthesis, 3 theoretical, 1 qualitative), q1–q3 · 2 of 39 report an effect size · 56 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Identified differences in how a learner learns: specific learning disabilities such as dyslexia and dyscalculia, autism, intellectual disability, and the special educational needs (an IEP in the United States, an EHC plan in England) that formally record them. In the research it appears as a population a design targets, as a moderator (an approach that works differently for these learners), and as a predictor of outcomes. It is distinct from access, which is about perceiving and operating the material (sensory and motor impairments, devices); a learner can need both. Attention-related differences such as ADHD sit on attention.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Many effective adaptations are explicit and structured: smaller steps, more worked examples, more practice with feedback, and fewer simultaneous demands.
  • What a learner is entitled to (accommodations, extra time, alternative formats) is set by law and by the learner's plan, not only by the designer.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners with dyslexia or another specific learning disability, autistic learners, learners with intellectual disability, and learners with a recorded special educational need.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Reading, mathematics and independent-living or transition outcomes, where most of the research is.

The evidence by question¶

  • Do learners with disabilities reach lower achievement, graduation and postsecondary outcomes than their peers, by how much, and does it differ by disability type? Students with disabilities attained postsecondary readiness and success outcomes at about one half to one third the rate of students without disabilities, the largest difference between any student group and its counterpart [+M]
  • Which instructional approaches improve outcomes for learners with learning, developmental or intellectual disabilities? Outcomes of students with disabilities improve when they receive specially designed instruction responsive to assessment data [+M]
  • Are learners with disabilities excluded from school by discipline more often than their peers? Students with disabilities are suspended at more than twice the rate of students without disabilities, and Black students with disabilities face the highest risk at 19% [+M]
