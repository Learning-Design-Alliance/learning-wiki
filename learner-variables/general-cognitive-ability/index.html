General Cognitive Ability¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables Evidence · 42 claims (23 for, 11 mixed, 8 against) · 35 studies (14 causal, 6 quant-synthesis, 6 review, 6 associational, 3 theoretical), q1–q4 · 9 of 35 report an effect size · 39 claims rest on one study

Description¶

General cognitive ability is what intelligence and reasoning tests measure: fluid reasoning, crystallised knowledge and processing speed. It is a strong predictor of achievement, and research uses it as a covariate and as a moderator: some designs, such as highly structured instruction, help lower-ability learners more, while discovery-based designs can favour higher-ability learners (aptitude-treatment interactions). It overlaps with prior knowledge and working memory, and studies often cannot separate them.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Aptitude-treatment interactions are often not replicated; treat a single finding of one with caution.
  • Structured guidance tends to narrow ability-related differences in outcomes.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners across the ability range, including those identified as gifted and those who struggle.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Achievement and transfer, and how much a learner gains from less-guided instruction.

The evidence by question¶

  • Does a learner's general cognitive ability predict how well they achieve, and how much of achievement does it explain? Secondary academic performance is predicted by cognitive ability more strongly than by curiosity [+M]
  • Do structured techniques and guidance help lower-ability learners more than higher-ability ones, narrowing the gap? In the few studies that split by ability, learning techniques helped low-ability students and not high-ability students [~M]
  • Does sorting learners into classes or groups by ability help them, and which learners gain or lose? Between- or within-class homogeneous ability grouping has a small negative effect on low-ability primary students but no effect on other students [~M]
