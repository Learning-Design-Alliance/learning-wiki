Affect Regulation¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables Evidence · 43 claims (34 for, 6 mixed, 3 against) · 40 studies (12 review, 6 causal, 6 qualitative, 5 associational, 5 design, 3 quant-synthesis, 3 theoretical), q1–q4 · 6 of 40 report an effect size · 42 claims rest on one study

Description¶

What a learner does with frustration, anxiety and failure while the work is still in front of them. It decides whether a hard check needs a recoverable framing and how failure is surfaced. Anxiety is not only unpleasant — it consumes the same working memory the task needs, so it degrades performance through a mechanism entirely separate from motivation [-M]. Naming an emotion measurably reduces its grip, which is why the strategies here look unexpectedly linguistic.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Decides whether a difficult check reads as recoverable, and how a wrong answer is surfaced.
  • Failure can be productive when it is designed and framed as generative rather than as a verdict [+M].
  • Vocabulary is the lever: a learner who cannot name the state cannot regulate it.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners with domain-specific anxiety — mathematics being the best-documented case.
  • Younger learners still building the vocabulary, and any learner in a high-stakes setting.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Affects performance on timed and high-stakes assessment more than on untimed work, which is a validity problem.
  • Regulation itself is a legitimate objective in social-emotional curricula.

Related Learner Variables¶

  • Belonging — decides whether a setback reads as difficulty or as evidence of not belonging.
  • Attention — anxiety competes for it directly, so the two compound.
  • Working memory — the resource anxiety spends.
