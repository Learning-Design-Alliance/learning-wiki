Academic Anxiety¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 175 claims (114 show a difference, 40 mixed, 21 no difference) · 107 studies (43 associational, 22 causal, 22 review, 7 quant-synthesis, 6 theoretical, 5 qualitative, 2 design), q1–q4 · 27 of 107 report an effect size · 168 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Academic anxiety is fear or tension aroused by a subject or by being evaluated in it. The research mostly studies three forms: mathematics anxiety, test anxiety and foreign-language anxiety, each measured with self-report scales. Anxiety predicts lower performance, partly because worry takes up working memory; it moderates instructional effects (timed or high-stakes formats hurt anxious learners more); and it is an outcome that some designs reduce, such as low-stakes practice testing and expressive writing before an exam. It is a narrower case of affect regulation.

How strongly it relates to achievement¶

Mathematics. These are correlations across meta-analyses (Breit et al. 2025, a systematic review of 30 meta-analyses; overview [~W]): how strongly learners who differ on this characteristic differ in mathematics achievement, not how much changing it would change achievement. Many of these characteristics are correlated with one another, and the review is about mathematics only.

  • Mathematics anxiety r = −.28 (k = 747), weaker in older samples; test anxiety −.23; general anxiety −.19 and −.09. Claim [+W]

Implications¶

Context¶

  • High-stakes, timed and public formats raise anxiety; frequent low-stakes practice tends to lower it.
  • An anxious learner's score can understate what they know.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners anxious about mathematics, tests or speaking a second language.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Performance under assessment, and willingness to take further courses in the subject.

The evidence by question¶

  • Do learners with higher anxiety about a subject or a test achieve less in it? Math anxiety has a small-to-moderate negative correlation with math achievement (r = −.28) in a meta-analysis of correlations from 223 studies [+M]
  • Does the format and setting of an assessment change how anxious learners are about it? Students taking online and proctored online exams report higher test anxiety than students taking on-campus exams and non-proctored online exams [+M]
  • Does private, low-pressure and repeated practice lower learners' anxiety about using a second language? AI reduces foreign language anxiety and strengthens self-efficacy and self-confidence, supporting the Affective Foundation Pathway [+M]
