## Description
Gender is recorded in most education studies, usually as a binary category taken from records or self-report. It appears as a predictor (differences in participation in STEM, in attitudes such as self-efficacy and anxiety, and in some outcomes), as a moderator (an intervention that helps one group more), and as a target (programmes to widen participation). Differences found are mostly small, vary by country and cohort, and track expectations, stereotypes and opportunity more than ability; average differences say nothing about any individual learner.

## Implications

### Context
- Differences in confidence and participation are larger and more consistent than differences in achievement.
- A design effect that differs by gender is a reason to look for what in the design or setting produces the difference.

### Target Learners
- Groups under-represented in a subject or field, such as women in physics, computing and engineering.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Participation and persistence in a field, attitudes (self-efficacy, interest, anxiety) and achievement.
