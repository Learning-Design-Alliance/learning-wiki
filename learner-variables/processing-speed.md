# Processing Speed

## Description
Processing speed (the LVN calls it speed of processing) is how quickly a learner perceives and processes information and formulates a response. Reading and mathematics both require it constantly: a reader takes in letters, words, punctuation and layout, a mathematics learner numbers, shapes and symbols, and each has to choose and apply the right skills fast enough to keep the whole task in view. It grows quickly in the early years and keeps improving through adolescence, may dip during puberty, and declines with age in older adults. It is measured with timed tasks, such as pattern-matching and cancellation subtests of cognitive batteries and rapid automatised naming, though naming tasks also draw on vocabulary. It is related to [working memory](working-memory.md), since faster processing lets more be rehearsed and held, and to fluid reasoning in [general cognitive ability](general-cognitive-ability.md). Slow processing is also reported in learners with hearing loss and with dyslexia, which overlaps [learning disabilities](learning-disabilities.md).

## Implications

### Context
- Timed tasks and fast-paced instruction disadvantage learners who process slowly; extra time and pacing control help.
- Fluency in basic skills, such as decoding and number facts, reduces how much has to be processed in the moment.
- Fatigue, alcohol, stress and poor health can slow processing temporarily.

### Target Learners
- Young children, in whom it is still developing quickly, and adolescents around puberty.
- Learners with dyslexia or hearing loss, and older adults.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Reading fluency and decoding efficiency.
- Arithmetic fluency and multi-step problem solving.
- Reasoning and communicating complex ideas.

## Measures
- **Woodcock-Johnson Tests of Cognitive Abilities** (Schrank, Mather, & McGrew, 2014): includes number-pattern matching, pair cancellation and letter-pattern matching subtests of processing speed.
- **Rapid Automatic Naming Tests (RAN)** (Denckla & Rudel, 1974): timed naming of familiar items, a common speed measure that also draws on vocabulary.

## Related Learner Variables
- [Executive Function](executive-function.md): faster processing supports inhibition by speeding the decision whether to hold back a response.
- [Working Memory](working-memory.md): faster processing lets more information be rehearsed and held.
- [Academic and Critical Literacy](academic-and-critical-literacy.md): processing speed is related to gains in academic literacies, and its decline to weaker health literacy in adults.
- [Access](access.md): hearing loss can slow processing because of the effort of listening.
- [Long-Term Memory](long-term-memory.md): weak stored representations slow retrieval and processing.
- [Physical Well-Being](physical-wellbeing.md): exercise may speed processing, and poor health is linked with slower processing.
- [Safety](safety.md): alcohol slows processing, and bullying is associated with slower processing in an unclear direction.
- [Communication Skills](communication-skills.md): early speed in processing language predicts later language development.
- [Critical Thinking](critical-thinking.md): age-related gains in speed go with better reasoning.
- [Decoding](decoding.md): faster processing makes decoding more efficient, and dyslexia often comes with slower processing.
- [General Cognitive Ability](general-cognitive-ability.md): faster processors do better on fluid reasoning tasks.
- [Sleep](sleep.md): sleep deprivation slows processing.
