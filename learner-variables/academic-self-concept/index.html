Academic Self-Concept¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 122 claims (83 show a difference, 28 mixed, 11 no difference) · 60 studies (33 associational, 10 causal, 10 review, 2 quant-synthesis, 2 qualitative, 2 design, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 15 of 60 report an effect size · 119 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Academic self-concept is a learner's self-perception of ability in a domain ("I am good at maths"). It is domain-specific: mathematics self-concept and verbal self-concept are only weakly related, because learners compare their own subjects with one another as well as with other learners (the internal/external frame of reference model). It is formed partly by comparison with classmates, so the same learner may report a lower self-concept in a high-achieving class (the big-fish-little-pond effect). Self-concept and achievement affect each other over time. It is related to but distinct from self-efficacy, which is confidence about succeeding at specific tasks.

How strongly it relates to achievement¶

Mathematics. These are correlations across meta-analyses (Breit et al. 2025, a systematic review of 30 meta-analyses; overview [~W]): how strongly learners who differ on this characteristic differ in mathematics achievement, not how much changing it would change achievement. Many of these characteristics are correlated with one another, and the review is about mathematics only.

  • Mathematics self-concept r = .43, the largest of the 18 personality-category correlates; verbal self-concept .12. Claim [+W]

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Grouping, feedback and comparison practices shape self-concept.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners with a low view of their ability in a subject.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Achievement, course choice and persistence in a subject.
