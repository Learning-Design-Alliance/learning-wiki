## Description
Vocabulary is the store of words a learner understands (receptive vocabulary) and uses (expressive or productive vocabulary), together with the strategies they use to work out unfamiliar words. It is measured with picture-vocabulary tests, in which a learner picks the picture matching a spoken word, with language samples that count the number and variety of words used, and, for older students, with measures of academic language features in writing.

Vocabulary supports reading comprehension and writing at every stage. When a reader meets too many unfamiliar words, comprehension breaks down, and this matters more as texts become more complex. In the early grades vocabulary also supports automatic spelling; by middle and high school vocabulary learning shifts from everyday words toward academic and content-specific words. Learning strategies for figuring out new words is described as a step toward fluent reading.

The page sits within the wiki's broader [reading and language](reading-and-language.md) page and is closely tied to [prior knowledge](prior-knowledge.md): knowing words and knowing about a topic support each other. [Morphological awareness](morphological-awareness.md) is one of the main routes to inferring the meaning of new words. Bilingual learners' vocabulary is spread across their languages, so a measure in one language shows only part of it.

## Implications

### Context
- Matters for any text-based instruction, and increasingly in secondary content areas with dense academic vocabulary.
- Print exposure and books in the home are associated with larger vocabularies.
- Children's vocabulary scores can be lowered by recent nearby violence, so a single score may reflect circumstances at the time of testing.

### Target Learners
- Learners from preschool through high school (the LVN records cover PK-12).
- Multilingual learners and English learners, whose vocabulary in each single language may initially be smaller.
- Learners with hearing loss who lacked full access to language early on.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Reading comprehension.
- Written expression, including spelling and complex sentences.
- Academic and disciplinary language.

## Measures
- **Peabody Picture Vocabulary Test, Fifth Edition (PPVT-5)** (Dunn, 2018): receptive vocabulary for learners of all ages (earlier edition: Dunn & Dunn, 2007).
- **Language Sample** (Miller & Iglesias, 2012): productive vocabulary from the number of different words, total words and type-token ratio in a learner's speech.
- **Nominalization measure** (Al-Adeimi, 2017): students' use of nominalisations in academic writing.

## Related Learner Variables
- [Prior Knowledge](prior-knowledge.md): a large vocabulary helps readers connect related ideas, and background knowledge deepens understanding of words in context.
- [Writing Skills](writing-skills.md): vocabulary supports spelling and mechanics and richer narrative and genre writing.
- [Access](access.md): children with hearing loss who lacked early access to language often have smaller vocabularies in English.
- [Home Learning Environment](home-learning-environment.md): more print exposure and more books at home go with larger vocabularies.
- [English-Learner Status](english-learner-status.md): bilingual children may at first know fewer words in each language than monolingual peers.
- [Safety](safety.md): recent nearby violence is associated with lower vocabulary scores.
- [Socioeconomic Status](socioeconomic-status.md): children growing up in lower-SES homes often show smaller school vocabularies, a gap reflecting circumstances and language exposure, not a cause.
- [General Cognitive Ability](general-cognitive-ability.md): vocabulary and verbal reasoning are tightly linked and grow together.
- [Working Memory](working-memory.md): working memory supports acquiring new words, and readers with smaller vocabularies may lean on it more.
- [Long-Term Memory](long-term-memory.md): once learned, word knowledge is stored in long-term memory.
- [Morphological Awareness](morphological-awareness.md): knowing word parts lets learners infer the meaning of new words.
- [Decoding](decoding.md): vocabulary breadth predicts sight recognition of written words.
