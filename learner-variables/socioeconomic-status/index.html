Socioeconomic Status¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables Evidence · 132 claims (90 for, 28 mixed, 14 against) · 97 studies (52 associational, 23 causal, 13 review, 5 theoretical, 2 design, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), q1–q3 · 9 of 97 report an effect size · 130 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A learner's economic and social position: family income or poverty (often measured by eligibility for free or reduced-price school meals), parental education and occupation, and the resources that follow from them: time, a quiet place to study, devices, money for fees and travel. In the research it appears as a predictor of outcomes (achievement and attainment gaps), as a moderator of instructional effects (a design that helps low-income learners more, or less), and as the population a programme targets. It is distinct from access, which is whether a learner can perceive and operate the material, and from time and continuity, though poverty constrains both.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Shapes the time, devices, bandwidth and quiet a course can assume outside class.
  • Costs a course passes on to learners (materials, travel, unpaid placements) fall hardest here.
  • Gaps by income are a baseline for reading an effect: a design that narrows a gap and one that raises everyone equally are different results.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners from low-income families, first-generation students, and learners whose paid work or caring responsibilities compete with study.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Achievement and attainment (completion, progression), where gaps by socioeconomic status are largest and most often measured.
