Motivation¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 233 claims (161 show a difference, 47 mixed, 25 no difference) · 185 studies (57 associational, 49 causal, 34 review, 15 theoretical, 14 quant-synthesis, 10 qualitative, 6 design), q1–q4 · 45 of 185 report an effect size · 221 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Whether a learner will spend effort here, and what makes it feel worth spending. It decides whether a page must earn attention before it can teach, and how much open-endedness is tolerable. The honest state of the evidence is that the mechanisms are well supported and the interventions are modest — growth-mindset programmes produce small average effects concentrated in specific subgroups [+W], which is a reason to design for motivation rather than to bolt an intervention on.

How strongly it relates to achievement¶

Mathematics. These are correlations across meta-analyses (Breit et al. 2025, a systematic review of 30 meta-analyses; overview [~W]): how strongly learners who differ on this characteristic differ in mathematics achievement, not how much changing it would change achievement. Many of these characteristics are correlated with one another, and the review is about mathematics only.

  • Mastery goals r = .17, performance-avoidance goals −.17, enjoyment .31, need for cognition .25 (see achievement goal orientation). Claim [~W]

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Sets how much a page must earn attention, and how much open-endedness a task can carry.
  • Autonomy, competence and relevance are the reliable levers; exhortation is not.
  • Choice is not automatically motivating — what is on offer shapes both the concepts learned and who feels invited [~M].

Target Learners¶

  • Learners with no prior stake in the domain, for whom relevance has to be constructed rather than assumed.
  • Learners whose past experience in the domain is of failure, where expectancy rather than value is the binding constraint.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Predicts persistence and effort allocation more than single-session performance.
  • Moderates every other dimension: a learner who stops does not benefit from any of them.

The evidence by question¶

  • Does autonomy-supportive teaching raise motivation and engagement compared with controlling teaching? Self-determination theory holds that autonomy support raises intrinsic motivation, and a meta-analysis finds expected tangible rewards undermine it [+M]
  • Do rewards and praise undermine voluntary engagement? Expected tangible rewards reduce later free-choice engagement with a rewarded task, while verbal praise does not [~S]
  • Do growth-mindset interventions raise achievement, and for which learners and in which settings? Growth-mindset interventions have weak average effects on achievement, including a negligible GPA gain (d = 0.11) for lower-achieving US ninth-graders in one preregistered national trial [~S]
  • Does learning with generative AI raise learners' motivation compared with learning without it, and where? GenAI-assisted education produces significantly higher learning motivation than non-GenAI approaches (g = 0.81) [+M]
