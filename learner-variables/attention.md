# Attention

## Description
How long a learner can sustain focus, and how readily something else takes it. Distinct from [working memory](working-memory.md): attention governs what *reaches* the store, capacity governs what it holds. It converts into segment length, distractor design, and how much a page may hold in one uninterrupted run. The design lever is mostly subtractive — the strongest findings here are about what to remove [-M].

## Implications

### Context
- Sets segment length and how much a page may present in one uninterrupted run.
- Interesting-but-irrelevant material is the classic trap: it raises reported engagement and lowers learning [-M].
- Signalling is the additive counterpart — directing attention rather than competing for it [+M].

### Target Learners
- Younger learners and anyone with attention-related differences, for whom segment length is not a stylistic choice.
- Learners in environments they do not control — noise, interruption, shared devices.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Affects anything requiring sustained processing: extended reading, multi-step problems, video.
- Largely irrelevant to short retrieval tasks, which is why brief checks survive conditions long tasks do not.
