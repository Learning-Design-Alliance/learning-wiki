Identity¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 64 claims (51 show a difference, 10 mixed, 3 no difference) · 35 studies (9 associational, 9 qualitative, 7 review, 5 causal, 3 theoretical, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 design), q1–q4 · 1 of 35 report an effect size · 63 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Identity is a learner's sense of self and understanding of who they are, which shapes how they engage with the world. It begins forming in infancy, develops most intensely during adolescence, and keeps changing in adult life. Commonly named aspects include race, ethnicity, gender, cultural background, language and disability status. In early childhood children begin to form identities as they learn about themselves and their world; in middle childhood they come to understand social categories in more depth and start to take on social identities through interaction with peers and educators; in adolescence peers and social life take on more weight and learners navigate social spaces more independently. The LVN summaries tie identity to learning in three ways: a positive ethnic-racial identity is associated with achievement and self-esteem, it can buffer the harm of discrimination, and identity-based stereotypes can produce stereotype threat and weaken belonging. It is measured mainly with ethnic and racial identity scales for older children and adolescents. The group categories themselves are not causes of learning outcomes: see the wiki's pages on race and ethnicity and gender, where differences are read as reflecting circumstances and treatment. The LVN covers identity for pre-kindergarten to grade 12 only.

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Materials and examples that represent learners' identities, and diverse role models, support belonging.
  • Educators can help learners recognise and value their identities, and learn about them from families.
  • Discipline policies and everyday interactions can carry identity-based bias that learners experience as harm.

Target Learners¶

  • Children and adolescents, especially in adolescence when identity development peaks.
  • Learners from historically and systematically excluded groups, multilingual learners, and learners who are Deaf, hard of hearing or blind.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Belonging, self-esteem and self-efficacy.
  • Academic engagement and achievement.
  • Communication and collaboration across cultures.

Measures¶

  • Multigroup Ethnic Identity Measure, Revised (MEIM-R) (Phinney & Ong, 2007): a brief survey of affiliation with one's ethnic group for older elementary students and adolescents.
  • Ethnic Identity Scale (Umaña-Taylor et al., 2004): a 17-item scale of ethnic identity exploration, resolution and affirmation.
  • Multidimensional Inventory of Black Identity, Teen (MIBI-T) (Scottham et al., 2008): a 21-item scale of the centrality, regard and ideology of racial identity.
  • Cross Racial Identity Scale (CRIS) (Vandiver et al., 2000): assesses six racial identity attitudes.
  • Ethnic Racial Identity Formation (Ikura et al., 2021): a brief on early-childhood identity that discusses measurement.

Related Learner Variables¶

  • Stereotype Threat: learners from stigmatised groups may be more aware of stereotypes and so more exposed to threat.
  • Belonging: stereotypes and lack of representation can undercut belonging, and diverse schools can support it.
  • Home Learning Environment: families teach children about their culture and identity, including through ethnic-racial socialisation.
  • Adverse Experiences: discrimination can be experienced as racial trauma, and a positive identity can buffer it.
  • Social Supports: what effective support looks like varies with culture, and support protects against discrimination.
  • English-Learner Status: many learners hold their first language as central to their ethnic identity.
  • Self-Efficacy: a strong ethnic-racial identity goes with higher self-esteem.
  • Affect Regulation: identity-based discrimination can harm mental health, and a strong identity helps buffer it.
  • Academic and Critical Literacy: a positive ethnic-racial identity is associated with achievement.
  • Communication Skills: language varieties and home languages are part of identity and shape communication.
  • Access: Deaf, hard-of-hearing and blind learners may hold these as important parts of who they are.
  • Socioeconomic Status: structural inequality means excluded groups are over-represented in low-income homes.
