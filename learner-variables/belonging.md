# Belonging

> **Learner Variable** · [All learner variables](index.md)

## Description
Whether a learner expects to be treated as a full participant here — and how much attention the question itself consumes. It is measured by self-report scales of social belonging, by stereotype-threat manipulations, and by behavioural proxies such as help-seeking and persistence after failure. Belonging is not a proxy for motivation: a learner can want the outcome badly and still spend working memory monitoring whether they are welcome, which is why brief interventions that change the *interpretation* of difficulty can move outcomes without changing the instruction at all [+M].

## Implications

### Context
- Changes who appears in a scenario, how feedback is framed, and whether a check reads as diagnostic or as judgment.
- Matters most at transitions and after early failure, when a learner is deciding whether difficulty means 'this is hard' or 'I do not belong here'.
- Framing effects are cheap and reversible; the underlying condition is neither. A belonging intervention is not a substitute for a setting that is actually inclusive.

### Target Learners
- Learners whose group is numerically or historically underrepresented in the domain, for whom ordinary difficulty carries an extra inference.
- Any learner new to a setting — the effect is about uncertainty, not identity alone.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Predicts persistence, help-seeking, and course completion more strongly than it predicts single-assessment performance.
- Moderates the effect of critical feedback: identical feedback reads as investment or as dismissal depending on it.
