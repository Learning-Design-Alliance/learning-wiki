# Reading and Language

> **Evidence** · 93 claims (52 for, 17 mixed, 24 against) · 76 studies (26 causal, 17 quant-synthesis, 15 review, 9 associational, 7 theoretical, 2 qualitative), `q1`–`q4` · 22 of 76 report an effect size · 86 claims rest on one study

## Description
What a learner can read without effort, and whether the language of instruction is the one they think in. LVN's largest factor family — decoding, vocabulary, fluency, composition, disciplinary literacy and language of instruction all sit here. The load-bearing fact is that decoding which is not automatic spends the same budget comprehension needs, so a reading problem presents as a thinking problem [+S].

## Implications

### Context
- Sets text density, glossing, the plain-language floor, and whether the language at home differs from the language of instruction.
- Vocabulary is incremental and context-dependent: exposure alone is unreliable, and explicit instruction alone is narrow — the pairing beats either [+M].
- A page that is hard to read is indistinguishable, in its results, from a page that is hard to understand.

### Target Learners
- Multilingual learners, for whom the gap between conversational and academic language is the usual issue rather than ability.
- Any learner in a discipline with its own register — mathematics and science included.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Gates comprehension of every text-delivered outcome, so it is a validity threat wherever reading is not the construct.
- Directly targeted where literacy itself is the goal.

## Related Learner Variables
- Working memory — effortful decoding consumes it before comprehension begins.
- Prior knowledge — vocabulary is partly a knowledge measure, which is why the two correlate so strongly.
- Access — audio and text-to-speech move the same content past a decoding barrier.
