Proportional Reasoning¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables
Evidence · 47 claims (31 show a difference, 14 mixed, 2 no difference) · 26 studies (10 causal, 8 associational, 2 qualitative, 2 design, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 8 of 26 report an effect size · 44 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Proportional reasoning is understanding ratio and how ratios relate to one another. The Learner Variability Navigator describes a ratio as a relationship between parts and wholes that can be written as a fraction, a percentage or a rate. It is assessed with tasks that ask learners to rank fractions shown in different forms (shaded shapes, numerals, decimals) by size, and with missing-value problems in which one quantity in a pair of equivalent ratios must be found.

LVN treats it as an essential part of arithmetic proficiency in primary school and as the entry point to algebra, geometry, and probability and statistics; it follows it from grade 3 to grade 10. Fraction understanding is central, and LVN connects it to difficulties with multiplication and division, to working memory, and to arithmetic fact retrieval. It extends number sense to rational numbers and supports algebraic thinking, geometric reasoning (similarity) and measurement (units).

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Representing fractions in several ways, and connecting them, supports fraction problem solving.
  • Comparing fractions can require suppressing the habit of judging by the larger number.

Target Learners¶

  • Upper-primary and lower-secondary learners learning fractions, ratios, percentages and rates.
  • Learners with weak multiplication and division or slow fact retrieval.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Fraction magnitude, ratio equivalence and rate problems.
  • Readiness for algebra, similarity and probability.

Measures¶

  • Ranking Proportions Task (e.g., Mazzocco & Devlin, 2008): Magnitude comparison for fractions and rational numbers where students rank order different representations of fractions including shaded circles, numerical fractions, and decimals.
  • Missing value problems (e.g., Lamon, 2007): Students are asked to determine which value is missing in a ratio equivalence context (e.g., how many sugars do you need to add to 6 lemons so your lemonade is the same sweetness as my lemonade with 2 sugars and 3 lemons?).

Related Learner Variables¶

  • Algebraic Thinking: fraction knowledge relates to equation knowledge and predicts improvement in solving equations.
  • Communication Skills: verbal reasoning, which LVN files under math communication, relates to accuracy on fraction problems.
  • Mathematical Flexibility: representing fractions in several ways supports fraction problem solving.
  • Measurement: the multiplicative relationship between quantities underpins understanding of a unit.
  • Operations and Place Value: difficulty with multiplication and division often goes with difficulty understanding fractions.
  • Self-Efficacy: students with higher mathematics self-efficacy, which LVN files under math mindset, did better on fraction tests.
  • Attention: attention in mathematics class predicts fraction problem solving and its improvement.
  • Working Memory: higher working memory goes with greater growth in fraction problem solving.
  • Arithmetic Fact Retrieval: better fact retrieval goes with greater improvement in fraction problem solving.
  • Number Sense: proportional reasoning reveals the multiplicative relationships inside place value.
  • Geometric Reasoning: similarity problems are solved with proportional reasoning.
  • Executive Function: inhibition helps when comparing fractions with the same numerator, against the pull of the larger denominator.

Examples¶

  • Build on children's informal proportional understandings to prepare for ratio and proportion
