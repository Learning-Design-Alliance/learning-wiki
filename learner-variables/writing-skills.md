# Writing Skills

> **Learner Variable** · [All learner variables](index.md)

## Description
Writing skills run from the foundational to the compositional. At the foundation are the motor skills of handwriting, knowledge of letters and print, spelling, punctuation, capitalisation and writing speed. Composition is the expression of ideas in writing: setting goals for a piece, deciding on content and structure, and shaping it for an audience and purpose. Genre knowledge is knowing the elements, vocabulary and order a narrative, persuasive or informative text calls for, and argumentative reasoning is the ability to make and support a claim with evidence and judge other people's arguments. The Learner Variability Navigator covers these as five factors across early childhood, the primary and secondary grades and adult learning.

They are assessed with curriculum-based measures (copying, dictation, spelling, short prompted stories), handwriting speed and legibility tests, written-expression subtests of achievement batteries, and genre-specific or argumentative writing tasks scored against standards or rubrics. LVN describes a developmental sequence: young children build alphabet knowledge, phonological awareness and handwriting first; as these become automatic, attention is freed for planning and generating ideas; adolescents learn to write for specific audiences and to build and analyse detailed arguments; and adults, especially in post-secondary settings, may need further support with structuring ideas. Adults and children with dyslexia may struggle with writing because of difficulties with decoding and spelling. Writing draws on [working memory](working-memory.md), [executive function](executive-function.md) and [self-regulation](self-regulation.md), and on [syntax](syntax.md) and [vocabulary](vocabulary.md).

## Implications

### Context
- Early grades usually teach letter formation and legibility before composition; automatic transcription frees attention for ideas.
- Secondary and post-secondary writing is genre- and discipline-specific, and adults returning to study may need its conventions taught explicitly.
- Planning and goal-setting strategies, models of the target genre and practice with argument are the usual supports.

### Target Learners
- Young children developing handwriting, spelling and print knowledge.
- Adolescents and adults asked to write in unfamiliar genres or academic disciplines, including adult English learners.
- Learners with dyslexia or motor difficulties that slow transcription.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Written composition quality, fluency and legibility.
- Genre-appropriate and argumentative writing, and evaluation of others' arguments.

## Measures
- **Wechsler Individual Achievement Test (Third Edition)** (Breaux & Frey, 2010): This multi-battery test can be used in a variety of settings to measure ability and screen for potential learning disabilities. There are extensive written expression sub-tests to analyze Composition abilities.
- **Common Core English Language Arts Standards for Writing**: Provides standards to assess students' mastery of macrostructure and microstructure elements of narrative, persuasive, and informative writing.
- **Common Core English Language Arts Standards for Reading: Literature and Reading: Informational Text**: Provides standards to assess students' mastery of reading different genres.
- **The Detailed Assessment of Speed of Handwriting (DASH)** (Barnett & Henderson, 2007): Assesses handwriting speed using five different subtests in children nine to 16 years and 11 months old.
- **The Woodcock Johnson Tests of Achievement** (Schrank et al., 2014): Contains subtests that assess knowledge of spelling, capitalization, and punctuation for individuals between two and 80+ years old.
- **CBAL Writing Assessments** (Deane et al., 2011): In this computer-based assessment, students are given a genre-specific writing task and realistic scenarios with corresponding texts to evaluate.
- **Performance Assessments** (McCurdy et. al, 2018): Students complete an argumentative writing task in which they evaluate an argument after listening to or reading a text.
- **Curriculum Based Measurement (CBM)** (McMaster et al., 2009): For younger learners, measures of letter and sentence copying, dictation, letter and sound writing, and spelling tasks help to assess Foundational Writing Skills. This measure has also been tested for more experienced early writers with extensions such as story prompts to assess idea generation.
- **Minnesota Handwriting Test** (Reisman, 1999): A norm-referenced test to analyze handwriting in early elementary school and help therapists and teachers to track handwriting progress over time.

## Related Learner Variables
- [Prior Knowledge](prior-knowledge.md): knowing both the topic and how the writing process goes makes new writing tasks easier, particularly for adults in academic settings.
- [Working Memory](working-memory.md): planning, transcribing and revising compete for working memory, which contributes to writing quality.
- [Access](access.md): lifelong hearing loss, especially among sign-language users, can make written English harder because the two languages' grammars differ.
- [Home Learning Environment](home-learning-environment.md): exposure to print at home and in daily life goes with stronger spelling.
- [Decoding](decoding.md): word-reading skills and spelling share a base, and writers with reading difficulties often struggle with writing too.
- [Executive Function](executive-function.md): cognitive flexibility and inhibition are linked to writing skill.
- [Processing Speed](processing-speed.md): faster processing goes with stronger writing achievement across the lifespan.
- [Affect Regulation](affect-regulation.md): anxiety about academic writing is common among adult learners and can get in the way of writing.
- [Motivation](motivation.md): stronger writers tend to be more willing to take on composition, and motivation in turn supports practice.
- [Vocabulary](vocabulary.md): a richer vocabulary supports more complex narrative writing and meeting a genre's communicative demands.
- [Attention](attention.md): sustained attention supports foundational skills such as spelling.
- [Visual Processing](visual-processing.md): visual-motor integration underlies handwriting speed and legibility.
