# Prior Knowledge

## Description
A learner's existing domain knowledge in the subject area before instruction begins — the organized network of concepts, procedures, and experiences that new learning must connect to. It is typically operationalized as a pretest score, a standardized prior-achievement measure, or instructor-rated expertise level, and functions as the single strongest predictor of learning gains in most instructional research [~S]. Prior knowledge is the learner-side variable that drives the [Expertise Reversal Effect](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md): the same technique that helps novices can hinder more knowledgeable learners.

## Implications

### Context
- Matters most when material is domain-specific and cumulative (mathematics, science, language learning); it matters less for tasks dominated by general reasoning or fluid ability.
- Determines the optimal level of guidance: low-knowledge learners benefit from worked examples and direct instruction, while high-knowledge learners benefit from problem-solving and reduced scaffolding [~S].
- Activation matters as much as possession — learners often hold relevant knowledge but fail to retrieve it unless instruction prompts them to do so [~M].

### Target Learners
- All learners, but the variable is most consequential for novices, who lack the schemas to chunk new information and are most vulnerable to cognitive overload [~S].
- Learners with misconceptions: prior knowledge that is wrong actively interferes with correct learning and requires explicit conceptual-change instruction rather than simple addition of content [~M].

### Target Learning Objectives
- Predicts achievement, comprehension, and retention across virtually all outcome types; also moderates transfer, since transfer depends on accessible, well-organized prior knowledge [~S].
- Used diagnostically to select instructional treatments and sequence content rather than as an outcome itself.

## Claims
- [Activating prior knowledge improves learning](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M] — prompts that retrieve relevant knowledge before instruction improve comprehension and integration.
- [Advance organizers improve learning](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M] — organizing frameworks are most effective when learners lack the knowledge to structure material themselves.
- [Analogical reasoning improves transfer](../claims/analogical-reasoning-improves-transfer.md) [+M] — analogies work by mapping new content onto knowledge learners already hold.
- [Chunking reduces working memory load](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M] — prior knowledge supplies the schemas that make chunking possible; novices must be given them.
- [Prior knowledge is not significantly related to test performance in this sample](../claims/prior-knowledge-not-related-to-performance.md) [-W] — a single-sample null finding (Nist & Hogrebe, 1985) that runs against the broader meta-analytic consensus and should not be generalized.

## Related Learner Variables
- Working memory capacity — interacts with prior knowledge: high prior knowledge compensates for limited working memory by enabling chunking.
- Reading fluency and automaticity — domain-general prerequisites that gate access to the knowledge being built.
- Self-efficacy — prior success in a domain builds the confidence that sustains effort when material gets difficult.

## Examples
- [Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md) — pre-instruction prompts (brainstorming, anticipation guides, KWL) that surface what learners already know.
- [Analogies and Prior Knowledge Activation](../elements/analogies-and-prior-knowledge-activation.md) — bridges new concepts to familiar ones.
- [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) — expository or comparative frameworks presented before the material.
- [4C/Four-Component Instructional Design](../patterns/4cid-four-component-instructional-design.md) — explicitly sequences learning tasks from simple-to-complex based on learner entry knowledge.
- [Cognitive Load Theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) — explains why novice/expert differences reverse the effectiveness of guidance and example formats.
