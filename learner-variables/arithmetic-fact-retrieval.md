# Arithmetic Fact Retrieval

> **Learner Variable** · [All learner variables](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Arithmetic fact retrieval is the ability to pull basic number combinations (3 + 4, 6 × 7) from long-term memory efficiently, accurately and flexibly, rather than working them out each time, so that they can be used inside larger calculations. It is usually assessed by having learners solve simple problems while their strategy is observed or self-reported (did they retrieve the answer or count it out?), and, for a fuller picture of accuracy, flexibility and efficiency, through interviews, observations and short untimed quizzes.

Facts are usually in place by the end of primary school, but the Learner Variability Navigator notes that fluent and flexible retrieval continues to support mathematics learning through high school. Learners with dyscalculia may have weaker representations of number, which can make facts harder to retrieve, and learners with dyslexia may also find retrieval harder because of its reliance on phonological processing. It builds on [number sense](number-sense.md) and on [long-term memory](long-term-memory.md), and is used inside [operations](operations-and-place-value.md). Knowing when retrieval is the right strategy is part of [mathematical flexibility](mathematical-flexibility.md). See also [learning disabilities](learning-disabilities.md).

## Implications

### Context
- Retrieval practice of facts sits alongside, not in place of, understanding of number relationships and strategies.
- Math activities at home, such as counting games, support fluent retrieval.
- Stereotype cues about who is good at mathematics can depress performance on fact tasks.

### Target Learners
- Primary-age children learning number facts.
- Older learners whose calculation is slowed by counting out facts, including learners with dyscalculia or dyslexia.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Fact fluency: speed, accuracy and appropriate use of retrieval.
- Multi-step calculation and later fraction problem solving.

## Measures
- **Open-ended formats** (Kling & Bay-William, 2014): Using a range of measures including student interviews, observations, writing prompts, and short, untimed quizzes around solving simple arithmetic problems can help capture students' accuracy, flexibility, and efficiency which are core components of Arithmetic Fact Retrieval.
- **Arithmetic Fact Retrieval Task** (LeFevre, Sadesky, & Bisanz, 1996): Assesses Arithmetic Fact Retrieval by asking students to solve simple math Operations and observing or asking for self-report on strategy use.
