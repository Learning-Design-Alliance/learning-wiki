# Geometric Reasoning

> **Learner Variable** · [All learner variables](index.md)

## Description
Geometric reasoning is the abstract thinking used to define shapes, analyse their properties and relationships, and argue about them; in the secondary grades it extends to formalising those arguments as written proofs. The Learner Variability Navigator notes that geometric knowledge gives concrete representations and models for abstract mathematical ideas, which can serve as an entry point to higher-order mathematical thinking. It is assessed with short geometry tasks that ask learners to explain their reasoning in writing or diagrams, which also reveal misconceptions, and with the geometry subtests of diagnostic batteries.

LVN follows it from grade 3 to grade 10. It is distinct from, but draws on, [spatial ability](spatial-ability.md): spatial ability is the capacity to picture and transform shapes, while geometric reasoning is the mathematical reasoning about them. It connects to [measurement](measurement.md) through area and volume, to [algebraic thinking](algebraic-thinking.md) through equations for shapes, and to [proportional reasoning](proportional-reasoning.md) through similarity.

## Implications

### Context
- Tasks that ask for explanation and argument, not only answers, show how learners are reasoning about shapes.
- Proof is introduced in the secondary grades and adds demands of logical coherence and written argument.
- Stereotype cues about who is good at mathematics can depress performance on geometry tests.

### Target Learners
- Upper-primary and secondary learners moving from recognising shapes to reasoning and proof.
- Learners with weaker spatial or visual-processing skills.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Understanding of geometric properties and relationships, including 3D shapes.
- Geometric argument and proof; area and similarity problems.

## Measures
- **Geometry Tasks with Explanation** (e.g., Carroll, 1998): Short geometry tasks that elicit reasoning and argumentation through writing, diagrams, and other representations can be used to assess students' understanding and misconceptions of basic geometric properties while giving insight into students' thinking.
- **KeyMath-3 Diagnostic Assessment** (Connolly, 2007): In the geometry subtest, students describe, analyze, and determine the relationship between shapes, and use visualization and formulas to solve geometric problems.
