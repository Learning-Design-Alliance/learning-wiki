Achievement Emotions¶

Learner Variable · All learner variables Evidence · 74 claims (50 show a difference, 15 mixed, 7 no difference, 2 unmarked) · 52 studies (15 causal, 14 associational, 8 qualitative, 7 review, 4 design, 4 theoretical), q1–q4 · 9 of 52 report an effect size · 71 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Achievement emotions are the emotions learners feel about learning activities (enjoyment, boredom, frustration) and about their outcomes (pride, shame, hope, hopelessness). In control-value theory they arise from how much control a learner feels over an activity and how much they value it. Positive activating emotions such as enjoyment go with engagement and achievement, deactivating ones such as boredom with disengagement, and the relationship runs both ways. Anxiety is an achievement emotion with its own page, academic anxiety; enjoyment overlaps with interest.

How strongly it relates to achievement¶

Mathematics. These are correlations across meta-analyses (Breit et al. 2025, a systematic review of 30 meta-analyses; overview): how strongly learners who differ on this characteristic differ in mathematics achievement, not how much changing it would change achievement. Many of these characteristics are correlated with one another, and the review is about mathematics only.

  • Enjoyment r = .31, boredom −.28. Claim

Implications¶

Context¶

  • Task difficulty, autonomy, relevance and feedback shape what learners feel during learning.

Target Learners¶

  • Learners who are bored or frustrated in a subject.

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • Engagement, persistence and achievement.
