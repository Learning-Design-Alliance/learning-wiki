# Collaboration

## Description
Collaboration is the ability to work with a diverse group toward a common goal: putting forward one's own ideas, listening to others' thoughts and needs, being flexible and compromising, and sharing responsibility for the result. It is measured in several ways, including observation, self-report, rating scales, standardised measures and performance tasks.

How learners collaborate is shaped by the learner, the immediate setting and the wider culture, including what a learner understands about social norms in different contexts. Children from different backgrounds may approach peer collaboration differently, and children who find social cues hard to read, for example because of language-based learning disabilities or ADHD, may engage less. When collaboration is supported, learners test ideas with peers, reason together and think critically and creatively, and it is described as strengthening peer relationships, thinking and learning outcomes. Coordinating attention with a partner develops in childhood; attending to both sides of an argument is still developing in adolescence.

It overlaps with [social awareness and relationship skills](social-awareness-and-relationship-skills.md), which collaboration both draws on and builds, and with [communication skills](communication-skills.md). Shared regulation of a group task is covered under [self-regulation](self-regulation.md).

## Implications

### Context
- Matters for any design built on group work, peer tutoring, projects or discussion.
- Cultural norms and caregivers' expectations shape how learners collaborate, so group structures that suit one community may not suit another.
- Learners with hearing or vision impairments may need support to share common ground in a group.

### Target Learners
- Learners from preschool through high school (the LVN record covers PK-12).
- Learners with ADHD or language-based learning disabilities, who may engage less in peer collaboration.
- English learners, for whom peer collaboration can be a source of language practice.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Shared work toward a common goal.
- Peer relationships and social-emotional skills.
- Critical and creative thinking through joint reasoning.
- Academic literacy and achievement.
