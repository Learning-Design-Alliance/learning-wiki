## Description
Reading fluency is reading text accurately, at an appropriate rate and smoothly, whether silently or aloud. It rests on the word-level skills beneath it: [decoding](decoding.md), orthographic processing, phonological processing, [morphological awareness](morphological-awareness.md) and an understanding of [syntax](syntax.md). It is measured by counting words read correctly within a time limit and by having students read passages aloud, scored for accuracy and rate.

The LVN record is written for grades 7 to 12. Fluency is described as reaching its developmental peak in the later elementary years and as not usually being taught explicitly in middle and high school, so adolescents whose fluency did not develop, including many with dyslexia, can face a persistent obstacle: fluent reading lets a reader attend to meaning rather than to working out words, and critical and disciplinary reading depend on it.

It is part of the wiki's broader [reading and language](reading-and-language.md) page, whose central point is that effortful word reading uses the capacity comprehension needs. Dyslexia is covered under [learning disabilities](learning-disabilities.md).

## Implications

### Context
- Matters in secondary classrooms where content learning runs through text and fluency is no longer taught explicitly.
- Background knowledge may help weaker readers understand text more fully.
- Reading fiction and other print exposure are associated with better fluency.

### Target Learners
- Adolescents in grades 7 to 12, especially those with dyslexia or weak word-reading skills.
- Learners with high levels of inattention, or with low emotional well-being and motivation, who tend to read less fluently.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Reading comprehension.
- Critical and disciplinary reading.
- Silent and oral reading rate and accuracy.

## Measures
- **Test of Word Reading Efficiency (TOWRE)** (Torgesen et al., 1999): number of printed words read correctly in 45 seconds.
- **CBM/ORF Reading Fluency Assessment** (Rasinski, 2004): students read three passages aloud, scored for accuracy and rate.
