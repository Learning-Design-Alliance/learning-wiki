## Description
Inferencing is the work a reader does to supply meaning a text does not state outright: linking one sentence to another, connecting the text to what the reader already knows or has experienced, and adding what is learned to existing knowledge. It supports reading comprehension, and the Learner Variability Navigator notes that it develops with age, moving from inferences drawn from within the text towards deeper inferences that draw on the reader's own knowledge. It is usually assessed through comprehension questions that ask for information the text implies rather than states; LVN lists no dedicated measure for it.

It matters most in middle and high school, where subject-specific texts assume background knowledge and expect readers to connect it to new information. It depends on [prior knowledge](prior-knowledge.md) and on [working memory](working-memory.md), especially for inferences that link ideas far apart in a long text, and it is part of what [academic and critical literacy](academic-and-critical-literacy.md) asks of a reader. It is a component of the comprehension side of [Reading and Language](reading-and-language.md).

## Implications

### Context
- Content-area reading in the secondary grades leans heavily on inferences that connect background knowledge to the text.
- Building background knowledge before reading, and asking questions that require readers to connect parts of a text, are the usual supports.

### Target Learners
- Adolescent readers who decode and understand sentences but miss what a text implies.
- Readers with limited background knowledge of a topic, and readers with lower working memory spans, for inferences across long texts.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Reading comprehension, especially of content-area and figurative texts.
- Following and evaluating arguments that leave steps implicit.

- [Metacognition](metacognition.md): awareness of one's reading goals and strategies goes with better inference-making and integration of ideas.
- [Syntax](syntax.md): trouble with sentence structure can carry over into trouble inferring figurative meaning.
