# Self-Regulation

> **Evidence** · 61 claims (43 for, 13 mixed, 5 against) · 50 studies (12 associational, 10 review, 9 quant-synthesis, 8 causal, 6 theoretical, 3 qualitative, 2 design), `q1`–`q4` · 11 of 50 report an effect size · 54 claims rest on one study

## Description
Whether a learner can plan, monitor and adjust without the structure being supplied for them. It decides whether a multi-week deliverable survives without scaffolding and pacing support. The finding that matters most for design is that self-monitoring is unreliable by default: learners judge fluency rather than learning, and confident misjudgement is the normal case rather than the exception [-M]. So the design job is supplying the external signal, not exhorting reflection.

## Implications

### Context
- Decides whether an extended deliverable needs imposed checkpoints or can be learner-paced.
- Self-assessment is inaccurate without training and without criteria — asking a learner how they are doing is not a measurement [-M].
- The strategies are teachable, which is what separates this from a fixed trait.

### Target Learners
- Learners new to a domain, whose monitoring has no accurate standard to compare against.
- Learners meeting an unfamiliar task genre — a first literature review, a first long project.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Predicts achievement across domains, and predicts completion of extended work far more strongly.
- Is itself a legitimate learning objective, not only a moderator.
