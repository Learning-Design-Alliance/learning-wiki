# Motivation

## Description
Whether a learner will spend effort here, and what makes it feel worth spending. It decides whether a page must earn attention before it can teach, and how much open-endedness is tolerable. The honest state of the evidence is that the mechanisms are well supported and the *interventions* are modest — growth-mindset programmes produce small average effects concentrated in specific subgroups [+W], which is a reason to design for motivation rather than to bolt an intervention on.

## Implications

### Context
- Sets how much a page must earn attention, and how much open-endedness a task can carry.
- Autonomy, competence and relevance are the reliable levers; exhortation is not.
- Choice is not automatically motivating — what is on offer shapes both the concepts learned and who feels invited [~M].

### Target Learners
- Learners with no prior stake in the domain, for whom relevance has to be constructed rather than assumed.
- Learners whose past experience in the domain is of failure, where expectancy rather than value is the binding constraint.

### Target Learning Objectives
- Predicts persistence and effort allocation more than single-session performance.
- Moderates every other dimension: a learner who stops does not benefit from any of them.

## Related Learner Variables
- Belonging — related but distinct: belonging is about standing, motivation about wanting.
- Self-regulation — motivation starts the effort, self-regulation sustains it past the first obstacle.
- Affect regulation — a motivated learner who cannot metabolise failure still stops.
