# State of the Evidence

> Generated by `scripts/build_evidence_report.py` from the claim pages' evidence codes and the `[±~][SMW]` markers on the pages that cite them. Do not edit by hand.

What the wiki's evidence adds up to. Every number counts **distinct studies**, keyed by DOI (or by author-year where a study has none), because one source often yields many claims. Nothing here is a verdict: tiers are reported as ranges and counts, and the impact codes are never averaged. `q`, `i`, kind and rigour are defined in the [Schema & Guide](CLAUDE.md) and in `evidence-scales.json`.

## In short

The wiki holds **20,379 distinct studies** behind 90,270 claims. What they add up to (the sections below have the detail):

- **Few studies can show a cause.** 30% are experiments or systematic reviews (`q3` or `q4`); only 1% are pre-registered trials or well-powered meta-analyses (`q4`). 60% are quasi-experiments, observational studies or narrative reviews, and 10% are case studies, opinion or theory.
- **Few print an effect size.** 4,853 of 20,379 studies (24%) report one for the finding the wiki cites; the rest report a significance test, a description, or nothing that says how large an effect was.
- **Strong work is not only trials.** 2,191 studies are rated `r3`, done well for their kind; 1,497 of them are qualitative, design, theoretical or review work, which the design tier alone would rank low.
- **Most findings stand alone.** 99% of claims rest on one study; a finding reported once, in one setting, has not been shown to hold elsewhere.
- **Not all research reaches design.** 78% of studies, and 51% of claims, are cited by a principle, pattern, element, strategy or design; the rest are recorded but not connected to a design decision, either because none has been written or because the finding does not bear on one.
- **Principles and patterns.** Of 224 pages written in the conditional format, 106 have research testing their central relationship, at least in part, and 118 carry a labelled default design because nothing in the wiki tests it. Their citations of claims are 3% strong, 29% moderate and 68% weak, and 54% supporting, 41% conditional and 5% against.

These figures describe two things at once: what education research has produced, and what this wiki has collected so far. As inclusion is held to a higher standard the second will move; the first is why it has to.
