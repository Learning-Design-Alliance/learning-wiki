# State of the Evidence

> Generated by `scripts/build_evidence_report.py` from the claim pages' evidence codes and the `[±~][SMW]` markers on the pages that cite them. Do not edit by hand.

What the wiki's evidence adds up to. Every number counts **distinct studies**, keyed by DOI (or by author-year where a study has none), because one source often yields many claims. Nothing here is a verdict: tiers are reported as ranges and counts, and the impact codes are never averaged. `q`, `i`, kind and rigour are defined in the [Schema & Guide](CLAUDE.md) and in `evidence-scales.json`.

## In short

The wiki holds **9,750 distinct studies** behind 40,367 claims. What they add up to (the sections below have the detail):

- **Few studies can show a cause.** 33% are experiments or systematic reviews (`q3` or `q4`); only 2% are pre-registered trials or well-powered meta-analyses (`q4`). 56% are quasi-experiments, observational studies or narrative reviews, and 11% are case studies, opinion or theory.
- **Few print an effect size.** 2,560 of 9,750 studies (26%) report one for the finding the wiki cites; the rest report a significance test, a description, or nothing that says how large an effect was.
- **Strong work is not only trials.** 1,217 studies are rated `r3`, done well for their kind; 763 of them are qualitative, design, theoretical or review work, which the design tier alone would rank low.
- **Most findings stand alone.** 99% of claims rest on one study; a finding reported once, in one setting, has not been shown to hold elsewhere.
- **Not all research reaches design.** 76% of studies, and 48% of claims, are cited by a principle, pattern, element, strategy or design; the rest are recorded but not connected to a design decision, either because none has been written or because the finding does not bear on one.
- **Principles and patterns.** Of 224 pages written in the conditional format, 106 have research testing their central relationship, at least in part, and 118 carry a labelled default design because nothing in the wiki tests it. Their citations of claims are 4% strong, 34% moderate and 62% weak, and 49% supporting, 45% conditional and 6% against.

These figures describe two things at once: what education research has produced, and what this wiki has collected so far. As inclusion is held to a higher standard the second will move; the first is why it has to.

## Toward pooled estimates

A pooled effect size needs effect sizes with their uncertainty, on one scale, for comparable contrasts and outcomes. Those are recorded in `observations/`, which holds **27 study records**: 2 report a standardised mean difference directly and 6 more report a statistic convertible to one (a correlation, an odds ratio, a partial η²). Fewer than three studies are on that scale at all, so **no pooled estimate is reported**. The wiki's `i` codes are not a substitute: they are bins, and averaging them would state a precision nobody measured.
