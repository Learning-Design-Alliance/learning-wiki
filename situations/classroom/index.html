Designing for classroom¶

Setting · school or university class with a teacher · All situations

Authentic Assessment¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Large class (more than about 60) or little assessor time (from the wording) Use peer assessment with the rubric for the draft; teacher feedback on a sample plus whole-class feedback on common problems; double-score only a sample of finals and products near the threshold. Peer assessment [+M]; structured criteria not shown to beat freeform peer review [~M], so do not expect the rubric alone to lift the peer effect

Balanced pronunciation curriculum covering suprasegmentals, segmentals, and fluency, with a short list of central features selected by diagnosis¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for a large class, or scarce facilitator time (a volunteer tutor, a brief check-in) (from the wording) Diagnose from a short read-aloud plus one spontaneous answer per learner, ideally screened by ASR transcripts; run segmental practice as ASR self-study; spend the teacher's minutes only on suprasegmental and fluency feedback and on the re-observation samples. Resource scarcity and large classes [~M] (a review of Bangladeshi classrooms) names the constraint; ASR diagnosis [+M]; guided ASR practice [+S], carried from Korean and other ESL/EFL learners

Cognitive Activation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with a teacher designing their own tasks Before teaching, check each planned task against step 4 (does the output go beyond the material?), with a colleague if possible; expect tasks planned as interactive to come out merely active. For teachers new to this, plan one rewritten task per lesson rather than a whole activating unit. Teachers had minimal success designing Constructive and Interactive activities [~M] and Students learned more in Constructive than Active activities [+M]; Most prospective teachers see constructivist conceptions as highly valuable but not very applicable [~W] (304 Turkish prospective teachers: 76% valued constructivist teaching highly but rated it hard to apply; beliefs, not task design; the one-task-per-lesson step is untested)

Coherence Principle¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many learners, a live lecture (from the wording) Keep the explanation itself free of digressions; tell the anecdote after the point it illustrates, and only if it is an instance of it Design principle effects moderated by pacing and complexity [~M]; placement untested

Collaborative Evaluation¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
A large class, or no teacher reaches each group (from the wording) Replace group consensus with the mean of four or five independent ratings per object, and give the reference to the whole class afterwards; discuss only the objects where the mean departs most from it. Peer judgment accuracy [~M]: with a well-specified rubric, averaged ratings by AP English students correlated with expert scores about as well as teachers' did. Correlational, one subject

Concreteness Fading¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many learners (from the wording) Do the concrete stage as a demonstration with a few learners acting it out, the iconic stage individually on paper; in discussion, take a learner's symbolic answer back down to the drawing or the object before moving on Unfolding knowledge elements produces deeper conceptual learning opportunities [+W] (opportunities in the discourse, not measured learning)

Principle

One teacher with a large class, or group testing (from the wording) Integrate the shared materials once; the benefit holds in group-tested settings Effects larger one-to-one but still large under group testing [~M] (d = .89 vs .65)

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher, a large class (from the wording) Use all-student response (cards, whiteboards, polls) for most questions and a simple tally of who answers publicly; correct conduct with low-profile moves first Wait time in classrooms: Questioning Strategies Improve Learning [+M]. Tally and response modes: untested

Distributed Cognition¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many groups, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a short check-in) (from the wording) Read each group's shared representation and role line instead of listening in; spend the check-in only on groups whose progress line has not moved or whose unaided check failed; when a group's work is visible on a shared board, use the freed time to raise the challenge rather than supply information Mostly untested; in one qualitative comparison of nursing problem-based-learning groups, the interactive-whiteboard group needed less late-stage teacher scaffolding than the traditional-whiteboard group (The IW-supported group needed less teacher scaffolding in the late problem-solving stage, where nurse and instructor discourse decreased, while scaffolding increased from early to late stage in the TW condition [+W]); contingent support is better supported one-to-one than at class scale (Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [~M])

Effective Classroom Management Plan Criteria¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The whole class is disruptive (from the wording) Start with steps 1 and 2: re-teach every routine for two weeks, with daily rehearsal, before adding any new consequence; tally disruptions before and after Universal classroom management [+M] and less disruption in all 12 studies [+M] (K–12 classrooms, against usual practice); clear behavior expectations [+M] (K–5)
One teacher with a large group (a lecture, a large class, no assistant) (from the wording) Rely on prevention and routines more than on individual redirection: a visible attention signal, short timed tasks with a clear start and end, a routine for asking for help without the teacher, and peer roles for materials; respond to the group, then to individuals after class untested proposal

Feedback Loops¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom, one teacher and a large group Collect every learner's attempt at once (mini-whiteboards, a poll, a short written item), give whole-class information on the commonest error, have everyone do one matched item, then go to those who still fail; use peers with a rubric for drafts; budget class time for the loop Trained teachers' loop time [+M] (secondary mathematics, time allocation only, no learning outcome); rubrics and peer feedback [~M] (rubrics changed peer comments, one study found less reflective feedback)

Flipped Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large group (100+) (from the wording) Use the opening check as a poll, pairs or small groups for application, and whole-class feedback on the commonest error, then go to groups still stuck; trained peer teachers or assistants can extend the circulation Active learning improves exam performance [+S] (held across class sizes, largest in small classes); Peer-teaching flipped classroom students show higher calculus conceptual understanding… [+M] (two intact classes; the procedure is not described on the claim page)

Formative Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
A large class with one teacher, and written work (from the wording) Add peer review so that each learner gets more feedback than the teacher alone can give; a fixed script is optional Peer assessment improves performance [+M]; Structured criteria are not shown to improve peer assessment [~M]

Fostering Communities of Learning (FCL)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many groups (30+ learners) (from the wording) Use role cards and question stems so groups can run without the teacher; the teacher circulates on a fixed rota and checks each research group once before jigsaw; use written exit checks to decide which group to visit. Contingent scaffolding [~M]: evidence mostly from one-to-one tutoring; its claim page notes contingency across a whole class is hard. The rota is untested

Functional Behavior Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The whole class is disruptive, not one learner (from the wording) Do not start individual assessments; put universal management in place first (taught routines, clear expectations, frequent positive contact), then reassess which learners still stand out Universal classroom management [~M] (12-study meta-analysis, K–12 classrooms; universal practices without functional information)
One teacher with a full class, no support staff (from the wording) Use a tally sheet with three columns (time, activity just before, what followed) during the two lessons where the behavior is worst, for one week; test one trigger change in the second week untested proposal

Game-Based Mastery Learning¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom, one teacher and many learners Learners work the levels on devices; the class keeps a common pace with a weekly level target; the teacher spends the session's last 10 minutes with learners who failed a check twice Mastery effects vary with pacing [~M] (group-based programmes had larger effects, no game layer); group-based mastery and attrition [+M] (one synthesis)

Gamification¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and a large group Use team scores from individual quizzes (step 5), updated once a week on a class board; the teacher reviews the board, not every point Group rewards on individual learning [+M] (school studies, four weeks or more, not gamified)

Graphic Organizers¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with a large group Have pairs build the organizer together; collect a few, project two that differ, and discuss which fits the content better and why Teacher-student interactive semantic mapping produced higher EFL reading comprehension posttest scores than teacher-initiated and student-mediated semantic mapping [+M] (EFL university students); Collaborative concept mapping enhances learning more than individual concept mapping [+W] (second-hand)

Include parents, teachers, or mentors in any self-regulation intervention and support their own skills and coaching capacity¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Setting is a school classroom and the problem is conduct, exclusions or suspensions Add a short exercise on the teachers' stance towards discipline (relationships first, the student's perspective) before or alongside the student programme; track exclusions by group of students Empathic discipline [+M] (middle-school mathematics teachers; overall effect small and marginal in the preregistered replication, larger for students at high risk)

Learning-for-Use (LfU) Model¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many learners, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a short check-in per group) (from the wording) Spend facilitator time on the syntheses, not the investigation; give groups written prompts and a checklist for the investigation; at each check-in ask one question ("what does your result tell you about the driving question?"). Contingent scaffolding [~M]: its evidence is mostly one-to-one tutoring, and the claim page reports contingency is hard across a whole class

Least Restrictive Environment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher, many learners, no aide Set up structured peer tutoring pairs and assigned group roles for the class; build presentation and response options into materials for everyone; the specialist co-plans for 15 minutes a week (proposal) instead of sitting in Peer Tutoring Improves Achievement [+M] (general school populations; carried to inclusion)

Modality Principle¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many, or online-self-paced with no teacher (from the wording) Narrated short segments with pause and replay; a short "what happens if" question after each Letting learners pace helped on complex material, while learner control in general showed almost no effect [~M]; the check question is an untested proposal

Multiple Methods of Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for a large group, or scarce marker time (volunteers, a busy shift, a tutor who checks in briefly) (from the wording) Keep one quick common method for everyone; use the second method only for learners near a decision or out of line with their other work; use trained peer review for the second reading of products; a 2-minute oral check per learner at most Peer assessment improves academic performance by a small average amount relative to no assessment or to teacher assessment [+M] (meta-analysis of 54 studies; about performance, not the accuracy of peer judgements); the targeting rule is untested

Note-taking¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large lecture group (from the wording) Guided-notes outline for everyone; two or three pauses per hour with a restatement written to a neighbour or a poll; collect a random sample of notes after the first week to check for errors and address the commonest one in the next lecture Guided Notes Improve Note Accuracy [+M] (one quasi-experiment with two intact undergraduate classes); pauses and sampling untested

Online Course Design (Community of Inquiry)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
online-self-paced with rolling enrolment, or one teacher for hundreds of learners (from the wording) Replace teacher feedback on each piece with automatic feedback on self-checks, a weekly teacher summary of common errors, and peer review against a rubric; keep the week-1 if-then plan; give the teacher's time to learners flagged by a missed week. Implementation intentions [+S] (the two field experiments were in large online courses); the rest is untested

PAIR-C Framework (Pattern, Agents, Interactions, Relations, Causality)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for 25–35 learners (from the wording) Prepare a card per expected misconception with the dimension it violates and the correcting sentence; circulate during step 4 and answer from the cards; collect written explanations to read between sessions. Contingent scaffolding [~M]: evidence mostly from one-to-one tutoring, and the claim page notes that contingency across 30 students is hard

Peer Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and a large group, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a busy shift, one tutor checking in briefly) Put the structure in the role cards and the check items, not in the facilitator: the facilitator samples two or three pairs per round, corrects the commonest error for everyone, and reads only the individual checks Structured Peer Tutoring Outperforms Unstructured [+M]; sampling is an untested proposal, and contingent help is shown one-to-one only (contingent scaffolding [~M]); in an IES study of mathematics curricula, teachers directing students to help one another, and asking the class whether it agreed with a student's answer, went with higher achievement [+W] (correlational, no effect size)

Peer Teaching¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher for 25–30 learners, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a busy shift, one tutor dropping in) Put the structure in the role cards and answer keys, not the adult: the adult samples two or three pairs a round, corrects the commonest error for everyone, and reads only the individual checks. Structured Peer Tutoring Outperforms Unstructured [+M]; the sampling rule is an untested proposal

Phonics¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a whole class of 25–30 (from the wording) Introduce the new correspondence to the whole class in 10 minutes with choral responses and every child showing a written answer at once (whiteboards); then small groups by placement for decodable reading while others practise independently; weekly one-to-one checks rotated across the class; give the lowest-placed group at least as many minutes of connected reading as the others, and regroup as checks are passed Phonemic Awareness Training Improves Reading [~M] (for phonemic-awareness training, small groups beat whole-class and individual delivery; carried to phonics); Low reading-group first graders read far fewer words than high-group peers, limiting elaboration [~W] (an observational finding reported second-hand: low-group first graders read 60 words in five days, high groups about ten times as many); the rotation and the equal-time rule are untested

Physical Activity¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or primary age, in a classroom Use the default as given: one or two bouts per session, 5 minutes vigorous or more than 10 minutes moderate-to-vigorous, between segments, with a fixed transition Classroom physical activity… [+S] (primary-school and 4–17-year-old children; on-task behavior); Energizers improved on-task behavior… [+M] (10-minute breaks, grades 3–4)
One teacher with a large group, or scarce facilitator time (a volunteer, a busy shift, one tutor checking in briefly) (from the wording) Use one fixed, scripted routine the group learns once and then leads itself (a student leader, a 5-minute video or audio track), so running it costs the facilitator only the start and the closing cue untested proposal

Positive Behavioral Interventions And Supports¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher, no school-wide system (from the wording) Run the universal tier in one room: three to five posted expectations, taught in the first week, a greeting at the door, specific praise, a short consistent correction; keep a simple tally of incidents. Universal classroom management [+M]; positive greetings at the door [+M] (middle school)

Pretesting (Prequestions Before Study)¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Lecture with slides, one teacher for many learners (from the wording) Multiple-choice pretest at the start of the lecture on paper or clickers; no individual feedback needed when the lecture answers each item Classroom pretesting improves high-stakes exam performance [+M] (ten-week course, pen-and-paper pretests, no feedback)

Process-based Writing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large class or many sections (from the wording) Peers give the first response with criteria; the teacher reads a sample of peer comments and responds to each writer on two or three priorities only; rotate 5-minute conferences so each writer gets one per composition; do not grade drafts Peer Feedback Improves Writing [+M] (peer vs teacher feedback not reliably different); Students report teacher feedback on writing is insufficient [~W] (interviews; teachers' load)

Programmed Instruction¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom, one teacher for many learners The software or the printed program confirms each response; the teacher sees who is branched to remedial frames twice and goes to them; the class keeps a shared unit date and takes the unit check together. Group-based programs had larger effects than self-paced ones [~M]; the teacher's routine is untested

Provide positive comments alongside constructive criticism when responding to student writing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for a large class, or scarce responder time (a busy term, volunteer tutors, one short check-in) (from the wording) Per draft: one specific strength, one priority change, one question, written in under 3 minutes; or a whole-class note on common strengths and one common problem, with individual comments only on the priority. Return comments before the grade Students report teacher feedback on writing is insufficient, and they value scores over feedback [~M] (one interview study of Chinese college English students, who reported teachers lacked time and that they cared more about scores); the minutes are an untested proposal

Rhetorical Skill Development¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large group (from the wording) Peer response in pairs against the written criteria (one group of three in rotation only if the number is odd); the teacher reads a sample of drafts and gives whole-class feedback on the commonest gap (usually the missing answer to an objection) Peer Feedback Improves Writing [+M] (higher education; carried to school classes untested)

Scaffolding¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom, whole-class inquiry with learners who differ in prior knowledge and reasoning Combine a static structuring scaffold on the task with teacher modelling and adaptive verbal prompts that problematise, and give both procedural and conceptual help where learners stall (for example on graphs); in one trial with third graders this raised posttest reasoning over the same inquiry unit without the scaffolds and almost removed the disadvantage of lower prior knowledge and reasoning. Expect the scaffolded version to lean on different capacities: inhibition predicted learning only under scaffolding, so check who is still left behind Scaffolded third-graders outperformed control students on irrelevant-evidence reasoning [+M]; Scaffolding almost fully diminished the disadvantage of lower logical reasoning [+W]; Inhibition predicted posttest scores only under scaffolding [~W]; The teacher as adaptive expert in real-time labs [+W]

Segmenting Principle¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for many learners (from the wording) Teacher pauses the shared video at the same breaks for everyone; use the break for a whole-class prompt rather than individual pacing Effects confirmed for system-paced segmentation [+M]; the prompt is an untested proposal

Self Affirmation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent, in a classroom with one teacher (middle or secondary school) Teacher hands out the sheet at the start of a lesson early in term; 15 minutes of silent writing; collect nothing, or collect sealed and unread self-affirmation claim [~S] (seventh-graders, in class; one positive and one null replication)
One teacher for many learners, or scarce facilitator time (a volunteer, one tutor checking in briefly) (from the wording) Use a printed or online sheet that runs itself; the facilitator only reads out the instructions and keeps time; there is nothing to mark untested proposal

Simulations & Immersive Virtual Environments¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child or school-age in a classroom Use a teacher-led screen simulation with a goal sheet for each run, pairs at a device, and a whole-class debrief that names the real-world phenomenon; keep runs short (10–15 minutes); where physical materials are scarce, a simulation taught with the same instruction can stand in for them the format is an untested proposal; the substitution rests on one study of one science skill with fourth- and fifth-graders (Physical and virtual instructional materials are equally effective for teaching fourth- and fifth-graders to design unconfounded experiments when instructional method is controlled [~M], no difference found, equivalence not tested); the guided-VR experiment is the nearest evidence on guidance, and teachers report AI and virtual labs let students "try all materials virtually first, then use real ones" (Teachers report AI tools enable authentic, contextualized science experiences [+W], teacher interviews only)

Social Presence¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Face-to-face classroom Presence cues are already there; spend effort on names, on the teacher's warmth and expectations, and on routines that let quiet pupils be heard (pairs before plenary), not on profiles Positive teacher–student relationships are associated with greater student engagement and, more weakly, with achievement [+M] (correlational meta-analyses, preschool to high school, plus a learner-centred synthesis; not a test of any routine)

Structured Peer Review¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher for a large class, or no teacher reaches each pair (from the wording) Two to three reviewers per draft at random, mixing reviewers' attainment; the teacher reads comments only where an author flags a disagreement, spot-checks a small sample of drafts against the peer scores, and gives one class-wide note on the commonest problem; use averaged ratings rather than single reviews. Peer feedback on writing [+M]: number of peers did not moderate the effect; several peer reviews beat one expert's [+W] on draft-to-final improvement in one 28-student experiment; peer accuracy [~M]: averaged ratings, one subject, correlational; MOOC grader bias [~W] varied with the gap between grader's and author's attainment, observational

Summative Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher marks for a large group, or marking time is scarce (volunteers, a busy shift, one tutor) (from the wording) Machine-score the knowledge items; judge a smaller set of performance tasks with a short rubric; double-mark a sample of about one in ten, not all; return results by outcome with one comment each. A computerised adaptive test can shorten a mastery test while reaching nearly the same decision An adaptive mastery testing strategy reduces test length by 30% to 81% while reaching the same mastery decision as the conventional test for 96% of trainees [+W] (a military-training simulation); the marking plan is untested

Support Early Language Development¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher for 15–20 children Rotate small groups of 4–5 for 10-minute dialogic sessions while others play; train assistants or volunteers to run some groups; check once a week that sessions happen and children talk on most pages the teacher-delivered replication failure in the hub [~S]; frequency of implementation moderates effects [~M]; group sizes are untested proposals

Supporting Students with Intellectual Disabilities¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher, a full general class, the student needs intermittent support (from the wording) Build the student's steps into the class task (same activity, smaller step, picture checklist), seat a trained peer partner, and check the student's attempt first when circulating Peer tutoring improves achievement [+M] (tutee ability level not a moderator, mathematics); the rest untested

Teach the conceptual underpinnings of L2 forms rather than only their structural aspects or formulaic units¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
One teacher with a large class (from the wording) Take the diagnostic sample as one shared short writing task, tally the commonest deviation for the class, and teach that first; use mini-whiteboards or a poll for the choice items conceptual deviations guide where to focus [+W]; the class-scale tally is untested

Technology should augment, not supplant, learning processes in student-centered environments¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
classroom with one teacher and many learners, teacher-facing AI alerts or dashboards Limit alerts to a few the teacher acts on; the teacher keeps their own observation round; the teacher decides re-teaching from the data Real-time AI alerts can expand teachers' situational awareness but in some cases overload teachers' attention or draw focus away from their own observation flow [~W] (K-12 review, reported second-hand); Teachers who positioned AI as a partner remained instructional leaders, weaving the tool into rich instruction and using score data to decide what to re-teach [+M] (qualitative)

Think-Pair-Share¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Some learners find speaking to the whole class hard (from the wording) Let the share be written or reported by the pair, not a forced individual public answer ND students avoid courses requiring public presentations [~W]

Treat talking and writing as means to learning through dialogue-based, student-centered instruction¶
