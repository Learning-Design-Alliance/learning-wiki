Designing for adult¶

Age · 18 and over, including university students · All situations

152 rows from 138 pages say what to change when the brief's age is adult; 59 of them cite a claim. Pages whose rows cite claims come first. Read the basis before relying on a row: many carry a claim to learners or settings it did not test, and say so.

Instructor Accessibility¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
online-instructor-led, asynchronous, adult learners working or caring Make the forum and private message the main routes with a published reply window of one working day; put live slots outside working hours and record them; accept questions as text or a short voice note Creating community and interacting with students online required deliberate, planned effort unlike spontaneous face-to-face co-presence [+W] (interviews with web-based high-school teachers; carried to adult courses); the channel choices are untested
Adults in work settings (workplace training, clinical placement, supervision) (from the wording) Name the person to ask on each shift or project and a back-up; agree an escalation route for urgent questions and a slower one for learning questions; schedule short regular meetings rather than relying on ad hoc asking Tutors and trainee researchers most value supportive attitude, mastery of the research field, and accessibility in a research project supervisor [+W] (online doctoral supervision; what people value, not an effect); the arrangement is untested

Personalization¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are young children (child), or adult Child: two options at most, chosen by the teacher from what the child said; personalize contexts more than paths; in kindergarten, expect a small gain from AI-driven personalized practice and do not let it replace the teacher's tailoring. Adult: ask what they already do and want; expect a wider spread of experience, and offer more paths and more choice Adult: Adult learner groups are more heterogeneous than child groups, calling for individualized instruction and varied learning strategies [+W] (essay, no data). Child: School level significantly moderates effects: AI-enabled personalized STEM education is more effective in junior and senior high school than in kindergarten [~M] (a subgroup comparison across RCTs in one meta-analysis: kindergarten g = 0.118 against 0.42–0.53 from elementary to senior high; between studies, not a test within one); the number of options is untested
Adults in work settings (from the wording) Use tasks and cases from each learner's own job as the contexts; let them choose which competencies to work on first after a diagnostic, since not all need the same ones Not all students would benefit from improving the same competencies, suggesting an individualized approach to education may be preferable to a one-size-fits-all approach [~W] (an issue brief's analysis of students' competencies and later outcomes; carried to adults in work); contexts: untested

Social Presence¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
online-instructor-led, asynchronous, text-only, adult learners who work or care for others Run the full default: course-tied introductions with two named replies, weekly named announcements, small stable groups, a reply frame, one optional live meeting a fortnight at a time outside working hours, recorded Online community required deliberate, planned effort [+W] (interviews with web-based high-school teachers; carried to adult courses); the steps are untested
Adults in work settings or professional programmes (an MBA, clinical or workplace training) (from the wording) Make introductions professional: role, expertise, a problem from their work they want help with; group learners so their expertise complements; let them judge peers by what they offer, and build peer review of work on that Peer monitoring among MBA learners goes beyond CoI social presence by involving professionally relevant evaluation of peers' contributions [~W] (interviews with six MBA students)

Adopt humanistic and radical approaches so adult learners develop as individuals and can question social injustice¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult in a voluntary community or adult-education course Run the default as written; take the opening list of learners' own questions as the source of later sessions' topics One adult educator's first-person reflection reports that facilitated class discussions left her feeling empowered as an individual contributor [+W] (one author's reflection on her own adult classes)
Adults in a work setting (workplace-clinical), or a mandatory course with a set target (from the wording) Keep the set target as the assessed goal; use the humanistic part (views heard and answered, sources with more than one position) inside it; make social questioning and any action optional and ungraded; do not ask learners to take positions in front of their managers untested proposal

Ask Experts¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult professionals or trainees in a workplace-clinical setting Hold consultations on real cases from the learner's work; the mentor models the decision on one case, then observes the learner on the next and debriefs; plan when the mentor stops being present Mentor modelling and a school-wide behaviour program's strategies transferred to the mentee's classroom management practices [+W] (one preservice teacher, qualitative case study)

Balanced pronunciation curriculum covering suprasegmentals, segmentals, and fluency, with a short list of central features selected by diagnosis¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in work settings (nurses, call-centre staff, engineers) with named listeners (from the wording) Take the feature list and tasks from their work talk (handovers, phone calls, explanations to clients); judge success by whether those listeners understand; set intelligibility, not accent, as the goal. Adults and native-like pronunciation [~M] (narrative digest); the work-task focus is untested

Blended Learning¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult working professionals (adult-workplace) Put content and repeated practice online where they can fit it around work; use in-person time for practice with equipment, cases or colleagues and for feedback on performance. Blended learning improves outcomes [~M], pooled mostly across higher education and professional training, with extra time and resources confounded

Build productive instructor-learner relationships so adults feel connected and take an active role in learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult professionals or advanced in the field (a workplace course, continuing education) Treat the relationship as between colleagues: replace the personal intake with "what do you want to use this for at work, and what do you already do?", let learners bring cases from their work from session 2, and invite them to correct you Four relational components—positive feedback, rapport, mutual respect, and reciprocal trust—form the relational framework of coaching [+W] (teachers and their coaches, focus groups); otherwise untested

Building Empathy¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Higher education or adult learners in experiential programmes (practicums, service learning, study abroad) (from the wording) Build in one-to-one contact with the people served and a structured reflection on what the learner understood of their view Students perceive that interaction in experiential learning fosters empathy [+W] (12 interviews, self-report); One-on-one contact with community partners disrupts stereotypes [+W] (a partner's report)

Cognitive Activation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adults or professionals (adult, adult-workplace) Draw the two cases for comparison from the learners' own work, differing on the one feature that matters, and ask what they share before naming the principle. Comparing contrasting cases improves learning [+M] (the transfer experiment was with undergraduates learning negotiation; untested in workplace training)

Compensate for age-related sensory impairments by combining audio input with visual presentation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Older adult learners, some with hearing loss, in a classroom with background noise Room fixes first (doors shut, machines off, face the group, seat by need); write every new item as it is spoken; repeat questions from the floor Spoken–written beats spoken-only [+M], carried from postsecondary learners without a recorded hearing loss; room fixes untested

Concreteness Fading¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults or undergraduates learning a formal structure (from the wording) Use a short concrete stage (one session or less) and move to iconic quickly Fading condition outperformed generic and concrete conditions [+W] (undergraduates); the length is untested

Considering The Opposite¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in work settings (workplace-clinical), e.g. diagnosis (from the wording) A diagnostic time-out before the decision is final: list the findings that do not fit, name the most dangerous alternative, say what test would separate them A systematic review of bias-mitigation training names considering the opposite as a previously validated way to reduce judgment biases, without testing it itself [+M], carried to clinical judgment, where it is untested in the wiki

Cultural & Life Experiences Connections¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult, in work or with family responsibilities Make cases from learners' work and lives the main material from the second session; ask each learner for one case per unit, and compare across them; avoid leftover school-age materials Many practices in adult literacy programs violate principles of learning for adults, including short-timeframe funding, isolated individualized learning, ignored learner experience, and non-meaningful materials [+W] (a 1993 handbook's review of earlier writing, naming ignored learner experience and reused elementary materials as common faults; no data, and no test of the alternative)

Collaborative critique-and-redesign [+M]: first-year psychology, single group, no control

Error Analysis¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
adult or professional, intermediate Collect a confidence rating on each answer; review confident errors first and in most depth; low-confidence errors may need re-teaching, not analysis. high-confidence errors [+M]; tested on recall, untested on professional judgement

Principle

adult learners re-entering study, or adult basic education Start from sources they already use (health, benefits, news, job information); teach the off-page move on those first; keep reading load of practice sites manageable and model searching step by step Untested; the claim's adults are university students. Evidence-Based Teaching and Scientific Reasoning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult (adult basic education, literacy, ESOL) Expect few rigorous comparisons; adopt as a one-term local trial with a written dropping rule; carry school evidence only with a note that it was not tested on adults There is not yet much rigorous evidence to guide decision making around instructional and support strategies for adult learners [~W]
Adults in work settings (training in a company, a health service, a public agency) (from the wording) Same steps; define the outcome as performance on the job at a stated delay, not end-of-course satisfaction; pilot with one cohort against the existing course untested proposal; the claims here are about schools and adult education

Pattern

Adults in work settings (adult-workplace, workplace-clinical) (from the wording) Use a recent work incident or case as the experience; make step 4 a statement of what they would do differently and why; make step 5 a question to take to a colleague or the next shift. Specific reflection prompts [+S] and guided reflection [+M], carried from undergraduates and graduate students to workplace learners

Explicit Instruction: Online Reading Strategies¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent or adult second-language readers Keep the strategy routine and name each move in plain language (the learners' first language allowed for the record); choose pages slightly below their reading level for the first sessions Reading Strategy Instruction Improves Comprehension [+S] (meta-analysis of strategy instruction for English L2 learners, print texts; carried to online)
adult learners in work settings Use the pages they actually read at work (an intranet, a policy portal, a supplier's documentation) and a real work question; the synthesis is the answer they would give a colleague untested proposal

Fostering Communities of Learning (FCL)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in a work setting (a professional learning cohort, workplace-clinical) (from the wording) Make the theme a recurring problem of practice; each member researches a case or source and teaches it back; the shared task is a protocol or guide the team will use; observe practice afterwards, not only self-report. Give the facilitator time to plan what each meeting asks members to reflect on, and treat disagreement about a case as material for the next round rather than a reason to close the meeting. Communities of practice principle, whose evidence is descriptive; mathematics PLC review [+M] (a review of descriptive studies, no comparison). One phenomenological study of three PLC leaders (Prytula 2012) reports that the PLC nurtured their metacognition [+W], that each leader's own metacognition shaped the work she led [+W], and one leader's view that disagreement catalysed learning [~W] (another adjourned to avoid conflict); a survey of small Chicago high schools found higher reported trust and collaboration [+W] (no effect size). None measures learning or practice against a comparison

Fostering Creative Thinking¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult professionals, in a workplace setting Use problems from their own work; run silent individual generation before discussion so seniority does not set the agenda; collect ideas anonymously; converge against the team's own criteria Anonymous real-time sharing mitigates evaluation apprehension [+W] (undergraduates, authors' account, carried to work teams untested)

Game-based Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult or professionals in a workplace-clinical setting Build the game around one job decision with real consequences in its rules; use it as one part of a training programme, not the whole of it; leave it open for repeated voluntary play; debrief against the real procedure; keep competition optional Supplement, not standalone [+M] and unlimited access [+M] (simulation games in training, pooled); the whole-task argument behind building around one job decision is untested

Principle

Use the adopters' own work tasks as the trial material. Put follow-up contact into the working week Follow-up contact related most strongly to adoption success [+M] (school dissemination; carried to workplaces untested)

Principle

Adult or professional: ask what they already do, and move guided items onto tasks from their own work as soon as the step is stable Child: Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [+M] (fourth and fifth graders, long division, 8 per condition). Adult: untested proposal

adult learners in work settings, or teachers adopting a reform Pilot the new method alongside current practice on one component; add monthly sessions where colleagues pass on what is new; let adults bring their own cases into the application half Mead Rapid Change Obsoletes Knowledge [+W] (Mead's argument as reported); untested as a design

Journaling¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in work settings (placements, clinical rotations, apprenticeships, coaching) (from the wording) A fieldwork log: after each shift or visit, one observed event, what was done and why, what to try next; the mentor reads the "try next" line before the next shift; a monthly review entry discussed with the mentor. reflection interventions [+M] (higher education, pooled across reflective activities; not specific to logs or workplaces)

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult jobseekers on low incomes who need work quickly Use an earn-and-learn route where one exists: paid from the start, wage raised at each credential; first credential in about six to eight weeks registered apprenticeship employment [+M] and earnings [+M] (adults in DOL-funded apprenticeships against community-college and employment-service comparison groups, ninth quarter; compares routes, not this design move); the weeks are an untested proposal

Pattern

Implementation intentions [+S]: worked when the obstacle was predictable and surmountable, not for lack of time; extension is untested

Mentor Support in Self-Paced Online Learning¶

Pattern

Experienced adult learners in work, choosing their own pace (from the wording) Keep the plan-and-obstacles activity, and let the learner choose check-in frequency after the first milestone; keep triggered outreach Implementation intentions worked when obstacles were work or family, not lack of time [+S]; fading schedule is untested

Mentoring and Coaching¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult college students at risk of leaving Assign a coach to each student with proactive outreach on enrolment, planning and obstacles; judge the programme on persistence first, and do not expect grades or completion to follow automatically InsideTrack© Coaching has potentially positive effects on college credit accumulation and persistence, based on one RCT meeting WWC standards [+M]; InsideTrack© Coaching shows no discernible effects on college degree completion (attainment) [~M]; In the Linkow et al. (2019) scale-up QED, coaching showed no statistically significant effect on cumulative college GPA (effect size 0.01, p = 0.82) [-M]

Mindfulness Activities¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adolescent or young adult, and the goal is better regulation of stress or emotion Plan a multi-week programme with practice in real situations and a debrief, not a one-off minute; keep the before-task routine as one part of it A practice brief reviewing self-regulation interventions reports substantial benefits of mindfulness programs for cognitive and emotional regulation, stress and mental health in adolescents and young adults [+M] (practice brief summarising a review of programmes; no effect size; does not test a single routine)
Adults in a work setting (adult-workplace), or adult learners arriving tired or fragmented (from the wording) One minute, framed as a focus reset rather than wellness or meditation; no inward-feeling language; open with the agenda straight after untested proposal; from the earlier page (adult learners balancing competing demands)

Modality Principle¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults (postsecondary, workplace training) (from the wording) Narrate system-paced explanations Mayer: 17 of 17 experiments with college learners, median d = 1.02 [+M]; animation or picture with voice beat picture with text for 36 adults [+M]

Modeling¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
adult learners in a work setting (workplace-clinical), or teachers in training Model the performance in the real setting (the mentor's own lesson, a supervised shift), then debrief the decisions it involved, then have the novice try the same move with the mentor watching Mentor modelling and a school-wide behaviour program's strategies transferred to the mentee's classroom management practices [+W] (one preservice teacher, qualitative case); Vicarious experiences are positively related to agriculture teachers' general teaching efficacy, with observing a first year teacher explaining 11% of variance [+W] (correlational, reported in a review)

Multimodal Instruction¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners work in a second language (adult L2 readers) Keep the text available alongside narration and video rather than removing it as redundant. Monitor comprehension of the words alone. Video with narration and text [+M]: one randomized study of Chinese-speaking university EFL learners, immediate test only; redundancy [~M] reports reversals for abridged text

Online Course Design (Community of Inquiry)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in work settings, studying around jobs and family (from the wording) Publish the full term's due dates in week 1; make the if-then plan name a work or family obstacle; offer a reduced catch-up route after a missed week. Implementation intentions [+S] (MCII helped most when the obstacle was predictable, such as work or family obligations); withdrawal reasons [~M] were led by no stated reason, transfer and schedule conflict in one programme, and dissatisfaction was rarely the stated reason [~W]

PAIR-C Framework (Pattern, Agents, Interactions, Relations, Causality)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are pre-service or practicing teachers (adult, postsecondary) learning natural selection Run the default arc as given, with the NetLogo moths and mice; add step 7 if they must teach recognizing emergence to pupils. The PAIR-C study [~M]: this population and domain, near transfer only

Self-Directed Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
adult learners in work settings, or adults returning to study Start from the learner's own work goal; break it into two-week process goals; offer a progress view and suggested next steps; leave the final judgement of relevance with the learner; for low-skilled adults add a named coach who checks in Adult learners expect AI to enhance relevance through real-time goal monitoring, goal breakdown, career companionship, and networking recommendations [~W] and Adult learners expect AI to enhance autonomy through choices, flexible learning spaces, metacognitive awareness, and structured self-assessment [+W] (interviews about expectations, not outcomes); the coach from coaching for low-skilled adults, untested

Signaling Principle¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in a short system-paced video (from the wording) Signal heavily: spoken overview, on-screen headings for each segment, a pointer or highlight on the part being described Signaling improves learning [+S] (design mattered more for system-paced lessons)

Simulations & Immersive Virtual Environments¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in work settings (vocational training, safety, customer handling) (from the wording) Build scenarios from the learners' own workplace incidents, including the rare and risky ones, not only volume practice of routine tasks; make errors produce their real consequences in the simulation; refer settings and moves to the real machine during the run; end with a supervised task at work Intelligent Extended Reality interventions in VET show consistent positive effects on procedural competence, practical skills, and learner motivation [+M] (review, no pooled effect); adult learners interviewed said they want simulations that closely resemble actual job scenarios (Adult learners expect AI to support transformative and experiential learning through assumption-challenging simulations, role play, and authentic virtual contexts [+W], expectations, not outcomes); one ethnography of forestry-machine simulator training found instruction centred on volume training of routine tasks rather than the risky situations that justify a simulator (Simulator-based VET instruction in this study centres on volume training of routine tasks rather than rehearsing specific high-stakes, risky situations. [~W]), with the simulator showing errors' consequences (Simulator feedback responds authentically to student error [+W]) and teachers using the real machine as the benchmark (VET teachers use the real machine as the benchmark of legitimacy for simulator instruction [+W]); observations of practice, no outcomes; workplace incidents untested

Social Interdependence¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult / postsecondary, in a live course Use small structured groups for problem sets, labs and design tasks, with individual assessment kept separate from the group product Small group learning improves STEM achievement [+M] (39 undergraduate STEM studies, d = 0.51; compared groups with non-group instruction and does not isolate interdependence)

Strengths-based Approach¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult in work settings (trainees, career changers, professionals) Ask what they already do at work that the course builds on, and use those tasks as entry points; feedback comes from the supervisor or mentor in person, naming a specific performance, rather than through a rating form Adults: untested proposal. Feedback channel: The effect of social persuasion on student teacher efficacy depends on its form [~M] (student teachers, reported in a review: verbal feedback from the cooperating teacher related to efficacy; a structured rating tool was detrimental against a control)

Structured Peer Review¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in a work setting (design teams, clinical or teaching placements) (from the wording) Review the real work product against the workplace's own standards; the author states what decision the draft must support; a senior colleague reviews alongside peers and requires a revise-and-resubmit where evidence is missing. Probing feedback with revise-and-resubmit [+W]: a case study of teacher candidates in clinical placements, instructor feedback not peer; progress was not linear

Support Early Language Development¶
