Designing for advanced¶

Expertise · substantial prior knowledge, or experts · All situations

108 rows from 107 pages say what to change when the brief's expertise is advanced; 73 of them cite a claim. Pages whose rows cite claims come first. Read the basis before relying on a row: many carry a claim to learners or settings it did not test, and say so.

Ask Experts¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on this task, or already self-diagnose most errors Drop the explanation and modelling steps; the learner presents work and their own critique, and the expert adds only what the learner missed; use peer critique between consultations Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (review of cognitive-load studies, not consultations; gives no crossover point)

Balanced pronunciation curriculum covering suprasegmentals, segmentals, and fluency, with a short list of central features selected by diagnosis¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced and want fluency or ease of understanding in long turns Weight the list towards thought groups, pausing, linking and intonation; use repeated retellings and listener feedback, not ASR, for these. Fluspeak [~W] (poor for advanced learners seeking fluency); immediate task repetition [+W]

Build productive instructor-learner relationships so adults feel connected and take an active role in learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult professionals or advanced in the field (a workplace course, continuing education) Treat the relationship as between colleagues: replace the personal intake with "what do you want to use this for at work, and what do you already do?", let learners bring cases from their work from session 2, and invite them to correct you Four relational components—positive feedback, rapport, mutual respect, and reciprocal trust—form the relational framework of coaching [+W] (teachers and their coaches, focus groups); otherwise untested

Classroom Space and Procedural Design¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners already advanced on the assignment type Give the criteria only; drop the stage list and example outline Expertise reversal [~M] (cognitive-load studies, carried to assignment structure they did not test)

Cognitive Flexibility¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Expertise intermediate or advanced, an ill-structured domain (clinical reasoning, law, policy) Use criss-crossing: revisit each concept in several short cases under different themes across sessions; drop the step-by-step part tasks. Presenting multiple cases from different perspectives supports transfer in ill-structured domains [+M]; tested with first- and second-year undergraduates, untested with professionals

Cognitive Styles¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the task, or advanced Novice: give the structure or worked model to all, regardless of style. Advanced: drop the frame and check the approach on one unaided task; prior knowledge, not style, sets how much guidance to give Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~S] (cognitive-load studies; does not address styles)

Collaborative Evaluation¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or practising professionals Have them co-construct criteria, judge genuinely borderline cases, and record reasoned disagreement instead of forcing consensus; use a rubric for the shared vocabulary, not as a score sheet. Rubric guidance [~M]: in a graduate course a rubric changed feedback content but restrained reflective and divergent commentary

Combine intentional and incidental vocabulary learning rather than relying on incidental learning alone¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced, already at about 95% coverage of their texts Shift most time to extensive reading and listening with few glosses; keep deliberate study for low-frequency words in their field and for the after-input routine Incidental vocabulary exposure produces reliable learning, but learners pick up only a small share… [~S] (why some deliberate study stays even for strong readers); ratio untested

Considering The Opposite¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or practising professionals Prompt only, with no supplied alternative; use the red-team version (one person argues against the leading proposal) on consequential decisions Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load studies, carried to this prompt); the red-team form is untested

Contiguity Principle (Spatial and Temporal)¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on this content (advanced), or already read this kind of diagram fluently Drop the explanatory text and give the diagram alone or with key labels; check with a changed-condition question Integration reverses into redundancy for experienced trainees [-M]; guidance that helps novices can become redundant [~S]; qualified by prior knowledge did not moderate design-principle effects overall [~M]

Debriefing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced, or experienced practitioners Learners choose the decision points and lead; the facilitator asks for alternatives considered and the reasoning behind them, and adds only what was missed; keep it shorter Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load studies; no study of debriefs)

Deliberate Practice¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced and have plateaued Let the learner pick the component from their own log, raise the standard, use outcome targets beside process goals, and give feedback mainly when the log shows a plateau Process goals lead to better skill acquisition for novices than outcome goals. [~M] (reports that a shift to outcome goals after mastery can help; high-school writing)

Design tools so productive usage is guaranteed by affordances and constraints rather than instructions¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on the task, or already use the tool efficiently Keep constraints light: block only the shortcut that skips the target; leave choice of tasks and features open In simulated mastery learning, system constraints left most other task-selection strategies' overpractice stable while sharply reducing it under the outlier risk-averse strategy [~W] (simulated learners in mastery learning; carried to real learners untested)

Develop Understanding¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced, or explain the idea correctly at step 1 Skip the worked cases; go to boundary cases, comparison with a neighbouring idea, and use in a richer task, with selective feedback. Expertise reversal [~M] (a review of cognitive-load studies; gives no crossover point)

Digital Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on the task, or succeed unaided on the first set Remove most hints and prompts; give mixed, whole-task items and feedback only on request Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (a cognitive-load review; gives no crossover point)

Disciplinary Literacy¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced (upper-level undergraduates, graduate students) or already read as insiders Skip most modelling; give harder or contested texts, ask for critique of a published argument, and check with one unaided text set before deciding Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load studies, not disciplinary reading)

Distributed Cognition¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on this task, or already succeed alone Give them a blank template rather than a laid-out representation, fewer assigned roles, and judge them on the unaided check from the first session Chunking reduces working memory load by grouping information into fewer, more meaningful units. [~S] (its Discussion: imposed chunking helps only until the learner can chunk unaided; carried from memory research)

Dual Coding¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on this content Drop most supplied visuals; ask for a redraw from memory or a learner-made summary diagram, checked against a model Graphic organizers for novices [~M] (the higher-ability subset showed no benefit; the authors say this cannot show maps fail them); the redraw is untested

Elaborative Reasoning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or intermediate on this topic Use why-prompts freely, on denser material, with fewer modelled answers; move early to learner-written questions Elaborative interrogation benefits increase as learners' prior knowledge increases [~M] (one study reported in a review: larger gain in the high-knowledge domain)

Employ flexible error treatment strategies matched to objectives, competence, affect, and correction effectiveness¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are intermediate or advanced, and the form has been taught Prompt first (repeat the error, give a rule clue, ask again); give the correct form only if the prompt fails. For an advanced learner who gets by with paraphrase, push for the exact form rather than accepting the workaround Prompts and explicit correction larger than recasts in classrooms [~M]; workaround row: advanced learners content with circumlocution risk fixed errors [~W] (one learner's diary)

Explicit Instruction: Internet Search¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners already pick key terms and revise on their first observed search (advanced searchers) Skip the modelling; give harder questions (unfamiliar field vocabulary, specialist databases, conflicting results) and peer comparison of search logs Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load studies, carried to searching)

Explicit Instruction: Math Strategies¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or already fluent in one method Skip single-strategy modelling; go straight to comparing their method with an alternative on problems where the alternative is quicker; mixed problems requiring a choice Comparing cases side by side improves learning and transfer by a moderate average amount, though in one algebra study it raised procedural knowledge and flexibility but not conceptual knowledge [+S] (seventh graders in the algebra study); Fading support promotes the transfer of responsibility from instructor to learner. [+S]

Explicit Instruction: Online Reading Strategies¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced online readers, or keep to a goal and join pages on the check task Drop the think-aloud and the curated sets; give one open-web synthesis task with a short debrief on their choices Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load studies; carried; gives no crossover point)

Feedback Loops¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced, or already accurate unaided Shorten external loops: ask for self-evaluation first, give information only on what the self-evaluation missed, and loop on harder or transfer items Expertise reversal [~M] (review of guidance studies, carried to feedback; no crossover point given)

Flexible Grouping¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on this task, or most already pass the check Skip the support group for them; form an extension group with a harder or wider task, or place them in mixed groups as explainers with an explaining role, not answer-givers Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load studies, not grouping); Learning By Teaching Improves Tutor Learning [+M] (tutors' own gains)

Goal Setting & Monitoring¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on the task (new procedure, first attempts); or intermediate/advanced Novices: process goals naming the strategy steps, split into proximal sub-goals, outcome goals only once the process runs unprompted. Experienced learners: outcome goals with a stated criterion, which they may set or negotiate (learner-set goals are an untested proposal) Process goals for novices [+M]; tested with high-school writers, untested in other domains

Graphic Organizers¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on this material, or already explain its structure Drop the provided organizer; ask learners to sketch their own quickly, or use none; spend the time on application Graphic Organizers Support Novice Comprehension [~M] (in five studies each, low-ability learners gained from maps and high-ability learners did not; underpowered subsets); Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (a review, not tested on organizers)

Guided Practice¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on this task, or succeed on the first item unaided Drop most guided rounds; give one check item, then independent or whole-task practice with selective feedback Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (review of cognitive-load studies; gives no crossover point)

Handouts/Online Guides/Visual Reading Aids¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on this material, or already find the essential points unaided Give a blank template or headings only, or no aid; drop marks and organizers that repeat what they can already see; keep only glosses for terms they do not know Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load review; gives no crossover point); Signaling Improves Learning [~S] (in one eighth-grade field study, signals linking text and pictures harmed high-prior-knowledge learners)

Holistic Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are intermediate or advanced for this content Replace the overview with the opening sketch; start at the first part they cannot explain; spend the time on integrative tasks Expertise reversal [~M] (review of cognitive-load studies, carried to overviews; no crossover point)

Interleaving¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on the kinds, or already strong in the subject Skip the blocked introduction, but check rather than assume that mixing helps them: give a short mixed check first, and if they already discriminate the kinds, spend the time on harder kinds instead In the few studies that split by ability, learning techniques helped low-ability and not high-ability students [~W] (interleaved practice had negative effects in the high-ability group; 6 studies of high-ability students across all techniques)

Keep the learner in control of the information flow with on-demand tools and supports¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced, following the scene without help Drop the glossary pass; keep replay and the response points; give helps only for idioms and cultural references Proficiency moderates gloss effects: vocabulary benefits appear at Levels 2 and 3 but not Levels 1 and 4 [~M] (no benefit at the highest level)

Knowledge Organization¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced, or already state the organization unaided Skip the given organization; ask them to rebuild it from memory, then reorganize it for a new task or critique where it fails Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (review of cognitive-load studies; carried to organizations, which it did not test)

Learning-for-Use (LfU) Model¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or already hold the idea Skip most of the construct phase: state the idea, and spend the time on harder consequential tasks and new cases; let learners design their own investigation. Problem-first effects rose with grade level [~M] (school and university grades, not expertise as such); otherwise untested

Massive Open Online Course (MOOC)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or practising professionals (a teachers' PD MOOC) Offer a placement check to skip modules; replace content questions with an application task in their own workplace, with evidence of practice (a lesson plan, a recorded session, a reflection) reviewed against a rubric; consider stackable micro-credentials for the applied tasks. One issuer reported that micro-credential stacks closed the gap between MOOC-Ed content and classroom practice [+W] (a q1 case report, no comparison)

Match the form and content of staff development interventions to clients' assessed Stages of Concern¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
People are experienced users (advanced in this innovation), or already show impact concerns Drop introductory sessions; give time to look at student work and effects, to collaborate and to try modifications; keep a short check for leftover management problems concerns moved from lower to higher stages over a year of use [+M] (associational, one curriculum); the matching move is untested

Minimize student frustration by making feedback clear, legible, focused, and transparent¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced writers, or already act on most comments Fewer comments, aimed at higher-level features; let them choose the feature they want feedback on; a full tool report is acceptable Students need clear guidance so they are not overwhelmed by Ecree's feedback, which may be most useful for advanced writers [~W] (one study, grade 8–11 English; the advanced-writer point is the brief's hedge)

Mnemonic Device¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or high-achieving on this content Skip the mnemonic for items they can already organise; give retrieval practice and use tasks instead; offer a mnemonic only for a truly arbitrary list In the few studies that split by ability, learning techniques helped low-ability students and not high-ability students [~M] (19 studies, all ten techniques pooled, not mnemonics alone)

Modality Principle¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners already know this content well, or have used the device or diagram before (advanced) Give the diagram with little or no accompanying text or narration; make narration optional The modality effect reverses with expertise [-M]

Modeling¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on this task, or succeed on the first item unaided Skip the full model: let them attempt first, then compare their work with an expert model and name the differences; model only the one new decision Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (review of cognitive-load studies; no crossover point)

Motivation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or already interested in the topic Do not add tangible rewards or points for taking part; offer harder goals and choices of topic or product Expected tangible rewards reduce later free-choice engagement with a rewarded task, while verbal praise does not [~S] (undermining is for tasks already found interesting); choice and challenge untested here

Multimodal Instruction¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on this content and unfamiliar with its representations; or advanced Novice: use words plus one visual type. Teach how to read it first (what the axes, arrows or symbols mean), add one explicit translation prompt per idea, and hold back a second visual type until learners can read the first. Advanced: cut to the most efficient representation, drop duplicating modes, and use translation tasks for checking rather than teaching. Novice: multiple representations [~M] (the review ties the benefit to representational competence). Advanced: untested proposal (the claim pages discuss expertise reversal; no entry here tests it for modes)

Note-taking¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on the content, or already take selective notes Skip guided notes and modelling; state the purpose, keep one recall review; let them choose the format Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (review of cognitive-load studies; not tested on note-taking aids)

Observation/Shadowing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or experienced practitioners (in-service teachers, senior staff observing each other) Let them set their own focus, observe whole live performances, and compare directly with their own practice; replace the designer's focus sheet with the observed person's question; shorten or drop the corrective part of the debrief Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load studies on instructional formats; carried to observation guidance)

Online Course Design (Community of Inquiry)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or practitioners (an MBA, a professional course) Use their work as the discussion material (cases from their own settings), let them facilitate earlier, and keep the content strand short; keep the fixed structure. Template and organisation ratings [+W] from one accelerated online MBA, descriptive; the rest is the earlier page's guidance

Pairing Non-examples with Examples¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced, or already discriminate the boundary on a check Skip anchors and labelled pairs; give only borderline cases, or have them build non-examples for others and defend them Expertise reversal [~S] (guidance in general, cognitive-load studies; gives no crossover point)

Peer Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced, or some are well ahead Let the more knowledgeable explain to those meeting the material (cross-age or cross-level), with a role card so they question rather than tell; give them harder material to prepare Older peers as tutors support gains for older and younger students [+W] (a narrative policy review, no data printed); Learning By Teaching Improves Tutor Learning [+S] for the explainer's side; first-year students in problem-based learning said selected and trained senior student tutors explained more clearly and in plainer terms than staff tutors [+W] (one focus group, perceptions only)

Peer Teaching¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Some learners are well ahead (advanced) or the school can use older pupils Use cross-level or cross-age pairs: the learner ahead teaches with a role card that makes them question rather than tell, and gets harder material to prepare. Check the taught learner alone, not only the pair. Older peers as tutors support gains for older and younger students [+W] (a narrative policy review, no data printed); limit: cross-age tutoring showed no effect on oral proficiency [~M]

Personalization¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are novice on this task, or advanced / already succeed on the first item Novice: more worked steps and closer items, fewer choices. Advanced: skip guided sections after an unaided check and go to harder or transfer items Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (review of cognitive-load studies; gives no crossover point)

Phonics¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners already decode accurately (advanced for decoding) Drop phonics lessons; give the screening only, teach the specific gaps it shows, and spend the time on fluency, vocabulary and comprehension Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (a cognitive-load review, carried to reading)

Positive Self-talk¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced, or the skill is already automatic Drop step cues; keep at most one motivational or focusing cue for pressure moments Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load studies of instructional formats; carried to self-talk untested)

Practice with Simulated People¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are experienced practitioners (advanced) Drop the worked example, use harder scenarios with complications, and put the time into reflection after each attempt Reflection phases did more at higher education levels [~M]

Procedural Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on this procedure, or complete the first item unaided Skip modelling and guided attempts; give mixed, spaced practice on full tasks and selective feedback, and refine weak components Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load studies; gives no crossover point)

Process-based Writing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Writers are advanced in the genre (they already plan and revise globally) Drop the scripted strategy and prompt cards; let the writer set the revision focus; keep one outside reader's response per piece Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load review, not writing); untested for writing

Professional development should guide teachers on integrating digital content and tools, not just offer technical know-how¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Teachers already operate the tool fluently (advanced users) but use it little in teaching Drop the technical walkthrough; spend the whole first session on subject examples and planning one lesson; start the follow-up cycle at once Teacher interviews: subject-specific use cases are the top stated PD need [+M] (stated need, 21 science teachers)

Project Space and Instructional Space¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or already hold most component skills Test out of components at launch; freezes become rare and learner-requested; use the instructional space mainly for automaticity of routines; let them attempt conceptual gaps before any instruction. Guidance advantage recedes with prior knowledge [~S] (the narrative review within that claim); timing untested

Responsive Teaching¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced, or already on track at the first elicitation point Keep the plan; one elicitation point to confirm, then move to harder or transfer items; drop the detour menu Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load studies; carried from guidance formats to teacher responsiveness)

Rhetorical Skill Development¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners already adapt to readers (advanced, experienced professionals) Drop scripted move-by-move routines; set outcome goals (the reader's response); give harder audiences, hostile questions and critique of published texts Process goals lead to better skill acquisition for novices than outcome goals. [~M] (suggests shifting to outcome goals after mastery; does not test advanced learners)

Self-Directed Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or experienced in the domain Skip the first stage of the arc; let them propose goals and resources from the start, with checkpoints every two weeks and a check of their criteria rather than their plan Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (cognitive-load review; gives no crossover point)

Sequencing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced on this target, or pass the prerequisite check Skip the early stages; start at the first stage they cannot do unaided; drop the primer and the part-task runs; use problem-first and mixed practice from the start Instructional guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M] (a review of cognitive-load studies; gives no point at which to switch)

Signaling Principle¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners already know much of the material (advanced) Drop the text–picture link cues; keep at most headings; let learners turn highlighting off Signaling improves learning [~M] (signals harmed high-prior-knowledge eighth graders in one field study)

Simulations & Immersive Virtual Environments¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are advanced or practising professionals Skip the primer and simplified runs; use unfamiliar or rare scenarios, time pressure and mixed cases; let learners lead the debrief against stated criteria untested proposal; the part-task finding is for absolute novices only (Part-task practice reduces cognitive load for absolute novices [~M])
Learners' prior knowledge of the domain differs (novice vs advanced) Give novices worked examples of an experiment or case before they run; give advanced learners reflection phases after the run; do not rely on prompts alone Prior knowledge moderates scaffolding effectiveness [~M]; Presence versus absence of individual scaffolding types did not support the added-value hypothesis, but scaffolding combinations partly did [~M] (higher education)

Social Presence¶

Principle
