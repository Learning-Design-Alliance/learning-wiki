Support Early Language Development¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Infants, or children not yet using words (from the wording) Respond to gestures, sounds and pointing as turns; name what the child attends to; use books as things to point at and talk about rather than to read through; keep exchanges to a few turns at a time across care routines untested proposal; the associational turn-count finding (conversational turns [+M]) and the child-directed speech element. The dialogic-reading trials did not include infants
Typically developing children of about 2 to 3 (from the wording) Make daily dialogic reading the core routine (completion and recall prompts, expansion, repeated books), one-to-one or in small groups, and coach the parents or staff who do it Dialogic reading improves expressive language [+S] (moderate effect against typical shared reading; larger for younger children)
Children of about 4 to 5 (from the wording) Shift to open-ended and "your own life" prompts and retelling by the child; add short, explicit teaching of a few selected words from each book; do not expect the same gain from reading alone Dialogic reading improves language outcomes [~S] (effect substantially smaller at 4–5); Explicit Vocabulary Instruction
Children with language delays or disabilities (from the wording) Use one-to-one or very small groups, more sessions, simpler prompts, and work with a speech-language professional; do not rely on a parent-led reading routine alone, and judge progress on the child's own talk (utterances, variety of words) Potentially positive effects for children with disabilities [+M] (two trials, 52 children with language delays); Dale et al. 1996 [+M] (gains on 2 of 6 measures); parent-led dialogic reading showed no significant differences [-M] (19 children); the hub's smaller effect for children at risk [~S]
Children who are deaf or hard of hearing (from the wording) Give early, full access to a language the child can perceive (including a sign language), with the same responsive turn-taking in that language; involve specialists early untested here; from the earlier page (early sign-language exposure is time-sensitive). No claim in the wiki was read for it
classroom with one teacher for 15–20 children (from the wording) Rotate small groups of 4–5 for 10-minute dialogic sessions while others play; train assistants or volunteers to run some groups; check once a week that sessions happen and children talk on most pages the teacher-delivered replication failure in the hub [~S]; frequency of implementation moderates effects [~M]; group sizes are untested proposals

Universal Early Literacy Screening¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Kindergarten (child, age 5–6) Screen letter naming and phonemic awareness; skip nonsense-word fluency if the other subtests are given; expect lower accuracy than in later grades Kindergarten DIBELS subtests predict first-grade instructional reading, with Letter Naming Fluency the strongest predictor [+M]; Classification accuracy of the screening cut scores is lower for Grades K-2 than Grades 3-8, attributed to a 12-36 month lapse between MAP Growth and criterion measures [~M]
A school serving many children from low-income families, or with high teacher turnover and low attendance (from the wording) Expect more children flagged; budget added instruction and staff for that number before screening; read the rate as a measure of the school's resources and the children's prior opportunities, not of the children School characteristics—percentage of low income students, teacher retention, and attendance—are associated with students' likelihood of being identified as at significant risk in early literacy screening [~W]
Many children sit just above or below the cut score (from the wording) Treat a band around the cut as borderline: a diagnostic look and a recheck rather than an automatic placement either way Exploratory analysis found no observed differences in later MCAS performance between students just below and just above screening risk thresholds [~W]

Autonomy¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Praise is part of the routine (children, after success) (from the wording) Praise the effort or strategy used, not the learner's intelligence Praise for intelligence undermines motivation after failure [~M]; Intelligence praise against effort praise [~M]
Young children (child, about five) in early literacy, and the brief asks for child-chosen activities Distinguish choosing which activity from thinking for oneself within it. Keep teacher-led activities in which children make their own connections (an open shared reading of a big book, a text the children compose with the teacher as scribe), and keep explicit teaching of the spelling system; do not rely on free choice of activity to reach it Autonomy at one level may inhibit another [~W]; Orthographic mastery needs instruction [~W]; Big-book reading and cognitive autonomy [+W]; Co-construction with the teacher as scribe [+W]; all from one report, argument and teacher report, no comparison

Educational Readiness¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Children are entering kindergarten or first grade (child, early years) Observe in play and short one-to-one tasks rather than a written test; plan play and movement into the day as teacher-side readiness; meet families before the start Difficulty adjusting to kindergarten and first grade can put young children at academic risk [+W] (a fact sheet's assertion, no data); family meetings: transition programme interviews [+W]; play and movement: untested, from the earlier page
A family or school is considering holding a child back a year, or having a child repeat kindergarten (from the wording) Recommend on-time entry with the entry check and bridging above; record the specific concern and plan an adjustment for it Redshirting kindergartners likely carries costs that outweigh its academic benefits [+W]; At the end of first grade, children whose kindergarten entry was delayed were behind their classmates who began kindergarten on time in math [+M], though they were slightly ahead in reading [~M]; US data, associational except one regression discontinuity
Readiness records concern young children, families or disabilities (sensitive) (from the wording) Share results with the family and the teachers who act on them, not more widely; record observations, not labels; let families see and correct what is recorded Untested proposal
The brief reports a mismatch learners actually showed (children unable to sit through long seated lessons; students who could not do the algebra in week 1) (from the wording) Use it as the first item on the prerequisite list and as the contrast in planning: what the setting assumed against what the learners did Untested proposal

Fostering Creative Thinking¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Short rounds (3–5 minutes), oral or drawn ideas collected by the teacher, play-like prompts, no grades on creative work, process and portfolio rather than product Concrete reward increases children's mean ideational fluency [~M] (fifth graders: reward per idea raised idea counts in the session, so a reward-free design trades some output for interest); process focus for young children is from the earlier maker-assessment page, untested
Generative AI is used with child learners As above, and treat any benefit as unestablished: keep the AI step short and after the child's own ideas, and check what the child produces without it Primary studies report possible benefits of GenAI for children's divergent thinking, narrative creativity and creative self-efficacy, without confirmed efficacy [~W] (a scoping review of 22 small, heterogeneous studies; none in the wiki separates assisted from unassisted performance in children)

Modality Principle¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Middle-school children (k-12) learning a science unit from animation (from the wording) Narrate; expect a smaller effect than in the adult lab studies. Adding the written words under the narration is not needed, but did no measurable harm in the same study The modality effect held for middle-school students learning electricity, d = .35 [+M] (quasi-experiment); adding written text to narration made no significant difference [~M]
Younger children, still learning to read (from the wording) Speech is the default for any accompanying explanation; untested as a modality comparison at this age. In an e-book whose aim is exposure to print, pair the narration with highlighting or a tracking pointer on the words being read, which draws children's eyes to the text Modality comparison untested; narration with finger-tracking animation directed bilingual preschoolers' looking to the print being read [~M] (attention measured, not learning; the effect stopped when the animation ended)

Positive Self-talk¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The goal is academic writing or problem solving, child or adolescent Teach self-instructions as part of the strategy (its steps become the cues) with goal setting and self-monitoring, over several sessions Strategy Instruction Improves Writing Quality [+M] (grades 4–10; self-instruction bundled with three other components)

Text-to-Speech¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child early readers building fluency Use speech or read-along for access to stories and content above their reading level, not as the main fluency practice; keep the child reading the words aloud themselves in separate practice, and check comprehension after read-along rather than assuming it Repeated listening improves oral reading fluency in second-grade students, with Listening Only outgaining Reading While Listening and Reading Only [~M] (one seven-week study of 75 second graders, reported second-hand in a review; reading while listening had the smallest fluency gain and an average comprehension loss; no statistical test printed)
Young children with an e-book or app, possibly in two languages (from the wording) Choose narration with a highlight that tracks the words, in the language whose print the child should look at; ask two or three story questions after, since attention to print did not reliably become comprehension Audio narration with finger-tracking animation directs bilingual preschoolers' attention to the target-language print in dual-language e-books, including the nondominant language [+M]; Multimedia features improved story comprehension in dual-language e-books (marginal trend) but not in single-language e-books [~M] (one eye-tracking study, bilingual preschoolers)

AI Tutoring¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Children or young adolescents (from the wording) Prefer a structured tutor or teachable agent with teacher oversight to open chat; test the tutor's safety over long multi-turn conversations and against requests to cheat, not only single prompts, and do not rely on an off-the-shelf safety filter Teachable agent improved fourth-graders' science reasoning [+M]; warning about AI mistakes raised seventh-graders' hint requests [+M]; multi-turn interactions raised attack success on conventional risks three to five times [-M] and safety classifiers passed exam-answer and cheating requests as safe [-M] (model evaluations, no learners)

Principle

Cognitive Flexibility¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Age child (under 12) Use two cases at a time, side by side, with the one dimension that differs named by the teacher; no free navigation among cases. untested proposal; no claim here tests case comparison with children
Age child, early years (about 2–5), a number concept reachable by several methods (estimating, counting, lining up) Embed the comparison in a purposeful choice ("which pile do you want?"); model one method and ask what its result means for the choice, then show that a second method gives the same answer; expect the child to propose the cross-check unprompted only later, across months. Digital Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (primary age) Use short sessions (10 minutes or less), supervised by an adult in the room; give feedback that needs little reading; avoid open typed answers. A simulation can stand in for physical materials if the same instruction goes with it session length and supervision are untested proposals; the substitution rests on Physical and virtual instructional materials are equally effective for teaching fourth- and fifth-graders to design unconfounded experiments when instructional method is controlled [+M] (one science skill; no difference found, equivalence not tested). Online-versus-classroom comparisons for school-age learners are almost absent: A systematic search found almost no rigorous studies contrasting online and face-to-face instruction for K–12 students: none with computable effect sizes through 2006, and only five meeting meta-analysis criteria through July 2008. [~M]

Emergent Curriculum¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are child (preschool to early primary) Use the full sequence: teacher-chosen entry points, daily observation notes, webbing with the children, threads of one to four weeks, and an adult in the activity guiding it toward the webbed ideas (asking, modelling, supplying tools) rather than leaving it as free play. Guided play [~M]: early-childhood meta-analysis, guided play ahead of direct instruction on early maths, shape knowledge and task switching and of free play on spatial vocabulary, no reliable difference on most outcomes; guided play, not emergent curriculum

Principle

Principle

Epistemic Cognition¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
child learners (primary school) Frame questions as "how does it happen?" and have learners compare a mechanistic and a non-mechanistic explanation of a familiar phenomenon, giving a reason; keep content to what they already know, since they reject explanations that conflict with their knowledge. Do not expect web-source evaluation routines to transfer down. Elementary students prefer mechanistic explanations [+W]: grades 2–6, observed in interviews, no instruction tested

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
