AI Tutoring¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The goal is an ill-structured judgement (writing, design, ethics, clinical reasoning) (from the wording) Use the tutor for questioning and critique of the learner's draft, not for producing it; unlock the AI only after the learner's own attempt; an AI-improved version of the learner's own practice draft, after the attempt, can teach; assess independent work Requiring engagement before the AI unlocked cut verbatim AI text in final essays (27% against 47%, d = −0.47) [+M]; AI-assisted cover-letter practice improved unaided writing a day later [+M] (after a lesson on the principles); Bounded support gave the best independent writing [~M]; originality gains vanished unaided [~M]; an LLM-assisted essay group did worse than a no-tool group across four sessions [-M] (small preprint, 18 completed the fourth session); scaffolded GenAI augmented rather than replaced judgement in a design-thinking course [+M] (student reflections)
Health professions (clinical interviewing, diagnosis) (from the wording) Use multi-agent simulated patients with scaffolding for interview practice, with faculty review of errors; test long dialogues, where role-play failures cluster Multi-agent simulated-patient training raised OSCE-aligned scores (71.8% vs 55.6%) [+M]; role-play failures rise after turn 6 [-M]

Massive Open Online Course (MOOC)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The target is an embodied or performed skill (music, laboratory, clinical, a language spoken aloud) (from the wording) Add recorded performance submissions judged against a rubric by calibrated peers, and periodic live sessions with an expert for a sample of learners; do not rest the skill on video and quizzes. Embodied music learning resists short videos and self-graded quizzes [-W] (a review's argument); rubric-guided peer rating [~M], tested on writing, carried to performance
Adults in work settings with predictable obstacles (shifts, family) (from the wording) Make the if-then plan name the obstacle and a fixed weekly slot; show it back at the midpoint; allow a two-week extension without penalty. Implementation intentions [+S]: worked when the obstacle was predictable and surmountable, not for lack of time; extension is untested
Learners will share private or sensitive material (their workplace, their health, their students) (from the wording) Make reflections private by default; let the learner choose whether a piece goes to peer review; allow a display name in the forum; tell learners how many people can see a post. Simulations & Immersive Virtual Environments¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in work settings (vocational training, safety, customer handling) (from the wording) Build scenarios from the learners' own workplace incidents, including the rare and risky ones, not only volume practice of routine tasks; make errors produce their real consequences in the simulation; refer settings and moves to the real machine during the run; end with a supervised task at work Intelligent Extended Reality interventions in VET show consistent positive effects on procedural competence, practical skills, and learner motivation [+M] (review, no pooled effect); adult learners interviewed said they want simulations that closely resemble actual job scenarios (Adult learners expect AI to support transformative and experiential learning through assumption-challenging simulations, role play, and authentic virtual contexts [+W], expectations, not outcomes); one ethnography of forestry-machine simulator training found instruction centred on volume training of routine tasks rather than the risky situations that justify a simulator (Simulator-based VET instruction in this study centres on volume training of routine tasks rather than rehearsing specific high-stakes, risky situations. [~W]), with the simulator showing errors' consequences (Simulator feedback responds authentically to student error [+W]) and teachers using the real machine as the benchmark (VET teachers use the real machine as the benchmark of legitimacy for simulator instruction [+W]); observations of practice, no outcomes; workplace incidents untested
One facilitator for many learners, or scarce facilitator time (volunteers, a busy clinical shift) (from the wording) Build the run's goal and prompts into the simulation; run in pairs or teams of three to four, one observer per team with a checklist; facilitator joins only the debrief, and debriefs the commonest error with the whole group untested proposal; contingent-support evidence is mostly one-to-one (Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. Structured Peer Review¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Quality turns on correctness (a calculation, code, a clinical or technical claim) (from the wording) Peers comment on clarity, structure and whether claims are supported; the teacher or an expert checks correctness unless there is an objective check reviewers can run (does the code pass the tests, does the answer match a worked one), in which case give them that check and let them judge against it. Peer accuracy [~M]: secondary-school writing only; peer ratings of programming assignments [+W] correlated with instructors' and were more accurate on objective than subjective judgements (one tool evaluation, read second-hand); students new to statistics [~M] valued peers' alternative explanations but did not trust peer feedback for final application, because their peers were beginners too (one qualitative case study); the split of roles is untested
Adults in a work setting (design teams, clinical or teaching placements) (from the wording) Review the real work product against the workplace's own standards; the author states what decision the draft must support; a senior colleague reviews alongside peers and requires a revise-and-resubmit where evidence is missing. Probing feedback with revise-and-resubmit [+W]: a case study of teacher candidates in clinical placements, instructor feedback not peer; progress was not linear

Summative Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult professionals, or in adult-workplace training (from the wording) Make the end-point a task from their work, observed or produced on the job, judged against a stated competency threshold; ask what they already do and test only what is new untested proposal; threshold decisions are held by Competency-Based Assessment
Goal is a complex-skill (a design, a case, a clinical or practical performance) (from the wording) Include a whole-task performance, scored with a rubric, plus short component items so a failure can be traced to a part A training-design argument, not tested by any study recorded here, holds that practising whole complex tasks improves transfer of complex skills [~W] (an argument about training, carried here to assessment)
The capability is one integrated performance (a clinical scenario, a full repair, a live service) and the brief asks for an authentic assessment (from the wording) Keep a single whole-task scenario as the judgment; do not split it into paper items. Put recall and recognition checks into the low-stakes practice before it; inside the scenario, prompt the learner to say decisions aloud from the start and tell them the assessor will not confirm answers, so "is that what you wanted?" gets no reply and is not marked down the whole-task claim [~W] is a design argument; the rest is untested

Technology should augment, not supplant, learning processes in student-centered environments¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Goal is a clinical or practical procedure (skill-performance) in workplace-clinical training Teach the procedure hands-on first; add VR for what cannot be seen (radiation, hazards); assess on the real procedure VR-based training with radiation field visualization received significantly higher ratings for radiation understanding and visualization than conventional training (r=0.878) [+M]; Conventional training received significantly higher ratings for perceived realism and immersion than VR-based training (r=0.774) [~M] (20 students with prior procedural training, self-ratings)
Adults in work settings or postgraduate researchers who will use the tool in their job (from the wording) Teach the tool as part of the work, with explicit human checks at each stage; agree with the learners which parts the tool may do (language polishing, searching, summarising) and which stay theirs (the idea, the argument, the judgement), starting from the line they already draw; still keep one unaided task for the core judgement Nearly all postgraduate research students in the study used GenAI across multiple research stages, positioning it as a supportive assistant with human oversight retained [+W] (focus groups, 27 of 28 students; reported use, not outcomes); in the same study, students rejected AI generation of research ideas, original arguments or whole papers while accepting surface-level support [+W] and found AI ideation acceptable only while they kept decision-making authority [+W] (one qualitative study, stated norms); the unaided task is an untested proposal

Traditional Lecture-Based Instruction¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The objective is a practical or clinical skill, and the brief wants the whole task kept whole (from the wording) Lecture only the knowledge the task needs, in one short segment; spend the rest on practicing the whole task with feedback, and assess the performance, not a written test. Lecture and 4C/ID matched on knowledge [~M]; 4C/ID scored higher on OSCEs, not significantly [~M]: one quasi-experiment with 21 emergency-care professionals; too small to settle either outcome
Adults in work settings with a short training slot (from the wording) Same as the skill row: a 10–15 minute explanation, then practice on the job task; do not expect the lecture to change performance by itself. Lecture and 4C/ID matched on knowledge [~M], carried from emergency-care consent training to other work settings; untested beyond it

Validity, Reliability, and Bias in Classroom Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The goal is a complex-skill or extended performance (a lab, an essay, a presentation, a clinical task) (from the wording) Use a performance task scored by a rubric with level descriptors and anchors; calibrate markers before marking and double-mark a sample; report the score with the number of tasks it rests on Raters differed in severity [~M] (open-ended items, three raters); rubric guidance improved peer raters' placement of writing on complex criteria [+M] (MOOC and graduate peers, carried to teachers); one class's rubric scores varied by a mean of 1.2 of 9 points across six scorers (engineering rubric low scoring reliability [~W])
Adults in work settings, or the capability is a job task (from the wording) Assess the task as it is done at work, with an observer checklist; use two observers on a sample; check validity by comparing the assessment's ratings with independent observation of practice An interview rating of teachers' use of an innovation correlated .65 with readers' ratings of written ethnographic protocols, which its authors read as support for its validity (LoU interview validated against observation [+W]; teachers, research instrument; the way validity was checked is carried, not a result about learners)

Fostering Communities of Learning (FCL)¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in a work setting (a professional learning cohort, workplace-clinical) Make the theme a recurring problem of practice; each member researches a case or source and teaches it back; the shared task is a protocol or guide the team will use; observe practice afterwards, not only self-report. Give the facilitator time to plan what each meeting asks members to reflect on, and treat disagreement about a case as material for the next round rather than a reason to close the meeting. Communities of practice principle, whose evidence is descriptive; mathematics PLC review [+M] (a review of descriptive studies, no comparison). One phenomenological study of three PLC leaders (Prytula 2012) reports that the PLC nurtured their metacognition [+W], that each leader's own metacognition shaped the work she led [+W], and one leader's view that disagreement catalysed learning [~W] (another adjourned to avoid conflict); a survey of small Chicago high schools found higher reported trust and collaboration [+W] (no effect size). None measures learning or practice against a comparison
