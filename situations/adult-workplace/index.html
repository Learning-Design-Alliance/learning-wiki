Designing for adult-workplace¶

Population · adults outside formal education · All situations

28 rows from 28 pages say what to change when the brief's population is adult-workplace; 11 of them cite a claim. Pages whose rows cite claims come first. Read the basis before relying on a row: many carry a claim to learners or settings it did not test, and say so.

Blended Learning¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult working professionals (adult-workplace) Put content and repeated practice online where they can fit it around work; use in-person time for practice with equipment, cases or colleagues and for feedback on performance. Blended learning improves outcomes [~M], pooled mostly across higher education and professional training, with extra time and resources confounded

Cognitive Activation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adults or professionals (adult, adult-workplace) Draw the two cases for comparison from the learners' own work, differing on the one feature that matters, and ask what they share before naming the principle. Comparing contrasting cases improves learning [+M] (the transfer experiment was with undergraduates learning negotiation; untested in workplace training)

Discussing Race¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
adult-workplace or professional training (teachers, social workers, counsellors, clinicians, managers) Use cases from the learners' own practice (a referral decision, a discipline record, an assessment); include practice at raising race with a client, student or patient through role-play with a debrief; build trust in the team before asking about the team's own data Trust: Continuous improvement for equity requires trust and courageous conversations; without trusting relationships, the improvement process will not help [+W] (one qualitative report of education leaders). Case choice: carried from Some SSWs hold deficit-based racial attitudes, conflating structural constraints with immigrant cultural values and misreading trauma responses [~W] (school social workers' open responses; shows what practitioners' cases can surface, does not test a training)

Elaborative Reasoning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult-workplace Have them connect each statement to a case from their own work ("where have you seen this, and why did it happen?") rather than to textbook examples Readers given a perspective or schema recall more text information than readers instructed to carefully read the story [+W] (one story-recall experiment reported second-hand in an essay; not tested with workplace learners); the work-case prompt is an untested proposal

Epistemic Games¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
adult-workplace learners in workplace-clinical training Use cases drawn from the learners' own workplace, a practitioner as mentor, and an outside-game task that matches a real work task; record transfer to work separately from in-game performance. Simulation training in the health professions [+M] (against no training); practice-near content [+W] (nurses' perceptions)

Experience with Languaging Activities (ELAs) guided by a sequence of experience-based questions¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in work settings (adult-workplace, workplace-clinical) Use a recent work incident or case as the experience; make step 4 a statement of what they would do differently and why; make step 5 a question to take to a colleague or the next shift. Specific reflection prompts [+S] and guided reflection [+M], carried from undergraduates and graduate students to workplace learners

Goal Setting & Monitoring¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
adult or adult-workplace learners Elicit the learner's own goal first and tie the task goal to it; add an if-then plan for the named obstacle (work, family time) Implementation intentions [+M]; mostly university students and online-course adults

Guide change efforts with systemic understanding, focusing interventions on one framework component at a time as needed¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adopters are adult professionals in adult-workplace settings (staff, clinicians, trainers) Map roles, workload, incentives and supervision as the subsystems. Use the adopters' own work tasks as the trial material. Put follow-up contact into the working week Follow-up contact related most strongly to adoption success [+M] (school dissemination; carried to workplaces untested)

Link program elements to workforce entry points and employer-recognized credentials so learners see an immediate economic payoff¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adults already in work (adult-workplace), seeking promotion Use tasks from their own job as the practice; choose credentials that count for promotion with their employer; schedule around shifts Most rural learner participants reported micro-credentials yielded increased opportunity for skill development, job promotion, salary increase [+W] (learner self-reports, four rural initiatives); the design moves are untested

Shared power: previously excluded students, families, and communities co-create and shape educational systems, policies, and practices¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
The excluded group is staff or instructors (adult-workplace), e.g. in a technology or evaluation design Frame them as part of the solution: design so the tool or policy supports their judgment, and give them a co-design role from the first session. Framing instructors as part of the solution shifted a design toward supporting instructor judgment [+W] (one project's dialogue)

Social Interdependence¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult-workplace teams Build belonging and shared goals (harmony, a sense of one team) rather than formal role dependence alone; measure what each member can do Social interdependence, not role interdependence, correlated with performance [~W] (40 brokerage offices, correlational; "social interdependence" there means team belonging, not goal structure)

Cognitive Styles¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult in a work setting (adult-workplace) Ask how they already tackle this kind of task at work; build the structure or check into their own job aid or form rather than a classroom frame, and let them keep it untested proposal

Compensate for age-related sensory impairments by combining audio input with visual presentation¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
adult-workplace training in a noisy setting (a shop floor, a ward) Explain away from the noise, then demonstrate on the floor; give a laminated written job aid with the key steps in the learner's own terms; agree visual signals for the noisy part untested proposal

Culturally Responsive Classroom Norms¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult, in adult-workplace or professional training Ask directly about expectations of hierarchy, questioning a trainer, and group versus individual credit; never treat silence as lack of knowledge; give written or private ways to raise a question untested proposal

Design tools so productive usage is guaranteed by affordances and constraints rather than instructions¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult in a work setting (adult-workplace), using a tool on the job Bound the assistant to checking and hinting during training tasks, and judge readiness on an unaided task from their own work; tell them why the tool is bounded untested proposal; the AI evidence is from students, not workers

Employ flexible error treatment strategies matched to objectives, competence, affect, and correction effectiveness¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult professionals using the language at work (adult-workplace) Ask which errors matter in their work (a misunderstanding with a client, a written report); treat those first, privately or in writing; leave the rest unless asked untested proposal; adults were the least represented group in the classroom meta-analysis (durable effect [~S])

Explicit Instruction: Math Strategies¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult in work settings (apprentices, trainees, adult-workplace), or in adult basic education Keep the brief's own sequence, setting and materials (the kitchen, the recipe card, the shift); teach the strategy on the work's own quantities at the point the work needs it; ask which methods they already use and compare one of theirs with the taught method; check with a work task (scale tomorrow's batch), never a classroom test untested proposal; the earlier page's source is an adult-education practice guide, not a claim

Feedback Loops¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in work settings (adult-workplace, workplace-clinical) Loop on real work outputs (a case note, a call, a procedure); a supervisor or peer reads one output per cycle against a stated criterion, and the next output is the re-observation untested; in one teacher-education case, probing feedback with required resubmission helped one candidate and not another (probing feedback [+W])

Holistic Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adults in a work setting (adult-workplace) Take the whole from their own work: the complete workflow or a real case they handle; elicit what they already do and start at the part where their account breaks down untested proposal; whole-task practice and transfer [+W] is an argument, not a study

Learning-for-Use (LfU) Model¶

Pattern

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
adult-workplace learners, the task is their own work problem Use a real case from their work as the motivating task and a real decision as the refine task; run the construct phase as short guided analyses with a facilitator synthesis. Untested in workplaces; problem-first effects rose with grade level [~M], g = 1.03 for postgraduates and professionals (education settings, not workplace training)

Match the form and content of staff development interventions to clients' assessed Stages of Concern¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in a non-school workplace adopting a new procedure or system (adult-workplace, e.g. clinical or office staff) Use the same diagnosis and stage-matched support, with the questions worded for their work; record whether the stages read the same way untested; every claim here is from teachers, carried by analogy

Mindfulness Activities¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adults in a work setting (adult-workplace), or adult learners arriving tired or fragmented One minute, framed as a focus reset rather than wellness or meditation; no inward-feeling language; open with the agenda straight after untested proposal; from the earlier page (adult learners balancing competing demands)

Observation/Shadowing¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
adult learners in a work setting (workplace-clinical, adult-workplace): new staff shadowing a colleague Shadow on a real shift in blocks of 1–3 hours, with a pocket focus card of two to four items; a 5-minute check-in before and a 10–15-minute conversation after; follow within the week with the learner doing the same task under supervision untested proposal; the debrief is carried from Contingent scaffolding improves learning more than fixed or absent support. [+M] (one-to-one tutoring of children)

Professional development should guide teachers on integrating digital content and tools, not just offer technical know-how¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are faculty in postsecondary teaching or trainers in adult-workplace settings Keep the order (goal, one function, examples in their discipline, plan one class session); replace school release time with a scheduled course-design hour; use a colleague in the same discipline as the follow-up untested proposal; the claims here are about school teachers

Strengthen ties between teachers and parents rather than pressing for remote sanctions behind teaching¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Adult learners (adult, adult-workplace), with no parents in the picture Read "parents" as the community the learner answers to (an employer, a sponsoring agency, family who shape study time); the move is the same: explain the course to them before relying on attendance rules or sanctions untested; carried by analogy from the school setting

Summative Assessment¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult professionals, or in adult-workplace training Make the end-point a task from their work, observed or produced on the job, judged against a stated competency threshold; ask what they already do and test only what is new untested proposal; threshold decisions are held by Competency-Based Assessment

Understanding must be cultivated through exploration, not described or told¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult in a work setting (adult-workplace) Start from a case from their own work as the problem; use their experience as material the analysis is built on, not a warm-up; name the principle with their cases Untested for adults at work; carried from a high-school English unit (Positioning personal experience as an epistemic resource supports expansion from inward to outward critical orientations [+W])

User-Centered Design for Learning¶

Principle

If the brief says… Then change… Basis
Learners are adult or adult-workplace users with little time Test in their workflow, on their work device, in 20–30 minute sessions; put the tasks they do most on the first screen untested proposal
