# Linking palpatory living anatomy to ultrasound windows before deep-structure scanning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course sequences training so that students first dedicate fifty hours to palpatory living anatomy (twenty tutored, thirty self-study), then learn knobology, then scan superficial structures. During probe-in-the-hand sessions, "each student practiced controlling the correspondence between palpatory detection and target structures" such as rotator cuff tendons, the ulnar nerve at the elbow and the median nerve at the wrist.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated probe-in-the-hand sessions with linear multi-frequency probe machines and tutor supervision
#### Constraints
- The authors state the intention was not to compete with clinical colleagues in teaching semeiotics, and that studies on deep structures were more challenging

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- surface anatomy palpation
- ultrasound recognition of superficial and neurovascular structures
