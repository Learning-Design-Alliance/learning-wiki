# Address communication, faculty training, curriculum integration, resource allocation, and hierarchical attitudes when implementing Sim-IPE in resource-limited settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that implementing simulation-enhanced interprofessional education requires acting on the barrier set their study identified. They state: "To overcome these challenges and fully leverage the benefits of Sim-IPE, it's important to address issues related to communication, faculty training, curriculum integration, resource allocation, and changing hierarchical attitudes." Concrete actions named in the article include harmonizing timetables so both professions share simulation times, co-designing scenarios with nursing and medical input, pairing debrief facilitators across professions, and defining clear marking guidelines.
