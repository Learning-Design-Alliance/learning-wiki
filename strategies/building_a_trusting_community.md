# Building a Trusting Community

## Description
The key step is building a trusting community by giving students time to reflect and write about elements of their lives and to share these reflections. Highlighting student voices is essential in making the connections that lay the ground for fruitful discussions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a commitment to creating inclusive and equitable learning environments. Access to multimedia tools, diverse stories, and expert-developed discussion guides. A willingness to address and mitigate personal biases.
#### Constraints
- Requires careful planning and facilitation to ensure respectful and productive conversations. May require additional training and support for educators and facilitators. Potential for discomfort or resistance from individuals with differing perspectives.

## Examples
- A high school math teacher has students write in journals so he can connect with them and how they're feeling about his class. A literature teacher asks a different student each day to bring in lyrics to a favorite song. Each morning, she plays the selected song and devotes 10 minutes to discussing its lyrics—and ties what students observe about the characters or mood of that song into a class text. Another way to set a trusting atmosphere is to bring people of color who are experts in their field (engineers, poets, or biologists) to speak to the class.
