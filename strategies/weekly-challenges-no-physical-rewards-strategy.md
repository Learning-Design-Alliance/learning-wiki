# Use weekly instructor-set challenges with no physical rewards to sustain intrinsic motivation on practice platforms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The lecturers set challenges on Codecademy such as highest score in 1 and 4 weeks and fastest to reach 50, 100, and 200 points, each with its own leaderboard on Blackboard, but deliberately gave no physical rewards. The stated rationale was "to allow faculty staff to evaluate whether the aim of winning a challenge was in itself enough as intrinsic motivation for students to complete their tasks". The article reports the challenges motivated the majority of students to perform weekly and engage with programming throughout the semester.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Weekly updating of leaderboards using the platform's Pupil Tracker data
- A platform exposing points, badges, and peer-comparable progress
#### Constraints
- Students later expressed concerns that some lesson contents were not a good fit to the syllabus and had ambiguous explanations and vague error messages

### Target Learners
- higher education students completing programming exercises

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained weekly engagement with programming practice
- completion of practical coding exercises
