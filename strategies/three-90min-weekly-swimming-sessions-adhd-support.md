# Advise three 90-minute weekly low-aerobic-intensity swimming sessions for students whose ADHD is negatively impacting learning and class engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 11 claims (11 for) · 4 studies (3 review, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's authors translate their trial findings into a concrete recommendation for schools: education systems and schools can promote frequent swimming sessions for students with ADHD within their communities. They state that "three 90-min sessions a week of low aerobic intensity swimming can be advised as a strategy to support students whose ADHD condition is negatively impacting on learning and engagement in classes." The trial's program used 12 consecutive weeks of three weekly sessions in a 20 m pool, structured as warm-up, aquatic exercise games, and cool-down.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sessions led by professional coaches with experience in water exercise, as in the trial's program
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a single trial with a small sample (n = 40); the authors note the sample size was small and recruitment needs improvement

### Target Learners
- Primary school children aged 9–12 with ADHD

### Target Learning Goals
- Support learning and class engagement for students whose ADHD condition is negatively impacting them; improve cognitive function, behavior, and academic performance
