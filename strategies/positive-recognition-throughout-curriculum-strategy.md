# Foster positive recognition of physics students throughout the curriculum, especially during year 1 and year 4 transition periods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors implement measures to foster positive, encouraging interactions with all students from introductory to graduate levels, because perceived recognition is the top predictor of physics identity in every year. It suggests encouraging majors "that they can excel in physics by working hard and providing appropriate support" especially during year 1 and year 4 transitions, and pairing recognition efforts with interventions targeting self-efficacy and belonging.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors and TAs must make a concerted effort to recognize students as capable of excelling in physics
#### Constraints
- The study is exploratory and the authors state more research is needed to confirm the differences found

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics majors
- first-year non-majors in physics courses
- first-year physics Ph.D. students

### Target Learning Goals
- physics identity
- self-efficacy
- sense of belonging
