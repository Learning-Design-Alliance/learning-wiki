# Design feedback conditions in which students feel understood, respected, and invited to actively process feedback, supporting emotion regulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers and instructors consciously design feedback conditions supporting both cognitive and emotional processing: feedback should focus on the task and process, emphasize what is going well, provide suggestions for improvement, and avoid an aggressive, disrespectful, or fatalistic tone. It stresses dialogue so students "feel understood and respected," teacher empathy, giving feedback a functional role, and allowing students space to make sense of it, creating "shared responsibility" between teachers and students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers and instructors able to apply established feedback guidance and engage in dialogue with students
#### Constraints
- It is not the instructor's role to act directly on students' emotions; feedback design is an indirect means of supporting them
- Asking teachers and nursing professionals to ensure application of emotion regulation strategies might be difficult

### Target Learners
- nursing students during internships
- higher education students receiving feedback

### Target Learning Goals
- constructive cognitive and emotional processing of feedback
- sustained motivation and learning-oriented behavior

### Affordances
- Integrated Model Internship Feedback Processing Nursing
