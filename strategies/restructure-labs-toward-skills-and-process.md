# Shift introductory lab goals and pedagogy toward scientific practices, emphasizing process quality over product and adding time to revise and troubleshoot

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 against, 1 unmarked) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Given the null content-reinforcement benefit and labs' high cost, the article recommends institutions consider alternative goals for which labs are uniquely effective, such as evaluating data and models, dealing with uncertainty, and designing experiments. It suggests small design changes: "even small elements of open-endedness in activities can improve student attitudes towards experimental physics", spreading a single experiment across multiple weeks to enable revision and troubleshooting, and "Shifting the emphasis of the lab activities towards the quality of students’ process rather than the product they obtain would be key to facilitating that development".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires reframing explicit lab learning goals away from content reinforcement and toward data, uncertainty, and experimental-design skills, with grading that values process
#### Constraints
- The article states it is unclear whether entirely open-ended project-based courses would achieve the desired goals at typical introductory enrollments, noting history suggests this is not sustainable

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating data and models
- dealing with uncertainty and variability in data
- designing experiments
- experimental physics attitudes
