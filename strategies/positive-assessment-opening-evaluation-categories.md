# Open each class with positive assessment of world religions while using explicit evaluation categories for religious movements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A didactic component the teacher used mandatorily: each class started with a positive assessment for world religions, for example stating that "Islam is one of the greatest and most tolerant of religions, and the Quran is also one the world's oldest holy books." The article reports this assurance made Muslim students trust their teacher and the assignments, and helped students of other faiths foster appreciation for Islam. It was combined with explicit evaluation categories contrasting, e.g., totalitarian sects and Islamic fundamentalism with traditional Islam, and with comparative study of the largest schools of thought without stressing any single version of Islam.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must not be deeply religious so as not to give preference to any religion
#### Constraints
- The article contrasts this with German practice of waiving evaluation categories, which it says left the learning process unfinished and prone to confuse students

### Target Learners
- Mixed Muslim and Orthodox Christian student groups in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Mutual appreciation of other faiths' religions
- Trust in the teacher and course assignments
- Distrust of pseudo-missionary thinking
