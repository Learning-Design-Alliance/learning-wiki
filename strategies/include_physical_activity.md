# Include Physical Activity

## Description
Brief breaks for active play can help children who struggle with attention. This may include bouncing on an exercise ball, breaking up learning into chunks with outdoor play times, or providing a quick stretching or jumping jacks break in the classroom. Starting with 15 minutes of active play before a challenging task can also help a child stay more engaged.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Space for physical activity, appropriate activities (e.g., exercise ball, stretching routines, outdoor play), and time allocated for breaks.
#### Constraints
- May be disruptive if not managed effectively. Some learners may be self-conscious or unable to participate in certain activities.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the learner's ability to stay on task after physical activity breaks. Track the frequency and duration of attention spans. Monitor the reduction of restless behavior.

## Impact
- Physical activity breaks can significantly improve attention and engagement, particularly for learners who struggle with sustained focus. They provide a necessary outlet for energy and reduce restlessness, thereby enhancing overall learning.
