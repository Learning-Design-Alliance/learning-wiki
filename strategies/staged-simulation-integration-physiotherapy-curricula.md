# Integrate simulation learning experiences in stages throughout the physiotherapy curriculum rather than as one-off exposures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incremental, coordinated use of simulation across the curriculum, citing Mori and colleagues' recommendation of "a staged application of simulation learning experiences throughout the physiotherapy curricula" and the challenge point framework's suggestion to use random simulation practice for learning and retention of difficult skills. This aligns with the WHO patient safety curriculum framework's call for an integrated approach making patient safety issues visible across the whole program. Students themselves requested more frequent exposures with different client scenarios.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordination of simulation with other learning and teaching approaches across the program
#### Constraints
- The article notes further exploration is needed on how best to stage development of safety skills and develop valid assessment criteria for patient safety

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physiotherapy students in early and later program stages

### Target Learning Goals
- patient safety skills during mobilisation and manual handling
- readiness for clinical placement
