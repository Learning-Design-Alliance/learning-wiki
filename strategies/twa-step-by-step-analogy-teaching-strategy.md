# Teach each topic with Glynn's step-by-step Teaching-With-Analogy (TWA) model using multiple concrete analogies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The experimental treatment applied Glynn's Teaching-With-Analogy model step by step to each topic, using six analogies drawn from the literature and developed from an analysis of science textbooks. Some analogies were demonstrated directly with tools such as train, aquarium, u-pipe and water circuits; others were drawn on the blackboard. Students were assisted to connect basic electricity concepts with the analogies through guiding questions, and afterwards the teacher re-explained similarities and differences between analog and target so students could re-organise incorrect connections.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Analogies should address the correspondence of attributes and relationships between the target concepts to make connections explicit
- The teacher must become familiar with students' difficulties in understanding scientific concepts to design meaningful materials
#### Constraints
- The study applied the strategy over a five-week period with four 50-minute periods on direct current circuits; the aim was not to test the effectiveness of any single analogical model

### Target Learners
- High school students in introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of electric current, resistance, and series and parallel circuits
