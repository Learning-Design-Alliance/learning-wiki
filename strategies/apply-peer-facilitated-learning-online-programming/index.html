Apply peer-facilitated learning in online programming courses to improve learning outcomes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teachers and course designers apply peer-facilitated learning in online programming courses, structuring students into teams with selected peer facilitators who guide a seven-phase peer coaching process covering problem selection, resource gathering, goal setting, solution development, and feedback. This strategy is grounded in the study's finding that peer-facilitated learning significantly improved all three outcome measures.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Selecting facilitators requires observing students' initial performance and interest before assigning roles

Constraints¶

  • Study conducted with first-year non-computer-science undergraduates in a VBA programming course

Target Learners¶

  • first-year undergraduate non-computer-science majors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • programming skills in VBA
  • enjoyment of learning
  • intention to learn

Claims¶

  • Online peer-facilitated learning significantly improves programming skills, enjoyment of learning, and intention to learn compared to non-peer-facilitated learning [+W]
  • The combined intervention of peer-facilitated learning and distributed pair programming does not outperform other conditions [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Learning Communities
  • Provide more opportunities for brainstorming and discourage imitation of teacher operations in distributed pair programming
