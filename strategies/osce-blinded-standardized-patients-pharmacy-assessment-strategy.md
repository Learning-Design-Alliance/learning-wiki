# Use an OSCE with blinded standardized patients and trained observers to assess clinical pharmacy competence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 3 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes using an objective structured clinical examination (OSCE) as an alternative to traditional written and oral examinations when both communication skills and application of clinical knowledge must be assessed. Ten tasks (5 theoretical, 5 practical) are administered in a circuit format, with practical tasks involving an actor playing a patient or physician. Performance in hard skills and soft skills is assessed by an additional observing person using an evaluation checklist; the actor and observer are pharmacists trained beforehand and blinded to group assignment. The article notes OSCE has become a well-established tool for assessing clinical competence since its introduction in the 1970s by Harden, but is not regularly used in German pharmacy education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pharmacists trained beforehand for their roles as standardized patient and observer to ensure standardized assessment
- Tasks pretested with preregistration pharmacists to ensure a realistic level of difficulty
- Blinding of the actor and additional observer to intervention or control group assignment
#### Constraints
- The stress of a new examination technique might have hampered students' performance, as OSCE is not used in German pharmacy education
- In further studies students should be prepared by practising the OSCE in advance

### Target Learners
- final-year pharmacy students

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing clinical knowledge, professional judgment, communication, interpersonal skills, problem-solving skills
