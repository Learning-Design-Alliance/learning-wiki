# Start accessible-material preparation months ahead: compile a complete materials list, allow early preregistration, and split conversion between commercial services and in-house work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because converting physics materials into Braille and tactile graphics is slow, the strategy is to begin before the semester: the disabilities coordinator, instructor and student determine tools early with the goal of delivering materials "to the blind student at the same time as the rest of the class", institutions allow early preregistration, and the instructor supplies a complete list of printed, graphical and web-based materials well before classes begin. Conversion is split by material type: commercial services for lengthy items such as textbooks when no trained Nemeth transcriber is available, and in-house conversion for small-scale, highly customized, or time-sensitive resources such as homework and exams.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A complete and comprehensive list of required materials provided to the disabilities coordinator as early as possible
- Access to Braille embossing printers; ideally two, one for course preparation and grading and a second for the student's independent personal use
#### Constraints
- Producing accessible textbooks and course materials using Braille and tactile graphics can take weeks or even months
- Even commonly used textbooks are often not available in Braille and may need manual transcription, especially for mathematical notation and complex diagrams

### Target Learners
- blind and visually impaired college students requiring accessible documents

### Target Learning Goals
- timely access to textbooks, handouts, worksheets, homework, and laboratory manuals
