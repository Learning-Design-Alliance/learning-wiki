# Counter skill decay with repeated training and periodic formative assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because both instructional groups showed significant competency decline at least 6 months after the course, the authors recommend scheduling repeated practice and periodic formative assessment after initial skills training. They suggest concrete curricular changes "could involve a longitudinal, modular concept to promote skills retention", particularly for complex skills that students cannot apply in daily routine.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Opportunities for repeated practice after the initial course, ideally embedded in a longitudinal curriculum
#### Constraints
- The recommendation addresses skills not part of students' daily routine; the article does not test the remedy itself

### Target Learners
- medical students learning complex psychomotor skills

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term retention of procedural and manual therapy skills
