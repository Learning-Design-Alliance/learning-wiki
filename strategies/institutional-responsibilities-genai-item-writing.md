# Institutions should actively disseminate exemplar prompts and provide secure platforms, faculty development, and quality-assurance mechanisms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The CCSD framework assigns institutions four interconnected responsibilities: fostering a culture of innovation, providing a secure and accessible GenAI tool, faculty development, and quality assurance with feedback mechanisms. The article advocates "the active dissemination of high-quality, institutionally supported prompts rather than relying on passive diffusion," embedding exemplar prompts in workshops, contextualising them to local curricula, and providing ongoing support. Institutions should also select platforms aligned with privacy standards and monitor psychometric performance to detect over-homogeneity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured engagement with educator beliefs; institution-vetted subscriptions or locally hosted open-source LLMs; targeted faculty development workshops
#### Constraints
- High subscription fees or technical requirements may widen equity gaps for under-resourced institutions; equitable access depends on broader structural mechanisms such as sector-wide negotiations or open-access educational LLMs

### Target Learners
- Faculty and academic institutions in medical and health professions education

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustainable, trustworthy adoption of GenAI in assessment design
