# Universities should provide exercise breaks, wellness programs, and physical activity campaigns for medical students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that only 54.8% of Jordanian medical-field students exercised and that lack of time and energy were the leading barriers, the authors recommend institutional action: "universities in Jordan ought to provide exercise breaks, improve wellness programs, and promote campaigns related to physical activities, such as activities that reduce stress and improve mental health." They add that improving students' knowledge about the health benefits of physical activity will be beneficial, and that college and student-related factors should be considered when designing interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional support from universities to embed exercise breaks and wellness programming in demanding medical curricula
#### Constraints
- The article's own data showed physical activity was not significantly associated with academic performance in this sample; the recommendation rests on health and well-being grounds and on prior literature

### Target Learners
- medical, dental, and PharmD students at Jordanian universities

### Target Learning Goals
- student health and well-being
- stress management
- academic performance support
