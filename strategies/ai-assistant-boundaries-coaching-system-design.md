# Design computational coaching systems to support coordination, preparation, and community-building while keeping live diagnosis and relational interaction human

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 4 studies (4 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that coaching technologies complement rather than displace coaches' situated expertise and relational labor. Systems "should aim to strengthen the coordination, community-building, and cross-title adaptation strategies that coaches have developed within evolving gaming ecosystems," and AI should be positioned for administrative and preparatory work — scheduling, FAQ answering, session summaries, student file updates — while live coaching and gameplay diagnosis remain human, since the "relational and diagnostic core of game coaching remains deeply tied to situated human expertise."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Client-facing automation must preserve the coach's personal presence and keep the coach informed
- Design must not assume coaching materials or expertise remain stable across game updates
#### Constraints
- Coaches resisted AI in live interactions where trust, relational engagement, and situated judgment are central; acceptance is bounded by negative past experiences with unnotified client-facing automation

### Target Learners
- freelance game coaches on gig platforms

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing administrative overhead
- sustaining student relationships and trust

### Affordances
- Dual Precarity Game Coaching
