# Use longitudinal follow-up assessments to evaluate knowledge retention and clinical application of TBL

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that future studies assess knowledge retention and clinical application over time rather than relying solely on immediate post-session measures. It states that future studies should "incorporate longitudinal assessments of knowledge retention and clinical application; for example, 6–12-month follow-ups with OSCEs or pre/post clinical performance." This responds to the study's limitation of measuring outcomes only immediately after the session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Follow-up assessments at 6–12 months using tools such as OSCEs or pre/post clinical performance measures
#### Constraints
- The current study measured outcomes only immediately post-session using short-term i-RAT/t-RAT scores and a self-reported satisfaction survey

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate health-sector students participating in TBL sessions

### Target Learning Goals
- Long-term retention of interdisciplinary clinical knowledge
- Transfer of learning to clinical practice
