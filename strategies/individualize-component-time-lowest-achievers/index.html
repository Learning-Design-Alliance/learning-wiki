Individualize intervention component time and intensity for students at the lowest achievement levels rather than relying on a standardized protocol¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Based on the finding that standardized Passport to Literacy benefited mid-to-higher comprehension students but not the lowest achievers, the authors recommend deviating from a standard protocol: "to individualize the amount of time in various components of the intervention to align with student needs." They suggest students in the lower posttest quantiles may have needed relatively more word study and fluency practice, more instructional time, or smaller groups.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Data on students' initial comprehension levels to identify who needs intensified or differentiated intervention

Constraints¶

  • The article states further research is needed to determine the active ingredients for these more intense interventions

Target Learners¶

  • upper elementary students with the weakest initial reading comprehension skills

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading comprehension
  • word study
  • fluency

Related Strategies¶

  • Intensify standard reading interventions by adjusting dosage, alignment, transfer scaffolding, behavior supports, and data-based individualization
