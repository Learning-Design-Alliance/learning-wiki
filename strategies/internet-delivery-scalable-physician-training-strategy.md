# Deliver physician behaviour-change training via the internet to enable wide, low-cost dissemination across health systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q4` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that outreach interventions relying on small groups of highly trained staff at research centres have questionable generalisability, whereas internet training "can be disseminated widely at low cost and does not require highly trained outreach facilitators to be on site". The trial showed such training achieved prescribing reductions across language, cultural, and health-system boundaries, supporting wide implementation in many health systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Iterative development of modules sensitive to cultural and national differences, with translation into the relevant national language
#### Constraints
- Long-term effects of the interventions on behaviour are unknown, although similar interventions have had lasting effects

### Target Learners
- Practising primary-care clinicians across countries and health systems

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing unnecessary antibiotic prescribing for respiratory-tract infections
