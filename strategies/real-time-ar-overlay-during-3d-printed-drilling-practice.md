# Add real‐time AR anatomical overlay to single‐session drilling practice on 3D‐printed temporal bone models for novices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable recipe: give novices the standard briefing (anatomical landmark review plus an instructional video) and then, during drilling on a 3D‐printed mastoid model, project a hologram of critical structures — "sigmoid sinus, facial nerve, horizontal semicircular canal, and tegmen" — onto the model in real time via a HoloLens with QR‐code auto‐registration. The trial tested exactly this addition against the same briefing without AR, using a single standardized model for the whole cohort.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- 3D‐printed temporal bone model with QR‐code registration surface, D2P software, and a HoloLens headset
#### Constraints
- Tested in novices with no prior temporal bone drilling experience after a single session; benefits for more experienced trainees may be smaller, per the authors' limitation discussion

### Target Learners
- medical students in their clinical clerkship
- novice surgical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- mastoidectomy drilling proficiency
- preservation of critical temporal bone structures
