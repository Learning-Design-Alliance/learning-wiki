# Two-session preparation-for-future-learning introduction to an active-learning course

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy redesigns the first two class sessions so students experience productive struggle before being told why. Session one covers the syllabus and grading without explaining the teaching method, then runs an invention activity; students submit one thing they liked and one they would change. Session two reviews their comments, has students predict which learning mode is most effective, then creates "a time for telling" by showing comparative data and discussing memory and the brain.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An invention or problem-solving activity that lets students generate ideas before instruction
- A second session in which student comments are reviewed and comparative evidence on learning modes is presented
#### Constraints
- The article notes possible confounds: instructor reputation, a move to an active learning classroom, and more careful group monitoring may have contributed to the improved reception

### Target Learners
- college students in introductory calculus-based mechanics for scientists and engineers

### Target Learning Goals
- student buy-in and receptiveness to active learning methods
