# Embed brief identity interventions within school-wide inclusive diversity-climate efforts; treat them as complements to, not substitutes for, structural change

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educational settings "foster environments where students can view and use their multiple identities as assets" and that brief interventions like multiple identity priming be embedded in school-wide efforts to build inclusive, critically reflective diversity climates. They stress that such interventions "are not substitutes for structural change" but may be valuable components of broader equity strategies, alongside addressing educator bias and increasing visibility of diverse role models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Local norms, visibility of role models, and perceived compatibility between cultural background and institutional expectations shape the salience and meaning of identity domains, so embedding must attend to these
#### Constraints
- The confirmatory three-way buffering test was nonsignificant in this study; the exploratory benefit is for second-generation students only and did not depend on stereotype activation

### Target Learners
- Adolescents in diverse secondary schools, including students of migrant descent

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing achievement gaps and supporting performance under evaluative threat
