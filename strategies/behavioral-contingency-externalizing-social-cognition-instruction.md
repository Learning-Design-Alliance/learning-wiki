# Add a behavioral contingency to social-cognition instruction for children with elevated externalizing behaviors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because children higher in baseline externalizing behaviors showed less improvement on the social-cognitive outcome, the authors recommend supplementing the emotion-recognition instruction with contingency management. As they write, "a behavioral contingency could be added to the instruction targeting social-cognition in order to improve task attention or completion", alongside closer monitoring and increased prompting so externalizing behaviors do not interfere with learning. The need for such adaptation appears small since externalizing did not moderate the other two outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School staff monitoring of externalizing behavior during instruction
- A reinforcement contingency tied to task attention or completion
#### Constraints
- Recommended only for children higher in externalizing behaviors; the moderating effect was small and limited to the social-cognitive outcome

### Target Learners
- elementary school children with ASD without intellectual disability with elevated baseline externalizing behaviors

### Target Learning Goals
- social-cognitive understanding (emotion recognition)
