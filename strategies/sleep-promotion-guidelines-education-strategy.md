# Implement institutional sleep guidelines, clarify 24-hour team responsibility with designated champions, and provide continuous evidence-based sleep education for night nurses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that nursing homes implement institutional guidelines for managing sleep disturbances based on evidence-based non-pharmacological interventions, clarify responsibilities so that sleep promotion becomes a 24/7 team task, and develop continuous education programs. The authors state: "Nursing homes should implement institutional guidelines in order to promote residents' sleep based on adequate evidence-based non-pharmacological interventions." They also suggest designated sleep nurses as individual champions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional policy documents guiding sleep management for day and night shifts
- Evidence-based training programs enabling nurses to provide best practice
- Clarified responsibilities for sleep and sleep problems across the 24-hour cycle
#### Constraints
- The article notes the 24-h responsibility is not yet implemented and that there are no German guidelines for sleep promotion comparable to those on other nursing topics

### Target Learners
- night-shift nurses and nursing assistants in nursing homes

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of sleep and sleep promotion
- competence in non-pharmacological sleep management
- documentation and standardized assessment of residents' sleep
