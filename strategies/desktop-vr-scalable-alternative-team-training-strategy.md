# Use accessible and scalable desktop VR as an alternative to face-to-face simulation for interprofessional team training when face-to-face training is not feasible

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article notes that "desktop VR is still more accessible and scalable to train large groups of learners" than head-mounted display VR, and that its evidence is timely given the rise of virtual learning platforms during the COVID-19 pandemic where face-to-face training may not be feasible. The strategy involves assigning interprofessional teams of medical and nursing students to a 2-h desktop VR simulation with scenarios, headsets for communication, and debriefing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A multi-user virtual reality platform with a patient avatar controlled by a trained simulated patient responding in real time using voice and actions
#### Constraints
- Blinding of participants to their allocated group was not possible; volunteer recruitment may introduce selection bias; HR data may be confounded by movement artefacts

### Target Learners
- medical students
- nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- recognising and managing clinical deterioration
- interprofessional collaboration and communication
