# Incorporate interactive first-hand accounts from persons with disabilities and direct disability exposure into health-professions curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building disability education around direct exposure to and interaction with persons with disabilities, in both in-person and virtual settings, which prior studies cited by the authors found to be "the most meaningful components of curricula focused on disability education". The clerkship implemented this through interactive first-hand accounts during didactic blocks and virtual encounters, alongside lectures on the ADA, ableism, and the disability paradox.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to persons with disabilities willing to share first-hand accounts, or virtual alternatives where in-person access is unavailable
#### Constraints
- The article notes many medical students lack access to academic physiatrists, and virtual exposure is offered as a route for students without a PM&R department at their home institution

### Target Learners
- medical students
- health professions trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- improve comfort and attitudes toward caring for persons with disabilities
- knowledge of ADA responsibilities
- counter ableist assumptions about quality of life
