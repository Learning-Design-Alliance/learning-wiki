# Improve nurses' narrative medicine competence through systematic training in listening, reading, questioning, writing and reflection, plus workload and scheduling changes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that nursing managers advance narrative nursing skills through "systematic training and lectures" and improve listening skills via "group discussions and real-world case study". They also recommend strategic scheduling, increased staffing and flexible scheduling to free communication time, and leveraging technology such as WeChat for post-discharge engagement. These target the identified barriers of skill deficits and time constraints.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Managerial action on staffing, scheduling and task distribution; time and space for patient-nurse communication
#### Constraints
- Time- and location-related constraints of busy clinical workloads; improving narrative competence is described as a time-consuming process

### Target Learners
- clinical nurses in Chinese hospitals, especially in secondary hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- narrative medicine competence, listening and communication skills, empathy
