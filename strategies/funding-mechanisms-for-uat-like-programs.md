# Create dedicated funding mechanisms for UAT-like research-engagement components in large scientific projects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that the astronomy community "identify and create funding resour ces and mechanisms to support programs such as the UAT," either as add-on funding to legacy grants or as stand-alone sources, possibly including UAT-like components in projects such as LSST and TMT. It also recommends that agencies increase investment in long-term projects that retain students through direct involvement and peer mentoring and promote women and underrepresented minorities in leadership. This addresses the stated lack of a support structure for such programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structure for support beyond standard research grants, since such programs do not fit standard Astronomy and Astrophysics Research grants or IUSE funding
#### Constraints
- The article notes tightened AST funding reduced its most recent grant to ∼$250k for three years, limiting student stipends and eliminating computer purchases

### Target Learners
- undergraduates at primarily undergraduate institutions, especially students from disadvantaged backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- early-career research involvement and STEM retention
- leadership development for women and underrepresented minorities
