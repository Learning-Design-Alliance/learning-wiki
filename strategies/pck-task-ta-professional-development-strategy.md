# Use TUG-K style PCK tasks in TA and new-faculty professional development, followed by discussion of common student difficulties

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends, based on graduate students' own remarks, that identifying the most common incorrect answer choices on PER-designed assessments be used in "professional development programs for TAs and new faculty if a discussion of common student difficulties ensues after performing the task". One interviewed graduate student remarked that such tasks would be useful because "if you don't know what their misconceptions are, how are you going to correct them?"

## Claims

- [Graduate students' PCK performance on the TUG-K task was significantly better in groups than individually, and discussions tended to shift answers toward more common introductory difficulties](../claims/group-work-improves-gs-pck-tugk.md) [+M]
- [First-year physics TAs performed better than random guessing at identifying introductory student difficulties on the TUG-K, but only on eight of the 17 questions with common difficulties](../claims/gs-pck-tugk-better-than-random-but-incomplete.md) [+W]
- [Graduate students found the task of thinking from an introductory student's point of view challenging, but reported it made them more aware of introductory students' patterns of reasoning](../claims/pck-task-raised-gs-awareness-reasoning.md) [+W]
- [Graduate students performed worse at identifying introductory students' major kinematics-graph difficulties than moderate ones (48% vs 61% average PCK score)](../claims/major-difficulties-identified-worse-than-moderate.md) [+W]
