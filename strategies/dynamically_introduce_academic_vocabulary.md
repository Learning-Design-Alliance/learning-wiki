# Dynamically Introduce Academic Vocabulary

## Description
Rather than presenting academic vocabulary as static lists to memorize, this strategy introduces target words through dynamic, contextualized encounters: a memorable story, a vivid example, or a humorous personal anecdote at first exposure, followed by repeated meetings with the word across varied authentic contexts — discussion, reading, writing, and application tasks. Each encounter adds nuance to the learner's understanding, so the definition is internalized as a usable tool rather than recited as a string.

## Design Implications

Vocabulary knowledge is incremental and context-dependent; words are acquired through multiple meaningful encounters, with direct instruction in context outperforming definitions alone [Stahl & Fairbanks meta-analysis of vocabulary instruction.](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543056001071) [+S]. The first encounter should be "sticky" — tied to a story, image, or emotional hook — because elaborative, distinctive encoding improves recall [Dual coding improves recall.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-recall.md) [+M]. Subsequent encounters must vary context and require active use; passive re-exposure produces much weaker gains than retrieval and production [Distributed practice improves retention.](../claims/distributed-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A small set of high-utility target words (Tier 2 general academic or Tier 3 disciplinary terms), selected for frequency and transfer value [Beck, McKeown, & Kucan's three-tier framework.](https://www.guilford.com/books/Bringing-Words-to-Life/Beck-McKeown-Kucan/9781462508167) [+S]
- Stories, anecdotes, or scenarios that embed each word naturally — ideally drawn from learner interests or current events
- Multiple follow-up contexts where learners *use* the word: [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), writing tasks, and [Application](../elements/application.md) activities
- Spacing across days and weeks so encounters are distributed, not massed [Distributed practice improves retention.](../claims/distributed-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S]

#### Constraints
- Time-intensive: crafting genuinely engaging stories for each word is costly, and the cost rises with word count [-W]
- Isolated "word of the day" routines without varied follow-up encounters yield minimal durable learning [Vocabulary gains require multiple varied encounters.](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543056001071) [-M]
- Overloading a lesson with too many new words exceeds working memory and degrades all of them [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [~M]
- Humor and story can overshadow the word itself if the anecdote is memorable but the word's meaning is incidental [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Story-anchored introduction**: teacher tells a funny or personal story using the word, then learners retell or adapt it
- **Word across texts**: the same word appears in a reading, a discussion prompt, and a writing task within a unit
- **Learner-generated contexts**: students create their own examples and non-examples, shifting from recognition to production
- **Morphological extension**: after the base word is established, introduce derived forms (*analyze* → *analysis*, *analytic*) to leverage word structure
- **Digital reinforcement**: spaced-repetition tools (e.g., [Quizlet](https://quizlet.com), [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net)) schedule re-encounters between class sessions

### Target Learners
- English learners and students with limited academic language exposure, who benefit most from rich contextualized instruction [Dialogic reading improves expressive language.](../claims/dialogic-reading-improves-expressive-language.md) [+M]
- Adolescent learners moving into disciplinary coursework, where Tier 2 words unlock comprehension across subjects
- All levels benefit, but highly proficient readers acquire many words incidentally from wide reading, reducing the marginal value of direct story-based introduction [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Vocabulary breadth and depth: flexible, context-sensitive word knowledge rather than rote definitions
- Disciplinary literacy: using the language of a field as practitioners do [Words as tools: academic vocabulary as language acquisition.](https://doi.org/10.1002/JAAL.00101) [+M]
- Reading comprehension: word knowledge as a gateway to text understanding

### Instructions
1. **Select 3–8 high-utility words** per unit, prioritizing words that appear across contexts ([Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md)).
2. **Craft a sticky first encounter** — a short funny or personal story, striking image, or [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) linking the word to familiar experience ([Activation](../principles/activation.md)).
3. **Give brief direct explanation** of the core meaning, then immediately check informal understanding ([Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md)).
4. **Plan varied re-encounters**: the word appears in a reading, a [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) prompt, and a writing or [Application](../elements/application.md) task within the week.
5. **Require production**: learners generate their own sentences, examples, and non-examples, or [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) to dramatize the word.
6. **Space re-encounters** across the unit and revisit words in later units so practice is distributed [Distributed practice improves retention.](../claims/distributed-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S].
7. **Assess in use**, not in isolation: score vocabulary use in speaking and writing rather than matching definitions.

## Examples
- **[Bringing Words to Life](https://www.guilford.com/books/Bringing-Words-to-Life/Beck-McKeown-Kucan/9781462508167)** (Beck, McKeown, & Kucan) — the robust-vocabulary-instruction framework on which this strategy is based: introduce words with student-friendly explanations and multiple contexts, then require interactive use.
- **[Word Generation](https://serpmedia.org/wg/)** (SERP Institute) — a published middle-school curriculum that introduces five high-stakes academic words per week and re-encounters them across discussion, reading, writing, and math activities.
- **A science teacher opens a unit on *inertia* by telling a story about a coffee cup left on the car roof** — students laugh, retell the story using the word, then meet *inertia* again in a reading, a lab report prompt, and a quiz two weeks later.
