# Multi-level intervention strategies for institutions, faculty and students to improve online engineering instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose intervention strategies "that can be employed by stakeholders at different levels to improve the online instruction of engineering courses." Institutions should budget for equipment, create virtual desktop environments, organize training workshops, provide an online syllabus template and a resource repository. Faculty should leverage the LMS, break lectures into shorter segments, encourage group discussion, be available during exams, provide a clear roadmap, share lecture recordings, administer practice exams, use open-book/randomized assessments, avoid camera proctoring, and employ home lab kits, recorded experiments and virtual labs. Students should use free smartphone scanning applications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional budget allocation and LMS infrastructure to provide equipment, virtual desktops and training workshops
- Faculty familiarity with LMS features such as randomized questions and question pools
#### Constraints
- The authors state that design, preparation and distribution of home lab kits or recording of experiments can be extremely time consuming for faculty given lab access restrictions and safety precautions
- Efficacy of the implemented interventions had not yet been evaluated; the authors planned a post-intervention survey at the end of Fall 2020

### Target Learners
- engineering undergraduate and graduate students at a minority-serving institution
- engineering faculty
- institution and engineering administration

### Target Learning Goals
- continuity and improvement of online engineering instruction
- hands-on training in online formats
- fair and rigorous online assessment
- student motivation and engagement
