# Use the existing Lady Health Worker programme as the delivery platform for community-based pre-eclampsia services rather than building a new cadre

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends leveraging the existing LHW programme infrastructure as the platform for community-based identification and management of pre-eclampsia, because LHWs "already perform regular home visits and deliver health promotion, preventive care and essential curative MNCH services". This strategy avoids the cost and delay of establishing a new community health worker cadre by adding pre-eclampsia tasks to a workforce that already has community trust, established visit routines, and government financial support. The article notes the entire budget of the existing LHW Programme is provided by the Government of Pakistan, indicating strong policy support for the tasks these providers undertake.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Existing community health worker programme with regular home visit routines and community trust
- Government or policy-level support for the expanded task set
#### Constraints
- Where the existing cadre is insufficient in number, the human resource shortage must be considered before redistribution of tasks
- Current LHW workload and compensation constraints limit how much can be added without review of existing task assignments

### Target Learners
- pregnant women in rural and urban Sindh, Pakistan, served by LHWs

### Target Learning Goals
- early identification of pre-eclampsia in community settings
- prompt referral of women with danger signs
