# Shadowing to Adjust Behavior Problems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Shadowing is an intensive, temporary behavior-support strategy in which an adult remains in close physical proximity (typically arm's reach) to a child who engages in harmful or disruptive behavior such as biting, hitting, or elopement. The adult's dual role is to physically pre-empt incidents by anticipating triggers and interrupting the behavior chain, and to coach replacement behaviors in the moment — prompting and reinforcing an appropriate alternative (e.g., "Use your words" or handing over a break card) before the harmful behavior occurs.

## Design Implications

Shadowing works because it collapses the delay between behavior, feedback, and reinforcement, which is critical for young children and learners with limited self-regulation; immediate, in-context prompting and reinforcement are far more effective than delayed correction [Feedback is most effective when delivered at the task and process levels, promptly and in context.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+M]. It is best understood as a form of intensive [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) embedded in the natural environment, and it should always be paired with a plan to fade adult proximity as the child's replacement behaviors become reliable [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility from adult to learner.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A functional hypothesis about what triggers and maintains the behavior (antecedents, function, reinforcers), typically from a brief [Functional Behavior Assessment](functional-behavior-assessment.md)
- A pre-taught replacement behavior the adult can prompt (requesting a break, asking for a turn, a signal card)
- Consistent adults who can commit sustained one-to-one attention for the shadowing period
- A reinforcement plan so replacement behaviors pay off better than the problem behavior
- A documented fading plan (reducing proximity, prompts, and monitoring over days or weeks)

#### Constraints
- Sustained one-to-one adult attention can itself become a reinforcer, increasing the behavior's frequency when shadowing is withdrawn [-M] — fading must begin early and be systematic
- Constant close proximity can feel intrusive or stigmatizing to the child and peers, undermining autonomy and peer relationships [~M]
- If implemented reactively (only hovering after incidents) rather than antecedently, it becomes surveillance and teaches nothing [-M]
- It is labor-intensive and not scalable; it is a short-term bridge, not a standing intervention [-W]
- Without a replacement behavior to prompt, shadowing only suppresses behavior temporarily and does not build skills [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Full shadowing** — arm's-reach proximity across settings, used for dangerous behaviors (biting, aggression)
- **Setting-specific shadowing** — proximity only during identified high-risk routines (transitions, unstructured play)
- **Faded shadowing** — planned reduction from arm's reach → across the room → periodic check-ins, with the adult moving to a [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) role
- **Peer-mediated variants** — training a peer buddy to prompt replacement behaviors as the adult recedes

### Target Learners
- Preschool and early-elementary children with emerging language who cannot yet self-monitor [Contingent, responsive adult scaffolding improves learning and behavior for young children.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Learners with disabilities (autism, developmental delays, emotional/behavioral disorders) whose behavior poses a safety risk to self or others
- Least appropriate for adolescents and learners with intact self-regulation, for whom constant proximity is intrusive and unnecessary [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Replacement behavior acquisition: prompting and reinforcing a functional communicative alternative in the exact context where the problem behavior occurs
- Self-regulation development: gradually transferring monitoring and prompting responsibility from adult to child [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility from adult to learner.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]
- Generalization: because coaching happens in natural routines, replacement behaviors are practiced where they must actually be used

### Instructions
1. **Assess function.** Complete a brief [Functional Behavior Assessment](functional-behavior-assessment.md) to identify triggers and what maintains the harmful behavior.
2. **Select and pre-teach a replacement behavior.** Choose a response that serves the same function (e.g., a break card for escape-maintained aggression) and teach it explicitly before shadowing begins, using [Modeling](../elements/modeling.md) and rehearsal.
3. **Shadow antecedently.** Stay within arm's reach during identified risk periods; watch for early trigger signs rather than waiting for the behavior.
4. **Prompt and reinforce in the moment.** At the first sign of a trigger, prompt the replacement behavior and deliver immediate, high-value reinforcement when the child uses it — feedback delivered at the process level, in context [Feedback is most effective when delivered at the task and process levels, promptly and in context.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+M]
5. **Interrupt safely if needed.** If the harmful behavior begins, block or redirect with minimal attention to the problem behavior and re-prompt the replacement.
6. **Fade systematically.** Reduce proximity and prompt intensity on a planned schedule as replacement behavior rates increase, moving to periodic [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) contact [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility from adult to learner.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]
7. **Review data and exit.** Track incidents and replacement-behavior use; exit shadowing when the child uses the replacement independently across settings.

## Examples
- **Preschool biting plan** — A teacher shadows a 4-year-old during free play, prompts "Can you tell Sam you want a turn?" when the child reaches for a toy, and reinforces the verbal request with immediate access; proximity is faded to across-the-room monitoring within three weeks.
- **Pyramid Model (Pyramid Equity Project)** — Tier 3 individualized support within the [Pyramid Model for Promoting Social Emotional Competence](https://challengingbehavior.org) uses one-to-one adult proximity with embedded coaching of replacement skills in early childhood settings.
- **Autism support classroom** — A paraprofessional shadows a student during transitions, prompting a break card before escape-maintained elopement occurs, then fades to a pre-arranged check-in at the classroom door.
