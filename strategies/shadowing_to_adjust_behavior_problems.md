# Shadowing to Adjust Behavior Problems

## Description
This is a behavior management technique where an adult closely follows a child, acting as their "shadow," to prevent harmful behaviors like biting or hitting. The adult stays within arm's reach to intervene immediately if the child attempts the behavior. The goal is to provide constant, direct supervision without making the child feel singled out or punished, allowing them to play and interact freely while ensuring the safety of themselves and others.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A designated adult, such as a teacher, aide, or trained volunteer. Consistent application of the shadowing technique. A clear understanding of the target behavior and intervention strategies.
#### Constraints
- Can be labor-intensive, requiring dedicated adult supervision. May not be suitable for all behaviors or children. Requires careful implementation to avoid making the child feel singled out or punished.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Primarily used with young children (e.g., preschool or early elementary) who exhibit behaviors that can harm themselves or others, such as biting or hitting.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Prevent harmful behaviors, ensure safety, break negative behavior cycles, support positive behavior development, and teach alternative communication strategies.

### Affordances
- Provides immediate intervention to prevent harm. Breaks negative behavior cycles. Allows the child to continue playing and interacting with peers. Offers an opportunity to teach alternative communication strategies.

### Personalization
- The intensity and duration of shadowing can be adjusted based on the child's specific needs and behavior patterns. Techniques can be combined with other strategies to support the child's development.
