# Use element-weighted rubrics and social moderation to standardise interpretation of marking criteria

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Differential interpretations of criteria between markers are a threat to assessment standards, with plentiful evidence that different markers grade the same work based on personal biases about criterion importance. The review's remedy is 'veering away from holistic marking criteria and using rubrics where each criterion element is separated out and given a pre‐agreed weighting', so the marker provides a value judgement only for each rubric element. A complementary strategy is social moderation of marking, where multiple markers initially grade the same small handful of submissions, discuss their marks, then mark the remaining allocated scripts. Students also need help internalising standards, since descriptors of successive grade levels can be unhelpful when vague qualifiers like 'Satisfactory', 'Good' or 'Excellent' are the only difference.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Unfamiliar marking-criteria terms need to be explained to students; actively engaging students in discussing marking criteria helps them understand what criteria exist, what they mean and how to apply them
#### Constraints
- This is flagged as a particular challenge in the Biosciences, where criteria typically involve judging content knowledge, analytical ability, clarity/structure and use of up-to-date evidence

### Target Learners
- higher education students and academic markers

### Target Learning Goals
- shared understanding of expected standards and assessment literacy
