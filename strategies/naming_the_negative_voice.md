# Naming the Negative Voice

## Description
Students give their negative inner voice a name to externalize it, allowing them to treat it as a separate entity. This helps students understand that they are not defined by their negative thoughts and can take back control.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time for students to brainstorm and choose names. A supportive environment for sharing names and descriptions. Encouragement from the educator to embrace the activity with enthusiasm.
#### Constraints
- Some students may find the activity silly or uncomfortable. It may be difficult for some students to separate themselves from their negative thoughts. The activity's success depends on students' willingness to engage in the process.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- K-12 students, particularly those who struggle with negative self-perception and fixed mindsets.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- To help students externalize their negative thoughts and understand that they are not defined by them.

### Affordances
- Promotes self-awareness by distinguishing between the self and negative thoughts. Supports emotional regulation by creating distance from negative emotions. Fosters a growth mindset by understanding that negative thoughts are not fixed.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to different age groups and learning styles. Students can choose names that resonate with their personal experiences. Educators can provide examples of names and descriptions to inspire students.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe students' engagement and enthusiasm during the naming activity. Assess students' ability to articulate the difference between themselves and their negative voice. Review students' reflections on how naming the voice has impacted their self-perception.

## Impact
- Research shows that externalizing problems is an effective tool for promoting growth mindset. By distancing themselves from their negative thoughts, students can take back control and view those thoughts as separate.
