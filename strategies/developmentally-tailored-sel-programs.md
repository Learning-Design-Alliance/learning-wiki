# Tailor SEL programming to developmental level, from preschool skill practice through adolescent status-and-respect opportunities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 8 studies (5 quant-synthesis, 2 review, 1 causal), `q3`–`q4` · 5 of 8 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends matching SEL approaches to children's developmental stage: young children benefit from programs focused on "skill development, skill supplementation and skill revision" using sharing, sociodramatic play, and teacher feedback; elementary practices include Morning Meetings and inquiry formats; adolescent programs build on desires for status and respect through community service and student-led discussions of developmental topics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation tailored to children's developmental level; adolescent programs must provide meaningful opportunities to use SEL skills
#### Constraints
- Isolated mindfulness programs show mixed results, suggesting integrated curricula with classroom teachers may be key

### Target Learners
- preschool children
- elementary students
- adolescents

### Target Learning Goals

- social-emotional skills supporting academic learning
