# Adopt computer-assisted exams as alternatives to paper-based tests once comparability is established, exploiting immediate feedback and reduced scoring burden

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their null mode effect, the authors recommend that "computers can be adopted as alternative testing tools by teachers, because doubts about the affinity of students' scores both on paper and on computer have been eliminated." They argue computer-assisted testing gives immediate feedback on incorrect and missing answers so students can focus on deficient points (washback), removes scoring burden and subjectivity, reduces human error, saves time, place and printing costs, and enables richer content such as visual cues and audio for communicative language testing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prior evidence that the computer-based version's reliability and validity coefficients are comparable to the paper-based version's
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a linear, fixed computer-assisted test in a single course; the authors note computer-adaptive testing was not used and results may not generalize beyond tertiary EFL teacher education

### Target Learners
- higher education students
- pre-service English teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- achievement assessment with immediate feedback
- efficient scoring and feedback practices
