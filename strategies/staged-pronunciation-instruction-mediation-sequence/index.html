Staged pronunciation instruction: imitation of model recordings, then paired CAPT practice, then individual CAPT practice, then reflection¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (2 for, 5 mixed) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2–q3 · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes a staged employment of mediating tools for pronunciation teaching. Students first listen and repeat after the model utterance to internalize the text recording; teachers then encourage students "to work in pairs to practice some CAPT software", where peers detect each other's errors and co-construct knowledge; students then work alone with the software to focus on individual problems, with CAPT feedback identifying errors hard to detect by ear; finally, students reflect on their practice. The sequence moves between inter-psychological and intra-psychological dimensions.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to CAPT software with model utterances and feedback
  • A teacher who invites students to make reflections on their practice

Constraints¶

  • The article presents this as a suggestion from one case study, not a tested sequence

Target Learners¶

  • College EFL learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English pronunciation, intonation, and prosody

Affordances¶

  • Vygotskian Sociocultural Mediation Pronunciation

Claims¶

  • CAPT software shows promising results for segmental pronunciation, while prosodic features and fluency still require further research and development [+M]
  • Whether individual or peer practice works better with ASR-based pronunciation training is unsettled: one study found individual work best, a meta-analysis found peer practice gave larger effects [~W]
  • Mediation type (self-access CAPT, collaborative CAPT, or MP3) produced no statistically significant difference in posttest reading performance across pronunciation, intonation, timing, or overall ratings [~W]
  • In learning-log reflections, the Collaborative CAPT Group reported the most gains and strategies while the MP3 Group reported the most difficulties [~W]
  • The Self-Access CAPT Group reported the highest frequency of self-monitoring of language learning but more difficulties and fewer strategies than the Collaborative Group [~W]
  • Students identified design limitations of the CAPT system's feedback, including fluctuating scores, lack of constructive improvement suggestions, and monotonous practice [~W]
  • Guided ASR practice improves overall pronunciation accuracy of Korean EFL learners more than ordinary classroom pronunciation practice alone [+M]
