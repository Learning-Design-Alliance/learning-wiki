# Use active methodologies, case studies, simulation, portfolios, flipped classroom, and problem-based learning to develop soft skills in nursing curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article documents faculty-reported strategies for soft skills development: active methodologies that "reinforce the role of the student as the protagonist of their own learning process," case studies mediating theory and practice, clinical simulation with defined objectives, portfolios for reflection on lived experiences, and flipped classroom and problem-based learning stimulating critical thinking. The article recommends greater pedagogical systematization and institutional support to consolidate these strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty development and pedagogical systematization with institutional support
#### Constraints
- Adoption of active methodologies alone may not ensure soft skills development without explicit articulation of soft skills objectives

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- communication, teamwork, critical thinking, problem-solving, emotional intelligence, empathy, and leadership

### Affordances
- Soft Skills Intrapersonal Interpersonal Taxonomy
