# Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 7 studies (4 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q4` · 1 of 7 report an effect size

## Description
PBIS is a proactive, school-wide framework for improving behavior and climate by *teaching* behavioral expectations the way academic content is taught, then consistently recognizing and reinforcing them. It is organized in three tiers: universal expectations for all students (Tier 1), targeted small-group supports (Tier 2), and intensive individualized interventions (Tier 3). The core premise is that students can only meet expectations they know, have practiced, and see reinforced — so the emphasis is on prevention and explicit instruction rather than punishment.

## Design Implications

PBIS applies behavioral learning principles at the systems level: expectations are defined operationally, taught explicitly, and followed by consistent reinforcement [Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md). Randomized trials show school-wide PBIS reduces office discipline referrals and suspensions and improves school climate [School-wide PBIS reduces disciplinary exclusions and improves climate.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018120) [+S], with effects strongest when implementation fidelity is high and sustained over multiple years.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A small set (3–5) of positively stated, school-wide expectations defined in observable terms
- Explicit lessons teaching what each expectation looks like in every setting (classroom, hallway, bus, cafeteria)
- A consistent acknowledgment/reinforcement system and a data system (e.g., office referral data) for monitoring and decision-making
- Administrative commitment and a team that meets regularly to review data and adjust supports
- A leadership team with administrator support and a defined set of 3–5 positively stated expectations
- Explicit lesson plans that teach expectations in each setting, like any [Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) sequence
- A reinforcement/acknowledgment system (tokens, praise, privileges) delivered consistently by all staff
- Behavioral data collection (referrals, attendance) reviewed regularly for tier placement decisions
- Family and staff buy-in; the framework fails when only some classrooms participate

#### Constraints
- Implementation without fidelity — expectations posted but never taught or reinforced — produces little or no benefit [Effects depend on implementation fidelity and duration.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1098300712463158) [~S]
- Punitive or inconsistent staff responses undermine the reinforcement system; partial adoption (posters without teaching) is a common failure mode
- Tier 1 alone is insufficient for students with intensive behavioral needs; without Tier 2/3 supports, effects for high-need students wash out [~S]
- Rewards perceived as controlling can crowd out intrinsic motivation for some students [Autonomy-supportive framing matters for intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~M] — acknowledgment systems should emphasize progress and competence rather than compliance-for-prizes
- Implementation without fidelity — expectations posted but never taught or reinforced — produces no measurable benefit [-S]; effects depend on sustained (3+ years) high-fidelity implementation
- Punitive or inconsistent staff responses undermine the reinforcement system; mixed messaging reduces effectiveness [~M]
- Tier 1 alone is insufficient for students with intensive needs; withholding Tier 2/3 supports while claiming "PBIS doesn't work" is a common misattribution [-M]
- Critiques note that acknowledgment systems can feel controlling or inequitable if rewards favor already-compliant students; culturally responsive adaptation is required [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Tier 1 (universal): school-wide expectations, acknowledgment systems, ~80% of students
- Tier 2 (targeted): check-in/check-out, small-group social skills instruction for ~10–15% of students
- Tier 3 (individualized): function-based behavior support plans, wraparound services for ~5% of students
- Classroom-level adaptations: [Classroom PBIS](../strategies/classroom_pbis.md) integrates expectations into routines, [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) routines, and precorrection of problem transitions
- **Check-In/Check-Out (CICO)** — a Tier 2 strategy pairing students with a mentor for daily goal-setting and feedback; widely replicated
- **Classroom-level PBIS** — the same teach-and-reinforce cycle applied to routines (transitions, group work) within a single classroom
- **Integrating with SEL** — combining PBIS with social-emotional curricula to address skill deficits alongside behavior

### Target Learners
- All students in a school benefit from universal expectations and predictable routines [School-wide PBIS reduces disciplinary exclusions and improves climate.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018120) [+S]
- Students with IEPs, 504 plans, or histories of disciplinary exclusion benefit most from the tiered escalation of support [Tiered supports reduce exclusionary discipline for high-risk students.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1098300709334798) [+M]
- Empathic, relationship-based discipline messaging is especially effective for students at risk of suspension [Brief intervention empathic discipline cuts suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+M]
- All students benefit from predictable, positively framed expectations [+S]
- Students with externalizing behavior or a history of discipline referrals benefit most from Tier 2/3 supports [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Behavioral self-regulation: knowing and demonstrating expectations across settings
- School climate and belonging: predictable, safe environments [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Social-emotional skills: cooperation, responsibility, and conflict resolution taught as explicit curricula
- Procedural fluency in behavioral routines (transitions, help-seeking, collaboration)
- Self-regulation and social skill acquisition
- School climate and engagement as preconditions for academic learning

### Instructions
1. Define 3–5 positively stated school-wide expectations (e.g., "Be respectful, be responsible, be safe") with behavioral examples for each setting — [State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md)
2. Teach expectations explicitly in every setting, using [Modeling](../elements/modeling.md) and [Act-It-Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) practice rather than posting rules alone
3. Establish a consistent acknowledgment system that reinforces expected behavior promptly — [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)
4. Use a continuum of consequences for problem behavior that is instructional, not exclusionary, wherever possible
5. Monitor office discipline referrals, attendance, and climate data on a regular cycle and adjust tier supports — [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)
6. Escalate support through tiers: [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and check-in/check-out for at-risk students, function-based individual plans for intensive need

## Description
PBIS is a multi-tiered behavioral framework in which schools explicitly define, teach, and reinforce a small set of positive behavioral expectations (e.g., "be safe, be respectful, be responsible") across all settings. Tier 1 applies universal expectations and acknowledgment systems to all students; Tier 2 adds targeted group supports (e.g., check-in/check-out); Tier 3 provides individualized, function-based behavior plans. The approach is carried out through direct teaching of routines, frequent positive acknowledgment, consistent consequences, and continuous data-based decision-making.

## Design Implications

PBIS treats behavior like an academic skill: it is taught, practiced, and reinforced rather than assumed or punished into shape. This aligns with [Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md) — reinforcement contingencies shape behavior — but extends it with explicit instruction and systems-level consistency. Meta-analytic evidence shows school-wide PBIS reduces office discipline referrals and suspensions and improves school climate [School-wide positive behavior support improves school climate and reduces discipline referrals.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S], with smaller but positive effects on academic achievement [+M].

2. Create a behavior matrix specifying what each expectation looks like in each setting (hallway, cafeteria, classroom).
3. Teach expectations directly using modeling and [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) — brief lessons, role-play, and practice in context, the way academic content is taught.
4. Establish an acknowledgment system that reinforces expected behavior at high rates initially, then thins toward natural reinforcement.
5. Respond to problem behavior with consistent, instructional (not purely punitive) consequences.
6. Collect referral and acknowledgment data monthly; use team review to move students into Tier 2 supports such as [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) structures or Tier 3 individualized plans with appropriate [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md).
