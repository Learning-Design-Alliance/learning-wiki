# Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
PBIS is a proactive, school-wide framework for improving behavior and climate by *teaching* behavioral expectations the way academic content is taught, then consistently recognizing and reinforcing them. It is organized in three tiers: universal expectations for all students (Tier 1), targeted small-group supports (Tier 2), and intensive individualized interventions (Tier 3). The core premise is that students can only meet expectations they know, have practiced, and see reinforced — so the emphasis is on prevention and explicit instruction rather than punishment.

## Design Implications

PBIS applies behavioral learning principles at the systems level: expectations are defined operationally, taught explicitly, and followed by consistent reinforcement [Behaviorism](../principles/behaviorism.md). Randomized trials show school-wide PBIS reduces office discipline referrals and suspensions and improves school climate [School-wide PBIS reduces disciplinary exclusions and improves climate.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018120) [+S], with effects strongest when implementation fidelity is high and sustained over multiple years.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A small set (3–5) of positively stated, school-wide expectations defined in observable terms
- Explicit lessons teaching what each expectation looks like in every setting (classroom, hallway, bus, cafeteria)
- A consistent acknowledgment/reinforcement system and a data system (e.g., office referral data) for monitoring and decision-making
- Administrative commitment and a team that meets regularly to review data and adjust supports

#### Constraints
- Implementation without fidelity — expectations posted but never taught or reinforced — produces little or no benefit [Effects depend on implementation fidelity and duration.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1098300712463158) [~S]
- Punitive or inconsistent staff responses undermine the reinforcement system; partial adoption (posters without teaching) is a common failure mode
- Tier 1 alone is insufficient for students with intensive behavioral needs; without Tier 2/3 supports, effects for high-need students wash out [~S]
- Rewards perceived as controlling can crowd out intrinsic motivation for some students [Autonomy-supportive framing matters for intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~M] — acknowledgment systems should emphasize progress and competence rather than compliance-for-prizes

#### Implementation Variability
- Tier 1 (universal): school-wide expectations, acknowledgment systems, ~80% of students
- Tier 2 (targeted): check-in/check-out, small-group social skills instruction for ~10–15% of students
- Tier 3 (individualized): function-based behavior support plans, wraparound services for ~5% of students
- Classroom-level adaptations: [Classroom PBIS](../strategies/classroom_pbis.md) integrates expectations into routines, [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) routines, and precorrection of problem transitions

### Target Learners
- All students in a school benefit from universal expectations and predictable routines [School-wide PBIS reduces disciplinary exclusions and improves climate.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018120) [+S]
- Students with IEPs, 504 plans, or histories of disciplinary exclusion benefit most from the tiered escalation of support [Tiered supports reduce exclusionary discipline for high-risk students.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1098300709334798) [+M]
- Empathic, relationship-based discipline messaging is especially effective for students at risk of suspension [Brief intervention empathic discipline cuts suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Behavioral self-regulation: knowing and demonstrating expectations across settings
- School climate and belonging: predictable, safe environments [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Social-emotional skills: cooperation, responsibility, and conflict resolution taught as explicit curricula

### Instructions
1. Define 3–5 positively stated school-wide expectations (e.g., "Be respectful, be responsible, be safe") with behavioral examples for each setting — [State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md)
2. Teach expectations explicitly in every setting, using [Modeling](../elements/modeling.md) and [Act-It-Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) practice rather than posting rules alone
3. Establish a consistent acknowledgment system that reinforces expected behavior promptly — [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)
4. Use a continuum of consequences for problem behavior that is instructional, not exclusionary, wherever possible
5. Monitor office discipline referrals, attendance, and climate data on a regular cycle and adjust tier supports — [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)
6. Escalate support through tiers: [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and check-in/check-out for at-risk students, function-based individual plans for intensive need

## Examples
- **[Center on PBIS](https://www.pbis.org)** — the national technical assistance center providing implementation guides, fidelity measures (e.g., TFI, SAS), and state network resources.
- **Maryland Safe and Supportive Schools / Maryland PBIS initiative** — the large randomized trial of school-wide PBIS across 37 elementary schools that documented reductions in suspensions and referrals [School-wide PBIS reduces disciplinary exclusions and improves climate.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018120) [+S]
- A typical Tier 1 implementation: students learn "Be Safe" on the bus through a taught lesson and role-play in the first week, bus drivers hand out acknowledgment tickets, and a team reviews referral data monthly.
