# Universities should implement targeted institutional strategies supporting student health during exam periods, including caffeine guidance, smoking cessation, sleep hygiene, and physical activity promotion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that universities support medical students during high-stress examination periods through a bundle of institutional strategies. It states these findings "highlight the need for targeted institutional strategies aimed at supporting student health during high-stress academic periods, including guidance on appropriate caffeine use, smoking cessation interventions, sleep hygiene education, and promotion of physical activity and stress management strategies". Educational campaigns should address caffeine timing and quantity, with specific attention to widespread waterpipe smoking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional capacity to deliver educational campaigns, smoking cessation initiatives, sleep hygiene education, and mental health and stress-management resources during exam periods
#### Constraints
- The article notes the underlying associations with academic performance were weak and multifactorial, and its cross-sectional design does not allow causal conclusions regarding the relationship between lifestyle behaviors and academic performance.

### Target Learners
- Medical students during examination periods

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining well-being and academic performance during high-stress academic periods
