# Introduce the OdonAssist device as a first AVB training tool in settings where assisted vaginal birth is underused

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the OdonAssist technique was easier to learn and required fewer repetitions among novices, the article recommends it as "a promising tool for introducing AVB into settings where AVB is underused". Its standardized approach is unaffected by fetal head position, reducing the training burden in low-resource or low-volume settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training on a simulator equipped with the OdonAssist device
- Instructors certified in the OdonAssist technique
#### Constraints
- The device received regulatory approval within the European Union in 2025; early clinical evaluations report favorable safety and feasibility outcomes, but this study tested learnability, not clinical outcomes

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals without previous experience in assisted vaginal birth

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquiring assisted vaginal birth skills with fewer practice repetitions
