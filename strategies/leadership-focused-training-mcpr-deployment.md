# Evaluate leadership-focused training targeting anticipatory guidance and explicit role assignment for mechanical CPR deployment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that training approaches address how teams lead the integration of the mechanical CPR device, since leadership was the subdimension most consistently associated with deployment efficiency. They propose that "Specific leadership behaviors, such as anticipatory guidance and explicit role assignment, may support more efficient device deployment" and that future studies evaluate "whether targeted leadership‐focused training improves MCPR deployment efficiency". This is framed as a direction for further evaluation, not a tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Leadership behaviors (anticipatory guidance, explicit role assignment) must be identifiable and coachable, e.g., via qualitative video analysis
#### Constraints
- The article reports an association with process metrics, not patient outcomes; efficiency gains translating into improved survival is an open question

### Target Learners
- Emergency department resuscitation team leaders
- Resuscitation teams using mechanical CPR devices

### Target Learning Goals
- Improve coordination and efficiency of mechanical CPR deployment, minimizing no-flow interruptions
