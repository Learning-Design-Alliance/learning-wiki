# Support faculty adoption and retention of RBIS through direct in-course help from a trained discipline-based science education specialist during initial implementation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's core recommendation is that a science education specialist (SES) trained in instructional methods work intensively with faculty during the first implementations of RBIS. The SES helps tailor strategies to the course and "is present during the implementation to provide immediate help and advice in overcoming problems and improving execution." The authors believe this factor outweighs general departmental support, and note such support may be providable more cheaply.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Specialists must be trained in best practices, potential implementation challenges, and the research on learning underlying the RBIS.
#### Constraints
- Guides posted on the CWSEI website under "Resources: Instructor Guidance" are described as "very different from having someone in the classroom that can help at a critical moment."
- The authors state it remains future research to confirm which support is dominant and the most cost-effective way to provide it.

### Target Learners
- University science faculty adopting research-based instructional strategies

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained adoption and faithful use of research-based instructional strategies in undergraduate science courses
