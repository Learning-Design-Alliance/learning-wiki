# Address the student perception of disjointed courses by deliberately integrating curricula into a cohesive learning block

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article identifies students' compartmentalized perception of courses as a learning bottleneck, building on Pace's notion of places where the stream of learning is apt to be obstructed. Its proposed remedy is to deliberately integrate the curriculum of courses so as to explicitly pull course subjects that may appear disjointed into a cohesive learning block with a unified mission and objective. The authors pose guiding questions about whether students grasp interrelatedness of courses, the purpose of non-engineering courses, and the relevance of content to real-world engineering problems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Program maps must genuinely be designed so skills and knowledge build across courses
- Data-driven discussion and environmental scans to identify the bottleneck before integration
#### Constraints
- The authors state they are in uncharted territory and anticipate implementation challenges for learners and the committee in the next couple of years

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students who perceive program courses as independent

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding interrelatedness of courses
- connecting course content to real-world engineering problems
