# Have introductory astronomy students measure waveform properties directly from plots with a straight edge and axis labels, then apply standard formulae to extract source parameters

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A step-by-step laboratory procedure in which students "make direct measurements from the ﬁgures in Section III using a straight edge and the axis labels". For monochromatic signals they measure period, amplitude and initial phase and, only if masses are assumed, compute orbital separation and distance; for chirping signals they measure period and amplitude at two times to deduce the chirp mass and then the distance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Waveform plots with labeled axes together with the waveform equations of Section II; the published activity includes "a keyed solution for the instructor".
#### Constraints
- The activity starts from clean waveforms: "This is the assumed starting point in the activity developed here." Identifying signals in noisy data streams is a separate challenge.
- Assuming component masses is risky: "similar chirp masses M can result from signiﬁcantly diﬀerent systems".

### Target Learners
- introductory astronomy students in laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- extracting astrophysical parameters such as orbital period, chirp mass and distance from gravitational wave signals

### Affordances
- Circular Binary Gravitational Wave Quadrupole Model
