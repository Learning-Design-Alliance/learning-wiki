# Combine interface-level scaffolding with explicit instruction and motivational support for durable LLM interaction habits

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 theoretical, 1 causal), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that structured-interface prompting behaviors did not transfer once scaffolding was removed, the authors recommend moving beyond temporary interface changes. They suggest structured interfaces could integrate more personalization, such as adaptive scaffolding or reflective follow-up questions, and that combining interface support with explicit instruction and classroom dialogue around effective LLM use could help learners better understand and retain productive strategies. They also stress taking students' prior LLM habits and expectations into account.

## Design Implications
