# Combine mindset shaping with goal-setting guidance and grit cultivation rather than promoting growth mindset alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that growth mindset's association with achievement is primarily indirect, the authors recommend that "educational interventions should move beyond simply promoting a growth mindset and adopt a more targeted, sequential approach": fostering mastery-approach and performance-approach goals while reducing performance-avoidance goals, and integrating grit cultivation—such as long-term project-based learning and self-regulation training—with goal-setting activities. They warn that promoting mindset alone "may not be sufficient, and could even be counterproductive without proper guidance".
