# Sequence the quizzes so prerequisite mastery gates access to later course work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The sequencing of the quizzes is described as important: "The course begins with the AMES math quizzes, and students are not allowed to move on until they have mastered all of them." After AMES, students proceed to CHAGS for chapter 1 homework and then QUICK for chapter 1, with perfect scores required before advancing to chapter 2. This strategy operationalizes completion of remediation early in the semester and reinforces keeping up with the course across the term.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- an internet delivery system that can withhold access to later quizzes until earlier ones are perfected
- problem pools sufficient to regenerate problems on repeated attempts
#### Constraints
- implemented in courses where completion of AMES, CHAGS and QUICK is a course-passing requirement

### Target Learners
- students in large introductory university physics courses, mostly first-year engineers

### Target Learning Goals
- early math remediation
- steady pacing through the course
- exam preparedness
