# Start pre-clinical courses oriented toward medical terminology and medical English reading early, especially in non-English-native countries

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their finding that baseline reading skills and medical terminology predicted final exam performance, the authors recommend an early-start approach: "starting courses oriented toward medical terminology and medical English education and reading medical articles or textbooks that focus on students' understanding of needed medical terminology at the earliest stage of pre-clinical education, especially in non-English-native countries, would be promising." The strategy targets the two baseline predictors their regression identified.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Baseline assessment of students' medical terminology and reading skills at course entry to identify starting levels.
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single course at one Japanese university; the authors note extrapolation to other medical schools presents challenges.
- The recommendation rests on observational associations, not a manipulated intervention.

### Target Learners
- Pre-clinical medical students in non-English-native countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Medical terminology knowledge and medical English reading comprehension
