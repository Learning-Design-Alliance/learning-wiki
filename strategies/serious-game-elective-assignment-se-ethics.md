# Use a choice-based serious game as an elective course assignment to introduce software engineering ethics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors argue that game-based learning suits software ethics because "This practice allows the students to experience real life situations, during playing and while having fun", presented in scenarios requiring moral judgement. They implemented this by embedding the Ethics Game as an elective assignment worth only 3% of the final grade, offered right after the lecture introducing ethical dilemmas, with pre-test, game play, and post-test plus scales required to finalize the assignment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A knowledge test administered before game access and repeated after completion
- Mobile devices or tablets with the storytelling platform application
#### Constraints
- Participation was elective and contributed only 3% of the final grade
- The authors advised students against purchasing passes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate computer engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- software engineering ethics knowledge
