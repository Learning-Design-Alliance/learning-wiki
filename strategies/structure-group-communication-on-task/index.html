Teachers should structure group work so student communication stays on-task and productive¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In the study's recommendation section the author argues that the power of cooperative learning lies in student interaction: to accomplish a group's task, students must exchange ideas, make plans and propose solutions, and exchanging alternative viewpoints stimulates broader thinking. The article assigns the teacher an active structuring role: "It is the teacher's job to encourage such exchanges and structure the students' work so their communication is on-task and productive."

Target Learners¶

  • eighth-standard mathematics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • intellectual growth through explaining ideas to others
  • social and personal development through group work

Claims¶

  • Cooperative learning produced significantly higher gain scores than lecture teaching for each of the knowledge, understanding and application objectives [+W]
  • Eighth-standard students taught mathematics through cooperative learning achieved higher gain scores than control-group students taught by lecture method [+W]
  • Control and experimental groups were equivalent at pre-test but differed significantly at post-test in mathematics achievement [+W]
  • Cooperative learning has drawbacks including slow learners depending on fast achievers, so teachers should stress both cooperative and individual work [~W]
  • Prior research cited by the article reports teacher and student support for cooperative learning in mathematics, with benefits that may take time to appear [+W]
