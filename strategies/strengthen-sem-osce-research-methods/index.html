Strengthen SEM OSCE research through larger samples, longitudinal data, and fuller assessment-quality measurement¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's forward-looking recommendation is to improve research methods for evaluating OSCEs in SEM. It urges "increasing the sample size of studies" and using longitudinal exam data to study re-failure rates, candidate characteristics, and examiner change over time, and calls for studies to involve "appropriate measurements of assessment quality such as utility, acceptability, and validity". It also highlights exploring the relationship between candidate characteristics and OSCE scores.

Requirements¶

  • Access to longitudinal SEM OSCE results data across years
  • Adequate sample sizes in future studies

Constraints¶

  • The review could not perform quantitative analysis due to the diversity of study designs and objectives

Target Learners¶

  • medical students
  • residents and fellows
  • practicing physicians

Target Learning Goals¶

  • valid and reliable assessment of sport and exercise medicine clinical competency

Claims¶

  • Candidates receiving additional musculoskeletal training (lectures, workshops, video, spaced education, or SCIM) scored higher or similar on OSCEs compared with controls [+W]
  • A six-station sport medicine OSCE showed high reliability and construct validity, with senior residents outscoring junior residents [+W]
  • Only a small fraction of published OSCE research in musculoskeletal and sport medicine was actually conducted in a Sport and Exercise Medicine setting [+W]
  • The Modified Angoff method was acceptable for setting different pass marks for senior and junior residents on the same OSCE [+W]
  • Improvement in Orthopaedic In-Training Examination scores showed no significant correlation with OSCE scores [+W]
  • Teaching musculoskeletal examination skills by trained patient educators produced no significant OSCE score difference compared with physician teaching [+W]
