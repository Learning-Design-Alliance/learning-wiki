# Future evaluations of the e-learning should reduce participant time-burden and boost retention through incentives, multiple reminders, gatekeeper support, and workplace-embedded designs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A strategy for future trials of professional e-learning, drawn from the pilot's feasibility findings. The article states that "Future evaluation of the e-learning needs to consider ways to reduce participant time-burden (e.g. fewer timepoints and/or shorter surveys)", and lists financial incentives/remuneration for time, mixed-mode multiple reminders, and gaining support from gatekeepers such as management. It also suggests hybrid implementation trials embedded within workplaces and broadening the stringent weekly-consultation eligibility criterion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Follow-up survey return must be improved beyond the pilot's rates before effects can be evaluated at scale
#### Constraints
- Time is a known barrier to health professional participation in research, as competing priorities take precedence; the pilot's 51% follow-up non-return among those who accessed their activity limits conclusions

### Target Learners
- credentialled diabetes educators participating in professional development research
- health professionals in workplace-embedded education trials

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating e-learning effects on knowledge, confidence, and clinical behaviour
- implementing diabetes distress support in routine care
