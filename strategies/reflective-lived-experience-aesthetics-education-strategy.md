# Use reflective learning, lived-experience sharing, and active participation in aesthetics-based nursing education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article attributes the intervention's effects to its interactive and reflective pedagogy: group discussions and scenario-based learning encouraged nurses to reflect on clinical experiences, and sharing lived experiences enhanced professional self-awareness and sensitivity toward patients' needs. The authors state that "the combination of reflective learning and experiential activities may have contributed to the development of more compassionate and aesthetically oriented nursing practices."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitated group discussion and scenario-based sessions
- Assignments in which participants reflect on and share their own aesthetic care experiences
#### Constraints
- The causal contribution of these pedagogical features is the authors' interpretation, not a separately tested component

### Target Learners
- Psychiatric nurses
- Nursing students in undergraduate curricula and continuing professional development

### Target Learning Goals
- Humanistic and compassionate care practices
- Sensory awareness and appreciation of the artistic and ethical dimensions of nursing
