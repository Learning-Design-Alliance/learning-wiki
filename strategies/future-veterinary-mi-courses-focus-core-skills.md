# Focus future veterinary MI courses on Cultivating Change Talk and Reflections and integrate MI training into veterinary education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future MI courses for veterinarians concentrate on the core skills where performance was lowest, and that MI training be introduced early in veterinary education rather than only in continuing professional development. They also suggest more practical training, adaptation of material to the veterinary context, an audiobook version of the literature, and simulated conversations with professional actors to increase practice opportunities. The article states: "The authors of the present study suggest future courses directed at veterinarians should focus more on the MI core skills of Cultivating Change Talk and Reflections, where skills were low."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Courses need sufficient practice opportunities and feedback, since the authors link low competency proportions to insufficient practice
#### Constraints
- The authors note time constraints make a prolonged course unlikely to be attractive, and recommend a web-based format to decrease travel time

### Target Learners
- veterinary students
- veterinarians in continuing education involved in herd health management

### Target Learning Goals
- Cultivating Change Talk
- constructing reflections
- providing information in a non-persuasive, non-judging style
