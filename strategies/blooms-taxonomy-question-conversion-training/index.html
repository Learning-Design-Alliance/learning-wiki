Use Bloom's taxonomy to convert lower-order questions into higher-order questions in faculty training¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A concrete training activity in which educators learn to write behavioral objectives at each level of Bloom's taxonomy, generate questions at each level, and convert lower-order questions into higher-order ones. The intervention's learning outcomes included the ability to "Convert lower order questions into higher order questions" and to identify characteristics of effective questioning. This strategy was part of the workshop content that produced significant gains in higher-order questioning.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training must cover types, levels, and effective questioning techniques, with practice writing objectives aligned to assessment strategies

Constraints¶

  • Question level must be appropriate to learners' familiarity with the content, per the article's discussion

Target Learners¶

  • higher education faculty learning to foster critical thinking

Target Learning Goals¶

  • asking higher-order cognitive questions
  • aligning objectives, teaching strategies, and assessment

Claims¶

  • A 40-hour critical thinking workshop intervention significantly increases educators' use of higher-order questions compared with a control group [+W]
  • The intervention significantly increases educators' encouragement of student questions and reduces dictation of notes [+W]
  • The intervention does not significantly change the total number of questions educators ask [~W]
  • The intervention does not produce a significant increase in educators' use of active teaching strategies relative to control [-W]
  • The intervention significantly increases educators' use of lecture with slide presentation relative to control [-W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Teachers should check question levels with software after writing papers and be trained in preparing higher-order questions
