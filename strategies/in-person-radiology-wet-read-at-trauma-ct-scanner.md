# Place trained radiology residents at the CT scanner with a joint checklist to provide wet reads for class 1 trauma panscans

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's clinical implementation strategy: upon completing individual simulation training, residents report to the CT scanner for class 1 trauma patients, review images while surgery is present, and complete the jointly created panscan checklist, with any major changes from the scanner report communicated to the trauma team by direct pager and phone call. The authors state the findings "support the value-added of an in-person radiologist at the CT scanner for whole-body trauma panscans" for timely detection of life-threatening injuries and improved professional relations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Residents must first complete the high-fidelity individual simulation training session
- A jointly created paper panscan checklist for documenting findings and timestamps
- The emergency department CT scanner reachable from the reading room (approximately 30 s at a brisk walk in this study)
#### Constraints
- The study was performed at a single suburban level 1 trauma hospital, and the authors state results may not generalize to differently sized hospitals or larger healthcare systems serving different patient populations; injury severity scores were not included

### Target Learners
- Junior radiology residents on trauma call

### Target Learning Goals
- Rapidly and accurately identify injuries requiring immediate surgical attention at the CT scanner
