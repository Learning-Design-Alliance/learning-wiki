Provide both a pre-populated exemplar microworld and a blank microworld to lower the entry barrier¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

To facilitate the low floor and ease-of-access that are pedagogic characteristics of the microworlds-based approach, learners were provided with two versions of the microworld. The first "provided a working exemplar of the microworld pre-populated with sample poetic structures and words"; learners were then given blocks in a blank microworld to encourage experimentation. This sequencing lets learners inspect a working program before building their own grammar structures and poem generators.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Pre-built programming blocks with space to add learners' own text, so learners can assemble programs without typing syntax

Constraints¶

  • The strategy was implemented in a bookable ICT room over three one-hour lessons within normal timetabled English lessons, with the author available in a technical support capacity

Target Learners¶

  • Year 8 secondary school learners new to programming

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding grammatical structures and poetic forms through experimentation with a poem-generating program

Affordances¶

  • Microworlds Guided Discovery Approach

Claims¶

  • A microworld-based intervention produced a modestly greater gain in computational thinking than comparison teaching, with a modest-to-moderate effect size (d = 0.50) that was not statistically significant [+W]
  • A microworld-based intervention produced a modestly greater gain in poetic thinking than comparison teaching, with a modest effect size (d = 0.46) that was not statistically significant [+W]
