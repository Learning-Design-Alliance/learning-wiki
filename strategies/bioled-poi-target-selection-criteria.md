# Select protein targets against five explicit criteria to maximize student research success

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Before each term, PhD-level instructors evaluate candidate proteins of interest against five printed criteria: a 3D structure is available; function is unknown/unreported; the putative function is likely enzymatic; the reaction can be monitored via spectrophotometric assays directly or via coupling; and all substrates, cofactors, and coupling enzymes are commercially available and affordable. The article also recommends "avoiding dehydrogenases with vague annotations" unless bioinformatic data strongly suggest a specific substrate, after one student group surveyed over 20 substrates with no positive results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Plasmid DNAs are mini-prepped and sequenced by instructional staff before the term begins
- Clones are requested from JCSG collaborators or purchased from ASU's Plasmid Repository
#### Constraints
- Some target POIs are misannotated or annotated at only low functional resolution, so chosen substrates may yield negative results
- Confidence and morale can become diminished in POI groups with negative outcomes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biochemistry lab students

### Target Learning Goals
- enzyme function determination
- experimental assay design

### Affordances
- Bioled Two Semester Inquiry Research Model
