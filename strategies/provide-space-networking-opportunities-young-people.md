# Provide networking opportunities in space for young people

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Drawing on the survey finding that building a space network was the most valued outcome of SSUK, the authors encourage 'likeminded colleagues to provide networking opportunities in space for young people'. The strategy is to create and maintain space-sector networks from a young age through alumni communities, mentors and industry-linked residential programmes, which the authors argue generates long-lasting impacts, particularly for women, and supports the future workforce the sector needs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A sustained alumni community or mentor network able to connect young people with the space sector over multiple years
#### Constraints
- Supporting evidence comes from a self-selected alumni survey that the authors note systematically skews towards engaged, recent alumni and mentors

### Target Learners
- young people interested in space careers
- secondary and university students

### Target Learning Goals
- building professional and peer networks in the space sector
- career pathway awareness and entry to the space sector

## Claims

- [Alumni most commonly reported gains in knowledge and understanding of space (87%), social confidence (83%) and teamwork (71%), with networking the most-cited career benefit](../claims/ssuk-skills-knowledge-social-confidence-teamwork.md) [+W]
