# Schedule the motivational intervention early in the academic year and consider longer practice sessions when recruiting students into first responder systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adapting recruitment implementation for first-responder programs: the motivational presentation should occur earlier in the academic year so it does not compete with end-of-year exam revisions, and practice sessions may need to be longer than 1 hour since students felt there was not enough time for hands-on practice. The authors state "the motivational intervention will take place sooner in the academic year to avoid interference with final revisions and exams" in their follow-up protocol.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a motivational presentation delivered within the students' regular course schedule, reachable via the university's streaming platform and mailing list.
- Requires trained and certified instructors and available training slots for the practice sessions.
#### Constraints
- The article states the late placement of the presentation and short sessions are hypotheses for the low enlistment rate, not tested findings; the follow-up study assessing these changes had not yet been conducted.

### Target Learners
- first-year medical and dental students

### Target Learning Goals
- basic life support and AED use
- motivation to enlist as first responders
