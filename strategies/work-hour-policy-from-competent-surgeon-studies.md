# Base surgeons' work-hour decisions on studies of competent practicing surgeons rather than on surgical learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "decisions regarding surgeons' work hours should be based on studies involving competent surgeons rather than on surgical learners or other professions." Because the study found that autonomous-stage surgeons retained technical dexterity postcall, restrictions extrapolated from residents or other safety-sensitive professions "could result in erroneous conclusions, reduced surgical output, and longer surgical waits for patients."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Policy-relevant evidence must come from performance studies of competent, practicing surgeons in the autonomous stage of skill acquisition.
#### Constraints
- The finding applies to surgeons unconsciously competent in basic laparoscopic tasks and to sleep deprivation below 3 hours of sleep in 24 hours; the authors do not imply surgeons can attend easy days intoxicated.

### Target Learners
- practicing staff surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining technical competence under postcall fatigue and informing work-hour policy
