Implement online active learning with attention to logistics, competency, content, delivery, and assessment.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review summarizes recommendations for implementing online active learning across five categories: logistics, competency, content, delivery/teaching, and assessment. These include ensuring "Access to technology," "Reliable connectivity," "Digital literacy and competency (also called digital readiness) among faculty and students," content that is "Suitable for online learning" and "Aligned with learning and outcome objectives," delivery that includes "Alignment with principles of adult and active learning," and assessment that provides "Timely and constructive feedback" and "Formative assessment."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to technology, reliable connectivity, adequate digital infrastructure and support, and functional platforms
  • Digital literacy and competency among faculty and students
  • Content suitable for online learning and aligned with learning objectives
  • Adaptability to varying digital environments and alignment with principles of adult and active learning

Constraints¶

  • Faculty-related disadvantages include a lack of digital competency, inexperience in delivering OAL, difficulty in building rapport, inability to read body language and make necessary adjustments, and increased time required to create learning materials and resources
  • Reported disadvantages for students include technical issues, distractions, limited interaction, difficulties in managing pre-class workload, and disengagement from peer sharing and presentations

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate dental students
  • dental faculty

Target Learning Goals¶

  • learning satisfaction
  • knowledge acquisition
  • skill development

Claims¶

  • Problem-based learning, case-based learning, small group discussion, flipped learning, and blended learning were the most common online active learning strategies employed. [+W]
  • Test results confirmed the improvement of knowledge through online active learning. [+W]
  • Comparative studies found mixed results when comparing online active learning with in-person active learning. [~W]
