Design CPR education as an ongoing, reinforcement-based process combining spaced intervals, feedback, and cognitive components¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 7 studies (4 quant-synthesis, 2 causal, 1 review), q1–q4 · 3 of 7 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends treating CPR education as an ongoing, reinforcement-based process rather than a one-time intervention, integrating "optimized training intervals, feedback mechanisms, and cognitive–behavioral components." It advises future research and training design to combine these components instead of evaluating isolated educational elements, with emphasis on long-term retention and real-world implementation in community settings.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training designs must allow repeated or spaced sessions rather than single-session delivery
  • Feedback mechanisms capable of making compression depth, rate, and recoil visible to learners

Constraints¶

  • Long-term durability of retention effects remains uncertain due to short follow-up periods in many studies

Target Learners¶

  • healthcare professionals
  • school students
  • laypersons

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustained CPR skill retention
  • accurate compression performance

Affordances¶

Claims¶

  • Feedback-based CPR training is associated with more accurate chest compression performance than standard instruction [+W]
  • Rhythm or pacing aids during CPR training were associated with more consistent compression rates [+W]
  • Reflective and cognitively oriented CPR approaches show potential for enhancing long-term skill retention, particularly at follow-up [+W]
  • Spaced CPR training is associated with improved skill retention compared with single-session massed instruction [+W]
  • Spaced Practice Improves Retention [+M]
  • Spacing out retrieval practice produces better long-term retention than massing it [+W]
  • Relearning-to-criterion designs confound distribution of practice with amount of second-session practice [~W]
