# Inclusive virtual course delivery: record everything, subtitle videos, schedule around participants' calendars, monitor participation, and diversify speakers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A set of delivery practices the authors recommend from their Lessons Learnt for virtual research-training courses. They state that "Hence all our activities were recorded and made available on YouTube so that participants could attend at their convenience", and that "we enabled subtitles on our videos" for participants from non-English speaking countries. They further recommend weekend scheduling, constant monitoring of participants via social media and email, and a variety of speakers from students to senior faculty to match participant levels and make the environment more inclusive.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recording infrastructure and a public channel such as YouTube for all activities
- Social media, Telegram and regular email channels for maintaining interaction and feedback
- Careful planning of talk schedules around participants' own classes and examinations
#### Constraints
- Constant monitoring of participants is described as very time consuming as well as difficult
- Internet connectivity problems, power issues and overlapping sessions affected participants

### Target Learners
- School teachers and interested students
- Undergraduate and postgraduate science students, including those from non-English speaking countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained participation in virtual data-analysis training
- Inclusive access to recorded research-training material
