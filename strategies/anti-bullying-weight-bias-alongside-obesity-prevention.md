# Pair school obesity-prevention programs with anti-bullying programs targeting weight-related bias, particularly for girls

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools address the psychosocial pathway linking weight status to achievement alongside prevention itself. Because weight-related teasing and its effects on achievement may be more prevalent among girls, the authors state that "anti‐bullying programs targeting weight‐related bias might be important alongside obesity prevention programs in schools to negate any adverse effect on academic achievement." This strategy pairs curriculum-based obesity prevention with anti-bullying work on weight bias.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools running obesity-prevention programs would also implement anti-bullying programs that target weight-related bias.
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a recommendation based on observed sex differences and cited teasing literature, not as a tested intervention in this study; the study is observational and causality cannot be established.

### Target Learners
- primary school children, particularly girls affected by overweight or obesity

### Target Learning Goals
- protecting academic achievement (NAPLAN literacy and numeracy) from the adverse effects of weight-related teasing and bias
