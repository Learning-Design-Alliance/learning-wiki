# Develop multiple-representation learning and homework materials in which students answer one question using several methods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' proposed response to the observed difficulties: multiple representation learning and homework materials in which "students would be made to answer a single question using different methods (matrix method, Dirac method, Bloch sphere, step-by-step circuit method, and conceptual explanation)" — answering one question using several approaches to master the methods and understand how they relate for solving problems. A sample question is shown in Fig. 9.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials must present a single question answerable by matrix, Dirac, Bloch sphere, step-by-step circuit, and conceptual methods
#### Constraints
- The materials are still under development; the authors plan to study their effectiveness in a future iteration of the qualitative study

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory quantum computing courses

### Target Learning Goals
- mastering multiple solution methods for quantum computing problems
- understanding how the methods are related for solving problems

## Claims

- [Students prefer matrix methods over Dirac notation, with fewer than 40% confident working with Dirac notation after the introductory course](../claims/dirac-notation-low-confidence-prefer-matrix.md) [+W]
