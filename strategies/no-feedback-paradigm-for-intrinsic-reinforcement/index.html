Use tasks without explicit feedback or rewards to study intrinsic reinforcement responses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends investigating intrinsic reinforcement using task paradigms that provide neither performance feedback nor explicit rewards. The authors selected the n-back task because "the n-back paradigm provides neither performance feedback nor explicit rewards, allowing investigation of intrinsic reinforcement responses outside of the context of a classic reward paradigm". This design isolates responses to correct performance itself rather than to externally delivered incentives.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A task difficult enough to generate errors, since robust VS activation to correct responses may only occur in more difficult tasks
  • A large sample, since incorrect trials are few at lower difficulty levels

Constraints¶

  • The n-back task produced few errors at lower levels of working memory load, resulting in few trials and empty cells

Target Learners¶

  • children
  • adolescents
  • young adults

Target Learning Goals¶

  • measuring intrinsic reinforcement and motivation during cognitive tasks

Affordances¶

  • Intrinsic Reinforcement Signals Vs Cognitive Tasks

Claims¶

  • Post-error slowing occurs in the 2-back condition despite absence of feedback [+W]
  • In youth performing an n-back task without feedback, bilateral ventral striatum activates more strongly to correct than incorrect responses [+W]
  • The VS correct-greater-than-incorrect response is present for both target and foil trials and is not a motor confound [+W]
