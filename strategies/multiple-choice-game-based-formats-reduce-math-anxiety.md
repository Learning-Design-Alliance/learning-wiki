# Use multiple-choice or game-based task formats and repeated exposure to reduce math anxiety, especially for high trait-anxious learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on the finding that production paradigms elicit more state math anxiety than decision paradigms, the article recommends task-design choices for educational settings. It states that "Integrating multiple -choice or game -based learning approaches, which have proven effective in typically developing and dyscalculic children 60,64, can reduce cognitive load and anxiety, particularly for those with high trait math anxiety." It further recommends that repeated exposure to mathematical tasks can help reduce anxiety over time, since initial anxiety is higher before students are familiar with a task type.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires attention to the response format of math tasks, which the article identifies as relevant for mitigating or amplifying anxiety during mathematics.
#### Constraints
- The article notes trait math anxiety was associated with performance challenges posed by task complexity but not by task format, so format choices target state anxiety rather than the complexity-related performance effect.

### Target Learners
- students with high trait math anxiety
- typically developing and dyscalculic children

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing state math anxiety during arithmetic
- maintaining arithmetic performance
