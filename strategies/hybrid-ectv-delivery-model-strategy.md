# Adopt a hybrid ECTV delivery model mixing face-to-face and remote modalities across training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that post-pandemic workplace-based assessment frameworks include remote ECTV modalities alongside face-to-face visits, because perceived educational utility did not differ significantly by modality. It states that "A 'hybrid' ECTV delivery model, combining a mixture of face-to-face and remote ECTVs across the course of training, may capitalise on the benefits of both assessment modalities, including resource optimisation, convenience, and varied learning opportunities." Remote inclusion may also allow additional ECTVs without substantive extra resourcing burden.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Remote ECTV rollout must consider and address information technology and logistical/practical issues, which the article notes have been acknowledged as problematic for remote assessment
#### Constraints
- The study's pandemic-era data collection context may affect generalisability of modality-comparability findings
- Potential differences in educational richness between remote formats and face-to-face direct observation were not captured in the present study

### Target Learners
- GP registrars in Australian vocational general practice training
- external clinical teaching visitors

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical competency development through formative direct-observation workplace-based assessment
