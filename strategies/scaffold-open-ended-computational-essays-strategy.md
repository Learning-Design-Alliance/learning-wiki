# Scaffold open-ended computational projects with example essays, starter simulations, and peer presentations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because students found full openness challenging, the article's pilot paired the open brief with concrete supports: an example computational essay, several basic Jupyter simulations students could build on, suggested investigation questions, and help-desk hours. End-of-semester presentations in mock research-group meetings provided collaboration and disciplinary authenticity. The authors plan to expand the project to be mandatory and to collect larger-scale data on "the types of guidelines and supports needed to run this kind of project."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A computation-rich environment where students already have a programming foundation, as in the Oslo physics major.
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a small, self-selected pilot; conclusions are preliminary.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics majors in computation-rich courses

### Target Learning Goals
- creative open-ended investigation
- computational thinking and literacy in physics
