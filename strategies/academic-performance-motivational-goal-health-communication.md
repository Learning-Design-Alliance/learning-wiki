# Use academic performance as a motivational goal expectation in health communication strategies targeting performance-driven groups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that health communication strategies aimed at groups not motivated by health improvement goals instead frame health behaviours around career- and performance-related benefits. They state that "academic performance could be a motivational goal expectation for health behaviour change for use in health communication strategies to reach those groups" who may be more inspired by performance improvements than by reducing disease risk. This strategy rests on the study's finding that sleep and fruit and vegetable consumption are the health behaviours most associated with academic performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Evidence on health behaviours' effects on non-health-related outcomes such as academic performance is necessary to target health communication at less health-conscious groups.
#### Constraints
- The study is cross-sectional and cannot establish causal relationships between health behaviours and academic performance.

### Target Learners
- university students

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance improvement via health behaviour change
