Authors of Literature-in-English texts should state the instructional objectives of each chapter or topic¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that textbook authors print the instructional objectives of each chapter or topic in Literature-in-English texts, so that students can guide their own study. The author cites Mkpa (1989), suggesting that students aware of a unit's instructional objectives can carry on with their studies in the teacher's absence and yield a better result than private study without objectives.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructional objectives must be stated per chapter or topic in the text

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • senior secondary school students studying Literature-in-English

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-directed study of Literature-in-English texts

Claims¶

  • Prior knowledge of topics and instructional objectives before instruction raises Literature-in-English achievement more than conventional teaching [+W]
  • In a quasi-experiment on giving students prior topics and instructional objectives, Literature-in-English achievement did not differ significantly by gender in an ANCOVA across treatment and control students [~W]
  • Treatment-group students scored a mean of 70.12 versus 48.03 for conventionally taught students on the Literature-in-English Achievement Test [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Include the behavioural objectives of each major topic at the beginning of each topic in mathematics textbooks
  • Issue students the topics and instructional objectives a day or two before instruction
