Extend discovery learning across subjects, school levels, adult education and teacher education¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

In its educational implications the article recommends broadening the use of discovery learning beyond the studied setting: 'Discovery learning can be used for learning different subjects and it can be extended to secondary level and higher secondary level.' It further suggests implementation in adult education, teacher education and alternative schools, and that late bloomers can be improved by using it. The recommendation rests on the study's finding of grammar achievement gains for standard V learners.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Activities designed for discovery learning, including diagnosing problems and designing the discovery learning task

Constraints¶

  • The supporting evidence comes from one week of grammar instruction with 60 standard V pupils in a single school

Target Learners¶

  • primary and secondary pupils
  • adult learners
  • pre-service teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • subject-matter learning across the curriculum
  • English grammar competency

Claims¶

  • Discovery learning strategies produce significant achievement gains in English grammar for standard V learners compared with conventional methods [+W]
  • Meta-analytic evidence cited in the article: enhanced discovery learning outperforms other instruction, while unassisted discovery does not benefit learners [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Extend co-operative grammar learning to other levels, subjects and languages
  • Extend CREACT practices across subjects and provide teacher training in the technique
  • Re-arrange and extend the web-assisted flipped-classroom activity materials to other science subjects and disciplines
  • Extend hybrid learning to teachers at all levels and to adult education
