# Use multiple exemplars with scaffolded discussion rather than mere provision, balancing teacher guidance against student co-construction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The paper recommends providing multiple, different exemplars discussed with peers and teachers to uncover the range of quality indicators, rather than merely uploading exemplars with little discussion. It draws on Orsmond et al. (2002), who found discussion of a range of exemplars without revelation of grades aided understanding of quality, and To and Carless's dialogic approach, while noting teachers may need to reserve their own evaluative judgement so students co-construct understandings of quality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Exemplars require commentary for students to use them to full advantage; tasks should be authentic so exemplars represent quality broadly in the discipline
#### Constraints
- Students have been shown to value teacher explanation more than their own interactions with exemplars, so a delicate balance of scaffolding without explicit telling is needed

### Target Learners
- higher education students

### Target Learning Goals
- noticing and articulating quality indicators in disciplinary work

### Affordances
- Evaluative Judgement Capability Definition
