# Coupled Model: pairing interactive simulations with real-time, open-format, scaffolded formative assessment on one device per student

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Coupled Model has each student use a single pen-enabled mobile device to run an interactive simulation before or during class and respond to open-format InkSurvey questions in real time. The instructor uses instantaneous responses to provide "timely scaffolded questioning and feedback", guiding students toward more mature understanding while they actively engage with content. The article reports this implementation is economically feasible using institution-provided or student-owned devices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Skillful instructor guidance while students use simulations, and careful monitoring as students draw conclusions; open-format (not multiple-choice) questions so higher levels of thinking can be probed.
#### Constraints
- Earlier attempts to gather real-time formative assessment to open-format questions on a meaningful time scale were described as a daunting task; the article's gains evidence comes from undergraduate engineering physics students with three semesters of physics and calculus.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate STEM students in engineering physics

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of abstract physics and statistics concepts such as phasors in circuits, Fourier analysis, diffraction, standard deviation of the mean, and least squares
