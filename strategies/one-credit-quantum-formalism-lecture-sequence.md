# Ten-lecture one-credit course sequence introducing quantum formalism through vectors, Dot-Vec notation, and Bloch cubes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article lays out a lecture sequence for a one-credit course, from vectors and unit vectors in two dimensions, through Dot products and Dot-Vec notation, Dirac notation analogy, Bloch cube state representation, Born rule, and quantum dynamics, to "Proving that unitary operators are represented by Bloch cube rotation" and a final assessment. The level of detail "can be expanded or compressed depending on the amount of time available". In the pilot course the only assessment was a midterm test, with no final exam and no out-of-class assignments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students enrolled with roughly one semester of introductory physics; the course was embedded in the "First Experiences in Quantum" (FEQ) program with research-group project credits.
#### Constraints
- Because grades were assigned by the research advisor, the course used only a midterm test as assessment; the article does not report learning outcome measures.

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduates in a research-exposure program

### Target Learning Goals
- learn Dirac notation and quantum postulates before the standard junior-level sequence

### Affordances
- Dot Vec Notation Bridge To Dirac
- Bloch Cube Manipulative
