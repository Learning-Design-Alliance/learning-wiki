# Deliver intervention programs within the educational context to improve healthy dietary and physical activity habits of adolescents

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools deliver intervention programs targeting healthy eating and physical activity, on the grounds that these behaviours related to motivation and learning strategies in the study. It concludes that "intervention programs within the educational context are recommended to improve healthy habits", and states that programs targeting these behaviours "should be a key priority for health and productivity" in older children and adolescents.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must target both healthy dietary habits and physical activity within the educational context, as the article recommends.
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional and correlational; the authors state the design precludes causal conclusions and call for an intervention program to verify the combined effect of MD and PA on cognition and academic performance.

### Target Learners
- Adolescents aged 14–16 in secondary education

### Target Learning Goals
- Healthy dietary and physical activity habits supporting motivation, learning strategies and academic achievement
