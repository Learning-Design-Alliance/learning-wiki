# Introduce tablet video analysis with one training lesson and prioritize it for reference-system topics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that teachers use tablet-PC video analysis in their own lessons because implementation required "little training required for the participating teachers and students" and "only one lesson has to be invested for the introduction of the tool". They further advise that augmentation with MERs is particularly conducive to understanding uniform motion relative to a reference system, so this should be considered when planning experimental learning environments on the topic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standardized introductory lesson covering the measurement methodology and application, including practice with sample videos unrelated to the topic
#### Constraints
- Positive effects were demonstrated only for a specific lesson scenario on uniform motion over four lessons; long-term use and more complex topics remain untested

### Target Learners
- secondary-school physics students in advanced courses

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of uniform motion and reference systems
