# Require students who fail by the fifth attempt to study related content before additional attempts, and grade numerical problems on written solutions with partial credit

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's discussion proposes concrete design moves for multi-attempt asynchronous assessments. For conceptual problems, "Students who failed on the fifth attempt should be required to study related content before being allowed to take additional attempts". For numerical problems, the article proposes mitigating negative impacts "by grading on students' written solution and assigning partial credit", and reducing extraneous quiz factors by requiring students to solve more than one numerical problem drawn from the problem bank.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A multi-attempt asynchronous assessment built on isomorphic problem banks, with a defined attempt limit
#### Constraints
- The five-attempt replacement recommendation is supported only for conceptual problems; numerical problems showed weaker association beyond two attempts

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals

- conservation of mechanical energy

- [In a Fall 2023 physics course, 49% of quiz attempters passed within seven attempts, over 90% of passers did so within five attempts, and exam accuracy ranged from 30% to 61% across problem types](../claims/quiz-pass-and-exam-accuracy-descriptives.md) [+W]
