# Use the analog classical computer thought experiment with explicit floating-point discussion to teach the quantum-classical distinction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that quantum computing educators use the analog classical computer model — a classical machine with continuously varying voltages between 0 V and 1 V — as an intermediate frame of reference, coupled with "an explicit discussion of ﬂoating point arithmetic as a way to efﬁciently represent continuou s quantities digitally to high precision." The authors state they "believe" this combination "will be a valuable t ool moving forward for quantum computing educators." In interviews, confronting students with the analog classical computer prompted shifts to entanglement-based explanations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An interviewer or instructor to pose the thought experiment and tailor follow-up questions to elicit cognitive conﬂict
#### Constraints
- The thought experiment was used in only some interviews, spontaneously in one graduate interview, and its instructional value has not been tested in classroom settings

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students in interdisciplinary quantum information science courses

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguishing truly quantum phenomena (entanglement, superposition) from analog-digital distinctions
