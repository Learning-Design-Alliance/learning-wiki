# Formally integrate intensive simulation-based surgical education into ophthalmology residency programmes as a complement to conventional teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that intensive simulation-based surgical education be formally built into residency structures because it produced rapid, sustained competency gains in a randomized trial. The authors argue SBSE "offers a safer alternative for junior surgeons to refine their skills in the absence of patient harm" and that formal integration "may result in better standards of surgical training". They also recommend SBSE complement rather than replace traditional surgical teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires validated competency assessment tools (the trial used the Sim-OSSCAR rubric with masked expert graders) and access to simulation materials including high-fidelity synthetic eyes
#### Constraints
- The article states transfer of surgical skills to the operating room can vary widely depending on the type and amount of simulation training received, and live-skill transfer could not be evaluated in this study

### Target Learners
- resident ophthalmologists with zero surgical trabeculectomy experience

### Target Learning Goals
- core trabeculectomy microsurgical skills such as scleral flap formation, releasable suturing and conjunctival suturing
