# Highlight measurement-uncertainty concepts in data collection through justified data-collection decisions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that data collection showed the lowest set-like reasoning and a shift toward N coding, the authors recommend that instructors highlight uncertainty concepts specifically in the context of data collection. Their concrete suggestion is to "provide students with opportunities to make and justify decisions about whether or not to collect additional data for a given measurement". They also state the results "support maintaining a focus on data comparison throughout the course", where the largest expert-like shifts were observed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Baselines from pre-post PMQ analysis identifying which aspects (data collection vs. comparison) need improvement
#### Constraints
- Grounded in point-set paradigm analysis, which does not address systematic uncertainty; the RD finding needs more qualitative verification due to the large fraction of N-coded responses

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in introductory physics lab courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Set-like (expert-like) reasoning about measurement uncertainty in data collection and data comparison
