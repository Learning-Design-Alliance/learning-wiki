# Introduce VR simulation during the preclinical phase of dental education, before students perform procedures on patients

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that simulation-based training with VR platforms "may be most beneficial when introduced during the preclinical phase of dental education—before students perform procedures on patients," supporting foundational psychomotor and cognitive skills in a low-risk, controlled environment. VR provides a standardized, immersive, and repeatable environment with immediate, objective feedback that reduces instructor burden, which the authors note is particularly beneficial in large cohort settings or institutions with limited faculty resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a VR simulator with haptic feedback and automated scoring, integrated into the preclinical curriculum before students begin clinical treatment of patients
#### Constraints
- The article states that the optimal timing, frequency, and curricular integration of VR simulation remain areas for further investigation

### Target Learners
- preclinical dental students in large cohort settings or institutions with limited faculty resources

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational psychomotor and cognitive skills for minimally invasive endodontic procedures
