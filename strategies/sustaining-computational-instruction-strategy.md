# Sustain computational instruction with flipped homework while replacing the faculty-intensive final project with peer review or student videos

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors report that faculty teaching both semesters of classical mechanics adopted the flipped instructional model and computational homework problems, but the computational final project was not sustained because it demands significant faculty time in a large-enrollment course. They suggest alternatives: "Other faculty might consider using peer-review, that is, having students grade each other using a modified version of our rubric," or having students create short videos rather than written reports.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty adoption of the flipped model and computational homework; for the final project, either smaller courses, more instructional time, peer grading with a modified rubric, or video reports
#### Constraints
- The final project caused one full week of content (separation of variables in spherical coordinates) to be dropped from the course; local conditions at other institutions will determine which aspects are used, adapted, or discarded

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics majors in middle-division lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained engagement with computational modeling across the course sequence
