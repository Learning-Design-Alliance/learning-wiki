# Show the instructional video immediately before the procedural assessment to ensure just-in-time use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study ensured the video functioned as a just-in-time resource rather than remote pre-reading: "Regardless of response, all participants were shown the video before proceeding with the same procedural assessment on the same infant LP model as performed at baseline". This design choice guarantees the video is viewed at the moment of performance, matching the JIT intent, and the authors suggest similar JIT tools can support rare procedures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A brief video based on a validated procedural checklist
- A simulator or clinical context where the procedure can be performed immediately after viewing
#### Constraints
- The authors note it is unclear to what degree attending physicians supervising trainees utilized the video as a JIT tool as intended

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine interns and junior residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural efficiency and competence in rarely performed procedures

## Claims

- [In one uncontrolled pre-post study, median LP completion time among EM interns fell from 12.9 to 10.3 minutes after viewing a brief just-in-time instructional video](../claims/jit-video-reduced-lp-completion-time.md) [+W]
- [Median major checklist steps missed on infant LP fell from 5 to 1 after the just-in-time video intervention](../claims/jit-video-reduced-missed-lp-steps.md) [+W]
- [Among individual major LP steps, completion of slow and smooth needle advancement (step 7.1) rose from 44% before to 100% after the intervention in one group measured before and after, with no comparison group](../claims/step-7-1-needle-advancement-significant-improvement.md) [+W]
