# Four domains of peer-review skill that can and should be taught to prospective reviewers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Arguing that the author skillset is not necessarily the reviewer skillset, the authors propose four teachable domains: "structure of the reviewer's comments"; "characteristics of reviewers' comments", including clarity, constructiveness, detail/thoroughness, fairness, knowledgeability and tone; "timeliness of the review report"; and "usefulness of the review report to editorial decision making and manuscript improvement". The proposal rests on a methodological systematic review identifying nine peer-review quality domains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Research on the actual content of peer reviewers' reports is needed to establish educational needs for peer reviewing
#### Constraints
- Existing educational interventions have shown underwhelming results and poor methodological quality, and such training is not commonly included in biomedical postgraduate education

### Target Learners
- prospective peer reviewers
- junior researchers and clinicians

### Target Learning Goals
- writing high-quality peer-review reports
