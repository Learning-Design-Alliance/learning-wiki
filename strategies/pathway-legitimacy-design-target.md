# Treat pathway legitimacy as a design target: ensure alternative routes are perceived as legitimate, usable, and socially recognized

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing multiple legitimate routes to valued goals, including non-linear timelines and supports, and attending to whether learners perceive those routes as legitimate in practice. It notes that where UDL specifies multiple means of action and expression as a design principle, possibility mindset tracks whether learners perceive those routes as legitimate, usable, and socially recognized. Without such beliefs, even well-designed UDL environments may fail to sustain engagement among students who have learned that alternative pathways are nominal or penalized.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Alternative pathways must be real, visible, and respected in practice, not merely available on paper; accommodation follow-through must be reliable across instructors and semesters
#### Constraints
- A single successful adjustment does not necessarily widen perceived possibility if the next instructor ignores the same plan or policies fail at implementation

### Target Learners
- Disabled and neurodivergent learners in K-12 through higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Persistence through route switching, delayed timelines, and alternative demonstrations of competence

### Affordances
- Possibility Mindset Three Belief Dimensions
