# Embed idioms in contextualized short stories matched to learners' proficiency level as supplementary home reading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that what is useful for students learning idiomatic expressions is "using contexts containing idioms compatible with the students' level of proficiency in L2". In the study, the teacher wrote idioms on the board and gave examples in class, then gave only the experimental group copies of supplementary stories containing the same idioms to study at home. The strategy treats context as central to constructing idiomatic meaning rather than teaching idioms as isolated items.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stories must contain the same idioms taught in class
- Idiom contexts must be compatible with learners' proficiency level
- Learners need repeated sessions of idiom instruction alongside the readings
#### Constraints
- Neither group in the study had access to the original coursebook or its exercises, so the readings were the only extended contextual exposure

### Target Learners
- lower-intermediate L2 learners
- EFL learners in private language institutes

### Target Learning Goals
- idiom learning
- conceptual fluency
- communicative competence in the target culture

### Affordances
- Conceptual Fluency Theory Danesi

## Claims

- [Providing lower-intermediate L2 learners with supplementary readings (short stories containing idioms) produces a statistically significant post-test advantage in conceptual fluency over idiom instruction alone](../claims/supplementary-readings-improve-conceptual-fluency.md) [+W]
