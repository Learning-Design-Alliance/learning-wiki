# Promoting Self-Directed Learning Strategies

## Description
Promoting self-directed learning involves explicitly teaching students strategies for problem-solving, metacognition, and self-reflection to become independent learners. This approach includes providing opportunities to practice these strategies and reflect on their effectiveness. Teachers help students name their processes and give them the language to match what they did, providing them with strategies they can use in future struggles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit instruction in problem-solving, metacognitive, and self-reflection strategies; opportunities for practice and feedback; and resources to support students in reflecting on their learning processes.
#### Constraints
- Requires time and effort from both students and teachers to develop and implement effective strategies. Students may initially resist taking responsibility for their learning or struggle to articulate their thought processes.

### Affordances
- Empowers students to take control of their learning, provides them with tools and strategies to overcome challenges, and promotes a deeper understanding of their learning processes.

### Personalization
- Teachers can tailor the strategies to meet individual student needs and provide personalized feedback to help students refine their approaches. Language prompts and strategy lists can be co-authored with students to ensure relevance and engagement.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe students' ability to apply strategies to solve problems, their level of metacognitive awareness, and their capacity for self-reflection. Measure students' independence and self-direction in their learning.

## Impact
- Research indicates that self-directed learning strategies lead to improved problem-solving skills, metacognitive awareness, and academic outcomes. Students become more engaged, motivated, and successful in their learning.

## Examples
- Teachers provide math problems to make students feel 'stuck', then prompt them to name their processes and strategies for getting 'unstuck'. Lists of strategies and language prompts are co-authored with students to support their self-talk and communication with partners.
