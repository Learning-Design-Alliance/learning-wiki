# Institutional policies allocating dedicated teaching time, formal recognition and comprehensive preceptor training to strengthen clinical teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 associational, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that institutions support preceptors through structural changes: protected time for teaching, formal acknowledgement of educational contributions, and training in clinical teaching, feedback and student support. They argue this addresses the workload constraints that limited personalised support in their data. As they state, "institutions should implement policies that allocate dedicated teaching time, formally acknowledge preceptors' educational contributions and provide comprehensive preceptor training". The recommendation arises directly from the divergence findings of this study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Preceptors must be given dedicated, protected teaching time within clinical workloads
- Institutions must formally acknowledge preceptors' educational contributions
#### Constraints
- Findings are based on a single institutional context, which may limit generalisability to other nursing education settings

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students in clinical practicum

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical competence and confidence
- professional identity development
- effective feedback and student support
