Train teachers and develop university courses on VTM as a student-centred methodology¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on their findings, the authors recommend holding training workshops for teachers on the preparation, activation, and implementation of VTM as a student-centred methodology, and developing university courses that teach student-centred methods such as VTM so prospective teachers are ready to use them. They also recommend disseminating the VTM experience across Jordanian governorates and conducting further quasi-experimental studies on other Arabic language skills such as reading, writing, anthem, and exercises.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Policy makers in the Ministry of Education supporting curricula reform focused on student-centred methodologies

Constraints¶

  • The authors note it remains untested whether VTM works better than other student-centred methodologies

Target Learners¶

  • third-grade students in Jordanian public and private schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Arabic language oral skills and other language skills

Claims¶

  • Teaching Arabic oral skills with a Virtual Trip Model produces significantly higher post-test achievement than traditional teaching among third graders [+M]
  • No significant gender difference in Arabic oral skills gains when both groups are taught with VTM or traditional methods [~W]
  • No significant interaction between teaching method and gender on Arabic oral skills achievement [~W]
