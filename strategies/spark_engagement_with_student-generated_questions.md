# Spark Engagement with Student-Generated Questions

## Description
Students formulate questions and investigate topics they are curious about or consider important. This method encourages inquiry and investment in the learning process by allowing students to drive their own learning, and it contrasts with traditional, teacher-directed instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires creating a classroom environment that values student questions and exploration. Demands flexibility in curriculum planning to accommodate student interests.
#### Constraints
- May require additional time and resources to support diverse student interests. Can be challenging to manage if student questions are misaligned with curriculum goals.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- K-12 learners, particularly beneficial for students of color who may experience disempowerment in traditional educational settings.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Increase student engagement and motivation, foster ownership of learning, encourage critical thinking, address student interests and needs.

### Affordances
- Empowers students by valuing their curiosity and questions. Liberates students by allowing them to determine their own learning paths. Fosters engagement by addressing topics that are personally meaningful to students.

### Personalization
- Allows students to choose topics and questions that align with their interests and cultural backgrounds. Adaptable to various subjects and learning levels.

## Assessment Evidence
- Evaluate the depth and quality of student-generated questions. Assess student engagement and participation in inquiry-based activities. Review student projects and presentations for evidence of learning and understanding.

## Impact
- Projects driven by student-generated questions lead to increased engagement and investment in the inquiry process, helping to create more equitable learning environments.
