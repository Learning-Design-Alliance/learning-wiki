# Use sequenced instructional materials, collaborative writing, and guided peer interaction to support persuasive writing in online and blended environments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The article's pedagogical recommendation is that "the findings highlight the value of sequenced instructional materials, collaborative writing, and guided peer interaction in supporting students' engagement with persuasive writing in online and blended learning environments." This strategy combines asynchronous materials on an LMS, synchronous live sessions, group drafting in shared documents, and rubric-structured peer exchange. It is grounded in the study's phenomenological findings rather than experimental evidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured weekly sequence of topics and materials
- Collaborative tools such as Google Docs
- A rubric or checklist to guide peer feedback
- Instructor moderation during feedback sessions
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from a single well-resourced private university context with eight participants

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduates in ESL/EMI higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Persuasive essay writing
- Collaborative writing skills
- Peer evaluation skills
