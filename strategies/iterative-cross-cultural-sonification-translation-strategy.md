# Translate a sonification-based educational resource through iterative collaboration between scientists and local musicians

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's translation process proceeded by identifying the most musical sonifications (musifications) for redesign, recording local Trinidadian performers on local instruments, and iterating with musicians until the rhythms could be spatially tracked without aliasing. The authors describe that "This naturally led to an iterative design process with multiple interactions between the scientists and musicians involved," with criteria that the result be both musically satisfying and data driven.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to local musicians and recorded performances
- Multiple iterations between the science team and musicians to resolve conflicts between rhythm and spatial tracking
#### Constraints
- Emphasis on rhythms conflicted with the original pitch- and timbre-based design; rhythmic hits had to avoid undesirable spatial aliasing during silent periods between hits

### Target Learners
- Audiences from the target musical culture, here English-speaking Caribbean learners and families

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining the original show's data-driven communication of astronomical concepts while making it culturally relatable
