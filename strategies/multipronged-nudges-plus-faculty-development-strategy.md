# Combine low-cost form-based nudges with faculty development and assessor feedback in multipronged approaches to improve assessment report quality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends deliberately combining feasible, low-cost nudges—such as restructuring assessment forms—with more resource-intensive faculty development, since no single intervention has yet produced the desired quality gains. It also suggests exploring other nudge-theory-aligned options: "Providing assessors with feedback has already been proven effective" and peer-performance information using social norms could be explored. Each intervention may have small effects alone, but combining them could meaningfully improve feedback and decision-making.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty development initiatives and assessor feedback mechanisms to complement form changes
- Assessment forms whose structure can be modified (comment box placement, dedicated recommendation boxes)
#### Constraints
- The article acknowledges that providing information alone is insufficient to change behaviour
- High-stakes uses of ITARs in residency applications could discourage supervisors from documenting areas for improvement despite nudges

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in clinical clerkship placements

### Target Learning Goals
- quality of workplace-based assessment feedback
- assessment for learning and decision-making in competency-based medical education
