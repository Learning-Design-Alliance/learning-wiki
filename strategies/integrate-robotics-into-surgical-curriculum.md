# Integrate robotic surgery training into the education of young professionals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend establishing robotic techniques in students' education because novices demonstrated a steep learning curve and strong interest in robotic technologies. They argue that "robotic training should be integrated into the curriculum if necessary," that students should train on simulators until confident before operating on patients, and that progress can be objectively checked. The recommendation rests on their finding that inexperienced students can learn robotic microsurgical skills rapidly despite initially slower operation times.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a robotic system such as the Symani for training
- Extensive training on the robot to fully utilize its advantages, per the authors
- Detailed surgical simulations repeated continuously before operating on humans
#### Constraints
- The authors note implementing training situations in the clinical daily routine is very expensive, and current systems carry high costs and longer set-up times

### Target Learners
- medical students
- young professionals and inexperienced surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- robotic microsurgical skills
- career preparation in robotic surgery and microsurgery
