# Add antimicrobial prescribing training to the medical curriculum before workforce entry

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that antimicrobial stewardship efforts "include the provision of additional training in antimicrobial prescribing to the future medical workforce". The rationale is that prescribing habits formed after graduation are difficult to change, so graduates should be equipped with adequate antimicrobial prescribing knowledge and skills "prior to their entry into the medical workforce". The survey findings of low ID confidence and knowledge motivate this recommendation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curriculum space and teaching capacity in infectious diseases and antimicrobial prescribing during the medical degree
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a voluntary cross-sectional survey that the authors note may over-represent academically engaged students

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students
- medical graduates entering the workforce

### Target Learning Goals
- appropriate antimicrobial prescribing
