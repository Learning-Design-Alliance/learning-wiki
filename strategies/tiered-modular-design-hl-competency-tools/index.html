Adopt a tiered or modular design for future competency measurement tools¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

To balance the need for standardization and adaptability, the review recommends that future tool development adopt a tiered or modular design, "incorporating core domains while allowing for flexibility and contextual adaptation". The authors suggest this could improve feasibility and use in health professional education, addressing the tension between validated standardized tools and context-specific needs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Core competency domains must be identified to anchor the modular structure

Constraints¶

  • Intended to balance standardization with the need for tools to be adaptable to specific contexts and cultural or geographical differences

Target Learners¶

  • Health professionals across diverse disciplines and settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Health literacy competency assessment

Claims¶

  • Heavy emphasis on knowledge assessment may limit measurement of complex competencies such as advocacy and system-level engagement [+W]
  • Only 16% of validated measures examined construct validity; none tested criterion validity [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Establish a centralized repository of health literacy competency measures
  • Open design directions for adaptive authoring: crowdsourced standards, individual differences, modular customization, path visualization, and instructor real-time control
  • Design GenAI tools with modular offloading control and meta-cognition support for learning contexts
  • Use a modular lesson structure to keep integrated AI curricula scalable under time constraints
