# Real-time HRV monitoring with individual thresholds to trigger expert support and prevent work-overload events

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend developing physiological workload monitoring in clinical and simulated procedural settings: "Future research needs to concentrate on the real-time analysis of heart rate variability and the definition of individual thresholds allowing a remote monitoring to trigger expert support and impede adverse events caused by work- overload". This strategy treats HRV as a workload correlate detectable before harmful overload, with thresholds calibrated between beginner and advanced practitioners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Thresholds must be defined between beginners and advanced practitioners to minimize false alarms during challenging situations
#### Constraints
- The article states transferability of its simulator results to real-life circumstances is limited, since workload levels could be considerably higher once real patients are involved

### Target Learners
- practitioners performing interventional procedures in training and practice

### Target Learning Goals
- managing cognitive workload during procedural skill acquisition and preventing overload-related adverse events

- Neurovisceral Integration Model Hrv Expertise
