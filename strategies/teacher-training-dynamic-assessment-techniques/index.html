Prepare teachers and school support specialists in the balanced use of static and dynamic assessment within teacher training programs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article argues that dynamic assessment is slowly embraced because "teachers are still unfamiliar with dynamic techniques and procedures", having remained in the care of diagnosis specialists. It endorses Lidz's earlier call for training both teachers and school psychologists in dynamic techniques for classroom as well as cognitive assessment, within teacher training institutions and programs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher training institutions and programs willing to include dynamic assessment content

Constraints¶

  • Practitioners have viewed these procedures as time consumers and therefore dispensable

Target Learners¶

  • teachers and school support specialists in training

Target Learning Goals¶

  • classroom use of dynamic assessment techniques to improve school performance and achievement

Claims¶

  • Exposure to dynamic assessment and potential giftedness scores each correlate significantly with posttest math scores [+W]
  • Dynamic assessment procedures produce significant effects on mathematics achievement among elementary school students [+W]
  • Potential giftedness, measured by teacher and parent nomination scores, enhances the effect of dynamic assessment on math achievement [+W]
  • Static individual assessment misses developing functions; ZPD-based assessment reveals potential competencies [+W]
  • Dynamic assessment reflects young children's learning potential more accurately than static tests, especially with minority and learning disabled children [+W]
  • The number of prompts children needed during training predicted mathematics and technical reading achievement, while static pre- and post-test scores did not [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use dynamic testing with graduated prompts scenarios as part of psycho-educational assessment to tailor instruction to individual needs
