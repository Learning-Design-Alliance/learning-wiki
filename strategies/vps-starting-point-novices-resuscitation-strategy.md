# Use VPS as a reasonable starting point for novices learning sepsis and trauma resuscitation, with attention to engagement and satisfaction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend VPS as an initial, scalable training modality for novices: "Our findings suggest that VPS is a reasonable starting point for novices for sepsis and trauma resuscitation, producing similar learning outcomes to IPS, with the bonus of potential time savings." They caution that "Learner time-on-task does not automatically follow VPS implementation" and suggest frameworks such as activity theory or motivational theories to understand engagement drivers educators can influence. They also propose exploring sequential integration of VPS followed by IPS in a simulation curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Attention to design and implementation to foster learner engagement and satisfaction, since time-on-task does not follow automatically from VPS availability
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a pilot RCT in one Singapore emergency department with a disproportionately male sample awaiting mandatory military service; generalizability may be limited

### Target Learners
- Novice junior doctors on emergency department rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- Sepsis and trauma resuscitation skills
