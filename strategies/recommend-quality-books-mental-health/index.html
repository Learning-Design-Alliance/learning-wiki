Recommend students read quality books as one possible way to improve mental health¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In the Discussion the authors state that "One possible way to improve mental health for students may be recommend students to read quality books", positioning school counselors as key actors since improving students' mental health is a key concern for them. They also call for future studies on the effectiveness of reading quality books on students' other psychological characteristics, follow-up studies on durability of self-esteem gains, and studies at different educational levels.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Books must be quality works appropriate to students' developmental level

Constraints¶

  • The durability of self-esteem development over time is uncertain and requires follow-up research

Target Learners¶

  • eighth-grade students
  • secondary school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mental health
  • self-esteem
  • reading comprehension

Claims¶

  • The authors attribute the control group's flat self-esteem partly to classic works being inappropriate for students' developmental and age levels [+W]
  • Before the intervention, experimental and control groups showed no significant difference in self-esteem pre-test scores, though descriptively favoring the control group [+W]
  • A 10-week reading program combining quality children's books and metacognitive strategy instruction raised eighth-grade students' self-esteem more than typical non-quality books [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • School counselors implementing cross-age peer mentoring as a research-based school-climate intervention in rural, high-poverty schools
