# Pair simulation-based communication practice with multisource assessment and debriefing involving medical and non-medical evaluators

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends including multisource assessment in simulation curricula because it "allow[ed] for the representation of different perspectives on communication skills, both in the assessment and debriefing process." Peer assessment let observing residents engage and become familiar with the target skills, and standardized-patient feedback in debriefing provided formative input rarely available in clinical settings. Debriefing occurred both after each scenario and at the end of the half-day course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rater-training for all assessors
- Assessment tool language consisting of non-medical terms to engage all assessors
- Consistent debriefing methods supported by pre-assigned readings and yearly faculty debriefing sessions
#### Constraints
- Ratings from medical and non-medical observers diverged over time, and the authors state further research is needed on how best to combine multisource assessment scores

### Target Learners
- OTL-HNS residents (PGY 1–5)

### Target Learning Goals
- Communication skills
- Giving and receiving formative feedback

### Affordances
- Otl Hns Simulation Multisource Communication Curriculum
