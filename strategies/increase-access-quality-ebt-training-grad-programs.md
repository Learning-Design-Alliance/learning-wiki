# Graduate training programs should emphasize increasing access to quality training in EBT strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their finding that most graduate students value EBT but few implement it and most perceive inadequate instructional support, the authors conclude that "graduate training programs should emphasize increasing access to quality training in EBT strategies." They further draw on the change literature to recommend intensive and ongoing training rather than one-time workshops, and suggest institutions "capitalize on these potential change-makers by engaging graduate students in institutional efforts to build a supportive climate for teaching."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must go beyond one-time boot camps; the article cites evidence that successful strategies lasted at least 4 weeks and often longer
#### Constraints
- The article notes that even term-long TA training courses may be insufficient in duration to incite long-term change

### Target Learners
- life science graduate students
- graduate teaching assistants

### Target Learning Goals
- developing instructional skills and fluency with evidence-based teaching strategies
