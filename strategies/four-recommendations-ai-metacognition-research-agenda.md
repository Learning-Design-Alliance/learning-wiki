# Four-part research agenda: longitudinal studies, hybrid frameworks, ethical frameworks, and underrepresented STEM domains

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review's conclusions propose four directions for advancing AI–metacognition research in STEM. First, "longitudinal and experimental studies should be prioritised to examine the long-term effects of AI-supported metacognitive interventions." Second, hybrid and posthumanist frameworks should be expanded to understand how human and machine cognition co-construct learning. Third, explicit ethical frameworks are required to ensure transparency, fairness, and accountability in AI-metacognitive systems. Fourth, future research should extend to underrepresented STEM domains—technology, engineering, and early childhood education—to promote inclusivity and equity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Few reviewed studies employed longitudinal or experimental designs capable of identifying causal or sustained effects, motivating the first recommendation
#### Constraints
- Research remains uneven across STEM domains, with limited representation in technology and engineering education as well as early-childhood and teacher-education contexts

### Target Learners
- STEM learners in underrepresented domains including technology, engineering, and early childhood education

### Target Learning Goals
- AI-mediated metacognitive development
