# Prioritise children vulnerable to falling behind in early and middle grades for educational interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educational interventions to promote good primary school progression should consider the trajectories identified in the study, noting when children begin to fall behind. It states that "Interventions should prioritise children whose parents are illiterate, children from low-income families, and children whose parents are subsistence farmers, as these children are more vulnerable to falling behind in the early and middle grades." This is a forward-looking recommendation grounded in the study's trajectory and sociodemographic findings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires longitudinal schooling data or monitoring capable of identifying when children begin to fall behind in grade progression.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is grounded in one cohort in rural Malawi; the article calls for more studies in sub-Saharan Africa to broaden understanding.

### Target Learners
- Primary-school children aged 6–13 in rural Malawi, especially children of illiterate parents, low-income families, and subsistence farmers

### Target Learning Goals
- Timely grade progression and prevention of falling behind in early and middle primary grades
