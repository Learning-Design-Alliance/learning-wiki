# Preliminary steps for introducing One Health into medical curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends starting points that fit constrained curricula: incorporate One Health principles into zoonotic disease teaching during early infectious disease coursework, add animal contact histories to clinical interviewing instruction using standardized patients and history checklists, and create clinical electives at zoos and other facilities. It notes "Clinical electives for interested medical students to explore One Health approaches have been developed at zoos and other facilities near medical schools [45] and show promise for reinforcing One Health concepts during clinical training."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stress clinical rather than population-level applications since most students plan clinical careers
#### Constraints
- Medical curricula are fully packed; standardized board examinations constrain content

### Target Learners
- medical students
- medical educators

### Target Learning Goals
- One Health principles in zoonotic disease training
- animal contact history taking
- experiential One Health learning
