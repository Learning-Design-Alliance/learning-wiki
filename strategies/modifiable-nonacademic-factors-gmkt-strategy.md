# Improve GMKT performance by strengthening favorable and weakening or compensating unfavorable modifiable nonacademic factors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that medical schools use the identified nonacademic predictors to design interventions for the GMKT, aiming at "strengthening favorable and weakening, rectifying, or compensating unfavorable factors that are modifiable". Concretely, the article names weight loss for high-BMI students, pertinent psychological courses targeting personality- and anxiety-related processes, and working memory span training as candidate avenues. The strategy is framed as helping medical students transition to qualified medical practitioners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of which predictors are modifiable for a given student; the article notes some factors such as father's education are not modifiable
#### Constraints
- Findings are retrospective associations that the authors state should be verified by prospective studies; nonacademic factors were assessed before GMKT in some participants and after in others, which may bias results

### Target Learners
- Medical students, graduate medical students, and residents in China preparing for the NMLE

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving GMKT performance, i.e., the ability to apply medical knowledge to clinical practice
