Use time spent on difficult test items as a source of information about students' social-emotional needs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends considering response process metadata as a source of information on social-emotional constructs. Because "the amount of time students spend on very difficult items is highly correlated with motivation and self-efficacy," educators and researchers using computer-based tests may examine difficult-item durations, after accounting for item difficulty and achievement, to help understand students' social emotional needs in math.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Computer-based tests that log item response times
  • Accounting for item difficulty and estimates of true achievement when interpreting durations

Constraints¶

  • Mean item durations across items generally do not show a strong relationship with these constructs, so the signal is specific to very difficult items

Target Learners¶

  • Students taking computer-based math assessments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Identifying students' academic motivation and self-efficacy needs
