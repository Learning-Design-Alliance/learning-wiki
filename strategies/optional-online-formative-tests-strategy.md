# Offer computer-based formative MCQ tests with automated feedback as an optional addition to pre-clinical integrated medical curricula, particularly for students with higher academic ability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends exploring repeated online formative MCQ tests with automated feedback as an optional curriculum component in pre-clinical integrated medical programs. It states: "Use of computer-based formative test with automated feedback can be explored as an optional addition to the curriculum of pre-clinical integrated medical program to improve performance of the students with higher academic ability." The recommendation rests on the higher Band 1 summative means and the positive correlation in the online group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online tests delivered via a learning portal (Moodle) with feedback constructed by content-lecturers and entered into the quiz software
- Content mapping and item analysis to ensure content validity and reliability
#### Constraints
- The article notes lack of incentives and absence of a lecturer-controlled environment may produce negative motivation; voluntary participation without transcript weight limited impact

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate medical students in pre-clinical integrated system modules

### Target Learning Goals
- improving summative assessment performance in integrated body-system modules

## Claims

- [Band 1 students with higher entry qualification in the online formative test group showed higher mean summative scores than similar-band students in the paper-based group](../claims/band1-online-group-higher-summative.md) [+W]
- [Mean formative test scores were significantly higher in the online group than the paper-based group (t(165) = 5.334, p < 0.05)](../claims/formative-scores-higher-online-group.md) [+M]
- [Gender patterns diverged across formats: male students scored higher in the online group while female students scored higher in the paper-based group on the summative assessment](../claims/gender-divergence-summative-format.md) [~W]
- [Repeated online formative testing with automated feedback produced no significant difference in summative MCQ scores compared with single paper-based formative testing with face-to-face feedback](../claims/online-formative-no-summative-difference.md) [~M]
- [Online formative test scores correlated positively and significantly with summative scores in the online group (r2 = +0.328, p = 0.008)](../claims/online-formative-summative-correlation.md) [+M]
- [Paper-based formative test scores showed a weaker, non-significant correlation with summative scores in the control group (r2 = +0.176, p = 0.076)](../claims/paper-formative-summative-correlation-weak.md) [~M]
