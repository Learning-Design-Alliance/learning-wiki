# Tailor math-talk interventions to children's inhibitory control levels rather than uniformly encouraging all parents to talk more about math

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that only children with higher inhibitory control benefited from parental number talk, the authors recommend that interventions "should be tailored particularly to children's propensity levels". Rather than uniformly encouraging all parents to talk more about math, interventions should consider children's inhibitory control. For children with low inhibitory control, the authors suggest either designing math discussion "that does not excessively taxing children's executive functioning resources" or training children's inhibitory control skills alongside encouraging more parent math talk.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of children's inhibitory control before assigning intervention components, since the benefit of math input differs by propensity level.
#### Constraints
- The article states these are future directions for intervention work; the supporting interaction was concurrent and non-causal, and experimental work testing whether children with differing inhibition benefit differently from math talk manipulations is still needed.

### Target Learners
- Preschool-aged children (3–4 years) with varying inhibitory control

### Target Learning Goals
- Early math achievement from parental math input

### Affordances
- Opportunity Propensity Interaction Early Math
