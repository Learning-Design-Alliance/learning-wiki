# Cover Copy Compare

## Description
Cover Copy Compare (CCC) is a self-managed [practice](../elements/practice.md) procedure for building fluency on items with a single correct written form — spelling words, math facts, sight vocabulary, geography labels. The learner looks at a model (e.g., a correctly spelled word), covers it, writes the response from memory, uncovers the model, and immediately compares their attempt to the correct version, correcting errors before moving on. The cycle repeats until the learner can produce the item accurately without the model.

## Design Implications

CCC packages several well-supported mechanisms into one low-cost routine: immediate feedback from comparison to the model, active retrieval rather than passive copying, and self-management of the study loop. Because the learner generates a response before seeing the answer, each trial functions as a retrieval attempt, which strengthens memory more than re-reading or copying alone [~M]. The procedure requires minimal teacher time once materials are prepared, making it practical for individualized fluency work.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A set of items with unambiguous correct written responses (words, facts, symbols)
- A response sheet with the model visible, a cover mechanism (folded paper, index card), and blank response spaces
- Brief learner training in the look–cover–write–compare sequence until it runs independently
- An error-correction rule: after a mismatch, the learner copies the correct response before the next trial

#### Constraints
- Ineffective for open-ended or constructed responses where no single correct form exists — the compare step has nothing to check against [-M]
- Provides accuracy feedback only; it does not teach understanding of underlying concepts, so it should follow — not replace — conceptual instruction on the target content [-M]
- Errors can be practiced if the compare step is skipped or rushed; supervision or self-monitoring checklists are needed during initial training [-W]
- Gains are item-specific; CCC builds fluency on trained items and transfers poorly to untrained, dissimilar items [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Traditional CCC**: full model visible each trial; suited to new or difficult items
- **Cover only after correct**: model remains visible until one accurate copy is made, then retrieval trials begin — lowers frustration for learners with memory difficulties
- **Peer-mediated CCC**: pairs administer the sequence to each other, adding social accountability
- **Technology-delivered CCC**: apps present the model, hide it on a timer, and auto-score the typed response (e.g., fluency tools built into [MobyMax](https://www.mobymax.com) style fact-practice platforms)
- **Incremental rehearsal hybrids**: CCC trials interleaved with known items to reduce learner frustration

### Target Learners
- Students in late elementary through secondary who have not reached fluency on grade-level spelling words or math facts [+M]
- Learners who benefit from structured, predictable, self-paced routines, including students with learning disabilities or ADHD [+M]
- Whole-class use as an independent station once the routine is trained [~M]
- Less appropriate for learners who still need conceptual development of the content (e.g., number sense before fact fluency) [-M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Automaticity on a bounded set of written responses — spelling, math facts, sight words [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M]
- Fluency maintenance through distributed, repeated retrieval
- Self-regulated study habits: learners run and monitor their own practice loop

### Instructions
1. **Prepare materials.** Create a response sheet: each item's correct model at the top or left, blank lines beside or below it. Group items in small sets (3–5 new items) to respect working memory limits [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
2. **Train the routine.** Model the full sequence — look, cover, write, uncover, compare, correct — using [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) narration, then have learners practice the sequence on easy items until it is habitual.
3. **Run practice cycles.** Learners complete repeated look–cover–write–compare trials per item, self-correcting mismatches immediately by copying the model.
4. **Monitor and fade.** Spot-check compare fidelity early; once accuracy is stable, shift to timed cold probes (write all items with no model) to verify fluency, and retire mastered items [Practice](../elements/practice.md) should move from model-supported to unaided recall as accuracy grows.

## Related Strategies
- **Incremental Rehearsal** — interleaves unknown items with known ones; often combined with CCC trials to manage frustration
- **Self-Monitoring Checklists** — supports the compare step when learners skip or rush it
- **Timed Fluency Probes** — the standard outcome measure for verifying that CCC trials produced automaticity

## Examples
- **Spelling intervention (Skinner et al., 1989 tradition)**: A student keeps a CCC folder of weekly spelling words; each word appears with two blank response lines, and the student completes three cover–copy–compare trials per word before the Friday test.
- **Math fact fluency**: A resource teacher uses CCC sheets for multiplication facts, then administers a 1-minute written probe weekly to track rate and accuracy; mastered facts move to a maintenance deck.
- **Peer-mediated CCC**: In a second-grade classroom, pairs take turns being "coach" and "writer," with the coach holding the cover card and checking the comparison, rotating roles mid-session.
