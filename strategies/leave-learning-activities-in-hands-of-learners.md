# Leave knowledge-generating activities such as making predictions in the hands of learners rather than demonstrating or deciding for them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A forward-looking recommendation drawn from the findings: as machines increasingly take over activities such as conducting experiments, educators should deliberately preserve opportunities for learners to perform knowledge-generating activities themselves, and support learners' agency over their learning. The article argues there is a fundamental learning benefit to doing so, because self-performed actions aid figuring out the causal structure of the world even when they do not change the evidence encountered.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning tasks must include a component the learner can perform volitionally (e.g., generating a prediction)
#### Constraints
- Based on a single computerized study with 5- to 7-year-olds in a science museum setting; the authors call for research on when activities should be left to learners

### Target Learners
- children aged 5-7

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of causal structure

## Claims
