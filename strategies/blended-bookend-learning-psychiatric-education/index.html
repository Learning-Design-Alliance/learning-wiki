Use blended bookend learning combining self-paced online modules with synchronous interactive discussion and case supervision¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review describes a blended learning model in which "there was self-paced virtual learning via online modules in the beginning, followed by synchronous in-person interactive discussion, and the approach ended with case supervision or reflection." Residents appreciated this approach for two reasons: self-paced modules allowed time for personalized learning and processing materials, and modules provided access to quality information linked directly to published sources. This strategy was applied in learning PTSD and in an integrative psychiatry curriculum.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to quality online modules linked to published sources
  • Synchronous discussion sessions and case supervision or reflection components

Constraints¶

  • The review notes this model was used in specific contexts (PTSD learning and an integrative psychiatry curriculum) rather than across all psychiatric education

Target Learners¶

  • psychiatric residents and trainees

Target Learning Goals¶

  • psychiatric topic knowledge
  • clinical reasoning through case discussion

Claims¶

  • Online modules show significant pre-post knowledge gains in psychiatric trainees [+W]
  • Trainer-led online training outperformed self-paced online training for behavioral activation skills [-W]
  • Online CBT training satisfaction was noninferior to in-person training [+W]
  • Remote psychotherapy supervision showed comparable skill quality to face-to-face supervision but was less preferred [~W]
