# Frontload experimentation and data analysis with real or virtual optical experiments before introducing mathematical formalism

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This strategy sequences inquiry-based experiments with optical phenomena before the corresponding mathematics is taught, prompting students to observe patterns and generalize. In the pilot course, students explored polarizer behavior before vectors and component bases were introduced. The authors report that "students found that exploring quantum optical phenomena prior to the introduction of mathematical models helped in the understanding and mastery of the material," and that the approach exposed students to noise, uncertainty, and modeling tasks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experiments, real or virtual, must be available before formalism is introduced, e.g. lasers and polarizing filters or web-based simulators
#### Constraints
- In the pilot's second year, remote instruction forced an entirely virtual approach to the experiments

### Target Learners
- High school students who have completed second-year algebra

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding quantum computing concepts such as quantum states and gate operators
- Experimental data analysis, modeling, and generalization skills in STEM
