# Use CPFA parameter patterns to identify unproductive practice contexts and redesign feedback and practice order

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
CPFA's interpretable parameter table lets designers pinpoint 'instructional activities (i.e., contexts of learning) that are unproductive for future performance'. Near-zero or negative coefficients flag contexts needing improvement, either to scaffold low-knowledge students or to make items more effective, and the model answers 'questions regarding the best order of practice for related skills and the appropriate amount of repetition'. In this study it motivated avoiding Product items and adding varied feedback after repeated failures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Randomized practice sequences so transfer symmetry can be compared without confounds
- Random-effect intercepts for students and items so learning parameters are not confounded with prior ability
#### Constraints
- Negative coefficients may reflect either poor learning from the item or sensitivity to low-ability students, so interpretation requires care

### Target Learners
- 6th and 7th grade pre-algebra students using computerized tutoring systems

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving transfer between related skill item-types and remediating misconceptions
