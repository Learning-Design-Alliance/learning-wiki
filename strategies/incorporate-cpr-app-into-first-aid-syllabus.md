# Incorporate the CPR app into the first aid course syllabus and have learners practise its operation during training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete implementation step drawn from the trial's finding that familiarization drove actual app use. The mandatory group was introduced to the app and practised its operation during hands-on CPR training, while the facultative group only received a download handout — and only half of that group used the app. The authors conclude: "Incorporating the app into the syllabus of first aid courses might therefore increase its usage."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time within the first aid course to demonstrate and let learners practise the app's operation.
#### Constraints
- Outside the study environment people often download apps without familiarizing with them, which the authors warn could lead to longer delays before starting CPR.

### Target Learners
- First aid course participants, including school pupils

### Target Learning Goals
- Willingness and ability to use real-time CPR guidance during an actual emergency
