# Attach skills-training models to a simulated patient so students attend to the person and practice communication during the procedure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A specific implementable design choice: wrap the training model around the forearm of a simulated patient rather than using a separate inanimate arm. The authors report that "The TM was bandaged on the arm of a simulated patient, which encourages the student to pay attention to the person, not only the arm, and provides opportunity for the communication with the patient", fitting recent attention to attaching models to human beings for more realistic experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulated patient (in this study, a peer student following the same course) available during practice sessions
#### Constraints
- The communication advantage was observed only on one assessment item; the model scenario was very simple, with peer simulated patients receiving no extra training

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing and medical students learning invasive procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural skill performance combined with patient communication during procedures
