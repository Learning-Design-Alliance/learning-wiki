# Deliver brief, interprofessional, in situ simulation drills in vacant clinical spaces within days of real cases to implement urgent workflow changes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a JIT in situ simulation implementation strategy: new SOPs were "converted into single-page checklists to be used in clinical practice as cognitive aids," and brief (<30 minute) drills were run in the actual ORs with mixed-discipline groups of up to 10, facilitated by 2 trainers. A train-the-trainer model (10 anesthesiologists and 2 nursing educators trained in 1 day) enabled scale-up to 12 sessions per day within 72 hours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A core simulation implementation team to develop scenario content and materials rapidly (within 24 hours) and designate faculty trainers
- Access to vacant or adjacent clinical spaces matching the real work environment
- Senior leadership advocacy to encourage participation without mandating it
#### Constraints
- Rapid organization occasionally produced groups where disciplines were not equally represented or groups were too large
- Rapidly changing protocols made keeping training materials up to date difficult

### Target Learners
- perioperative staff: anesthesia providers, surgeons, nurses, OR attendants and technicians

### Target Learning Goals
- new COVID-19 perioperative workflows including preoperative huddle, PPE donning/doffing, ICU-to-OR patient transfer, and airway management with enhanced infection control
