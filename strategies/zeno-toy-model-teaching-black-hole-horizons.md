# Use the Achilles-tortoise Zeno time toy model to teach black hole horizons without the Schwarzschild metric

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Teach the frozen-star puzzle by first walking students through the Zeno time coordinate of the Achilles-and-tortoise race, then mapping the insight onto Schwarzschild coordinates. The article states the model "allows students to understand the limitations of Schwarzschild coordinates near a black hole, even without understanding the details of Schwarzschild coordinates". Zeno's story helps because "students intuitively know the right answer — there is no doubt that Achilles does catch the tortoise", so seeing a time coordinate under which he never arrives prepares them for the black hole case.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Courses that use high-school level mathematics and simplified calculations but no metric or tensors
#### Constraints
- Suited to introductory settings that do not introduce the Schwarzschild metric and its tortoise coordinates

### Target Learners
- non-physics-majors
- students in astronomy and astrophysics 101 courses

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding that the frozen-star effect is a coordinate effect
- understanding that objects can fall into black holes
