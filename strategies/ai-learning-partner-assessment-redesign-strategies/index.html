Embed AI as a learning partner and redesign assessments for AI-rich contexts¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The review's recommendations section advises instructors to proactively incorporate AI tools such as ChatGPT or domain-specific tutors as learning aids rather than view them only as threats, for example tasking students to use an AI tutor to draft solutions then critique or improve them. It also recommends redesigning assessments to emphasize critical thinking, creativity, and personal input, such as open-ended reflections, oral defenses, or applied projects, and transparently incorporating AI usage where suitable.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Clear guidelines on which AI uses are permitted for assignments; instructors must teach students to verify AI outputs and cite sources

Constraints¶

  • The review notes AI can currently solve standard questions, so rote-memorization assessments are vulnerable; AI carries risks of confident but incorrect answers, hallucinated information, and biases

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical thinking, academic integrity, responsible AI use, higher-order skills

Claims¶

  • Students' main AI challenges are content reliability and depth, and most expect AI to serve as tutor, creative partner, and critical thinking partner rather than mere content generator [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Engage students in active learning assignments using generative AI with structured reflection and critique of AI outputs
  • Build educational AI with pedagogical guardrails such as withholding direct solutions and embedded reflection steps
  • Embed AI training for pre-service science teachers within science pedagogy and inquiry-based teaching rather than general tool operation
  • Address generative AI in higher education through assessment redesign and proportional reflective practices rather than surveillance or punitive controls
  • Design PjBL projects so students must evaluate, critique, and improve AI-generated outputs rather than prohibiting AI use
  • Strategies for integrating GenAI in STEAM teaching: outcome-anchored lesson planning, differentiation, inquiry and project variants, assessment redesign, and integrity as pedagogy
  • Incorporate collaborative learning activities within AI literacy teaching, attending to activity modality and integration with other activities and assessments
  • Shift from punitive detection to proactive educative approaches, co-created detailed guidelines, and assessment designs GenAI cannot do well
  • Embed ethical inquiry into AI-supported instruction by having learners compare AI-generated texts with peer-reviewed sources
  • Embed GenAI into existing pedagogical strategies instead of undertaking full curriculum redesign
  • Redesign assessments to require critical thinking, originality, interactive activities, and real-time or proctored formats
  • AI-generated-essay-then-critical-evaluation task sequence for teaching critical evaluation skills
