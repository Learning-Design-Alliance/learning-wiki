Design empathy training as focused two-method combinations (didactic plus rehearsal or reflection) rather than stacking all five methods¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that program developers focus on the combinations the NMA identified as most effective rather than relying on common practice: "our study shows that NMA can be an insightful tool for identifying effective interventions, thus providing some direction of what key ingredients program developers and educators should focus on, rather than solely relying on what is common practice". Since feedback appeared in four of the five most frequent interventions but was absent from three of the five significant combinations, dropping feedback may also be more cost-effective. The article concludes interventions "need not be expensive nor prolonged to be effective".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Use combinations the network meta-analysis ranked highly, such as didactic with rehearsal or didactic with reflection

Constraints¶

  • The two-method finding rests on few studies with wide confidence intervals; the authors state replication is needed

Claims¶

  • Network meta-analysis ranks didactic-plus-rehearsal as the most effective empathy teaching-method combination, with six combinations significantly beating pure control [+M]
  • Interventions using two teaching methods outperformed those using three, though based on few studies with wide confidence intervals [+M]
  • Including didactic teaching is associated with greater intervention effectiveness for teaching empathy to physicians [+W]
  • Behavioural empathy interventions for physicians and medical students improve empathy skills with a moderate pooled effect (d = 0.50) [+W]
  • Most studies combined multiple teaching methods, typically didactic instruction followed by templates, writing practice, and feedback [+W]
