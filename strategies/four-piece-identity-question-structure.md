# Four-piece question structure eliciting identity statements in the pilot survey

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Each pilot survey question statement was broken into four pieces: two Likert-scale closed response items letting participants endorse value statements, and two free response questions asking participants to explain their reasoning, aiming for the connection to the identity framework. The first two criteria of the identity statement definition were "built directly into the prompt." Questions asked both the participant's own perspective and what an experimental physicist might say, following the ECLASS strategy, to prime participants to think about specific communities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Large numbers of responses to justify removing redundant questions and confirm interview representativeness
#### Constraints
- In preliminary trials participants supplied the more expert response before the more personal one, so both were asked transparently

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics laboratory students, including non-majors

### Target Learning Goals
- eliciting identity statements connecting lab practices to identity framework components

### Affordances
- Identity Statement Three Criteria
