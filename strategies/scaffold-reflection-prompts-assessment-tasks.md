# Scaffold reflection prompts in assessment tasks early, then remove the scaffolding to promote autonomy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because students' reflections during physics problem-solving are crucial and cognitively demanding yet often absent from written work, the authors recommend modifying task features so reflection elements are more conspicuous. Instructors can "scaﬀold the task prompting students to demonstrate proﬁciencies associated with reﬂections" at an earlier stage, then remove the scaffolding as students progress through the curriculum to promote their autonomy to engage in reflection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Applies to paper-based assessment tasks whose written solutions fail to mirror students' verbal reflection subcodes (R1, R2, R3)
#### Constraints
- Scaffolding should not guide any specific problem-solving patterns; tasks should allow students to construct solutions on their own to preserve the authenticity of the scientific practices

### Target Learners
- introductory-level physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- reflecting on mathematical results to make sense of them during physics problem-solving
