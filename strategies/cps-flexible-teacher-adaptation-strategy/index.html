Implement CPS flexibly, adapting to teachers' curricular demands and teaching styles¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study deliberately gave teachers "substantial freedom to adapt the CPS approach to their particular class and student population", reasoning that no curricular intervention will be implemented unless teachers can use it to fill their instructional needs. Teachers devoted at least two class periods per topic unit, used cooperative groups, and graded use of the approach, each adapting implementation to their own style.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Minimal training (a half-day session with researchers) with example problems solved using CPS
  • Detailed CPS solutions provided to teachers as new material is covered

Constraints¶

  • Fidelity of implementation varied across teachers; more experienced teachers used the approach for a subset of problems while the inexperienced teacher implemented it with high fidelity

Target Learners¶

  • high school physics teachers and their students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • integrating conceptual knowledge with problem solving

Affordances¶

  • Cps Principle Justification Plan Framework

Claims¶

  • CPS classes scored higher on conceptual questions than traditional classes, significantly at schools A and B [+W]
  • CPS students performed better on independent district-wide tests administered after the course [+W]
  • Categorization, finding-errors, and equation-instantiation tests showed no significant differences between CPS and traditional classes [-W]
  • CPS-taught high school physics classes scored higher on problem solving measures than traditionally taught classes, significantly at school C [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use technology-based CPS with prompted questions and conceptual feedback to reduce writing burden
  • Permit teachers to adapt and integrate programme materials in their own way in future trials, and consider remote delivery of training and support when scaling
