# States and scholarship-granting organizations can consider performance-based scholarships to incentivize academic success while providing additional aid

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q4` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that policymakers and scholarship-granting organizations seeking to be more purposeful with scholarship dollars adopt performance-based scholarships, which tie additional financial aid to academic benchmarks for low-income students. The recommendation rests on the replicated randomized controlled trial evidence showing generally positive impacts on academic performance across diverse settings and student types.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear academic benchmarks must be defined for scholarship payments
- Funding must supplement, not replace, existing need-based aid
#### Constraints
- Results were less consistent for outcomes such as persistence, and one site showed no significant benchmark impact

### Target Learners
- Low-income postsecondary students

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance, credit accumulation, and degree attainment
