Offer developmental writing students the feedback type that suits teacher needs, student learning style, and the assignment¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because the study found no significant difference among oral, written, or combined feedback, the author recommends teachers "consider providing developmental writing students with the type of feedback that suits the needs and personality of the teacher and the learning style of the student". Teachers can use oral feedback at some stages of the writing process and written at others, then offer students their preferred type on later assignments. The recommendation rests directly on the study's null result across delivery methods.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must receive feedback on rough drafts and be expected to revise and resubmit

Constraints¶

  • The study measured only revisions to one narrative paragraph assignment at a single community college

Target Learners¶

  • developmental writing students at a community college

Target Learning Goals¶

  • revising writing for content, structure, grammar, and style

Claims¶

  • Feedback delivery method made no statistically significant difference in grammar-and-style error counts measured by the Editor program [+W]
  • Feedback delivery method (oral, written, or combined) made no statistically significant difference in developmental writing students' rubric-scored revisions [+W]
