Use conversation analysis of real interactions to underpin and enhance role play and simulated patient training rather than replace it¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends grounding simulated patient scenarios in conversation analytic analysis of real interactions from the setting in question, so that simulators portray actual interactional practices of patient groups. Analysis can identify how patients ask questions, respond to information delivery, and voice information needs, informing actor training and scenario design. The authors argue CA should "enhance real-time simulated interaction, rather than provide a substitute for it".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • At least one team member with expertise in conversation analysis
  • Recordings or transcripts of real interactions from the relevant clinical setting

Constraints¶

  • Labour intensive and relatively costly compared with some other CST interventions
  • Contextualisation matters: generic best-practice recommendations are discouraged in favour of setting-specific grounding

Target Learners¶

  • Healthcare professionals in communication skills training
  • Trained simulated patients and actors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Authenticity of simulated clinical interactions
  • Communication skills for challenging consultations

Affordances¶

  • CST interventions underpinned by conversation analysis show improved interactional outcomes in neurology, psychiatry and aphasia settings [+W]
  • Conversation analytic comparisons show role-played and simulated interactions differ systematically from real interactions [+W]
