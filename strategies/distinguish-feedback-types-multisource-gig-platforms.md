# Distinguish the purposes and scales of coexisting feedback sources in educational gig platforms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's first design consideration holds that when multiple feedback sources coexist where learner feedback carries strong evaluative weight, systems should "distinguish their purposes and scales". In the Ringle study, presenting both sources with similar 5-point scoring schemes created "a false impression of equivalent evaluations" and confusion. Explicitly clarifying what each feedback measures and how to interpret scores helps tutors make sense of multisource feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback systems must clarify what each feedback source measures and how to interpret its scores.
#### Constraints
- Applies to settings where learner feedback carries strong evaluative weight and multiple feedback sources coexist.

### Target Learners
- Newly onboarded online language tutors on gig platforms

### Target Learning Goals
- Tutor understanding of performance standards and interpretation of multisource feedback
