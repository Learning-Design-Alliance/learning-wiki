# Habit-based intervention implications: habituate the instigation of effective strategies, not their full execution

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that interventions leverage behavior-change principles from habit theory, organizing implications into three categories: Targeting Habit Formation Mechanisms, Developing Action Control Skills, and Leveraging Existing Habits (Table 1). It stresses that "fully habitualizing the use of effective strategies is unrealistic and should not be the goal" because they remain effortful; instead, strengthening habits of strategy instigation could help students begin using effective strategies even when motivation or self-control is low.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Context-dependent repetition of effective strategy use in response to strong contextual cues, supported during early intervention phases with external scaffolding
#### Constraints
- The article states fully habitualizing effective strategy use is unrealistic since such strategies remain effortful and demanding; it explicitly does not propose a new habit-based framework

### Target Learners
- students in secondary and higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- initiating and sustaining use of effective learning strategies such as self-testing and spaced practice
