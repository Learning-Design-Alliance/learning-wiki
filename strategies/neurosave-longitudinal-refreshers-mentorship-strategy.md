# Pair cadaveric workshops with a longitudinal pathway of refreshers, mentorship and task trainers to consolidate skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because retention at follow-up was variable, the article recommends that a single workshop be embedded in a longitudinal training pathway. It states that "a longitudinal training pathway e.g. periodic refreshers, in-theatre mentorship, and access to task trainers may be necessary to consolidate skills", citing programmes that longitudinally integrate simulation and have shown sustained confidence gains over several years. The strategy treats the one-day course as an entry point rather than a complete intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to task trainers and in-hospital mentorship between refreshers
#### Constraints
- Retention in this pilot was assessed only in a small, heterogeneous follow-up cohort (n = 7)

### Target Learners
- general and pediatric surgeons trained in the initial workshop

### Target Learning Goals
- consolidating and retaining cranial trauma procedural skills over time
