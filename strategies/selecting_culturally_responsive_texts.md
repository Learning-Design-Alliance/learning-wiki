# Selecting Culturally Responsive Texts

## Description
This strategy involves auditing and curating classroom libraries, assigned readings, and multimedia texts so that learners encounter characters, authors, and perspectives that mirror their own identities and open windows into others' experiences (Bishop, 1990). Selection is guided by criteria of authenticity, complexity, and representation — prioritizing #OwnVoices authors and avoiding tokenism — and the texts are then integrated into instruction rather than displayed as symbols of diversity.

## Design Implications

Culturally responsive texts raise engagement and comprehension by connecting new material to learners' existing knowledge and identities, consistent with culturally relevant pedagogy's emphasis on academic success through cultural competence [Ladson-Billings argues culturally relevant pedagogy sustains student identity while supporting academic achievement.](https://doi.org/10.1080/00405849509543675) [+M]. Relevance alone is not sufficient: the texts must be paired with structured discussion and reflection to build perspective-taking rather than remaining unread on shelves.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A regular audit of classroom and curriculum texts for representation across culture, language, ability, and family structure
- Selection criteria that distinguish authentic, complex portrayals from stereotyped or "tourist curriculum" inclusion
- Instructional integration: texts used for [Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md), [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), and [Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md), not just display
- Learner voice in selection, so collections respond to students' interests and identities rather than adult assumptions

#### Constraints
- Surface-level diversity — adding a few "multicultural" books without changing instruction — produces no measurable engagement or identity benefit and can reinforce stereotypes [-W]
- Texts that portray a culture only through trauma or suffering narrow learners' sense of that group's humanity; a deficit-only framing undermines the empathy and identity goals [-W]
- Token representation (a single character standing in for an entire group) can trigger stereotype threat rather than belonging [-W]
- Texts selected for cultural relevance but far above learners' reading level lose the comprehension benefit; relevance does not substitute for [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Whole-class core texts vs. [Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md) and independent reading libraries
- Digital discovery tools (e.g., [OurStory](https://ourstory.weneeddiversebooks.org)) that match learners to diverse titles by interest
- Cross-curricular adaptation: diverse primary sources in history, culturally situated contexts in mathematics word problems, diverse authorship in science readings

### Target Learners
- Learners from historically underrepresented groups, for whom seeing their identities reflected supports engagement and self-efficacy [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- All learners benefit from windows into other perspectives, which supports [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md) [+W]
- Most valuable when texts match learners' independent or instructional reading levels; relevance cannot compensate for unreadable text [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Identity development and sense of belonging
- Perspective-taking and empathy for experiences beyond one's own
- Critical literacy: analyzing whose voices are present, absent, or stereotyped in texts
- Comprehension and engagement through [Activation](../principles/activation.md) of prior knowledge and experience

### Instructions
1. Audit the current collection and curriculum for representation gaps, using a tool such as [Teaching Tolerance's Reading Diversity tool](https://www.learningforjustice.org/professional-development/reading-diversity)
2. Apply selection criteria: authenticity (prefer #OwnVoices authors), complexity (avoid single-story portrayals), and range (joy and everyday life, not only struggle)
3. Integrate selected texts into instruction through [Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md) with structured [Peer Discussion](../elements/peer-discussion.md)
4. Prompt learners to connect texts to their own experience and to contrast perspectives across texts, supporting [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) and reflection
5. Involve learners in ongoing curation — book reviews, wish lists, and recommendations — so the collection evolves with the class

## Examples
- **[We Need Diverse Books — OurStory](https://ourstory.weneeddiversebooks.org)** — a discovery tool that matches learners with diverse titles through interest quizzes
- **[1000 Black Girl Books Resource Guide](https://grassrootscommunityfoundation.org/1000-black-girl-books-resource-guide/)** — a curated, learner-initiated collection built by Marley Dias, frequently used as a model for student-driven curation
- **[Learning for Justice Reading Diversity](https://www.learningforjustice.org/professional-development/reading-diversity)** — an audit tool applying critical-literacy criteria to classroom texts
- A middle-school ELA teacher replaces a single-novel unit with paired texts: a #OwnVoices novel alongside a canonical text, with discussion prompts comparing whose perspective is centered
