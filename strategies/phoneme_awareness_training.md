# Phoneme Awareness Training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Phoneme awareness training is explicit, systematic instruction in identifying, segmenting, blending, and manipulating the individual speech sounds (phonemes) in spoken words. Typical activities include oral segmentation ("say the sounds in *ship*"), phoneme counting with manipulatives such as chips or blocks to represent each sound, and phoneme manipulation tasks (substituting, deleting, or adding sounds). Because English spelling maps phonemes to graphemes, this oral-language skill is a prerequisite for understanding how the alphabetic principle works in both reading and spelling.

## Design Implications

Phoneme awareness is one of the best-established causal predictors of reading and spelling success, and training it produces reliable gains in both, especially when instruction is oral-only, brief, and combined with letters [Phonics instruction outperforms whole-word approaches for generalization.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [+S]. Training is most effective when it is systematic and sequenced from easier tasks (blending, segmenting) to harder ones (deletion, substitution), and when each phoneme is anchored to its grapheme rather than taught in isolation [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M] — grouping sounds into onset-rime or spelling patterns reduces the working-memory burden of full segmentation.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A planned phoneme sequence (typically starting with continuous sounds and two-phoneme words before moving to blends and three-plus phoneme words)
- Concrete manipulatives (chips, blocks, Elkonin boxes) or gestures to make each sound tangible
- Daily short sessions with frequent [Practice](../elements/practice.md) and immediate corrective [Feedback](../elements/assessment.md)
- Integration with letter-sound instruction once segmentation is established

#### Constraints
- Gains from phoneme awareness training alone fade unless connected to print; letter-sound linkage is what transfers to reading and spelling [Phonics instruction outperforms whole-word approaches for generalization.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [~S]
- Less effective for learners with severe or persistent phonological deficits, who may need more intensive, small-group or one-on-one intervention over longer periods [~M]
- Whole-class oral-only delivery loses effectiveness once most children have mastered segmentation; continuing it for all wastes instructional time [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Oral-only formats (sound games, clapping syllables-to-phonemes) work best before print is introduced
- Manipulative-based formats (Elkonin boxes, sound chips) bridge oral and written forms
- Embedded formats within a full phonics program (e.g., segmenting before spelling a word) produce the strongest spelling outcomes
- Small-group delivery outperforms whole-class and one-on-one formats in meta-analytic comparisons [~M]

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten and first-grade children who have not yet developed segmentation ability [+S]
- Struggling readers and poor spellers at any age, for whom remediation of phoneme-level skills remains effective [+M]
- Learners at risk of dyslexia, who benefit from earlier, more intensive, and more explicit delivery [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Phonemic segmentation and blending as foundations for decoding
- Spelling accuracy through sound-to-letter mapping
- Automatic word recognition, which frees attention for comprehension [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Begin with oral blending and segmenting of two-phoneme words using [Practice](../elements/practice.md) with manipulatives (one chip per sound).
2. Model each task explicitly before learners attempt it, following a [Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) sequence: model, guided practice, independent practice.
3. Provide immediate corrective [Feedback](../elements/assessment.md) during guided practice, correcting errors by re-modeling rather than re-explaining abstractly.
4. Link each phoneme to its grapheme once segmentation is secure — have learners move a chip as they say each sound, then write the corresponding letter.
5. Progress to phoneme manipulation (deletion, substitution) only after segmentation is accurate and automatic.
6. Monitor progress with brief segmentation and spelling probes and regroup learners accordingly.

## Examples
- **Elkonin boxes** — learners push a counter into a box for each phoneme heard in a word, then replace counters with letters; a standard component of [Reading Recovery](https://readingrecovery.org) lessons.
- **[Heggerty Phonemic Awareness Curriculum](https://heggerty.org)** — daily 10–12 minute oral lessons sequencing blending, segmenting, and manipulation across a school year.
- **[Jolly Phonics](https://www.jollylearning.co.uk)** — embeds phoneme awareness within a synthetic phonics program using actions and songs for each sound.
- **"Sound workout" with manipulatives** — small-group sessions where learners represent each sound in a word with a block before spelling it.
