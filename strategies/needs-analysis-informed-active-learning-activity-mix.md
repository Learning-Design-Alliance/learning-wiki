# Use a needs-analysis-informed mix of games, role-play, group work, and student-made materials to teach a foreign-language unit

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Before implementation, the researchers interviewed three English teachers and 15 sixth-grade students to identify difficulties and preferences, then organized activities accordingly. "In the experimental group, the techniques based on an active learning approach were used, such as game s, role-playing, group work, dis cussion, brainstorming and project work." Students made direction dialogues and place-direction posters, played word bingo and similar games, and used a smart board and flashcards.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A needs-analysis phase (semi-structured interviews with teachers and students who experienced the unit) to identify where students had difficulty and which activities they prefer
- The same teacher conducted lessons in both groups over four weeks (12 course hours)
#### Constraints
- Applied to one four-week unit in one secondary school; the authors note crowded classrooms and time inadequacy can hinder such methods

### Target Learners
- Fifth-grade secondary school students (early adolescent EFL learners)

### Target Learning Goals
- English unit learning outcomes: vocabulary, forming sentences, giving and understanding directions, pronunciation
