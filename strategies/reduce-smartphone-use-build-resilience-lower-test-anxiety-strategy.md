# Reduce classroom smartphone use and strengthen resilience while lowering test anxiety to support secondary students' achievement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's discussion recommends that schools address smartphone addiction directly and also act on the two mediators found significant. It states that "decision‐makers should conduct some studies on reducing smartphone use in the classroom and provide training to reduce general smartphone addiction" and that "actions should be taken to reduce cognitive test anxiety and increase the academic resilience of middle school students." This dual strategy follows from the partial mediation findings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools and decision-makers able to implement studies and training on reducing smartphone use, and to take actions targeting test anxiety and resilience
#### Constraints
- The article notes the cross-sectional data prevented establishing a real cause-effect relationship, and findings cannot be generalized to the regions since cities were not selected by statistical regional units

### Target Learners
- seventh- and eighth-grade middle school students

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement (GPA)
