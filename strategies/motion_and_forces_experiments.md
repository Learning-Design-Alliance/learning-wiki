# Motion and Forces Experiments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Hands-on physical science investigations in which learners manipulate variables — ball mass, ramp height, pendulum length, surface friction — to observe and measure how forces affect motion and energy transfer. Learners generate predictions, run trials, record measurements, and reconcile results with their initial conceptions. The strategy sits at the intersection of inquiry-based science and embodied learning: the phenomenon is physically experienced, not merely described.

## Design Implications

Physical manipulation of concrete materials grounds abstract force-and-motion concepts in sensorimotor experience, which supports conceptual change in science domains [Physical experience enhances science learning.](../claims/physical-experience-enhances-science-learning.md) [+M]. However, unguided "discovery" with apparatus alone produces weak learning; experiments work best when embedded in structured sequences with explicit goals, prediction prompts, and instructor follow-up [Structured inquiry with scaffolds outperforms minimally guided discovery.](../claims/productive-failure-improves-conceptual-learning.md) [~M]. Comparing outcomes across deliberately varied conditions helps learners abstract the underlying relationship rather than memorizing a single trial's result [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Apparatus that makes the target variable salient and measurable (ramps, pendula, spring scales, toy cars, motion sensors)
- A prediction step before data collection, so learners commit to a conception that results can confirm or violate
- Structured recording sheets that focus attention on the manipulated and measured variables
- Instructor-led consolidation after data collection — the experiment does not teach the concept by itself [Discovery learning without guidance underperforms explicit follow-up.](../claims/productive-failure-improves-conceptual-learning.md) [~M]

#### Constraints
- Unguided exploration of apparatus consumes working memory on equipment handling rather than the target concept [Reducing extraneous load is essential for novices.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [-M]
- Measurement noise and procedural error can send learners toward misconceptions if results are not reconciled with canonical physics
- Learners often confirm their prediction regardless of data (confirmation bias); requiring written predictions with reasoning before trials reduces this
- Purely hands-on activity without a conceptual follow-up produces activity without learning — "hands-on" does not guarantee "minds-on" [Guided inquiry requires integration of doing with explanation.](../claims/physical-experience-enhances-science-learning.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Prediction–observation–explanation (POE)** cycles: predict, run the trial, explain any discrepancy
- **Contrasting cases**: run two conditions differing in one variable (e.g., long vs. short pendulum) and compare periods [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M]
- **Simulations paired with physical labs**: [PhET simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu) let learners isolate variables that are hard to control physically, then verify in the physical lab
- **Structured vs. open inquiry**: full structure for novices, fading toward open investigation as learners gain experimental control [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- K–12 and introductory physics students encountering Newtonian mechanics for the first time [Physical experience enhances science learning.](../claims/physical-experience-enhances-science-learning.md) [+M]
- Learners holding intuitive misconceptions (e.g., "heavier objects fall faster") who benefit from seeing their prediction violated
- Less effective for learners who already command the formal model and would profit more from quantitative problem solving [Guidance should match expertise level.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of force, motion, energy transfer, and proportionality
- Scientific practices: controlling variables, measuring, recording, and interpreting data
- Conceptual change: replacing intuitive physics with canonical models through prediction violation [Prediction violation drives conceptual change.](../claims/productive-failure-improves-conceptual-learning.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Activate prior conceptions.** Pose the question ("What determines how far the car rolls?") and have each learner write a prediction with reasoning before touching equipment.
2. **Demonstrate the procedure.** Model one trial with apparatus, narrating variable control and measurement technique, so handling does not consume cognitive resources during investigation ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)).
3. **Run structured trials.** Learners manipulate one variable at a time across contrasting conditions and record measurements [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M].
4. **Compare results to predictions.** Facilitate discussion of discrepancies between predicted and observed outcomes; treat high-confidence wrong predictions as productive learning events [High-confidence errors improve retention.](../claims/high-confidence-errors-improve-retention.md) [+M].
5. **Consolidate with formal explanation.** Connect the data pattern to the canonical model (e.g., F = ma, conservation of energy) — the experiment motivates the formalism, not replaces it.
6. **Fade toward open inquiry.** As learners gain control, shift to student-designed investigations of a new variable [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

## Related Strategies
- Prediction–observation–explanation cycles — the canonical lesson structure for prediction-violation experiments
- Simulation-based inquiry — virtual manipulation when variables are impractical to control physically
- Compare-and-contrast cases — the contrasting-conditions design that drives abstraction

## Examples
- **[PhET Interactive Simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu)** (University of Colorado Boulder) — "Forces and Motion: Basics" and "Pendulum Lab" simulations used before or alongside physical apparatus to isolate variables.
- **FOSS (Full Option Science System)** — published K–8 curriculum built around hands-on force-and-motion investigations with structured student notebooks ([https://www.fossweb.com](https://www.fossweb.com)).
- **CPO Science Foundations of Physics** — lab-first units where students build ramps and collision tracks before formal derivations.
- **ISLE (Investigative Science Learning Environment)** (Etkina & Van Heuvelen, Rutgers) — students replicate classic experiments, identify patterns, and derive physical laws from their own data.
