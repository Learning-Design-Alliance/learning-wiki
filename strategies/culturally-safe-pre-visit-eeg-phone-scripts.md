# Use standardized, community-reviewed pre-visit phone scripts that explain EEG, ask about typical hairstyles, offer scheduling around style removal, suggest capping-friendly styles, and warn about post-visit washing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends creating a “brief, standardized calling script that is culturally safe, then piloted and reviewed by members of the parti- cipant community,” used before the lab visit. The scripts should “t ell parents about EEG,” gather information about how a child typically wears their hair, offer to schedule around long-lasting styles, suggest styles optimal for capping, and inform families that hair will need washing after the visit. Example scripts from the Baby’s First Years project are provided as supplements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Staff must be familiar with common participant hairstyles and engage in a non-judgmental, competent manner; scripts should be piloted and reviewed by community members.
#### Constraints
- Open, unstructured conversations can end in miscommunications or terminology differences, reducing participant confidence in the research team.

### Target Learners
- Infants and very young children from Black and/or Latinx families participating in pediatric EEG research

### Target Learning Goals
- Increasing consent, assent, and high-quality data collection from Black and Latinx participants in developmental neuroscience studies
