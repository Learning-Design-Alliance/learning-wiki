# Seven tips for designing out-of-class life science assessments that maintain learning despite GAI usage

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article distills its findings into seven recommendations (Table 3): "Design open-ended text-based questions that demand scientific precision", prepare rubrics emphasizing justified technique and control selection, prioritize apply-level Bloom's questions, incorporate image inference, combine multiple-choice with image inference to force hallucinations, use in-class activities for learning objectives, and test all assessments with GAI. These target maintaining student learning regardless of GAI access.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessments must be capable of promoting student learning regardless of GAI usage, given unequal student access to or reliance on GAI tools
#### Constraints
- Lessons were derived from one graduate-level molecular biology course grounded in experimental design; whether they apply to other disciplines or teaching goals remains to be determined

### Target Learners
- first-year doctoral students in biomedical and life sciences

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental design
- scientific precision in hypothesis generation and control justification
- critical evaluation of GAI-generated answers
