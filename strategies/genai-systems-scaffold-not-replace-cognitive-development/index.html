AI-assisted learning systems should scaffold cognitive development rather than replace it, and require active student contribution to preserve ownership¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's design implications section recommends that GenAI-assisted learning systems "scaffold cognitive development, not replace it", adapting to promote content retention and engage a useful level of cognitive load grounded in current cognitive load theory. It further states systems "must support active contribution to preserve ownership", either by requiring user input integrated into a solution or by eliciting individual involvement in creation. These recommendations target institution, educator, and student levels.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Systems should use current cognitive load theory information as a foundation for individualised cognitive load management of users.

Constraints¶

  • The recommendations are motivated by a single between-subjects experiment with 55 novice undergraduates completing three introductory C tasks in 30 minutes.

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate computer science students
  • novice programmers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • durable retention of programming content
  • sense of ownership over submitted work

Related Strategies¶

  • Research directions: scaffold reasoning without displacing epistemic agency and rethink curricula to preserve core human capabilities
