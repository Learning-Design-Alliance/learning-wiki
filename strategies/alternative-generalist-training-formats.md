# Alternative generalist-training formats include extending question-list oral qualifying examinations across departments, postdoctoral laboratory rotations, and topic-specific mini-courses or summer schools.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Beyond the qualifying-examination program, the article outlines alternatives. Caltech's Computation and Neural Systems program gives students a list of 100 questions as preparatory material for an oral qualifying examination with five faculty members; the article asks whether this could extend to "a list of 500 questions" spanning multiple subjects. Other possibilities: a several-year postdoctoral fellowship rotating through laboratories, "hacker school"-style mini-courses designed by experts for graduates trained in another field, and postdoctoral programs built around multiple topic-specific summer and winter schools across subjects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Any such program would require an organization supporting students' growth for many years, and several years longer than customary graduate-student support, given the effort's experimental nature.
#### Constraints
- The author states it is difficult to imagine that some trial and error would not be required in the design of such a program.

### Target Learners
- advanced graduate students and postdoctoral researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- acquiring graduate-level working knowledge of multiple scientific fields
