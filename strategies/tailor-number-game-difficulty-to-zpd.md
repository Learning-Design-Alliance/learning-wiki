# Tailor number-game difficulty to each child's zone of proximal development rather than using a fixed interval for all

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that future number-line interventions provide a tailored number interval matched to what a child cannot yet do alone but could achieve with structured support, drawing on Zone of Proximal Development theory. It notes the current study's one-size-fits-all approach meant "the game is not equally beneficial for children at differing stages of numerical knowledge acquisition," and that some children may need adult support beyond the encouragement provided.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of each child's current numerical knowledge to select an interval beyond independent achievement but reachable with structured support
#### Constraints
- The article states it remains an open question what level of game difficulty and support is necessary for a number line game to improve numerical skills

### Target Learners
- four- to five-year-old children with varying levels of numerical knowledge

### Target Learning Goals
- early numerical skills, including number line understanding and number sequence knowledge
