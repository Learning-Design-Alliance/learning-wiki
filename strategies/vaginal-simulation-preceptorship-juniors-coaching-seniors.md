# Include procedure-specific low-fidelity vaginal surgery models in ObGyn residency surgical teaching curricula, with preceptorship for juniors and coaching for seniors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (7 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their trial results, the authors "recommend inclusion of our vaginal surgical models in the surgical teaching curricula for ObGyn residency programs" and plan local implementation. They further argue that early and advanced learners have varied needs: junior learners may benefit from surgical preceptorship and proctorship, while senior learners may better advance through a coaching model using guided discovery for progressive improvement of specific skills. The trial showed it is possible to initiate first-year residents into advanced vaginal surgery with no additional complications under supervision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Academic sites with a mandatory urogynecology rotation and preceptor surgeon educators dedicated to routine instruction
- Blinded OR proctors distinct from simulation preceptors for assessment
#### Constraints
- In the research trial, residents had no protected time for didactic learning, model practice, or OR assistance, making recruitment and retention extremely challenging

### Target Learners
- Junior gynecology residents, especially PGY-1
- Senior residents requiring skill refinement

### Target Learning Goals
- Transferable operative skill in vaginal surgery
- Surgical self-confidence and procedural knowledge
