Strategies

Assign student teams to research and present real mining case studies with peer evaluation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Students self-selected into teams of two or three to identify and present a 10-min case study of a geological problem at an active mine, worth 15% of the course grade. "All students in the audience participated in peer review of each presentation." The article reports this exposed students to 14 additional examples of applied geoscience beyond what instructors could provide, and that students enjoyed becoming experts on their chosen topics.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Class time allotted for presentations at almost every meeting
  • A rubric and peer evaluation form distributed in the first week
  • Faculty-presented example case studies to illustrate satisfactory versus incomplete presentations

Constraints¶

  • Students may gravitate toward examples from concurrent courses, creating repetition; scheduling misalignment can leave teams without sufficient background

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate engineering students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • identifying real-world geological problems and their engineering solutions

Claims¶

  • Mining engineering students' confidence that they could provide concrete examples of applied geoscience rose significantly over a course combining case studies and active learning [+W]
  • Students gained significant improvement in exposure to geoscience concepts through the case study–based curriculum [+W]
  • Students became significantly more willing to convince others that geoscience is important in mining after the intervention [+W]
  • Students with prior internship experience had more positive initial attitudes toward geoscience [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Introduce each geoscience topic through a real-world applied context before presenting supporting science
