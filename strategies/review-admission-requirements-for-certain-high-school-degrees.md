# Medical schools should review admission requirements for students holding certain high school degrees and consider additional cognitive assessment before admission

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that because some regional high school certificates rely heavily on school-provided grades, medical schools should re-examine how these applicants are assessed for admission. The authors write that "it could be recommended for medical schools to review the requirements of students admitted with certain high school degrees" and that these students "would need additional cognitive assessment before their admission into a medical school".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to applicant high school degree type information during admission decisions, as in the Jordanian system where admission is based solely on high school degree grades
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is motivated by the article's finding that Kuwaiti, Qatari and Saudi certificates rely on school-provided grades rather than fully standardized examinations; the underlying performance differences were observed at a single institution

### Target Learners
- Medical school applicants and admitted medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Selecting applicants likely to succeed academically in medical school (on-time graduation and graduating GPA)
