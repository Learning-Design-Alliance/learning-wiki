# Sequence YPT participation as a capstone: build on students' existing soft and physics skills, ensure preparation and support, and link YPT to regular classes and other extracurricular activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (5 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report distils its findings into thirteen guidelines for developing soft skills through inquiry-based learning in YPT. Teachers are advised to treat YPT as a "capstone" element, ensuring students have sufficient soft and physics foundations before participating, providing preparation and support for junior students, and positioning YPT as complementary to regular physics classes and other extracurricular activities rather than a substitute.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need sufficient existing soft skills and physics foundations before joining YPT-related activities to avoid feeling overwhelmed by the events' requirements.
#### Constraints
- The guidelines rest on self-reported perceptions from students and a self-selected sample of YPT-experienced teachers, and the authors note cross-national differences may require more adaptation of preparatory classes than teachers initially assume.

### Target Learners
- high-school physics students, particularly those in their final or last-to-final school years

### Target Learning Goals
- soft-skill development (teamwork, information location and use, creativity, presentation, debating, English) and research-task performance in inquiry-based physics learning
