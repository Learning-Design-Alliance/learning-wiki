# Combine autonomy-supportive parenting training with peer-environment cultivation and group-targeted interventions to support achievement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implications section recommends that parents "maintain appropriately high educational expectations while providing children with autonomy-supportive environments" that foster self-regulation and constructive peer relationships, and that "Prevention and intervention programs should therefore include explicit training in autonomy-supportive parenting practice." Schools should cultivate positive peer environments through collaborative learning, peer mentoring and classroom structures fostering academic engagement. Interventions should be tailored by achievement level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires parents and educators to coordinate: high expectations plus autonomy support at home, and academically engaging peer structures at school.
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from Chinese middle-school classrooms; the article notes peer influence was stronger for low achievers, so strategies should differ by group.

### Target Learners
- Chinese middle-school adolescents (grades 7–9)

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement
- Positive friendship selection
- Academic engagement and motivation
