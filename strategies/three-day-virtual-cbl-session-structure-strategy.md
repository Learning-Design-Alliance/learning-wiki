# Design virtual case-based learning as a three-phase, three-day flipped sequence combining team case discussion, peer presentation, and expert-supported conclusion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable recipe: divide students into teams of 5–7 with trained moderators; Day 1, facilitated case discussion in breakout rooms (60–90 min) with preparation of a teamwork report; Day 2, team presentations (max 10 min each) with inter-team feedback (60–120 min); Day 3, small-group work on questions with moderator-led discussion and expert summary (~120 min). "Each virtual case-based discussion consisted of three phases and was completed in three days." Clinical scenarios should be co-designed by basic scientists and clinical educators.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained moderators and an orientation pack covering teamwork, problem solving, clinical reasoning and the platform
- A web-conferencing platform with breakout rooms (Big Blue Button)
#### Constraints
- Tested as an extra-curricular intervention at one institution during the pandemic

### Target Learners
- first- and second-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- motivation to learn basic sciences
- peer learning and collaboration
