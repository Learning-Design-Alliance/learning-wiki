# Design PLL sessions around student leaders facilitating complete problem-based learning processes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that PLL designers and coordinators build sessions around student leaders facilitating a complete problem-based learning process, since this task type was the only significant moderator of the PLL effect. The authors state that "student leader facilitation of problem-based learning during PLL sessions was associated with a significant effect after controlling for the session duration and training characteristics." The strategy treats PBL tasks, in which students identify and solve problems with leader scaffolding, as the backbone of session design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Student leaders need facilitation and interaction skills as well as subject content expertise to guide PBL processes
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from face-to-face post-secondary sessions; no online studies were included in the analysis

### Target Learners
- Post-secondary undergraduate students, especially those in foundational lower-level courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Cognitive achievement, particularly conceptual knowledge in introductory STEM and allied-health courses
