Use kindergarten-age screening information to identify children at potential risk of math difficulties and provide access to early support¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends transferring prediction models of the kind it validated into school practice: kindergarten measures across arithmetic, cognitive, home-environment, parental, motivational, behavioral, and gender domains are entered into a trained model whose output flags children at potential risk, enabling timely support before difficulties consolidate in later grades. The authors state that in a school setting, early prediction results could be used to identify children at potential risk of developing MD and to provide access to early support. A reduced six-predictor model eases everyday use because assessing all possible predictors for every child is quite challenging in practice.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Kindergarten-age assessment of the model's predictor measures (a minimal set of six top-ranked features suffices per the reduced model)
  • A trained and evaluated prediction model before deployment

Constraints¶

  • The model's positive predictive value was low (.245–.252) due to low MD prevalence, so flagged children include many false positives
  • Prediction identifies risk without explaining underlying mechanisms; inference models remain needed for intervention design

Target Learners¶

  • kindergarten-age children (age 6)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • early identification of and support for children at risk of math difficulties

Affordances¶

  • Neural Networks Prediction Learning Difficulties

Claims¶

  • Kindergarten counting, arithmetic, and letter-knowledge measures correlate with Grade 6 arithmetic fluency (r up to .45) [+M]
  • At a sensitivity of .672 the MD prediction model showed specificity of .754–.762 but a low positive predictive value (.245–.252) [~M]
  • A multilayer perceptron trained on 49 kindergarten-age measures predicts Grade 6 math difficulties with an AUC of .818 [+M]
  • A reduced model using only the six top-ranked predictors maintains relatively similar MD prediction performance (AUC .776) [+M]
  • The most important kindergarten predictors of Grade 6 MD are RAN, spatial relations, arithmetic skills, number concepts, attention-orientation, and counting [+M]
  • Only four screeners demonstrated acceptable diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing children with or at-risk of mathematical learning difficulties [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use the SYMP Test as a quick screening measure to identify children at risk for mathematical difficulties or dyscalculia
  • Assess Arabic numeral identification and symbolic magnitude estimation to identify children likely to struggle with math
  • Early identification battery for children at risk for long-term mathematics difficulties
  • Response To Intervention
  • Provide early, effective, targeted intensive intervention to disrupt persistent trajectories of reading difficulty
  • Identify children with weak inhibition skills in kindergarten and provide intervention or training programs to support later monitoring accuracy
  • Use broader baseline cognitive predictors beyond conventional phonological measures to detect at-risk learners and personalize literacy intervention
  • Develop screening measures and targeted interventions specific to the preterm population's learning difficulties
