Develop political or policy education and programs for nurses based on the five-factor competence framework¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends using its synthesized framework as the basis for education: it "provides a conceptual framework for nurses' competence in healthcare policy and for developing political or policy education or programs to strengthen this competence." In the discussion the authors argue that "enhancing nurses' healthcare policy competence through formal education or program development is essential for increasing their healthcare expertise," targeting the identified factors and subthemes as curriculum content.

Claims¶

  • Healthcare expertise—knowledge of healthcare environments, systems, policymaking processes, and policy-relevant information—is a factor of nurses' healthcare policy competence [+W]
  • Intrinsic motivation—awareness of healthcare issues, social responsibility, and commitment to policy advocacy—is a factor of nurses' healthcare policy competence [+W]
  • Meta-synthesis of 27 qualitative studies synthesized nurses' healthcare policy competence into five factors, 15 themes, and 34 subthemes [+W]
  • Policy development—setting strategic policy agendas, conducting policy research, and developing policy and legislation—is a factor of nurses' healthcare policy competence [+W]
  • Policy intervention—policy decision-making, legislative engagement, and policy monitoring and improvement—is a factor of nurses' healthcare policy competence [+W]
  • Political skills—communication, negotiation, and lobbying; networking and social coalition; and forming public opinion—are a factor of nurses' healthcare policy competence [+W]
