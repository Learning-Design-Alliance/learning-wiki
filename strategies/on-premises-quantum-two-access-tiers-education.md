# Use two access tiers (pulse-level and circuit-level) when teaching with an on-premises quantum computer

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article sorts educational applications into two access tiers: "(1) experiments/lab sessions that involve accessing the hardware physically or through the pulse-level interface, and (2) accessing the quantum computer through the circuit-level interface." Pulse-level sessions cover calibration, benchmarking and oscilloscope pulse exploration; circuit-level sessions run algorithms with error mitigation. The article argues this cultivates the next generation of quantum scientists and engineers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Physical or pulse-level access to the on-premises system, since hands-on experimentation lets students conduct pulse-level experiments, change the calibration, or connect external periphery
#### Constraints
- Some applications are available only for an on-premises quantum computer

### Target Learners
- university quantum computing students
- quantum hardware lab learners

### Target Learning Goals
- bridging abstract quantum circuit descriptions and the physical hardware
- developing calibration and benchmarking skills
