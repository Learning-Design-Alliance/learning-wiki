# Structure virtual conferences as recurring short evening sessions with public general sessions and private cohort-building sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
For its virtual conference, the NAC replaced a multi-day in-person event with two-hour evening sessions twice weekly over five weeks, approximating the usual 20 hours of in-person content while capping weekly burden at 4 hours. Each session split equally between an open general session for research talks and networking and a private session with guided cohort-building activities, with consistent start times and separate Zoom links to lessen technological demands.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Consistent start times across weeks to encourage timely attendance of participants
- One virtual platform (Zoom) with separate links for general and cohort-building sessions to lessen technological demands on participants
#### Constraints
- Static meeting times produced consistently inconvenient overnight or early morning hours for the farthest participants
- The five-week duration became minorly taxing, with an emotional toll expressed in later weeks; the authors recommend limiting future virtual conferences to a maximum of three weeks
- Cohort-building sessions were not recorded, making attendance crucial

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and alumni researchers in astronomy and physics support programs

### Target Learning Goals
- presenting summer research
- professional networking
- cohort community building
