# Use narrated case-based mobile virtual patient applications as a supplement to traditional instruction to improve clinical reasoning skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's first recommendation is that "Narrated case-based mobile virtual patient applications can be used to improve students' clinical reasoning skills", positioned as an assistive technology added to, not replacing, the traditional environment. The strategy involves students working narrated, realistic cases on their own smartphones with instant feedback, at a time and pace of their choosing. The authors also recommend seeking educators' opinions and ensuring device and IT infrastructure prerequisites.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have mobile devices able to run the application and adequate IT infrastructure (internet-connection speed, etc.), which the authors call a prerequisite for successful practices
- Educators' opinions should be sought and their involvement in the process ensured
#### Constraints
- The authors state virtual patient tools are not a sufficient strategy for teaching clinical reasoning alone and cannot replace real clinical practice

### Target Learners
- health professions students, particularly physiotherapy and rehabilitation students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning skills
- preparation for real-world patient care
