Give pre-service teachers feedback on their teaching practice through focus group discussions based on their diaries and observation records¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teaching staff and school mentor teachers give candidates feedback about their own practices and provide environments where candidates share observations and thoughts about their own or friends' practices. In the study this was implemented as focus group discussions grounded in the PSTs' kept diaries and observation forms, and the experimental group's self-efficacy gains were significantly higher than the control group's. The authors also suggest extending such practices to actively teaching teachers so they can discuss their lessons and themselves.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • diaries and observation records collected during practice to ground the discussions
  • weekly or biweekly focus group discussion sessions with a small group of PSTs
  • an instructor who reviews records, categorizes problems, and prepares solutions or literature support

Constraints¶

  • in this study no method was tested in the control group, so comparisons with techniques such as micro-teaching remain for future research

Target Learners¶

  • pre-service teachers
  • practicing teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • professional self-efficacy
  • teaching skills in planning, participation, strategies, and classroom management

Claims¶

  • After focus group discussions, pre-service teachers reported increased awareness of practice problems and developed broader alternative solutions [+W]
  • Focus group discussion feedback during teaching practice raises pre-service teachers' self-efficacy perceptions more than teaching practice alone [+W]
  • Pre-service teachers reported that focus group discussions enabled progress in lesson planning, classroom management, and teaching strategies [+W]
  • Pre-service teachers' teaching-practice problems cluster into planning, student participation, teaching strategies, and classroom management themes [+W]
