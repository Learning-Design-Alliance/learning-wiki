# Implement ABSL lessons through structured argumentation activities sequenced within the 5E instructional model

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q1`–`q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementation strategy organizes ABSL teaching as a weekly plan of argumentation activities — concept cartoons, competing theories, predict-observe-explain, expressions tables, news, and a migration story — delivered within the "5E model steps (i.e., Engage, Explore, Explain, Extend, and Evaluate)". Materials and the implementation process were planned with the ADDIE instructional design model, students worked in groups of five, and a mysterious event activity opened lessons to raise interest before subject teaching began.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher guideline and weekly lesson plan specifying subjects and activities for each topic
- An initial familiarization period; the authors assisted implementation in the first three weeks so students understood the activities
- A pilot implementation to identify and correct deficiencies in activities before actual implementation
#### Constraints
- Students reported initial difficulties due to inexperience with the approach and problems in the discussion process

### Target Learners
- seventh-grade middle school students

### Target Learning Goals
- social studies unit content mastery
- argument construction and justification skills
