# Design creative problem-solving activities accounting for the effort of sustaining creative behavior

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that sustaining creative behavior across a repeated task is effortful and rare, the article recommends that "The difficulty of sustaining a creative behavior should be considered when engaging adults in the development of creative problem-solving activities." The authors also propose exploring how to overcome the social representation of creativity as a genius-based Eureka moment, reframing it as an effortful, persistent behavior requiring inhibition of conservative intentions and regulation until a creative solution is reached.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities must give learners the opportunity to choose between repeating a known solution and developing a new one
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a repeated educational robotic task with adults; transfer to other technologies needs further study
