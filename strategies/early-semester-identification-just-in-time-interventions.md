# Identify at-risk students at the start of a semester and deliver just-in-time mental health interventions to protect both psychological health and academic achievement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that universities use depressive symptom trajectory findings to spot students heading toward unfavorable trajectories early in the term. It states that "targeting at-risk students at the start of a semester may potentially prevent negative impacts not only on psychological health, but also on academic achievement," and that trajectory research could "enable university systems to identify students who may be at risk for unfavorable mental health trajectories, and aid in the creation of just-in-time interventions." The recommendation is grounded in the study's observational associations, not a tested intervention.
