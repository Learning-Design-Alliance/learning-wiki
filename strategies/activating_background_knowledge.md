# Activating Background Knowledge

## Description
Activating background knowledge involves prompting learners to recall and connect prior experiences, information, and understandings before engaging with new content. This can be achieved through questions, discussions, or brief activities that encourage learners to think about what they already know about the topic. Activating prior knowledge helps learners make connections between new information and their existing mental models, enhancing comprehension and retention. This strategy is particularly effective before reading a text or starting a new unit of study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Well-crafted questions or activities that prompt learners to recall and connect to their prior knowledge. Teachers need to be prepared to facilitate discussions and validate learners' contributions.
#### Constraints
- May require careful planning to ensure that prompts are relevant and accessible to all learners. Some learners may feel uncomfortable sharing personal experiences or may have limited prior knowledge on the topic.

### Personalization
- Teachers can differentiate prompts and activities based on learners' individual backgrounds and experiences. Learners can also choose to share personal stories or examples related to the topic.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the level of learner engagement in discussions, the depth of connections they make between new and existing knowledge, and their ability to apply prior knowledge to solve problems or answer questions.

## Impact
- Research supports that activating background knowledge improves comprehension, retention, and engagement. By connecting new content to what learners already know, educators can create more meaningful and effective learning experiences.
