# Include a judgment-based measurement checked by two independent methods to teach observer-dependent error

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The damping-time measurement is deliberately qualitative: students judge when the amplitude has fallen to roughly 1/e of its initial value using thread-shadow lines on a screen. The article lists its objectives: "to acquaint students with measurements based on their own judgment, therefore giving rise to large errors, to show how an appropriate technique can make it easy to take an apparently difficult reading, and to check a physical law by linearising an exponential decay experimental curve." Direct and straight-line-fit estimates are then compared for compatibility.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two different methods of measuring the same quantity so results can be cross-checked for systematic errors
#### Constraints
- The value is strongly dependent on the observer's judgment, giving rise to large errors

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- linearising an exponential decay
- assessing compatibility of measurement methods
