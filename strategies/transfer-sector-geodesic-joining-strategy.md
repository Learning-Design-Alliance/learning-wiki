# Use Lorentz-transformed transfer sectors to join spacetime sectors and continue geodesics across boundaries

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In spacetime, joining neighbouring sectors requires a Lorentz transformation rather than the rotation used in the purely spatial case, because the light cones of both sectors must coincide. A transformed sector serves as a transfer sector: the geodesic is continued straight onto it and then transferred back onto the neighbouring sector in its original symmetric shape by reversing the Lorentz transformation. Double transfer sectors improve precision when the line segment within a sector is very short.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants must be familiar with the Lorentz transformation
- Several representations of the sector obtained by Lorentz transformations with different velocities are needed to draw a geodesic at a stretch
#### Constraints
- When a line passes close to a vertex, the segment within a sector can be very short, making the construction imprecise; double transfer sectors solve this

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students
- pre-service physics teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- constructing timelike geodesics in curved spacetime
- understanding the role of the Lorentz transformation in spacetime geometry
