# Combine targeted physician education on risk stratification, dual antiplatelet therapy, and vascular imaging with structured referral pathways and expanded neuroimaging access

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend a multifaceted approach to improving TIA care in Nigeria: "targeted physician education, development of simplified national protocols, and expansion of access to neuroimaging services". They specify structured training programs focusing on early risk stratification, dual antiplatelet therapy, and vascular imaging, alongside regional stroke centres, dedicated TIA clinics, and rapid-access TIA pathways adapted to resource constraints.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured training programs addressing the knowledge gaps the survey identified (dual antiplatelet therapy, vascular imaging, risk stratification)
- Institutional and policy support to establish referral pathways and expand neuroimaging access
#### Constraints
- The article notes such models must be adapted within the Nigerian healthcare system while accounting for resource constraints
- The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference about what improves outcomes

### Target Learners
- Licensed practicing physicians in Nigeria, including house officers, medical officers, residents, and consultants

### Target Learning Goals
- Guideline-based TIA diagnosis, risk stratification, antithrombotic treatment decisions, and urgent referral practice
