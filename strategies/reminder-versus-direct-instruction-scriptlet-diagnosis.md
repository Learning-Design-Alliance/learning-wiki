# Choose reminder versus direct instruction according to whether the missing scriptlet is in the learner's repertoire

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This implementable strategy operationalizes adaptive external support: when a scriptlet is "repeatedly missing in a certain play", the internal play is assumed absent from the learner's repertoire and "a direct instruction by the feed back authoring tool would work better than a reminder instruction"; when a scriptlet is sometimes available and sometimes missing, the script is partially present but not enacted, and a reminder instruction works better. Positive-feedback reminders are included but should not be overused.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Continuous assessment of learner performance during CABLe, e.g., via authoring-tool algorithms, to distinguish absent components from non-enacted ones
#### Constraints
- Prompts for positive feedback, if used too frequently, might distract students from engaging in actual CABLe
- It is a challenge to clarify whether a missing scriptlet reflects lack of knowledge or a functional play not enacted

### Target Learners
- individual learners and learning groups engaged in computer-supported collaborative argumentation

### Target Learning Goals
- enacting targeted argumentation activities such as warranting claims and building on others' reasoning

### Affordances
- Stog Script Theory Of Guidance Cable
