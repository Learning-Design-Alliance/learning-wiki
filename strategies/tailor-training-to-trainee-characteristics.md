# Tailor infectious-disease training programs to trainees' sex, education level, professional background, and prior experience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing personalized training pathways that account for learner characteristics: multi-level content catering to beginners and highly educated learners, and dedicated modules by background, "such as providing more practical operation training for nurses and more clinical decision-making training for physicians". It argues this tailoring enhances the efficacy and relevance of infectious disease training and should inform future training plans, especially for public health emergencies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of trainee demographics, education, professional background, and prior experience before or during program design
#### Constraints
- The study's cross-sectional design cannot establish causal associations
- Findings come from a single hospital setting and may not generalize across healthcare contexts

### Target Learners
- Medical staff of varied professional backgrounds and education levels in infectious disease settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical skills for infectious disease management
- Infection control competencies
