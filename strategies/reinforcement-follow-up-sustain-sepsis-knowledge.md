# Schedule reinforcement follow-up when designing sepsis education interventions to sustain nurses' knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because knowledge gains from CBT plus HFS faded by 90 days, the authors recommend building a reinforcement process into any sepsis educational intervention. They cite evidence that emailed content summaries every 2 weeks plus staff-meeting and huddle discussions supported 90-day knowledge retention. As they conclude, "a process for follow‐up which provides educational reinforcement must be considered in order to sustain an increase in nurses' knowledge."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A scheduled follow-up mechanism during the 90 days after the intervention, such as periodic email summaries and huddle discussions
#### Constraints
- The reinforcement evidence cited comes from a heart-failure education study, not from this sepsis intervention itself

### Target Learners
- registered nurses on general hospital wards

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of sepsis knowledge over 90 days
