# Design curriculum with decreasing levels of support across semesters to foster transfer

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that, if initial gains are not sustained, educators should "consider designing curriculum that includes decreasing levels of support to assist students to incrementally incorporate strategies over several semesters." The intent is to bolster transfer of study strategies to other academic settings, because the subsequent course in this study (General Chemistry 2) did not maintain the embedded-support format. This is a forward-looking design recommendation grounded in the study's open question about long-term success.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Support services continue to be provided to students initially identified as underprepared in subsequent, higher level courses
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a recommendation for future practice, contingent on whether initial success is sustained beyond the first course

### Target Learners
- College students initially identified as academically underprepared in mathematics

### Target Learning Goals
- Transfer study strategies to subsequent problem-solving courses
