# Time sleep-related support services for the freshman transition and high-stress academic periods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that student wellness programs and academic support services target sleep promotion at moments when sleep is most tightly coupled with academic outcomes: the freshman transition, where the sleep–GPA association is strongest, and high-stress periods such as midterms and finals, when sleep duration reaches its seasonal nadirs. The authors write that "targeted sleep interventions during high-stress academic periods (midterms, finals) could be beneficial". This is a recommendation grounded in observational associations, not a tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional wellness or academic support infrastructure capable of delivering sleep programming at identified points in the academic calendar
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is correlational; the authors state experimental or quasi-experimental designs would be needed to establish that improving sleep causes better academic performance

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate students
- undergraduate students during examination periods

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining adequate sleep to support academic performance
