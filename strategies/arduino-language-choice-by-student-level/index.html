Choose Arduino programming language by student level: visual languages (e.g., S4A, Entry) for elementary students, textual languages for high school students¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends matching the Arduino programming language to students' level rather than applying textual programming unconditionally. It suggests "it may be desirable for elementary school students to use visual programming languages such as Scratch for Arduino (S4A) and Entry and high school students to use textual programming languages such as sketch programs," and encourages teachers to experience maker activities with textual programming.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers who apply the textual programming language need more knowledge of C-language basic grammar and syntax, per the article's discussion.

Constraints¶

  • The textual-language advantage comes from a meta-analysis of Korean studies and contradicts several prior studies favoring visual languages; the article attributes it partly to teacher computer-teaching ability differences.

Target Learners¶

  • primary and secondary school students in Korea learning programming and engineering

Target Learning Goals¶

  • programming and engineering education through Arduino-based making activities

Claims¶

  • Effect sizes of Arduino-based education are statistically larger for textual than for visual programming language [~W]
