# School-based counselors and medical providers should screen for depression alongside abuse in adolescents and young adults

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that screening for depression accompany abuse screening when supporting at-risk AYAs of color, because depression rather than abuse alone predicted lower grades and the combination shaped outcomes in unexpected ways. It states there should be "recommendations for school-based counselors and medical providers to screen for depression along with abuse in AYAs in order to determine how best to support this population." The strategy targets identification of students needing academic and mental-health support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Screening instruments for depression (BDI-FS) and structured abuse screening methods as used in the study
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted in one urban adolescent health center with self-reported abuse and grades; the authors note reliability limits of self-reported abuse histories

### Target Learners
- Urban African American and Latino adolescents and young adults ages 14–24

### Target Learning Goals
- Identifying and supporting academic achievement among youth with abuse and/or depression histories
