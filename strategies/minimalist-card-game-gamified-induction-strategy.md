# Use minimalist card games (no more than eight instructional steps) as an accessible starting point for gamified learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend simple analogue card games as a low-investment route into gamified learning: "The games presented in this study provide a simple and accessible starting point for integrating gamified learning into the curriculum". They link this to minimalist design, noting "each of the three evaluated card games contained no more than eight instructional steps", which reduces confusion, simplifies delivery for educators and lets students play independently or collaboratively. The structure is described as a transferable model for other subject areas.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear instructions and strong constructive alignment so students understand how to play and the learning content is conveyed
#### Constraints
- No direct comparison to lecture or traditional approach was made in this study
- A key barrier is limited staff time and resources for developing or procuring such materials

### Target Learners
- foundation year students
- early-stage higher education students

### Target Learning Goals
- health and safety knowledge
- engagement and enjoyment
- transferable skill development
