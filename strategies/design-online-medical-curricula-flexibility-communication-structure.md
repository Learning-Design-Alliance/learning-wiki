# Designing future online medical curricula around flexibility, mindful communication, longitudinal patient contacts, and transparent structure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article derives design guidance from its explanatory model for curriculum planners adapting competency-based programmes under distancing. It recommends that future online curricula 'need to stress and support the potentialflexibility in time, pace and space, to foster binding relationships between students and teachers through mindful communication and longitudinal contacts, and to be transparently structured for learners to monitor their learning progress as a prerequisite for their motivation'. It further calls for clear interview guidelines and reflection elements on non-verbal communication.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear rules on procedure and content for online history-taking with patients
- A stable internet connection, working camera and microphone, and a single e-learning platform with unambiguous student identification
#### Constraints
- Digital patient encounters 'should generally remain a makeshift solution' according to the authors
- It remains to be seen whether experiences apply to curricula re-implementing direct patient contact

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- medical faculty teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical competency acquisition, patient communication, self-organized learning under distant-learning conditions
