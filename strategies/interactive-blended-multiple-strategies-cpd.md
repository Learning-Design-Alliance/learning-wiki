# Design continuing professional development as interactive blended programmes with multiple teaching strategies and self-set quality improvement targets

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educational strategies aimed at changing physicians' behaviour be interactive, use multiple teaching strategies, and incorporate active learner participation, multiple learning experiences and reflection on real-life experience, consistent with adult learning theory. It states: "It is recommended that educational strategies should be interactive and include multiple teaching strategies, for example, group discussions about evidence and setting quality improvement targets". The programme operationalized this through e-learning, focus groups, a learning conversation and self-formulated improvement targets executed in daily practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- content anchored in evidence-based practice guidelines
- facilitated small groups and trained teachers with a shared handbook
- follow-up over months so targets are executed in daily practice
#### Constraints
- the authors found only a moderate effect on laboratory-documented testing behaviour despite integrating multiple teaching strategies, concurring with evidence on the difficulty of changing physicians' behaviour

### Target Learners
- practising physicians and GP trainers in continuing medical education

### Target Learning Goals
- translating guideline recommendations into daily-practice clinical behaviour
