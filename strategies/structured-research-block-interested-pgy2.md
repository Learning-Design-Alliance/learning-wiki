# Offer a structured research block option for interested PGY-2 residents rather than universal scholarly activity requirements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that residency programs should offer a structured research block option allowing interested residents to conduct research during their PGY-2 year, rather than implementing universal requirements. The authors state that "the effectiveness of protected time for research is well-established in postgraduate medical education" but note that only 22-32% of teaching hospitals in Japan provide protected research time. This strategy targets supportive environments for interested trainees rather than mandatory participation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs need to identify residents interested in clinical research and provide protected research time during PGY-2
#### Constraints
- The article states its study does not identify an optimal start point or support a need for universal early participation

### Target Learners
- Japanese PGY-1 and PGY-2 junior residents

### Target Learning Goals
- EBM competency
- clinical research skills
- future academic engagement
