# Use open-ended research projects on QISE applications to societal challenges (especially medical technologies) as the vehicle for introducing quantum concepts to students without prerequisites

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 against) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy is to let students lead with their interests by choosing a societal challenge or current technology (e.g., drug discovery, brain imaging) and independently research how quantum sensing or computing applies to it, producing a poster or paper. The article states this approach can "allow students to lead with their interests, thereby getting them more deeply engaged in the material" and provides a framework for covering a topic that is otherwise difficult to cover beyond superficially. It was implemented in the Pathways quantum vision posters and the Ideas course final projects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A project prompt asking students to identify a current issue or technology addressable through quantum innovations, with research into breakthroughs needed, pros and cons, and obstacles
- Space in student products (papers give more room than posters) for discussing ethical issues
#### Constraints
- Students received little direct instruction on ethics and without readings and discussion time their ethical considerations stayed superficial
- Understanding is limited in a conceptual course with no math prerequisites
- Effective as recruitment only if it occurs early, likely in high school, with students who do not self-select into quantum programs

### Target Learners
- high school students
- undergraduate students
- graduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding quantum information science and engineering concepts and applications
- understanding limitations of quantum technologies
- ethical reasoning about emerging technologies
