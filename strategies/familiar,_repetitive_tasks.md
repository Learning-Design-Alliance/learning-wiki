# Familiar, Repetitive Tasks

## Description
Providing learners with familiar, quiet, and repetitive fine motor and visual tasks to complete at the beginning of the day or during transition times to promote calmness and focus. These tasks can include stringing beads, simple put-in tasks, sorting activities, and file folder tasks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A collection of familiar, repetitive tasks and materials. A designated space for completing these tasks. Clear guidelines for participation.
#### Constraints
- Some learners may find these tasks boring or unchallenging. Requires careful planning to integrate these tasks into the learning schedule. May require ongoing support and monitoring to ensure engagement.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Learners who experience anxiety, sensory overload, or difficulty transitioning between activities

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote calmness, improve focus, provide a sense of predictability

### Affordances
- Provides a sense of predictability and routine. Allows learners to focus on a simple, familiar task. Can be integrated into various learning activities and settings.

### Personalization
- Offer a variety of familiar, repetitive tasks to meet the diverse needs and preferences of learners. Allow learners to choose the type and duration of tasks that work best for them. Provide options for individual or group activities.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learners' engagement and participation in these tasks. Collect feedback from learners on the effectiveness of different tasks. Track changes in learners' behavior, attention, and emotional regulation.

## Impact
- Research indicates that familiar, repetitive tasks can reduce anxiety and promote calmness. Studies have shown that routine and predictability can improve attention and behavior in learners with autism spectrum disorder.
