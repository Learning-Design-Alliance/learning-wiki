# Require novices to use a goal-aligned form initially, then fade to a goal-free interface

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that because novices often have no plans and underuse help, "it seems prudent to require novices to use a goal-aligned form initially". As learners internalize plans and become more competent, the scaffolding can be faded, since memory theory suggests removing the goal-aligned form's contextual cues would increase transfer. This follows Singley and Anderson's argument that fading goal scaffolding should increase transfer to solving problems without the tutor.
