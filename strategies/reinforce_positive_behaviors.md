# Reinforce Positive Behaviors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Reinforcing positive behaviors means deliberately noticing and responding to desired learner actions — effort, cooperation, self-regulation, correct responses — with contingent praise, acknowledgment, or rewards, rather than attending only to misbehavior. Grounded in operant conditioning [Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md), the strategy increases the future frequency of reinforced behaviors when the reinforcer reliably and immediately follows the behavior and is explicitly tied to it.

## Design Implications

Contingent, specific praise for appropriate behavior is among the most consistently supported classroom management practices, increasing on-task behavior and reducing disruption when applied systematically [Simonsen et al.'s review identifies group contingency and praise practices as evidence-based.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1098300707311369) [+S]. Effectiveness depends on *what* is praised: feedback that names the behavior or process ("you checked your work before handing it in") outperforms generic approval ("good job") and person-directed praise [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. Rewards can backfire when they are tangible, expected, and unrelated to competence, undermining intrinsic motivation for inherently interesting tasks [Extrinsic rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation under specific conditions.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627) [~S] — though the undermining effect is smaller or absent when rewards convey competence or are delivered as informational praise [Meta-analytic evidence suggests rewards do not uniformly damage intrinsic motivation.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.115.3.363) [~M]. Design for autonomy support: frame reinforcement as information about competence rather than control [Autonomy-supportive environments sustain intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M], consistent with [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md).

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clearly defined, observable target behaviors that learners understand
- Immediate, contingent delivery — the reinforcer must follow the behavior, not precede or accompany it
- Specificity: praise that identifies the behavior and why it matters, not blanket approval
- A high ratio of positive to corrective interactions (commonly cited as at least 4:1 in PBIS implementations)

#### Constraints
- Tangible, expected rewards for tasks learners already find interesting can reduce intrinsic motivation [Extrinsic rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation under specific conditions.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627) [-M]
- Praise directed at the person ("you're so smart") promotes fixed-ability beliefs and avoidance of challenge relative to process praise [-M]
- Reinforcement that stops abruptly produces a rebound of the behavior; intermittent schedules and gradual fading are needed for maintenance [~S]
- Public praise can embarrass older learners or those with social anxiety, suppressing rather than strengthening the behavior [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Behavior-specific praise**: verbal acknowledgment naming the behavior ("You waited your turn to speak")
- **Group contingencies**: the whole class earns a shared reinforcer when collective criteria are met (e.g., [Classroom Dojo](https://www.classdojo.com)-style point systems)
- **Token economies**: points or tokens exchanged later for privileges; useful for establishing new behaviors, then faded
- **Private or nonverbal reinforcement**: nods, proximity, written comments — for learners for whom public attention is aversive
- **Self-reinforcement**: learners monitor and record their own behavior, shifting locus of control toward self-regulation

### Target Learners
- Young learners and novices still establishing basic classroom routines and work habits [+S]
- Learners with behavioral or attentional difficulties, for whom structured positive reinforcement is a core component of interventions like PBIS [+S]
- Adolescents and adults, for whom private, competence-focused acknowledgment works better than public token systems [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural and behavioral routines: establishing classroom norms, participation habits, self-regulation
- Motivational dispositions: persistence, help-seeking, effort attribution
- Social-emotional behaviors: cooperation, turn-taking, respectful discourse

### Instructions
1. Define 2–3 observable target behaviors and tell learners explicitly what they are and why they matter.
2. Actively scan for occurrences of the target behavior; reinforce within seconds, naming the behavior specifically (process-level feedback [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]).
3. Pair reinforcement with a brief rationale connecting the behavior to learning ("Rereading your draft caught two errors — that's what good writers do").
4. Prefer informational praise over tangible rewards for tasks with inherent interest [Extrinsic rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation under specific conditions.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627) [~S]; if using tokens or points, tie them to competence and progress.
5. Fade continuous reinforcement to an intermittent schedule, then shift learners toward self-monitoring and self-reinforcement to build durable, intrinsically motivated behavior [Autonomy-supportive environments sustain intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M].

## Related Strategies
- [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) — regular one-to-one contact creates the relationship context that makes individualized reinforcement credible
- [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) — coaches model and prompt the specific praise behaviors teachers then apply independently

## Examples
- **PBIS (Positive Behavioral Interventions & Supports)** ([https://www.pbis.org](https://www.pbis.org)) — school-wide systems in which staff deliver behavior-specific praise and token reinforcement at high ratios, with data-based fading as behavior stabilizes.
- **[Classroom Dojo](https://www.classdojo.com)** — digital point system for immediate, visible reinforcement of named behaviors; works best when points are tied to specific feedback rather than used as a standing leaderboard.
- **Good Behavior Game** — a group contingency in which teams earn privileges by collectively meeting behavioral criteria; strong experimental support for reducing disruption [+S].
