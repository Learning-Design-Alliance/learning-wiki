# Manipulate self-efficacy by combining social-comparative feedback with information about the malleability of the ability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A two-fold self-efficacy induction procedure: participants receive bogus normative feedback about their performance percentile relative to peers, plus a fictional scientific narrative stating that the ability required for the task is either entirely malleable with practice or fixed. The article reports that "both positive socio-comparative feedback and perceived malleability of a task has been shown to enhance while negative socio-comparative feedback or perceived non-malleability of a task has been shown to decrease self-efficacy beliefs", and the two are combined to maximise belief change in either direction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback must be delivered privately after a performance block, framed as a percentile comparison against a described peer sample, and paired with a consistent conception-of-ability message.
#### Constraints
- The feedback is deceptive; the article excluded all seven participants who reported concern about the accuracy of the feedback after debriefing interviews.

### Target Learners
- adult learners in experimental or training settings

### Target Learning Goals
- raising or lowering learners' self-efficacy beliefs about their task ability
