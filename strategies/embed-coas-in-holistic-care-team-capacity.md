# Embed required outcome assessments within holistic, patient-centered rehabilitation plans and expand multidisciplinary team capacity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Participants agreed that required assessments should be embedded within a holistic treatment plan considering patient function, quality of life, and personal goals, rather than serving reimbursement alone. The article reports that completing assessments for reimbursement can shift clinical focus away from holistic care, and that even modest staffing increases — such as adding a nurse or allied health professional — could significantly enhance care delivery by improving coordination and reducing individual workload. The New Brunswick adult SMA clinic, with a physiatrist-led multidisciplinary team in a tertiary rehabilitation centre, is regarded as a model of best practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multidisciplinary staffing and publicly funded PT/OT service capacity, which the article notes vary substantially across Canadian sites
#### Constraints
- The physiatrist-led multidisciplinary model is not common and does not reflect the reality at most centres across Canada

### Target Learners
- clinical evaluators and program leads in neuromuscular care

### Target Learning Goals
- integrating outcome assessment into meaningful clinical decision-making and comprehensive rehabilitation
