# Use repeated mystery shopping visits with immediate verbal feedback and self-evaluation scoresheets as a coaching technique for pharmacy staff

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention uses trained student mystery shoppers presenting scripted direct-product requests weekly, then returning within 5 minutes to give staff a blank scoresheet for self-evaluation and verbal feedback based on performance. The authors conclude "Mystery shopping with feedback should be explored as a means to train pharmacy staff in an appropriate provision of non-prescription medicines" and suggest exploring the methodology in other healthcare settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Staff consent including consent to audio recording must be obtained before visits occur
- Mystery shoppers must be trained to enact scenarios and provide feedback (here a 2-day training programme)
- Feedback should be delivered promptly after the interaction, within 5 minutes in this study
#### Constraints
- The study was not truly covert because staff consented in advance, which may have influenced handling of requests
- Pharmacies were restricted to metropolitan Sydney and participation was voluntary, possibly favouring better-performing pharmacies
- The datasheet used has not been validated for use in this setting

### Target Learners
- Community pharmacy staff, both pharmacists and non-pharmacist assistants

### Target Learning Goals
- Appropriate management of non-prescription medicine requests for minor ailments, including questioning, counselling and referral
