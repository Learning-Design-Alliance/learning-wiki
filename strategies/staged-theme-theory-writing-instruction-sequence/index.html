A staged 10-week writing instruction sequence: two weeks on theme/rheme, three weeks on TP patterns, then consolidation through reading and model-text analysis¶

Strategy · All strategies

Description¶

The article's instructional recipe embeds Theme Theory teaching inside normal classroom writing lessons. The author "spent two weeks on the in struction of theme and rheme and three weeks on thematic progression", then devoted three weeks to consolidation: applying thematic analysis in reading comprehension and appreciating CET-4 model writing from the perspective of thematic structure. Adequate time was deliberately given because the theory was new and difficult for students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The same course book and syllabus across classes
  • Adequate learning time, since Theme Theory is new and difficult for university students

Constraints¶

  • The sequence was trialed over only 10 weeks with one major in one university, so generalization "has to be made with caution"

Target Learners¶

  • Second-year Chinese university English majors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Writing coherence
  • Overall EFL writing proficiency

Affordances¶

  • Theme Theory Thematic Progression Framework

Claims¶

  • After Theme Theory instruction, students used a broader variety of theme types, especially textual, multiple and clausal themes [+W]
  • After instruction, the experimental class used more thematic progression patterns and less constant-pattern predominance than the controlled class [+W]
  • The two classes were equivalent in writing competence and thematic structure before instruction [+W]
  • Sample analysis indicates good compositions are distinguished by varied, well-linked theme choices and thematic progression patterns [+W]
  • Instruction in Theme Theory improves EFL students' writing coherence more than traditional form-focused writing instruction [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Guide reading with targeted questions that direct attention to grammatical, lexical and structural features needed for the writing task
