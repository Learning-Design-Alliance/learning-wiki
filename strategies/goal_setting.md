# Goal Setting

## Description
Goal setting establishes specific performance or learning targets that learners pursue over a defined period. It is carried out by having learners (or instructors with learners) articulate concrete, measurable objectives — ideally proximal subgoals en route to distal aims — and then monitor progress against them. The mechanism is motivational and attentional: specific goals focus effort and persistence in ways that vague intentions ("do your best") do not.

## Design Implications

Goal setting is one of the most robust findings in organizational and educational psychology: specific, difficult goals reliably outperform "do your best" instructions for tasks on which effort and persistence matter [Locke & Latham's goal-setting theory](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.2.267) [+S]. Proximal subgoals are especially powerful for learners, because near-term targets provide immediate feedback about progress and build a sense of efficacy that sustains engagement with distal goals [Proximal subgoals raise self-efficacy and free-choice persistence in children.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.73.5.648) [+S]. Goal setting is a core phase of [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md): learners who set process-oriented goals and monitor progress against them show stronger achievement than those who only receive external targets [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Goals that are specific and measurable ("solve 10 two-step equations correctly" rather than "get better at algebra")
- A proximal/distal structure: short-term subgoals that ladder up to a longer-term target
- A feedback or monitoring mechanism so learners can see progress ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), progress dashboards, [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
- Learner participation in setting the goal, which supports ownership and autonomy [Autonomy-supportive contexts raise intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Goals focused narrowly on performance outcomes can reduce intrinsic interest and exploration in tasks that are already intrinsically motivating [~M] — specific goals narrow attention to the measured behavior at the expense of unmeasured learning
- For complex or novel tasks, aggressive performance goals can impair learning; learning-oriented goals (focus on strategy acquisition) work better until basic competence is established [~S]
- Goals set entirely by others, without learner input or perceived rationale, can undermine autonomy and produce compliance rather than commitment [Autonomy-supportive contexts raise intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [-M]
- Unmonitored goals decay quickly; without [feedback](../elements/assessment.md) and progress checks, goal-setting effects fade within days [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Process vs. outcome goals**: process goals ("use the planning template") suit novices; outcome goals ("score 80%") suit learners with established strategies
- **Self-set vs. assigned**: assigned goals work nearly as well when rationale is explained; participatively set goals increase commitment on difficult tasks [~W]
- **Implementation intentions**: pairing a goal with an if-then plan ("when I sit down to study, I will first review yesterday's errors") substantially increases follow-through [+M]
- **Micro-goals**: breaking a session into achievable micro-goals reduces avoidance in low-efficacy learners — see [Achievable Micro-Goals](achievable_micro-goals.md)

### Target Learners
- Learners with low self-efficacy or avoidance tendencies, for whom proximal subgoals build efficacy through early success [Proximal subgoals raise self-efficacy and free-choice persistence in children.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.73.5.648) [+S]
- Adolescents and adults capable of self-monitoring; younger learners need externally managed goal structures and frequent check-ins
- Underperforming students benefit disproportionately from structured goal-setting interventions [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Persistence and effort regulation on extended tasks ([Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md))
- Skill development requiring distributed practice over time [Distributed practice improves retention.](../claims/distributed-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S]
- Metacognitive development: planning, monitoring, and self-evaluation phases of self-regulation

### Instructions
1. Diagnose the learner's current performance level to calibrate goal difficulty ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
2. Co-construct a specific, challenging distal goal with the learner, explaining its rationale
3. Break the distal goal into proximal subgoals — daily or weekly targets that are achievable within days ([Achievable Micro-Goals](achievable_micro-goals.md))
4. Attach an implementation intention to each subgoal (when/where/how)
5. Schedule progress monitoring with feedback ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))
6. Adjust difficulty upward as competence grows, shifting from process goals to outcome goals

## Examples
- **Writing portfolios (secondary English)**: students set a weekly revision goal (e.g., "strengthen one paragraph's evidence") tracked in a conference log with the teacher, then evaluate against a rubric.
- **Khan Academy** (https://www.khanacademy.org): learners set weekly energy-point and mastery goals; dashboards display progress toward course mastery, making the proximal/distal structure visible.
- **Duolingo** (https://www.duolingo.com): daily XP goals and streaks operationalize proximal goal setting with immediate feedback; the streak mechanic leverages goal-gradient effects on persistence.
- **Morisano et al. (2010) goal-setting intervention**: struggling university students who completed a structured goal-setting program improved GPA relative to controls, with effects mediated by reduced negative affect.
