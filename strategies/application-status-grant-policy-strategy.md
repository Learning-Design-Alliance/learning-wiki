# Use application-status information to sharpen grant eligibility policy: lower the threshold rather than raise grant amounts, and explain eligibility rules to discourage ineligible applications

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's policy analysis of the stratum results recommends that, from a cost-effective perspective, education administrations lower the eligibility threshold to allow more applicants to receive grants rather than increase the grant amount, since effects are larger for always-applicants. It also recommends that administrations "better explain the rule of eligibility to potential applicants to discourage ineligible students from applying", reducing unnecessary processing effort for students and administration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Availability of application-status data alongside eligibility and receipt data
- Principal stratification analysis revealing heterogeneous effects across application strata
#### Constraints
- Recommendation is specific to the Italian university grant system with threshold-based eligibility around 15000 euros

### Target Learners
- first-year university students from low-income families

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing university dropout
- efficient allocation of financial aid resources
