# Use a DPI cut-off score of 3 to warrant specialist referral for root canal treatment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends retaining a DPI overall score of 3 as the referral threshold because "DPI's broader assessment justifies the current cut‐off of score 3 for specialist referral due to the increased agreement with ECS, E‐CAT and EA at this threshold." The strategy works by summing DPI domain scores (medical/dental/social history, structural integrity, endodontic status, periodontal condition) and referring cases scoring 3 or more to a specialist unit, since additive points in other domains typically push cases beyond a score of 2 even when the endodontic domain alone would warrant an advanced pathway.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- DPI domain scores must be obtainable from patient records and radiographs, including medical history and restorative status
#### Constraints
- Validated only in a secondary-care dental hospital sample with predominantly high-complexity cases; the authors state a future primary-care study is needed to gauge cut-off scores for level 1 and 2 cases
- Radiographic-only assessment of structural integrity, particularly beneath full coverage crowns, may lead to inaccurate pre-operative complexity scores

### Target Learners
- General dental practitioners making referral decisions
- Postgraduate endodontology trainees and specialist units receiving referrals

### Target Learning Goals
- Selecting cases appropriate for specialist rather than generalist management
- Ensuring trainees receive cases matched to their skill level
