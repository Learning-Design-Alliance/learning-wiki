Strategies

Adopt a girls' enrollment and persistence program across a broader set of schools, with a more rigorous follow-up study¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the GOAL results, the authors recommended that the Liberia Ministry of Education adopt a similar program across a broader set of schools, noting that "individual scholarships to girls are more effective than grants to schools" while strongly caveating that comparison. They also recommend the program as "a good candidate for a more rigorous study to confirm these findings," given the conservative bias in the current estimates.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A comparison-group design with baseline outcome data to estimate effects

Constraints¶

  • Current estimates are conservative because crossover effects on boys are likely stronger for grants than for scholarships

Target Learners¶

  • Girls in Liberian primary schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Enrollment
  • persistence
  • completion and grade promotion

Claims¶

  • All three GOAL intervention models increased girls' enrollment, completion, and grade promotion in Liberian primary schools [+W]
  • Scholarship effects on girls' outcomes were larger than school-grant effects [+W]
  • Effects on completion and promotion were almost twice as large as effects on enrollment [+W]
  • Estimated effects ranged from 8 to more than 75 percent of baseline levels depending on intervention and outcome [+W]
