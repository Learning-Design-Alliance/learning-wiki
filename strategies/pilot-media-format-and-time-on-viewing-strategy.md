# Pilot-test media format before adopting mobile social learning technologies, and log time-on-viewing in future studies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend a preparatory and measurement strategy for educators considering mobile video learning. They advise that "it may be helpful to conduct a pilot study with the goal of determining which media format is most beneficial for medical students' learning and demonstrating clinical skills before making a final decision about adopting" mobile social learning technologies. They also recommend that future researchers measure time-on-viewing (also called time-on-learning) the videos and assess where students viewed them, since "Time-on-viewing is a significant factor in learning a skill and may be logged in a personal journal by the learner."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A pilot study comparing media formats should precede adoption decisions; future studies should measure time-on-viewing and location-of-learning, potentially via a learner-maintained personal journal.
#### Constraints
- The study's data suggest the intervention group accessed the videos just before the 3-month follow-up assessment rather than throughout the 12 weeks, so viewing behavior must be tracked to interpret effects.

### Target Learners
- Medical students in learning resource center settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical skills competency, confidence, and knowledge retention
