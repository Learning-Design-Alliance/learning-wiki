# Design lab activities and lectures around measurement uncertainty: non-verification measurements, peer data comparison, and dedicated uncertainty lectures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The transformed course replaced verification labs with activities where students measure unknown quantities, use prior measurements to make predictions, and discuss results with peers, "comparing data to decide if their diﬀerent results agree with each other." Four of six lectures focused entirely on measurement uncertainty concepts, presenting measurement as sampling from a distribution. These design choices gave students repeated opportunities to consider and communicate both the value and uncertainty of results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lab activities must involve measuring quantities students would not know beforehand, rather than verification of known values; lectures and activities must be aligned to an explicitly stated measurement-uncertainty learning goal.
#### Constraints
- The transformed course still operated in many ways as a traditional lab course, with prescriptive activities and significant scaffolding, due to logistical constraints such as 20-30 graduate teaching assistants and 35-45 weekly lab sections.

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics lab students

### Target Learning Goals
- demonstrating set-like reasoning when evaluating measurements
