# Use VSA error analysis to derive learning points for clinical teachers, such as teaching judicious, step-wise use of investigations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that clinical teachers use the error types identified from incorrect VSA answers as prompts for reflection and teaching change outside examination preparation. For each of the four errors the authors derived a learning point, for example to "Teach students about the judicious and step‐wise use of investigations alongside the avoidance of unnecessary tests." Other learning points cover identifying the most important investigation result, requesting radiology specifically, and avoiding trigger words as the main determinant of pattern recognition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clinical teachers need information about the common errors identified in student VSA answers to use as a prompt for reflection.
#### Constraints
- The study is descriptive and retrospective; the article states further work is needed to determine optimal approaches and to prospectively evaluate the impact of teaching changes.
- The article states the frequency of each error type depends in part on question content, so the discussion may not represent the most prevalent errors across the entire domain.
- The article states errors may not be reproducible if the assessment method is widely adopted and may vary locally between medical schools.

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- applied medical knowledge
- clinical reasoning and diagnostic investigation
