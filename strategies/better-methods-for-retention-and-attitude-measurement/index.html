Strategies

Adopt better methodologies and validated tools for identifying long-term EBP competency retention and for measuring attitude change¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review calls for future studies to "adopt better methodologies and validated tools for identifying EBP competencies retention in the long term and for determining the extent to which attitudes change following the implementation of an educational intervention". It notes validated tools for knowledge and skill acquisition exist and are widely used, but similar validated tools for attitude change are lacking, and self-reports are unreliable for long-term attitude measurement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Validated assessment instruments beyond student self-reports
  • Follow-up assessments extending beyond immediate post-test intervals

Constraints¶

  • The review states most studies' pre- and post-test duration was too short, which may lead to recall bias

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate health students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Long-term retention of EBP competencies and reliable measurement of attitude change

Claims¶

  • EBP attitude change depends on measurement timing: immediate post-test measures show no difference while longer-term measures show improvement [+W]
  • A theory-based EBP intervention produced a small significant increase in EBP use but no significant difference in EBP future use [~W]
  • The duration of EBP interventions, varying from some hours to even months, was not related to students' EBP competence [~W]
