# Teach literature searching with an iterative search-sort-inspect-repeat algorithm using field tags, search-string perturbations, and citation sorting

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a four-step algorithm for finding influential papers on a general topic or a specific research project. Searching uses field tags and perturbations; sorting orders results by citations or date; inspecting top-cited articles reveals recurring keywords that let students refine the search string and repeat. The authors state that "Searching the literature is a skill, and students need to realize that “practice makes perfect.”"

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a database that allows sorting by number of citations, such as ISI Web of Science
- An initial idea of appropriate general search terms developed through discussions with an experienced researcher
#### Constraints
- ISI and similar databases are usually subscription-only services; Google Scholar citations are inaccurate and it does not allow advanced sorting; arXiv contains un-refereed material and does not enable advanced sorting

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students
- graduate students
- novice researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- efficiently find influential papers on a topic
- avoid duplicating prior work
- develop a thorough yet concise list of references
