# Use multiple-choice review questions constructed to guarantee every student engages with a known durable misconception

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The author used an in-class multiple-choice problem on parity in quantum field theories to review material and expose a durable misconception: students think a field's behavior under parity is intrinsic to the field, when it is actually a property of the Lagrangian. Multiple-choice problems "can be constructed — here, by including option c) — to guarantee that everyone engages with the misconception," and they leave no question about whether students understood the idea. Despite earlier readings and explicit discussions, a large fraction of the class chose c), c), and c), and almost no one had a perfect score.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The correct answers and the misconception-triggering distractor must be known in advance from prior years of teaching
- Review activities should come after the relevant reading and force reliance on memory rather than texts or notes
