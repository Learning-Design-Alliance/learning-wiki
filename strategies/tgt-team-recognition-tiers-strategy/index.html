Use TGT tournaments with team recognition tiers to motivate students¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes teams' recognition as the stage where "students feel compelled to study physics because their group can be a super team with an average score of 50. It can be a great team with the mean score of 45. In addition, it can be a good team with the average score of 40". Teachers can implement tournament points pooled within teams and recognise teams at these printed average-score tiers.

Claims¶

  • Learning model and motivation interact significantly in determining physics learning outcomes [~W]
  • High-motivation students taught with TGT achieve higher physics learning outcomes than high-motivation students taught conventionally [+W]
  • Students taught with the TGT cooperative learning model achieve higher physics learning outcomes overall than students taught with conventional learning models [+W]
  • Low-motivation students taught with TGT show learning outcomes not significantly lower than conventional students, though the two cells differ significantly [~W]
