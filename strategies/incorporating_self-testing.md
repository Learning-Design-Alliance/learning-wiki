# Incorporating Self-Testing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Self-testing reframes testing as a learning event rather than a measurement event: learners answer questions — flashcards, practice quizzes, free recall prompts — before, during, or after instruction, with no grade consequences. The act of retrieving information from memory strengthens retention far more than rereading, and the results show learners *what they do not yet know*, directing subsequent study. The instructor's role shifts from administering tests to supplying retrieval opportunities and teaching learners to interpret their own errors.

## Design Implications

Retrieval practice produces durable, transferable memory traces that rereading and highlighting do not [Testing effect improves long-term retention relative to restudying.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S]. The diagnostic function matters as much as the mnemonic one: self-testing calibrates learners' judgments of knowing, countering the fluency illusions created by rereading [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]. Errors made during low-stakes testing are not harmful — when followed by feedback and when learners are confident-but-wrong, correcting them produces especially strong retention [High-confidence errors improve retention after correction.](../claims/high-confidence-errors-improve-retention.md) [+M]. Feedback should follow quickly and address the task and process, not just supply the correct answer [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A bank of questions that targets the same knowledge later assessed summatively, at comparable difficulty
- Immediate or rapid feedback so errors are corrected while the retrieval attempt is still active ([Practice](../elements/practice.md) followed by [Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md))
- Explicit framing that these are learning tools, not surveillance — no grade penalty for wrong answers
- Time in class or in the platform workflow for retrieval attempts, not just content delivery

#### Constraints
- Testing only *recognition* (multiple choice) when the summative assessment demands *recall* produces weaker transfer; format should match the target task
- Self-testing without feedback lets errors persist and can entrench misconceptions [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [-M]
- Learners with high prior knowledge may gain little from simple factual retrieval and benefit more from generative formats such as [Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md) [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]
- If stakes creep back in (leaderboards, public scores), anxiety and surface strategies return, undermining the reframing that makes the strategy work

#### Implementation Variability
- **Instructor-paced:** opening or closing each class with a two-question quiz (low-tech, no grading)
- **Learner-paced:** flashcard or quiz apps with spaced scheduling; learners choose topics where confidence is lowest
- **Free recall:** brain dumps or concept mapping from memory after reading, requiring no question bank
- **Pretesting:** asking questions *before* instruction; even failed retrieval attempts prime subsequent learning [~M]

### Target Learners
- All learners benefit, but gains are largest for those who would otherwise study by rereading [Testing effect improves long-term retention relative to restudying.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S]
- Anxious learners, because frequent no-stakes exposure to test formats reduces test-specific arousal
- Learners with poor calibration, who overestimate their mastery after fluent rereading [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Long-term retention of factual and conceptual knowledge
- Metacognitive calibration: accurate judgments of what is and is not yet known
- Self-regulated learning: using self-assessment to plan further study [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Identify the knowledge and skills the summative assessment will target; write or select questions at that level of challenge.
2. Schedule short retrieval events early and often — start of class, end of segment, weekly — rather than one pre-exam review ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
3. Require an actual retrieval attempt (write, say, or select) before any material is consulted; guessing counts.
4. Provide immediate feedback focused on why an answer is right, not just what it is ([Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md)).
5. Revisit missed items after a delay — spacing the retest multiplies the retention benefit [Testing effect improves long-term retention relative to restudying.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S].
6. Teach learners to read their own results: what to restudy, what to stop studying — making the diagnostic explicit.

## Related Strategies
- Spaced repetition — scheduling self-tests over time compounds the retrieval benefit
- Formative feedback loops — self-testing supplies the data that feedback acts on
- Rereading replacement — substituting retrieval for passive review in study-skills instruction

## Examples
- **[Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net)** — spaced-repetition flashcards that schedule each item for review just before predicted forgetting; widely used in medical education.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — mastery quizzes between video segments; wrong answers route learners to hints and prerequisite content rather than a grade.
- **Daily low-stakes quizzing in introductory psychology** — Roediger and colleagues' classroom studies showed students earned roughly a grade-step more on exam questions that had appeared on prior quizzes.
- **"Brain dump" closures** — students write everything they remember from a lesson on a blank sheet, then compare against notes; no technology or question bank required.
