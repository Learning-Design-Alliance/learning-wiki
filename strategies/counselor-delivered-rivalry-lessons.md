# Deliver rivalry curriculum through school counselors in small-group or one-on-one settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that curriculum and story developers consult with school counselors rather than classroom teachers to deliver the Sport Rivalry Man lessons. Counselors meet students regularly in individual or small group settings, allowing in-depth conversation about the stories' outcomes and tailoring of lessons; a smaller number of trained professionals also permits "a more consistent, efficient, and ultimately effective feedback loop" between developers and implementers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- regular communication between researchers, story and curriculum developers, and counselors
- counselor access to students on a repeated and regular basis
#### Constraints
- the quasi-experiment itself was conducted in a college setting; grade-school use is proposed as future work given proper coordination with school counselors

### Target Learners
- elementary-age students
- students working with school counselors individually or in small groups

### Target Learning Goals
- addressing bullying and appropriate decision-making
- understanding group membership and inclusion
