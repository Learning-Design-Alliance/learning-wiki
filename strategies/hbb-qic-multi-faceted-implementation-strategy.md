# Combine HBB training with daily skill checks, pre-birth preparation, self-evaluation checklists, and weekly review meetings to sustain neonatal resuscitation competency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 11 claims (9 for, 2 against) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

## Description
The HBB-QIC is a multi-faceted implementation strategy pairing the two-day Helping Babies Breathe training with ongoing workplace activities: "daily bag-and-mask skill checks, preparation for resuscitation before every birth, self-evaluation and peer review on neonatal resuscitation skills and weekly review meetings". Units set QIC goals, place mannequins at unit entrances for daily checks, attach self-evaluation checklists to clinical record forms, mount HBB schematic posters at resuscitation tables for peer review, and hold weekly review meetings facilitated by the unit in-charge. The strategy aims to translate short-course training into retained clinical competency among birth attendants in low-resource delivery units.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained facilitators (HBB trainers and unit in-charges) to run the two-day training and weekly review meetings
- Equipment and materials in each delivery unit: bag-and-mask kits, bulb suctions, mannequins for daily skill checks, self-evaluation checklists attached to clinical record forms, and HBB schematic posters at resuscitation tables
#### Constraints
- Weekly review meetings "may not be suitable in primary care settings where there are fewer nursing staff"; the combination of strategies has not been evaluated in primary care settings

### Target Learners
- Nurse-midwives and other birth attendants in hospital delivery units in low-resource settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Neonatal resuscitation knowledge and bag-and-mask ventilation skill retention at 6 months after training
