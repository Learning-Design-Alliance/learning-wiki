# Teachers can use the REACT strategy to train students' mathematical representation ability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the lower mean error percentage of the REACT group, the article recommends that "REACT strategy can be used by teachers to train students' mathematical representation ability". The recommendation rests on students being actively involved in constructing knowledge, working through worksheets, and freely asking the teacher and peers when difficulty arises, so difficulties are resolved and straightened out more quickly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation of the five REACT phases (Relating, Experiencing, Applying, Cooperating, Transferring) with hands-on activity and worksheets enabling student-teacher and peer interaction
#### Constraints
- Supported here by a quasi-experiment with static-group comparison, no pretest, and no reported significance testing

### Target Learners
- eighth-grade junior high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- mathematical representation ability (visual, verbal, symbolic)

### Affordances
- [React Strategy Contextual Learning](../theories/constructivism.md)
