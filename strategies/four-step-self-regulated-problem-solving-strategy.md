# Teach a four-step verbalized problem-solving strategy (identify problem, propose solution, implement, evaluate)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students were taught to ask and verbalize four questions in sequence: "What is the problem?", "What can I do about it?", implement the proposed solution, and "Did that fix the problem?" Students rehearsed the questions aloud until they could recall the steps, then used the strategy (with cue cards when necessary) during daily classroom challenges. Each student was given three to five daily teacher-arranged opportunities to practice the steps on their self-selected target behavior.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers create three to five daily opportunities for students to perform the target behavior and practice the problem-solving steps; students repeat the questions aloud until the trainer is assured they understand and can recall the steps and sequence
#### Constraints
- Students were not expected to ask the questions out loud in the general education classroom setting; in this study, instruction was delivered in general education content classes over three to nine instructional sessions

### Target Learners
- students with mental retardation or developmental disabilities
- middle school students in general education

### Target Learning Goals
- classroom social and communication skills
- following directions
- contributing to class discussion
