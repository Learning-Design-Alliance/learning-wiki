Design assignments and assessment environments with observability of cognitive participation in mind¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that educational institutions design assignments and assessment environments so that cognitively relevant forms of participation leave examinable evidence. It argues that "educational institutions can potentially design assignments and assessment environments with observability in mind", noting Direction is comparatively easy to elicit because students must ordinarily initiate requests, while Integration and Evaluation may require more deliberate opportunities for re-entry. The objective is not to prescribe a single interaction sequence but to require students to do something with AI contributions rather than merely receive them.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Activities must create opportunities for learner re-entry after AI contributions so that uptake, inspection, and judgment can become observable

Constraints¶

  • Observability is partly a property of the task environment; the article states this design problem deserves further investigation rather than claiming a validated intervention

Target Learners¶

  • students using generative AI in coursework

Target Learning Goals¶

  • applying, comparing, inspecting, diagnosing, and judging AI contributions rather than merely receiving them

Affordances¶

  • Epistemic Ownership Four Capacities Framework

Related Strategies¶

  • Environmental Interaction Analysis
  • Tag assessments with explicit taxonomy levels to analyse whether exam difficulty distribution matches instructional intent
