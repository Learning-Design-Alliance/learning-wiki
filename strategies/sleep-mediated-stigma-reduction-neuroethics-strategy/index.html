Sleep-mediated social evaluation updating as a candidate approach for reducing stigma toward disadvantaged groups, with mandatory neuroethical safeguards¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes applying sleep-mediated social evaluation updating — playing acoustic name+positive trait pairs during slow-wave sleep — to reshape evaluations of people carrying negative social labels, noting "possibilities for changing people's stigma and attitudes toward disadvantaged groups". Because the same mechanisms could enable covert influence (e.g., political propaganda or commercial manipulation), the authors pair this application strategy with a call for "proactive neuroethical guidelines", consent protocols, and regulatory frameworks governing application boundaries.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Auditory cueing must be delivered during slow-wave sleep with continuous polysomnographic monitoring so cues are not played during wakefulness.
  • Pre-sleep encoding of face-name-trait associations must occur first, since the manipulation builds on encoded associations.

Constraints¶

  • The authors warn sleep-based interventions "could be exploited for unethical purposes" and demand consent protocols and application boundaries.
  • Evidence so far is a single controlled laboratory study with young Chinese adults and only unattractive female faces; no wakefulness control or follow-up test was included.

Target Learners¶

  • adult learners in laboratory sleep-learning contexts

Target Learning Goals¶

  • updating social evaluations and attitudes toward stigmatized individuals

Claims¶

  • Mere acoustic name exposure during sleep without positive traits does not enhance mental facial representations of the named person [~W]
  • Sleep cueing with name+positive trait pairs biases trait memory toward more positive and less negative memories of the cued person [+W]
  • Sleep cueing with name+positive trait pairs improves explicit attitudes: likeability, social willingness, and trust choices favor the cued person [+W]
  • Sleep cueing with name+positive trait pairs improves implicit attitudes (IAT D-scores) toward the cued person [+W]
  • Acoustic name+positive trait pair cueing during slow-wave sleep increases the perceived attractiveness of the cued person's mental facial representation [+W]
  • The number of slow oscillations during sleep is positively correlated with attractiveness ratings of faces associated with cued names but not uncued names [+W]
