# Pair opposing-result groups and have them exchange raw data to drive discussion of data analysis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy structures the moment of cognitive conflict in the bouncing-ball lab. When the two groups reach opposite conclusions, the instructor pairs neighboring groups with opposing results and has them email each other their raw position vs. time data so each team can examine the other's work. The article reports students first accuse each other of error, then discover "the other people’s data gives the examining students’ initial results."
