# Shift electrodynamics instruction toward interpretation of equations and graphical, qualitative exercises at the expense of algebraic manipulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that traditional electrodynamics instruction, which heavily emphasizes calculation, should be rebalanced: 'we suggest more attention should be paid to the interpretation of the equations and setting up the problem at the expense of doing algebraic manipulations.' They also advise putting more effort into graphical-representation exercises in both physics and mathematics instruction, since such representations help conceptualize the abstract structures of vector calculus. Future plans include student interviews and an iteratively designed tutorial on Maxwell's equations in differential form.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must be redesigned so students practice linking mathematics and physics with a more qualitative approach
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is grounded in one small exploratory study (N=30 pretest, N=19 post-test) at a single institution

### Target Learners
- second-year university physics majors in intermediate electrodynamics

### Target Learning Goals
- interpreting divergence and curl in graphical and physical contexts
- conceptual understanding of Maxwell's equations in differential form
