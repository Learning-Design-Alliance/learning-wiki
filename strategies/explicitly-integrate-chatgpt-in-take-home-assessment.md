# Explicitly integrate ChatGPT into take-home assessments rather than banning or detecting it

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends consciously involving ChatGPT in take-home exams instead of only avoiding or detecting its use, arguing that "the required involvement of ChatGPT seemingly improved the integrity of the take-home assessment". Instructors pose higher-order questions as usual, mandate ChatGPT as one primary source among others, and evaluate the prompt handling, the commentary on AI responses, and the concluding statement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions must invoke critical thinking and problem solving, and learners must report prompts and responses in raw form before commenting and concluding
#### Constraints
- The author frames the outcome cautiously, writing that neither the quality of the assessment nor the integrity is compromised based on the theory and practice of one trial

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in technical education programs

### Target Learning Goals
- critical evaluation of AI-generated answers and integrity-preserving assessment practice
