# Use reassurance and open discussions about medical student syndrome to support health professions students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation is that health colleges address MSS, which it calls "a growing problem in health colleges", through reassurance and discussion. It states that "the prevention of this phenomenon may be greatly enhanced by reassurance and discussions about this syndrome", and additionally recommends more studies and focus group interviews with students to explore the problem deeply. The discussion also suggests targeted psychological training prior to employment or internship might prevent distress.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation in health professions colleges where students study diseases and may develop health-related anxiety during training
#### Constraints
- The study design does not allow conclusions about causation, and the recommendation is stated by the authors rather than tested in this study

### Target Learners
- health professions students (medical, dental, pharmacy, EMS, nursing)

### Target Learning Goals

- protecting academic performance
