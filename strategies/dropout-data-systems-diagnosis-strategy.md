# Use longitudinal student-level data systems to diagnose dropout scope and identify at-risk students before key transitions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The guide's first recommendation directs schools to build comprehensive, longitudinal, student-level databases with unique IDs covering absences, grade retention, and low achievement, and to review data regularly with emphasis on transitions. The panel states that 'Regularly analyzing student data is the critical first step both for determining the scope of the dropout problem and for identifying the specific students who are at risk of dropping out.' Schools should designate staff to monitor incoming, current, and departed students and follow up as needed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal student-level databases with unique statewide identifiers
- A designated staff member or team to regularly monitor data and follow up with students
- Accurate withdrawal and transfer documentation with verified enrollment
#### Constraints
- No studies directly evaluate the effect of using data systems on staying in school, progressing in school, or completing school, so the evidence level is low

### Target Learners
- Middle and high school students at risk of dropping out

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate diagnosis of dropout risk and timely identification of students needing targeted support
