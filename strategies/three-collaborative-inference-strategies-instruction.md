# Teach three collaboration strategies for drawing collaborative inferences: information connection, information pooling, and information take-up

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's explicit strategy instruction taught students three named collaboration strategies in an approximately 850-word expository text read before the training case. The "information connection strategy" is a general inference-drawing strategy of looking for interconnections between incoming and old information; the "information pooling strategy" tells speakers to share information they cannot yet fit into the bigger picture; the "information take-up strategy" tells listeners to focus reactions on the new information in a partner's contributions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Strategy application must be practised on a carefully designed training case in which collaborative inferences are needed for the correct solution
#### Constraints
- In the experiment, instruction without adaptive tutoring during practice offered no measurable advantage over case-based training alone at transfer

### Target Learners
- university-level students collaborating on distributed-information problem-solving tasks

### Target Learning Goals
- drawing collaborative inferences that integrate partners' unique information
