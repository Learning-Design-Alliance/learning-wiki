# Conduct competence training as practice-based group-learning with familiar colleagues and context-aware instructors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's findings support organising competence-building courses as group-learning among colleagues from the same unit, with instructors who know the participants and their workplace. Participants reported this created a safe environment for practical exercises, enabled personalised feedback and locally relevant patient cases, and gave the training "transfer value" to clinical practice. The article recommends regular group-learning sessions and identifying specific learning and training needs, particularly concerning task delegation and standardised tools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Familiar colleagues and instructors from the participants' own context; cases and scoring tools relevant to the participants' clinical settings
#### Constraints
- Courses with unknown instructors were experienced as less relevant and produced reluctance to disclose competence gaps

### Target Learners
- Interprofessional primary healthcare teams (RNs, assistant nurses, social educators)

### Target Learning Goals
- Shared observational competence, interprofessional communication and collaboration
