# Individualize simulation training schedules by profession and skill element rather than applying one fixed interval

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends tailoring training frequency and dose to the individual provider: "This offers the potential for individualized training schedules." Because skill elements differed in whether frequency or dose predicted success, and because professions differed in clinical exposure and required training loads (midwives and pediatricians needed no cases for ventilation rate while anesthetists needed 36), personalized schedules could make efficient use of HCPs' valuable time in busy health care services.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Logged training data with objective outcome measures to identify per-person or per-profession thresholds
#### Constraints
- The single model for the whole diverse group was, in the authors' words, a rather blunt tool; analysis by profession was only possible for one skill element

### Target Learners
- Health care providers from multiple professions involved in neonatal resuscitation

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintenance of neonatal ventilation competence at minimal training cost
