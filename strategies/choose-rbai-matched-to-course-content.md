# Choose assessments by matching instrument topic coverage, level, and format to your course content and goals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The resource letter closes each topic section with concrete recommendations for selecting among available instruments. For kinematics and forces it advises: "Use the FCI if you want a broad understanding of what your students understand about kinematics and Newton's laws, and lots of comparison data. Use the FMCE if you want a more thorough understanding... in one-dimension." Similar recommendation paragraphs appear for energy, rotation, E&M, circuits, and quantum assessments, always tying the choice to course content match, comparison data, and desired depth.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of the topics, instructional level, and format of candidate instruments, as provided in the letter's tables
#### Constraints
- Broad-topic assessments give a better sense of learning across many topics but probe each topic less deeply, so results carry more uncertainty

### Target Learners
- university physics and astronomy instructors choosing assessments for their courses

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing and improving the effectiveness of teaching through standardized measurement
