Organize self-regulated collaborative learning environments in higher education where students plan, work with peers and self-evaluate¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that self-regulated jigsaw IV increased achievement, the authors recommend that "self-regulated and collaborative learning environments, which offer students the opportunity of making their own plans within the framework of their goals, working together with their peers, self-evaluating and correcting themselves at the end of the process, should be organized" in higher education. They also call for new studies adaptable to the higher education period and suggest teachers practice more and receive seminars, courses and symposia to sustain motivation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers guide and assist students to learn to use self-regulation skills

Constraints¶

  • The study is limited to one grammar unit and a specific sample, so results cannot be generalized to all other units and students

Target Learners¶

  • university students in foreign language courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic achievement in grammar and lifelong self-regulation skills

Claims¶

  • Self-regulated jigsaw IV did not significantly increase students' attitudes towards the English course; the significant gain-score difference reflected a control-group decline [+W]
  • Interviewed experimental-group students reported using self-regulated learning strategies across forethought, performance and self-reflection phases in their independent studies [+W]
  • Both groups improved significantly on the achievement test from pre-test to post-test, with the experimental group reaching a much higher post-test mean [+W]
  • Self-regulated jigsaw IV produced significantly higher achievement gain scores than traditional teaching in a university EFL grammar unit [+W]
  • Jigsaw IV instruction produced a statistically significant pre-to-post achievement gain in the experiment group [+W]
