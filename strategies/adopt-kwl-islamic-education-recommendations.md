# Adopt K.W.L in Islamic education teaching, teacher training, and curriculum design

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on the study's significant post-test advantage for K.W.L, the article recommends that "Islamic education teachers should adopt K.W.L in teaching", that supervisors train teachers to implement it, and that curriculum designers include K.W.L in curricula. It also recommends future studies on relationships between K.W.L and problem-solving or thinking strategies. The strategy positions the teacher as motivator and discussion leader while students determine prior knowledge, ask questions, and evaluate their own understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supervisor-led teacher training in implementing K.W.L
- curriculum materials reconstructed around the Know–Want–Learn stages
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is one quasi-experimental study of two textbook units in Tafila, Jordan, conducted in the second semester of 2016

### Target Learners
- 8th-grade students in Islamic education

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of religious concepts
- student participation and self-directed learning
