# Combine monetary inducements with group-based competition for cash rewards to motivate lower-class adolescents

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper proposes dividing students into groups of three, four, or five, constituted by teacher assignment or student preference, competing for cash rewards against one another or against a uniform standard, with payments disbursed by each group's average attainment. It argues this arrangement "would identify the interests of the peer group [with those] of the individual student", so peer pressure encourages attainment and mutual assistance. Internal allocation of winnings could be left partly to the group's discretion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School grades must first be made salient to the children by linking academic achievement to monetary rewards, since the assumption that grades carry intrinsic value cannot be made in lower-class schools.
#### Constraints
- The paper states the motivational problem applies from the middle primary grades to the second year of high school; before fifth grade peer group control is modest, and after the second high-school year other extrinsic rewards are expected to operate.
- Many design parameters (frequency of reinforcement, group size, group constitution, reward amount, evaluation mechanics, reward criterion, proportion of groups rewarded) are stated to require experimental investigation.

### Target Learners
- Lower-class adolescents, middle primary through early high school grades

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement and motivation toward academic goals
