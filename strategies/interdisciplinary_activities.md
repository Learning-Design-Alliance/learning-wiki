# Interdisciplinary Activities

## Description
Interdisciplinary activities present issues, themes, and problems that require skills and knowledge from more than one subject to address or answer. These activities encourage learners to make connections between academic disciplines, building a more complete understanding of the topics they're studying. The interdisciplinary approach can be implemented through entire units, class-long exercises, or short activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need to have a strong understanding of multiple disciplines. Access to varied resources such as news clips, textbooks, and multimedia. The ability to create relevant and engaging problems.
#### Constraints
- May require coordination with other teachers or departments. Requires careful planning to ensure activities align with learning objectives. Can be time-consuming to prepare.

### Affordances
- Improves analysis abilities by using approaches from different disciplines. Challenges pre-existing ideas to help identify bias. Provides practice for solving problems outside school walls. Builds informed and complete understandings of topics.

### Personalization
- Activities can be tailored based on learner interests, learning styles, and the specific context of the subjects being studied. Adaptable for various class sizes and schedules.

## Examples
- News Analysis: Using a news clip about a local store closing, learners determine an ideal new location, applying skills from different subjects. Historical Pen Pals: Learners take on the role of historical figures and write to each other about relevant events, building knowledge of history and creative writing skills. Math Gym: Combining math, science, and physical education, learners estimate and measure distances during long jumps, delving into physics and body mechanics.
