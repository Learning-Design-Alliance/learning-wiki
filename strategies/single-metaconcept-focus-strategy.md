# Teach a single linguistic metaconcept in depth rather than several related metaconcepts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on teacher feedback from a previous intervention, this study revised the design so that instead of four related metaconcepts, the intervention concentrated on one, valency. The article explains that "students would have the opportunity to process the newly taught information more effectively", limiting the amount of new linguistic terminology. The intervention still related this single metaconcept to familiar traditional concepts such as verb, subject, direct object and indirect object across four lessons of 50 minutes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instruction manual for teachers and a student assignment booklet with pen-and-paper assignments
#### Constraints
- The intervention had to fit four lessons due to teachers' limited time

### Target Learners
- 14-year-old ninth-grade pre-university students in L1 Dutch classes

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of valency and related traditional grammatical concepts
