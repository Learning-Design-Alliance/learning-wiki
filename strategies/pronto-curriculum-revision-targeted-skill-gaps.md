# Revise the simulation curriculum to emphasize rapid identification of non-vigorous neonates, initial resuscitation steps, vital-sign recognition without counting, and timely PPV initiation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the triangulated findings, the authors state that "[t]hese results have informed the next iteration of the PRONTO curriculum," which will emphasize quick identification of non-vigorous neonates, starting resuscitations with appropriate initial measures, recognizing vital sign abnormalities without counting specific rates, and timely initiation of effective PPV. This translates the measured gaps — including missed initial steps and inaccurate rate counting — into targeted curricular emphases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ongoing mentor-delivered simulation training with video-assisted debriefing to practice the revised emphases
#### Constraints
- The article states further improvement will likely require addressing barriers that go beyond the scope of clinical skills training, such as equipment availability and staffing

### Target Learners
- Auxiliary Nurse Midwives and General Nursing and Midwifery nurses in rural primary health centers

### Target Learning Goals
- Rapid identification of non-vigorous neonates, correct initial resuscitation steps, and timely effective positive pressure ventilation
