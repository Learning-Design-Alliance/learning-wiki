# Train AI feedback models on context-specific educational data including student, course and instructor data

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend moving beyond generic language models by developing AI systems that "can ingest and understand the unique characteristics of a course, students and course instructors." They specify that training data should include student strengths, weaknesses and progress, course content such as syllabus and assessment rubrics, and examples of the instructor's feedback and teaching methods so the AI can emulate the instructor's voice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Comprehensive context-specific data: student performance and progress data, instructor observations, course content and rubrics, and examples of the instructor's prior feedback
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a recommendation based on its findings, not a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- higher education students in programming courses

### Target Learning Goals
- actionable, context-aligned feedback on student work

## Claims

- [Both instructor and AI-generated feedback were perceived positively, with instructor feedback rated higher descriptively on all four perception dimensions](../claims/both-feedback-types-perceived-positively.md) [~W]
- [Students receiving instructor feedback showed significantly greater improvements in lab assignment scores than students receiving AI-generated feedback, after controlling for initial knowledge](../claims/instructor-feedback-greater-lab-score-improvements-than-ai-feedback.md) [~W]
