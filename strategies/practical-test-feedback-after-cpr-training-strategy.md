# Add a short practical test with individual feedback directly after standard CPR training in school lessons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that after standardised practical CPR training, students individually perform a short practical test in an out-of-hospital cardiac arrest scenario and then receive individual feedback. The authors conclude that "A practical test with feedback in connection with CPR training is an efficient strategy to increase learning outcome, both practical skills and self-rated knowledge, when teaching seventh grade students." The test took 3 minutes and feedback 2 minutes, fitting a single whole-class lesson run by school teachers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must fit into one lesson in whole-class set-up and be provided by teachers at the school; the test requires an individual training manikin and a skill reporting system, with feedback partly based on Hattie and Timperley's feedback model.
#### Constraints
- The design did not allow the authors to explain whether the effect came from the additional supervised hands-on time or from the feedback; a feedback device was not tested.

### Target Learners
- Seventh grade students (13 years of age) in compulsory school

### Target Learning Goals
- Practical cardiopulmonary resuscitation skills and willingness to act in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest situations
