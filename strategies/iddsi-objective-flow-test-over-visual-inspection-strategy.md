# Replace sole reliance on visual inspection of thickened liquids with objective IDDSI tools such as the syringe flow rate test or manufacturer ratios

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that clinicians, educators, patients and caregivers stop relying solely on individual visual estimates of thickened-liquid consistency and instead use objective verification. It states educational programmes "should advocate the use of objective methods, such as the IDDSI syringe flow rate test or adhering to the formula for the corresponding level". The strategy is grounded in the study's findings that visual accuracy fell below 50% and did not improve with experience, and that over-thickening risks dehydration and aspiration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the IDDSI syringe flow rate test equipment or the manufacturer's mixing formula at the point of care
- Training of multidisciplinary staff (SLPs, dietitians, nursing, foodservice) and caregivers in the objective method
#### Constraints
- The supporting accuracy findings come from a video-based online survey in one geographical region, which the authors state may underestimate real-world accuracy and limit generalisation

### Target Learners
- Practising speech-language pathologists
- Postgraduate SLP students
- Clinical educators across allied health fields
- Patients and caregivers

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate preparation and verification of prescribed thickened-liquid consistency
- Replacing untaught visual-inspection habits with objective IDDSI testing procedures
