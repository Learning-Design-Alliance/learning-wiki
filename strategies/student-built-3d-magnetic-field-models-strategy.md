# Have students build and explore their own multi-dimensional wire configurations (solenoids, Helmholtz coils, toroidal systems) with the superposition routine before lengthy theoretical derivations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article suggests an exercise in which students 'create their own models in more than two dimensions for specific distributions', because an arbitrary system is defined only in terms of the coordinates of the wires, so a 3D arrangement can show more complex and at the same time more realistic configurations. Recommended targets include finding the magnetic field of 'solenoids, loops conﬁgurations like Helmholtz coils or even a toroidal system' before performing exhausting theoretical calculations. Students change input parameters and explore the impact graphically.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the python routine for a finite wire (Fig. 2), which the article describes as usable 'without a deep programming knowledge'.
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a complement to classroom experiments and a step before performing exhausting theoretical calculations, not as a replacement for them.

### Target Learners
- university students in introductory electromagnetics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- applying the superposition principle to composite magnetic systems
- building and exploring computational models of magnetic fields
