# Use meta-principles of physics (unity of nature, plenitude, mathematical beauty) as didactic themes when comparing quaternion and matrix representations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The paper recommends structuring discussion of competing theoretical representations around three meta-principles from Kragh's biography of Dirac: the unity of nature ("that all nature is amenable to the same kind of theoretical treatment"), the principle of plenitude ("anything which is not prohibited is compulsory"), and the principle of mathematical beauty. It warns that "all of these possible dilations remain in-adequate didactically if epistemological conditions, philosophical views, or ... the current Zeitgeist of the surroundings within which the students and teachers are acting are not simulta-neously considered." Comparing rotation matrices and quaternions gives students access to these debates, e.g. whether complex rotation angles are physically feasible.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A comparison of two theoretical representations (quaternions vs. rotation matrices) that students can analyze for unity, feasibility, and structural simplicity
#### Constraints
- The outcome of the plenitude discussion may be open and may lead to an inverse meta-principle, as the paper notes

### Target Learners
- university-level physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- philosophical and epistemological understanding of theory evaluation in physics
- actively finding a convincing standpoint rather than receptively following discussion
