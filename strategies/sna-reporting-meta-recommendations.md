# Report study design around the big questions, document methodological choices, and decide early on standardization of network comparison

## Description
The article closes with meta-recommendations for PER researchers adopting egocentric, qualitative, or mixed-methods network analysis. First, "Answer the 'big questions' posed above not just in study design, but when reporting results" so other researchers can understand claims and spot gaps. Second, "Be clear and descriptive about methodological choices," since the purpose of mixing methods and post-data-collection processing is often under-described. Third, decide early how much standardized or quantified comparison between networks is desired, balancing uniform prompts against the freedom of less-structured interviews.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies using less-common tools such as essays should document how they go from the original data form to encoded network ties.
#### Constraints
- For detailed quantitative study, prompt sequence and wording should be as uniform as possible, which may conflict with less-structured interview freedom.

- physics education researchers
- physics departments
