Design HOTs lessons around concepts, schemas, metaphors, visualization and inference¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Drawing on Thomas and Thorne (2009), the article specifies that "lessons designed to improve higher order thinking skills should include" five components: concepts as ideas around which groups of ideas revolve, schemas built by connecting concepts, metaphors/similes/analogies for abstract concepts, visualization of ideas and concepts, and inference as drawing conclusions from evidence. It pairs this with a nine-item list of teaching moves, including teaching concept of concepts, moving from concrete to abstract and back, question-answer relationships, brainstorming activities, and emphasizing feedback generation for students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teaching materials must be designed around daily life problems, confusing situations and deep questions
  • Effective teaching strategies and suitable learning environments are needed to facilitate HOTs growth

Constraints¶

  • The article notes HOTs may be easy to develop for some students but not others, so consensus for creative and cultural education is crucial

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students
  • language learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • higher-order thinking skills
  • critical thinking
  • problem solving

Claims¶

  • HOTS-oriented teaching improves disposition towards critical thinking subscales including truth-seeking, open-mindedness, self-confidence, and maturity [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use real-world problems, open-ended class discussions, and inquiry-oriented experiments as higher order thinking strategies
  • Infuse specific higher-order thinking skills into each phase of Engineering Drawing lessons through real-world problem assignments, questioning, discussion and student presentation
  • Teach prepositions by presenting the core spatial sense first, then deriving metaphoric senses, supported by diagrams and schemas
