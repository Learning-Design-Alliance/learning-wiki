# Tailored institutional support for fasting students: flexible schedules, sleep hygiene programs, and fatigue monitoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that universities adapt policies during religious observances such as Ramadan. It states that "universities should consider implementing tailored support systems during Ramadan, including flexible class schedules, awareness programs on sleep hygiene, and access to psychological counseling for at-risk students." It also proposes monitoring fatigue levels to identify struggling students and potentially adjusting exam schedules or offering remote learning options during peak fasting periods. These recommendations follow directly from the study's findings that fatigue is negatively associated with performance and that sleep disruption was near-universal in this sample.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions need a way to identify students experiencing high fatigue levels so support services can be targeted, as the article proposes monitoring fatigue levels
#### Constraints
- The article frames these supports for periods of intensive religious observance such as Ramadan, where fasting disrupts sleep and elevates fatigue; the evidence base is a cross-sectional self-report study, so the authors call for longitudinal follow-up with objective measures

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical-college students (medicine, pharmacy, dentistry, nursing) observing Ramadan fasting

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining academic performance and student wellbeing during religious observances
