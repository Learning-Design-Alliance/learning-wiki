Future AI-WTC research should use longitudinal designs, triangulated WTC measures, diverse samples, comparative tool research, pedagogically guided AI integration, and theory-driven models¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The review identifies five interrelated gaps across methodological, contextual, population, technological, and theoretical dimensions and highlights "the need for longitudinal designs, triangulated WTC measures, diverse learner samples, comparative AI tool research, pedagogically guided AI integration, and dynamic, theory-driven integrated models". These directions aim to advance evidence-based AI integration for enhancing EFL learners' WTC and provide guidance for teachers, curriculum designers, and AI developers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Researchers and designers should address the identified methodological, contextual, population, technological, and theoretical gaps when studying or deploying AI for WTC

Constraints¶

  • Current evidence is concentrated in Asian EFL contexts, university-level samples, and generative AI chatbots, limiting generalizability of existing findings

Target Learners¶

  • EFL learners across age groups and proficiency levels
