# Start adult learners in homogeneous small groups and use individual instruction with gradual release

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
For adult learners (twenty-five or older, returning after a year or more), the paper recommends starting nontraditional students in homogeneous small groups during initial re-entry to college, citing Steltenpohl and Shipton (1986), because small-group library instruction provides a climate where they are at ease asking questions and can build support systems. Most ongoing work with adult learners takes the form of individual instruction and brief reference-desk transactions, with initial high support followed by less support and more challenge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recognition of adult learners' time constraints, divided roles, and initial lack of self-confidence
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is attributed to Steltenpohl and Shipton (1986) rather than tested in this paper

### Target Learners
- adult learners returning to higher education after an absence of one year or more

### Target Learning Goals
- library research skills and confidence in using academic library resources
