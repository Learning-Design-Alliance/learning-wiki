# Standardize recruitment and training of peer tutors to maintain pedagogical rigor in peer-assisted learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the qualitative boundary condition that students sometimes doubted the accuracy of senior students' knowledge, the authors highlight "the necessity for institutions to standardize the recruitment and training of peer tutors to maintain pedagogical rigor." In this study, mentors were recruited from the top 30% of their class and completed a 10-hour training program covering NM principles, PAL facilitation techniques, case discussion leadership, and feedback provision, with weekly faculty debriefing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment to selecting and preparing peer tutors
#### Constraints
- Efficacy of peer-assisted learning is sensitive to the perceived expertise of the peer mentors

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining pedagogical quality in peer-led learning environments
