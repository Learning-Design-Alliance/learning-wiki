# Use the leaking-pendulum period question to surface and correct the pendulum-length misconception in class

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Pose the question of what happens to a simple pendulum's oscillation period as its mass varies, and work through the derivation showing that F = m(dv/dt) holds only for constant mass, then introduce the moving center of mass. The author argues this discussion overcomes "erroneous notions carried forward by students as to what constitutes a pendulum's length due to picking up only the results of approximations and ignoring the rigorous definition." It also serves as an exercise in evaluating centers of mass of shapes and body systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor must present the full momentum-based derivation and the center-of-mass definition, not just the small-angle approximation result

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- rigorous definition of pendulum length
- center-of-mass calculation
- variable-mass dynamics
