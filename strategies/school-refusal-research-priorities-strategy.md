# Prioritize long-term follow-up studies, independent replications, and larger samples in school refusal treatment research

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors' forward-looking research recommendations for the school refusal treatment field. They state that future research will benefit from designs that reduce bias, "independent replica- tions of the manualized treat- ments examined in this review, and longer term evaluations of effects of treatments", and larger samples permitting moderator and mediator analyses. They also recommend that future studies consider treatments other than variants of CBT for rigorous evaluation, and that mediation analyses on posttreatment and follow-up data determine which youth increase attendance because of anxiety reduction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Larger sample sizes are needed because school refusal is a complex phenomenon and larger samples permit more sophisticated moderator and mediator analyses
#### Constraints
- Only one study in the review reported follow-up outcomes for both treatment and comparison groups, so there is insufficient evidence on whether treatment effects sustain

### Target Learners
- children and adolescents of primary or secondary school age with school refusal

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained school attendance
- reduced anxiety over the longer term
