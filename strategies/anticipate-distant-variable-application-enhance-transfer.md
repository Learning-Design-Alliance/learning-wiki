# Enhance transfer by making students anticipate distant, dissimilar application contexts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that, based on their findings, the mere expectation of distal (vs. proximal) learning applications will improve transfer, and that instructors might enhance transfer by making students anticipate exams that are distant in time and different in context from the learning context — administered by a different teacher, in unknown format, and in the distant future. This is offered as a practical, far implication for education, where variability in training is already known to relate to better transfer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The expectation must be established while learning occurs (in the experiments, instructions preceded or accompanied learning)
#### Constraints
- The authors call this a far implication: the studies were conducted with ring-stimulus predictive learning tasks, not educational transfer tasks, and the authors note transfer is a hard question deserving extension of this research

### Target Learners
- students in educational settings preparing for future assessment

### Target Learning Goals
- transfer of acquired knowledge to new contexts and formats
