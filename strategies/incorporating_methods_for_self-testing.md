# Incorporating Methods for Self-Testing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 15 studies (7 quant-synthesis, 4 causal, 2 review, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q4` · 5 of 15 report an effect size

## Description
Self-testing reframes assessment from a grading event into a learning activity: students actively retrieve information — via flashcards, practice questions, or free recall — instead of rereading notes. In culturally responsive and low-stakes classrooms, frequent non-graded checks let students monitor progress without the anxiety attached to high-stakes testing. The strategy has two components: the instructor builds frequent retrieval opportunities into instruction, and students are explicitly taught self-testing techniques they can apply independently.

## Design Implications

Retrieval practice produces substantially more durable learning than restudying, yet students rarely choose it spontaneously because it feels harder and less productive than rereading [Testing enhances long-term retention more than restudying.](../claims/spaced-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S]. Because self-testing also functions as self-assessment, it builds accurate metacognitive monitoring — students discover which topics they only *recognize* versus can actually *reproduce* [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]. Feedback on self-tests should target the task and process, not just correctness [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A bank of low-stakes questions or prompts aligned to learning goals ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
- Explicit instruction in self-testing techniques — free recall, flashcards, question generation — since students default to passive rereading
- Rapid feedback loops so errors are corrected before they consolidate ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md))
- A classroom norm that errors during self-testing are diagnostic information, not failure
- A bank of questions that targets the same knowledge later assessed summatively, at comparable difficulty
- Immediate or rapid feedback so errors are corrected while the retrieval attempt is still active ([Practice](../elements/practice.md) followed by [Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md))
- Explicit framing that these are learning tools, not surveillance — no grade penalty for wrong answers
- Time in class or in the platform workflow for retrieval attempts, not just content delivery

#### Constraints
- Students frequently judge rereading to be more effective than testing, so they abandon self-testing when unsupervised [Learners' judgments of learning are often inaccurate and favor passive restudy.](../claims/high-confidence-errors-improve-retention.md) [~M] — fluency from rereading creates an illusion of mastery
- Self-testing of isolated facts can promote memorization without conceptual integration unless questions require inference or application [Retrieval practice benefits are smaller for complex, inference-based tasks.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [~M]
- If self-tests carry grades, anxiety and surface strategies return, undermining the reframing that makes the strategy work [-M]
- Students with very low prior knowledge may retrieve little and experience frustration; early self-tests may need open-book or cued formats [~W]
- Testing only *recognition* (multiple choice) when the summative assessment demands *recall* produces weaker transfer; format should match the target task
- Learners with high prior knowledge may gain little from simple factual retrieval and benefit more from generative formats such as [Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md) [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]
- If stakes creep back in (leaderboards, public scores), anxiety and surface strategies return, undermining the reframing that makes the strategy work

#### Implementation Variability
- Free recall ("brain dumps") after a lesson or reading segment
- Flashcard systems with spaced scheduling (e.g., [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net), [Quizlet](https://quizlet.com))
- Clicker or low-stakes online quizzes at the start of class ([Quizzes](../elements/quizzes.md))
- Student-generated questions exchanged with peers, which adds generative processing
- Cued or open-book self-testing for novices, fading supports as competence grows
- **Instructor-paced:** opening or closing each class with a two-question quiz (low-tech, no grading)
- **Learner-paced:** flashcard or quiz apps with spaced scheduling; learners choose topics where confidence is lowest
- **Free recall:** brain dumps or concept mapping from memory after reading, requiring no question bank
- **Pretesting:** asking questions *before* instruction; even failed retrieval attempts prime subsequent learning [~M]

### Target Learners
- All learners benefit from retrieval practice, but it is especially valuable for students who rely on rereading and highlighting as their default study method [Testing enhances long-term retention more than restudying.](../claims/spaced-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S]
- Students developing self-regulated learning habits, who need structured opportunities to practice monitoring their own understanding [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Anxious test-takers, for whom frequent ungraded checks reduce the stakes of assessment [~W]
- Anxious learners, because frequent no-stakes exposure to test formats reduces test-specific arousal

### Target Learning Goals
- Long-term retention of factual and conceptual knowledge [Testing enhances long-term retention more than restudying.](../claims/spaced-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S]
- Metacognitive accuracy: knowing what you know and what needs restudy
- Self-regulated learning: planning, monitoring, and adjusting one's own study behavior
- Metacognitive calibration: accurate judgments of what is and is not yet known

### Instructions
1. Teach the rationale: show students evidence that testing is a learning event, not just a measurement event, and contrast it with the illusion of mastery from rereading.
2. Model a self-testing technique with a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) — demonstrate generating questions from a text, attempting free recall, then checking and repairing gaps.
3. Build frequent low-stakes retrieval into class sessions using [Quizzes](../elements/quizzes.md) or [Practice](../elements/practice.md) — short, ungraded, with immediate feedback ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)).
4. Schedule repeated retrieval over time rather than massing it in one session [Testing enhances long-term retention more than restudying.](../claims/spaced-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S].
5. Have students track their own results and plan restudy accordingly, connecting self-testing to [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) cycles.
6. Fade instructor-provided questions over time, shifting question generation to students ([Fading](../elements/fading.md)).

## Examples
- **[Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net)** — spaced-repetition flashcard software that schedules each card for review just before predicted forgetting; widely used in medical education.
- **[Quizlet](https://quizlet.com)** — self-testing modes (Learn, Test) that convert study sets into retrieval practice with feedback.
- **Retrieval practice routines (e.g., "brain dumps")** — teachers end a lesson with two minutes of ungraded free recall, then students compare their recall against notes to identify gaps ([RetrievalPractice.org](https://www.retrievalpractice.org)).
- **Khan Academy** — mastery-based practice questions with hints and immediate feedback, letting students self-test before unit assessments.
- **[Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net)** — spaced-repetition flashcards that schedule each item for review just before predicted forgetting; widely used in medical education.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — mastery quizzes between video segments; wrong answers route learners to hints and prerequisite content rather than a grade.
- **Daily low-stakes quizzing in introductory psychology** — Roediger and colleagues' classroom studies showed students earned roughly a grade-step more on exam questions that had appeared on prior quizzes.
- **"Brain dump" closures** — students write everything they remember from a lesson on a blank sheet, then compare against notes; no technology or question bank required.

## Description
Self-testing reframes testing as a learning event rather than a measurement event: learners answer questions — flashcards, practice quizzes, free recall prompts — before, during, or after instruction, with no grade consequences. The act of retrieving information from memory strengthens retention far more than rereading, and the results show learners *what they do not yet know*, directing subsequent study. The instructor's role shifts from administering tests to supplying retrieval opportunities and teaching learners to interpret their own errors.

### Instructions
1. Identify the knowledge and skills the summative assessment will target; write or select questions at that level of challenge.
2. Schedule short retrieval events early and often — start of class, end of segment, weekly — rather than one pre-exam review ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
3. Require an actual retrieval attempt (write, say, or select) before any material is consulted; guessing counts.
4. Provide immediate feedback focused on why an answer is right, not just what it is ([Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md)).
5. Revisit missed items after a delay — spacing the retest multiplies the retention benefit [Testing effect improves long-term retention relative to restudying.](../claims/spaced-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S].
6. Teach learners to read their own results: what to restudy, what to stop studying — making the diagnostic explicit.
