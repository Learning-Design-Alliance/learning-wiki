# Self-Reinforcement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Self-reinforcement involves individuals selecting a reinforcer and rewarding themselves for reaching or exceeding a set criterion. The learner — not the teacher — administers the reward, which shifts the locus of behavioral control from external management to self-management. Rooted in [Social Learning Theory](../theories/social-learning-theory.md) and [Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md), it is a core component of [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md): learners observe their own performance, compare it against a standard, and deliver consequences to themselves [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].

## Design Implications

Self-reinforcement converts reinforcement contingencies from teacher-controlled to learner-controlled, which builds a transferable self-management skill rather than dependence on external rewards. Because learners set and evaluate their own criteria, the strategy also develops self-monitoring and goal-setting habits that persist after the reinforcer is removed [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]. Involving students in selecting reinforcers supports autonomy, which strengthens intrinsic motivation over time [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear, objective, measurable goal set jointly by teacher and student ([Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M] — early goals should specify what to *do*, not just outcomes)
- Student selection of the reinforcer, e.g., from a menu or rank-ordered list
- Agreed evaluation procedures so the student can judge criterion attainment independently
- Student administration of the reinforcer upon meeting the criterion
- Ongoing [Practice](../elements/practice.md) opportunities on which the contingency operates

#### Constraints
- What is reinforcing varies by age, personality, culture, and gender; a menu chosen by the teacher may hold no value for the student [-M]
- If criteria are vague or self-judged leniently, students can reward themselves without genuine performance gains [-M]
- Over-reliance on tangible reinforcers can undermine intrinsic motivation for tasks students already find interesting [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~M] — prefer informational, task-based reinforcers
- Requires students to delay gratification; very young learners or those with attention difficulties may need teacher verification before self-administration [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Reinforcement menu or rank-ordering: student chooses from teacher-approved options
- Self-administered points or tokens exchanged later, versus immediate self-rewards
- Criterion levels can be raised progressively as performance improves, fading the contingency toward natural consequences
- Pairing with [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) so self-evaluation is calibrated against accurate performance information

### Target Learners
- Students who need help staying on task and sustaining motivation [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Learners developing self-regulation, for whom administering their own contingencies builds monitoring and evaluation skills [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Students preparing for independent adult living, where self-managed reinforcement replaces external supervision

### Target Learning Goals
- Behavioral and attentional goals: increasing time on task and task completion
- Self-regulation: goal setting, self-monitoring, self-evaluation [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Motivational goals: building persistence and self-efficacy through criterion attainment [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Set a goal for receiving rewards.** Negotiate a clear, objective criterion with the student; prefer process-oriented criteria for novices ([Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]).
2. **Select a reinforcer.** Ask the student to suggest reinforcers or choose from a menu; student choice supports autonomy [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M].
3. **Determine evaluation procedures.** Agree how performance will be measured and who verifies criterion attainment; provide [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) to calibrate self-judgment.
4. **Administer the reinforcer.** The student delivers the reward upon meeting the criterion, during regular [Practice](../elements/practice.md) sessions; gradually raise criteria and fade the contingency to build durable self-management.

## Related Strategies
- Self-monitoring — the evaluation component on which self-reinforcement depends; learners must track performance before rewarding it
- Goal setting — supplies the criterion that triggers self-administered reinforcement
- Token economies — the teacher-administered counterpart; self-reinforcement transfers control of the same contingency to the learner

## Related Elements
- [Practice](../elements/practice.md) — the performance context in which criteria are met and rewards earned
- [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) — accurate performance information keeps self-evaluation honest
- [Enhance retention and transfer](../elements/enhance-retention-and-transfer.md) — fading self-reinforcement toward natural consequences supports durable self-management

## Examples
- **Self-managed math fluency practice:** A student sets a criterion of 30 correct facts per two-minute [practice](../elements/practice.md) sprint, self-scores, and awards five minutes of free reading time upon meeting the criterion; the criterion rises weekly.
- **Writing workshop:** A student selects from a reinforcement menu (choice of next writing topic, seat location) contingent on completing a draft meeting a co-set checklist, self-evaluating against the checklist before claiming the reward.
- **Study skills intervention:** A college student in a self-regulated learning course logs study sessions, compares them to a weekly goal, and self-rewards with a chosen leisure activity — building the self-management habits emphasized in [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) curricula.
