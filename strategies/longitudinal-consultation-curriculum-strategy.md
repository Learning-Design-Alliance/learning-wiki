# Implement consultation communication training longitudinally across the undergraduate medical education span

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structuring consultation communication training as a longitudinal experience rather than relying on informal, on-the-fly instruction. It concludes that "Developing and implementing longitudinal consultation curricula, with a didactic during the pre-clinical curriculum and simulation-based instruction during the EM clerkship, can help address the current deficit in undergraduate medical education." The discussion argues that on-the-fly instruction is not adequate and that formal training should exist at various levels of training, introducing the skill before clerkships and reinforcing it in a fourth-year capstone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A didactic session during the pre-clinical curriculum introducing the standardized consultation model
- Simulation-based instruction with structured practice during the EM clerkship
#### Constraints
- During year 1 of implementation the same learners could not be studied over time to determine whether receiving the entire curriculum has any long-term educational benefit

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical education students

### Target Learning Goals
- consultation communication before clinical clerkships
- consultation skills prior to entering residency training
