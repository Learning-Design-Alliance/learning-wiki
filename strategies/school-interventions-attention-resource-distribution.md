# Address self-identified attention problems through school-context interventions that incorporate students' diverse attentional-resource distribution, while avoiding ADHD overdiagnosis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, rather than pursuing formal ADHD diagnosis, schools design interventions targeting students' self-perceived attentional difficulties. It states students' "self-identified attention problems be addressed by the students themselves, by designing interventions within the school context that incorporate the diverse ways in which students distribute their attentional resources in the pedagogical activities planned by teachers."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be designed within the school context and account for the diverse ways students distribute attentional resources in teacher-planned pedagogical activities.
#### Constraints
- The article states overdiagnosis of ADHD is not recommended, given adverse effects of pharmacological overtreatment and stigmatization; causality in the symptom–achievement relationship cannot be assumed from this cross-sectional design.

### Target Learners
- Primary and secondary school students (ages 9–18) in Chile

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving academic achievement across subject areas for students with externalizing symptomatology
