# Eight state and local strategies to address California teacher shortages

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 causal), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on research on attracting and retaining teachers, the report identifies "eight strategies that state and local agencies in California can implement in order to address ongoing teacher shortages, particularly in high-need fields and schools." These include maintaining high-retention pathways such as residencies and Grow-Your-Own programs, service scholarships, mentoring and induction, streamlined entry, early recruitment pipelines, improved compensation and working conditions, strong school leaders, and strengthened workforce data systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- State and local agency implementation, with sustained (not one-time) funding, since the report notes nearly all recent initiatives were funded only on a one-time basis in the state budget.
#### Constraints
- The report notes the largest investments are for programs not yet fully launched and many will take years to produce graduates; efforts have yielded only modest results so far.

### Target Learners
- TK–12 students in California public schools, particularly in high-need fields and schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Equitable access to fully prepared, credentialed teachers; a stable, diverse, and experienced teacher workforce
