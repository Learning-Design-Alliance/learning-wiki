# Use a mixture of dissection, plastic models, and 3D software in anatomy teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article concludes that anatomy teaching should combine modalities rather than rely on one: "The conclusion of this article is to use a mixture of the above teaching methods." Dissection is called a gold standard but constrained by cadaver shortage and laboratory time, while 3D software and plastic models are described as easy to use, cheap, and available at home for later study and doubt-clearing. The strategy assigns each tool the role its evidence supports: dissection for highest attainment and perception, plastic models as preparation for cadaveric laboratories, and 3D software for self-study and spatial exploration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to cadaveric material, plastic models, and 3D anatomy software, with lectures and laboratory sessions of equal exposure time across modalities as in the trial
#### Constraints
- The article states results cannot be generalized because commercially available 3D software and models differ in quality, and the findings cannot be implemented for cross-sectional or surface anatomy

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Gross anatomy knowledge (structure identification and application-level understanding)
