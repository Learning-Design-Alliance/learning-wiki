# Use LLM-based simulation to let trainees practise PFA conversations repeatedly, flexibly, and remotely

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends LLM-based simulation as a scalable complement to actor-based PFA training, which is limited by cost, actor availability, and scheduling. Trainees "can practice conversations repeatedly and flexibly, addressing traditional limitations of psychosocial intervention simulations, such as the limited availability of standardized patients and faculty". The asynchronous and remote nature of LLM-based training expands accessibility for trainees in geographically isolated regions, and AI-based environments foster psychological safety by letting students make mistakes without real-world consequences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration with rigorous instructional design and ongoing human oversight
#### Constraints
- Simulations occasionally suffered from overly stereotypical characterizations, semantic misunderstandings, and difficulties strictly following trainer instructions

### Target Learners
- PFA trainees, including lay providers and first responders, particularly in geographically isolated regions

### Target Learning Goals
- PFA provision skills such as active listening, calming techniques, and needs assessment, plus emotional resilience
