# Structured preparation and coaching of teaching assistants before delivering inquiry laboratories

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because inquiry laboratories differ sharply from what most teaching assistants experienced in their own studies, the article recommends careful preparation across several areas: understanding the laboratory's purpose, a prepared structure for the first session, "asking questions to help students answer their own", awareness of the experiment's nuances without pointing them out, and agreed standards for notebook keeping and data analysis. The authors state that "the coaching and training of teaching assistants should occur in preparation for the experiment", and that a testing event offers a low-pressure environment for assistants to gain experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Communication to assistants of the laboratory work's nature and purpose
- Agreed standards for aspects such as notebook keeping and data analysis approaches
#### Constraints
- Stated in the context of a first-year thermodynamics inquiry laboratory at Stockholm University

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics students (via their teaching assistants)

### Target Learning Goals
- effective facilitation of student decision-making in inquiry laboratories
