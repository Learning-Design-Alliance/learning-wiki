# Design curricula giving students opportunities to critically engage with AI and AI-generated content through validation, interpretation, and revision tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that "Curricula and instructional design should aim to pro- vide students with opportunities to critically engage with AI and AI generated-content in ways that align with emerging disciplinary use." Because concerns centered on potential superficial understanding, it proposes tasks requiring "validation, interpretation, robustness checking and revision of work, including AI-generated output, as well as comparison be- tween AI and human-generated work." Such tasks allow AI to support ideation, exploration, and rapid feedback without displacing disciplinary thinking, and may make student thinking visible.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks must require validation, interpretation, robustness checking and revision of work, including AI-generated output
#### Constraints
- The article warns AI can promote superficial engagement if tasks do not require deeper engagement with cornerstone concepts

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics and chemistry students in STEM higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Disciplinary thinking, critical evaluation of AI outputs, and authentic (non-superficial) learning with AI-supported tasks
