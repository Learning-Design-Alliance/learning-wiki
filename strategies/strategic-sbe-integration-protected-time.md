# Integrate SBE mandatorily into neurosurgery curricula with protected time alongside clinical rotations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that simulation-based education not merely be available but be deliberately embedded: SBE "should be strategically Integrated into existing neurosurgery curricula, making it mandatory and scheduling it alongside clinical rotations with protected time to attend." Without this, the authors warn, SBE may be deprioritised due to clinical duties. Supporting measures they name include training bodies recognising SBE as essential, multi-institutional support, remote or virtual platforms, low-cost tools, equipment loans from industry partners, and faculty training in simulation pedagogy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Protected time scheduled alongside clinical rotations
- Training bodies must recognise SBE as essential and work with health service administration to balance service demands with educational needs
- Faculty training in simulation pedagogy for quality performance feedback, targeted instruction and objective assessment
#### Constraints
- The article states that without protected scheduling, SBE may be deprioritised due to clinical duties

### Target Learners
- Junior and mid-level neurosurgical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition of prioritised technical procedural skills in a safe learning environment before real patient encounters
