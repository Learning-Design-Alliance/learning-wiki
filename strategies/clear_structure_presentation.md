# Clear Structure Presentation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Clear structure presentation organizes content into an explicit, visible hierarchy before and during instruction: outlines, section headings, advance organizers, signposting language, and typographic signaling cues. The learner is never left to infer how ideas relate; the organization is made perceptible so that working memory is spent on comprehension rather than on reconstructing the author's logic.

## Design Implications

Signaling — headings, previews, pointer words, and emphasis — reliably improves selective attention and recall by directing learners to the organizing structure of material [Emphasis and relevance cues direct learner attention to structurally important content.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [+S]. Structuring also reduces extraneous load: when the outline is given, learners need not hold unorganized fragments in working memory while searching for relationships [Chunking content into organized units reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. The strongest implementations pair a *global* organizer (an outline or advance organizer presented up front) with *local* signals (headings, transitions, highlighting) so the same structure is reinforced at both scales.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A coherent underlying structure identified before presentation — the designer must know the hierarchy, not just the content
- An advance organizer or outline presented before the detail ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
- Consistent heading levels, section lengths, and typographic conventions throughout the material
- Signaling cues (bold, callouts, "three reasons…" framing) that map onto the actual structure rather than decorating it

#### Constraints
- Signaling helps novices but can be redundant or even harmful for learners with high prior knowledge, who process the structure anyway [Guidance that helps novices becomes less effective as expertise grows.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Over-signaling — highlighting too much — eliminates the contrast that makes emphasis useful; when everything is marked, nothing is [Emphasis and relevance cues direct learner attention to structurally important content.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [-M]
- A visible structure imposed on genuinely non-hierarchical or ill-structured content can mislead learners into forcing false organization onto the material [-W]
- Structure cues do not substitute for the connections themselves; headings without coherent prose between them produce an outline illusion [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Advance organizers** — abstract, comparative frameworks presented before the material ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
- **Signaling in text** — headings, previews, summaries, pointer words, typographic emphasis
- **Signaling in multimedia** — on-screen headings, voice emphasis, and highlighted key steps in narrated animation [Media features such as headings and emphasis affect what learners recall and retain.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [+M]
- **Outlining tools** — learner-facing document outlines and navigation panes that let learners re-orient at any point ([Document Outline Tool](../strategies/document_outline_tool.md))

### Target Learners
- Novices, who lack the schemas to infer structure from unorganized content and benefit most from externally supplied organization [Chunking content into organized units reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Learners with limited working memory capacity or reading difficulties, for whom explicit structure reduces navigational and integrative load
- Less beneficial for advanced learners, who may find detailed signposting redundant [Guidance that helps novices becomes less effective as expertise grows.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Recall and retention of organized bodies of factual or conceptual content
- Building accurate schemas of how a domain's ideas relate hierarchically
- Efficient navigation and re-orientation in long-form or reference material

### Instructions
1. Analyze the content and commit to an explicit hierarchy — what are the top-level ideas, and what is subordinate to what?
2. Present an advance organizer or outline before the detail ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md)), framing what learners are about to encounter and how it connects to prior knowledge.
3. Chunk the content into sections that each map to one node of the hierarchy ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md)).
4. Signal the structure locally: consistent headings, preview sentences, transitions, and sparing emphasis on the few structurally critical points ([Headings and Highlight Strategy](../strategies/headings_and_highlight_strategy.md)).
5. Close sections with brief summaries that restate position in the hierarchy, and keep the full outline visible (sidebar, agenda slide) throughout.

## Examples
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — unit → lesson → exercise hierarchy with persistent progress navigation, so learners always know where they are in the structure.
- **Mayer's multimedia signaling research** — narrated animations with on-screen section headings and highlighted key steps produce better transfer than identical animations without signals ([Mautone & Mayer, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.27.3.760)).
- **Textbook chapter openers** — the standard outline-plus-learning-objectives opener is a mass implementation of this strategy; its persistence reflects consistent evidence for signaling effects.
