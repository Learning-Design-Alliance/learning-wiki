# Identify desirable difficulties prospectively using three criteria: task relevant, novel, and potentially solvable by the learner

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article endorses criteria, highlighted in Hodges and Lohse's analysis, for identifying desirable difficulties in advance: difficulties should be "task relevant, novel (i.e. not something the learner is already doing), and potentially solvable by the learner." The solvability criterion is one the authors say they frequently emphasize to educators: difficulties must challenge learners but not exceed what they can eventually overcome. Adaptive learning schedules tailoring difficulty to past successes are named as good instantiations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Difficulties must present challenges the learner can eventually meet or overcome; the learner must be equipped by prior learning to respond successfully
#### Constraints
- The authors note that interpreting errors as opportunities for enhanced learning is much easier said than done

### Target Learners
- learners across education settings, including motor-skills learners and K-12 students

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term retention and transfer through appropriately calibrated challenge
