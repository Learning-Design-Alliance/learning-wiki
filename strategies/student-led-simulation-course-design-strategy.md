# Student-led curriculum design with faculty revision for a simulation course

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The ABCS3 curriculum was proposed largely by medical students themselves: "medical students suggested most of the core parts of its curriculum", including the topics of the ten scenarios and lectures, the learning objectives, and the evaluation tools, after which "the academic professors revised the design process and made appropriate adjustments". This student-led, faculty-approved division of labour is offered as a workable route to introducing simulation into a traditionally oriented curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A student scientific body capable of drafting modules, objectives, and evaluation tools
- Senior academic faculty willing to review and adjust the design
#### Constraints
- The article notes the initiative arose in a country in economic crisis with a traditionally oriented "see one, do one, teach one" model; approval by senior faculty was still required

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical knowledge and skills through simulation
- student engagement in curriculum reform
