# Case-study-focused PBL unit design combining weekly in-person tutorials, case-based practicals and H5P interactive activities for large cohorts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (6 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
A unit-design strategy embedding problem based learning throughout a large-enrolment undergraduate unit: weekly in-person small-group tutorials in which students diagnose patients from clinical presentation and laboratory findings, practical classes redeveloped as clinical laboratory-based case studies, and interactive H5P online activities offered as formative support. The authors report that “Case study based tutorial and laboratory PBL exercises, when incorporated into curriculum and unit design can improve student experience and feedback in biomedical science”. They believe the approach could extend to other disciplines and blended delivery, while recommending in-person tutorials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Worksheets published at the start of the unit so students can access them ahead of sessions; tutorial sessions run in person with staff facilitating small-group discussion, and no pre-session completion required.
#### Constraints
- The authors strongly recommend face-to-face in-person tutorials to increase active participation and engagement; initial student hesitancy with student-led sessions required early introduction of group work; low engagement with optional online activities suggests more signposting is needed.

### Target Learners
- second-year undergraduate Biomedical Sciences students in large cohorts (more than 200 per year)

### Target Learning Goals
- applying biological principles to clinical case study interpretation and patient diagnosis
- critical evaluation and problem-solving
- independent learning
