# Institutions should adopt clear, prominently displayed AI policies and cultivate AI literacy for ethical, employability-oriented use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend that doctoral-granting institutions develop "clear and prominently displayed policies regarding ethical AI use and academic integrity", because participants reported differing or hidden course AI policies. Policies should "clearly demarcate appropriate AI usage" while accommodating diverse student approaches, and should pair with AI literacy education so students understand AI capabilities, limitations, and ethics—also an emerging employability skill. Instructors are advised to integrate AI tools into curricula while guarding against dependency that could undermine independent thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment to policy development and AI literacy education across disciplines
#### Constraints
- The article notes it remains unclear whether AI integration could create dependency that undermines students' independent writing

### Target Learners
- doctoral students, faculty, administrators and policymakers in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- ethical AI use, academic integrity, AI literacy, workforce readiness
