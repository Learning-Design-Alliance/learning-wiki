Strategies

Enrich blended courses with varied authentic online resources and mobile-device access, per learner recommendations¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In the interviews, participants recommended making blended learning more efficient by using various resources and mobile devices. One participant described "authentic resources apart from the teacher include online books, websites and the various files associated with the course", and another preferred the smartphone because it makes "learning possible anytime anywhere". The article reports these as learners' own suggestions for promoting the blended learning approach.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learners need access to internet-connected mobile devices; the article notes student ownership of such devices is very common in Iran

Constraints¶

  • The same interviews warn that technology distractions and limited interaction can reduce online-learning efficiency

Target Learners¶

  • University EFL learners, including working students studying part-time

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English vocabulary learning and preparation for tests such as TOEFL

Claims¶

  • Participants reported drawbacks of blended learning: limited student-teacher interaction and distraction from ICT tools [-W]
  • Iranian EFL learners held predominantly positive attitudes toward blended vocabulary learning, citing flexible timing, personalized learning, and the systematic app design [+W]
  • Interviewed students reported increased motivation and learning support from mobile learning, alongside technical problems [+W]
  • Mobile devices make education more accessible anytime and anywhere, but this attractiveness comes with limitations for adoption and usage [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide reliable Internet/Wi-Fi and sufficient devices when implementing mobile learning
  • Teachers and intervention designers should use mobile devices thoughtfully alongside other teaching approaches, ground designs in existing evidence, and evaluate rigorously
  • Address technology access disparities with offline or low-tech alternatives when implementing gamified learning
