# Use controlled feedback mechanisms in chatbot-based exam practice to promote critical thinking and iterative learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that medical educators and exam designers implement structured feedback signals when using LLM chatbots for exam practice. It argues that "controlled feedback mechanisms could enhance the pedagogical role of chatbots—not just as static responders, but as adaptive tools that promote critical thinking and iterative learning." This rests on the observation that both models showed modest final-attempt improvement after a standardized negative-feedback flag, which the authors link to reinforcement learning from human feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standardized feedback signal (e.g., flagging a response as not factually correct) delivered within the chatbot interaction
#### Constraints
- No real-time learning occurs during user interaction; the article states neither model truly learns in the traditional human sense during testing, and improvements were modest and not statistically significant

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- Ophthalmology residents
- Medical specialty exam candidates

### Target Learning Goals
- Answering ophthalmology multiple-choice questions with accurate, evidence-based reasoning
