# Use branched-narrative virtual patients with video-recorded standardized patients to bring case-based learning to health-science MOOCs at scale

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates integrating branched-narrative virtual patients into a MOOC as a scalable alternative to standardized patients, which are "impossible at a massive scale in distance education." The design used a professional actor and film team, matched branches to case objectives, linked the virtual patient to course material when needed, and allowed learners to see outcomes of their choices in text or video. The authors conclude that "virtual patients might well be introduced in other health-science related MOOCs."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Following the 12 tips (guidelines) for authoring virtual patient cases provided by Posel and colleagues
- A platform supporting online authoring, export of activity logs, and case exchange (Open Labyrinth was used)
- Video recording resources and clinician involvement for role-played clips
#### Constraints
- Development of two interactive virtual patients required 150 h of work; the authors note unqualified self-treatment could be a potential issue in other healthcare-related MOOCs

### Target Learners
- health professions students and practitioners
- MOOC participants in health sciences

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- applying behavioral medicine interventions
- case-based learning at scale
