# Scaffolded progression to independent clinical practice: graded movement from observation to assisted practice to supervised independent care

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Preceptors in this study described deliberately moving learners from observation to assisted practice and finally to supervised independent care as competence increased, assigning manageable real-world tasks while ensuring patient safety. As one preceptor described: "I gradually involve students in patient care... while I supervise and discuss potential future implications." This scaffolded progression reportedly fostered both skill mastery and learner confidence, and it converged with positive quantitative ratings for teaching strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ongoing supervision during progressively independent tasks
- Task assignment matched to the student's current competence level
#### Constraints
- Described only for final-year BSN students in critical care placements at one Pakistani institution

### Target Learners
- fourth-year undergraduate nursing students in clinical practicum

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- psychomotor skill mastery
- learner confidence and independent practice
