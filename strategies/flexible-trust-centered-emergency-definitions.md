# Define emergencies with flexibility and trust rather than rigid proof-based criteria

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy sets broad guiding principles for emergency aid eligibility — focusing on short-term, unexpected disruptions to enrollment — instead of rigid definitions. Practitioners described adopting "dollar thresholds below which documentation is not required, allowing staff to respond quickly while preserving student dignity." Moving from proof-based to trust-centered models is presented as helping aid reach students when it matters most.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional definitions that serve as guardrails protecting limited funds while allowing flexibility in interpreting student needs
#### Constraints
- Practitioners must still ensure funds are reserved for the most pressing crises; the stay-enrolled-versus-dropping-out framing does not apply to recurring emergencies

### Target Learners
- College students facing short-term financial emergencies, particularly students of color and students from low-income backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- Supporting persistence and preventing stopping out during financial crises
