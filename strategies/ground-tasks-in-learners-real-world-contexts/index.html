Ground problem solving tasks in learners' real-world contexts to reduce extraneous cognitive load¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q1–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends incorporating learners' real-world context meaningfully during problem solving interactions, because learning in familiar settings reduces extraneous cognitive load and supports retrieval of information from long-term memory. Learners' township socioeconomic background was used to generate effective instructional conditions, with tasks such as currency exchange and hire purchase giving meaning to everyday experience.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tasks must connect to learners' everyday experiences and familiar settings

Constraints¶

  • The article notes element interactivity may appear very high if the problem context is not familiar to test-takers, heightening extraneous load

Target Learners¶

  • Grade 10 learners from township settings in Gauteng, South Africa

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Problem solving performance in financial mathematics

  • CBPSI significantly improved problem solving performance of Grade 10 learners from pre-test to post-test [+W]
