# Integrate the validated iVR simulator into early orthopedic training as a platform for deliberate practice with individualized feedback, pending longitudinal confirmation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating the simulator into early orthopedic training "as a structured platform for deliberate practice and assessment, with the potential for performance data to guide individualized feedback and progression toward clinical readiness, pending confirmation from longitudinal training studies". They chose iVR simulation as the educational strategy in a six-step curriculum development approach because it supports deliberate practice, directed self-regulated learning, and automated objective assessment and feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Confirmation and refinement of proficiency cut-scores after repeated exposure and automated feedback
#### Constraints
- Transfer of simulator-acquired skills to the operating theatre has not yet been established; haptic limitations narrow the range of skills that can be effectively taught

### Target Learners
- orthopedic residents

### Target Learning Goals
- technical skills development in distal radius fracture fixation
- fluoroscopy acquisition and interpretation

## Claims

- [A Contrasting Groups' Method pass/fail standard of 34.0 points completely discriminated between novices and experienced surgeons, with no false positives or false negatives](../claims/cgm-cut-score-34-complete-discrimination-drf.md) [+W]
- [Experienced surgeons significantly outperformed novices on nine of 32 individual simulator metrics, while novices outperformed surgeons on one metric (distance from screw to fracture lines), which was excluded as construct-irrelevant](../claims/individual-metric-contrasts-nine-favor-surgeons-one-reverse.md) [+W]
- [After excluding one construct-irrelevant metric, the remaining 31 simulator metrics showed acceptable internal consistency (standardized Cronbach's alpha = 0.79)](../claims/internal-consistency-alpha-079-31-metrics.md) [+W]
- [A composite total simulator score on an expert-informed iVR simulator significantly discriminated between novice and experienced orthopedic surgeons after a single procedure (Cohen's d = 4.2)](../claims/ivr-total-score-discriminates-novices-experienced-drf.md) [+W]
- [No significant group difference in procedure time or number of fluoroscopic images; experienced surgeons used more fluoroscopy, which the authors argue should not compromise care quality](../claims/no-significant-difference-time-fluoroscopy-count.md) [~W]
