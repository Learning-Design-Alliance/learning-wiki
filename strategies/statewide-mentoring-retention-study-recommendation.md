# Conduct a larger-scale, statewide study of mentoring features and retention to detect small or moderate relationships

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report recommends scaling up evidence generation: "A larger-scale study, such as one that includes all first-year teachers in the state, could more reliably identify small or moderate relationships between mentoring features and teacher retention as statistically significant." A statewide survey with a similar response rate could detect retention differences as small as 4 percentage points, though it would still not establish causality. Such evidence would let leaders develop reliable guidance prioritizing promising mentoring activities and topics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adding mentoring questions to a statewide survey of all first-year teachers, which the original study could not do because of survey length constraints
#### Constraints
- Even a larger study's design would not necessarily enable determining causality

### Target Learners
- All first-year teachers statewide

### Target Learning Goals
- Teacher retention
- Mentoring program improvement
