# Integrate formal self-evaluation curriculum with timely supervisory feedback to develop accurate resident self-assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 4 studies (2 associational, 1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "integrating a formal curriculum on self-evaluation with timely supervisory feedback may further enhance the capacity of a resident to accurately appraise their own performance as well as enhance their effectiveness in providing feedback to junior learners." Self-assessment is positioned as particularly valuable where expert faculty availability is limited. The authors plan to develop this curriculum reinforced through postprocedure feedback sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Self-assessments must be developed properly to be a reliable metric
#### Constraints
- Prior research on self-assessment accuracy has yielded inconsistent results, complicated by cognitive biases such as the Dunning-Kruger effect

### Target Learners
- Medical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate self-assessment of procedural performance and reflective practice
