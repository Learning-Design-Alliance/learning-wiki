# Community Building

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 5 studies (3 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 5 report an effect size

## Description
Community building is the deliberate design of interpersonal structures, norms, and shared activities that create belonging, trust, and mutual accountability among learners. It is carried out through early relationship-establishing routines ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md)), collaborative structures with interdependent goals, sustained discussion practices, and explicit norms for participation and feedback. In online settings it requires intentional scaffolding of social presence, since community does not emerge spontaneously without co-presence.

## Design Implications

Sense of belonging predicts persistence and engagement, particularly for at-risk learners, and brief belonging-focused interventions measurably improve outcomes [Brief social-belonging interventions improve academic outcomes for at-risk students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. Community building works because it converts learning from an individual performance into a shared enterprise: learners explain, question, and hold each other accountable, which raises the quality of discourse and elaboration. Its effectiveness depends on structure — groups need individual accountability and interdependent rewards, or stronger members carry the work while others coast [Cooperative learning works when group goals are paired with individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Early and repeated low-stakes opportunities for learners to know one another ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), icebreakers with substantive content)
- Explicit norms for participation, feedback, and disagreement, co-developed or clearly taught
- Tasks that require genuine interdependence, not merely parallel individual work ([Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md))
- Instructor presence and responsiveness, especially in asynchronous environments ([Community of Inquiry](../principles/community-of-inquiry.md))
- Visible, co-developed norms for discussion, feedback, and disagreement
- Interdependent tasks in which learners genuinely need one another's contributions

#### Constraints
- Unstructured group work without individual accountability reduces learning for weaker members and breeds free-riding [Cooperative learning works when group goals are paired with individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [-S]
- Forced social interaction can increase anxiety and reduce participation for learners with social anxiety or those in the minority on identity-relevant dimensions [~M]
- Community built only through icebreakers, without shared intellectual work, decays quickly; belonging is sustained by joint task engagement, not social activity alone [~W]
- In large-enrollment or short-duration courses, the overhead of community routines can exceed their payoff [~M]
- Forced or performative socializing ("icebreakers" unrelated to content) can feel coercive and waste time, undermining credibility [~W]
- Community built only through unstructured social channels tends to exclude quieter or marginalized learners; structure and multiple participation modes are needed [~M]
- In very short courses or high-turnover cohorts, deep community investment may not pay off before the group dissolves [-W]
- Large anonymous cohorts (e.g., MOOC-scale) resist community formation; smaller persistent subgroups work better [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Online/asynchronous:** discussion norms, video introductions, small persistent breakout groups, instructor social-presence cues ([Community of Inquiry](../principles/community-of-inquiry.md))
- **Project-based:** team charters, role rotation, peer feedback protocols
- **Ongoing cohorts:** communities of practice where expertise is distributed and newcomers move from periphery to core participation ([Communities of Practice](../principles/communities-of-practice.md))
- **Online:** discussion rituals, small persistent groups, video introductions, instructor weekly presence announcements
- **In-person:** seating and grouping routines, class jobs/roles, shared rituals and celebrations

### Target Learners
- First-generation, minority-status, and transition-stage students, for whom belonging uncertainty is highest [Brief social-belonging interventions improve academic outcomes for at-risk students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Online and distance learners at risk of isolation and attrition
- Less beneficial as a distinct strategy for mature self-directed cohorts who already have strong external professional networks [~W]
- Online and distance learners at risk of isolation and disengagement [+M]
- Less critical for fully self-paced, solitary learning formats, where social structures may add overhead without benefit [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Dispositional goals: persistence, motivation, and identity as a member of a disciplinary community
- Collaborative competence: giving and receiving feedback, negotiating meaning
- Deep conceptual learning through [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) and peer explanation
- Dispositional and affective goals: persistence, motivation, identity as a member of a field
- Collaborative competence: giving and receiving feedback, productive disagreement
- Sustained engagement in long-horizon learning (degree programs, professional communities)

### Instructions
1. Open the course with structured introductions tied to content, not just personal trivia — a [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) that surfaces prior experience with the subject matter.
2. Co-construct or explicitly teach participation and feedback norms; revisit them after the first collaborative task.
3. Design early tasks as small-group work with interdependent roles and individual accountability [Cooperative learning works when group goals are paired with individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S].
4. Maintain instructor presence through regular announcements, personalized feedback, and visible responsiveness to learner contributions.
5. Build toward sustained structures — persistent teams, peer review partnerships, or a shared artifact — so relationships accumulate around intellectual work rather than social events alone.

## Examples
- **PBL teams with role rotation and team charters** — interdependent roles (facilitator, recorder, skeptic) with individual deliverables prevent free-riding while building team cohesion.
- **Persistent small discussion groups in MOOC-scale courses** — platforms such as Coursera cohort models assign learners to stable small groups rather than open forums, which measurably increases sustained participation.
- **[Communities of Practice in professional development](https://www.wenger-trayner.com/introduction-to-communities-of-practice/)** — Wenger's framework applied in teacher induction programs, where novices move from peripheral participation to full membership over time.
- **P2PU (Peer 2 Peer University)** — learning circles where small groups meet in person around online courses; community is the retention mechanism. [https://www.p2pu.org](https://www.p2pu.org)
- **El Sistema–inspired ensemble programs** — musical community and collective performance function as the primary motivator for sustained practice.
- **First-year seminar programs** (e.g., learning communities at community colleges) — cohorting students across linked courses to build peer networks; associated with improved persistence [+M].

## Description
Community building is the intentional cultivation of relationships, shared norms, and collective identity within a learning group. It is carried out through structured early interactions (introductions, [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md)), ongoing collaborative routines, and design decisions that make learners interdependent — so that participation feels socially meaningful rather than merely required.

## Design Implications

Community building also supports motivation through relatedness, one of the three basic psychological needs [Autonomy-supportive environments increase intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]. It is not a soft add-on: without trust and shared norms, [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) activities degrade into parallel individual work.
