Simulation-based pre-validation of adaptive coaching systems before classroom deployment¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The authors advocate evaluating adaptive coaching logic on LLM-based simulated learners before classroom deployment, citing practical, ethical, and institutional considerations. They state deploying "an adaptive coach whose regulation behaviour has not yet been internally validated risks exposing real students to interventions whose timing and intensity have not been stress-tested." Simulation provides controlled access to a thousand-learner sample stratified across cognitive profiles and supports ablation and sensitivity analyses a classroom timeline could not accommodate.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Simulated learner agents must be characterized by cognitive traits (initial capability, learning rate, cognitive capacity) with interaction patterns calibrated to human data.

Constraints¶

  • The authors state simulation is a methodological precursor, not a substitute for classroom validation; it does not speak to educational effectiveness, learner experience, classroom integration, equity, or transfer to authentic practice.

Target Learners¶

  • Researchers and designers of adaptive learning systems

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Responsible pre-validation of adaptive coaching interventions

Related Strategies¶

  • Use data-grounded simulation as a pre-deployment close-the-loop tool for ITS design decisions
  • Pre-deployment gate and per-epoch helpfulness rollback for vendors shipping LLM tutors to K-12 learners
  • Use LLM persona simulation only for preliminary experimentation, stress testing, and hypothesis generation, with separate validation for consequential applications
  • Benchmark AI assistants on specialized educational tasks before classroom deployment
  • Evaluate LLM-based tutors with human-inspired methods such as simulated students and behavior analysis before classroom deployment
  • Use simulated students to test and compare adaptive-system strategies before engaging human students
