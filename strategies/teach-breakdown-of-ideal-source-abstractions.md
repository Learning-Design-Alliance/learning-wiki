# Teach the breakdown of idealized circuit abstractions so students better understand their limitations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors use the singularity analysis to show where idealized models fail. It argues that "The ideal voltage sources and ideal current sources are mathematical abstractions that will make the analysis of real circuit problems easier", yet this point is not stressed when teaching circuit analysis. By teaching the breakdown of these abstract ideas, the author believes students can better understand their limitations, and the new method lets students see an application of the abstract mathematical concept of the limit of a function in the context of electric circuits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need the analytical tools of the limit-of-a-function concept as presented in the paper
#### Constraints
- An experiment to determine i1 and i2 in the ideal parallel-battery circuit is impossible because ideal voltage sources do not exist in the real world

### Target Learners
- pre-college physics students
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the behavior of multiple batteries connected in parallel
- understanding the limitations of ideal voltage and current source models
