# Sequence frequency-based corpus activities for rapid term acquisition followed by concordancing activities for deeper semantic processing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a hybrid, scaffolded curriculum for medical English programs that pairs the two best-performing corpus methods in sequence: frequency analysis first for fast mastery of term formation, then concordancing for contextual depth. The authors state that "medical English programs should adopt a scaffolded curriculum that sequences frequency-based activities for rapid term acquisition, followed by concordancing activities to deepen semantic processing", arguing this can optimize both immediate and long-term retention. They add the model is feasible with open-access corpus tools and can be tailored to diverse medical subfields.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors trained in corpus tools; the authors recommend professional development programs emphasizing training in corpus tools for effective classroom implementation
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a small single-institution study of eight cardiovascular terms, which the authors say may not represent the breadth of medical terminology required in clinical practice

### Target Learners
- EFL medical students with intermediate English proficiency (CEFR B1–B2)

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of specialized medical terminology in English
