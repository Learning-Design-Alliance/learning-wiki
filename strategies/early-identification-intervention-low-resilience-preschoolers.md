# Early identification of and targeted intervention for preschoolers with low cognitive or low emotional/behavioral resilience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends targeting children with low cognitive resilience or low emotional and behavioral resilience during the preschool years for early identification and intervention to promote later academic success and school engagement. It suggests testing whether interventions "such as Dialogic Reading [68], designed to improve language ability among young children with low cognitive resilience" provide long-term benefits, and trauma-focused cognitive behavioral therapy for children with low emotional and behavioral resilience. It further recommends school-based interventions using "integrative strategies to target the various dimensions of school engagement" during early school age.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Screening or profiling of resilience during the preschool years to identify children in the low cognitive or low emotional/behavioral groups
- Interventions delivered early enough to precede school entry
#### Constraints
- The article recommends testing whether such interventions provide long-term benefits; it did not itself evaluate them
- Generalizability is limited to children involved with the child welfare system

### Target Learners
- Preschool-aged children (3–5) with low cognitive or low emotional/behavioral resilience in the child welfare system

### Target Learning Goals
- Language development and academic achievement
- Emotional and behavioral school engagement

### Affordances
- Early Childhood Resilience Three Profile Taxonomy
