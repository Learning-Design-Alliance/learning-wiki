Distinguish academic AIED from the commercial sector and develop ways to hold the sector to account¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends the academic AIED community make clear the difference between itself and the commercial AIED sector, whose primary focus is profit and marketable products. It proposes that academics “challenge the commercial sector more often”, noting the community “is uniquely placed to test the technical validity of the models being generated by the commercial sector”, and suggests developing accountability mechanisms as a route for collaboration with other knowledge traditions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Responsibility on both critics and the community to be precise about what, and who, is being critiqued

Constraints¶

  • The relationship is complex: the commercial sector can provide valuable access to funding, data and markets for a community committed to interventions in practice

Target Learners¶

  • Academic AIED researchers; policymakers and educators evaluating commercial AI products

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Protecting the integrity and educational relevance of AI in education research and products

Related Strategies¶

  • Conduct ed-tech pilots by articulating the instructional need, setting clear product-review criteria, involving educators in planning, supporting educators throughout, gathering data from all users, deciding from data, negotiating purchasing options, and sharing results
