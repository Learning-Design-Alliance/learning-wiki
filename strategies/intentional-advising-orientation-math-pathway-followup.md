# Intentional advising: individually contact interested students at orientation, plan mathematics pathways, and follow up after grades post

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Instead of randomly assigning students to a faculty advisor, faculty with specific knowledge of the STEM program requirements personally advise students who express physics or engineering interest during summer orientation. Students whose mathematics placement is below first-semester calculus are advised with a plan for completing the required mathematics series so physics study can commence. Faculty then maintain continuing contact through the fall semester and contact students again after final grades post, so unforeseen issues such as needing to retake a course are addressed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty advisors with specific knowledge of the requirements for completion of the STEM program of interest
- Time- and labor-intensive continuing contact throughout the semester and after grades post
#### Constraints
- The article notes this strategy is time- and labor-intensive

### Target Learners
- entering first-year students interested in physics or engineering, especially those below calculus placement

### Target Learning Goals
- showing each student a path to program completion regardless of mathematics entry point
- timely completion of required courses and prerequisites
