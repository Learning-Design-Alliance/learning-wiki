# Target academic interventions at children with CKD from low-SES families using tutoring, progress monitoring, and proactive school–health communication

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that because SES disadvantage compounds the academic risks of CKD, interventions should target low-SES children with CKD, developing "active, robust, and sustainable strategies to monitor progress and promote positive educational outcomes". Proposed mechanisms include proactive formal communication policies between caregivers, clinicians, teachers, and education support staff, remote learning support, and tutoring. The authors cite review evidence that tutoring, feedback and progress monitoring, and cooperative learning were effective for low-SES students generally, while noting intervention impact depends on local context and must be adapted to CKD.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ongoing dialogue between caregivers, children, clinicians, teachers, and education support staff to set attainable goals
#### Constraints
- The cited intervention review could not explain why some strategies were more effective than others; impact depends on local context and interventions need modification for CKD

### Target Learners
- Children and adolescents aged 6–18 with chronic kidney disease from low socioeconomic status families

### Target Learning Goals
- Numeracy and literacy performance
- Maintaining learning opportunities despite school absence from medical care
