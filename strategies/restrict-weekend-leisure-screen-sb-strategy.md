# Restrict leisure screen-based sedentary behavior on weekends to support academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's conclusion recommends that strategies to promote academic performance should focus on limiting screen-based sedentary behavior, and states that "restrictions on any type of screen-based SB for leisure on weekends are beneficial to AP in Chinese students". The strategy targets weekend leisure screen time specifically, because weekend screen-based SB showed the strongest negative association with academic performance in this sample.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Applies to students aged 8–19 in the studied Chinese school context; the association was measured with self-reported SB questionnaires and official end-of-semester examination scores.
#### Constraints
- The study is cross-sectional, so the authors state it "could not infer their causal relationships"; the recommendation rests on associative evidence only.

### Target Learners
- Chinese students aged 8–19 years in primary, junior high, and senior high schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance across Chinese, mathematics, English, and science courses
