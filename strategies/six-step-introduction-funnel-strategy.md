# Draft the introduction through a six-step funnel from big picture to study preview

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The guide gives an ordered drafting sequence for introductions: present the problem in wide context, narrow to the specific sub-topic, point out gaps in existing knowledge, present what you did and define the exact problem, describe what was done, and, in long papers, preview the paper's structure. A good introduction is 'concise, focusses on the main issues, avoids repetition and includes references to relevant work', presents all background relied on later, cites modern work as well as key highly cited papers, and cites only statements needing references.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All background information relied upon in the rest of the paper appears in the introduction
- Read and check every paper cited to confirm it supports the claim
#### Constraints
- Unless writing a full review, do not cite every paper on the topic; citing a review risks not crediting original work

### Target Learners
- master's and PhD level students writing their first scientific paper

### Target Learning Goals
- draft a concise, well-referenced introduction that motivates the study
