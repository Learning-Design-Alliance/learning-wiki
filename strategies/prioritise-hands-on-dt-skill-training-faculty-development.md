# Prioritise sustained hands-on DT process-skill training and authentic practice over trait-based selection in faculty development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that skills and experience, not traits, predict DT-aligned teaching behaviours, the article recommends that faculty development initiatives prioritise structured, sustained training in DT process skills such as rapid prototyping and empathy mapping, with authentic teaching practice opportunities. The authors state that "faculty development initiatives must shift from isolated mindset training toward sustained, workplace-based engagement with DT processes—such as prototyping and iterative active-learning redesign", and that institutions should abandon one-off seminars in favour of continuous, longitudinal mentoring models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Continuous, longitudinal mentoring models rather than one-off seminars
- Institutional support that legitimises productive failure and experimental iteration
#### Constraints
- Findings reflect the institutional and curricular structures of Taiwanese higher education; cross-national transferability requires further study

### Target Learners
