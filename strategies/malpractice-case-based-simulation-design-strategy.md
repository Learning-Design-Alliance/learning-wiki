# Design computer-based simulation training around common malpractice cases identified from forensic experience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that emergency medicine simulation content be selected by first evaluating the characteristics of common malpractice cases and then presenting those cases to students in a computer-based simulation system. The authors state that "characteristics of common malpractice cases should be evaluated and then presented to students by computer-based simulation systems for providing an adequate medical training in emergency department." In their own study the simulated cases (organic phosphate poisoning, gastric perforation, stab wound, deep vein thrombosis, drug allergy) were drawn from malpractice patterns seen in emergency clinics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to forensic medicine or malpractice case data to identify commonly mismanaged emergency presentations
- A computer-based simulation system able to present those cases to students
#### Constraints
- The authors note computer-based simulation programs were not commonly used in Turkish medical education at the time of writing

### Target Learners
- Final-year medical students on emergency medicine rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate diagnosis and multi-step treatment of common emergency cases
- Avoiding errors that lead to malpractice
