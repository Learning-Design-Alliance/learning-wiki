# Play background music at a moderate sound pressure level (~70 dB) during novice laparoscopic skills training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's conclusion recommends that, rather than rigorously turning music off, background music at a specific sound pressure level may enhance training performance: "It can be assumed that background music at a specific SPL even might enhance performance more than turning off music rigorously." In the study, the same music compilation was played from a standardized speaker at 50–80 dB while novices performed timed box-trainer exercises, with 70 dB producing the best absolute times.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardized sound source and measured, protocolled SPL (calibrated meter, adjustments by the tutor)
- Music chosen that participants do not find distracting (most participants reported no distraction)
#### Constraints
- Benefit shown only for laparoscopic novices in a standardized simulation setting, not a real OR; transferability of simulator results to the OR is not yet clarified; effect was significant in only two of three exercises

### Target Learners
- Medical students and surgical novices in skills-lab training

### Target Learning Goals
- Psychomotor laparoscopic skills acquisition and task completion speed

## Claims

- [Music at 70 dB SPL yields the best absolute laparoscopic box-training performance, significantly faster than control in exercises (a) and (c)](../claims/70db-music-best-laparoscopic-box-training-performance.md) [+W]
- [Relative performance improvements from first to third run were highest at 60 dB overall (32.2%) but not statistically significant](../claims/first-to-third-run-improvements-not-significant.md) [~W]
- [Exposure to lower (50 or 60 dB) or higher (80 dB) SPL music did not significantly influence training performance compared to control](../claims/low-and-high-spl-no-performance-effect.md) [~W]
- [Most participants (81.4%) did not feel distracted by the music, and overall course satisfaction was high (9.2/10)](../claims/low-subjective-distraction-high-satisfaction-music-training.md) [+W]
