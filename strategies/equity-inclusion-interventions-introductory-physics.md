# Improve equity and inclusion in introductory physics, including interventions to boost self-efficacy, growth mindset and sense of belonging

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that physics departments direct equity and inclusion efforts at the introductory physics sequence, the only part of the curriculum showing gender-imbalanced performance. It states the findings "further suggest the need for ef- forts towards improving equity and inclusion in introduc- tory physics courses, including interventions designed to boost students’ self-eﬃcacy, growth mindset and sense of belonging in physics". It also urges a curriculum cohesive in "its positive and inclusive environment" as well as content, and urges researchers at other institutions to perform similar analyses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Departmental access to institutional course-grade data to identify where performance is gender-imbalanced, as done with 15 years of data at this university.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation targets the introductory physics sequence, where the gender differences were observed; the grade analysis does not account for other pathways such as self-efficacy and sense of belonging, as the authors note.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics majors, particularly first-year students in large-enrollment introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained participation and success in the physics major; equitable performance for women in introductory physics
