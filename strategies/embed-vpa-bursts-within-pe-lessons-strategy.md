# Embed short vigorous activity bursts into existing PE lessons to raise adolescent activity without disrupting curriculum delivery

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's design recommendation is to add vigorous physical activity inside regular PE lessons rather than as separate provision: a 10-minute active warm-up progressing from light to moderate intensity with bursts of VPA, plus short 2-minute infusions during the lesson. The authors state that "The intervention may be incorporated in PE lessons without taking up additional PE time; it should not interfere with the curriculum and does not require extensive training of teachers", and highlight scalability and ease of dissemination for wider use. Fidelity is monitored through teacher logs, accelerometer measurements and a participant survey.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- PE teachers deliver the warm-up and infusions in every Year 8 PE lesson after brief training, with video demonstrations and reminder emails supporting implementation
#### Constraints
- The strategy is designed for school PE settings; the article notes that less than 50% of a typical PE lesson is spent in moderate-to-vigorous PA, which the infusions are intended to offset

### Target Learners
- Adolescents in secondary school PE, including pupils from lower socio-economic backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- Increase vigorous physical activity and fitness during the school day, with hypothesised benefits for attainment, cognition and mental health
