Administer an elaboration intervention combining an advance organizer with cognitive modelling when learners work on complex worked examples¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The elaboration intervention consisted of an advance organizer designed as "a structured scheme of twelve guiding questions" directly related to each worked example, followed by a short cognitive modelling phase in which the instructor demonstrated how to use the organizer and explicated his own example elaboration. This combination supported far transfer and increased mindfulness. The article recommends the approach for research methods training.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The modelling phase must refer directly to the advance organizer; introducing guiding questions without simultaneous modelling is pedagogically not well justifiable according to the article

Constraints¶

  • The two components were not experimentally isolated, so effects are attributable only to their combination

Target Learners¶

  • advanced university students in educational science

Target Learning Goals¶

  • far transfer of applicable knowledge
  • mindful, reflective learning

Claims¶

  • The elaboration intervention increases self-reported mindfulness during learning with worked examples [+W]
  • The elaboration intervention is necessary for far transfer: only the group with instructional support outperformed control on the complex transfer task [+W]
  • Mindfulness correlates with far transfer performance but not near transfer performance [+W]
