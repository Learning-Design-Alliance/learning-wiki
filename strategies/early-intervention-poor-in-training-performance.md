# Use in-training examination scores to identify students needing early intervention to reduce final OSCE failure rates

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using the observed correlations between in-training exams and the final OSCE as predictors: "Use of these predictors would offer a better opportunity to provide students with the necessary intervention at an early stage to reduce failure rates". In this program the in-training OSCE is graded, and feedback is provided to failing students, while Mini-Cex sessions during training are ungraded but give immediate feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-training OSCE and written scores that correlate with final OSCE performance, as observed in this program
#### Constraints
- Studied in one medical school without a comparison group; correlation-based prediction, not a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- 6th-year medical students in pediatrics clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical competence in history taking, physical examination, communication and data interpretation
