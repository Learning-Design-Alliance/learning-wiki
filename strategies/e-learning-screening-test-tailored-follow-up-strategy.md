# Regular e-learning followed by a screening test with tailored follow-up for nurses' drug dose calculation skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that nurses regularly attend an e-learning course followed by a screening test to uncover weak calculation topics, with more tailored follow-up offered to those who need further training. They argue e-learning is flexible and cost-effective for health services, and that "a combination of different learning and teaching strategies do result in better retention of drug calculation skills compared with lectures alone".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Professional leadership in health institutions should facilitate and encourage nurses to improve their drug dose calculation skills; time and resources must be available for further training activities.
#### Constraints
- E-learning had more dropouts and lesser learning outcome for nurses with low pretest skills in this study, so weak-knowledge learners may need classroom or tailored formats.

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses in hospitals and primary healthcare

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintenance and improvement of drug dose calculation skills for patient safety
