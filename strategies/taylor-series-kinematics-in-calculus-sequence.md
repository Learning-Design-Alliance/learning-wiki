# Teach Taylor series kinematics in the introductory calculus sequence to leverage prior physics knowledge and motivate Taylor series

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends teaching Taylor series kinematics at the end of the sequences and series unit in calculus 2, where most students have already met the constant-acceleration equations in prior physics courses. The author argues this leverages "the concrete physical context of kinematics to help motivate initial student interest in Taylor series," a topic many students do not appreciate until needed later. In-class activities focused on deriving and discussing the kinematic equations, with video derivations as a post-class resource.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students have prior exposure to constant-acceleration kinematics from physics
- Access to the author's video derivations as post-class resources
#### Constraints
- The pilot was low-stakes and initial; generalized motion and higher-order derivatives were not discussed in the pilot activities

### Target Learners
- calculus 2 students
- high school AP Calculus BC students

### Target Learning Goals
- motivate interest in Taylor series
- connect calculus content to physics

### Affordances
- Taylor Series Kinematics Framework
