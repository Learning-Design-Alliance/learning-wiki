# Select PD applicants by fit to maximize gain per invested resource, using a brief finalist interview

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article identifies four broad empirically noted applicant types — ideal candidates, overqualified, underqualified, and 'experience collectors' — and argues that with limited resources, "educators must be selected so as to provide the largest 'lever arm' per dollar spent or hour invested in their training." Ideal applicants are ready to do research but have not yet done it, already use data with students, and can handle a fluctuating 13+ month commitment. A recently implemented brief (under 15 minute) online interview of short-listed applicants helped eliminate experience collectors, who initially present as qualified but lack the drive to do the work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An application and interview process to distinguish applicant types before offers are made
#### Constraints
- In practice it can be hard to separate experience collectors from ideal candidates paralyzed by unfamiliar material

### Target Learners
- Educators applying to teacher research experience programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Authentic scientific research experience leading to wide sharing of the experience
