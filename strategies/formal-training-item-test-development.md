# Provide test developers formal training in item and test development and validation to reduce construct-irrelevant variance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that test developers such as course directors and instructors receive formal training in item and test development and validation, because the sensitivity review traced most DIF-flagged items to flaws such as poor writing and unclear images. As the conclusion states, "test developers (e.g., course directors and instructors) should have formal training in item and test development and validation." The recommendation rests on the study's item-review findings rather than on a test of the training itself.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions must offer or arrange formal training in item writing, test development, and validation for course directors and instructors who write exam items
#### Constraints
- The article offers this recommendation without empirical evidence that the training itself reduces DIF or item flaws

### Target Learners
- medical school course directors and instructors who write preclinical exam items

### Target Learning Goals
- writing valid, unbiased test items that minimize construct-irrelevant variance in preclinical assessments
