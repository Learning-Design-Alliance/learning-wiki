# Facilitated middle-out departmental change process with five inter-related components

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The middle-out approach guides departments through five inter-related components drawn from the organizational change literature: developing a department vision, revising assumptions about teaching and learning, developing capacity to meet learning goals, integrating teaching and learning goals with research and other departmental functions, and developing a collaborative process for continuous assessment and innovation. Individual faculty interviews create "mental maps" of beliefs that serve both to identify productive change areas and as intervention tools revealing assumptions and incongruities. The anticipated effort lasts 1-2 years, anchored by a 1-2 day departmental retreat establishing 30-day, 90-day, and one-year goals and working groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 1-2 day departmental retreat to develop vision, mental maps, assessment criteria, and forward-going goals
- Regular departmental meetings to assess progress and adjust the process
- Facilitators from the project team
#### Constraints
- The process was only beginning in the Charms Department at the time of writing; impact measurement was still being developed

### Target Learners
- Whole STEM departments, including faculty and leadership

### Target Learning Goals
- Aligning departmental culture with the six core commitments
- Building capacity to meet learning goals within and across courses
