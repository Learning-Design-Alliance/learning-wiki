# Formative Feedback Loops

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A formative feedback loop is a repeated cycle of (1) eliciting evidence of learner understanding, (2) interpreting it against clear success criteria, (3) delivering actionable feedback, and (4) giving learners the opportunity and time to act on that feedback before the next assessment point. The loop — not the feedback message alone — is the unit of design: feedback that is never acted on is wasted [Feedback is most effective when learners have opportunities to act on it.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S].

## Design Implications

Formative feedback loops operationalize [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md): assessment events become instructional rather than merely evaluative, and achievement gains from formative practices are among the largest documented in education [Formative assessment practices improve achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. The critical design constraint is that feedback must arrive while learners can still use it — feedback on a final submission arrives too late to change learning. Loops also depend on learners being able to interpret feedback; without shared criteria and exemplars, even high-quality comments are misread or ignored.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear success criteria shared with learners before work begins ([Rubrics](../elements/rubrics.md), exemplars, or [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
- Low-stakes checkpoints that elicit evidence frequently ([Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md), drafts, quizzes)
- Feedback that specifies the gap *and* a next action, not just a grade or judgment
- Protected time and a required mechanism for revision ([Practice](../elements/practice.md), resubmission, [Revision](../elements/revision.md))

#### Constraints
- Feedback paired with grades cancels the feedback: learners attend to the number and ignore the comments [-S] — deliver them separately or delay the grade
- Feedback that is too vague ("good work") or too dense (overwhelming lists of errors) produces no behavioral change [~M]
- Loops collapse when there is no opportunity to revise; feedback without a subsequent action point is purely evaluative [-S]
- Overly frequent feedback can undermine [Autonomy](../principles/autonomy.md) and self-regulation, creating dependence on external evaluation [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Teacher-to-learner:** instructor comments on drafts, conferences, whole-class feedback on common errors
- **Peer loops:** structured [Peer Review](../elements/peer-review.md) with criteria; benefits reviewers as much as recipients [~M]
- **Self-loops:** learners compare work to exemplars or rubrics, building self-assessment accuracy
- **Automated loops:** adaptive platforms deliver immediate item-level feedback and adjust difficulty ([Adaptive Learning](../patterns/adaptive-learning.md))

### Target Learners
- Struggling learners benefit most from tight, frequent loops with high teacher involvement [+S]
- Advanced learners benefit from delayed, less scaffolded feedback that prompts self-evaluation [~M]
- Novices need feedback focused on task and process, not self ("you're smart" feedback is ineffective or harmful) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Skill refinement: closing specific gaps between current and target performance
- Self-regulated learning: internalizing the evaluate–revise cycle ([Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md))
- Conceptual change: surfacing and correcting misconceptions before they consolidate

### Instructions
1. Establish success criteria and share exemplars before the task ([Rubrics](../elements/rubrics.md))
2. Elicit evidence through a low-stakes checkpoint ([Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md), draft, quiz)
3. Deliver feedback that names the gap and one concrete next action ([Action-Oriented Feedback](action-oriented_feedback.md))
4. Require a revision or re-attempt that applies the feedback ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
5. Re-assess against the same criteria and repeat, fading support as accuracy grows

## Examples
- **Draft–feedback–revision cycles in writing instruction** — Black & Wiliam's review documents large gains when teachers embed comment-based feedback and revision into ordinary classroom work.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — immediate item-level feedback with hints and mastery tracking; each exercise attempt is a micro-loop.
- **[ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org)** — math homework platform delivering instant feedback and reporting error patterns to teachers for the next lesson's loop.
- **Studio-style critique in design education** — public, criteria-referenced critique followed by iteration on the artifact.
