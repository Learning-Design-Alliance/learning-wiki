# Web Annotation Tools

## Description
Learners use free web-based tools to annotate online documents, including slideshows, blog posts, or PDFs. These tools often have group chat functions, facilitating the assignment of groups for giving and receiving feedback on work in progress. This method enables direct, contextual feedback on digital content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to web annotation tools, digital documents to annotate, and clear guidelines for providing constructive feedback.
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness depends on learners' familiarity with the tools and their ability to provide thoughtful, relevant feedback. May require initial training on tool usage.

- Higher education, adult learners, and professional development programs where learners engage with digital documents.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Facilitate peer review, provide contextual feedback, enhance collaborative learning, and improve digital literacy.

### Affordances
- Provides a direct and contextual way for learners to give and receive feedback on digital content. Enhances collaborative learning by facilitating group discussions and annotations. Improves digital literacy through the use of online tools.

### Personalization
- Different tools offer varying features such as group chat, assignment management, and annotation types, allowing instructors to choose the most suitable platform for their needs.
