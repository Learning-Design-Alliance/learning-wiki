# Explicitly train students to transfer story-based contextualization to novel situations before exams

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend going beyond implicit transfer: "explicitly condition students to use the story to contextualize content and then apply that contextualization to new situations." Because exam questions required describing situations analogous to those in the story, the authors suggest exam items could present situations from the story itself or that students be directed to practice transferring knowledge in exercises prior to exams. They also suggest revisiting the story supports spaced repetition and that storytelling could give course material intrinsic value, increasing motivation and hope.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A story already integrated into the course whose situations can be mapped onto assessment questions
#### Constraints
- A more extensive integration of a story into the course could require substantial effort

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in science lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- transfer of contextualized functional knowledge to new situations
- hope and motivation in a traditionally unpopular topic
