# Provide full feedback via injected test emails in anti-phishing training, since embedded training only feedbacks failures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends feedback-based interventions modeled on vigilance research, where "signal injection and performance feedback" improved detection in sonar watchstanding, baggage screening, and medical diagnosis by artificially injecting tests throughout normal task performance and giving feedback on success. Popular workplace embedded training (e.g. Wombat Security, PhishMe) sends artificial phishing emails and tells employees when they fall for them, increasing subsequent detection performance, but "it provides only partial feedback. Individuals who never click on the artificial phishing emails never receive notice that they made the right choice." The article proposes testing full feedback to support self-regulated learning, and notes error management training, which emphasizes learning from errors via emotional self-regulation and metacognition, as a parallel approach.
