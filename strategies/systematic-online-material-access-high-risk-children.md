# Provide systematic online material and ensure high-risk children have access to adequate learning environments ahead of school closures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that, to counter learning losses from future closures, policy makers and educators provide children with systematic online material and prioritize access for high-risk children. It grounds this in the finding that "low-performing students benefited more from systematic online-learning methods" and that the positive-effect studies all used familiar online-learning software. The recommendation appears in the discussion as a forward-looking policy measure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need access to adequate learning environments, in particular high-risk children
- Online material should be systematic and, ideally, familiar to students before an interruption of in-person teaching
#### Constraints
- The review notes the evidence comes from a small number of countries, clustered around developed countries, with no studies from developing countries available yet

### Target Learners
- primary and secondary education students
- high-risk and low-SES students

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining student achievement in mathematics and reading during school closures

## Claims

- [Studies in which students used familiar online-learning software reported positive achievement effects during school closures](../claims/covid-closures-positive-effects-online-software.md) [+W]
