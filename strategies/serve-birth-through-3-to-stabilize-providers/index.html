Use local resources to serve children birth through age 3 to stabilize community providers as 4-year-olds move into TK¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

As universal transitional kindergarten expands, community-based providers lose 4-year-old enrollment; the report recommends that local leaders use local resources to serve younger children instead. The report states that "using local resources to serve children birth through age 3 can help bolster community providers whose 4-year-old enrollment declines as TK expands." Because serving younger children is more expensive, requiring smaller group sizes and age-appropriate facilities, sites paired this with funding: San Francisco used commercial rent tax revenue for infant and toddler slots and facility grants, and Alum Rock Union converted an underenrolled elementary school into a birth-to-5 campus.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Grants and technical assistance to modify facilities and train staff to work with younger children
  • Funding sufficient for the higher cost of serving infants and toddlers

Constraints¶

  • Serving younger children is more expensive than serving 4-year-olds because they need smaller group sizes and age-appropriate facilities

Target Learners¶

  • Children birth through age 3
  • Children from low- and moderate-income families

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Access to high-quality early learning environments
  • Continuity of a mixed delivery provider system

Related Strategies¶

  • State supports for UPK expansion: fund facilities and ratio implementation, update reimbursement rates, offer curriculum and assessment technical assistance, and expand professional development and inclusive-classroom supports
  • Support coordinated enrollment across the mixed delivery system to ensure family choice and provider stability
  • Four-part state policy agenda: coherent administration, universal affordability, a well-qualified workforce, and quality improvement for all programs
  • Expand transitional kindergarten and preschool by braiding UPK funds with hiring, culturally responsive curriculum, and community college partnerships
