Use metalinguistic corrective feedback during focused communicative tasks to develop both implicit and explicit grammatical knowledge¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 causal), q3 · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that language teachers and program designers present explicit form focus instruction with explicit corrective feedback in the form of metalinguistic information. In the study, both groups learned five target structures through focused tasks designed to encourage use of particular linguistic forms; the only difference was that "the experimental group received metalinguistic feedback and the control group received no feedback", provided to the whole class or individual students at the time of learners' errors. The article also proposes interactional feedback devices such as clarification requests, comprehension checks, confirmation checks, and repetitions to draw attention to ungrammatical forms.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learners should be at a level where they have partial explicit knowledge of the targeted structures, since the study tested structures learners had already begun to acquire

Constraints¶

  • Results are from adult learners over 20 in a one-month intensive course; the article states they "could not be generalized to the adolescent students" and do not account for entirely new structures

Target Learners¶

  • adult lower-intermediate ESL learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • implicit and explicit knowledge of English grammatical structures (modals, past tense -ed, present perfect, comparatives, unreal conditionals)

Affordances¶

  • Weak Interface Position Corrective Feedback

Claims¶

  • Experimental-group posttest means exceed control-group means on both implicit and explicit knowledge measures [+W]
  • Explicit form focus instruction with metalinguistic corrective feedback significantly improves adult ESL learners' explicit grammatical knowledge [+W]
  • Explicit form focus instruction with metalinguistic corrective feedback significantly improves adult ESL learners' implicit grammatical knowledge [+W]
  • Corrective feedback on second-language errors has a significant effect on L2 development that is maintained on delayed tests [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Embed metalinguistic corrective feedback briefly within communicative tasks without disrupting communicative flow
