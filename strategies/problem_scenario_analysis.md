# Problem Scenario Analysis

## Description
Providing students with real-life problem scenarios (e.g., cheating, bullying) and having them analyze the situation, consider possible consequences, share their decisions, and reflect on their reasoning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prepared problem scenarios, class time for analysis and discussion, and materials for recording decisions and reflections.
#### Constraints
- Scenarios may not always reflect the specific experiences or cultural backgrounds of all learners. Some learners may dominate discussions. Students may struggle to apply the skills to real-life situations.

### Target Learners

- All learner levels, including K–12, higher education, and adult learners in professional development programs

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Enhance problem-solving skills, promote ethical decision-making, develop empathy, improve communication skills, and encourage reflection on decision-making processes.

### Affordances
- Provides practical application of decision-making skills, encourages collaborative problem-solving, promotes understanding of different perspectives, and fosters self-reflection.

### Personalization
- Scenarios can be tailored to align with specific age groups, interests, or real-world situations. Learners can be given options for how to present their decisions and reflections. Support can be provided for analyzing situations and evaluating consequences.

- Observe student participation in discussions. Review student-generated analyses of scenarios. Evaluate student reflections on their decision-making processes.

## Impact
- Case-based learning has been shown to increase student engagement, develop critical thinking skills, and enhance problem-solving abilities. Real-world scenarios make learning more relevant and meaningful.
