# Provide early intensified support for struggling arithmetic learners and design mathematics environments that promote positive emotions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that early arithmetic fluency is linked with later mathematics performance and with learning-related emotions, the authors recommend that "intensified educational support should be provided especially for those struggling to learn basic arithmetic skills already from the early school years". They further recommend teachers create learning environments promoting positive emotions and alleviating negative ones, for example "giving suitably challenging mathematics tasks related to students' interests, providing positive feedback, showing interest in mathematics". This becomes more critical as students get older, since positive emotions decrease and boredom increases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness that developmental trajectories of mathematics emotions and arithmetic skill vary significantly already in early grades
#### Constraints
- The recommendation's urgency increases with age, as enjoyment decreases and boredom, especially, increases over the school years

### Target Learners
- students in the first three school years, especially those struggling with basic arithmetic

### Target Learning Goals
- arithmetic fluency and positive mathematics-related achievement emotions
