# Structured facilitation of virtual meetings: preparation, ground rules, equitable turn-taking, and post-meeting reflection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Adapting Mittleman et al. and Clawson et al., the review provides facilitation strategies organized by meeting phase. Before the meeting, chairs should test technology in a "dry run" and have a 'Plan B'; during the meeting they should "Ensure that there is turn-taking and roughly equitable contributions to the discussion, particularly from those connected virtually"; at the end they should summarize decisions and gather reflections using 'Stop,' 'Start,' and 'Continue' prompts. These strategies aim to mitigate disengagement, misunderstanding, and conflict in virtual promotions meetings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An effective chair who establishes clear goals, structures, and norms, and monitors committee performance through check-ins or anonymous surveys
#### Constraints
- The strategies are drawn from virtual-meeting facilitation literature outside medical education; many also apply to face-to-face meetings

### Target Learners
- Chairs and members of virtual promotions and competence committees

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective, equitable participation and high-quality group decisions in virtual meetings
