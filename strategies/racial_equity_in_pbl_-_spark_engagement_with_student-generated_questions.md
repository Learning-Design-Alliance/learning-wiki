# Racial Equity in PBL - Spark Engagement with Student-Generated Questions

## Description
This strategy positions student-generated questions — not teacher-assigned prompts — as the driving force of project-based learning (PBL). Instead of presenting content and tasks, the facilitator invites students to ask what they genuinely want to know about a topic, then organizes the project around those questions. The equity rationale is explicit: traditional transmission-style instruction can function as one more site of disempowerment for Black and Latinx students, whereas authoring the questions that drive inquiry positions them as intellectual agents [Ladson-Billings' culturally relevant pedagogy](../principles/active-learning.md) [+M].

## Design Implications

Student question generation increases engagement because it builds on authentic curiosity and gives learners ownership of the inquiry path; research on classroom questioning shows that student-authored questions promote deeper cognitive engagement and richer discussion than teacher-only questioning [Chin & Osborne's review of student questioning](https://doi.org/10.1080/03057260808591649) [+M]. The strategy also connects to evidence that autonomy-supportive instruction raises intrinsic motivation [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S] and that belonging and agency matter for outcomes among marginalized students [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. Engagement gains are strongest when student questions are treated as consequential — when the project visibly changes shape in response to them [Engle & Conant on productive disciplinary engagement](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci4004_1) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator genuinely open to student-driven inquiry, including questions that diverge from the planned unit
- Classroom norms that make questioning safe and expected (see [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) routines for building discussion culture)
- A protocol for generating, sharing, and prioritizing questions (e.g., the Question Formulation Technique)
- Willingness to map student questions back to curriculum standards during planning

#### Constraints
- Student questions may not align with mandated standards or pacing guides; alignment work shifts to the planning phase and can increase teacher workload [-M]
- Open-ended question generation can produce diffuse, unfocused inquiry if the facilitator does not help students converge on workable questions [-M]
- Tokenizing risk: if student questions are collected but the project proceeds as planned, students perceive the invitation as inauthentic, which can depress rather than raise trust and engagement [-W]
- Novice learners may generate questions that are too broad or factual to sustain a project without [Scaffolding](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) of question quality [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Full question-driven projects:** the entire PBL arc stems from a prioritized set of student questions
- **Hybrid entry points:** teacher supplies the anchor topic or [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md); students generate the sub-questions that structure investigation
- **Question walls / parking lots:** ongoing collection of questions that feed mini-inquiries within a larger unit
- **Structured protocols:** the Question Formulation Technique (Rothstein & Santana) adds scaffolds — producing, improving, prioritizing, and planning use of questions

### Target Learners
- All grade levels; question generation scales from early elementary (oral questioning) to adult education
- Black and Latinx students who may experience teacher-directed instruction as disempowering; authoring questions affirms intellectual agency and supports culturally responsive practice [Ladson-Billings, culturally relevant pedagogy](https://doi.org/10.1080/00405849509543675) [+M]
- Disengaged or historically marginalized learners for whom autonomy and relevance are key motivational levers [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]
- Students new to inquiry may need explicit modeling of question quality before generation is productive [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Engagement and investment: increasing motivation to sustain a multi-week project
- Inquiry skills: formulating, refining, and prioritizing researchable questions
- Empowerment and agency: positioning students as co-designers of learning
- Content mastery: standards-aligned understanding reached through student-owned investigation

### Instructions
1. **Open with an anchor experience** — a text, [Case Study](../elements/case-studies.md), community issue, or provocative artifact related to the unit theme, to activate curiosity [Activation improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
2. **Generate questions** — students produce as many questions as possible without evaluation, using a protocol such as the [Question Formulation Technique](https://rightquestion.org) (produce → improve → prioritize)
3. **Prioritize and connect** — the class sorts questions into open/closed and researchable/not, then selects the questions that will drive the project; the facilitator maps them to standards
4. **Make questions consequential** — post the driving questions visibly and revisit them at each project milestone so students see their questions steering the work [Engle & Conant, productive disciplinary engagement](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci4004_1) [+M]
5. **Structure inquiry** — organize investigation, [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), and [Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md) around the prioritized questions, offering [Belonging](../elements/belonging.md)-affirming opportunities for students to pursue questions connected to their own communities
6. **Close the loop** — in final reflections and presentations, students answer their original questions and evaluate which remain open, treating unanswered questions as legitimate intellectual outcomes

## Related Strategies
- Activating prior knowledge — anchor experiences work best when they surface what students already know and wonder about
- Community-based learning — student questions about their own communities ground projects in authentic local contexts
- Choice boards — a structure for letting students pursue their prioritized questions through differentiated pathways

## Examples
- **The Question Formulation Technique** ([Right Question Institute](https://rightquestion.org)) — a published protocol used across K–12 and higher education in which students produce, refine, and prioritize questions that then drive lessons and projects; documented in Rothstein & Santana's *Make Just One Change* (2011).
- **A middle school science class** identifies environmental problems in its own neighborhood (air quality near a highway, access to clean water) and formulates the research questions that structure a data-collection project, connecting standards-aligned content to lived experience.
- **A history class** studying a standard unit on civil rights generates questions about local housing and school segregation, then investigates archival records from their own city, presenting findings to a community audience.
