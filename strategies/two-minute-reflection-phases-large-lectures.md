# Insert a 2-min reflection phase after each topic in large lectures, prompted by what was learned and what still needs thought

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's intervention embedded a short reflection phase after every teaching topic in mandatory large-group lectures. The three teaching topics per lecture were "each immediately followed by a 2-min reflection phase", either individual reflection with personal notes or group reflection. All phases used a prompt asking "What have I learned? What do I additionally need to think about?" to direct students to their knowledge gaps.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A reflection prompt asking what students learned and what they additionally need to think about
- Lecture content structured into time brackets with active elements
#### Constraints
- A comparison of both reflection methods to the same lecture without reflection is missing, since the plain lecture would reduce teaching time by 6 min per lecture
- It is unclear if the method itself or the introduction of individual self-learning phases into the lecture provided the retention effect

### Target Learners
- fourth-year medical students in lecture cohorts of more than 200 students

### Target Learning Goals
- short-term and long-term memory retention of lecture content
