# Use superficially easy introductory-level problems with intermediate-level students to expose algebraic sign competence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend problems that look introductory but demand sign consistency for intermediate-level students: such problems 'helps expose students' understanding of mathematics and their competence in using mathematics to express physical concepts'. The two-charge superposition problem, often given to introductory students, requires careful attention to the sign of algebraic expressions and consistency among directions, values, and signs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A problem whose surface simplicity hides sign-consistency demands, so that sign handling becomes visible in student reasoning
#### Constraints
- The sign is not the only difficulty: the other students in the data also struggled with determining the distance from the two point-charges to the field point in different regions

### Target Learners
- intermediate and upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- using mathematics to express physical concepts and manipulate algebraic signs consistently
