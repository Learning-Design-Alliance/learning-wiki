# Use low-cost task simulators within high-fidelity scenarios as a first step in a train-the-trainer cascade for bleeding control

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that healthcare educators and supervisors use the low-cost simulator approach to foster bleeding-control training among professionals and students, and that this basic training "could serve as the initial step in a train-the-trainer cascade, allowing for more knowledge distribution and reaching a broader audience". The authors also propose broadening the simulator's use to develop a first responder training approach with healthcare professionals and laypersons.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training delivered by experienced instructors; in this study military nurses provided 90-minute specialised training based on the first-care provider model
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a one-group pre-post design with nursing students only; the authors call for randomised trials and extension to other populations before wider claims

### Target Learners
- nursing students
- healthcare professionals
- laypersons

### Target Learning Goals
- bleeding control proficiency
- haemorrhage control self-efficacy

## Claims

- [Nursing students' knowledge of bleeding control rose from pre-training to immediately post-training and remained above pre-training levels at three months after training with a low-cost haemorrhage-control task simulator in a high-fidelity scenario, in one uncontrolled pre-post study](../claims/task-simulator-improves-bleeding-control-knowledge.md) [+W]
- [Training with the task simulator significantly increases nursing students' perceived self-efficacy in haemorrhage control, with a decline but retention above baseline at three months](../claims/task-simulator-increases-self-efficacy.md) [+W]
- [In one uncontrolled pre-post study, the volume of simulated blood lost while controlling haemorrhage fell from pre-test to post-test after training with the task simulator and rose at three months but remained below baseline](../claims/task-simulator-reduces-blood-volume-lost.md) [+W]
- [In one uncontrolled pre-post study, nursing students' time to control simulated exsanguinating haemorrhage fell after training with the task simulator, then rose at three months but remained below pre-test](../claims/task-simulator-reduces-time-to-control-bleeding.md) [+W]
- [In a one-group pre-post study, only about half of final-year nursing students demonstrated tourniquet-based haemorrhage control at baseline, while preintervention time and blood loss were inadequate](../claims/baseline-poor-haemorrhage-control-nursing-students.md) [+W]
