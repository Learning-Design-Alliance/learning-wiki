# Use a CI model to identify students showing low conscientious behaviour early in training to permit intervention or support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing the Conscientiousness Index early in medical training because negative undergraduate behaviour has been associated with postgraduate disciplinary action. An effective early measure "would permit early intervention to support such individuals or could be used to prevent progression to the later stages of a medical programme". The authors argue the CI's ease of collection, quantitative output and recording of actual behaviour make it suitable for flagging individuals at risk of unprofessional behaviour, and suggest the approach could extend to other professions such as teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A CI model must be implemented so that longitudinal, objective behavioural data are collected rather than subjective observations.
- For admissions-stage screening, CI data would need to come from a student's previous institution, which the authors note is easier for graduate applicants.
#### Constraints
- The authors note applicants could fake desired traits at interview, though longitudinal CI measures actual behaviour; they also report previously finding no CI % difference between graduate and non-graduate entrants.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- students in other professional training programmes such as teaching

### Target Learning Goals
- professional behaviour and identification of unprofessional conduct early in training
