# Use reflection, formative assessment and final assessment to close CMODS lessons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends a three-part assessment strategy for the lesson-summary stage when computer modeling systems are used. The abstract reports "the justification of such forms of evaluation as reflection, formative evaluation, final evaluation". Reflection is classified by functional content (mood and emotional state, activity, content of learning material), with techniques such as Bloom's daisy questions, a fishing net table, and an assumption tree. Formative assessment focuses on personal progress rather than the grade.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formative assessment requires a clear algorithm for determining the grade that is understandable to the student
#### Constraints
- Final assessment is positioned for determining quality of mastery of a specific topic, not for ongoing progress monitoring

### Target Learners
- students of general secondary education

### Target Learning Goals
- self-analysis of learning
- monitoring current mastery of a topic
