# Establish standardized team-based training programs and troubleshooting protocols covering console surgeons and surgical assistants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that RATS programs go beyond console-surgeon training: "teams must share basic strategies and protocols to manage unexpected challenges" and "Establishing standardized training programs and rigorous troubleshooting protocols may be essential to further reduce system failures and maintain patient safety." The rationale is that most malfunctions were setup-related problems that assistants resolved, and assistant turnover prolonged non-console time. The authors also cite multi-institutional evidence that structured implementation strategies, including simulation training, supervised case progression, and team-based learning, improved outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must cover the whole team, including surgical assistants, not only console surgeons
#### Constraints
- The authors note further prospective, multicenter studies are needed to validate training models

### Target Learners
- thoracic surgeons in training
- surgical assistants in robotic surgery programs

### Target Learning Goals
- robotic system setup and troubleshooting proficiency
- shared operative strategies for unexpected challenges
