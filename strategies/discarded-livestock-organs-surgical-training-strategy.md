# Use discarded inedible livestock organs as surgical training models to reduce live-animal use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A sourcing strategy for surgical simulation training: instead of killing laboratory animals solely for training, harvest organs that meat processing plants discard as inedible and convert them into training models via NVP fixation. The authors state, "By replacing the organs of livestock that are discarded because they are not typically used as edibles with training models, we believe that more laboratory animal lives could be saved." The NVP-fixed organ can be stored for an extended period, securing a larger supply of training models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supply chain from meat processing plants providing discarded organs with preserved vascular access for perfusion
#### Constraints
- The strategy is demonstrated for kidneys and, in preparation, the liver; extension to other major organs is planned but not yet validated

### Target Learners
- surgeon trainees
- medical students in surgical skills courses

### Target Learning Goals
- open and minimally invasive surgical procedures such as partial nephrectomy
