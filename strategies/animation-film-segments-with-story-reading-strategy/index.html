Pair short-story reading with short animation-film segments in EFL reading lessons¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q1–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's implemented recipe: teach story-book chapters twice weekly over three months and present animation films of "60 minutes divided into 6 to 7 minutes in each session" in the experimental group. The films present "the story through the movement of events, sound effects and the speech of the characters", letting students see, hear and interpret difficult events, and were followed by interviews showing learners were satisfied with the approach.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Animation content matched to the story-book chapters being read
  • Short film segments (6-7 minutes) integrated into regular reading sessions

Constraints¶

  • Reported pitfalls from learner interviews: taking a lot of time, poor relevance, poor listening, lack of interest

Target Learners¶

  • pre-intermediate EFL learners aged 13 to 15

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading comprehension of short stories
  • positive attitudes toward reading class

Affordances¶

  • Dual Coding Theory Animation Reading Framework

Claims¶

  • EFL learners taught reading comprehension with animation films outperformed traditionally taught controls on a posttest [+W]
  • Experimental-group learners expressed positive attitudes toward animation films in the reading class [+W]
  • The experimental and control groups did not differ significantly in reading comprehension at pretest [+W]
  • The authors selected the animation film using criteria of soundtrack, animated character, short duration, age-appropriate content, and depiction of planetary motion [~W]
  • A short animated film transmits a high density of information that cannot be learned in a single viewing and can negatively affect understanding [-W]
