# Perspective Taking Prompts

## Description
Perspective taking prompts direct learners to explicitly consider how another person — a character, historical actor, user, peer, or stakeholder — perceives a situation, what they know, what they want, and why they act as they do. They are carried out through structured questions ("What is she feeling here? Why might he have made that choice?"), writing tasks, role assignments, or [Role-Play](acting-role-play.md) scenarios that require learners to reason *from within* another position rather than merely *about* it.

## Design Implications

Explicit perspective taking improves social understanding, empathy, and intergroup attitudes, particularly when prompts require learners to generate the other person's mental state rather than passively receive it [Perspective-taking and empathy instruction improves intergroup attitudes.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M]. The mechanism is elaborative encoding: adopting a viewpoint forces learners to connect new material to goals, constraints, and emotions, producing richer retrieval routes than third-person summary. In subject-matter contexts (history, literature, design, ethics), prompts convert abstract content into situated reasoning and expose learners to multiple legitimate framings of the same event [Multiple perspectives and cases support flexible knowledge.](../claims/cognitive-flexibility-theory-multiple-cases.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task where at least two plausible viewpoints exist and the target viewpoint is *inferable* from available evidence, not arbitrary
- Prompts that demand articulation — writing, discussion, or performance — not just silent consideration
- Follow-up comparison: learners should contrast their adopted perspective with the original one or with peers' perspectives ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), [Debate](debate.md))
- Teacher framing that distinguishes *understanding* a perspective from *endorsing* it, especially for morally objectionable viewpoints

#### Constraints
- Perspective taking can backfire when the target is an adversary or out-group member: learners may project their own views onto the other ("egocentric bias") rather than genuinely adopting theirs [-M] — instructing learners to use their *own* knowledge of the other person, or to write from the other's first-person voice with evidence constraints, reduces this
- Effortful perspective taking consumes working memory; for novices still mastering domain content, prompts can compete with content learning [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [~M] — sequence prompts after basic content understanding, not during initial exposition
- Prompts applied to ill-chosen targets (e.g., "how does the molecule feel?") can encourage anthropomorphic misconceptions rather than disciplinary insight
- One-off prompts produce fragile effects; sustained, repeated perspective taking across contexts is needed for durable attitude change [Brief single-session interventions show weak long-run effects.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Written voice adoption**: learners write a diary entry, letter, or decision memo in the other person's first-person voice, grounded in textual evidence
- **Structured question stems**: "What does X know? What does X want? What is X afraid of?" applied at decision points in a narrative or case
- **Role assignment in simulation**: learners hold assigned positions ([Assigned Positions](../elements/assigned-positions.md)) in a structured [Debate](debate.md), requiring advocacy of a view they may not hold
- **Design/user empathy**: in engineering or product work, prompts ask learners to reason from a user's goals and limitations (e.g., accessibility personas)
- **Empathic discipline framing**: teachers prompted to consider a misbehaving student's perspective respond more constructively [Brief empathic discipline intervention reduces suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Middle childhood through adulthood; perspective taking gains are largest when learners already have some theory-of-mind and domain knowledge to draw on
- Learners in heterogeneous or intergroup settings, where structured perspective taking reduces stereotyped attributions [Perspective-taking and empathy instruction improves intergroup attitudes.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M]
- Less effective for very young learners who cannot yet distinguish another's beliefs from their own, or for novices still building baseline content knowledge [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Social-emotional learning: empathy, emotion recognition, interpersonal problem solving
- Disciplinary interpretation: understanding historical actors, literary characters, and stakeholders from the inside
- Argumentation and ethics: anticipating counterarguments and stakeholder interests before constructing a position ([Argument Construction](../elements/argument-construction.md))
- Design and communication: reasoning about audience needs and user constraints

### Instructions
1. Select a decision point or conflict in the material where the target person's knowledge, goals, or emotions diverge from the learner's own.
2. Pose the prompt in first-person, evidence-constrained terms: "Write what this person is thinking at this moment, using only what they could know."
3. Have learners produce an artifact — written voice, oral statement, or enacted [Role-Play](acting-role-play.md) — rather than merely answer verbally.
4. Debrief by comparing perspectives: contrast the adopted view with learners' original judgments and with peers' versions ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).
5. Repeat across varied cases so the habit generalizes beyond a single character or group.

## Related Strategies
- [Acting-Role-Play](acting-role-play.md) — embodied enactment of a perspective; stronger but more resource-intensive than written prompts
- [Debate](debate.md) — assigned-position debate operationalizes perspective taking as advocacy
- [Case-Based Learning](case-based-learning.md) — cases supply the situated detail that makes perspective prompts answerable
- [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) — affective prompts directed at the self; perspective taking prompts direct the same move at others

## Examples
- **Facing History and Ourselves** (https://www.facinghistory.org) — history curriculum built around structured prompts asking students to write and discuss from the viewpoints of participants in historical atrocities and resistance, with explicit norms separating understanding from endorsement.
- **Perspective-taking writing in literature instruction** — students compose a character's diary entry at a turning point, citing textual evidence for each inference, then compare entries in small groups.
- **Empathic discipline prompts for teachers** — brief online modules asking teachers to reappraise student misbehavior from the student's perspective improved teacher–student relationships and cut suspension rates [Brief empathic discipline intervention reduces suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+M].
