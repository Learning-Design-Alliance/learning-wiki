# Implement adaptive difficulty schedules and prefer variable-priority training over fractionated part-task training for complex multitask skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's implications for training direct designers to "strive to implement adaptive logic for ID schedules" and to be "wary of using PTT techniques for complex multitask skills, either minimizing the number of part-task trials or adopting VPT strategies as an alternative." The strategy follows directly from the meta-analytic moderators: adaptive ID produced a 36% benefit while fixed-step ID produced a 23% cost, and VPT preserved time-sharing practice that fractionated PTT prevented. Designers should also weigh learner experience and skill type, since selection of training strategy "is not one size fits all."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adaptive ID requires technology for real-time (or semi-real-time) performance measurement and adaptive decisions on how much intrinsic load to increase.
#### Constraints
- Fixed-step difficulty increases produced a transfer cost, so adaptive logic is required for ID to succeed.
- PTT remains usable for sequentially performed parts (segmentation showed neither cost nor benefit).

### Target Learners
- novice adult learners of complex perceptual-motor or multitask skills

### Target Learning Goals
- transfer of training to whole-task performance
