# Deploy low-cost hands-on lab kits simultaneously across face-to-face, hybrid, and remote modalities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 review), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structuring instructional lab experiments as economical, mailable kits so a single activity serves in-person, hybrid, and remote learners at once. Kits let a brick-and-mortar course move fully online without disruption, since "students using the kits in a brick -and-mortar setting can simply take the kits home and continue the lab class remotely". The burette experiment instantiates this: under $30 in materials, safely completable from home, and added to course cost as a fee.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- kits must be affordable, safe for unsupervised home use, and buildable/operable by students without instructor or technician intervention
#### Constraints
- no fully online ABET-accredited mechanical engineering bachelor's degree program yet existed at the time of writing, per the article's citation

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students
- high school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- hands-on experimentation in energy-thermal-fluid sciences across multiple learning modalities
