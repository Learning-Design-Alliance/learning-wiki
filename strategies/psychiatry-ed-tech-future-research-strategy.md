# Future psychiatry education technology studies should use active comparators, standardized assessment tools, and long-term follow-up

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that future research focus on large-scale, well-controlled studies comparing innovative technologies to active learning interventions, assessing long-term retention, and exploring cost-effectiveness and scalability. It specifically recommends validated assessment methods such as the Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE) and Objective Structured Assessment of Technical Skills (OSATS), which "offer reliable and reproducible ways to evaluate clinical competence and procedural performance." It also calls for clear frameworks and detailed methodological design guidance to enhance the maturity of these technologies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Comparisons against active learning modalities rather than outdated or baseline methods
- Standardized assessment tools such as OSCE and OSATS for comparability
#### Constraints
- The current evidence base's methodological heterogeneity made meta-analysis impossible; publication bias may have favored positive results

### Target Learners
- medical students
- psychiatry residents

### Target Learning Goals
- psychiatric knowledge
- procedural skills
- diagnostic reasoning
