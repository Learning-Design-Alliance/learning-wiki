Strengthen future FYS/LC evaluations with propensity score analysis, engagement and satisfaction measures, and multi-cohort follow-up¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends several improvements to future evaluations of FYS and FYS/LC interventions: more deliberate experimental designs using propensity score analysis to define the value added of the LC component, adding engagement and satisfaction measures, and following multiple cohorts through to graduation. The authors note that "evaluation over multiple years may be more revealing" and that interventions "need time to show fruit."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A more deliberate experimental design with more closely matched groups or statistical control using propensity score analysis.
  • Inclusion of an engagement measure like the National Survey of Student Engagement and a satisfaction measure like the Student Satisfaction Inventory.

Constraints¶

  • Four-year and six-year graduation rate comparisons are years away, so current results can be seen as preliminary at best.
  • With over 20 learning communities and sections of the FYS, approaches among instructors and peer mentors could impact outcomes differentially.

Target Learners¶

  • Undecided first-time-in-college (FTIC) students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved evaluation of intervention effectiveness on retention, GPA, and learning outcomes

Claims¶

  • The FYS produced the highest GPA and good-standing outcomes while the FYS/LC produced the highest retention, suggesting differential intervention effects [+W]
  • Fall-to-spring and fall-to-fall retention rates did not differ significantly between intervention and control undecided students [~W]
  • Statistically significant GPA and good-standing advantages for intervention groups disappeared by fall-to-fall measurement [~W]
  • The three intervention sub-groups did not differ significantly on pre-matriculation at-risk characteristics [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use a Freshman Interest Group (FIG) model learning community as a resource-parsimonious retention intervention
