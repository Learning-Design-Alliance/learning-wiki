Keep sessions voluntary to identify less useful teaching; reserve mandatory attendance for crucial content¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The review proposes using voluntary attendance as a signal of perceived session utility: "Keeping sessions voluntary can help to ascertain which lessons are more useful, while enforcing mandatory attendance can help to ensure crucial knowledge is imparted". This dual approach lets educators identify weak sessions while ensuring essential content reaches students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Attendance must not be compulsory, so attendance rates can reflect perceived session utility

Constraints¶

  • The review notes this marker is no longer suitable if compulsory attendance is mandated

Target Learners¶

  • medical students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • identifying and improving teaching session quality

Claims¶

  • Mandatory attendance policy initially raised attendance but was abolished due to dissatisfaction, administrative burden and declining performance [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Weekly trainee-led CBL sessions with case presentation, self-directed research between sessions, and attendance and utility tracking
