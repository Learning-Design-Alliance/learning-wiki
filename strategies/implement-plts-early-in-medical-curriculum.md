# Implement peer-led teaching sessions predominantly in the early phases of medical education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating peer-led teaching sessions in the early phases of medical education, where foundational science content benefits most. The authors write that "incorporating PLTS predominantly in the early phases of medical education could optimize learning outcomes" and that "Early implementation of PLTS in medical education could be helpful for students adjusting their study routines." They also recommend building "a robust yet qualified group of peer tutors" and leveraging program data to demonstrate benefits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Second-year students who have recently completed the material and scored in the upper 25th percentile on relevant exams served as instructors
#### Constraints
- Benefit was observed for foundational science content only; the advantage did not extend to organ systems physiology content in this study

### Target Learners
- First-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Foundational science knowledge for NBME-style examinations

## Claims

- [First-year medical students who attended at least five peer-led teaching sessions scored significantly higher on the first basic-sciences NBME exam than non-attendees, by 4.28% on average](../claims/plts-attendees-higher-basic-science-exam-428-percent.md) [+W]
- [The exam-score advantage of peer-led teaching session attendees did not extend to the second organ-systems-physiology examination](../claims/plts-advantage-not-significant-physiology-exam.md) [~W]
- [PLTS attendees and non-attendees were comparable on MCAT scores, first-generation status, and socioeconomic background before both examinations](../claims/plts-groups-comparable-mcat-demographics.md) [+W]
