# Action Planning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Action planning is the process by which learners translate self-reflective insight into concrete, explicit plans for future behavior — specifying what they will do, when, where, and how. It typically follows a cycle of [self-assessment](../elements/assessment.md) or reflection and produces a written or verbal commitment ("Next time I write an essay, I will outline my argument before drafting"). In the self-regulated learning literature, action planning sits at the intersection of the forethought and self-reflection phases: it converts evaluative conclusions into proximal intentions and implementation intentions [Gollwitzer's implementation-intention framework](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.54.7.493) [+S].

## Design Implications

Action planning closes the loop between reflection and behavior change; without a specific plan, reflective insight rarely alters subsequent performance [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]. Plans are most effective when they are specific, proximal, and framed as if-then contingencies rather than vague aspirations [Gollwitzer, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.54.7.493) [+S]. Follow-up structures — [check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md), [coaching](../elements/coaching.md), or [action-oriented feedback](action-oriented_feedback.md) — are needed to keep plans active, since unmonitored plans decay quickly [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A prior reflective activity that surfaces what worked and what did not ([activities for student self-reflection](activities_for_student_self-reflection.md))
- A structure that forces specificity: action, context, timing, and success criteria
- A follow-up mechanism ([check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md), portfolios, or learning logs) that revisits the plan
- Learner ownership — plans imposed by the instructor undermine the commitment function [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]

#### Constraints
- Vague plans ("try harder," "study more") show little effect; specificity is the active ingredient [Gollwitzer, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.54.7.493) [-M]
- Outcome-focused plans can demotivate novices who cannot yet connect actions to outcomes [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [~S]
- Over-planning without execution opportunities breeds planning fallacy; plans must be paired with enactment and revision cycles
- For learners with very low self-efficacy, ambitious plans can backfire and reduce persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Micro-goals:** break plans into small, immediately achievable steps ([achievable micro-goals](achievable_micro-goals.md))
- **Implementation intentions:** if-then format ("If I get stuck on a problem for 10 minutes, then I will re-read my notes")
- **Structured templates:** KWL charts, learning contracts, or exam wrappers that prompt plan formation after assessment
- **Conferencing:** one-on-one [check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md) where teacher and learner co-construct the plan

### Target Learners
- Adolescents and adults developing self-regulation; younger learners need heavy scaffolding of the planning format [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Struggling learners benefit especially, but plans must be modest and achievable to protect efficacy [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Advanced learners may find templated planning redundant and can self-plan [Expertise reverses the benefit of high guidance.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-regulation: goal setting, monitoring, and strategic adjustment
- Metacognition: connecting reflection to future strategy selection
- Habit and behavior change: study habits, revision practices, collaboration behaviors

### Instructions
1. Prompt reflection on a completed task: what worked, what didn't, and why ([activities for student self-reflection](activities_for_student_self-reflection.md)).
2. Have learners state a specific, process-focused goal — an action, not an outcome [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M].
3. Convert the goal into an implementation intention with context and timing [Gollwitzer, 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.54.7.493) [+S].
4. Schedule a follow-up [check-in](../principles/check-ins.md) to review progress and revise the plan.
5. Fade the scaffolding over time so learners internalize the plan–act–reflect cycle [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

## Examples
- **Exam wrappers** (e.g., those published by [Carnegie Mellon's Eberly Center](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/designteach/teach/examwrappers.html)) — after an exam is returned, students answer structured prompts and write a concrete study plan for the next assessment cycle.
- **Learning contracts** in competency-based and adult education programs, where learners commit to actions, timelines, and evidence of completion, reviewed at scheduled conferences.
- **Implementation-intention prompts in LMS checklists** — platforms such as Canvas allow instructors to attach "if-then" planning prompts to assignment releases, prompting students to schedule when and where they will work.
