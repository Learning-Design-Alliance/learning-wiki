# Multimodal Composition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Multimodal composition asks learners to create texts that combine multiple modes of communication — words, images, sound, movement, spatial layout — rather than relying on prose alone. Learners produce slide shows, video essays, audio documentaries, infographics, websites, and other artifacts, and in doing so learn what each mode affords and constrains. Digital literacy — the ability to locate, evaluate, use, and create information with digital tools — is both a prerequisite and an outcome.

## Design Implications

Multimodal composition leverages the fact that words and pictures are processed through partly separate channels, and that coordinating them well improves learning while coordinating them poorly harms it [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [~M]. The pedagogical value lies less in the technology than in the rhetorical decisions learners must make: choosing modes deliberately for audience and purpose, and articulating why a mode fits the message.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to computers, multimedia software, and internet connectivity; training in composition techniques for each mode
- Explicit design criteria so learners understand that mode choice is assessed, not decorative
- An authentic audience or purpose, which gives mode decisions real stakes ([Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md))
- Time for iteration — multimodal work is substantially more revision-intensive than prose

#### Constraints
- Production demands (software learning curves, rendering, editing) can consume working memory and time that should go to rhetorical learning, effectively adding extraneous load [Cognitive Load Theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) [-M]
- Unequal access to devices, software, and bandwidth can turn the assignment into a measure of household resources rather than learning
- Assessment is difficult: rubrics that privilege polish over rhetorical reasoning reward technical skill rather than learning
- Learners with weak prior knowledge of a topic may produce visually impressive artifacts with shallow content; mode choice does not substitute for substantive understanding

#### Implementation Variability
- Low-tech variants: poster sessions, zines, storyboards, annotated slides — the pedagogy survives without expensive tools
- Scaffolded variants: provide templates or exemplar analyses before free composition ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) professional examples builds the evaluative vocabulary learners need)
- Individual vs. collaborative production; collaborative variants add coordination load but build shared critique skills

### Target Learners
- Higher education and professional development learners, who can manage the production demands while focusing on rhetorical decisions
- Learners whose strengths lie outside prose — multimodal options increase task value and can raise persistence [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Less suitable as a first exposure to a content domain; learners need enough domain knowledge to judge what their composition communicates [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Digital and media literacy: understanding what platforms and modes allow, disallow, and require
- Rhetorical reasoning: matching mode, medium, and audience to purpose
- Deep processing of content: transforming source material into a new representational format forces generative encoding [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. Analyze exemplars: have learners annotate how professional multimodal texts use each mode ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md))
2. Define audience and purpose before choosing tools ([Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md))
3. Draft a design plan specifying which modes carry which content and why
4. Compose, applying multimedia design principles — avoid redundant narration over identical on-screen text, and segment content into learner-paced units [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [+M]
5. Peer critique focused on rhetorical effectiveness, then revise ([Application](../elements/application.md))
6. Reflect: learners articulate what each mode contributed and what it constrained

## Examples
- A first-year composition course where students produce a video essay remixing a research paper, with a written rationale defending each mode choice
- [NWP Digital Is](https://digitalis.nwp.org) — the National Writing Project's collection of multimodal composition classroom practices
- Pre-service teacher programs requiring an audio documentary on a community issue, assessed on rhetorical effectiveness rather than production polish
