# Reduce mobile phone dependence by offering non-mobile-based activities and monitoring tools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
To counter the detrimental impact of mobile phone dependency on study engagement, the article recommends that "schools and families should collaborate to offer students a greater variety of non-mobile-based entertainment and learning opportunities", such as sports, social events and reading, alongside developing time management and mobile usage monitoring tools, and prompt professional psychological counseling for students showing symptoms of dependence. It grounds this in its finding that mobile phone dependence negatively predicts study engagement and mediates the time management effect.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educational institutions recognize mobile phone dependency within students' mental health education frameworks, and schools and families collaborate on alternatives.
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional; the article notes it cannot establish definitive causal relationships.
