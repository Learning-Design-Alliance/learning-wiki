# Cultivating Belonging Through Environmental Cues

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy deliberately designs the physical, social, and communicative environment of a learning space so that learners from all backgrounds perceive themselves as welcome and capable members of the community. Cues include who is visibly represented (posters, examples, role models), the language used in materials and discourse, and the everyday signals of inclusion (greetings, norms, accessibility of help). Because belonging is inferred from ambient information rather than stated assurances, the environment itself does much of the communicative work.

## Design Implications

Belonging is a psychological state that predicts engagement, persistence, and achievement, and it is highly sensitive to situational cues rather than stable traits [Walton & Cohen's brief belonging interventions produced lasting GPA gains for marginalized students.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1198364) [+S]. Learners actively scan environments for signals about whether "people like me" succeed here; when those signals are absent or negative, belonging uncertainty rises and performance suffers [Cheryan et al. showed that stereotypical computer science environments depressed women's interest even when no people were present.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0013496) [+S]. The strategy connects to [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md): belonging is one of three basic psychological needs, and environments that satisfy it support intrinsic motivation [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Audit of existing environmental cues: who is represented in displays, examples, and named contributors; what the space implicitly says about who belongs
- Diverse, authentic representation in materials — examples, case studies, and role models that reflect the learner population without tokenism
- Consistent inclusive language in syllabi, feedback, and classroom discourse (e.g., normalizing struggle as part of learning, avoiding fixed-ability framing)
- Visible help-seeking pathways: office hours, norms that normalize questions, peer community structures ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))

#### Constraints
- Cues are read differently by different learners; a representation intended as inclusive can read as tokenistic if it is superficial or disconnected from actual treatment [-M] — surface diversity without equitable participation can backfire
- Environmental cues cannot compensate for exclusionary interactions; a single public humiliation or stereotype threat experience outweighs weeks of positive decor [-S]
- Effects are strongest for learners experiencing *belonging uncertainty*; learners with secure belonging may be unaffected, and over-signaling can feel patronizing [~M]
- One-shot "diversity" displays decay; cues must be maintained and matched by ongoing relational practice or they lose credibility [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Physical spaces:** wall displays, seating arrangements, lab/studio design, accessibility features (see [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md))
- **Curricular materials:** diverse authors, examples, and names in problems; representation in images and videos
- **Social rituals:** greeting routines, community-building openers, norms co-created with learners
- **Digital environments:** course site imagery, discussion norms, instructor presence and tone in announcements

### Target Learners
- Students from groups underrepresented in a field or institution, who face higher belonging uncertainty [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- First-generation and transfer students navigating unfamiliar academic cultures
- All learners benefit from clear, welcoming norms, but the marginal effect is largest for those at risk of disengagement [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Affective and motivational outcomes: engagement, persistence, willingness to seek help
- Dispositional goals: identity development ("I am a science person"), community membership
- Indirect support for cognitive goals: learners who feel they belong participate more, generating the practice and feedback that drive learning

### Instructions
1. Audit the current environment for representation, language, and signaling — photograph the space, review materials, and note who is present and absent.
2. Redesign displays, examples, and materials so learners encounter authentic, non-stereotypical representation of people like themselves succeeding in the domain.
3. Establish and model inclusive discourse norms — normalize error and struggle, use welcoming language, and respond to help-seeking in ways that reinforce membership.
4. Pair environmental cues with relational practices — [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) routines, structured [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), and feedback that communicates high expectations plus confidence in the learner.
5. Re-audit periodically and with learner input; cues must track the actual community, not an imagined one.

## Examples
- **Cheryan et al.'s computer science classroom studies** — replacing Star Trek posters and video game paraphernalia with nature posters and neutral decor significantly raised women's interest in CS, demonstrating that objects alone carry belonging signals ([https://doi.org/10.1037/a0013496](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0013496)).
- **Walton & Cohen's social-belonging intervention** — a brief session reframing worry about belonging as common and temporary improved GPA and health among African American college students years later ([https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1198364](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1198364)).
- **Elementary classroom libraries** — curated collections such as those promoted by [We Need Diverse Books](https://diversebooks.org) ensure learners see themselves in texts, pairing environmental representation with [Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md).
