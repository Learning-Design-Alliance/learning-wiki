# Use should-do instructions and rating formats to improve SJT favourability among minority applicants without disadvantaging majority applicants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's practical implications for admission committees are, first, that "using should-do instructions as opposed to would-do instructions increases the SJT's favourability among ethnic minority applicants" and, second, that men and first-generation university students prefer rating formats. Because majority applicants did not differ across the design features, these choices can raise minority favourability without unfavourable perceptions among majority applicants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An SJT used in admission whose response instructions and response format can be manipulated, with applicant perceptions monitored.
#### Constraints
- Perceptions were based only on a short description and two example items; the authors advise assessing perceptions after completing a full SJT used for actual selection.

### Target Learners
- medical school applicants, including ethnic minority and first-generation applicants

### Target Learning Goals
- widening access to medical school through fairer-perceived selection
