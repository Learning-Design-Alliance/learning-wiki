# Develop portable low-cost COTS-based simulators to enable ubiquitous training and presurgical warm-up in minimally invasive surgery

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends exploring COTS gesture-detection technology as the basis for portable, low-cost simulation for skills learning and training in minimally invasive surgery, motivated by the high cost of existing virtual and augmented reality simulators. The authors conclude that "COTS devices applied to hand and instrument gesture–based interfaces in the field of simulation for skills learning and training in MIS could open up a promising field to achieve ubiquitous training and presurgical warm up". The strategy builds on validity evidence that simulation in virtual reality environments is effective for motor skills learning in MIS.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- New COTS technologies that allow hand gestures and instrument movements to be detected
- Development and validation of new simulation models in virtual environments
#### Constraints
- The field was still in the exploratory phase in areas requiring touchless manipulation within environments that must adhere to asepsis and antisepsis protocols, such as angiography suites and operating rooms
- Most identified articles were proof-of-concept or prototype user testing and feasibility testing studies
