Strategies

Have a different student open each lesson with a self-chosen quotation to stimulate interest and motivation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A specific implementable routine from the formative experiment: during the pedagogical experiment in the experimental group "each lesson started with citations prepared by students (each lesson—different students)". Because each student chooses a quotation he or she is interested in rather than one required by the program, the practice evokes response and stimulates interest in English. Over a year students accumulate a baggage of proverbs and aphorisms that makes their speech richer and more expressive.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must be given freedom to choose quotations that interest them personally rather than program-required ones

Constraints¶

  • The article reports this routine with 16-year-old senior-level students in subgroups of 13

Target Learners¶

  • senior secondary EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • motivation for language learning
  • linguistic interest
  • oral communication skills

Claims¶

  • The experimental group showed increased motivation and improved communication skills after citation-based lessons, while the control group remained virtually unchanged [+W]
  • A series of lessons using citations raised senior students' proficiency in using quotations, with 85% of the experimental group reaching the high level versus none in the control group [+W]
