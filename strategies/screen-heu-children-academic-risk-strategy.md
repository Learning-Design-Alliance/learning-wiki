# Screen children HEU for risk of poor academic achievement and study modifiable contributors with multi-level mixed methods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that, given the large and expanding population of children HEU, research and practice should identify modifiable contributors to poor academic performance, develop screening tools to identify children most at risk, and design interventions to mitigate risk. They state that "screening tools are urgently needed to identify children most at risk for poor academic achievement" and that future studies should use multi-level mixed methods measuring factors such as illness of parents or siblings, school absenteeism, bullying, stigma and household internet access.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Future studies of actual academic performance among children HEU using a multi-level mixed methods study design measuring familial and societal factors, plus qualitative in-depth interviews capturing family dynamics over time
#### Constraints
- The article notes societal factors were not measured and limited data were collected on potential familial factors in this quantitative analysis

### Target Learners
- School-aged children (standard 3 through 6, ages 7–14) who are HIV-exposed uninfected in Botswana public primary schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance in Mathematics, Science, English, Setswana and overall primary-school grades
