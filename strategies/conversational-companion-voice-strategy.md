# Write explanatory text in a conversational first-person voice that anticipates reader difficulty

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The book adopts a companion-like authorial voice: "I wrote a lot of this book as though I was sitting next to you and explaining it in person." The author positions himself as "a guide, or a significant perturbation" easing the transition to physics-level mathematical thinking, and explicitly acknowledges that "learning is very hard." The strategy includes giving readers permission to skim: "Whenever you ﬁnd my explanations long-winded, feel free to proceed through faster," and inviting them to return to earlier sections when stuck.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The author must anticipate where readers will get stuck and provide back-references they can follow.
#### Constraints
- The voice is calibrated for first- and second-year undergraduates; the author expects the book will be less central later in an undergraduate career.

### Target Learners
- first- and second-year undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- independent use of the text as a self-study reference
- reducing math anxiety during the high-school-to-college transition
