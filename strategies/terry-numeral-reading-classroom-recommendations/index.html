Apply numeral-reading findings to classroom teaching through Terry's recommended practices¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The reviewer highlights that Terry's monograph closes with guidance for teachers grounded in the eye-movement findings on how numerals and problems are read. The final chapter is described as "a valuable discussion of the numerous practical applications of the conclusions to classroom teaching" which "contains definite recommendations which should find their way into practice". The strategy is to translate findings about re-reading problems and partial first readings of numerals into arithmetic instruction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need familiarity with the monograph's conclusions about numeral and problem reading

Constraints¶

  • The review characterizes the study as introductory to more elaborate detailed inquiry, so the recommendations rest on preliminary findings

Target Learners¶

  • school pupils learning to read numerals and arithmetic problems

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improved reading and comprehension of numerical problem material

Claims¶

  • Partial first reading of numerals supports a quicker grasp of a problem's meaning and conditions [+W]
  • Arithmetic problems are re-read before computation, and first readings capture only short, common or round numbers [+W]
  • Adults read ordinary prose much faster than arithmetic problem material or isolated numerals [+W]
