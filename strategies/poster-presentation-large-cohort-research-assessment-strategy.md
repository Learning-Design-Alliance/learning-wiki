# Use structured poster presentations with a marking rubric to assess research projects in large undergraduate cohorts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advocates poster presentations as an assessment format where staff are few and student numbers are large. Students present group posters to academic staff and peers, assessed with a structured marking rubric, with verbal feedback after each presentation. The authors conclude that "the poster presentations were effective and efficient for assessing the research of a large cohort of students" and that the approach "can be adapted to many other settings, including other fields in health sciences".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured marking rubric used by staff assessors; supervisor guidance during poster development; allocated presentation and question time per group
#### Constraints
- Students reported crowded, noisy venues as a challenge; one staff member noted weak student participation in question-and-answer sessions; the authors state findings may have limited generalisability because of the unique context

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in large cohorts

### Target Learning Goals
- demonstrating ability to collect, analyse, interpret and present research data
