# Use simulation training prior to actual surgery when elective surgical experience for trainees is disrupted

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends simulation training before actual surgery in circumstances where trainees' elective surgical experience declines, such as during the COVID-19 pandemic. It states: "simulation training prior to actual surgery will benefit residents and patients alike". The recommendation rests on the trial's evidence that simulator-based preparation reduced intraoperative errors among novices early in their learning curve.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a surgical simulator with demonstrated validity for the target procedure
#### Constraints
- The article frames this in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic and declining elective surgeries; it does not claim the strategy was tested in that setting

### Target Learners
- ophthalmology residents whose operative exposure is reduced

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining and acquiring surgical skills for cataract surgery before live procedures
