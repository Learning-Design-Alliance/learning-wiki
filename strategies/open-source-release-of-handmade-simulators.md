# Release handmade simulators as open technology under a Creative Commons license to broaden access and encourage reproduction and improvement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors publish how to make the simulators freely and keep them as open technology (open source), with all simulators carrying a Creative Commons license and available to be reproduced or adapted in other services on a public website. The aim is that people "can access and reproduce the simulators, expanding the resources that teachers who work in the training of new professionals have at hand". The publication of new ideas and simulators and the reproduction and modification of existing ones is allowed and encouraged, with citation of the original authorship.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Citation of the original authorship when simulators are recreated and adapted to different realities
#### Constraints
- The article found no evidence that hyper-reality of a simulator improves participant learning, so low cost and sometimes low fidelity do not appear to be an obstacle to use

### Target Learners
- teachers and trainers of health professionals
- students in resource-limited settings

### Target Learning Goals
- broadening access to simulation-based training
- reproducibility of low-cost simulators
