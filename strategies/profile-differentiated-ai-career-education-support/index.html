Differentiated, profile-matched university AI-readiness and career-education support instead of uniform AI-literacy programs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that universities replace uniform AI-literacy programs with support matched to students' AI-attitude profiles. For detached students, the priority is "to increase the salience and career relevance of AI through discipline-specific tasks, guided reflection, and mastery experiences"; anxious students need structured skill development and realistic labor-market information; vigilant students need support for psychological sustainability. Institutionally, AI education should be integrated with career development through joint programming by academic programs and career centers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Assessment or identification of students' AI-attitude configurations to target differentiated support
  • Integration of AI education with career development rather than treating it as a separate technical service

Constraints¶

  • Institutional communication should remain balanced: overly optimistic messages may encourage uncritical reliance, whereas alarmist messages may intensify anxiety without improving preparedness

Target Learners¶

  • first-year university students across the four AI-attitude profiles

Target Learning Goals¶

  • AI creative self-efficacy, career crafting, and self-perceived employability

Affordances¶

  • Configurational Bivalent Ai Attitude Framework

Claims¶

  • Latent profile analysis of six AI-attitude indicators yields four profiles: Positive Empowerment, Anxious Acceptance, Low-Perception Detached, and High-Perception High-Vigilance [+W]
  • Latent career adapt-abilities profiles differ in AI anxiety, with the low group most anxious and the high group least anxious [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Stratified career development education and psychological interventions tailored to career adapt-abilities profiles
  • Integrate AI literacy, digital skill development, and technology-focused career planning into coursework, with targeted support for higher-anxiety groups
  • Differentiated AI literacy training matched to acceptance profiles, with interventions beyond operational skills for Resistant Skeptics
  • Provide differentiated scaffolding matched to teachers' behavioral-cognitive profiles when supporting multi-agent AI workflow design
  • Differentiate T2I pedagogy by student profile and adopt a stage-sensitive approach to AI literacy and assessment in studio courses
