# Scale up simple school-based experiments to realistic monument dimensions, turning the outdoor classroom into a mathematics laboratory

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends a sequence in which pupils run simplified experiments at school (Stone Race moving a weighted box, Dig a Hole with replica tools, density measurement with displacement vessels), then scale results up to realistic masses and volumes and apply them to a surveyed monument. The authors state that "applying their result in the outdoor classroom environment allowed the students to exper ience the simplifications of the models developed in the lab". Surveying at Arbor Low involved assuming the ditch was circular with a triangular cross-section.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School-based experiments that illustrate how to simplify problems and test initial assumptions before the field visit
#### Constraints
- Simplifications must be discussed with students, e.g. the ditch was much deeper in the past

### Target Learners
- Upper primary and secondary pupils working in small teams

### Target Learning Goals
- Applying basic numeracy to estimate construction effort, stone mass and build time of ancient monuments
