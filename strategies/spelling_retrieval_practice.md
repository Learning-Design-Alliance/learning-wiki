# Spelling Retrieval Practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Spelling retrieval practice asks learners to produce a word's spelling from memory — via dictated spelling tests, spaced quizzes, or write-from-prompt activities — instead of passively studying word lists or copying words. Each retrieval attempt strengthens the orthographic memory trace and provides feedback about which spellings have and have not been mastered. It is the application of [retrieval practice](../elements/practice.md) to the orthographic domain.

## Design Implications

Retrieval — attempting to recall spelling before seeing the correct form — produces stronger long-term retention than restudying the same words, an effect well established across verbal learning tasks [+S]. The act of generating the letter sequence, even when the attempt is imperfect, engages deeper processing than reading or copying, which can create fluency illusions in which words "look familiar" but cannot be produced accurately [-S]. Feedback after each attempt is essential: without correction, errors may be rehearsed and consolidated [~S]. Spacing retrieval sessions across days rather than massing them in one sitting substantially improves retention [~S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A word set appropriate to learners' current orthographic knowledge
- A production task (writing from dictation, spelling from a definition or sentence context) that requires full retrieval, not recognition
- Immediate corrective feedback showing the correct spelling after each attempt
- A schedule that revisits words at expanding intervals ([Spaced Repetition](../elements/spaced-repetition.md))

#### Constraints
- Copying and re-reading word lists produce familiarity without production ability [-S] — learners and teachers often mistake this fluency for mastery
- Retrieval before any initial instruction produces high error rates and can entrench misspellings in learners with no orthographic foundation [~S]; an initial study phase must precede testing
- Pure whole-word retrieval without attention to morphological or phonological patterns limits transfer to untaught words [~M]
- High-stakes spelling tests with public error can create anxiety that displaces working memory resources needed for retrieval [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Look–Say–Cover–Write–Check**: a self-managed retrieval cycle widely used in UK primary classrooms; the "cover" step forces retrieval, though the "check" step is often skipped, degrading the feedback loop
- **Spaced digital drills**: tools such as [Spellzone](https://www.spellzone.com) or [Spelling Shed](https://www.spellingshed.com) schedule word retrieval at expanding intervals with immediate feedback
- **Sentence-dictation**: retrieval embedded in meaningful writing context, adding the load of simultaneous composition
- **Morphology-focused retrieval**: retrieving spellings of word families (*sign → signal → signature*) to build generalizable orthographic knowledge

### Target Learners
- Beginning readers and spellers building a sight vocabulary, once automatic word recognition is developing [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M]
- Struggling spellers, who benefit most from the explicit feedback loop that retrieval provides, provided errors are corrected immediately
- Less effective as unguided testing for learners with no prior exposure to the word forms; an initial study phase is required [~S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Automatic, accurate production of high-frequency and curriculum word spellings
- Retention of irregular spellings that cannot be derived from phonological rules
- Transfer of orthographic patterns (morphological, etymological) to new words

### Instructions
1. **Initial study**: present the word with its meaning and a note on any tricky part (e.g., "hear the *-able*, remember it's *-able* not *-ible*") — [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) the word into pronounceable or morphological units reduces load [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
2. **Cover and retrieve**: hide the word and have learners write it from memory — the retrieval attempt, not the copying, drives learning
3. **Immediate feedback**: show the correct spelling; learners compare and self-correct, rewriting the word once
4. **Space retrieval**: retest the same words after 1 day, 3 days, then a week, dropping words only after consistent correct retrieval ([Spaced Repetition](../elements/spaced-repetition.md))
5. **Embed in writing**: transfer check via dictation sentences or authentic writing conferences, since isolated test accuracy does not guarantee use in composition [~M]

## Examples
- **Look–Say–Cover–Write–Check** routines in UK primary literacy programs (e.g., [Twinkl spelling resources](https://www.twinkl.co.uk)) structure daily self-administered retrieval cycles.
- **[Spelling Shed](https://www.spellingshed.com)** schedules game-based spelling retrieval with adaptive word lists and immediate feedback.
- **[Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net)** flashcard decks with audio prompts and typed answers implement expanding-interval spelling retrieval for second-language orthography.
