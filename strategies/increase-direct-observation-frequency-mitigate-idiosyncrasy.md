# Increase the frequency of direct observation of trainees by different raters to mitigate rater idiosyncrasy and support assessment for learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
As a response to rater idiosyncrasy rooted in distrust of single high-stakes OSCE judgements, the authors recommend spreading judgements across more observations: "increasing the frequency of direct observation of trainees by different raters could mitigate for this idiosyncrasy and provide timely and useful feedback". Multiple observations by different raters dilute any single examiner's tacit constructs and shift assessment towards learning rather than judgement alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple raters available to observe the same trainee on separate occasions
- Feedback channels that deliver timely observations to the trainee
#### Constraints
- Offered as an argument ('it could be argued'), not a tested intervention in this study

### Target Learners
- medical trainees and final-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate clinical competence assessment
- feedback for learning
