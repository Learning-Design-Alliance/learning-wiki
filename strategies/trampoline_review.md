# Trampoline Review

## Description
Trampoline Review is a kinesthetic strategy in which learners rehearse or retrieve academic content while bouncing on a mini-trampoline or performing comparable rhythmic gross-motor movement (marching, jumping jacks, ball tossing). The movement is intended to raise arousal and engagement while the learner performs retrieval of the target material. The strategy is best understood as retrieval practice delivered under elevated physiological arousal, not as a distinct memory mechanism.

## Design Implications

The learning benefit comes almost entirely from the retrieval component, not the bouncing: active recall is among the most robust findings in memory research [Testing strengthens retention more than restudying.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S]. Acute moderate exercise can modestly enhance subsequent memory performance, but the evidence for *simultaneous* movement during encoding or retrieval is weaker and mixed [~W]. Design the activity so that the motor task is automatic and low-demand; any movement requiring its own cognitive resources competes with retrieval for working memory.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A safe, supervised space and equipment (mini-trampoline, open floor, or balls)
- Review content structured as recall prompts — questions, facts, sequences — not passive rereading ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
- Movement that is rhythmic and automatic, leaving working memory free for retrieval
- A follow-up check that verifies recall without the movement ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))

#### Constraints
- Complex or novel motor tasks during review reduce recall by dividing attention between movement and content [-M]
- The "kinesthetic learning styles" rationale is not supported: matching instruction to a preferred style does not improve outcomes, and the learning-styles hypothesis has been repeatedly discredited [X]
- Elevated arousal from vigorous exercise can impair fine-grained encoding if the content is new or demanding [~M]; benefits appear mainly for simple, well-practiced material
- Safety and classroom-management overhead is high; group bouncing is disruptive in typical classrooms [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Whole-class format: teacher asks questions; students bounce while answering chorally
- Station rotation: trampoline as one review station among several, limiting disruption
- Low-equipment substitute: ball toss, marching, or seat-based rhythmic movement preserves the arousal component without safety overhead
- Timing variation: movement *before* review (warm-up) is better supported than movement *during* review [~W]

### Target Learners
- Young learners with high movement needs, for whom the activity functions primarily as engagement and behavior regulation rather than a memory mechanism [~W]
- Learners reviewing well-consolidated material (facts, multiplication tables) where retrieval is automatic enough to survive divided attention
- Not specifically beneficial for learners labeled "kinesthetic learners" — style-matching adds no measurable benefit [X]

### Target Learning Goals
- Retrieval and fluency for declarative content: facts, vocabulary, number facts
- Engagement and on-task behavior during review sessions
- Not suited to conceptual understanding, problem-solving, or transfer goals

### Instructions
1. Select material learners have already encountered; structure it as rapid recall prompts rather than new exposition.
2. Demonstrate the movement and establish safety rules before content begins ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)).
3. Run short bouts (2–5 minutes): learner bounces or moves rhythmically while answering prompts aloud; keep movement automatic.
4. Provide immediate corrective responses to answers ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)).
5. Follow with a seated, movement-free retrieval check to confirm learning transfers out of the movement context.

## Related Strategies
- [Spaced repetition](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) — the retrieval schedule matters far more than the movement; trampoline review should be scheduled across days, not massed
- [Self-explanation](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) — for conceptual content, replace bouncing with prompted explanation, which has stronger support

## Examples
- Elementary classroom: students bounce on a mini-trampoline station while reciting multiplication facts, rotating through in small groups with the teacher checking answers.
- Language class: learners toss a ball while conjugating verbs; the catcher answers, then throws to the next learner.
- Warm-up variant: three minutes of jumping jacks *before* a quiz review, exploiting post-exercise arousal without dividing attention during retrieval.
