Use adaptable collaboration scripts that fade structure in or out over time according to students' needs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because the effect of the PFB request diminished after more practice occasions, the authors propose that "adaptable external collaboration scripts could be a possible solution, in which provided structure could be faded in or out over time, according to students' needs". This addresses the diminishing benefit of fixed structure across feedback cycles.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • multiple practice occasions or feedback cycles so that structure can be faded as students become more proficient

Constraints¶

  • the effect of the additional structure diminishes over time

Target Learners¶

  • first-year higher education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • peer feedback quality
  • writing product improvement

Claims¶

  • The peer feedback request condition showed a significant PFB score increase from time 1 to time 2 (11.88 points), but only at the initial stage of performance [+W]
  • Peer feedback quality and product quality increased over time for all conditions, with no significant differences between conditions at time 2 and time 3 [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Choose reminder versus direct instruction according to whether the missing scriptlet is in the learner's repertoire
  • Implement a structured PFB template and foresee multiple practice occasions when involving students as assessors and assessees
