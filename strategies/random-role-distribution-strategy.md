# Distribute role-play roles randomly rather than letting students choose

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that students allowed to choose showed a gender bias in role selection, with female students more often taking the patient's role, the authors "suggest that the roles be randomly distributed rather than allowing students to choose". This is intended to prevent an association between nervousness and role. If students do choose, the authors recommend encouraging female students in particular to play the doctor's role, and focusing more on theoretical background in feedback sessions, especially with male students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A professor or instructor to assign roles
- Enough role sets for the group size
#### Constraints
- If both roles must be played by every student, class size drops to about 10 students, which the authors state was not possible at their university due to a lack of resources

### Target Learners
- 2nd-year preclinical medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Doctor-patient communication skills
- Managing difficult communication situations

## Claims

- [Role choice showed a gender bias: 62% of male students but only 40% of female students chose the doctor's role](../claims/gender-bias-in-role-choice.md) [+W]
- [Students who played the patient role were more nervous, wanted to play more roles, and rated the experience as less important than doctor-role players](../claims/patient-role-players-more-nervous-lower-ratings.md) [+W]
