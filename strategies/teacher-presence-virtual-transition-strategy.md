# Maintain teacher presence and support mechanisms during virtual or emergency remote learning transitions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's findings indicate that teacher interaction is the most important factor in knowledge acquisition and that it increased for AA and LA students during virtual learning. The authors state that "in this new context their 'presence' and support was fundamental for many students" and that motivation drops "should alert the educational community and especially those responsible for building support mechanisms for the mental health of students." This strategy recommends prioritizing teacher availability, consultation hours, and mental-health support when transitioning to virtual instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers available for virtual consultation and interaction
- Institutional support mechanisms for student mental health
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a first-year pandemic transition and could be strongly influenced by the change between contexts

### Target Learners
- Higher education students, particularly low- and average-achieving groups

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining knowledge acquisition and motivation during virtual instruction
