# Use extra-school Family Group Conferences to raise parental involvement without adding burden to schools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because teacher overload is already a relevant issue, the authors looked for extra-school interventions and chose FGCs as "a light and low cost intervention" that can be implemented without additional efforts for schools and without direct involvement of social services. Schools refer at-risk students anonymously via class coordinators using a specific form, while a local social organization and a university run the conferences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A local social organization and research partner to run the process, with funding and school cooperation for referral
#### Constraints
- Schools referred fewer students than requested (262 of about 450 asked for), and only 84 of 131 assigned to treatment accepted the intervention

### Target Learners
- at-risk lower secondary students with school problems

### Target Learning Goals
- increase parental involvement
- reduce dropout risk

## Claims

- [Family Group Conferences improved at-risk lower-secondary students' perceived parental involvement, relations with teachers, self-efficacy about learning and school satisfaction at one year](../claims/fgc-improves-student-wellbeing-outcomes-rct.md) [+W]
- [FGC showed no impact on hard educational performance outcomes (failure, final marks, truancy) at the end of the school year](../claims/fgc-no-impact-hard-outcomes.md) [-W]
