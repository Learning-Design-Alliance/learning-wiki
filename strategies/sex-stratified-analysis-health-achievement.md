# Analyze health-nutrition and achievement data separately for boys and girls to detect sex-specific risk-factor associations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (3 for, 4 mixed) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
After detecting considerable sex differences in academic achievement and cognitive function, the authors decided to perform separate analyses for boys and girls, which they list as a strength that allowed "new insights into potential factors underlying sex differences in academic achievement and cognitive function." This stratified modeling revealed associations (stunting, dietary diversity, food insecurity with girls' grades) that pooled analysis would have obscured. Researchers studying health and achievement in LMIC school settings can apply this approach.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adequate sample size within each sex stratum to support separate regression models
- Measurement of candidate risk factors alongside achievement and cognitive outcomes
#### Constraints
- The article notes a paucity of studies systematically addressing sex differences among school-aged children in marginalized LMIC areas

### Target Learners
- Primary school children in marginalized communities

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding how health and nutrition risk factors relate differently to academic achievement and cognitive function by sex
