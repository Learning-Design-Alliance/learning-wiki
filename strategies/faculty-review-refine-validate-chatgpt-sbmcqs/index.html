Have educators review, refine, and validate ChatGPT-generated SBMCQs before use in assessment¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's central recommendation is human-in-the-loop quality control for AI-generated assessment items: faculty highlighted "importance of educators in reviewing, refining, and validating ChatGPT-generated SBMCQs to ensure their appropriateness within the educational context". In this study, refinement took the form of fine-tuning ChatGPT output (e.g., simplifying wording) while preserving scenario, structure, and answer options, followed by blinded expert review and item analysis. FGD participants additionally proposed faculty training, clear development guidelines, and pre-implementation checklists.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Faculty expertise in assessment design and the target curriculum to review and refine generated items
  • Structured prompts and validation of generated items against quality criteria before use

Constraints¶

  • The study used ChatGPT 4.0 (March 2023 version) in undergraduate physiology with first-year MBBS students; quality concerns reported include cultural and linguistic bias for regional contexts

Target Learners¶

  • First-year undergraduate medical (MBBS) students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Assessment of physiology competencies via scenario-based multiple-choice questions targeting application and analysis

Claims¶

  • ChatGPT-generated SBMCQs skewed easier than faculty-generated SBMCQs, whose difficulty was predominantly in the appropriate range [+W]
  • ChatGPT-generated SBMCQs contained a higher rate of nonfunctional distractors than faculty-generated SBMCQs (33.75% vs. 13.75%) [+W]
  • Faculty focus groups raised concerns about authenticity, validity, and reliability of ChatGPT-generated SBMCQs, including cultural and linguistic bias [+W]
  • Faculty-generated SBMCQs met all three ideal item-analysis criteria more often than ChatGPT-generated SBMCQs (19 vs. 9 of 40) [+W]
  • Faculty-generated SBMCQs showed superior discrimination indices and distractor effectiveness compared with ChatGPT-generated SBMCQs [+W]
  • AI-generated assessment items frequently need faculty correction, supporting AI as a draft generator within quality assurance [+W]
