Replace massed Halstedian-style training with spaced VR simulator-based psychomotor training for novice doctors¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that, given faster skill acquisition under spaced schedules, surgical trainees can reach proficiency in less total time by practising on VR simulators in a safe virtual environment before operating. It states it "would be reasonable to abandon the traditional Halstedian approach and its shortcomings, and instead implement VR simulator-based training using a spaced training schedule". Spaced curricula can be adjusted to individual schedules and learning curves.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • VR simulator access so novices reach a minimum proficiency level before applying skills in the operating room
  • spacing intervals compatible with clinical practice, e.g., daily or weekly sessions

Constraints¶

  • Some trainees may need more time or more repetitions to reach proficiency
  • busy schedules of junior residents may be a barrier to implementation

Target Learners¶

  • surgical novices
  • novice residents
  • medical students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • surgical psychomotor skill acquisition to proficiency

Affordances¶

  • Reconsolidation Account Of Spacing Psychomotor

Claims¶

  • No difference in bronchoscopy skill test scores between one-day spaced and weekly distributed training in Bjerrum et al. [~W]
  • Daily training on consecutive days appears more effective than weekly or massed schedules for VR-based psychomotor skill acquisition [+W]
  • Short-interval (2.5-minute) spaced practice outperformed massed practice on a laparoscopic transfer task in Mackay et al. [+W]
  • Massed learners' performance declined after about four repetitions while spaced learners' performance rose toward a proficiency plateau [+W]
  • Spaced VR simulator training yields better performance and faster skill acquisition than massed training for surgical psychomotor skills [+W]
  • The optimal spacing interval is bounded by reactive inhibition at short intervals and skill deterioration at long intervals [~W]
  • Spaced trainees completed endoscopic exercises 18.7% faster than massed trainees in Verdaasdonk et al. [+W]
