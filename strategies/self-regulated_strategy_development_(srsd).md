# Self-Regulated Strategy Development (SRSD)

## Description
SRSD is an instructional approach designed to enhance students' writing skills by addressing their affective, behavioral, and cognitive characteristics. It integrates findings from cognitive development, behavior, and affect research. The model supports students in developing writing knowledge, monitoring their writing, and fostering positive attitudes towards writing and themselves as writers. Teachers provide scaffolding to develop skillful strategy use, self-regulation, and understanding of strategy limitations. The core goals are to assist students in developing knowledge about writing, support their ability to monitor and manage their own writing, and promote positive attitudes about writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit instruction, scaffolding, collaborative learning, assessment of student needs, flexible curriculum integration, support network for teachers
#### Constraints
- Requires significant teacher training and effort. May not be uniformly effective for all students without adaptation. Can be time-intensive to implement fully.

### Target Learners

- Students facing difficulties in writing, including those with learning disabilities, across elementary, middle, and high school levels

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Develop writing skills, improve self-regulation, foster positive attitudes towards writing, enhance strategic performance

### Affordances
- Improves writing quality, knowledge, and approach, and enhances self-efficacy. Promotes active collaboration, individualized instruction, and criterion-based progression. Addresses affective, behavioral, and cognitive aspects of writing.

### Personalization
- Instruction is individualized based on students’ characteristics and skills. Strategies and instructional components are selected and modified to fit individual needs, with differentiated self-instructions, goals, and feedback.

## Assessment Evidence
- Improvements in writing quality (length, structure, content), changes in attitudes toward writing, and increased use of self-regulation strategies. Observation of strategy use and student self-assessment.

## Impact
- Studies show significant improvements in the quality, length, and structure of students’ compositions. Students show improved planning, revising, and substantive content. Gains are maintained over time and generalize across settings.

## Examples
- SRSD has been used to teach planning and revising strategies, including brainstorming, self-monitoring, and organizing writing content. Examples include the SPACE strategy (Setting, Purpose, Action, Conclusion, Emotions) for story writing and the TREE strategy (Topic sentence, Reasons, Examine reasons, Ending) for opinion essays.
