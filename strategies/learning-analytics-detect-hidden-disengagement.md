# Use learning analytics from VLE logs rather than student feedback alone to detect low engagement with pre-class work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article highlights "the importance of using learning analytics, where possible, to access data from students on a course when measuring the success of a new teaching method or intervention". Course evaluation feedback was consistently positive even before Level Up!, so relying on student feedback alone would have missed the true extent of the lack of engagement in pre-lecture materials. Moodle activity logs allowed instructors to track quiz completion, timing and performance to inform active learning sessions and future teaching decisions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to VLE activity logs and, for dissemination, ethical approval with student consent to use their data
#### Constraints
- Only engagement with formative reading quizzes was measured; textbook readings rely on a manual completion indicator and could be exposed to non-meaningful engagement

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students on courses with online pre-class activities

### Target Learning Goals
- Monitoring and improving engagement with pre-class learning materials
