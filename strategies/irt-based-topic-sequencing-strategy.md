# Sequence and time astronomy instruction using IRT-derived difficulty orders tailored to audience age

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends determining the most persistent misconceptions with IRT and using CRC intersection coordinates to order topics from easiest to hardest, separately for adults and for children/adolescents. By determining the most persistent misconceptions, instructors can suggest “topics requiring care in presentation and, in all likelihood, more time in the classroom.” The authors propose teaching directly to the most challenging misconceptions and allocating classroom time logically.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administration of a misconceptions inventory collecting the age at which each misconception was dispelled
- PCA to establish unidimensional item groups before IRT analysis
#### Constraints
- The predicted teaching orders are not yet experimentally validated; the authors state they are presently testing the effectiveness of this order compared to textbook order

### Target Learners
- college introductory astronomy students
- K-12 children and adolescents

### Target Learning Goals
- replacing astronomy misconceptions with scientifically correct concepts
