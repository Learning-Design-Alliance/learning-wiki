# Stratify embodied Tai Chi tasks by proficiency: simplified movements for beginners, autonomy in creation for advanced learners, paired practice for similar-ability peers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends differentiated instructional support matched to learner proficiency in embodied bilingual courses. For lower-level students, "simplified movement teaching is adopted"; for higher-level students, "autonomy in movement creation is granted"; and for students of similar ability, "two-person group exercises are designed to enhance emotional needs." An example is beginners first drawing one-handed "雲手" circles before attempting full movement sequences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers able to assess proficiency levels and adjust task complexity and support type accordingly
#### Constraints
- Derived from teacher reports and cross-sectional subgroup findings, not from an intervention test

### Target Learners
- University students in bilingual Tai Chi elective courses at varying proficiency levels

### Target Learning Goals
- Competence development
- Autonomy
- Relatedness and emotional connection
