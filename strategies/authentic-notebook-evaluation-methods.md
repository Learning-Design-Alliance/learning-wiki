# Evaluate notebooks holistically on interpretability rather than format, using peer feedback, oral quizzes, or a reproduction practicum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article reports that researchers' responses on evaluation were quite universal: none suggested grading 'the format, neatness, organization, or degree of detail of the entries.' Instead, entries should be interpretable as a whole, so a student could open the notebook to an arbitrary page and explain what occurred and why. Suggested methods are peer-to-peer analysis of notebooks with feedback on understandability, instructor oral examinations about specific entries across timescales, and a lab practical reproducing an earlier experiment using only the notebook.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Evaluation should focus on whether entries are interpretable and make sense in the broader context of the notebook, aligning grading with authentic documentation practice.
#### Constraints
- The article notes these approaches might be time consuming for instructors.

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics laboratory students

### Target Learning Goals
- producing interpretable notebook entries
- reflecting on how entries are recorded
