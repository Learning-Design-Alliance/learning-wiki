Develop and test interventions and teacher reflective practices to harness emotions as malleable factors in K-12 settings¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Drawing on the Owens and Hudson perspective, the editorial reports that teachers and administrators working in high demand and low control settings experience emotional exhaustion, increased teacher turnover, and decreased teaching performance. It suggests "developing and testing interventions and teacher reflective practices to harness necessary emotions," treating emotions as a malleable factor in K-12 settings shaped by the shift to digital learning.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Recognition that the burden of shifting to digital learning has been borne primarily by K-12 teachers and administrators

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 teachers and administrators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Emotional regulation and reduced emotional exhaustion in digital learning contexts

Related Strategies¶

  • Measure and respond to student emotions continuously in digital platforms and promote mindfulness and emotional awareness
  • Foster positive emotions, support self-regulatory skills, and provide emotional supports for students shifting to digital learning
  • Secondary-school personality change intervention: teach traits, self-assess, nominate traits, and complete weekly trait-relevant challenges
  • Take A Break Station
