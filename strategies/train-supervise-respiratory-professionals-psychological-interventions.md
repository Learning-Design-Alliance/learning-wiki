# Train and supervise respiratory healthcare professionals to deliver psychological interventions, with competency assessment before practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that respiratory healthcare professionals can take on delivering psychologically based interventions if three supports are in place: a robust training programme with competency assessment before practice, ongoing supervision, and a comprehensive intervention manual with prompt resources. The authors state "Respiratory healthcare professionals can be trained to deliver interventions underpinned by psychological approaches" and that "supervision is essential to provide support for clinicians and to ensure quality of clinical care." Selection should also screen for commitment to a biopsychosocial approach, since some individuals were unsuitable even after repeat training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Competency assessment (e.g. video-based role play with a simulated patient) before approval to deliver
- Fortnightly or equivalent ongoing supervision during delivery
- A comprehensive intervention manual and session resources such as prompt cards
#### Constraints
- Resource constraints may hinder incorporation of psychological support if it is perceived to take additional time; the approach is not suitable for all clinicians

### Target Learners
- Respiratory nurses, physiotherapists and occupational therapists in primary and community care

### Target Learning Goals
- Delivering cognitive behavioural interventions for anxiety and depression in long-term conditions
