# Provide regular simulation training for all providers, with additional individualised training for those lacking competency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that training of all providers be regular, noting that "annual training is sufficient for those who are already proficient, but additional and individualised training should be arranged for those lacking sufficient competency". Their clinic sustains weekly sessions plus monthly walk-in skills stations, with the proportion of at-least-once-trained providers varying between 70% and 80% despite staff turnover, which they believe sufficed to improve shared knowledge of SD management and teamwork.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A dedicated team of educators and institutional investment so staff can be regularly released from clinical duties
- Certified simulation trainers and a simulation centre
#### Constraints
- Annual training of all providers is difficult in a sizeable clinic with continuous staff turnover; the authors caution against assuming the impact of simulation training on all obstetric emergencies is comparable

### Target Learners
- Midwives
- Doctors working in obstetrics

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining competency in shoulder dystocia and obstetric emergency management
- Team performance under emergency conditions
