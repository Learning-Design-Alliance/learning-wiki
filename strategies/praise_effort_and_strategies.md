# Praise Effort and Strategies

## Description
When learners succeed, praise their efforts and strategies rather than their intelligence. Praising intelligence can undermine motivation and performance, as learners may view intelligence as a fixed trait. In the face of failure, they may display less task persistence, less task enjoyment, and overall worse performance. Focus on the process and the strategies used to achieve success to foster a growth mindset.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires instructors to observe learners' processes and strategies carefully. Instructors need to provide specific and meaningful feedback that focuses on effort and strategies.
#### Constraints
- Generic praise can be ineffective and may not promote a growth mindset. Praise must be specific and focused on the process to be effective.

### Target Learners

- All learner levels, especially those who are sensitive to failure or tend to attribute success to innate ability rather than effort

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote a growth mindset, increase task persistence, improve task enjoyment, and enhance overall performance

### Affordances
- Encourages learners to focus on the process of learning and the strategies they use, rather than on their perceived intelligence. Helps learners see that effort and effective strategies lead to success.

### Personalization
- Feedback can be tailored to individual learner strengths and areas for improvement. Instructors can provide specific examples of effective strategies that learners used.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learner persistence in the face of challenges, monitor their task enjoyment, and track their overall performance. Compare the effectiveness of praising effort and strategies versus praising intelligence.

## Impact
- Research shows that praising effort and strategies promotes a growth mindset and increases task persistence and enjoyment, leading to improved performance. Praise for intelligence can actually undermine motivation and performance.
