# Narrow the target age band or split the program when implementing R@W as a standard educational tool

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on participant and parent feedback, the authors recommend adapting the program's age targeting: "If implementing R@W as a standard educational tool for all patients with JIA, we would suggest either selecting patients aged 9 to 11 years, or developing two different programs – one for 8 to 11-year-olds and another for 11 to 13-year-olds." This responds to feedback that assignments were too childish for older children and too difficult for younger ones. Most suggestions for improvement related to the age categories.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback collection via anonymous end-of-program questionnaires for participants and parents to identify age-appropriateness problems
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a program designed for children aged 8 to 13 with low disease activity in the Dutch context

### Target Learners
- children aged 8 to 13 with juvenile idiopathic arthritis

### Target Learning Goals
- age-appropriate delivery of disease education and self-management skills
