# Use trained raters, a crossover design, and comparison of learning modalities when implementing checklist-based IO assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
For future implementation of checklist-based assessment of IO access, the authors recommend raters with prior education in the checklist, a crossover design, and testing different learning modalities against each other. They further suggest that raters jointly observing and discussing cases of good and poor performance and agreeing on a score may ensure more uniform rating, and that training raters beforehand may decrease the likelihood of guessing and further increase inter-rater reliability for both non-expert and expert raters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rater education in the checklist before assessment
#### Constraints
- The pilot used three untrained raters of differing expertise, and the expert rater rated lower than the non-expert raters

### Target Learners
- Medical students undergoing OSCE assessment of procedural skills

### Target Learning Goals
- Reliable assessment of IO access competence
