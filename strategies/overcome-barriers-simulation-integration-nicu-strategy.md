# Address cost, time and organizational barriers to integrate simulation into NICU training, risk management and quality improvement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends addressing barriers to fully integrate simulation into the wider clinical setting, listing common barriers as "cost, time, organizational challenges, unwillingness of staff, and absence of support from policy makers". It proposes attenuating material cost through innovative solutions adapting available material, recycling clinical equipment for repeated use, sharing or renting simulation equipment, and addressing staffing and time limitations at an institutional level. Research on cost-effectiveness is deemed essential for buy-in from decision makers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional-level attention to organizational challenges such as poor staffing and limited availability of time
#### Constraints
- There are still no clear studies showing cost effectiveness of simulation

### Target Learners
- NICU clinicians, multidisciplinary teams, and health care institutions

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining procedural competency, team performance, patient safety, and quality improvement in neonatal care
