# Write 'Reading the content in…' readings that unpack the conceptual knowledge coded in anchor equations, and require the full annotated form of Newton's second law

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (3 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author writes readings that 'specified a basic equation and codified the conceptual physics that was coded in the equation', e.g., for Newton's second law displayed with diacritical markers (vectors, subscripts) showing what each detail specifies. He does not accept 'F = ma' as a correct form of N2 and encourages writing 'a = Fnet/m' so students recall that the sum of forces causes acceleration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Readings must be paired with problems that require calling on the unpacked conceptual content
#### Constraints
- The author notes this stress on the general validity and full form of Newton 2 is important 'at least for a while'

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- recalling relevant conceptual knowledge when solving problems
- distinguishing core principles from toy models such as F = kx
