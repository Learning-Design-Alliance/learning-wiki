# Pair individual implementation support with a departmental commitment to transformed teaching, including incentives, TA training, and shared RBIS expectations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Beyond individual specialist support, the article identifies a departmental environment as the second major factor in continued RBIS use: department heads explicitly supporting transformation, incentives such as course buyouts and extra TAs, regular exchange of ideas and challenges, RBIS-focused TA training, and clear expectations in multi-instructor courses. The authors caution that this "support" often becomes "pressure", yet pressured faculty still continued when SES support was present.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Department heads must explicitly support the transformation effort, which is a requirement for receiving CWSEI funding.
#### Constraints
- Departmental expectations can become pressure, and pressured faculty initially "have bought into the concept less" than spontaneous adopters.

### Target Learners
- University science faculty in departments undergoing course transformation

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained adoption of research-based instructional strategies across departmental courses
