# Anchor TA professional development in simulated grading activities that provoke cognitive conflict between stated goals and practices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A professional development strategy in which TAs grade authentic student solutions in simulated quiz and homework contexts, articulate their scoring reasons, and discuss them with peers so that inconsistencies between stated formative goals and correctness-focused practice surface. The article expected such encounters to make TAs 'aware of existing inconsistencies, thus causing a cognitive conflict which may motivate changes in their grading decisions'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator who coordinates class work and leads a discussion highlighting grading best practices, including the disadvantages of grading focused exclusively on correctness.
#### Constraints
- In this study the activities did not produce significant changes in TAs' grading perceptions over one semester of teaching.

### Target Learners
- first-year postgraduate physics teaching assistants

### Target Learning Goals
- grading practices that encourage effective problem solving approaches
