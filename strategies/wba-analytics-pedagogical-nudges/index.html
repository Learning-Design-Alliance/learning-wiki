Pedagogical nudges triggered by WBA analytics to encourage engagement¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors propose using the analysis findings to trigger targeted nudges (e.g. targeted email messages, notifications) in workplace-based assessment. Examples include messaging students whose "assessment data suggests that student has feedback from few selected healthcare professionals", suggesting skills often completed together, reminding students about peer assessment, and prompting students with few completed assessments to attempt a common skill. Clinical assessment colleagues had already explored nudges to encourage at-risk students to engage in WBA.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Ongoing analysis of student WBA data to identify relevant situations in which a nudge can be triggered

Constraints¶

  • Grounded in an exploratory case study of Year 1 cohorts; the paper reports these as possible future interventions, not tested effects

Target Learners¶

  • Year 1 undergraduate medical students, including at-risk students with low engagement

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Increasing engagement with workplace-based assessment
  • Broadening interaction with healthcare professionals and clinical skills

Claims¶

  • Assessor role diversity is greater in secondary care placements and the most common assessor type shifted from nurse (2016/17) to clinical educator (2017/18 and 2018/19) [+W]
  • Consultation skills were the most frequently chosen WBA clinical skills across all three Year 1 cohorts, followed by diagnostic procedures [+W]
  • The diversity of clinical skill categories chosen by Year 1 students increased between 2016/17 and 2018/19 [+W]
  • Process mining of WBA logs shows students typically start with Handwashing, then Pulse and History, with frequent looping back to basic clinical skills [+W]
  • Student reflections share common frequent words across placements but differ between primary and secondary care and across year groups [+W]
  • Year 1 medical students complete more WBA assessments in secondary care placements, especially when secondary care is the first placement block [+W]
  • Predictive learning analytics can generate early warnings of dropout risk and trigger targeted interventions in adult education [+W]
