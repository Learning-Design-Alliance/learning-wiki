# Automated video review of a standardised operative step to track trainees' learning curve

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose using AI-driven operative phase detection to automate measurement of the VPDT so that skill assessment does not depend on time-consuming manual video review. "In practice, trainees would be able to record their operations and submit this for automated review with the output used to track their progress along the learning curve." The strategy supports competency-based surgical training by giving trainees and programmes a repeatable, standardised-step skill indicator, and the authors note it could also serve as a key performance indicator or textbook outcome parameter.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Accurate AI-driven operative phase detection models
- Routine recording of laparoscopic operations
#### Constraints
- Retrospective analysis was used in this study to avoid real-time AI-driven clinical decision-making; outcome linkage not yet demonstrated in a powered cohort

### Target Learners
- General surgery trainees in competency-based training programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- Monitoring operative technical skill development over the learning curve
