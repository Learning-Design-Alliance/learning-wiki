# Test generalizability of curiosity and interest constructs across age groups and socio-cultural contexts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends contextualizing curiosity and interest research in age-related and socio-cultural contexts, since existing concepts derive mainly from Western student samples. It invokes principles of relative universality: objects, contents, frequencies, and process parameters may vary widely, but component structures and relations with origins and outcomes should be equivalent across contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Definitions based on components can be used across populations only to the extent that components are invariant
#### Constraints
- If components differ across populations, it would be necessary to use different concepts to gauge curiosity and interest in different populations

### Target Learners
- students across age groups, cultures, and academic domains

### Target Learning Goals
- valid cross-cultural and developmental measurement of curiosity and interest
