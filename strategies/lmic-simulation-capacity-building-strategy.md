# Build LMIC web-based simulation capacity through global collaboration, train-the-trainer programs, and sustainable funding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends linking distant medical educators from first-world countries with healthcare professionals in LMICs as a first step toward an educational pipeline for a web-based simulation network, with "a gradual deployment of qualified simulation educators along with a subsequent online 'train the trainer' program" to establish a feasible and cost-effective program. It identifies four significant factors: learner requirements, educator vision, global collaboration, and sustainable funding. Healthcare institutions are urged to formally include simulation in their budgets and collaborate with regional centers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formal inclusion of simulation in institutional budgets for long-term financial support
- A unified national organization including relevant policy-making bodies promoting cross-sectoral coordination
#### Constraints
- Promoting community acceptance is challenging in regions where traditional teaching methods are deeply rooted

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals, trainees, and simulation educators in low- and middle-income countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Establishing sustainable, cost-effective simulation-based medical education programs in resource-constrained settings
