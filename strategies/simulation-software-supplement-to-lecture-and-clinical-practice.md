# Use educational simulation software as a supplement to lectures and hospital-based clinical practice in nursing education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that nursing educational software should be layered on top of conventional teaching rather than replacing it: if educational simulation software is used "in addition to the traditional methods of lecturing for theoretical topics and attendance at the hospital for clinical (practical) topics, the level of students' knowledge will be improved". The authors also recommend that institutions make such software widely available across devices and support students lacking technology or internet connectivity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Contingent on validation, data privacy considerations, and alignment with national/international educational standards
- Institutional support for students who may lack the necessary technology or internet connectivity
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single-center study; the authors recommend further investigations across multiple universities or healthcare settings

### Target Learners
- Nursing students and interns in clinical training

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical skill acquisition and bridging the gap between theoretical knowledge and practical performance
