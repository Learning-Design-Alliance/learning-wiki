# Design instructional environments that trigger favorable epistemological stances rather than confronting and replacing mis-epistemologies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's instructional implication is that instructors need not confront and replace apparent mis-epistemologies; instead they can shape contexts so that more productive stances are activated. As the authors put it, "instructors can create instructional environments that tend to trigger more favorable epistemological stances." This follows from the finding that Jim's stance was flexible and cue-responsive rather than a fixed deficit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must attend to contextual cues that activate different epistemological stances in the moment
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a single case study of one student in a clinical interview; the article does not test the instructional recommendation empirically

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering and physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- mathematical sense-making in physics
- reconciling formalism with everyday experience
