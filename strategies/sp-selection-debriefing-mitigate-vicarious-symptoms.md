# Select standardized patients considering health anxiety and personal history of the simulated condition, and use structured debriefing after simulations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends mitigating vicarious symptoms through SP selection and post-simulation support. It states such symptoms "could, at least partially, be mitigated if SPs were selected without a history of personal exposure to the condition or scenario being simulated", and that "implementing structured debriefing sessions after the simulations has been proposed as a potential strategy to alleviate these symptoms". It also suggests integrating IAS into the SP selection process to improve profile precision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to SP socio-demographic and health-anxiety information (e.g., IAS scores) before OSCE assignment
#### Constraints
- No intervention to address VSs was implemented in this study due to ethical constraints, so mitigation strategies were not directly evaluated

### Target Learners
- standardized patients in OSCEs
- medical educators and OSCE organizers

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing vicarious symptoms and psychological impact on standardized patients
- maintaining consistent SP performance and equity across students
