# Use seminar quizzes as low-stakes formative tools for readiness, feedback and identifying struggling students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy opens each flipped classroom seminar with a short individual quiz to activate acquired knowledge, increase motivation and give students feedback on how well they have learned the objectives of the associated case or study instructions. Quiz results go into each student's portfolio so staff can see who is struggling and provide additional support, and quiz results can indicate student readiness for class activities. The article reports that "a motivating effect was observed, even though the quiz results were not included in students’ overall course grades." Early on, some students perceived quizzes as grades and tried to cheat, so clearer explanations of the quizzes' purpose were given.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Quizzes are not included in course grades
- Results are placed in student portfolios for monitoring
#### Constraints
- Some students initially perceived the quizzes as grades and explored ways of cheating, requiring clearer explanations

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-testing knowledge against case learning objectives
- continuous knowledge acquisition across the semester
