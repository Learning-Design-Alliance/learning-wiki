# Instructors can reduce blank-related grade inequity by minimizing opportunities for blank leaving or using grading practices that limit the impact of blanks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that instructors "should either consider approaches that minimize possibilities for blank leaving (allowing re-takes on exams, giving take-home exams, or creating shorter exams) or use grading practices that minimize the impacts of blanks (like standards-based grading, or the 4.0 scale)." Mastery-type grading methods such as standards-based or specifications grading do not average attempts on the same content, so a blank can be replaced with another score; minimum grading raises low grades to 50%, shrinking the impact of uncollected items.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires instructors to change the assessment or grading scheme itself, rather than trying to change student behavior
#### Constraints
- The article notes that practices addressing students directly, such as growth-mindset or stereotype-threat approaches, necessarily use a student deficit model because they change students and not the assessment

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory physics courses, particularly historically marginalized and first-generation students

### Target Learning Goals
- fair collection of assessment data and reduction of grade bias from uncollected exam items
