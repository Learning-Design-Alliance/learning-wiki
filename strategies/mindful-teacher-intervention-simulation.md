# Mindful teacher intervention juxtaposed with simulation possibilities as a catalyst for learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers employing simulations intervene mindfully, since learning possibilities in a simulation session can easily be glossed over. Things taken for granted need to be brought into focal awareness, which may happen when students spontaneously bring two aspects of the simulation together, are shown something by the simulation, or are questioned by an intervener such as a fellow student or teacher. The authors conclude such intervention is a powerful tool for learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The simulation must have relevant features, learning tasks must be manageable and seem relevant to students, and interventions must be mindful.
#### Constraints
- Learners working with learning-focus D could probably meaningfully work on their own, setting their own learning agenda, without such intervention.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students using simulations as lecture supplements

### Target Learning Goals
- bringing central features of a physics phenomenon into focal awareness
