# Weight situational judgement test components and interviews more heavily, or adopt individualised contextual review, to widen access where cognitive tools narrow it

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (3 for, 4 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that to alter the demography of those offered places, schools could weight the UKCAT's SJT component, which measures personal attributes, more heavily than the cognitive ability components, use interviews differently, or shift towards an individualised approach considering applicant background and life experiences. It cites recent research suggesting "the SJT may not favour those from more privileged educational backgrounds". The authors frame this as requiring schools to decide what they want selection to achieve.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools must decide their selection priorities, since the weighting and sequencing of methods depend on the aim
#### Constraints
- The article states medical schools may not be ready to weight the SJT more heavily, and that moving personal-values assessment to the forefront would not be embraced by many schools

### Target Learners
- medical school applicants from under-represented groups

### Target Learning Goals
- widening access to medical education
- increasing diversity of the medical profession
