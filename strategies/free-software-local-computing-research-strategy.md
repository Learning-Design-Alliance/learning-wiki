# Use free, locally runnable quantum chemistry software to open research opportunities where computational resources are scarce

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend basing education and research training on freely downloadable software that runs on any Linux system, so that students and researchers in resource-limited settings can do meaningful work locally. They argue this "does not replace the need for local computational resources for education and for research which should not always require large amounts of computational resources." The strategy pairs tool access with mentorship identifying important fundamental problems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Freely downloadable software executable on local machines
- Mentors with enough knowledge to identify important fundamental problems
#### Constraints
- Governments in developing countries frequently have little or no funds for scientific research; local resources may still be limited relative to high-performance computing centers

### Target Learners
- graduate students and researchers in developing countries
- students with limited access to computing resources

### Target Learning Goals
- research-level computational chemistry skills
- independent scientific contribution with limited infrastructure
