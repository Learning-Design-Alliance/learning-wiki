# Use 3D-printed patient-specific models as a complementary tool within CBL sessions on complex orthopaedic conditions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "This interactive learning method can be used as a complementary tool to enhance traditional classroom instruction, especially in understanding complex medical and surgical concepts". In implementation, each 3D+CBL subgroup of 10 students received the 3D-printed model of the case patient during scenario discussion and case analysis, alongside the same CBL cases and lectures given to both groups. The authors also argue 3D models compensate for scarce anatomical specimens and avoid ethical issues of real specimens.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cases with available patient imaging for model reconstruction
- Group-based CBL session structure with lecturer debriefing
#### Constraints
- The article notes the approach was studied only in DDH teaching at a single institution and recommends extending it to other orthopaedic areas such as knee and spinal disorders

### Target Learners
- fourth-year undergraduate clinical medical students in orthopaedic clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding complex anatomical and surgical concepts
- clinical reasoning and treatment planning for DDH
