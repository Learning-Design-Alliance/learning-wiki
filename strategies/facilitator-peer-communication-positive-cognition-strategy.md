# Facilitators support peers sharing a patient by structuring communication to establish positive cognition and promote performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that facilitators actively manage communication among peers who simultaneously share one simulated patient. Because peer simulation raised objective stress during patient assessment, the authors argue that positive cognitive preparation and encouragement from team members can focus students on issues rather than threats. As printed: "Facilitators may be able to facilitate communication between peers who share the same patient at the same time, establish positive cognition, promote performance and operate peer simulation more effectively."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator who can structure communication between peers sharing the same patient during the simulation
#### Constraints
- The article notes the present study did not reveal any differences in performance between the individual and peer simulations, so the performance benefit is proposed, not demonstrated

### Target Learners
- Third-year undergraduate nursing students in simulation practice

### Target Learning Goals
- Postoperative patient assessment and vital sign measurement skills
