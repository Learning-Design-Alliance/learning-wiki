# Combine CBL and PBL approaches in clinical teaching settings to target both theory and practical outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that in clinical teaching settings educators employ case-based learning combined with problem-based learning, carrying out targeted teaching on both theoretical and experimental outcomes. They base this on RCT evidence that the combination "shows an obvious learning improvement effect", helping students apply knowledge and enrich experience through solving problems, alongside the NMA finding that CBL leads for clinical-curriculum theory and PBL for practical skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators working in clinical teaching settings with clinical-curriculum content
#### Constraints
- The NMA itself excluded studies combining two or more methods in the same course, so the recommendation rests on other RCT evidence rather than this NMA's pooled estimates

### Target Learners
- medical students in clinical curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- theoretical knowledge application
- clinical practical skills
- problem-solving
