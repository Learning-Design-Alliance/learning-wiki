# Design rubric criteria and model instructions in observable, verifiable terms with citable evidence requirements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends formulating assessment criteria in observable and verifiable terms, avoiding formulations assessable only through an overall reading. Instructors should explicitly require the model to support each score with a concrete reference to the report text (citable evidence), include a structured checklist per item replicating the teaching team's checks, and ask the model to state explicitly when it cannot verify an aspect for lack of legible evidence rather than completing the response with a plausible but unsupported interpretation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rubric criteria written in observable, verifiable terms; instructions requiring citable evidence for each score.
#### Constraints
- These conditions improve clarity and evidential basis but the article ties them to making instructor verification easier, not to guaranteeing correct assessment.

### Target Learners
- higher education instructors designing AI-supported assessment

### Target Learning Goals
- evidence-based feedback on laboratory reports
