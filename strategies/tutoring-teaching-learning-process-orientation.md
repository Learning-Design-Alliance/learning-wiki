# Tutoring the teaching-learning process to orient and act multifactorially on academic performance.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Following Garbanzo (2007), the authors held tutoring sessions with participating students and list pedagogical results: observing teaching-learning more closely, orienting about values and competencies appreciated in the classroom, and acting with students with slower academic performance by assigning them representative activities valued by the group and professors. This orients action multifactorially rather than only with global exam results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assigning representative activities that are valued by the greater group and by the professors
#### Constraints
- The study aimed at methodologies that promote transference of knowledge; further study of strategy combinations is needed

### Target Learners
- medical students with slower academic performance

### Target Learning Goals
- improving academic performance
- integration in the process of transferring knowledge
