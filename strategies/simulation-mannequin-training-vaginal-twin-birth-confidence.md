# Use simulation-based training with obstetric mannequins to build provider confidence and skill in vaginal twin birth

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a concrete training recipe: quarterly sessions combining a lecture with hands-on mannequin practice for the remainder of the day, covering twin delivery, breech delivery, shoulder dystocia, operative vaginal delivery, and other obstetric emergencies. Providers reported that this training improved their confidence, knowledge, and skill. The authors also note emerging innovations to increase simulation fidelity, such as "using water‐filled balloons with a fetal doll inside to mimic the feel of performing breech extractions with an intact amniotic sac."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must be repeated quarterly rather than delivered once
- Access to obstetric mannequins for hands-on practice
#### Constraints
- The authors note that reskilling is not an immediate process and encourage further innovation to help simulation training approach the realism of attending live births

### Target Learners
- obstetricians
- midwives
- resident physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- vaginal twin birth skills
- breech extraction of the second twin
- confidence in managing vaginal twin labor
