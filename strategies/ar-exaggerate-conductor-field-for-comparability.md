# Artificially exaggerate the conductor's magnetic field in the AR visualization so learners can compare it with the external field at comparable strengths.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because the conductor's field is far weaker than the external field except very close to the wire, the AR environment includes an option to artificially exaggerate the conductor's field, effectively turning up the current, so both fields are comparable in strength near the conductor. The authors state this lets learners "interact more extensively with the visualizations provided by the AR learning environment, thereby increasing their opportunity to learn about the Lorentz force and improve their representational competence".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An AR environment that visualizes both the external magnetic field and the conductor's magnetic field, with a control for scaling the conductor's displayed field strength.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the two field strengths are only comparable for very large currents or very small distances from the conductor, motivating the artificial exaggeration.

### Target Learners
- High school students
- University physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding the Lorentz force
- Representational competence with vector fields
