# Teach Maxwell's equations as simultaneous relationships with emphasis on field sources, using examples, coupled-equation checks and unified-field tutorials

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends teaching-learning sequences in which students learn that Maxwell's equations "do not give information about the sources of the fields but rather describe relationships between different quantities and determine their temporal evolution." Emphasis should be placed on fields verifying all four equations simultaneously, on problems analyzing hypothetical field configurations, and on field-line analysis for different charge and current distributions. Tutorials should develop the view that electric and magnetic fields are parts of one electromagnetic field.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adequate presentation of Maxwell's equations and choice of examples showing associations rather than cause-effect relationships
#### Constraints
- Designed for introductory university physics courses, in both static and time-varying field situations

### Target Learners
- introductory university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- avoiding causal linear reasoning about field sources
- understanding the unified electromagnetic field
- interpreting Maxwell's equations as a coupled system
