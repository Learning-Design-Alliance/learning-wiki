# Institutions should pilot low-stakes, prespecify evaluation indicators, and scale only after governance, faculty capacity, and equity safeguards are in place

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a pragmatic institutional model: "begin with low-stakes pilots in one or two activities, prespecify local evaluation indicators, review unintended consequences; and scale only after governance, faculty capacity, and equity safeguards are in place". Local pilots should evaluate educational outcomes such as learner participation, quality of action plans, change between repeated attempts, learner trust, faculty time burden, and equity of access rather than technology adoption metrics alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions should specify which AI tools are approved, what data may be entered, who can access learner outputs, and how long data are retained
#### Constraints
- Feasibility depends on faculty workload, local technical support, availability of approved tools, and willingness to adopt new workflows

### Target Learners
- Institutional leaders and assessment leaders in health professions education

### Target Learning Goals
- Responsible scaling of AI-assisted formative assessment across a curriculum
