# Implement structured clinical debriefing programs supported by faculty development and interdisciplinary prompting, with a less active team member as facilitator

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing clinical debriefing with a structured approach supported by faculty development and interdisciplinary engagement, noting that "more than half of CDs were prompted by other members of the interdisciplinary team" even though most were led by medical staff. Citing the literature, it suggests "a less active member within the team or external provider facilitate discussions" to mitigate the potential bias of busy team leaders, and states implementation is a key consideration to ensure success. The 'points of action' section ends each debriefing and includes the offer of further support if required.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured facilitation approach with a cognitive aid
- Faculty development and interdisciplinary engagement
- Opt-out available for team members and an offer of further support at the close
#### Constraints
- Unintended negative consequences and staff dissatisfaction are a risk if CD programs are implemented poorly; the long-term consequences of clinical debriefing remain uncertain

### Target Learners
- Frontline clinical educators and simulation educators facilitating debriefings in emergency departments

### Target Learning Goals
- Team reflexivity and shared learning after clinical events
- Reinforcement of good habits and translation of best practices to clinical settings
