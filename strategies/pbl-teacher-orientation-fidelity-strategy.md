# Train and orient teachers on PBL and have a PBL-experienced instructor teach the experimental lessons to ensure fidelity of treatment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because PBL was novel to the participating teachers, the researchers gave the four experimental-school teachers comprehensive orientation on the principle behind PBL and, as printed, "one of the researchers (first author) taught students in the experimental group in order to ensure fidelity of treatment." The article reasons that few teachers understand the difference between a traditional and a problem-based approach, and that many who do are unsure how to implement it. The control-school teacher was deliberately not trained and was not told the study's details, to prevent bias.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need knowledge of problem-based learning before it is introduced into classrooms; orientation sessions covering weekly activities and content areas; unscheduled observation visits to monitor the control condition
#### Constraints
- The control teacher received no PBL training and the study's purpose was withheld from him and school management, raising questions the article does not address about informed consent at the teacher level

### Target Learners
- Senior secondary Further Mathematics students; participating graduate mathematics teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Faithful implementation of problem-based learning in Further Mathematics instruction
