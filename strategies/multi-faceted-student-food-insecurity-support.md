# Extend institutional food insecurity support beyond food provision to multi-faceted programmes including financial management, budgeting, and nutrition education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that higher education institutions, which it calls "in a strategic position to help address and mitigate the impact of food insecurity on students", go beyond the most commonly cited interventions (food pantries and voucher schemes). Citing Hickey et al., it states that "multi-faceted approaches are more likely to succeed", so support programmes should add financial management, budgeting, and nutrition education components, alongside voluntary disclosure systems for timely intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional systems allowing students to voluntarily disclose their need for food insecurity support
#### Constraints
- The review presents this as a recommendation informed by prior intervention literature, not as an intervention tested within this review

### Target Learners
- higher education students experiencing food insecurity

### Target Learning Goals
- mitigating the impact of food insecurity on academic performance and educational success
