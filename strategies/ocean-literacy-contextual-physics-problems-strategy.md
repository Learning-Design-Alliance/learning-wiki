# Design physics problems set in local ocean-literacy contexts so students connect physics to maritime life

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes writing physics problems whose contexts come from the maritime life of West Sulawesi — ships, currents, anchors, fishing and sea tolls — so that physics is met through "examples that are easily found in real life". It supplies five fully worked problems spanning vector analysis, linear motion, projectile motion, Newtonian dynamics and static fluid, intended for direct classroom use within the 2013 curriculum's scientific approach. The author argues this contextual grounding can give students meaningful experience and support Indonesia's maritime-axis vision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must select maritime problems that fit the physics material, since "Not all physics material can be integrated maritime insight".
#### Constraints
- The article notes contextual models have a weakness: they are "naturally limited in its scope and can only be applied successfully to the fields being studied by students", as illustrated by the Physics for the Health Sciences textbook being too difficult for chemistry students.

### Target Learners
- high school physics students in coastal regions such as West Sulawesi

### Target Learning Goals
- vector analysis
- linear motion
- projectile motion
- Newtonian dynamics
- static fluid concepts connected to maritime applications
