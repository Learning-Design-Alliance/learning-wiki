# Challenge students in small-group work to formulate a written open question on disease mechanisms to expose conceptual misunderstanding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
At the start of a small-group work session the tutor invites each student to think about and write one additional open question on the topic, aiming at "a question on disease mechanisms (conceptual understanding) and not mere factual knowledge". Students need not answer it; the written question is handed in and later assessed by experts to uncover misconceptions. The article frames this as a safe, small-scaled setting in which students actively test their mental models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A guided tutor instruction (as printed in the article’s Appendix) and a small-group setting of 12–15 students with a safe learning environment
#### Constraints
- Participation in the SGW session was voluntary, which the article states may have introduced selection bias toward more motivated students

### Target Learners
- second-year bachelor medical and biomedical science students

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of disease mechanisms
- identification of misconceptions
