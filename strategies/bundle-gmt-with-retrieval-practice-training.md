# Bundle GMT with explicit training on desirable difficulties, metacognitive miscalibration, and effective study strategies such as retrieval practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that rather than abandoning GMT, "research should investigate whether GMT might be more impactful if accompanied by training in effective learning strategies, such as retrieval practice and spacing, along with training in creating a resilience to the negative feelings caused by more desirably difficult strategies." GMT would act as a stepping-stone toward tolerating the discomfort inherent in effective strategies, potentially boosting usage and impact of both GMT and retrieval practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit instruction about desirable difficulties, metacognitive miscalibration, and effective study strategies accompanies the GMT regimen
- Training that builds resilience to the negative feelings caused by desirably difficult strategies
#### Constraints
- The authors note it "may not be an easy or straightforward task to scale up to classrooms and schools to bundle them; it may be a heavy lift logistically"
- The proposal is for future research to test, not an already-validated intervention

### Target Learners
- students across US educational institutions where GMT is already adopted
- at-risk or disadvantaged students whom the review suggests may selectively benefit

### Target Learning Goals
- willingness to adopt and persist with retrieval practice
- tolerance of the discomfort inherent in effective learning strategies
