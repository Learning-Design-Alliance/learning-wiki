# Embed educational technologies in structured curricula grounded in established learning theories

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that emerging technologies such as Physlets, Andes, and MBL tools be implemented within structured curricula founded on well-established principles of learning, including constructivism, cognitive conflict in conceptual change, and social learning. It argues that Physlets "may have their greatest impact on students’ conceptual learning when supported by a structured curriculum that makes use of collaborative learning under in- structor supervision," and that Andes would need supplementation with unguided problem solving and explicit metacognitive direction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Student adherence to the technology's intended activity structure needs monitoring, and collaborative learning under instructor supervision should accompany use
#### Constraints
- The article notes technologies alone do not offer a panacea to the problems plaguing physics education; instructional methods in most university physics courses still differ little from those of a century ago

### Target Learners
- introductory university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual change
- expert-like problem solving
- inquiry-based laboratory learning
