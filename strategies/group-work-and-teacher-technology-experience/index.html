Strategies

Have learners complete tasks in small groups and build teachers' experience with technology-based instruments¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's pedagogical implications include doing tasks in groups, which the authors say can improve "not only the learners’ language skills and sub-skills, but also their social interactions", and improving teachers' experience with technology-based instruments for foreign language learning. Both groups in the study worked in six sub-groups of five students, with the researcher guiding as needed.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Language teachers should become familiar with computers and other technology-based instruments to employ tasks, following Hubbard (2006)

Constraints¶

  • The article notes many current language teachers have limited experience with CALL software from the learners' perspective and may be novices at using technology for teaching

Target Learners¶

  • EFL learners in junior high school
  • language teachers and curriculum designers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English grammar learning
  • social interaction skills

Claims¶

  • Both CALL-based and written-question tasks produce positive effects on EFL grammar learning [+M]
  • EFL learners doing CALL-based tasks outperform learners doing written-question tasks on a grammar post-test [+M]
  • The authors observed higher motivation among learners doing CALL-based tasks than among those doing written-question tasks [+W]
  • The experimental and control groups were homogeneous in grammar knowledge before the intervention [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Select motivating tasks and include more motivating practices in course books and curriculum programs
