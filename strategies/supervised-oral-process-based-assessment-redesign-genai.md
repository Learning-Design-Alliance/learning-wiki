# Redesign assessment toward supervised, oral, process-based and authentic formats to verify students' own reasoning under GenAI

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Participants described moving away from unsupervised written essays toward more face-to-face, oral and closely supervised assessment in response to GenAI. Strategies named include invigilated examinations for essential skills, oral vivas with differing scenarios to observe clinical reasoning, rubrics written to identify AI use, and process-based marks such as "five marks for showing us a screenshot of their literature search terms from a database". The article presents these as academics' early adaptation efforts rather than tested interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment tasks must continue to evaluate students' own reasoning, judgement and application of knowledge, per the article's recommendations section.
#### Constraints
- The article notes concerns that exams may be ineffective if students relied on GenAI throughout the rest of the course; these approaches also require careful design.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- verifying students' own knowledge and clinical reasoning
- academic integrity
- readiness for safe practice
