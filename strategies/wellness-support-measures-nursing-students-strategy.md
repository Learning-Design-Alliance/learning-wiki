# Institutional wellness and support measures: diet, activity, sleep, task management, counseling, peer mentoring, flexible scheduling, and participatory teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete recipe set the article offers for institutions seeking to improve students' HRQoL and reduce burnout. The article states that measures for reducing burnout include "considering students' wishes and needs when setting tasks, specifying tasks when necessary, teaching time management and planning skills, and reducing stress" and that "providing extracurricular and fun activities, fostering a friendly environment based on effective communication between teachers and students, and offering ongoing academic and functional counseling can create a more supportive atmosphere." Additional measures include counseling and wellness services, peer mentoring for first-year students, stress and time management workshops, flexible scheduling, and participatory teaching methods to raise intrinsic motivation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional capacity to offer counseling services, wellness programs, peer mentoring, and curriculum-integrated workshops
#### Constraints
- The strategies are recommended, not tested; the article calls for longitudinal studies of targeted HRQoL interventions on academic outcomes

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students, including first-year students entering peer mentoring

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic success (GPA), reduced academic burnout, and increased intrinsic motivation
