# Assessment of a computational CURE using research-validated surveys, coursework analysis, and instructor observation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends assessing each CURE to inform improvement after each class. "Enhanced learning of the class material can be assessed by analysis of students' class work", while attitudes and scientific thinking can be measured with "two research-validated surveys": the Colorado Learning Attitudes about Science Survey (CLASS) pre/post and the CURE survey, "which includes assessing the feeling of community". Instructor observation of students at work, questions and discussion, assignment analysis, and course evaluations are also named as valuable sources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to research-validated instruments (CLASS; the CURE survey)
- Collection of student coursework and raw calculation data for analysis
#### Constraints
- Confusing lab-manual sections, difficult concepts, and tool limitations should feed back into revisions of the course and the tools

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students in CURE courses

### Target Learning Goals
- attitudes about science and scientific thinking
- sense of community and belonging in research
