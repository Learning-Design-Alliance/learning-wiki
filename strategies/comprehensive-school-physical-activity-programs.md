# Comprehensive School Physical Activity Programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A Comprehensive School Physical Activity Program (CSPAP) is a whole-school framework, promoted by the CDC and SHAPE America, that coordinates physical activity across five components: physical education, physical activity during school, physical activity before and after school, staff involvement, and family and community engagement. Rather than relying on physical education class alone, CSPAP treats the entire school day and environment as an instructional setting for accumulating the recommended 60 minutes of daily moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) and for building lifelong movement habits.

## Design Implications
CSPAP operationalizes the recognition that health behaviors and learning are intertwined: systematic reviews find that increased school-based physical activity time does not harm — and may modestly support — academic achievement [Donnelly et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000901) [+M]. Because no single component delivers enough activity, the design problem is coordination: each component reinforces the others, and physical education serves as the instructional anchor that teaches the skills and dispositions other components let students practice. Success depends on a designated CSPAP leader (typically a physical educator), administrative support, and embedding activity into classroom routines rather than treating it as an add-on.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trained CSPAP champion, usually the physical education teacher, to coordinate across components
- Administrative buy-in and policy support (e.g., scheduled activity breaks, safe routes to school)
- Quality [physical education](../elements/physical-education.md) as the foundation — activity without instruction produces movement but not competence
- Classroom teachers equipped with brief, structured movement strategies such as [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) or [Active Recess](../strategies/active-recess.md)

#### Constraints
- Replacing physical education with unstructured activity time degrades outcomes; instructional PE is the component most associated with motor skill and fitness gains [-M]
- Classroom-based activity breaks fail when they are unstructured, disruptive, or perceived by teachers as competing with instructional time [~M]
- Whole-school adoption without staff training and a coordinator typically collapses into isolated, unsustainable efforts [-M]
- Effects on academic achievement are small and indirect; framing CSPAP as an achievement intervention overpromises [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Before/after-school programs range from supervised open play to structured clubs (e.g., marathon kids, walking school buses)
- Classroom integration ranges from short energizers to movement-based academic content (e.g., acting out vocabulary or math facts)
- Family/community engagement ranges from take-home activity logs to community partnerships with parks and youth sports

### Target Learners
- All K–12 students, with equity as an explicit goal — CSPAP is designed to reach students who lack access to organized sport or safe neighborhood activity [+M]
- Students with low activity levels benefit most from multiple low-barrier entry points; competence-building experiences support sustained participation, consistent with evidence that [self-efficacy predicts academic persistence](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M] and analogous motivation dynamics in physical domains

### Target Learning Goals
- Health-related fitness knowledge and habitual MVPA accumulation
- Motor skill competence and movement confidence
- Dispositions toward lifelong physical activity
- Classroom engagement and on-task behavior as a proximal academic benefit [+M]

### Instructions
1. Designate a CSPAP leader and form a school activity committee
2. Audit current activity opportunities across the five components and identify gaps
3. Strengthen [physical education](../elements/physical-education.md) as the instructional core
4. Add during-school activity: classroom movement breaks, active recess, [active transitions](../strategies/active-transitions.md)
5. Add before/after-school options and staff wellness participation
6. Engage families and community partners; evaluate MVPA levels and adjust annually
