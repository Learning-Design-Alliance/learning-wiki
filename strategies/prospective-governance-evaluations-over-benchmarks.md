# Prioritize prospective, in-curriculum evaluations of AI governance interventions over additional isolated benchmark studies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends reallocating scholarly attention from capability benchmarking to testing governance interventions in real curricula. It names "honor-code adaptations, AI-disclosure norms, faculty training programs" as examples that should be "tested in real curricula rather than proposed in commentary." Future research should test disclosure policies, faculty-development programs, learner-use standards, assessment safeguards, equity-oriented frameworks, and human-oversight mechanisms across diverse medical-education environments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be evaluated prospectively in authentic educational settings rather than proposed in commentary.
#### Constraints
- Governance models developed at well-resourced centers may not generalize to institutions with fewer technological, financial, and faculty-development resources.

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- Residents and trainees in health professions education

### Target Learning Goals
- Responsible and ethical use of AI in learning and assessment
- Preservation of independent clinical reasoning
