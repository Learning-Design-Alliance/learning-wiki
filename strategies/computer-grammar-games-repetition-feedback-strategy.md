# Use computer-based grammar games to provide repetition, drill, and stress-free immediate feedback for young learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends exploiting two features of computer-based activities in young learners' grammar classes. First, computers "can present the activities as many times as needed", supporting the repetition, drill, and practice the authors call highly important for young learners. Second, software feedback is given in the absence of the teacher, so students can learn from mistakes in a stress-free atmosphere, which the authors say improves confidence as well as learning. Game designers are also warned that activities that are too easy or too difficult will discourage use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful selection and design of appropriately difficult computer-based activities
- Software that provides immediate feedback on performance
#### Constraints
- Without careful design, counterproductive results from CALL application should be expected

### Target Learners
- young elementary EFL learners

### Target Learning Goals
- grammar learning and learner confidence
