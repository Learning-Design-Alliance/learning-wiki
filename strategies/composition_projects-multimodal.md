# Composition Projects Multimodal

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Multimodal composition projects ask learners to produce original artifacts that deliberately combine modes of meaning — writing, visuals, audio, video, gesture, and spatial layout — rather than text alone. Learners plan, draft, and revise across modes, making rhetorical decisions about which mode best serves their purpose and audience. The composition process, not just the artifact, is the site of learning.

## Design Implications

Multimodal composition leverages the productive overlap of verbal and visual representation: presenting and producing content in complementary modes supports encoding and recall [Combining words and visuals improves learning over words alone.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-recall.md) [+S]. Because learners choose modes, media, and framing, the work builds ownership and intrinsic motivation [Supporting learner autonomy increases intrinsic motivation and engagement.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]. However, production quality is not learning quality — decorative or seductive visuals chosen for polish rather than meaning can add load without adding understanding [Decorative illustrations do not improve learning.](../claims/decorative-illustrations-do-not-improve-learning.md) [+M]. Design decisions about mode should be tied to rhetorical purpose, and the cognitive load of learning new tools should be managed separately from the load of learning the content [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — see [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md).

### Context
#### Requirements
- An authentic purpose and audience; multimodal work gains coherence when it addresses real readers or viewers ([Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md))
- Explicit instruction in mode affordances — what video does that prose cannot, when a diagram outperforms a paragraph
- Time and scaffolding for drafting and revision across modes, with [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) criteria that evaluate rhetorical choices, not technical polish
- Tool instruction delivered just-in-time so software learning does not crowd out content learning

#### Constraints
- Tool complexity can overwhelm working memory; when learners must simultaneously learn unfamiliar software and unfamiliar content, both suffer [Cognitive overload degrades learning outcomes.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-S]
- Assessment is harder to standardize; rubrics that privilege polish over reasoning reward technical skill rather than understanding
- Learners with limited access to devices, bandwidth, or quiet production space are systematically disadvantaged
- Open-ended multimodal tasks can flounder without prior knowledge or models; unguided discovery during production is inefficient for novices [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Remix tasks** — learners re-represent existing content in a new mode (turn an essay into a podcast), isolating the translation skill
- **Scaffolded progression** — start with single-mode-plus-annotation, then two modes, then full multimodal projects
- **Collaborative production** — team projects distribute technical roles and add peer feedback; collaborative writing improves text quality and transfers to multimodal drafting [Collaborative writing improves text quality.](../claims/collaborative-writing-improves-text-quality.md) [+M]
- **Process portfolios** — collect drafts, storyboards, and reflection alongside the final artifact to make thinking assessable

### Target Learners
- Adolescents through adults; younger learners benefit from heavily scaffolded versions with limited toolsets
- Learners whose strengths are non-verbal — multimodal options give them a legitimate channel for demonstrating understanding [~M]
- Advanced learners ready for rhetorical decision-making; novices need more constrained task structures first

### Target Learning Goals
- Deep understanding: re-representing content in a new mode forces generative processing
- Communication and rhetorical skill: audience analysis, mode selection, design reasoning
- Self-regulation: managing a long, multi-stage project builds planning and revision habits [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md)

### Instructions
1. Define an authentic task with a real or realistic audience and purpose ([Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md)).
2. Analyze mentor texts — study 2–3 published multimodal artifacts, asking what each mode contributes ([Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) can structure this comparison).
3. Plan in storyboard or wireframe form before production, mapping content to modes.
4. Draft with just-in-time tool instruction, keeping tool learning separate from content decisions ([Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md)).
5. Critique drafts against criteria focused on rhetorical effect, using structured peer feedback ([Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — feedback is most effective at task and process levels [Feedback most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]).
6. Revise, publish to the audience, and reflect on mode choices in a written or recorded designer's statement.

## Related Strategies
- Peer feedback and critique cycles — multimodal drafts need structured response before revision
- Process portfolio assessment — makes the composition process visible and gradable
- Remix and translation tasks — a lower-floor entry point into multimodal reasoning

## Examples
- **[National Writing Project](https://www.nwp.org)** — extensive practitioner work on digital and multimodal composition in K–12 and adult classrooms, including the *Digital Is* collection of classroom cases.
- **Podcast units in secondary English** — students research a topic, script, record, and publish an episode; scripting is revision of prose for the ear, making audience awareness concrete.
- **Explainer video assignments in STEM courses** — students produce a 3-minute video explaining a concept; the requirement to visualize mechanisms exposes shallow understanding that a written summary can hide.
- **StoryMap projects (e.g., [Knight Lab StoryMap](https://storymap.knightlab.com))** — learners combine maps, images, and narrative text, forcing spatial and verbal reasoning to align.
