Teachers can create interleaved assignments from existing blocked textbook materials by pulling one problem from each of many assignments¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because the greatest barrier to implementation is the scarcity of interleaved assignments in textbooks and workbooks, the article recommends practical remedies: "teachers can create interleaved assignments by simply choosing one problem from each of a dozen assignments from their students' textbook." Teachers may also search the Internet for mixed review or spiral review worksheets and use practice tests from high-stakes testing organizations. The authors ultimately hope publishers replace a portion of blocked practice with interleaved practice in revised editions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to a textbook or workbook with many blocked assignments covering the relevant skills

Constraints¶

  • The article notes no previous study of interleaved mathematics practice has measured time on task, so per-unit-time efficiency is untested

Target Learners¶

  • middle school mathematics students
  • mathematics students generally

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improved delayed test performance in mathematics
  • strategy selection across mixed problem types

Affordances¶

  • Interleaving Three Strategies Account

Claims¶

  • A pilot study with 83 seventh graders found a large interleaving benefit (d = 0.97) preceding the main trial [+W]
  • The interleaving effect on delayed test scores remained significant in a multilevel model controlling for teacher effects, with no teacher-by-condition interaction [+W]
  • Teachers implemented interleaved practice without training and, surveyed before learning the results, mostly judged it useful and viable though slightly harder and more time-consuming [+W]
  • Mostly interleaved mathematics practice outscored mostly blocked practice on an unannounced delayed test in a large cluster randomized classroom trial [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Include a small block of problems of a new skill at the start of an assignment, then interleave the rest
