# Integrate task- and movement-level assessments into procedure-level multilevel evaluation frameworks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's central forward-looking recommendation is to build multilevel frameworks that aggregate fine-grained assessments into comprehensive evaluations. It observes that "most studies assess isolated tasks or gestures" and that future work "should seek to integrate task- and movement-based assessment into procedure-level evaluations to create more robust and generalizable models for assessment." Such aggregation could make assessments more robust, interpretable, and applicable to actual surgery, supporting unified profiles of surgical competence rather than single-level scores.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Methods that aggregate lower-level assessments into larger comprehensive evaluations
#### Constraints
- Most existing research has not combined metrics across levels into a full procedure assessment

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees and residents in robotic surgery programs

### Target Learning Goals
- comprehensive profiling of surgical technical competence across task, gesture, and procedure levels
