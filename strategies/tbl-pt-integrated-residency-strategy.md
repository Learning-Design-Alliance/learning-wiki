# Integrate TBL readiness testing with peer-taught clinical skill practice in dental residency training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's TBL+PT intervention combines TBL's readiness-assurance structure with peer-taught clinical skills practice: students complete pre-class preparation, take IRAT and TRAT tests, discuss complex cases in teams, and then "worked collaboratively within teams to learn and practice clinical skills," with some members demonstrating and others observing and giving feedback. Instructors monitored interactions and intervened to clarify misconceptions. The article reports this strategy significantly improved theoretical scores, most OSCE skills, engagement, and satisfaction versus traditional teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Same faculty across conditions with structured feedback during practice sessions
- Structured peer evaluation sessions where team members observe each other's performance
- Instructor monitoring with intervention to clarify misconceptions and demonstrate correct techniques
#### Constraints
- May require more time for students and faculty preparation and face tutor variability, addressable through structured training, clear guidelines, and continuous feedback
- Single-center, small-sample study; cohort grouping by academic year may have introduced unmeasured confounders
- Residents rotate through departments for only 3–6 months, complicating consistent implementation

### Target Learners
- first-year dental residents in standardized residency training

### Target Learning Goals
- theoretical knowledge in oral medicine
- clinical procedural skills
- teamwork and communication
- student engagement
