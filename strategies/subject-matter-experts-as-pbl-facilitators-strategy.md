# Replace non-subject-matter 'lay' preceptors with content-specific experts when implementing PBL in contexts where learners lack prior exposure to the pedagogy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
At UNAERP, the PBL curriculum begun in 2003 used non-subject-matter 'lay' preceptors, "formally schooled in the PBL process", who were replaced by content-specific experts in 2013 on the recommendation of the institutional Planning Group. In this low prior-exposure context, students strongly preferred expert facilitators across all six evaluated roles. Both facilitator types completed more than 20 hours of PBL training of lectures, guided observation, and hands-on practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All facilitators, expert or not, must be equally versed in the PBL method prior to facilitating, via training of more than 20 hours including lectures, guided observation, and hands-on practice with actual students
#### Constraints
- This recommendation rests on student perceptions, not objectively measured achievement; the authors did not assess first-year students, so findings may not generalize to all learners
- The literature remains debated: some studies found experts superior, others did not, and some yielded mixed results

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in a region where active-learning pedagogies are not widely used in university or pre-university settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective PBL tutorial facilitation supporting knowledge construction, self-study motivation, and cognitive learning achievement
