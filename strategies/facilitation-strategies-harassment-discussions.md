# Facilitate harassment discussions by educating organizers, partnering with campus centers and experts, using external facilitators over multiple semesters, providing mental health access, setting ground rules and expectations, arranging quiet and counselor rooms, holding men-only caucus groups, and sharing institutional policies

## Description
The letter offers concrete facilitation ideas for departments discussing sexual harassment findings. These include educating oneself about "harassment, oppression, and difficult conversations," partnering with Women's Resource Centers and Equity & Inclusion Offices, co-thinking with Gender Studies and Ethnic Studies faculty, and hiring "an external facilitator to guide a sequence of discussions over the course of multiple semesters." Additional steps address participant safety and preparation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Organizers should read about harassment, oppression, and difficult conversations, and bring expertise from anti-sexism and anti-racism work explicitly to the discussion
#### Constraints
- The authors note sexual harassment is a cultural issue and that cultural change is a slow process, which is why a sequence of discussions across multiple semesters is suggested

### Target Learners
- physics department members
- faculty and researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- facilitating safe and productive conversations about sexual harassment
- departmental cultural change toward a harassment-free environment
