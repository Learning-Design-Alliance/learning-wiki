# Use familiar debriefing frameworks (PEARLS, the Diamond, Scottish centre debrief model, Gather-Analyze-Summarize) to structure meta-debriefing conversations and role-model ideal debriefing behaviors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that meta-debriefers use published debriefing frameworks to provide conversational structure, debriefing the debrief "in the same manner as we would conduct debriefings following simulated scenarios." Frameworks named include PEARLS, the Diamond, the Scottish centre debrief model, and Gather, Analyze, Summarize, which "can be adapted and/or combined to meet local needs and contexts." Structured group meta-debriefing promotes self-assessment and combines focused facilitation with directive feedback, and it lets meta-debriefers role-model ideal debriefing behaviors, showcasing practical applications of theoretical concepts—especially valuable for novice debriefers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Meta-debriefers should use frameworks suited to their local context; debriefer self-assessment, reflection, and critical thinking remains at the heart of the interaction
#### Constraints
- Frameworks may need adaptation or combination for local needs; the article notes some authors use a modified hybrid of PEARLS and the Diamond incorporating plus-delta

### Target Learners
- Simulation faculty debriefers, particularly novices who rigidly display newly learned theoretical behaviors

### Target Learning Goals
- Debriefing facilitation skills
- Self-assessment and critical reflection on debriefing practice
