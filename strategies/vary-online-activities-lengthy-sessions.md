# Vary online teaching and learning activities to counter fatigue and anxiety in lengthy online sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that Chinese users became more critical of online education after the pandemic, and that keywords such as 'sleepy' and 'anxious' appeared in the content analysis, the authors recommend that a greater variance in teaching and learning online activities is needed to maximize the value of 'staring at screen' during lengthy online sessions. They also note that expectations for learners to be highly self-disciplined and self-regulated are challenging to meet, and that reaching the financially disadvantaged population should be central to the field.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designers must attend to learner autonomy, since the authors link negativity to forced, involuntary adoption of the online mode
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on observational social media sentiment data, not on a controlled test of varied activities

### Target Learners
- K-12 and higher education students in emergency remote learning
- female students, who showed elevated negative sentiment

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustaining engagement, motivation, and positive attitudes during lengthy online learning sessions
