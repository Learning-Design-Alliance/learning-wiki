# Provide text access during closed-ended practice testing to support reasoning and comprehension

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
When learners can consult the text while answering practice questions, they receive implicit feedback by comparing their reasoning with the text and are "encouraged to construct meaning through direct engagement with the text." Experiment 4 showed that making the text available during practice tests improved both practice-test accuracy and later short-answer comprehension. This supports open-book practice testing as an implementable activity, with the caveat that it did not remove the format difference between multiple-choice and true—false practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must have the text accessible during the practice activity; the final test is completed after a delay without text access.
#### Constraints
- The multiple-choice advantage over true—false practice persisted even when the text was available during practice.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in content courses

### Target Learning Goals
- comprehension of expository text after a delay
