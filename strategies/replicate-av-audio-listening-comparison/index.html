Replicate the audio-visual vs. audio-only listening comparison with other populations and tasks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (3 for, 2 mixed, 2 against) · 4 studies (3 causal, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

To permit greater confidence in the results, the article proposes further research directions: replication with male vs. female learners, replication with a different age group, and replication with tasks other than the ones employed in the study. This extends the comparison beyond the original all-male intermediate sample and task set.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to learner samples differing in gender, age, or task type from the original study

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • EFL learners of other ages and both genders

Target Learning Goals¶

  • L2 oral accuracy

Claims¶

  • Audio-visual listening tasks produce higher oral accuracy than audio-only listening tasks for intermediate Iranian EFL learners [+W]
  • The audio-visual and audio-only groups were homogeneous in general L2 proficiency before treatment [+W]
  • Audio-visual lecture clips produce better comprehension than audio-only clips, and the multimedia advantage holds at accelerated speed [+M]
  • The comprehension cost of instructor visuals replicates across different instructors and lecture lengths in a combined analysis [-W]
  • Adding visuals of the instructor to online video lectures impairs comprehension relative to audio-only and audio+text formats [-W]
  • Preregistered Wave 2 replicated Wave 1's finding of poorer audio-visual associative learning in children with dyslexia [~W]
  • Children with developmental dyslexia reach lower asymptotic accuracy in non-linguistic audio-visual associative learning than age-matched and reading-matched typically-developing peers [~W]
