# Spaced recall of representationaly rich worked solutions as an in-class activity emphasizing expert practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (3 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a methodology in which students watch a video solution to a representationaly dense physics problem, immediately recall it, and then recall it again in sessions spaced days or weeks apart, consulting notes only when stuck, with alternate versions given at the end. The author argues this structure shifts the burden so that "translating between representations the smaller encumbrance," encouraging expert practices such as deriving equations from diagrams and reasoning rather than memorizing whole solutions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems chosen for representational density, where the solution is aided by translating a diagram into the appropriate equation
- Multiple recall sessions spaced over days or weeks, since immediate recall relies on short-term memory
#### Constraints
- The methodology was only performed on two students who had previously passed introductory physics

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- translating between diagrams and equations
- symbolic manipulation of physics equations
- expert-like problem-solving practices

## Claims

- [During immediate recall students referenced diagrams to explain equation sources, but at the first spaced recall neither used the diagram to derive the equation](../claims/diagrams-communication-not-generation.md) [~W]
- [Students found diagrams much easier to remember than equations, but omitted non-salient diagram features such as bar widths encoding relative magnitude](../claims/diagrams-easier-to-recall-than-equations.md) [~W]
- [After spaced recall with interviewer prompting, both students reported becoming much better at translating between diagrams and equations](../claims/spaced-recall-improves-diagram-equation-translation.md) [+W]
- [Consistent success at recalling physics solutions did not occur until after at least one unsuccessful spaced recall session, with improvement that was not monotonic](../claims/spaced-recall-success-after-unsuccessful-session.md) [~W]
- [Spaced recall encouraged strategic memorization of parts of the solution (the diagram) combined with reasoning through the rest](../claims/strategic-memorization-with-diagram-reasoning.md) [+W]
- [The most salient remembered feature of the solutions was that one equation was to be substituted into another, more prominent than how to derive the equations](../claims/substitution-most-salient-recalled-feature.md) [~W]
- [On alternate transfer problems, students correctly translated new diagrams into equations in 5 of 6 cases, though most solutions were incomplete or contained minor errors](../claims/transfer-alternate-problems-five-of-six.md) [+W]
