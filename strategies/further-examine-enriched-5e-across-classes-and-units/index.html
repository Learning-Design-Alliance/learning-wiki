Strategies

Further examine enriched 5E, cooperative, and 5E models across classes, units, and stakeholder views¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's forward-looking recommendation is that "In future studies, the effectiveness of the enriched 5E model, the cooperative learning model and the 5E instructional model can be further examined in different classes and with different course units." It adds that "it would be beneficial if teachers and students' views on these teaching methods could be further explored." This follows from the finding that method effectiveness differed between classes and subjects in this study.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Quasi-experimental comparisons across different classes and course units, plus collection of teacher and student views

Constraints¶

  • The present study covered only one course unit (light) in one school with one teacher

Target Learners¶

  • seventh-grade secondary school science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic achievement in science
  • scientific attitudes

Claims¶

  • The 5E instructional model enriched with animations produced higher seventh-grade achievement on the light unit than the 5E model enriched with cooperative learning and the 5E model alone [+W]
  • Both the animation-enriched and cooperative-learning-enriched 5E models improved seventh-graders' scientific attitudes relative to the 5E model alone, with no difference between the two enriched groups [+W]
  • The three intact classes were equivalent in prior achievement and scientific attitudes before the light-unit intervention [+W]
