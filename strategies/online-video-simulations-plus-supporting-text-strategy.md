# Provide online video simulations of practical skills with supporting text as an addition to usual face-to-face teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests that "the development of online video simulations based on clinical scenarios may be useful in teaching and learning practical skills". The strategy gives learners on-demand access to accurate demonstrations and supporting text so they can view, revise and consolidate skills at a time and place of their choice, supplementing live demonstration, practice and tutor feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video-clips of therapist-patient simulations plus supporting text describing aim, rationale, equipment, key points, common errors and progression
#### Constraints
- The article tested this as an addition to high face-to-face teaching hours, not a replacement; effectiveness during clinical practice remains unexamined

### Target Learners
- health professional students learning practical clinical skills

### Target Learning Goals
- practical skill performance and clinical reasoning (providing a rationale for a skill)
