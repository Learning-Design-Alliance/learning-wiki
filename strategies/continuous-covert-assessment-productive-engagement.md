# Use online platforms for frequent, covert assessment of learners and define productive-engagement metrics for education A/B tests

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (3 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that A/B testing speed requires a change in how evaluation is performed. Online platforms should allow more frequent assessing of learners without them knowing they are actually being assessed, complementing formative assessments (frequent, teacher-discretionary, not captured by platforms) and summative assessments (scarce, authority-mandated). New metrics such as productive engagement — minimising time on the platform while maximising learning — and enjoyable focused time must be defined to help educational A/B tests deliver value.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Platforms must retain rich contextual data so assessment and engagement metrics can be computed continuously
- Stakeholders such as school principals should receive insight about progress to preserve engagement
#### Constraints
- Long spacing between assessments (as in Norway and Sweden) makes assessment results less well suited for the short interventions often evaluated within RCTs

### Target Learners
- school pupils and learners on online education platforms

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring learning progress and productive engagement continuously during digital learning
