# Primary Language Support with Bilingual Texts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy incorporates texts and resources in a learner's primary (L1) language — bilingual picture books, dual-language editions, and multilingual digital libraries — to scaffold comprehension of new content while the learner develops proficiency in the target language. It rests on the linguistic interdependence hypothesis: skills and knowledge acquired in the L1 transfer to the second language, so continued L1 literacy development supports rather than competes with L2 acquisition [Cummins' interdependence hypothesis.](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543076003367) [+M]. Beyond comprehension, bilingual texts validate learners' cultural and linguistic identities, supporting engagement and belonging.

## Design Implications

Bilingual texts let multilingual learners access grade-level concepts through their stronger language while simultaneously building target-language vocabulary and structures, rather than being limited to texts matched to their current L2 proficiency [Vocabulary instruction for English learners benefits from L1 supports.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654308318492) [+M]. The strategy works best when L1 and L2 texts are deliberately connected — previewing a concept in the L1, then encountering it in the L2 — rather than offered as parallel, unlinked options.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to high-quality bilingual or dual-language books (classroom libraries, digital collections such as the [International Digital Children's Library](http://en.childrenslibrary.org) or [Unite for Literacy](https://www.uniteforliteracy.com))
- Knowledge of learners' home languages and literacy levels in those languages
- Instructional routines that explicitly bridge the two languages — e.g., [Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md) in the L1 before L2 reading, or [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) cognates and key terms in both languages
- An asset-oriented classroom climate where home-language use is welcomed, not merely tolerated

#### Constraints
- Availability of bilingual materials is uneven; low-incidence languages are often poorly served, and translations vary widely in quality and cultural authenticity [-M]
- Poorly designed bilingual texts (literal translations, culturally incongruent illustrations) can misrepresent content or stigmatize home languages [-W]
- If L1 texts are used only as a crutch for content access without any bridge to L2 literacy, transfer of vocabulary and structures is weaker [~M]
- Over-reliance on translated materials can reduce learners' exposure to comprehensible target-language input, slowing L2 development [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Paired texts:** the same book in two editions, read first in L1 then in L2
- **Embedded bilingual format:** side-by-side or interlinear dual-language texts
- **Preview–View–Review:** content previewed in L1, taught in L2, reviewed in L1
- **Digital audio support:** [Audiobooks](../principles/audiobooks.md) in the L1 paired with L2 print to decouple decoding from comprehension
- **Family engagement:** sending bilingual books home extends L1 literacy development beyond the classroom

### Target Learners
- Newly arrived immigrants and newcomers with strong L1 literacy but limited L2 proficiency [L1 literacy skills transfer to second-language reading.](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543076003367) [+M]
- Emergent bilinguals in early grades, where bilingual picture books support [Dialogic Reading](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654308318492)-style shared reading at home [+M]
- Learners at risk of subtractive language shift, where continued L1 literacy protects long-term academic outcomes [~M]
- Less necessary for learners already proficient in the target language, for whom L1 texts may add little [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Content-area comprehension: accessing science, social studies, and math concepts at grade level before L2 proficiency catches up
- L2 vocabulary and literacy development through cross-linguistic transfer of cognates and comprehension strategies
- Identity and belonging: seeing one's language and culture represented in the curriculum [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Inventory learners' home languages and L1 literacy levels (family survey, [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) conversations).
2. Curate bilingual texts matched to content units and learner interests; verify translation quality and cultural authenticity.
3. Preview key concepts and vocabulary in the L1 before L2 instruction ([Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md), [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md)).
4. During L2 reading, support cross-linguistic connections through [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) cognates and bilingual glossaries.
5. Review and consolidate in the L1, then bridge to L2 output through [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) retellings.
6. Assess comprehension in whichever language reveals what learners know, not only in the target language ([Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md)).

## Examples
- **Preview–View–Review with bilingual science texts:** a newcomer reads a bilingual picture book on the water cycle in Spanish before the English-language science lesson, then reviews key vocabulary in Spanish afterward — accessing grade-level content while building English science vocabulary.
- **Dual-language classroom libraries:** programs such as dual-language immersion classrooms maintain parallel L1/L2 libraries and schedule independent reading in both languages, protecting L1 literacy growth during L2 development.
- **Family literacy take-home kits:** schools send bilingual books home with dialogic-reading prompts in the home language, leveraging parents' stronger language for shared reading.
