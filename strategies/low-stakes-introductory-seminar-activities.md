# Use low-stakes introductory seminar activities (asking about figures, news stories, prepared questions) before reading full papers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The guide recommends that beginners start seminar participation with bounded, low-stakes contributions rather than full paper readings. Suggested activities include looking at featured astronomy images, preparing one question about a paper someone else suggested, bringing an interesting figure for local experts to explain, and asking about a news story's backstory. These let novices engage the community while building comfort.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to an active seminar or journal club community and to experts willing to explain figures and news items
#### Constraints
- Framed for new students who may not yet feel comfortable in the research world; activities precede full-paper reading

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate astronomy students new to research seminars

### Target Learning Goals
- participating in scientific discussions
- building comfort in a research community
- formulating questions about scientific literature
