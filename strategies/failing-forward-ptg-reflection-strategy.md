# Normalize failure and integrate post-traumatic growth through faculty disclosure of mistakes and structured reflection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends a concrete recipe for building resilience: normalize constructive failure as part of learning, have attending physicians openly share their own career mistakes and lessons learned, and incorporate post-traumatic growth (PTG)—positive psychological change from struggling with challenging events. Adverse events such as difficult cases or errors can be reframed via "structured reflection sessions" that help learners process emotions, identify lessons learned, and build skills for future challenges, with positive reinforcement of growth after mistakes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty willingness to openly discuss their own mistakes
- Structured reflection sessions after adverse events
- Positive reinforcement of growth and progress, particularly after mistakes
#### Constraints
- Mistakes are often currently treated as failures rather than opportunities for growth, so cultural shift is required

### Target Learners
- medical students
- residents (house staff)

### Target Learning Goals
- resilience
- growth mindset
- learning from errors
