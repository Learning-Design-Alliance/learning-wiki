# Provide explicit verbal instructions and facilitator prompting to guide students to articulate reasoning during in-class group work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Generative instructors in this study did not assume students would reason aloud unprompted: they "provided explicit verbal instructions to guide students", for example telling students early in the semester to justify answers rather than just share a letter choice. They also acted as facilitators with small groups, asking follow-up questions such as "Why would you do that?" to prompt students to explain the rationale behind their ideas rather than supplying answers themselves.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-class tasks must require students to develop and articulate reasoning, not merely recall answers
- Instructors must resist providing answers in favor of asking follow-up questions
#### Constraints
- The article notes up to 50% of generative instructors' class time was still spent with students listening rather than working

### Target Learners
- college students in large undergraduate life sciences courses

### Target Learning Goals
- generating explanations supported with reasoning
- deep conceptual understanding
