# Adaptive responses to carelessness should take probable student affect into account and tailor messages to highly engaged students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because carelessness is concentrated among highly engaged, generally successful students, the article recommends that interventions differ from typical disengagement responses. It states that "adaptive responses to carelessness should take probable student affect into account" and that it "may be appropriate to tailor messages given to careless students to the motivational needs of highly engaged students", potentially leading to interventions very different from those typically given to disengaged students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires detection of carelessness and inference of probable student affect within the learning system
#### Constraints
- Carelessness remains associated with failing to attend college even after controlling for student knowledge, so addressing it matters especially in high-stakes situations such as standardized exams

### Target Learners
- highly engaged, generally successful middle school and high school mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing careless errors among engaged students
- preventing disengagement of successful students
