# Develop physical activity or sports programs that strengthen peer relationships to improve the academic performance of adolescents experiencing depression

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, because peer relationships carry the indirect path from depression to academic performance, schools should design physical activity and sports programs whose explicit aim is building peer relationships rather than merely increasing activity. As the abstract states, "it is important to develop a physical activity or sports program to strengthen peer relationships to improve academic performance." The discussion adds that "participating in various sports programs to improve peer relationships may more positively impact academic performance" than merely encouraging activity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must be aimed at strengthening peer relationships, since peer relationships are the pathway through which the academic benefit operates
#### Constraints
- For female students with high physical activity, the indirect effect through peer relationships was offset, so activity alone does not guarantee the benefit
- Gender identity of the sport was not measured, so which activities build peer acceptance for which students remains open

### Target Learners
- Chinese adolescents experiencing depressive symptoms
- middle school students

### Target Learning Goals
- improving academic performance
- strengthening peer relationships
