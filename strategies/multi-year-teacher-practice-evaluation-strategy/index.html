Evaluate interim assessment impacts on teacher practice over multiple years as teachers learn to use the tool¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that future research test whether impacts on teacher practice grow over time. They note the year-one results pertain "to the first year of the intervention, when teachers are likely still learning how to use the assessment tool and integrate it into their instructional practice," and conclude: "Future research should explore the hypothesis that impacts on teacher practice grow over time as teachers learn to use the assessment tool." This follows evidence that such interventions may take multiple years to affect student performance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Multi-cohort or multi-year designs tracking teacher practice with detailed logs beyond the first implementation year

Constraints¶

  • Year-one null findings may reflect control teachers' use of other interim assessment tools and limited statistical power

Target Learners¶

  • K-8 students and their teachers in schools adopting interim assessments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved achievement through sustained data-driven instructional change

Affordances¶

  • Interim Assessment Differentiation Theory Of Action

Claims¶

  • Difference-in-differences analyses of practice changes around Acuity test windows were broadly confirmatory of null effects, with mixed signs and few significant estimates [+W]
  • Treatment-control contrasts in use of small-group and individual math instruction were positive and of substantial magnitude but not statistically significant [+W]
  • Access to the Acuity Predictive interim assessment produced no statistically significant treatment-control differences in math content coverage in any of seven content areas in year one [+W]
  • No significant treatment-control difference in targeted (enriched or remedial) math instruction, though the estimate's sign and magnitude suggest Acuity teachers may increase targeted instruction [+W]
  • Time-series plots showed selective periods of higher engagement in specific practices among Acuity teachers, but these did not cohere into a broader pattern [+W]
