# Integrate simulation sessions into unit-level strategies offered in the first months of work to support newly graduated nurses' transition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study concludes that "Early interventions, through integration of simulation sessions into strategies offered at the unit's level may be useful to ensure an effective working transition." This strategy involves healthcare organisations incorporating simulation into their transition support approach, with sessions delivered in the first months of working experience and tailored to the unit's patient profiles and scope of practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Healthcare organisations must allocate resources for simulation access during the transition period
#### Constraints
- Recommended based on preceptor perceptions and self-assessed competence data; outcomes of simulation in the early transition phase have not yet been investigated

### Target Learners
- newly graduated registered nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- competence development across diagnostic functions, managing situations, therapeutic interventions, quality assurance and working role
