# Develop school-based healthy-lifestyle education and free intervention programs (workshops, afterschool programs) targeting eating, sleep, and screen-time habits

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q3` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that high school policy makers and educators act on the study's predictive findings by promoting healthy lifestyles through structured school offerings. They suggest "developing new courses that will help educate students in adopting a healthy lifestyle" and free intervention programs via "organizing workshops and afterschool programs" that promote improving eating habits (e.g., more fruits/vegetables), earlier bedtimes, and less screen time. The recommendation rests on the study's longitudinal associations rather than on a tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires school policy maker and educator buy-in to adopt and apply policies promoting healthy lifestyle habits
#### Constraints
- Findings are limited to higher-than-average academic performers from a single French-Canadian public high school in Montreal, Canada
- Lifestyle habits were self-reported
- The recommendation rests on predictive associations, not on a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- high school students (grades 7–9)

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance
- cognitive control
