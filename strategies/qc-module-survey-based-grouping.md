# Use surveys of student familiarity to form mixed-ability groups and tailor topic emphasis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In the 25-student implementation, students were split into groups "with mixed and complementary abilities", with the mix determined from surveys designed to help determine students' familiarity with quantum mechanics and computing. By assessing students' strengths, material was presented in a manner appropriate to their abilities and interests. The article states that knowledge of students' strengths should guide teachers' choice of topics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-course surveys assessing familiarity with quantum mechanics and computing
#### Constraints
- The grouping approach was used in the traditional teaching mode with a diverse class; the flipped classroom implementation used self-chosen topics in smaller groups instead

### Target Learners
- high school students in classes with mixed physics, mathematics, and computer science backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- quantum mechanics and quantum computing concepts matched to student strengths
