# Make hemostasis tools such as tourniquets readily available in public areas so Stop the Bleed training translates into real emergency response

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors argue that standardized bleeding-control curricula depend heavily on access to equipment including tourniquets, and that lack of cost-free availability prevented participants from mounting an appropriate trauma response. They recommend that "professional tourniquets should be readily available in a public area or commercially in stores, especially in China", drawing an analogy to AEDs designed for use by anyone regardless of training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Public or commercial availability of hemostasis tools and materials at the time of an emergency
#### Constraints
- Without accessible tools, the authors state the STB course would be a waste of time, cost, and usefulness

### Target Learners
- Medical students and the general public trained in Stop the Bleed

### Target Learning Goals
- Applying hemorrhage-control skills in real trauma emergencies
