Expose physics students to computer-assisted STAD cooperative instruction to improve performance¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on its findings, the article recommends that "Physics teachers should expose their students to computer-assisted STAD cooperative instructional strategy so as to improve their performance in physics." It further recommends that ministries and stakeholders organize workshops on computer-assisted cooperative learning, that teacher education programs prepare teachers in such innovative strategies, and that instructional designers develop relevant computer-assisted instructional packages for Nigerian schools.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher training in the package and cooperative strategy; computer laboratory facilities; available CALP-style packages

Constraints¶

  • Evidence base is one quasi-experimental study in two Nigerian public schools; the article itself notes prior mixed findings for STAD versus conventional methods

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school physics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • physics achievement and retention

Claims¶

  • Nigerian SS II physics students taught with computer-assisted STAD cooperative learning achieved significantly higher post-test scores than students taught with Individualized Computer Instruction [+W]
  • Retention of physics content: the Results section reports a significant STAD-versus-ICI difference at p = 0.053, while the Discussion reports no significant difference in retention [~W]
  • Nigerian secondary students taught physics with computer-assisted STAD and LTM cooperative strategies achieved significantly higher posttest scores than students taught with individualized computer instruction [+W]
  • Computer-assisted STAD and LTM cooperative settings produced significantly higher physics motivation than individualized computer instruction [+W]
  • All three groups gained from pretest to posttest, with the computer-assisted STAD group showing the highest mean gain score in physics achievement [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide CAI packages across school subjects in school computer labs with dedicated time, staffing and funding
  • Use computer-assisted Jigsaw II cooperative learning to teach secondary school physics.
