# Guided-question and emotional-appreciation routine for narrative text comprehension

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementable routine in which the facilitator asks guided questions focusing on deep comprehension, then encourages students to focus on the beauty of plots or storylines, share feelings triggered by the text, appreciate characters' emotions, and self-reflect by evaluating the characters' emotions and their own. Students were guided to use the strategy until adept and to use it on their own in other learning activities, aligned with Gunning's (2008) six steps from simple to complex activity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitator-guided questions (examples in Appendix 1, adapted from Idol, 1987)
- Progression from simple reading activity to more complex activity
#### Constraints
- Evaluated in an 8-week quasi-experiment with intact classes, not random assignment

### Target Learners
- Form Two secondary school students (average age 14)

### Target Learning Goals
- Higher-level (inferential, evaluative, appreciative) comprehension of narrative texts
- Emotional expression and self-reflection during reading
