Note Taking Structures¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 15 studies (6 causal, 5 quant-synthesis, 4 review), q2–q4 · 3 of 15 report an effect size

Description¶

Note taking structures are pre-organized formats — Cornell pages, comparison matrices, skeletal outlines, guided notes — that shape how learners record information during a lesson. Rather than transcribing freely, learners place content into designated regions or fields, which imposes an organization on the material and creates a built-in review artifact.

Design Implications¶

Structured notes work through two mechanisms: they reduce the transcription burden during encoding, freeing working memory for comprehension Chunking reduces working memory load. [+M], and they impose a generative organization that supports later retrieval. The largest gains come when learners review their notes — the encoding benefit of taking notes alone is modest, but reviewing structured notes produces substantial retention gains Distributed practice improves retention. [+S]. Structure matters most when the material itself is poorly organized or delivered at high density; when a lecture is already well-organized, imposed structure adds little Cognitive overload degrades learning. [~M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A template or scaffold matched to the content structure (chronology → outline; comparisons → matrix; cause–effect → flow diagram)
  • Brief instruction in how to use the format, including the review routine (e.g., Cornell's cue-column summarization)
  • Pauses or pacing that allow learners to process and record rather than transcribe verbatim

Constraints¶

  • Verbatim transcription is associated with poorer conceptual learning than generative summarizing Laptop note taking tends toward verbatim transcription and shallower processing than longhand. [-M] — a structure that merely channels transcription does not fix this
  • Highly constrained templates can mismatch the content, forcing ideas into inappropriate slots and distorting understanding [~W]
  • Learners with strong prior knowledge and existing note systems may find imposed formats redundant or disruptive [~W]
  • Providing complete instructor notes removes the generative processing entirely; guided notes with deliberate blanks outperform full handouts [-M]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Cornell format: page divided into cues, notes, and summary; supports self-testing during review
  • Guided notes / skeletal outlines: instructor provides headings and blanks; effective for learners with weak note-taking skill or processing-speed challenges
  • Matrix notes: rows and columns for comparing entities; particularly strong for relational content
  • Graphic organizers / mapping: spatial arrangement of concepts; pairs well with Dual Coding Dual coding improves recall. [+M]

Target Learners¶

  • Novices and students with weak self-regulation, who benefit most from external organization Advance organizers improve learning. [+M]
  • Learners with working memory or processing-speed limitations, for whom guided notes reduce transcription load Cognitive overload degrades learning. [+M]
  • Less beneficial for experts, who already organize content effectively on their own [~W]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Retention of factual and procedural content from lectures or texts
  • Organization of relational knowledge (comparisons, hierarchies, cause–effect chains)
  • Study-skill development: building durable self-testing and review habits

Instructions¶

  1. Choose a format that mirrors the content's structure; provide the template in advance as an Advance Organizer.
  2. Briefly model completing the format on a short segment, thinking aloud about what merits recording and why (Think-Aloud).
  3. Deliver content with pauses at structural junctures so learners summarize in their own words rather than transcribe.
  4. Build in a review routine — cue-column self-questioning (Cornell) or blank-filling (Annotating) — within 24 hours and again at spaced intervals Distributed practice improves retention. [+S].
  5. Fade the scaffold: move from full templates to learner-designed notes as skill develops.

Related Strategies¶

  • Annotating — a text-based cousin: marking and marginalia serve the same organize-and-generate function on source documents
  • Summarization — the generative act structured notes should prompt at review time
  • Retrieval Practice — Cornell cue columns convert notes into self-test prompts
  • Use the coaching framework as a structured discussion guide with a note-taking grid and study group questions
  • Notetaking practices: verbatim statements, contextual notes, and 24-hour review

Examples¶

  • Cornell note-taking system (Walter Pauk, Cornell University) — widely adopted across secondary and higher education; cue/notes/summary layout with a mandated review step.
  • Guided notes in large lectures — instructors distribute partial outlines with key blanks; research in introductory college courses shows higher exam performance than free note taking.
  • Comparison matrices in history instruction — students record causes, actors, and outcomes of multiple conflicts in a grid, supporting later argumentative writing.
