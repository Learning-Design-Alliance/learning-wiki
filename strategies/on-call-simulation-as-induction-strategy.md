# Incorporate on-call simulation into departmental inductions, especially in specialties with limited prior exposure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends induction programmes incorporate on-call simulation, particularly where participants have limited previous clinical exposure. Three reviewed studies incorporated on-call simulation into inductions or advocated making it mandatory. In UK rotational training, a targeted specialty-specific simulation of common tasks can serve as a vital adjunct to on-call confidence. Simulation plays a key role in inductions addressing clinical scenarios, hospital logistics and human factors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Departmental inductions with time to run simulation alongside lecture and tutorial formats
#### Constraints
- Lectures remain used when time is limited and changeover is immediate, as they reach larger groups faster without simulation resources

### Target Learners
- newly qualified foundation doctors
- final year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- transition into on-call work
- confidence in unfamiliar specialty settings
