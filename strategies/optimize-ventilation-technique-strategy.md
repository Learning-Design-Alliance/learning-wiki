# Direct educational strategies toward optimizing ventilation techniques in CPR training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because compression quality improved with feedback but ventilation targets were not met in either group, the authors conclude that "educational strategies should be directed toward optimizing ventilation techniques to match the improvements observed in compression quality." This may include two-rescuer BVM technique, since the authors note "BVM ventilation performed by two rescuers is more effective than when performed by a single individual."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training that addresses mask sealing and airway alignment, the deficiencies the authors observed
#### Constraints
- Feedback devices measuring tidal volume cannot reflect respiratory system physiology such as airway resistance or lung compliance

### Target Learners
- medical students learning bag-valve-mask ventilation

### Target Learning Goals
- delivering effective ventilations within the 500–600 mL tidal volume range during CPR
