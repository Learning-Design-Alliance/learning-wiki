# Combine academic and nonacademic assessments in fellowship selection via a dual-assessment approach on a virtual platform

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that selection processes incorporate both academic and nonacademic evaluation because the two represent distinct skill sets. It states that "a combined evaluation that incorporates both domains may more effectively identify well-rounded candidates for ophthalmology fellowships" and that "This dual-assessment approach can be implemented through a virtual platform, enabling a more holistic and equitable evaluation of applicants." The recommendation rests on the study's findings of higher SJT scores and non-significant correlation between the two assessments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured rubrics for both components
- Interviewers blinded to prior academic performance
- Separate independent evaluators for each domain
#### Constraints
- Retrospective design limits evidence on long-term predictive validity; the authors call for prospective, longitudinal studies to validate the approach

### Target Learners
- ophthalmology fellowship applicants
- medical trainees in entry-level fellowship selection

### Target Learning Goals
- holistic candidate evaluation covering intellectual ability and interpersonal, ethical, and professional qualities
