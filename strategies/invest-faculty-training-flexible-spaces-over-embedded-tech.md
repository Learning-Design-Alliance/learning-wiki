# Spend limited resources on faculty training, flexible technology-free spaces, and instructional staffing rather than embedding expensive classroom technology

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on equivalent learning across a SCALE-UP and a traditional section, the authors recommend that "scarce resources may be best spent on 1) training faculty, 2) providing flexible spaces rather than embedding expensive technology into rooms, and 3) maintaining smaller sections or providing well-trained graduate teaching assistants or undergraduate learning assistants." They note renovations cost substantially less without embedded technology: $30,000 versus $128,000–$192,000 for technology-inclusive SCALE-UP renovations at their institution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must involve carefully designed active learning; the article states carefully designed instruction is a necessary prerequisite to learning regardless of room type.
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that until mechanisms by which such changes produce improved outcomes are better understood, investments of scarce resources should be made cautiously.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nonmajors biology students

### Target Learning Goals
- content knowledge in biotechnology and molecular biology
