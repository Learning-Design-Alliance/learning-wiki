# Using culturally relevant and historically significant events as contexts for teaching physics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article advocates using authentic cultural and scientific contexts — here, Chile's unbeaten 1949 equestrian high-jump world record — as teaching resources for physics. It states that "The use of culturally relevant and historically significant events, such as the Huaso jump, represents a valuable strategy for science education", proposing that this interdisciplinary approach may promote meaningful learning, motivation, and a more comprehensive understanding of natural phenomena.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A real event with available audiovisual records and a known reference quantity (the 2.47 m obstacle height) suitable for quantitative analysis
#### Constraints
- Deviations in acceleration and velocity highlight limitations of simple physical models when applied to real biological systems

### Target Learners
- science students, particularly in the Chilean context where the event is a cultural milestone

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of uniformly accelerated motion, energy, and momentum conservation
- student motivation and engagement
- understanding the scope and validity of physical models
