# Use a portable ultrasound simulator as an alternative to traditional live-instructor FAST skills training when training time and resources are limited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
For deployed mixed-provider teams with limited training time and resources, the article recommends considering a portable US simulator in place of traditional live instructor and model training. In its pilot randomized trial, simulator training produced no statistically significant difference in FAST image acquisition or interpretation skills compared to traditional training, and the authors conclude a portable US simulator appears to provide equivalent skills training in this initial pilot study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulator-based didactics and deliberate practice with real-time feedback must cover the FAST knowledge and hands-on skills; blinded skills assessment with a standardized direct observation tool is needed to verify performance
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a pilot study with a sample size fixed by the number of deployed team members; the authors call for larger studies and measures of short- and long-term clinical performance

### Target Learners
- disaster response team members with limited training time and resources

### Target Learning Goals
- FAST image acquisition and interpretation skills
