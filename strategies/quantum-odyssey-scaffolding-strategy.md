# Scaffold puzzle-based learning with hints, on-screen explanations of puzzles, and links to theory and real-world applications

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends scaffolding visual puzzle play so that trial-and-error does not substitute for understanding. Participants "felt hints and explanations were needed at earlier stages, that is before trial and error, rather than after", and "Above all participants wanted links to theory and real-world applications to be made". The authors responded by adding hints, providing explanation of gameplay alongside the visuals, and writing a complete narrative text for all puzzles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hints should be available when players are stuck to mitigate excessive trial-and-error play
- Puzzle visuals should remain visibly displayed while explanations are provided
#### Constraints
- Without narrative framing, older A-level and HE participants did not see the links to real quantum mechanics and questioned what was being learned

### Target Learners
- secondary school students
- undergraduate students without physics backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- learning quantum computational concepts intuitively
- connecting visual puzzle solutions to underlying quantum theory
