# Introduce a Specialized Kankor with field-of-study-specific scoring as an intermediate bridge toward better admission

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because eliminating Kankor is judged extremely challenging in the short term, the author proposes a transitional design: "To transition gradually, Specialized Kankor may serve as an intermediate bridge to enhance the existing admission processes." It would revise the scoring system by field of study — science-related fields requiring strong mathematics and science knowledge, theology-like fields not — and group fields into main streams so specialized exams are feasible. Questionnaire results from university and high school administrators reportedly show everyone believes Specialized Kankor better identifies candidates' skills and competence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The article recommends categorizing fields of study into main streams first, since offering a specialized Kankor for every existing field of study may be difficult.
#### Constraints
- The article states the current Kankor structure is not the proper medium to identify candidates' competence, and that candidates' uninformed choice of field of study might lead to no admission.

### Target Learners
- Kankor candidates (Afghan high school graduates seeking higher education)

### Target Learning Goals
- Better identification of candidates' skills and competence and allocation of human capital into appropriate fields of study
