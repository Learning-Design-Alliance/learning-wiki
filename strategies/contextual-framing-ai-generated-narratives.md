# Add contextual framing (historical introduction) before AI-generated narrative gameplay

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Expert participant P3 identified that users need to understand the historical and methodological context underlying AI-generated content, noting that "certain expressions that the LLM rightly reads from the original text, have to be seen in its colonial context". The authors call this contextual transparency and recommend that AI-native educational interfaces include framing mechanisms such as introductory content or companion materials. In response, the team added an introductory text explaining Malinowski's 1914 arrival, his four-year immersion, and participant observation methodology.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An AI system transforming historical or culturally sensitive texts needs explicit framing of the source material and the AI's relationship to it
#### Constraints
- The introduction emphasizes that the game can only approximate Malinowski's complex ethnographic experience

### Target Learners
- non-specialist players of AI-native educational games
- undergraduate and graduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical interpretation of AI-generated historical and ethnographic narratives
