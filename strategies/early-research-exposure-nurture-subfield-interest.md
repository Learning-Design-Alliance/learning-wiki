# Engage undergraduates in research early in their academic careers to nurture and shape subfield interest

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends engaging undergraduates in research early, based on its finding that most students who discussed research experiences were entering their final year, with fewer juniors, sophomores, and one freshman contributing. It argues that early exposure stimulates curiosity and shapes interests in specific physics subfields, so that "universities and colleges can nurture students’ interests, encourage exploration of diverse subfields, and lay the foundation for future contributions to the field of physics."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Research opportunities (faculty-led projects, summer programs, internships) accessible to undergraduates before their final year
#### Constraints
- Discussion of research experiences was concentrated among final-year students, suggesting early opportunities were not widely available in the studied settings

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics majors

### Target Learning Goals
- development of interest in specific physics subfields
