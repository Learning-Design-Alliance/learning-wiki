# Secure managerial commitment, train collaborators on PBL and collaboration skills, use a memorandum of agreement, and monitor and evaluate the collaboration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's recommendations state what is critical for making collaboration in PBL implementation work: "Managerial commitment, training of collaborators on PBL and collaboration skills, memorandum of agreement, monitoring and evaluation are critical." It further recommends that formal agreements be in place detailing the roles and responsibilities of collaborators, and that accredited providers of the practical component be educated and trained on PBL and collaboration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Good relationships maintained between academia and healthcare facilities, with formal agreements detailing roles and responsibilities
#### Constraints
- The model has not yet been tested; the authors call for more research to pilot the model and evaluate collaboration at different levels of operations

### Target Learners
- nurse educators, nurse managers and preceptors in nursing education institutions and clinical services

### Target Learning Goals
