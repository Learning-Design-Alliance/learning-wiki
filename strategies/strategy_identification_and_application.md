# Strategy Identification and Application

## Description
This strategy involves explicitly teaching problem-solving strategies and providing opportunities for students to practice using them when they feel 'stuck.' Students are encouraged to be mindful of how they are getting themselves unstuck and to name their process. This helps them develop self-directed learning skills. The teacher checks in to help students name their process by asking questions like, 'How did you get yourself unstuck?' or 'What was your first step? What are you doing now? What might you try next?' As students talk about their process, the teacher adds to a list of strategies that students are using, giving them the language to match what they did so that they now have a strategy they could use in other times of struggle.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit instruction on problem-solving strategies. Opportunities for learners to practice using these strategies. A supportive environment where learners feel comfortable making mistakes and reflecting on their process.
#### Constraints
- May require significant time and effort to develop a repertoire of problem-solving strategies. Some learners may struggle to identify and apply appropriate strategies. Requires consistent reinforcement and modeling.
