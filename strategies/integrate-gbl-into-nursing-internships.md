# Integrate structured, scenario-based game-based learning into clinical nursing internships alongside standard instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating game-based strategies into nursing curricula as a student-centered approach to bridge theory–practice gaps. Implementation replaces instructor-led case discussion and debriefing time with structured gameplay embedded in the existing course, keeping total contact hours identical, and pairs gameplay with clinical practice and theoretical review in each session. The authors state these findings "support the integration of game-based strategies into nursing curricula as a student-centered approach to bridge theory–practice gaps and foster durable self-regulatory capacities."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Game complexity must be matched to students' prior knowledge and cognitive load capacity
- Alignment with curriculum and sufficient intervention duration
- Fidelity monitoring such as session checklists and a structured facilitator guide
#### Constraints
- The article cautions GBL is not a universally effective solution; mixed evidence exists for problem-solving skills and clinical reasoning, and effects may depend on game–learner fit, curriculum integration, and intervention duration

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students in clinical specialty placements, particularly mental health nursing

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-directed learning
- Academic self-efficacy
- Clinical reasoning and therapeutic communication
