Assess and tailor language therapy by comprehension phenotype rather than spoken-language level alone¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that language therapists characterize individuals by language comprehension phenotype in addition to the conventional nonverbal/minimally verbal/verbal categorization, noting that all study participants were verbal "and still fell into the three distinct language com- prehension phenotypes". With a clearer phenotype picture, "language interventions can be more effectively tailored to each individual": command-phenotype individuals may benefit more from exercises focused on color, size, and number modifiers, since syntactic exercises might be too challenging. Improved assessments at the level reached by typical children aged 2 to 4 years could enable earlier recognition, earlier intervention, and better progress tracking.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An assessment capable of placing an individual into one of the three comprehension phenotypes (command, modifier, syntactic), e.g., via parent report or improved comprehension assessments

Constraints¶

  • In the absence of pharmaceutical treatments capable of improving a language phenotype, language therapy remains the primary approach, per the authors
  • All study participants were verbal with normal speech, so the recommendation is grounded in verbal individuals

Target Learners¶

  • verbal children and adolescents with language comprehension deficits across ASD severity levels and other diagnoses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • language comprehension of commands, modifiers, and syntactic structures

Affordances¶

  • Pfs Pfm Memory Recall Language Framework

Claims¶

  • Hierarchical clustering of 15 language comprehension abilities in verbal individuals with language deficits yields three distinct ability clusters: command, modifier, and syntactic [+W]
  • Clustering participants independently yields three language-comprehension phenotypes present at 44.7%, 25.0%, and 30.3% of the sample [+W]
  • Syntactic-phenotype prevalence decreases and command-phenotype prevalence increases with autism severity [+W]
  • Principal component analysis independently separates command, modifier, and syntactic language comprehension items [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Match language exercise difficulty to the child's assessed receptive and expressive phenotype level
