# Implement targeted structural reforms for tourniquet safety training: standardized national training modules, mandatory simulation-based certification, competency reassessment, institutional protocols, and interdisciplinary safety checklists

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose moving beyond generic calls for more training to specific, implementable structures for physician education in pneumatic tourniquet use. They state that "targeted and implementable strategies are required," listing "standardized national training modules, mandatory simulation-based certification before independent tourniquet use, periodic competency reassessment, formal institutional tourniquet safety protocols, and interdisciplinary perioperative safety checklists." These respond to findings that 54.6% of surveyed physicians had no formal training and that knowledge gaps persist even among senior specialists.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured, guideline-based education replacing reliance on informal residency experience
- Competency verification before and after initial independent tourniquet use
#### Constraints
- The article grounds this strategy in self-reported, cross-sectional survey data that cannot establish causal links between knowledge deficits and complications

### Target Learners
- Orthopedic specialists
- Orthopedic residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe pneumatic tourniquet application, including pressure individualization, safe duration, complication recognition, and IVRA-related risk management
