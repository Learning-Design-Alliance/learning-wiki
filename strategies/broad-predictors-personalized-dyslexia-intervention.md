# Use broader baseline cognitive predictors beyond conventional phonological measures to detect at-risk learners and personalize literacy intervention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (5 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building predictive models of intervention responsiveness from broader psychological assessment data, extending beyond phonemic-awareness measures rooted in phonological theory. The authors state that "A predictive model on intervention responsiveness that is trained on broader psychological data allows for more inclusive detection of at -risk learners across a diverse special needs population" regardless of conventional symptomology. Subgroup profiles then support matching intervention to student learning characteristics, including monitoring even children who perform well at baseline.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Baseline cognitive, achievement and/or behavioural assessment data beyond traditional dyslexia measures; a trained predictive model with explainer output and clustering to form actionable subgroup profiles
#### Constraints
- Subgroup sample sizes can be small by chance, limiting conclusive longitudinal analysis; findings rest on an opaque-orthography (English) intervention context and retrospective recruitment

### Target Learners
- Students with dyslexia or at-risk learners in special education

### Target Learning Goals
- Literacy intervention responsiveness and long-term reading and spelling development
