# Use competing-effects problems where the powers of parameters differ to show quick intuition can mislead about which effect dominates

## Description
The article warns that fast-thinking intuition says two opposing effects "are likely to cancel," and that examining the powers in the functional dependence reveals which effect matters more. Its example: artery resistance Z = 8μL/πR⁴, so a 10% radius constriction outweighs a 10% viscosity reduction because "the resistance is inversely proportional to the fourth power of the radius."
