# Implement the teach-and-question assignment with brief in-class training, example videos, partner-finding support, required audio recordings, and scattered instructor feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes how to implement the TQ assignment in a large course: students meet a peer outside class for at least 30 minutes per unit, alternating teacher and questioner roles, with the questioner asking high-level questions such as "How…?," "Why…?," "What would happen if…?". The course dedicated about 15 minutes of class time to a demonstration, provided good and bad example videos, offered an optional partner-finding homework session, required audio recordings for credit, and gave scattered feedback on random snippets to increase accountability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors provide lists of unit learning objectives to guide student TQ sessions
- Students are trained on what constitutes a quality TQ session at the beginning of the semester
- Sessions are audio-recorded and submitted for credit
#### Constraints
- The assignment requires no class time but about 30 minutes of student time outside class per unit
- Students self-select partners, so the design could not assess effects of partnership type

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large general biology courses

### Target Learning Goals
- deep understanding of unit learning objectives across the biology curriculum
- verbal explanation and questioning of course material
