# Use outdoor experiential activities engaging the five senses to activate adult learners' affective domain in environmental education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends rich learning environments built from outdoor environmental activities, social interaction, and activities engaging the five senses in order to develop the affective domain of adult learners. Its findings support this: "OEEE was also observed to be stronger due to the outdoor nature of its applications", and the outdoor programme activated curiosity and anxiety more than the indoor or traditional programmes. Concrete activities included observing the Ida Mountain Forest microclimate, measuring stream water features, snorkel diving to observe marine biodiversity, and catching insects used for biological pest control.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities designed in accordance with Kolb's experiential learning theory, with concrete experience and reflective observation stages facilitated for all groups
#### Constraints
- Behaviour change was excluded from the research scope because identifying it requires a much longer time period than the project allowed

### Target Learners
- adult in-service teachers across 13 specialist teaching fields

### Target Learning Goals
- activation of affective-domain concepts (curiosity, anxiety, interest, motivation) regarding environmental subjects
