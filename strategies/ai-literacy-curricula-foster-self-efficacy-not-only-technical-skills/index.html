Design AI literacy curricula that foster self-efficacy beliefs alongside technical skills to protect against overreliance¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that AI literacy curricula should not focus narrowly on technical skills, since "AI literacy curricula that focus narrowly on technical skills (how to prompt, how to use AI tools effectively) may inadvertently increase dependency". Instead, curricula should also foster self-efficacy beliefs about AI — students' sense that they can think with AI rather than through AI — and build general academic self-efficacy, which the authors argue may be at least as protective as teaching students to use AI well. The recommendation rests on the regression findings that skill-based literacy positively predicted dependency while both self-efficacy beliefs negatively predicted it.
