# Targeted educational initiatives, simulation-based learning and interdisciplinary case discussions to close rare-disease knowledge gaps in paediatric training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends strengthening paediatric training on rare neuromuscular disorders such as DMD. Its conclusion states that "Policymakers and training institutions should prioritise simulation-based learning, interdisciplinary case discussions and the development of clinical guidelines to enhance preparedness across all paediatric practice levels." It also argues targeted educational initiatives can directly improve care quality for children with DMD in Oman and that findings provide an evidence base for curriculum development and national guidelines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Targeted instruction should focus on junior residents, who scored lowest, particularly in management and applied decision-making
#### Constraints
- The evidence base is a cross-sectional survey with a 54.6% response rate; the authors note the design limits the ability to assess knowledge changes over time or infer causality

### Target Learners
- paediatric residents (R1–R4)
- practising paediatricians

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of DMD genetics, clinical and laboratory features, and management
- applied clinical decision-making for rare neuromuscular disorders
