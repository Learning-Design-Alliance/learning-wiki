# Improve international assessments for evaluating instructional practices via panel designs, student-questionnaire practice items in every wave, more refined items, and no unsupported quality scales

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes four concrete improvements to make TIMSS more informative for evaluating instructional practices. These are: strengthening the design "so that the same students are tested more than once", including measures of instructional practices in the international student questionnaire in every wave, refining the under-specified items so they tap specific aspects such as cognitive activation, and not providing instructional-quality scales for which there is no evidence that each item represents quality teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Measures of instructional practices must be included in the international student questionnaire for cross-country examination of relations to achievement.
- Refined items are needed because assessing the actual quality of instructional activities requires more refined items than current TIMSS questionnaires.
#### Constraints
- TIMSS data are anonymized, so linking to prior achievement data is generally not possible except in special cases like Swedish TIMSS 2015.

### Target Learners
- eighth-grade mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- mathematics achievement
- evaluation of instructional practices
