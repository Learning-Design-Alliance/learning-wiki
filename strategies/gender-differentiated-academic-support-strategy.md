# Gender-differentiated academic support: encourage female students to verbalise their studies and explicitly support male students' orientation to study

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the finding that female students were more confident in grades, studying and attendance but less confident in verbalising, the authors propose differentiated support. They ask whether "the female students be encouraged to talk more about their subject of study with academic staff in tutorials, seminars, lectures and other conversations, and should they be encouraged to worry less", and answer that "male students should be explicitly encouraged, guided and supported to be more like their female counterparts in the approach they take to their studies". The strategy targets educators working with student groups rather than changing personality itself.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- It operates at the educator level because "personality itself is not malleable, certainly over the short term, within an educational establishment".
#### Constraints
- Gender alone did not weigh significantly on the ABC subscales in the regression models, its impact being "overshadowed by the impact of the personality variables".

### Target Learners
- male and female undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- orientation to academic study, confidence in study behaviours, verbalising confidence about one's studies
