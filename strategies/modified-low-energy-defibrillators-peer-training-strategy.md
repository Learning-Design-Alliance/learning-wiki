# Modified low-energy defibrillators, clip-on electrodes and nearby senior supervision to enable safe peer-led defibrillation training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
To make peer-led defibrillation training safe without direct supervision, the authors modified equipment and arranged supervision: "two defibrillators were bought and modified to be able to only give a 2 Joule shock, whatever energy was selected", matching the therapeutic dose for a 500 gram baby. Clip-on electrodes replaced hand-held paddles, defibrillators were clearly marked as non-therapeutic and kept locked away, and a senior doctor was always within 20 metres of the room. This allowed peer-led groups to practise on mannequins safely.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A senior doctor always within 20 metres of the room when the defibrillators were being used
- Defibrillators marked clearly as not for therapeutic use and kept in a locked room away from clinical areas
#### Constraints
- No level of shock would be completely free of danger, as the article states

### Target Learners
- fifth-year medical students learning advanced resuscitation

### Target Learning Goals
- safe and effective manual defibrillation
- advanced resuscitation per ILCOR guidelines
