# Adopt bivectors gradually by planting seeds during traditional cross product instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because a wholesale switch to bivectors is socially impractical — students must engage with a literature that uses cross products — the authors recommend incremental integration: "we could begin by planting seeds of the bivector description during traditional cross product instruction." Concrete moves include constructing an oriented tile as an intermediate step to the cross product, drawing torque and angular momentum as "decorated" vectors, labeling planar torques τxy rather than τz, and mentioning the bivector description in upper-level classes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Individual instructors introduce pieces gradually rather than switching all at once
#### Constraints
- Teaching the vector and bivector languages simultaneously comes across to students as repetitive and confusing

### Target Learners
- introductory through upper-level undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the cross product and right-hand rule via oriented tiles
- building conceptual foundations for later bivector formalism
