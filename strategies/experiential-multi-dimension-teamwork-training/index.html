Use experiential, multi-dimension teamwork training activities rather than lecturing alone¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 quant-synthesis), q1–q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the moderator findings, the authors recommend that teamwork training incorporate experiential activities providing active ways of learning and practising teamwork, and that interventions target more than one teamwork dimension. Suggested activities include workshop exercises such as working through case studies, critiquing video vignettes of optimal versus suboptimal teamwork, discussing and setting teamwork-related goals and action plans, simulations of likely team tasks, and in-situ team reviews/briefings before, during, and after task execution.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training should involve active, experiential activities and target at least two teamwork dimensions to improve teamwork behaviors

Constraints¶

  • Didactic education alone did not significantly improve teamwork behaviors (d = 0.19) in the moderator analysis

Target Learners¶

  • team members in health care, aviation, military, academic, industry, and laboratory settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • teamwork behaviors including goal setting, communication, coordination, reflection, conflict management, and social support

Claims¶

  • Workshop, simulation, and team-review methods significantly improve teamwork behaviors, while the pooled effect of didactic education alone was not significant [+W]
  • Teamwork training showed significant effects on teamwork behaviors when two or more teamwork dimensions were targeted, but not when only one was [+W]
  • Teamwork training interventions produce significant positive effects on team performance in controlled studies [+W]
  • Teamwork training interventions produce significant positive effects on teamwork behaviors in controlled studies [+W]
  • The review attributes implementation-strategies effectiveness to experiential, interactive, and patient-centered learning methods alongside traditional lectures [+W]
  • Adding simulation to online patient-safety training improved nursing students' patient safety competency, but adding classroom teaching to clinical simulation did not further improve teamwork attitudes [+W]
