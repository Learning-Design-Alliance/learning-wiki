# Provide ready-made integrated conceptual and quantitative problems as homework in graduate core courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
Integrated conceptual and quantitative problems are homework problems in which students solve quantitative problems like those already assigned, but which also include 'conceptual questions that help students to contemplate qualitative implications of the quantitative procedures involved'. In this article, the researchers proposed assigning such problems in graduate core courses, and most instructors said they were willing to assign them if good problems were made available, since they lack time to construct them. The strategy targets the gap the study documents between algorithmic calculation skill and conceptual recognition of what the mathematics means.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Good integrated conceptual and quantitative problems must be made available to instructors, who admit they do not have the time to make such questions
#### Constraints
- The textbooks currently used in the core courses do not include such problems
- Some instructors were skeptical about including conceptual questions, concerned that homework assignments are already long and that conceptual questions may increase the time taken to finish the assignment and increase graduate student workload

### Target Learners
- first-year physics graduate students enrolled in core courses

### Target Learning Goals
- mathematical sense making and drawing conceptual inferences from quantitative tools
- connecting the mathematics and physics concepts
