# Future help research should use specified help measures, distinguish help-giving from help-receiving, and test sex differences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend refining the measurement of adolescent help in future work. They state "future work may need a more specified measure (e.g., helping with academic work might be quite different in terms of intimacy and bonding than helping with interpersonal conflicts)", that research on school-based status and achievement "may also want to distinguish between help‐receiving and help‐giving", and that further research is needed on whether effects differ for boys and girls, given help is more salient in girls' friendships.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal peer nomination designs capable of separating help-giving and help-receiving perspectives
#### Constraints
- The present study measured help only from the receiver's perspective and in a broad sense, and was limited to the classroom setting

### Target Learners
- early adolescents

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding help exchange in peer relations and its role in friendship
