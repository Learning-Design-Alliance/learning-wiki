# Explain the incremental knowledge-building journey and make assessment rules of the game explicit to students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that the incremental approach to knowledge-building, and its intrinsic connection with mathematics, be explained to students so they can "see" the learning journey ahead. They further suggest that making the underpinning "rules of the game" more visible and explicit at every level could lead to enhanced learning and improved performance, especially for cue-sensitive students who strategically direct study efforts based on perceived assessment signals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment demands that vary by level, so the signals to be made explicit differ across years of study
#### Constraints
- Recommended based on a single-lecturer case study with limited generalisability; not tested empirically in this article

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and postgraduate physics students, especially cue-sensitive students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding what is valued at each stage of study; directing study effort appropriately
