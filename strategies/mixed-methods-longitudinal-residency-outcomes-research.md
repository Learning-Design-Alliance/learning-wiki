# Use both qualitative and quantitative research designs, including longitudinal follow-up, to clarify which aspects of residency training best prepare doctors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's research agenda calls for diversifying the evidence base on residency training and patient outcomes. It states that "both qualitative and quantitative research designs are needed to clarify which aspects of residency training best prepare doctors to deliver a high quality of care." It further recommends research on the training background of practicing physicians and prospective longitudinal follow-up of residents after finishing their residencies, noting that multicenter longitudinal databases exist but lack patient outcomes for individual doctors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration between researchers and clinicians and diversification of research perspectives to enrich clinical, health services and medical education research fields
#### Constraints
- Investigating causal factors that explain the relationship between residency training and patient outcomes is described as difficult

### Target Learners
- medical education researchers
- health care services researchers
- residency program evaluators

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating the effect of residency training on patient outcomes during and after training
- identifying causal factors linking training aspects to care quality
