# Use the QKD-entanglement QuILT as homework after lecture on entanglement, in class with group work, or as think-pair-share/clicker questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (3 design), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend three flexible implementations after students learn entanglement: (1) assign the tutorial as homework so instructors "circumvent having to utilize in-class time to teach about QKD via lecture-based instruction"; (2) use it in part or in full in class with students working in small groups while the instructor circulates; (3) frame tutorial questions as think-pair-share or clicker questions so instructors can obtain feedback on class difficulties and fine-tune instruction. The tutorial is designed to allow instructors flexibility in their preferred instructional methods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should first be taught entanglement including Bell states, since the QKD protocol relies on entanglement
#### Constraints
- If used in class with limited time, only parts of the tutorial may be covered based on instructor preference

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics majors
- interdisciplinary undergraduate science and engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding quantum key distribution concepts relevant to quantum information science

## Claims

- [Students in both QM and interdisciplinary QCQI courses performed comparably on the eavesdropper question after the QuILT regardless of whether they had prior lecture-based instruction on QKD](../claims/comparable-performance-regardless-of-qkd-lecture.md) [+W]
- [Students' QKD concept scores showed only marginal changes one month after the QuILT, and performance on the far-transfer Bell-state question rose from 72% to 90% in one group measured before and after, with no comparison group](../claims/one-month-retention-qkd-quilt.md) [+W]
- [In classes that received in-class lecture on QKD, students' performance improved significantly from pre-test to post-test after engaging with the QuILT, with large effect sizes on the eavesdropper-detection question](../claims/quilt-entanglement-qkd-pre-post-gains.md) [+W]
- [After lecture-based instruction, students struggled with how eavesdropper detection works in entanglement-based QKD, and with conditional probability reasoning about correlated measurement outcomes](../claims/student-difficulties-qkd-eavesdropper-conditional-probability.md) [+W]
- [Upper-level undergraduates' average scores rose from 56% on the pretest to 91% on the posttest after working through the QKD QuILT](../claims/quilt-qkd-prepost-average-score-improvement.md) [+W]
- [Upper-level undergraduate students show large pretest-to-posttest gains on quantum key distribution concepts after working through the QKD QuILT](../claims/qkd-quilt-pretest-posttest-gains.md) [+W]
