# Use DSJ as an objective waypoint metric marking transition from simulated learning to supervised practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose DSJ as a metric giving trainees and trainers "an objective measure of their progress over which could be used in addition to other metrics" based on image data, signifying "the transition from simulated learning to supervised practice". They recommend integrating ultrasound simulation into curricula and promoting self-directed learning, and combining DSJ with traditional assessments such as logbooks, OSATs and image audit rather than replacing them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- DSJ measurement during simulated scanning tasks
- Combination with other assessment metrics; a single metric alone is unlikely to represent experienced skill
#### Constraints
- DSJ alone cannot be assumed to equate to clinical competence
- Further work is required to correlate DSJ with traditional training tools

### Target Learners
- obstetric ultrasound trainees
- ultrasound trainers and programme directors

### Target Learning Goals
- tracking technical skill progression through ultrasound training
