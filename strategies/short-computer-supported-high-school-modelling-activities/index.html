Implement modelling as short, computer-simulation-supported activities at the high school level¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on its moderator analyses, the article concludes that "the setting that produces the most optimal learning effects is short activities conducted at a high school level supported by computer simulations as a medium for modelling". The strategy directs curriculum designers toward brief, simulation-supported modelling tasks for high school mathematics rather than semester-long treatments. The article also recommends balancing provided guidance, since overly detailed procedure descriptions without room to explore are not effective inquiry.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Careful inquiry planning coupled with availability of interactive media
  • a balance between what students input and the degree of provided guidance needs to be established and controlled

Constraints¶

  • The article cautions the medium itself will not generate learning; concepts reside in what students actually do and experience

Target Learners¶

  • high school mathematics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mathematics achievement through modelling activities
  • developing modelling competencies

Claims¶

  • Computer simulations yield a slightly higher achievement effect than pen-and-paper modelling activities [+W]
  • Mathematical modelling produces a moderate positive effect on high school and college students' mathematics achievement compared with traditional instruction [+W]
  • High school students show a larger achievement effect from mathematical modelling than college students [+W]
  • Shorter modelling treatments produce larger achievement effects than semester-length treatments [+W]
