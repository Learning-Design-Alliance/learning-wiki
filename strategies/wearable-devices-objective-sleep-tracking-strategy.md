# Use wearable devices as an objective alternative to self-report for tracking student sleep in academic-performance research

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends wearable wrist watches or trackers as an objective way to estimate sleep activities that traditionally relied on subjective self-report questionnaires. Devices worn at home or in dormitories offer high ecological validity, and device-derived measures apply "the very same standards" across participants. The authors state that "using electronic devices, especially wearable wrist watches or trackers (e.g., Fitbit and the one used here), can be considered an alternative for researchers to investigate relationships between academic performance and sleep activity".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants must wear the device consistently across the tracking period; the study relied on approximately two weeks of wear.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the specific in-house questionnaire used alongside the device was simplified and may not capture the multi-facets of sleep; comprehensive questionnaires are recommended for future studies.

### Target Learners
- college students

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance (GPA) prediction research
- sleep monitoring in educational settings
