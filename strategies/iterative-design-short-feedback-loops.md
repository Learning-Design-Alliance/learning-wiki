# Use iterative design with short feedback loops and multiple user-feedback methods during product development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The guide recommends working closely with users during development through iterative design, defined via the OET guide as "the process of using short feedback loops to define assumptions, create prototypes, and obtain early user feedback". Concrete methods listed include creating a Minimum Viable Product and showing it to users early, conducting focus groups and interviews, utilizing A/B testing to decide between features, and reviewing product analytics to see where people get stuck. Suggested channels for finding users include education social-media chats, help tickets, and compensating teachers for their time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to users (students, parents, teachers, administrators) willing to give feedback, and channels such as #edtechbridge chats or help-ticket data to reach them
#### Constraints
- Teacher time should be valued: the guide advises listening to teachers, offering free or reduced-price versions, and compensating them when possible

### Target Learners
- ed tech product developers
- K-12 students and teachers as product users

### Target Learning Goals
- improving product usability and fit to learner needs through user feedback
