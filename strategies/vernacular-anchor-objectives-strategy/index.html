Use a vernacular-language anchor character to state objectives and guide learners¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In each learning scenario, a familiar fairy-tale figure (the fairy) acts as an anchor that states the learning objectives and outcomes to students in their own vernacular (Marathi), connects prior knowledge to new concepts, and provides a guidance system of rows and signs. The article reports this creates curiosity and sustains attention, while activity instructions are also given in the vernacular so learners understand what to do after observing the scenario.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An anchor figure familiar and appealing to the children
  • Activity instructions delivered in the learners' vernacular medium

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • Young primary school learners studying through a regional language medium

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding learning objectives and continuously engaging with English learning scenarios

Claims¶

  • Anchoring activities in familiar contexts, such as photos of the children's own school, immediately engaged every child [+W]
  • Pre-test diagnosis showed most grade-3 students had not attained writing and reading competencies in English after grade 2 [+W]
  • MSBLP remedial teaching produced significant gain-score differences over traditional teaching in English skills among grade-3 primary students [+W]
  • The post-test difference between groups on the Match the Shapes competency was not statistically significant [~W]
