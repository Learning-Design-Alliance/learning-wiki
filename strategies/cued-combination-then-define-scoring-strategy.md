# Score vocabulary by cued combination, then ask the subject to define the words failed, to add words to the score

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author describes an administration routine in which the vocabulary test is given "by cued combination as above", after which "The subject is later asked to define any words he has failed to combine, which adds a few words to his score." Because a subject who knows a word can complete it but cannot guess it if unknown, the completion stage screens for usable word knowledge, and the definition stage recovers additional credit from words known definitionally but hard to combine.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cue sentences must give an appropriate context for the word to be tested; guessing must not supply the missing part
- Scoring must distinguish responses by combination from responses by definition
#### Constraints
- Most words seem harder to combine by this method than to define under Terman's liberal standards, which allow using the word in a sentence; combination testing also tests spelling

### Target Learners
- patients with aphasia or word-finding difficulty
- test subjects in vocabulary assessment

### Target Learning Goals
- vocabulary knowledge assessment
- word meaning

### Affordances
- Word Use Versus Definition Dissociation
