# Integrate simulation-based education into traditional lecture-based teaching during the clinical apprenticeship phase of medical imaging courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a complementary teaching model in which theoretical knowledge is delivered by traditional didactic lectures while the clinical apprenticeship phase uses simulation-based education. The authors state this integration "bridges theoretical knowledge with clinical practice seamlessly" and propose extending it toward a hybrid model combining SBE with online platforms and virtual reality, structured on a "theory-simulation-practice" framework.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Same faculty team and standardized lesson plans across formats to ensure pedagogical consistency
- Simulation facilities, trained standardized patients, and additional preparation time
#### Constraints
- The article notes SBE requires 40% more preparation time, dedicated simulation facilities, and SP training costs, and calls for cost-benefit analyses

### Target Learners
- non-radiology specialty medical undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- medical imaging knowledge and image interpretation
- practical clinical skills
- communication and teamwork competencies
