# Faculty stepping out of the room as a tool to increase small-group discussion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend deliberately scheduling periods of faculty absence within small-group case discussions to promote student-led participation. As a result of their findings, 'we now encourage faculty to use stepping out of the room as a tool to increase small group discussion,' and clerkships 'may choose to have more small group sessions without faculty presence, or with intermittent faculty presence.' Faculty give scripted instructions before leaving for a standardized amount of time while students continue the case discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty provide scripted instructions and leave for a standardized amount of time, as in the study protocol
#### Constraints
- Findings are based on self-reported perceptions within one Family Medicine clerkship; the authors call for further research into learning outcomes such as test scores or clinical performance

### Target Learners
- Third-year medical students in clerkship small groups

### Target Learning Goals
- Case-based clinical reasoning, student participation, and group discussion quality
