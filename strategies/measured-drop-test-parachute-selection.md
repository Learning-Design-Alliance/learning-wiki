# Select parachutes by measured drop tests comparing descent speed and stability before flight

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because students bought different parachutes, "they decided to choose the best one by calculating their speeds with a constant mass". The recipe: launch each parachute from a fixed altitude with a constant object mass, film from far away to avoid parallax error, divide the height into fixed steps, compute per-step speeds and their arithmetic mean, and compare mean speed and standard deviation across parachutes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A tall building with many equal-height storeys and permission from the manager
- a fixed constant mass for all drops
- filming far from the building and throwing so the initial non-steady portion of descent is minimized
#### Constraints
- The first part of the descent is not at constant speed, so launches were performed to limit its contribution

### Target Learners
- seventeen-year-old high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental design
- descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation)
- engineering selection decisions
