# Emphasize systematic use of behavioural marker systems for NTS assessment in HEMS, including fatigue management

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that HEMS organizations move beyond ad hoc assessment toward systematic use of behavioural marker systems for non-technical skills. Its conclusion states that "The use of behavioural marker systems systematically in HEMS should be emphasized" and that "Systematic assessment of NTS, including fatigue management, should be a future focus area in HEMS". This responds to the finding that assessment frequency lags training frequency for all professional groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment tools suited to the HEMS setting; the article notes AeroNOTS has only recently been developed and is not yet fully validated
#### Constraints
- Fatigue and stress management may be difficult to detect and rate unless extreme symptoms are displayed, and there is no consensus definition of fatigue or standardized survey instrument for EMS worker groups

### Target Learners
- physicians, HEMS crew members and pilots in helicopter emergency medical services

### Target Learning Goals
- assessment of non-technical skills, including fatigue management
