# Have surgical residents regularly review and assess their own and others' procedure videos as part of training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating video-based assessment of surgical procedures into resident training so residents can self-assess. It argues that if residents "regularly review and assess their own surgical procedures as well as those of others through video analysis, they might learn more quickly and enhance patient care", and that scaling CVS scoring creates feedback opportunities that may speed progress and mitigate bile duct injury.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to unedited, anonymized recordings of laparoscopic procedures and an online video assessment platform on which to score them
#### Constraints
- Scoring videos using assessment tools is inherently time-consuming, which the authors note motivates future AI support

### Target Learners
- surgical residents learning laparoscopic cholecystectomy

### Target Learning Goals
- attainment of the critical view of safety
- faster progress along the operative learning curve
