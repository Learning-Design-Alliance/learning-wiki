# Faculty in both departments should give physics applications in math courses, present math more precisely and organized in physics courses, and be explicit about notation and convention differences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article closes its survey with students' own suggestions to faculty about helping students understand material common to both subjects. Students suggested math professors "give real-world and physics applications" and suggested physics professors "be more organized with the pr esentation of material, be more clear/precise/rigorous with the math." To both, they suggested being clear about notation and convention differences, such as factors of 2π in Fourier transforms, and providing resources on math/physics connections. The authors suggest increased faculty awareness of these differences could facilitate knowledge transfer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must be aware of the differences in presentation, rigor, and conventions between mathematics and physics courses.
#### Constraints
- Suggestions come from a small qualitative sample of 20 survey respondents at one institution and reflect student perceptions rather than tested interventions.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate mathematics and physics majors, including double majors and majors-with-minors

### Target Learning Goals
- applying mathematical knowledge in physics contexts
- knowledge transfer between mathematics and physics
