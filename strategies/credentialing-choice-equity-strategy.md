# Use examination-choice factor evidence to promote equitable access to preferred recertification assessment options

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that certifying bodies should use evidence on factors associated with examination choice to meet credentialing needs: "Understanding these factors is crucial for meeting credentialing expectations and promoting equitable access to preferred assessment options." Because choice patterns differ by age, gender, race, specialty, and geography, programs can use these findings to ensure PAs can access their preferred format. The authors also recommend qualitative research to uncover self-reported reasons for choices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administrative and profile data on demographic, practice, and prior-examination characteristics of certificants
#### Constraints
- The study did not assess self-reported reasons for choosing one assessment over the other, technology barriers, or whether some PAs simply missed the PANRE-LA application deadline

### Target Learners
- Physician associates/assistants maintaining board certification

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintenance of certification and recertification assessment participation
