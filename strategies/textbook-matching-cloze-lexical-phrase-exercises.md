# Direct textbook exercises at form and meaning of lexical phrases using matching and cloze tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the occurrence of chunks in discourse is not sufficient for noticing, the article recommends that "textbooks include exercises focusing on lexical phrases, such as matching and cloze tasks where learners' attention is directed to form and meaning of authentic patterns". This addresses students' slow development of chunk competence and the failure of mere exposure. It complements classroom time being too limited to explicitly teach a vast number of formulaic strings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Exercises must direct learners' attention to both form and meaning of authentic patterns.
#### Constraints
- The article notes there are different ways to motivate chunk learning but it is unpredictable how effective they are for particular students.

### Target Learners
- EFL learners, including low-intermediate tertiary students

### Target Learning Goals
- noticing and retention of formulaic sequences (multiword expressions)
