Address the access gap before depth stratification, and use examination reform as the primary lever¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's policy findings prescribe sequencing and lever selection: "the access gap and the Python–C++ trade-off are distinct problems needing distinct responses", to be addressed "in sequence, since depth stratification matters only once access is substantially closed". Because national examinations are the most powerful lever, "Intentional exam reform toward Python and AI-relevant tasks" can produce rapid system-wide shifts without curriculum revision, as Poland demonstrates.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Identification of the system's governance archetype to select the transferable mechanism

Constraints¶

  • No single mechanism transfers wholesale across governance contexts
  • Exam-led transitions are less predictable than decree-based ones

Target Learners¶

  • Upper secondary students across all tracks

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Equitable access to programming and AI literacy at adequate depth
