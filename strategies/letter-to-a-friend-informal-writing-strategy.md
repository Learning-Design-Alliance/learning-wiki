# Use an ungraded letter-to-a-friend writing assignment as a low-stakes, high-reward formative assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students write an informal letter to a friend explaining the most interesting concept learned in class, defining key terms and providing examples, without concern for spelling or grammar. The article calls it "a low stakes assignment with high reward": it lets students "engage in reflection and analysis without the stress and anxiety of a formal, graded assignment", and grading entails a simple pass or fail while letting the instructor gauge understanding. It can also serve as a formative assessment of content strengths and weaknesses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Letters are typed, at least one double-spaced page and no more than two pages, focusing on content rather than length
#### Constraints
- A majority of students found the assignment too vague regarding which topics to focus on

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in lower-division courses

### Target Learning Goals
- elaborative processing of course content
- self-explanation and summarisation
- metacognitive awareness of understanding
