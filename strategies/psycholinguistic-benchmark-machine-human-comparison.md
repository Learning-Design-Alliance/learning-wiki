# Benchmark machine and human learners with a battery of psycholinguistic tests

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends constructing a cognitive benchmark of psycholinguistic tests targeting the two developmental puzzles: bootstrapping and developmental trajectories. Tests should "satisfy three conditions: they should be valid (measure the construct under study as opposed to something else), re- liable (with a good signal to noise ratio), and administrable (to adults, children and computers alike)". Machines need task interfaces to extract responses equivalent to human ones.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Task interfaces supplementing learning algorithms so responses can be extracted for each test
- Removal of biases or knowledge of the desired response from instructions, apparatus and interfaces
#### Constraints
- The benchmark targets Marr's computational/informational level and does not assess biological plausibility

### Target Learners
- human infants, human adults, and computational language-learning systems

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring state of linguistic knowledge across development and across linguistic structures
