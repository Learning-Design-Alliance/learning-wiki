# Use biologically primary contexts, guided play and exploration to bridge the evolutionary mismatch and support secondary learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The paper recommends using biologically primary contexts for presenting secondary information to increase motivation and performance, citing Lespiau and Tricot. It also suggests that "Learner engagement in a guided social play or exploration (traditional means of primary learning) could also facilitate cognitive mechanisms necessary for effective learning secondary knowledge in a more motivated and enjoyable way", bridging the evolutionary mismatch between primary-learning activities and those needed for secondary learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities remain supplementary vehicles to enhance explicit instruction rather than replacements for it
#### Constraints
- The traditional evolutionary approach assigns unguided exploratory or play-based activities diminished roles, mostly as supplementary instructional vehicles

### Target Learners
- school and higher-education learners acquiring secondary knowledge such as literacy, numeracy and scientific skills

### Target Learning Goals
- increased motivation and performance when learning biologically secondary content
