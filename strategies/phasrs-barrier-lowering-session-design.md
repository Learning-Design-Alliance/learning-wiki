# Lower participation barriers with presenter slide sets, discussion questions, and opening personal check-ins

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Three concrete session-design moves for online discussion groups: (1) leaders prepare slide sets so that students who did not read or fully understand the article can still contribute, making "participation barriers for the rest of the members of the group as low as possible"; (2) facilitators pepper slides with discussion questions and welcome interruptions, targeting about half presentation and half discussion time; (3) the first several minutes of each session are devoted to personal check-ins and informal chat to build relationships in an online format.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator who switches fluidly between presentation and discussion modes rather than delivering an extended monologue
- Video-chat platform and shared scheduling documents
#### Constraints
- The article warns slide sets can tempt facilitators into extended monologues; interruptions and discussion questions are the stated countermeasure

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in online or hybrid courses

### Target Learning Goals
- active participation in scientific discussion
- community-building in online settings
