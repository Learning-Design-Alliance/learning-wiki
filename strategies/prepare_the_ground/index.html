Prepare the Ground¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Preparing the ground is the deliberate work done before a difficult conversation — especially about race, identity, or injustice — to make that conversation productive rather than harmful. It includes building relational trust over time, co-constructing discussion agreements, establishing a shared factual and conceptual vocabulary, and surfacing participants' prior beliefs so the dialogue starts from common ground rather than ambush.

Design Implications¶

Difficult dialogues collapse when participants lack trust in the facilitator and each other, or when they lack the conceptual language to name what is happening Race conversations stall without facilitation structures and shared norms. [+M]. Preparation shifts the cognitive and emotional load of the conversation away from the moment of conflict: participants who have already agreed on norms and acquired basic vocabulary can spend their attention on the content of the disagreement rather than on managing threat. This is a form of Cognitive Load Management applied to affective load — reducing the extraneous demands (fear of saying the wrong thing, uncertainty about rules) that otherwise consume working capacity during charged exchanges.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Time invested in relationship-building before the high-stakes conversation, not simultaneously with it
  • Co-constructed community agreements (not facilitator-imposed rules), revisited and revisable
  • A shared vocabulary for the topic — e.g., distinguishing prejudice, discrimination, and systemic racism — so participants can describe experiences without relitigating definitions mid-dialogue
  • Facilitator credibility: demonstrated willingness to engage with the topic themselves, not just referee others
  • A mechanism for surfacing prior beliefs and emotional states in advance (Check-In, anonymous pre-surveys, Activation of prior knowledge)

Constraints¶

  • Trust built in a single preparatory session rarely survives genuine conflict; if agreements are not tested and repaired after smaller ruptures, they fail when it matters [-W]
  • Over-preparation can become avoidance: groups that endlessly negotiate norms without ever engaging the topic use preparation as deferral [~W]
  • Agreements framed as "safety" (protection from discomfort) can suppress the productive discomfort that race dialogue requires; reframing toward "bravery" with accountability better supports learning [~M]
  • Participants who experience the topic as personally harmful (e.g., targeted students) may need opt-out or alternative pathways, not just preparation

Implementation Variability¶

  • Sustained model: norms and trust built across a semester-long course, with race dialogue embedded in an existing sequence of increasingly risky exchanges
  • Workshop model: intensive preparation compressed into a pre-dialogue session — agreements, vocabulary primer, and a low-stakes practice conversation on a less charged topic
  • Scaffolded disclosure: participants first write or discuss experiences in identity-affinity or small groups before whole-group dialogue, reducing exposure risk
  • Pre-reading with response: shared texts establish common factual ground and give participants an object to discuss instead of each other

Target Learners¶

  • Mixed-identity groups where participants enter with unequal stakes and unequal knowledge about race Race conversations stall without facilitation structures and shared norms. [+M]
  • Learners with low prior exposure to structured disagreement, who conflate conflict with relationship failure
  • Less necessary — though still beneficial — for groups with established norms and prior successful dialogue history

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Perspective-taking and empathy across identity difference
  • Conceptual understanding of systemic and structural accounts of racial inequality
  • Discourse skills: engaging disagreement without personalizing it
  • Metacognitive awareness of one's own emotional responses and defensive reactions

Instructions¶

  1. Establish relationships and baseline trust through low-stakes Collaborative Learning tasks before any charged content is introduced.
  2. Co-construct discussion agreements with participants; distinguish explicitly between comfort and growth, and agree on repair procedures for when agreements are broken.
  3. Build shared vocabulary through assigned readings or mini-lectures so definitional debates do not consume dialogue time (Advance Organizers help structure this).
  4. Surface prior beliefs and emotional states via anonymous pre-surveys or Check-In routines; use them to anticipate flashpoints.
  5. Run a low-stakes practice dialogue on a moderately charged topic, then debrief the process itself before escalating to race.
  6. During the actual dialogue, refer back to the agreements; afterward, debrief and repair, treating the conversation as evidence for revising the ground rules.

Related Strategies¶

  • Activating Prior Knowledge — surfacing existing beliefs before new content is a core preparation move
  • Establishing Discussion Norms — the agreements component of ground preparation
  • Facilitating Difficult Dialogues — the in-conversation counterpart to this pre-conversation work
  • Prepare teachers and students with awareness, vocabulary, and facilitation knowledge before implementing Socratic Circles

Examples¶

  • Arao & Clemens' "brave space" redesign — a widely used exercise in which a class critiques a standard "safe space" ruleset (e.g., "agree to disagree") and rewrites each agreement to name what participation actually requires, building ownership of norms before charged content begins.
  • Teaching Tolerance / Learning for Justice — "Critical Practices for Anti-bias Education" framework, which sequences community-building and identity work before explicit anti-bias content.
  • Intergroup Dialogue programs (University of Michigan model) — semester-length curricula that dedicate the opening weeks to relationship-building and shared vocabulary before structured race dialogues.
