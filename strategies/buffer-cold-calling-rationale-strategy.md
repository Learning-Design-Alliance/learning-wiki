# Instructors should explain the rationale for active learning practices and buffer cold calling with peer discussion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors reduce active-learning anxiety by being transparent and deliberate about implementation. It states instructors should "be more intentional about presenting students with their rationale for active learning use," and for cold calling specifically, talk with students about why it is used and let students discuss answers with classmates before being called on to buffer feelings of being singled out. It also suggests daily use may desensitize students to the anxiety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must be willing to explain their rationale for active learning practices to students, perhaps informed by surveys or feedback forms about how practices cause anxiety.
#### Constraints
- The study did not examine interventions that might help anxious students, so the impact of these coping strategies was not tested in this article.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large introductory STEM lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing classroom anxiety while maintaining active learning participation and persistence in the major
