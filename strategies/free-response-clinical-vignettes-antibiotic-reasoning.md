# Use free-response clinical vignettes rather than multiple-choice questions to simulate real-life antibiotic decision making

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy assesses and teaches therapeutic reasoning by presenting case-based vignettes with free-text answers instead of multiple-choice options, so learners must explain their choices and can see that several antibiotic choices may be acceptable. The authors chose this format "to resemble real life decision making in infectious diseases" and note vignettes were deliberately designed with prescribing conundrums such as acute renal failure, drug allergies, and immunocompromised state to help learners navigate the PEST algorithm.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A scoring rubric, built by consensus of infectious disease physicians, that accepts several correct antibiotic choices per vignette
- Time within the session for pre- and post-assessment and written feedback
#### Constraints
- The authors note the rubric they used may be biased towards a post-intervention improvement

### Target Learners
- Internal medicine interns
- Early clinical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- Therapeutic reasoning in antibiotic selection
- Navigating prescribing conundrums in real clinical scenarios
