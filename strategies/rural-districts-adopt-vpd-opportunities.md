# Rural districts can take advantage of virtual professional development opportunities for their science teachers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the instructional supports in this study were delivered online, the authors argue these resources can reach science teachers regardless of location. They conclude that "rural districts can take advantage of VPD opportunities for their teachers", especially where recruiting science teachers or providing content-specific in-service PD is challenging. The strategy pairs group VPD sessions with embedded virtual mentoring and coaching, including real-time feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Virtual delivery infrastructure such as the APXIS platform (laptop, external video camera, video-conferencing and scheduling platforms) and trained coaches
#### Constraints
- VMC-RTF dosage in this study was limited to two 30-minute sessions due to Hurricane Harvey delays; the authors suggest more frequent VMC-RTF might yield further pedagogical change

### Target Learners
- Fifth-grade science teachers in rural or remote districts

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving science instructional practices, including scaffolding, leveled questioning, and collaborative grouping
