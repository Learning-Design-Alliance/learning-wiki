# Strengthen training actions on physical activity advice for oncology health professionals, especially nurses, via continuing education and congresses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that scientific societies and decision-makers build structured training in physical activity advice into oncology professional development, prioritizing nurses, who showed lower knowledge and lower access to congress-based learning. It states participants "thought they need more information in order to recommend PA for their patients" and proposes continuing education as the starting point for dismantling advising barriers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must address both knowledge of benefits across the cancer care pathway and perceived patient-safety barriers reported by clinicians.
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single-institution cross-sectional survey whose results, per the authors, cannot be generalized across or outside the country; the recommendation rests on self-reported knowledge and views, not on tested training outcomes.

### Target Learners
- oncology physicians
- oncology nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of physical activity benefits across the cancer care pathway
- ability to advise patients on physical activity safely
