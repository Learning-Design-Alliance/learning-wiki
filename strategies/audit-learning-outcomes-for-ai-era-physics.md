# Audit which conceptual and mathematical skills should form the desired learning outcomes of physics courses in a future where physicists solve problems through dialog with AI systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author recommends that physics education respond to GPT-4-level performance not only through academic-integrity concerns but by re-examining course goals. He argues that, like an integral table in the age of computer algebra, "much of our current curricula may be misaligned with a future in which physicists solve problems through innovative prompting and dialog with an AI system," while students still need their own intuitive grasp of basic physics concepts. The recommendation is therefore a renewed audit of intended learning outcomes rather than a specific instructional recipe.

#### Requirements

#### Constraints

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students at universities

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of Newtonian mechanics
- mathematical skills in introductory physics
