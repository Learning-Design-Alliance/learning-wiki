# Use varied in-class group-work techniques (structured/unstructured discussion, circle of voices, fishbowl) to implement flipped learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends engaging flipped-classroom students through multiple group-work techniques during class time. In the studied course, "different type s of group work techniques were used, including structured/unstructured discussion, circle of voices and fishbowl formations", replacing the traditional lecture with discussion and in-class activities. The discussion attributes the approach's success partly to increased interaction, scaffolding from instructors, and collaborative dialogue with peers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor able to engage students and provide clarification for difficult concepts during the FL activity
- Classroom arrangements appropriate for group activity
#### Constraints
- Some students initially had difficulty adjusting to group work, though in this study students did not hold negative attitudes toward it

### Target Learners
- undergraduate pre-service teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- active participation
- interaction and communication with peers and instructor
