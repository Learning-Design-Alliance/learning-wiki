# Use paired resident–intern observation instruments to give structured feedback on consult calls and inform supervision decisions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using its EPA-based instruments so a supervising resident observes the intern's consult call, completes the Resident Supervisor Instrument, provides feedback immediately or later the same day, and the intern completes a self-reflection instrument. The authors state the instruments "lend themselves to a supervisor and learner, not necessarily a resident and intern," and that the information "can be used in clinical competency committees when assessing how well a learner is meeting his or her goals."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Approximately 10 minutes of training on instrument use at the start of the rotation
- Instruments accessible in the workrooms during and after the call
#### Constraints
- The instrument should be tested further, ideally across different institutions with larger sample sizes
- Ideally used at the beginning of the year to assess interns at the start of their training

### Target Learners
- internal medicine interns
- medical students
- senior residents

### Target Learning Goals
- consultation-request skills
- self-assessment accuracy
- supervision-level calibration
