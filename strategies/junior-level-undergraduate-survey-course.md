# Schedule a yearly national junior-level undergraduate survey course to recruit students into AS&E and traineeship programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article repeatedly recommends initiating a national junior-level undergraduate recruiting course to alert students to the breadth of the field and range of technical opportunities. The course "should be targeted for both physics and engineering students, more accessible than USPAS Fundamentals, and serve as an overview highlighting the breadth of science and technology opportunities in the field." It is intended to draw in uncommitted undergraduates at an effective age for reaching women and underrepresented groups, recruit for traineeships and APS DPB, and potentially generate outreach materials such as recorded lectures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Must be introductory to be accessible to students unfamiliar with the field
- Should reach junior-level students in both engineering and physics
#### Constraints
- Present USPAS session sizes (7-8 concurrent classrooms) are fully committed and cannot be expanded without much higher cost; time phasing and coordination with other school activities would be important

### Target Learners
- Junior-level undergraduate students in physics and engineering
- Students who have not yet committed to specific fields of study

### Target Learning Goals
- Awareness of the breadth of accelerator science and engineering career opportunities
- Awareness of traineeship opportunities for graduate studies
