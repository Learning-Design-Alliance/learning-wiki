# Begin reform with quick hits on highly visible problems to build collective agency, then sustain a long-term focus on the instructional core

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This two-part strategy pairs early, visible wins with sustained attention to teaching quality. As the article puts it, "principals often begin by attacking and solving quickly some highly visible problems," such as finding supplemental space to relieve overcrowding, because such initiatives "provide concrete signs of change and develop a collective sense of agency." In parallel, principals concentrate long-term on strengthening faculty quality through recruiting talented new teachers, targeted staff development for all teachers, and organizational changes promoting best practices, while managing day-to-day decisions to maximize instructional time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Discretionary resources under local control to finance improvements and hire additional teachers
#### Constraints
- Stirring rhetoric quickly appears hollow if not accompanied by complementary action

### Target Learners
- Elementary school students and teachers in Chicago public schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Maximized instructional time and improved student achievement
