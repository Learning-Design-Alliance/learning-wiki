# Use VR as a primary instruction method for spatial astronomy topics and expand to parallax, binary stars, Hubble expansion, and the Doppler effect

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors' forward-looking plan after the pilot: they are now confident enough in the VR approach to test it as the primary instruction method for Moon phases and eclipses, against control groups without the prior traditional lecture, and to develop additional VR activities on spatially demanding topics. They state that "possible topics amenable to VR include parallax, binary stars, Hubble expansion, and the Doppler effect", and that offering more VR activities in a lecture or laboratory class can reduce training overhead.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- User-friendly software for creating virtual scenarios to reduce development time
- VR hardware and staffing sufficient for lecture- or laboratory-class deployment
#### Constraints
- A major limitation is the time needed to develop VR scenarios, even before writing a scripted lesson plan

### Target Learners
- Introductory college astronomy students

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding spatially and temporally complex astronomy topics such as Moon phases, eclipses, parallax, binary stars, Hubble expansion, and the Doppler effect
