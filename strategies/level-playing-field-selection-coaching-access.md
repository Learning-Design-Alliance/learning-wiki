# Level the selection playing field by providing coaching to disadvantaged groups or using processes less amenable to coaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes options to address the equity concern that preparatory activities favour applicants who can access them. It states: "Options to level the playing field may be the provision of coaching activities to disadvantaged groups, using processes shown to be less influenced by coaching such as the MMI, scores on such tests be weighted accordingly to account for level of disadvantage" or new processes such as personality and emotional intelligence testing. It cautions that applicants will likely adapt to any new process.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Selection processes must be designed with awareness that coaching is associated with successful outcomes
#### Constraints
- The article notes changing the selection process is complicated and costly, and applicants will adapt to new processes to increase their likelihood of being selected

### Target Learners
- applicants to undergraduate medical school

### Target Learning Goals
- fair and equitable selection into medical school
