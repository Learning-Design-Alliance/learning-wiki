# Begin a physics problem by predicting from remembered experience, then check against calculation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The lecture recommends that when a physics problem describes a situation learners have actually seen, they should visualize it and predict an answer from memory of similar events before any mathematics. The bucket lecture asks how long the fall will take—blink of an eye, time to jump, or time for a conversation—and later confirms that the calculated characteristic time "probably agrees with your initial guess." This grounds abstract estimation in intuition and provides a diagnostic for the calculation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must have direct experience of situations similar to the problem
#### Constraints
- The lecture presents this as appropriate when the problem describes a familiar, observable situation

### Target Learners
- Physics students at any level with everyday experience of falling objects

### Target Learning Goals
- Connecting physical intuition with quantitative estimation
