Apply authentic materials in teaching listening comprehension and have students practice with them independently¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on its finding of a significant effect, the article recommends that English teachers apply authentic materials when teaching listening comprehension, and that students practice with authentic materials by themselves. The article states "The English teacher can apply authentic materials in teaching listening comprehension in the classroom because based on the researcher's finding; it was found that there is a significance effect of authentic materials on the students' achievement in listening comprehension." It further suggests independent student practice to encourage confidence and improve achievement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to authentic materials such as songs, which the study sourced from singers

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on a single small-scale experimental study with purposive sampling in one Indonesian junior high school

Target Learners¶

  • EFL junior high school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • listening comprehension
  • learner confidence in listening

Claims¶

  • EFL junior-high students taught listening comprehension with authentic materials scored significantly higher on a post-test than students taught with non-authentic materials [+W]
  • The experimental group taught with authentic materials (songs) improved significantly from pre-test to post-test in listening comprehension [+W]
  • 84% of treatment-group students reported their TOEIC listening skills were enhanced through the CALL course with authentic materials [+W]
  • Iranian EFL teachers hold positive attitudes toward using authentic listening materials and report enhanced listening comprehension relative to simplified materials [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Introduce authentic listening materials to lower-level learners by simplifying the task, grading the text, and staging the listening
