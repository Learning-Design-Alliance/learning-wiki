# Teach electrodynamics with Maxwell's equations written in a unit-system-independent notation using three constants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy writes the laws of electromagnetism with three constants — α0 in the Lorentz force, ke = 1/ε̃0 in the electric flux law, and km = μ̃0α0 in Ampère's law — so that "the two remaining arbitrary constants define different unit systems". Students learn one set of equations and recover any unit system by substituting printed constant values. The author reports using this procedure consistently in lectures since 1985, inspired by Jackson's book.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The electromagnetic field must first be defined through interactions with other systems, introducing charge q and the fundamental variables E and B via the Lorentz force.
- Students must substitute the printed constant values for the desired unit system.
#### Constraints
- The notations as given are specific for rationalized unit systems; non-rationalized systems require converting the factor 1/4π in the potential solutions (ε̃0 → ε̃0/4π, μ̃0 → 4πμ̃0).

### Target Learners
- university students in electrodynamics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- write and manipulate Maxwell's equations consistently across unit systems
- understand which constants are conventional versus physical
