# Speech-to-Text Technology

## Description
Speech-to-text technology converts spoken language into written text, benefiting individuals with dyslexia, dysgraphia, or physical disabilities by enabling them to express themselves in writing without the barriers of spelling, handwriting, or typing. This technology has evolved significantly since its inception in 1936. The workflow involves speaking into a device (computer, tablet, or smartphone) equipped with speech recognition software, which then transcribes the spoken words into text. Users may need to correct errors and add punctuation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A device (computer, tablet, or smartphone) with a microphone and speech recognition software or app. A quiet environment is recommended to minimize background noise and improve accuracy. Training may be required to familiarize users with the software and optimize performance.
#### Constraints
- Accuracy can be affected by background noise, accents, and unclear speech. Some speech recognition software may struggle with complex vocabulary or grammatical structures. Editing and proofreading are often necessary to correct errors and add punctuation. There may be a learning curve associated with mastering the software and speaking clearly.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Students and adults with dyslexia, dysgraphia, physical disabilities, or anyone who struggles with typing or handwriting

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enable students with dyslexia and dysgraphia to express themselves in writing, improve writing speed and fluency, enhance access to digital content, support searching the internet

### Affordances
- Reduces the cognitive load associated with writing, allowing individuals to focus on content and ideas rather than the mechanics of writing. Provides an alternative means of written expression for those with physical limitations. Builds confidence and independence by enabling individuals to create written content more easily.

### Personalization
- Users can choose from various speech recognition software options and adjust settings like language, accent, and speed. They can also train the software to recognize their voice patterns for improved accuracy. The technology supports diverse devices and platforms, including computers, tablets, and smartphones.

## Assessment Evidence
- Measure writing speed and fluency with and without speech recognition software. Evaluate the accuracy of transcriptions produced by the software. Gather feedback from users on their experience and perceived benefits. Track improvements in writing quality and confidence over time.

## Impact
- Studies show speech recognition software improves writing skills for students with learning disabilities by reducing the cognitive demands of spelling and handwriting. Accuracy has increased dramatically in recent years, making this a valuable tool for improving written communication and academic performance.
