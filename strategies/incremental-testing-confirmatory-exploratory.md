# Use small-scale, low-stakes incremental testing with both confirmatory and exploratory measures, scaling up progressively

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The chapter prescribes preliminary tests outside classes first, then small-scale tests in low-stakes classroom settings, chosen to expose potential failure mechanisms while limiting harm. Because proven instructional methods interact with unique cultural and organizational contexts, confirmatory quantitative measurements (such as test scores) should check whether expected benefits appear, while exploratory qualitative measurements — including observing classrooms — surface unanticipated effects. Early successes should be communicated to stakeholders.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Capacity building should be limited to the scale of the next upcoming round of testing
- A rigorous approach to measurement and monitoring must accompany the testing
#### Constraints
- Scaling up may reveal organizational structure and policy issues invisible in small-scale tests; unforeseen setbacks are expected at each expansion

### Target Learners
- students and teachers in the pilot settings, and implementation teams

### Target Learning Goals
- adapting the intervention, team, and theory of change based on test evidence
