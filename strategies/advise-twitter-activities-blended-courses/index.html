Advise teachers to use Twitter learning activities in blended courses integrating face-to-face and online discussion¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the study's results, the author recommends that teachers and actors in online education adopt Twitter to pursue learning activities within a blended course combining face-to-face learning and Twitter learning activities. The article states: "the researchers advise teachers and actors in online education to use Twitter to pursue learning activities in a blended course integrating both face-to-face learning and Twitter learning activities." Listed benefits include facilitating online discussion and social interaction, ease of use, and knowledge sharing.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must agree to enter into flows with their students, with the teacher becoming a facilitator of learning rather than the sole holder of knowledge

Constraints¶

  • The supporting evidence comes from a single course, Computers in Education, which the author says should be replicated in other online disciplines

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students
  • graduate students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • online discussion of course content
  • collaborative knowledge construction
  • social interaction in blended courses

Claims¶

  • Students in the Twitter-supported blended course reported positive attitudes at the technological, individual, and community levels of activity theory [+M]
  • Female master's students in a Twitter-supported blended course achieved higher post-test scores than students in a 100% in-class course [+M]
  • Students in the Twitter-supported blended course reported more positive attitudes toward the learning process than students in the fully in-class course [+M]
