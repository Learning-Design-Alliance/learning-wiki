Reclassify English learners using criteria grounded in the federal EL definition, without requiring minimum content-assessment performance for exit¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The paper recommends conceptualizing reclassification criteria using the federal definition of an EL, addressing the linguistic contribution to academic performance and societal participation "while not requiring a minimum level of performance on an academic content assessment for exit". It recommends studies relating ELP assessment results to academic outcomes on Smarter Balanced, PARCC, and stand-alone states' summative assessments, plus standardized rubrics or rating scales to support and standardize local criteria for classroom participation and societal participation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Empirical demonstration that meeting multiple criteria addressing the ELP dimensions of the federal EL definition removes linguistic barriers to meeting expectations
  • Studies conducted over time using empirical data from several states

Constraints¶

  • Several states have laws requiring the use of state academic achievement measures in exit decisions, which the paper notes as a challenge
  • The societal-participation dimension is currently the least operationalized aspect of the federal LEP definition

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 English learners approaching reclassification to former-EL status

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English proficiency sufficient to succeed without specialized linguistic and academic support services

Affordances¶

  • Four Stage Common El Definition Framework

Related Strategies¶

  • Consider policy design carefully to ensure equitable reclassification opportunities for EL students
