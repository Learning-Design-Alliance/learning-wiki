# Integrate conversational AI with responsive feedback into children's educational video content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "future educational video resources could benefit from integrating conversational technologies to promote active science learning through television shows". The strategy is to have the media character ask children questions during the episode, listen, and provide contingent feedback tailored to each response, rather than pausing for generic replies. The authors note developer tools from major technology companies streamline the technical complexity, and that generative AI could lessen development costs and improve quality at scale.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Speech recognition and natural language processing integrated into the video content
- Structured dialogue with specific learning objectives: asking questions, listening in, and providing responsive feedback
#### Constraints
- Generative AI is "not suitable for use in unsupervised interaction for children"
- Potential algorithmic bias may create barriers for children from minoritized language backgrounds

### Target Learners
- Young children watching educational television, especially those from under-resourced households

### Target Learning Goals
- Active science learning from video
- Sustained verbal engagement with media content
