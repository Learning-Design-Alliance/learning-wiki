# Use LLMs cautiously as supplementary learning tools in dental education, with output verification and professional supervision

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that, given pooled LLM accuracy of about 64%, dental educators may use LLMs as supplementary tools for "generating explanations, highlighting key concepts, and supporting formative self-testing" while correctness must be verified, especially in high-stakes domains. The strategy positions LLMs as education and structured knowledge support rather than autonomous clinical recommendation, with outputs interpreted critically under professional supervision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Outputs must be interpreted critically and under professional supervision, with correctness verified by the user.
#### Constraints
- The article states LLMs are not suitable for autonomous clinical decision-making and that observed accuracy should not be interpreted as evidence of safe independent clinical reasoning.

### Target Learners
- dental students
- dental hygiene students
- dental trainees preparing for licensing and specialty examinations

### Target Learning Goals
- dental knowledge assessment and examination preparation
- formative self-testing
- explanation of key dental concepts
