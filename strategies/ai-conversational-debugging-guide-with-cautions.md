# Encourage students to use AI as a conversational debugging guide while informing them of LLM imperfections and cautioning against unjustified confident claims

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that educators treat off-the-shelf LLMs as conversational debugging guides for analog circuits, provided students are told about the models' weaknesses in breadboard and PCB schematic recognition. Instructors should "caution students against LLMs' potential unjustified confident claims" and emphasize fundamental concepts and independent, critical thinking so students retain control of their debugging process. This strategy follows directly from the study's observed error and confidence themes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be informed of the imperfections of off-the-shelf LLMs in breadboard or PCB schematic recognition tasks
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence comes from an exploratory study whose sample size of 17 is a small, self-selected group, so more data collection may be needed for general and robust conclusions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate electrical and computer engineering students in laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- analog circuit debugging
- critical thinking about AI suggestions
- circuit fundamentals
