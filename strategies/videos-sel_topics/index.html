Videos: SEL Topics¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

This strategy uses short video clips depicting social-emotional learning (SEL) topics — empathy, self-regulation, conflict resolution, growth mindset — as a stimulus for discussion and follow-up activities. Video's narrative and visual format simplifies abstract or emotionally complex topics through storytelling, making them accessible and personally applicable. The video is a prompt, not the lesson: learning happens in the structured discussion and application that follow.

Design Implications¶

Video combines visual and verbal channels, which can support comprehension of emotionally charged or socially nuanced content when segments are kept short and narration aligns with imagery Media combinations affect recall and retention. [+M]. Because SEL content is learned partly through observation of modeled behavior, video functions as vicarious social modeling — learners observe how characters recognize emotions, make decisions, and repair relationships Building Empathy. The critical design move is what surrounds the clip: discussion prompts that activate learners' own experiences and structured follow-up activities convert passive viewing into personal connection Activation.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Short (typically 2–6 minute), age-appropriate clips with authentic, relatable scenarios rather than didactic lectures
  • Prepared discussion prompts that move from comprehension ("What did the character feel?") to application ("When have you felt this?")
  • A facilitation plan ensuring psychological safety, inclusivity, and respect for diverse perspectives and lived experiences
  • A follow-on activity requiring learners to apply or rehearse the skill (Application, Act It Out)

Constraints¶

  • Viewing without structured discussion produces little durable learning; passive watching fosters an illusion of understanding [-M]
  • Clips depicting emotionally intense scenarios (bullying, family conflict) can dysregulate learners with relevant trauma histories; preview and offer opt-out or alternative framing [~M]
  • Effectiveness collapses when videos are culturally mismatched to the class or feel contrived; relevance to learners' backgrounds is a precondition, not a bonus [-M]
  • Overly long or lecture-style videos exceed attention limits and dilute the emotional hook that makes the strategy work [-M]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Pause-and-talk: stop the clip at decision points and ask learners to predict or advise the character before watching the outcome
  • Character perspective-taking: learners narrate the situation from different characters' viewpoints, then compare
  • Student-created video: older learners script and film their own SEL scenarios, shifting from consumption to production
  • Home–school extension: clips shared with families to prompt conversation beyond the classroom

Target Learners¶

  • K–12 learners, particularly elementary and middle-grade students for whom narrative video lowers the barrier to discussing emotions [+W]
  • Multilingual learners, who benefit from visual narrative supporting verbal content Media combinations affect recall and retention. [+M]
  • Adult learners in advisory, counseling, or workplace SEL contexts, provided scenarios reflect adult situations rather than school-age ones [~W]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Social awareness and empathy: recognizing emotions and perspectives in others
  • Self-regulation strategies: observing modeled coping and conflict-resolution moves
  • Personal connection and transfer: linking SEL concepts to learners' own lives and relationships
  • Vocabulary for emotion and social situations, supporting later discussion and self-report

Instructions¶

  1. Preview and select a short clip matched to learners' age, culture, and current SEL focus; screen it for emotional intensity and relevance.
  2. Activate prior experience with a brief prompt or turn-and-talk before viewing (Activation).
  3. View the clip, pausing at key decision points if using the pause-and-talk variation (Demonstration).
  4. Facilitate structured discussion moving from comprehension to personal application (Class Discussion).
  5. Apply or rehearse the skill through role-play, journaling, or a real-world commitment (Application, Act It Out).
  6. Close with reflection — one sentence connecting the strategy shown in the video to something learners will try themselves.

Related Strategies¶

  • Act It Out — the natural follow-up: rehearsing the social behavior the video modeled
  • Activating Prior Knowledge — the pre-viewing move that anchors the clip in learners' experience
  • Class Discussion — the core processing mechanism between viewing and application
  • Use temperament-based classroom sessions with puppets, videos and activities to build empathy and dilemma resolution

Examples¶

  • ClassDojo — "Big Ideas" video series on growth mindset, empathy, and perseverance, designed as short episodes with built-in discussion questions for elementary classrooms.
  • Flocabulary — hip-hop videos on managing frustration, building empathy, and other life skills for K–12, each paired with activities and lyric-based discussion prompts.
  • Second Step (Committee for Children) — published SEL curriculum using scripted video scenarios of peer conflict as the stimulus for skill rehearsal in weekly lessons.
  • Sesame Workshop — video resources on emotional literacy for early-childhood settings, used in both classrooms and family engagement programs.
