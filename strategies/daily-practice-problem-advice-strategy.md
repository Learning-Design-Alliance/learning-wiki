# Advise students to attempt at least one or two practice problems every school day of the term, and have teachers encourage daily problem practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article closes with explicit advice to students and teachers grounded in its quantified gains. Students are told: "Attempting at least one or two problems each school day of the term will likely lift your grade by one-quarter point", and because gains are quadratic in log study volume, doubling from two to four questions per day benefits the grade even more. Teachers are advised that "Encouraging students to do at least one problem a day puts them above the median study habit; on average, this will land them above their expected grade", with study benefiting students by up to three-quarters of a letter grade.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a practice problem bank such as Problem Roulette with a large library of topically organized past-exam questions
#### Constraints
- Gains are on-average outcomes; significant residual scatter in grade remains, so individual variation trumps mean trends

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory physics and STEM courses, especially those needing encouragement to study

### Target Learning Goals
- improving final course grades through increased formative practice volume before examinations

- [Mean final course grade rises quadratically with the logarithm of term-aggregated practice-study volume, with an overall gain of 0.77±0.12 grade points between 1 and 1000 questions](../claims/log-quadratic-grade-gain-practice-volume.md) [+W]
- [Practice-study grade gains persist across the range of pre-college math test score, with similar gains in the lowest, intermediate, and highest test-score terciles](../claims/study-gains-persistent-across-math-ability-terciles.md) [+W]
