# Integrate application-oriented QISE content into existing academic pathways, including a quantum applications course for all STEM majors and upskilling certificates

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that academic courses and programs integrate application-oriented QISE content into existing pathways, based on managers' anticipated need for professionals who can apply quantum knowledge across sectors. It suggests "institutions could consider offering at least one course in quantum applications for all STEM majors" and that "upskilling opportunities such as certificates can help individuals who are in adjacent industries transition into quantum-related roles." It also recommends continued investment in QISE education at all levels to build a layered talent pipeline.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordination among stakeholders — industry, academia, and government — so industry partners with educators to articulate evolving workforce needs and institutions respond with aligned programs.
#### Constraints
- The article notes educational planning is complicated because managers' responses about current roles emphasized advanced quantum expertise while their projections described a broader workforce, and the manager sample is not representative of the full range of quantum company types.

### Target Learners
- STEM majors at bachelor's and master's levels
- professionals in adjacent industries seeking upskilling
- doctoral students in physics, engineering, and computer science

### Target Learning Goals
- applying quantum information science knowledge across applied domains
- preparing for quantum manufacturing, technician, and integration roles
