# Transform the culture of physics so it signals to female students that they belong, alongside boosting science capital and utility value

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that boosting the value of and access to capital is necessary but insufficient; the field itself must change. It argues "We need to transform the field of physics so that it signals to female students that they belong there", echoing Cheryan and colleagues. Recommended actions include highlighting the utility value of physics in culture, scientific literacy and employment, boosting science capital, providing group learning experiences in introductory physics, and creating more welcoming environments for female students in physics and computer science.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions are most useful at stages of education prior to university, as intentions to study science can be formed by early secondary school.
#### Constraints
- The authors note they do not want to force students to study in areas where they do not want to be, and that culture change in the field may require forced institutional changes because established actors benefit from preserving the order.

### Target Learners
- female undergraduate physics students
- high school students choosing science subjects

### Target Learning Goals
- increasing female participation and persistence in physics

### Affordances
