# Evaluate VR exhibition learning tools through post-experience user interviews plus twofold online surveys

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because full VR access is constrained by space and device availability, the article proposes gathering user feedback twofold: interviews after headset engagement with the full experience, and surveys covering museum VR experiences, curatorial activities, and 2D UI feedback. Interviews probe ease of use, dialogue conciseness, annotation usability, and VR issues such as dizziness; surveys compare perceived value with and without a classroom framing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Users who have engaged with the full VR experience using a headset for the interview component
#### Constraints
- Spatial constraints and constraints on the availability of devices mean a full-blown VR experience might not always be available

### Target Learners
- art and archaeology class participants
- museum VR experience users

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating usability, engagement, and learning outcomes of VR curatorial features
