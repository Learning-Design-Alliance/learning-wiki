Use AR applications with natural hardware markers for applied assembly and practice topics in vocational courses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends AR as an alternative for topics requiring learner application and practice: "thanks to simultaneous interaction it provides, AR offers an important alternative for topics that need learner application and practice". Using the hardware units themselves as natural markers lets learners see assembly steps directly on the parts rather than switching between a separate manual and the workpiece, which the authors link to shorter assembly time and less requested help.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An AR application presenting instructions simultaneously on the real image of the parts being assembled
  • Devices capable of running the marker-based AR application

Constraints¶

  • Smartphone screen sizes limited the experience in this study; the authors suggest AR goggles as more appropriate devices for future studies

Target Learners¶

  • vocational school students in applied technical courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • applied assembly and maintenance skills
  • computer hardware knowledge

Claims¶

  • AR instruction has no significant effect on motherboard assembly skills self-efficacy [~W]
  • AR instruction has no significant effect on motherboard assembly theoretical-knowledge self-efficacy [~W]
  • AR-supported learners complete motherboard assembly in shorter time and with less help than print-material learners [+M]
  • Test-group students rated the mobile AR application very positively: 92% found it helpful for learning, 89% interactive, 86% positive about wider course use, and only 4% distracted [+M]
  • AR-based instruction increases computer hardware course achievement compared to print materials in vocational education [+W]
