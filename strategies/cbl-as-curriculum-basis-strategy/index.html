Use case-based learning as a basis for developing curricula to improve cognitive, social and affective skills¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the study's findings, the authors recommend that "CBL can be taken as a basis for developing curricula and the effectiveness of these curricula can be viewed in terms of improving the cognitive, social and affective skills of students", including within other courses in different departments of the Faculty of Education. They further recommend longitudinal research on how students develop attitudes towards their professions and investigation of different CBL-based methods at different education levels.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Cases, study questions, small group work, class discussions, and permanence activities as in the implemented curriculum

Constraints¶

  • The authors note it is necessary to investigate the reflection of this study on different socio-cultural and larger working groups

Target Learners¶

  • pre-service teachers
  • higher education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • cognitive skills
  • social skills
  • affective characteristics
  • attitudes towards the teaching profession

Claims¶

  • Students reported that case-based learning increased interest in the course and awareness of the teaching profession [+W]
  • Students reported cognitive benefits of case-based learning, with CBL2 frequencies higher but time-management problems reported [+W]
  • Case-based learning groups achieved significantly higher post-test acquisition scores than control groups in an Introduction to Educational Science course [+M]
  • Students reported that case-based learning positively affected socialization, communication and cooperative working [+W]
  • CBL1 and CBL2 implementations did not differ significantly from each other in post-test acquisition scores [~M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Scaffold method choice by student progression: CBL early, transitioning to PBL and TBL
  • Combine CBL and PBL approaches in clinical teaching settings to target both theory and practical outcomes
