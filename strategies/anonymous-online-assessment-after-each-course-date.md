# Use anonymous online self- and external assessment after every course date to monitor and improve bedside teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's course was continuously evaluated by emailing students and lecturers an anonymous online questionnaire after every course date, rating clinical abilities in three categories on the German grading system, plus free-text fields for improvement suggestions and praise. The authors state this procedure "has already been used successfully in previous publications" and that feedback from these evaluations was the basis for drawing up and implementing the concrete course improvements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Email contact with all students and lecturers after each course date
- Weekly reminders to hand in the assessment
- A defined grading system shared by students and lecturers to reduce differences in evaluation criteria
#### Constraints
- Most students and lecturers did not report back, making a representative evaluation difficult; response rates varied substantially between semesters (9% to 35% for students, 25% to 55% for lecturers)

### Target Learners
- Fourth-year medical students in bedside courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Monitoring development of history-taking, examination, diagnostic and therapeutic abilities

## Claims

- [After the improvement measures, fewer students complained about group size and lack of examination support, and more praised lecturers' motivation and case discussion](../claims/free-text-feedback-shift-after-measures.md) [+W]
