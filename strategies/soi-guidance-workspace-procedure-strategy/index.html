Guide students through Select-Organize-Integrate steps with an application's multiple document functions before the task¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A concrete procedure the article implemented for strategy guidance in a tablet-based multiple document task. Before starting, students were instructed to "access documents and highlight important information in each document during reading, select and extract task-relevant information in each document and move it to the workspace, and organize and integrate extracted information in the workspace by comparing and contrasting information across documents". A cue card reminding them of the steps was available throughout the task, after a training task on another topic using a checklist of all application functions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The application must provide functions for simultaneous document access, highlighting, extraction to a workspace, and spatial organization
  • Guidance targeting all three SOI processes must be given before the task, with a reminder cue available during the task

Constraints¶

  • The procedure was implemented and tested with undergraduates on one socio-scientific issue; it targeted step three processes of the MD-TRACE model

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students working with multiple documents

Target Learning Goals¶

  • selecting, organizing, and integrating information across multiple documents

Affordances¶

  • Soi Model Meaningful Learning

Claims¶

  • Guided LiquidText users redistribute task time toward document processing and away from writing [+W]
  • Students guided to use LiquidText show lower acceptance of tablets for multiple document work than controls despite better performance [-W]
  • Guided use of a multiple document reading application yields higher multiple document comprehension than access-only, unguided, or traditional pdf viewer use [+W]
  • Unaided access to multiple document reading functions alone does not improve comprehension over a traditional pdf viewer [+W]
