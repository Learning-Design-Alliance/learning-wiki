# Present the professor's completed worksheet after group presentations as cognitive feedback by comparison

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
After each group presents its worksheet, the professor delivers a mini-lecture showing the expert-completed worksheet so students can compare their work against expert performance in key components of clinical reasoning. The article frames this as cognitive feedback that "helps learners reflect on the quality of their work, ultimately leading to its elaboration," enabling students to construct more elaborate illness scripts. The authors attribute the observed flexibility-in-thinking gains partly to this feedback mechanism combined with collaborative worksheet completion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator-completed version of the worksheet for each case
- Time in class for a mini-lecture following group presentations
#### Constraints
- The article notes this feedback discussion was lacking in previous worksheet studies, positioning it as this study's contribution

### Target Learners
- preclinical medical students in small-group settings

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- illness script elaboration
