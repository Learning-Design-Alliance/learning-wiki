# Use eye-tracking feedback and gradually increasing case complexity to train targeted visual search and cognitive-load management in ECG education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that cardiology training programs help novices develop targeted visual strategies focused on diagnostically critical areas, so they can "reduce their cognitive load and improve their interpretation speed and accuracy". They suggest eye-tracking feedback letting trainees compare their gaze patterns with experts', plus stress-management techniques and simulations that gradually increase in complexity, to build resilience to the cognitive demands of emergency settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires eye-tracking equipment and expert gaze-pattern references for feedback; training programs must address novices' heightened mental workload and stress during ECG interpretation.
#### Constraints
- The authors state findings are pending external validation from a small eight-participant pilot in a controlled environment, and future intervention studies are needed to verify whether real-time feedback shortens the learning curve.

### Target Learners
- Cardiology residents and trainees in emergency and acute cardiac care settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate and rapid ECG interpretation
- Targeted visual search strategies
- Cognitive load management under time pressure
