# Run history-taking sessions with individual reflection, group discussion, and Pendleton-rule peer feedback supported by instructor probing questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete session recipe from the ten-week Introduction to Clinical Medicine course: one student takes a history from a simulated patient while peers observe via CCTV; the interviewer reflects individually, each group holds a five-minute discussion, and five volunteers give roughly thirty minutes of Pendleton-rule feedback. When students struggled, instructors guided them with probing questions such as "What did they do well? In what ways was it well?" Sessions were video-recorded and distributed as learning materials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained instructors who can guide students with probing questions when they struggle to reflect or give feedback
- Simulated patients and an observation setup (CCTV) so peers can watch the history-taking
- A comprehensive instructor course on guiding reflection and feedback, with guided questions approved by a medical education expert
#### Constraints
- Three of fifteen sessions were lost to technical errors in this implementation
- The authors report that untrained staff joining intermittently generated ineffective or inappropriate feedback that created an unsafe learning environment, and recommend future courses use only trained instructors or make course objectives explicit to extra staff

### Target Learners
- fourth-year undergraduate medical students transitioning from pre-clinical to clinical years

### Target Learning Goals
- comprehensive patient history-taking
- reflective practice using Gibbs' cycle
- peer feedback using Pendleton's rule

### Affordances
- Gibbs Reflective Cycle Six Stages
