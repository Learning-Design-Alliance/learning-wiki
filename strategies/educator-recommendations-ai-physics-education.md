# Six recommendations for educators addressing AI in Physics education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 4 studies (2 qualitative, 1 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article posits six recommendations for educators: transparency about LLM capabilities, cautioning students against overreliance, avoiding teaching AI interaction techniques, changing some assessment methods, anticipating evolving capabilities, and ethical considerations including equitable access. Educators "should openly discuss its strengths and weaknesses with students, especially its propensity to produce plausible yet occasionally incorrect or incomplete answers", and should not mandate premium, potentially inaccessible technologies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators aware of current LLM capabilities in Physics question answering, essay writing and coding
#### Constraints
- Recommendations are tied to the current state of LLM technology; the article notes it remains uncertain whether future models will improve further or approach an asymptote

### Target Learners
- GCSE, A-Level and university Physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Protecting assessment fidelity and genuine learning experiences in Physics education
