# Use motivational interviewing, health behavior goal setting, and relaxation training in brief resilience curricula for responder populations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The DWRT curriculum moves beyond psychoeducation and emotional support by incorporating empirically supported psychological techniques. Chapter 4 uses "Motivational interviewing informed techniques ... to engage participants in self-assessment and goal setting" and teaches simple relaxation techniques such as deep breathing and visualization. Participants self-assess health behaviors, set personal health behavior goals, and plan resilience increases at individual and organizational levels. This concrete activity sequence fits a single 4-hour session for working responders.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A participant training manual, instructor training manual, and digital presentation
- Instructors trained to deliver the interactive experiential activities
#### Constraints
- Content should be adapted to the culture and unique concerns of the responder community (e.g., privacy concerns of police officers)

### Target Learners
- adult disaster workers and emergency responders in workshop settings

### Target Learning Goals
- self-assessment of health behaviors
- setting personal health behavior goals
- using relaxation techniques
