# Use process-tracing assessments, such as annotated portfolios of AI dialogues, to capture epistemic growth in agent-mediated learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because generative AI contributes substantively to academic work and detection tools show limited reliability, the paper recommends assessment that captures how students interact with AI tools rather than static products. "Portfolios of AI dialogues, annotated with student reflections on decision-making and critical turning points, can offer evidence of epistemic growth." It also cites hybrid models such as the FACT framework combining AI-supported tasks with AI-free baselines to safeguard foundational knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators assess the quality of dialogic engagement, not just the final output
#### Constraints
- Traditional evaluation methods emphasising static products are, in the authors' opinion, ill-suited to agent-mediated learning processes; current AI-text detection tools are vulnerable to false positives, false negatives, and circumvention

### Target Learners
- higher education students working with AI agents

### Target Learning Goals
- critical engagement with AI-generated content
- epistemic growth
- process-oriented inquiry skills
