# Position intelligent tutors as part of the teacher's toolkit that prepares students for open-ended, self-directed activities outside the tutor

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The interview recommends viewing cognitive tutors “not as a substitute for teachers but as a part of the toolkit for teachers that would enhance their ability to do the things they can uniquely do.” Rather than embedding all open-ended work in the tutor, tutoring environments can prepare students for open-ended activities elsewhere: tutors isolate the hardest core skills and give learn-by-doing practice, while self-directed projects and collaborative activities “can be more productive with the right kind of ITS preparation and follow-up.” The interview calls for research tracking the bouncing back and forth between structured tutor practice and open-ended work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Underlying deep cognitive analysis must be done well and effectively; teams must collaborate on integration
#### Constraints
- How much open-ended problem solving and reasoning tutors can support, directly or via preparation, remains a key open question not yet adequately addressed

### Target Learners
- secondary and post-secondary students using intelligent tutoring systems

### Target Learning Goals
- core reusable skills such as critical thinking and argumentation; effective participation in self-directed collaborative projects
