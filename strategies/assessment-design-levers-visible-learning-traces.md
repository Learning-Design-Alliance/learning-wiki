# Assessment design levers that make learning visible and reduce the payoff from generic generated text

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends concrete levers: staged submissions (proposal, outline, annotated bibliography, draft, final) that create a record of development; short oral or in-class verification with standardised prompts and preparation time; and personalised, critique-based tasks such as 'evaluating an AI-generated answer and correcting its authorities'. These levers allow institutions to permit legitimate language support while still detecting substitution through construct-relevant evidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Verification components must not become a language performance test; prompts should be standardised with preparation time provided
- Staff need calibration through exemplars, short training modules, and moderation guidance
#### Constraints
- Inconsistency between staged work and explanation should trigger further inquiry rather than an automatic misconduct finding

### Target Learners
- EAL and multilingual students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Evidencing disciplinary reasoning and judgement over time rather than fluent text production
