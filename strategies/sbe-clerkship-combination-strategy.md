# Combine simulation-based education with clinical clerkships to compensate for low-frequency clinical skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that medical educators embed simulation-based education alongside clinical clerkships so that skills students rarely get to perform on real wards, such as emergency response, can still be practiced. The authors write that "simulation-based education (SBE) can be a powerful solution to compensate for the lack of opportunities to exercise these skills," and give the example that "medical students can rephrase the resuscitation utilizing simulator which they watched in the emergency ward." They also suggest using SBE for formative assessment in clinical settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to simulation resources and opportunities to integrate SBE into the clerkship period
#### Constraints
- Motivated by skills students are not permitted to perform alone, such as advanced life support; the article presents this as a proposed solution, not a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- 5th-year medical students in clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- Low-frequency clinical and emergency skills not exercised during routine clerkships
