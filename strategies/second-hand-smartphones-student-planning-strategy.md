# Collect second-hand smartphones and involve students in planning to improve applicability of exergaming interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Teachers proposed concrete strategies to overcome the materials barrier: three teachers from different schools considered a feasible solution to collect old mobile phones for schools, borrowed from people or donated, organizing a small campaign with teachers and parents. The intervention also increases its applicability if help is sought to plan it with students, with previous preparation work and students who are more used to dealing with exergames helping during sessions. Good group organization capacity was also needed, such as rotating access to exergame sessions across weeks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- organization of a small campaign with teachers and parents to collect donated smartphones
- previous preparation work and student help during sessions
#### Constraints
- smartphones have presented problems similar to dance mats, such as run down batteries, outdated models or technological failures

### Target Learners
- primary school students in Physical Education

### Target Learning Goals
- applicability of technology-based PE interventions
