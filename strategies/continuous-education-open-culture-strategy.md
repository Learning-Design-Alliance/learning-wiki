# Combine continuous patient safety education with organizational efforts to build an open, collaborative safety culture

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that organizations provide continuous and repeated patient safety education while fostering openness and collaboration so nurses report incidents without fear. It states that "Education should be managed to meet minimum standards, and hospital‐specific policies should be tailored according to each institution's size and characteristics." Education programs should address both technical and nontechnical skills, since skills showed the strongest association with activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Organizational support, structured educational programs, and ongoing training to enhance communication and collaboration
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a cross-sectional secondary analysis with a convenience sample from specific South Korean regions, limiting causal inference and broader applicability

### Target Learners
- clinical nurses in general hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- patient safety activities including incident reporting, teamwork, and technical and nontechnical safety skills
