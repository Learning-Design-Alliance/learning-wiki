# Deliver a single near-peer-led OSCE-style tutorial with demonstration, hands-on practice, and logical organisation as an adjunct to faculty-led musculoskeletal teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article supports integrating near-peer teaching as a practical adjunct to faculty-led musculoskeletal education, particularly in pre-clinical years. The tutorial combined "demonstration and hands-on practice" with a logically organised, stepwise progression through the examination. Students valued the integration of clinical reasoning, anatomy, and pathology, and the opportunity to observe correct technique after attempting it themselves.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A near-peer tutor at least one year senior to learners
- Peer-reviewed tutorial content approved by a clinical supervisor
- A structured rubric-aligned OSCE-style format
#### Constraints
- Skills such as special testing require repeated practice and longer-term follow-up to observe similar gains
- A single session on one topic may not generalise across multiple sessions or other musculoskeletal topics

### Target Learners
- second-year medical students in a problem-based learning program

### Target Learning Goals
- upper limb musculoskeletal examination performance
- self-reported confidence in clinical examination

### Affordances
- Cognitive Social Congruence Npt Framework
