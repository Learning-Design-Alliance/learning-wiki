# Reading Aloud

## Description
Encouraging students to read questions and their own writing aloud leverages strengths in auditory processing. Students with strengths in auditory processing can often discern what good writing "sounds like," which can improve their composition skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classroom environment that supports reading aloud without distractions or self-consciousness. Guidance on how to provide and receive constructive feedback.
#### Constraints
- Some students may feel self-conscious about reading aloud in front of others. Requires explicit instruction on what to listen for when reading aloud.

### Target Learners

- Students with strengths in auditory processing, students who need to improve their writing skills

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve composition skills, enhance auditory processing, promote self-correction, build self-awareness of writing quality

### Affordances
- Reading aloud allows students to identify errors and areas for improvement in their writing through auditory feedback. It can also help them internalize the rhythm and structure of effective writing.

### Personalization
- Students can choose to read aloud to themselves, to a partner, or to the whole class. Feedback can be provided by the teacher or by peers.
