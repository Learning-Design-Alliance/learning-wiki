# Integrate contextualized online materials with real or simulated clinical practice elements into traditional paramedic education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that, because no dosage error is acceptable for patient safety, paramedicine educators should combine online materials with elements from real or simulated clinical practice within traditional instruction. They argue their contextualized and visualized e-learning material alone "failed to provide such a level of practice environment experience for paramedic students," which the blended condition partially remedied through classroom contact.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- E-learning materials must be contextualized and visualized to approximate clinical environments; classroom or clinical components supplement them
#### Constraints
- Written test performance may not predict practical success; long-term retention of gains is uncertain

### Target Learners
- Paramedic / EMTP students in undergraduate health programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Medication dosage calculation competency and patient safety
