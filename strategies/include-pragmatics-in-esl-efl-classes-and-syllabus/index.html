Include pragmatic issues in ESL/EFL classes and in syllabus design¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's pedagogical implication is that "language teachers should take this fact into consideration to include issues of language pragmatics in ESL/EFL classes" and that syllabus planners should not ignore pragmatic knowledge in syllabus design. It is offered as a contribution to research on the positive effect of instruction on pragmatic competence in second and foreign language learning.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers and syllabus planners must treat pragmatic aspects of language use as curricular content, not only grammar and vocabulary

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is grounded in the Iranian EFL context, where learners lack direct contact with native speakers

Target Learners¶

  • EFL learners in institute-based English courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • contextually appropriate language use, including speech acts such as refusals

Claims¶

  • Control-group exposure to dialogs without pragmatic instruction produces no significant change in refusal performance [+W]
  • Explicit instruction of refusals significantly improves Iranian EFL learners' pragmatic performance of refusals [+W]
  • Explicit instruction outperforms implicit instruction in boosting Iranian EFL learners' pragmatic performance of refusals [+W]
  • Implicit instruction of refusals also significantly improves pragmatic performance, but less than explicit instruction [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Integrate mobile-application TE-DCTs into L2 pragmatics instruction to build metapragmatic awareness of nonverbals
  • Organize telecollaborative partnership environments linking EFL students with university students in English speaking countries
