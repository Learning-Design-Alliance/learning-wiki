# Design pediatric procedural preparation with interactivity and real-time feedback rather than passive immersion alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing immersive simulations that embed goal-oriented, gamified tasks with real-time feedback when preparing adolescents for motion-restricted medical procedures. Its findings show that "immersion alone is insufficient without task engagement", and that feedback and intentional design are important "in training for motion-restricted procedures". The authors also suggest hybrid approaches, such as pairing the VR game with a brief 360° video, and adaptive difficulty mechanisms and personalized feedback for diverse learners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standalone VR hardware with head tracking and real-time feedback mechanisms; task engagement, not just immersive exposure
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a small sample (N=11) with short 3–4 minute exposures; the 360° video elicited mixed responses due to potential discomfort and lack of agency

### Target Learners
- older children and adolescents preparing for MRI or similar medical procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural stillness
- procedural knowledge and preparedness
- anxiety reduction

## Claims

- [Immersive VR conditions outperform 2D video on knowledge retention, and Game-VR yields the highest overall preparedness](../claims/game-vr-highest-preparedness.md) [+W]

- [A strong majority of adolescent participants (8 of 11) preferred the gamified VR modality, and none preferred passive VR](../claims/game-vr-preferred-modality.md) [+W]
