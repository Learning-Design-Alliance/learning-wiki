Integrate ChatGPT into Quantum Theory (and similar STEM) courses as a supplement to traditional teaching, with students verifying chatbot answers collaboratively¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q2–q4 · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article supports embedding ChatGPT-supported activities in Quantum Theory courses where students ask questions, receive step-by-step explanations, and verify the chatbot's solutions in groups with teachers. Students themselves stated that "incorporating ChatGPT into the Quantum Theory curriculum would be extremely beneficial", valuing its interactive, personalized support that supplements traditional teaching. Because the chatbot errs on graphs and complex math, answers should be checked against textbooks, articles, and other resources.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Supplement rather than replace traditional teaching, with additional resources available for topics lacking in-depth explanations and for graphical or complex mathematical content

Constraints¶

  • ChatGPT cannot draw graphs between two variables or interpret graphic equations, and occasionally makes unexplainable mathematical errors, so its answers require verification

Target Learners¶

  • Grade 12 / upper-secondary STEM students
  • students hesitant to seek clarification in conventional classrooms

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Fundamental Quantum Theory concepts, problem-solving skills, and scientific questioning aptitudes

Claims¶

  • ChatGPT-enhanced instruction produced significantly higher overall post-test achievement in Grade 12 Quantum Theory than traditional teaching, with a large effect size (η² > 0.14) [+W]
  • Students reported that ChatGPT enhanced engagement, comprehension, problem-solving, and perceived relevance in Quantum Theory [+W]
  • Students found using a chatbot to solve a photoelectric-effect problem and evaluating its answer insightful, and the authors propose group checking as a way to verify chatbot answers [+W]
  • AI-supported instruction has a moderate positive effect on student achievement in STEM education (g = 0.670 under a random effects model) [+W]
