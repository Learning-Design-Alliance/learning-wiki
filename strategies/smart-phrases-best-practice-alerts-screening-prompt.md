# Use electronic health record tools (smart phrases, best practice alerts) to prompt obstetric history screening

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the conclusion the authors recommend testing health-information-technology prompts as a complement to provider education. They state that "Future studies should investigate if the use of smart phrases (also known as dot phrases) and best practice alerts could help identify women with adverse pregnancy outcomes and should also examine novel educational approaches to increase screening knowledge." The strategy targets the documentation gap observed in their chart reviews.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clinical visit templates that can capture whether obstetric history screening was performed, since the authors note template limits made documentation an imperfect proxy
#### Constraints
- Proposed for future study only; the article did not implement or test smart phrases or best practice alerts

### Target Learners
- Internal medicine primary care physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- Prompt identification of women with adverse pregnancy outcomes and increase screening knowledge during well-woman visits
