# Structure poster presentations around the claim-evidence-reasoning formulation to build communication skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Each group made a poster and gave a two-minute presentation in the style of a scientific presentation, encouraged to frame results using the claim-evidence-reasoning formulation described in the lab manual. The authors state the goal was to give students practice in communicating ideas in an understandable way and to build confidence. Every group member was encouraged, but not required, to speak during the presentation. Rubrics with Emerging, Accomplishing, and Mastering levels were used to score presentations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- More time than anticipated was needed; the authors suggest poster templates or a focused class discussion on what makes a good poster and how to distill key facts
#### Constraints
- The authors found it difficult to fill out rubrics during the very short presentations, and concluded some content-goal expectations, especially for Mastering, were quite high

### Target Learners
- community college students in introductory science labs

### Target Learning Goals
- communicating scientific results to peers
- conveying results in a way that makes sense to classmates
