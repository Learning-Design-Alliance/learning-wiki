# Routines And Signals

## Description
Routines are rehearsed, repeatable procedures for recurring classroom activities (entering, distributing materials, transitioning between tasks, submitting work); signals are brief cues — verbal, visual, or auditory — that trigger a specific learned response (attention, silence, movement). Together they automate the operational layer of a learning environment so that instruction, not management, consumes instructional time.

## Design Implications

Routines and signals reduce the working-memory and attentional demands of non-instructional activity by making classroom behavior automatic, preserving limited attention for content [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Classroom management research consistently links well-taught routines to higher engagement and more time on task, particularly when procedures are explicitly taught, rehearsed, and briefly reinforced early in a course [~S]. Predictable structure also supports learners who struggle with unstructured environments and contributes to a sense of safety and belonging [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of the recurring activities worth routinizing (typically transitions, materials, start/end of session)
- Explicit teaching of each routine: explain, model, rehearse, and briefly re-practice when it degrades
- Signals that are distinct, few in number, and consistently paired with the same expected response
- Low-cost reinforcement early on, fading as routines become automatic

#### Constraints
- Routines taught once but never rehearsed decay quickly; inconsistent enforcement is worse than no routine [~S]
- Over-routinization can suppress productive autonomy and discussion — routines should govern logistics, not thinking
- Signals lose effectiveness when overused or when the expected response is not consistently reinforced [-M]
- Rigid routines imposed without rationale can undermine perceived autonomy, reducing intrinsic motivation [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Entry routines**: a warm-up task visible before learners arrive (e.g., "do-now" prompts) eliminates dead time
- **Attention signals**: call-and-response phrases, raised hand, or chimes; effectiveness depends on rehearsal, not novelty
- **Digital environments**: recurring channel structures, posting templates, and predictable weekly rhythms serve the same function in online courses
- **Fading**: mature classrooms can hand routine ownership to learners (materials managers, discussion chairs)

### Target Learners
- Young learners and novices in a new environment, who lack schemas for how the setting operates
- Learners with attention or executive-function challenges, for whom predictable structure reduces extraneous load [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+M]
- Adult learners benefit from lighter versions: predictable session structure and clear norms rather than rehearsed signals

### Target Learning Goals
- Prerequisite to nearly all goals: maximizing allocated instructional time and attention
- Self-regulation: internalized routines scaffold learners' own planning and transitions
- Classroom discourse goals: routines for turn-taking and accountable talk make structured discussion possible

### Instructions
1. List the recurring activities in the course and select the 3–5 highest-frequency ones to routinize.
2. For each, define the procedure in concrete steps and a trigger ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) can anchor entry routines).
3. Teach each routine explicitly: explain why, [Demonstrate](../elements/demonstration.md) it, rehearse it, and give brief feedback.
4. Introduce one [attention signal](../elements/attention.md), rehearse it to a standard, and use it consistently.
5. Rehearse routines whenever they degrade; fade reinforcement as they become automatic.
6. Open sessions with a predictable [check-in](../elements/check-in.md) or warm-up so the routine itself delivers instructional value.

## Examples
- **Responsive Classroom** (https://www.responsiveclassroom.org) — the "Morning Meeting" and "Interactive Modeling" routines explicitly teach procedures through demonstration and rehearsal.
- **KIPP schools' SLANT routine** — a rehearsed attention routine (Sit up, Listen, Ask questions, Nod, Track the speaker) pairing a signal with a learned response.
- **University discussion courses** — a standing "think-pair-share" routine with a consistent timer signal automates transition into structured peer discussion.
