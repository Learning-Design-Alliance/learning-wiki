# Use deliberate concealment (e.g. hiding the blow dryer) to make a demonstration's observation seem impossible, then devote class time to debunking the trick

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper recommends adding deception so the observation is even more unexpected. The authors "modified the classical Bernoulli's principle demo where a ping-pong ball floats above a blow dryer by hiding the blow dryer below a table", with loud magic-ambiance music covering the noise so the ball appears to levitate. Class time is then devoted to debunking the trick and initiating an inquiry process.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ambiance music sufficiently loud to cover the concealed apparatus's noise; a determinable preconception that the concealed mechanism violates
#### Constraints
- Deception must eventually be revealed so the inquiry process can begin

### Target Learners
- high school and college physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding Bernoulli's principle / buoyancy-related phenomena through inquiry
