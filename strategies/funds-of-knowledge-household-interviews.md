# Funds of Knowledge Household Interviews

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Teachers visit students' homes as participant-observers "with the purpose of identifying and documenting existing knowledge", unlike traditional home visits about a student problem. In the Tucson project each teacher chose two or three students, conducted three two-hour interviews with each student and family about the household's origins and social and labor histories, and wrote field notes afterward.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers choose two or three students each at their own discretion.
- Conduct three two-hour interviews with each student and his or her family.
- Write field notes after each interview for discussion in the after-school labs; some teachers also kept a personal field journal to monitor their reflexive process.
- Enter the home as a learner, willing to interact, prepared to document what is learned.
#### Constraints
- Teachers may feel discomfort entering a household like a private investigator and asking a wide range of questions.
- Requires many after-school hours for interviews and writing.

### Target Learners
- Language minority and working-class students and their families
- Teachers acting as researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- Document household funds of knowledge such as farming, construction, trade, business, and finance
- Build teacher understanding of households' sociopolitical and economic context
