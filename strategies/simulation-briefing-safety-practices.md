# Briefing practices facilitators use to establish a safe simulation learning environment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes concrete facilitator practices for establishing safety: politely presenting themselves and clearly explaining learning objectives at the initial briefing, sharing stories from their own practice including past mistakes, using humor and laughter, explicitly discussing stress and anxiety, assuring students the simulation is not a test, ensuring team confidentiality, and prohibiting photos or recordings. One facilitator told students: "Here you are allowed to make mistakes, this is no exam, we have not gathered you [here] to test you on anything."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator who leads the briefing before the simulation scenario
#### Constraints
- Described in the context of second-year nursing students' high-fidelity simulation training in Norwegian universities

### Target Learners
- nursing students in simulation-based training

### Target Learning Goals
- participating in debriefing without fear
- learning from mistakes in simulation
