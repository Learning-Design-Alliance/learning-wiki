# Use objective blood loss measurement in future obstetric training research in low-resource settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future research in low-resource settings measure blood loss objectively rather than relying on visual estimation, because subjective measurement leads to lower estimates of PPH incidence and weakens study power. They state: "It is important to emphasize the need for objective blood loss measurement in future research in low-resource settings." They note this would improve reliability of outcomes and may improve quality of care through better detection of major haemorrhage. They also recommend future studies consider a randomised design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Objective measurement tools (the authors explored point-of-care Hb measurements and blood collection bags)
#### Constraints
- In this study, objective measurement did not fit the research budget and met resistance from nurse midwives with high workload; visual estimation was used in about 90% of births

### Target Learners
- researchers evaluating obstetric training in low-resource settings

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate measurement of postpartum haemorrhage incidence and management outcomes
