State-level continuous improvement using longitudinal data, common placement benchmarks, innovation incentives, and barrier removal to identify effective developmental education practices¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that states establish data systems tracking student progression milestones through developmental education, adopt college-readiness assessment policies with comparable start points such as placement assessment scores to enable "apples to apples" comparisons and benchmarking, and provide incentives and supports for leaders, practitioners, researchers, and policymakers to analyze outcomes data in ongoing continuous improvement. States should also bring practitioners, researchers, and policymakers together regularly to share outcomes data and best practices.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • State-level longitudinal data systems tracking intermediate and final outcome measures disaggregated by socioeconomic status, race and ethnicity, enrollment status, and other subgroups

Constraints¶

  • The article notes institutions will need to try strategies that may not fit within existing state policies, rules, and regulations, so barriers such as traditional-length academic semesters must be eliminated

Target Learners¶

  • community college students who test into developmental education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving completion of developmental education requirements, persistence, transfer, and graduation

Claims¶

  • Experimental and quasiexperimental evidence on developmental education is mixed, with studies reporting moderately positive, little to no, and negative impacts [+W]
  • Regression-discontinuity studies of developmental education apply only near the placement cut score and aggregate across institutions, risking false negatives about effective local practices [+W]
  • Experimental research on developmental education is in extreme shortage, with the Kingsborough learning communities random assignment study the most well-known example [+W]
