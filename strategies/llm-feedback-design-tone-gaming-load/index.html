Design LLM feedback systems to retain encouraging tone, constrain behavioral gaming, and reduce overwhelming cognitive load¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article derives three design directions for future LLM-generated feedback systems in online higher education. First, retain encouraging tones because perceived encouragement enhanced academic performance, while keeping feedback concise since an encouraging tone may increase feedback length and overwhelm the receiver. Second, constrain behavioral gaming, for example by imposing a mandatory delay before a second attempt or structuring the outer loop of task sequencing. Third, shift from information transmission to a "feedback-and-respond" approach in which learners respond to reflective prompts or generate self-explanations before resubmission.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Feedback systems must be able to generate encouraging first-layer feedback and elaborated correct-answer feedback via distinct theory-grounded prompts, as in the study's RAG-based GPT-5 pipeline

Constraints¶

  • The study's learning-by-doing tasks were relatively low-level and may not have fully engaged higher-order thinking
  • The study focused on a single course context, and effects may differ across disciplines
  • Process data relied mainly on log traces

Target Learners¶

  • U.S. college students in online higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding and application of the multimedia principle in instructional design

Related Strategies¶

  • Upgrade retrieval to Agentic RAG and adopt context-aware emotive narration to reduce cognitive load
