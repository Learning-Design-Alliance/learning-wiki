# Design composite STEAM tasks that jointly evidence technical fluency, ethical reasoning, collaborative practice and design iteration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review's instructional implication is that STEAM instruction should move beyond technical fluency toward composite tasks. The authors state: "Instruction should move toward composite tasks that jointly evidence technical fluency, ethical reasoning, collaborative practice, and design iteration, aligned with recent calls for human-centered, holistic AI education". Concrete suggestions include pairing implementation with error analysis, bias checks and model critique in Technology subjects, and requiring one unified submission with a technical deliverable, an impact brief and a design rationale evaluated with cross-disciplinary rubrics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cross-disciplinary planning, e.g. joint planning with Technology and Engineering to couple ethical analysis and creative imagination to technical artifacts
#### Constraints
- Current practice is strong on AILE 1.1 to 1.3 and limited on creative imagination, ethical awareness, Managing AI and Designing AI; integrated cross-disciplinary designs are still uncommon

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in middle and high school STEAM contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- Integrated AI literacy spanning technical, ethical, collaborative and design elements
