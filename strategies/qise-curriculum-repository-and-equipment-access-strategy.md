# Curated repository of vetted QISE curricula plus multi-mechanism access to laboratory equipment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A combined implementation strategy from the workshop's curriculum and equipment discussions. A center would host "a repository of quality and vetted educational resources for educators and the public alike," building on prior models such as PhysPort and nanoHUB, curating a database spanning the field with tie-ins to different science, engineering, and technology coursework, and disseminating informal activities through networks such as the National Q-12 Education Partnership's QuanTime program. For equipment, the center would compile cost-effective alternatives meeting learning objectives, organize collaborative purchasing among consortia of institutions to gain economies of scale, and broker industry partnerships for remote access to advanced equipment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cooperation of consortia of institutions for group purchasing
- Industry partnerships willing to provide remote access to equipment
#### Constraints
- The article states group purchasing would be more challenging for pre-college institutions
- Implementation of curricula currently appears uneven and located at few institutions or programs, and some resources sit behind paywalls or membership requirements

### Target Learners
- K-12 students
- undergraduate students (2- and 4-year colleges)

### Target Learning Goals
- Hands-on experimental knowledge and skills in QISE
- Quantum concepts across formal and informal settings
