# Use complex problems in class and target PI questions at the key decision points of the solution pathway

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Rather than working through a progression from simple to complex problems, the author recommends spending scarce class time on one or two of the most complex problems, using PI questions to direct conversation to the hardest parts. He writes that PI questions suggested "focus students' attention on the key decisions that one must make when solving a particular problem," such as choosing integration limits, applying a coordinate system, and constructing mathematical representations. Once students understand the reasoning behind each decision, they can transfer it to new situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Choose problems requiring students to apply as much astrophysical information and as many problem-solving techniques as possible
- Resist doing several worked examples after introducing a new method; make students use it
#### Constraints
- Problems requiring only a limited number of techniques may not prepare students for complex problems coordinating multiple pieces of information

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate astronomy students

### Target Learning Goals
- quantitative problem-solving
- expert-like problem-solving techniques
