# Use short in-house videos to complement lectures by making instruments, locations, and processes accessible, then engage students with a question

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Rather than recording entire lectures, the authors recommend short videos that show what a lecture hall cannot: instruments, field sampling, sample preparation, and remote or slow processes. Their sequence is "show, elaborate, and engage students in a reasonable manner with it": an about-8-min case-study video (cadmium determination in chocolate) is commented live, followed by a multiple-choice vote via the classroom response system, then display of the vote distribution and explanation of the solution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Considerable production effort (the 8-min chocolate video took about 3 days to plan, record, and edit)
- Presentation software already in use, which makes incorporating video clips simple and straightforward
#### Constraints
- Videos do not provide hands-on experience
- Third-party material protected in teaching is not cleared for public release of lecture recordings; access to recordings should be limited to course participants

### Target Learners
- undergraduate analytical chemistry students

### Target Learning Goals
- introducing quantitative element analysis and making analytical instruments and procedures visible
