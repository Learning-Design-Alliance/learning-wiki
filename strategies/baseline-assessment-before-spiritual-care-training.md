# Assess nurses' spiritual care competencies as a baseline before implementing tailored training programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that organizations first measure nurses' readiness and competencies in integrating patients' religion/spirituality, then deliver training tailored to the specific needs, culture, and context of their healthcare environment. Because competency levels differed by hospital type and setting characteristics, a one-size-fits-all program is unlikely to fit; the authors state "it is crucial for nurses, particularly those working in psychiatric hospitals, to receive training that is tailored to the specific needs, culture, and context of their respective healthcare environments."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Baseline assessment of nurses' readiness and competencies before implementing appropriate training programs
#### Constraints
- Findings come from quota sampling in two teaching hospitals in southeastern Iran and may not represent all nurses; the authors call for studies in diverse cultural contexts

### Target Learners
- Nurses in general and psychiatric hospitals
- Nursing students in academic education

### Target Learning Goals
- Integrating patients' religion/spirituality into holistic nursing care
