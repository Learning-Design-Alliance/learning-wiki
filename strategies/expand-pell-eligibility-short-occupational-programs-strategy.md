# Expand Pell Grant eligibility to short occupational programs as a strategy for helping displaced workers and low-income adults earn credentials quickly

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report presents waiving Pell rules so funds can be used for short occupational training as a policy strategy for rapid credentialing. It notes that "strategies to help displaced workers and low-income adults earn credentials with the potential to improve their job prospects quickly may be of particular interest now, given the changing economic conditions due to the coronavirus pandemic."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must meet Pell income criteria, be un- or underemployed, and enroll in a short occupational program aligned with local or regional workforce needs.
#### Constraints
- Average cost of expanding eligibility was about $1,800 per student in this study, and labor-market returns remain open questions.

### Target Learners
- Displaced workers
- Low-income adults

### Target Learning Goals
- Earning occupational certificates or credentials quickly to improve job prospects
