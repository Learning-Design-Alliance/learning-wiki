# Use everyday objects in demonstrations and investigations so lens phenomena connect to students' daily lives

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors deliberately used commonplace materials — magnifying glasses, drinking glasses filled with water and sugar water, light bulbs, cardboard mailing tubes — in starter demonstrations and investigations. They state they made "a concerted eﬀort to use materials that related to daily life" so students would not feel alienated by specialized lab equipment, and so learning could be reinforced outside the classroom. Telescopes were built from mailing tubes and inexpensive education-grade lenses mounted with weather-stripping foam tape.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials must still allow investigable questions; the authors note the sugar-water glasses were not carefully crafted lenses, making lens material the most challenging topic
#### Constraints
- Specialized equipment can be beneficial for consistent and accurate quantitative results, per the authors' attitudinal goal statement

### Target Learners
- non-science majors without strong science backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing that lenses and telescopes can be found or constructed with commonplace objects
- developing a more positive view of science
