# Use student error tasks in tandem with traditional problem-solving exercises, in class or on exams, to foster metacognitive skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend pairing error-identification tasks with conventional problem solving: "Identifying errors is a slightly different, but complementary, challenge compared to traditional problem-solving exercises." Together the two formats may help students learn which steps work and foster metacognition, which prior studies identify as a key novice-expert difference. Error tasks can be used as in-class collaborative activities or as assessment questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supply of authentic student errors to encode into the fictional snippet
- Familiarity with similar evaluation-level formats such as student debates is helpful but not required
#### Constraints
- The authors have to date only used student error tasks on exams; in-class use is suggested by analogy to student debates, not yet tested by them

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students, including those in large-enrollment classes

### Target Learning Goals
- metacognitive problem-solving skills
- recognizing which problem-solving steps are correct

- [On its first exam use with 358 PHYS 115 students, the student error task yielded a mean score of 60% (median 70%, SD 26%), with 13% of students identifying all three embedded errors](../claims/student-error-task-first-exam-results.md) [+W]
- [Exam responses frequently exhibit combinations of the four errors in a single response, so instructors must be prepared to identify multiple errors at once](../claims/students-combine-multiple-vector-errors.md) [+W]
- [Some students incorrectly apply the Pythagorean theorem, inserting individual components or individual vector magnitudes into the theorem rather than the total components](../claims/students-misapply-pythagorean-theorem-vectors.md) [+W]
- [Some students incorrectly relate the signs of electric field or force components to the signs of the charges, e.g., attaching a negative sign to any component of a negative charge's field or taking absolute values of all charges](../claims/students-misrelate-charge-signs-to-component-signs.md) [+W]
- [Approximately 5-15% of exam responses project r (or r2) rather than the entire vector when finding components, occurring in 16% of PHYS 115, 9% of PHYS 118, and 6% of PHYS 119 responses](../claims/students-project-r-not-entire-vector.md) [+W]
