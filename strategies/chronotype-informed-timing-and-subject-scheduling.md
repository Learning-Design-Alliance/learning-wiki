# Assess students' chronotype before first year and assign late chronotypes to later school timings, and schedule math in the last hours of morning school days

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes two implementable scheduling strategies. First, "assessing students ' chronotype before starting their ﬁrst year of secondary school and assign those with later chronotypes to later school timings", acknowledging that chronotype change during secondary school depends on baseline chronotype. Second, "reorganizing the order of school subjects, letting math for the last hours of the school day, particularly during morning school timing", because the chronotype effect on math is strongest in the morning and for older students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools must operate multiple timings and/or control over subject ordering within the day; chronotype assessment requires an instrument such as the MCTQ
#### Constraints
- The article notes the change in chronotype throughout secondary school also depends on baseline chronotype, and stresses the need for translational studies and more local evidence

### Target Learners
- Adolescents entering and progressing through secondary school

### Target Learning Goals
- Math and language academic performance across school timings
