# Water-balloon analogy and guided group discussion on the real shape of the Earth

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Activity 1 uses a carnival water balloon spun on a table to show that rapid rotation flattens a deformable body at the poles and bulges it at the equator, mirroring Earth's real figure. The article recommends the teacher 'fomentar en los alumnos la expresión de las ideas con que llegaron a clase, y alentar la discusión de ellas en grupo', then converge students' conceptions toward current scientific models, supported by a foam sphere with a darkened equatorial band and printed data on the 0.34% polar flattening.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Water balloons, a 12cm foam sphere with a 16cm rod as rotation axis, small working groups, and students recording individual answers in notebooks before group discussion
#### Constraints
- The flattening is so slight (about 0.34%; the polar circumference only about 21.7km shorter than the equatorial) that it is not discernible from space, unlike Saturn or Jupiter

### Target Learners
- upper secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing that rotation rate can modify significant aspects of a celestial body
- understanding Earth's oblate shape
