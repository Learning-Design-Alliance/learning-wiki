# Challenge Based Learning

## Description
Challenge Based Learning (CBL) is a collaborative, inquiry-driven framework in which learners move from a broad "big idea" (e.g., sustainability, health) to a learner-defined challenge, investigate it through research and fieldwork, then design and implement an evidence-based solution with a real audience. Developed by Apple (2008) with educators, it structures this work through three phases — Engage, Investigate, and Act — each with guiding questions, activities, and resources.

## Design Implications

CBL situates learning in authentic problems, which raises perceived task value and sustains engagement [Learners invest more effort when they perceive a task as relevant and valuable.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]. Because learners work on a whole, ill-structured problem rather than isolated subskills, CBL builds transferable whole-task competence, but only when learners have enough prior knowledge to avoid being overwhelmed by unguided search [Whole-task practice improves transfer of complex skills.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [+M]. The framework's scaffolds (guiding questions, structured activities, checkpoints) are what distinguish it from unguided discovery.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A genuine, open-ended challenge connected to a "big idea" that learners can act on locally
- Collaborative team structures with defined roles and shared artifacts ([Collaboration](../elements/collaboration.md))
- Access to real-world resources, data, experts, or community partners
- Sustained time blocks; CBL typically spans weeks, not single sessions
- Facilitator skill in guiding without solving — questioning, checkpointing, and [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) rather than lecturing

#### Constraints
- Weakly scaffolded versions collapse into unguided discovery, which is ineffective for novices [Minimal-guidance instruction underperforms scaffolded instruction for novice learners.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [-S]
- Deep content coverage can suffer; CBL must be deliberately aligned to curriculum standards or key concepts get skipped [~M]
- Group work without structure and individual accountability allows free-riding and uneven learning [~M]
- Assessment is difficult to standardize; success depends on authentic rubrics and process evidence, not exams alone

#### Implementation Variability
- **Scale**: full-semester challenges vs. short 2–3 week mini-challenges
- **Locus of the challenge**: teacher-nominated big idea with learner-defined challenge questions (typical entry point) vs. fully learner-generated challenges
- **Domain**: science (environmental audits), civics (community campaigns), media/technology (app or prototype design)
- **Hybrid forms**: CBL phases interleaved with direct instruction on needed concepts, blending [Explicit Teaching](../patterns/explicit-teaching.md) with inquiry

### Target Learners
- Learners with moderate prior knowledge who can conduct guided investigation without drowning in search [Whole-task practice improves transfer of complex skills.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [~M]
- Adolescents and adults; younger learners need heavily simplified challenge structures and more facilitator scaffolding
- Learners disengaged by abstract instruction — authentic purpose and audience increase persistence [Learners invest more effort when they perceive a task as relevant and valuable.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Less suitable as a first exposure for complete novices, who need foundational [Procedural Information](../elements/procedural-information.md) before open investigation

### Target Learning Goals
- Whole-task problem solving: framing, investigating, and acting on ill-structured problems [Whole-task practice improves transfer of complex skills.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [+M]
- Collaboration and communication: negotiating roles, producing shared artifacts
- Research and information literacy: gathering, evaluating, and synthesizing evidence
- Agency and self-efficacy: completing a real solution builds confidence that transfers to future challenges [Self-efficacy predicts persistence in academic tasks.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Engage** — present a big idea, have teams generate essential questions and commit to a specific, actionable challenge ([Challenge Identification](../elements/challenge-identification.md))
2. **Investigate** — teams plan and conduct research: background reading, expert interviews, data collection, field observation; facilitator provides just-in-time instruction on needed concepts
3. **Act** — design, implement, and document an evidence-based solution with an authentic audience ([Act](../elements/act.md), [Application of Knowledge](../elements/application-of-knowledge.md))
4. **Assess and reflect** — evaluate solution quality and process using rubrics; deliver feedback on strategy and collaboration, not just the product [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and self-regulation processes.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
5. **Publish and debrief** — share results publicly and reflect on what was learned and what would be done differently

## Related Strategies
- [Project-Based Learning](project-based-learning.md) — CBL is a structured variant with an explicit Engage–Investigate–Act arc and a required real-world action
- [Problem-Based Learning](problem-based-learning.md) — similar inquiry structure but typically anchored in a case rather than a learner-defined challenge
- [Case-Based Learning](case-based-learning.md) — provides the investigation material CBL teams often need
- [Service Learning](service-learning.md) — overlaps in the Act phase when solutions serve a community partner

## Examples
- **[Challenge Based Learning (Digital Promise / Challenge Institute)](https://www.challengebasedlearning.org)** — the official framework site with implementation guides and example challenges from K-12 and higher education
- **Apple's original CBL pilots (2008–2009)** — documented classrooms in six countries where students tackled challenges such as energy conservation and community health, producing measurable behavior-change campaigns
- **University engineering capstones** — teams identify a local infrastructure or sustainability challenge, investigate with municipal data, and prototype solutions presented to city stakeholders
