# Graphic Organizers for Pre-Reading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 13 studies (5 review, 4 causal, 4 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q4` · 4 of 13 report an effect size

## Description
Students identify the text structure (compare/contrast, cause/effect, problem/solution, sequence) before reading and complete a graphic organizer that mirrors that structure as they read. The organizer functions as an [Advance Organizer](../elements/advance-organizers.md): it provides a skeletal framework that learners populate during reading, converting prose into a spatial representation of the text's logic. KWL charts and similar pre-reading frames also activate prior knowledge and set a purpose for reading.

## Design Implications

Graphic organizers reduce extraneous load during reading by externalizing the text's macrostructure, letting working memory attend to meaning-making rather than holding intermediate ideas [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Their effectiveness depends on matching the organizer to the actual text structure — a mismatched organizer forces learners to impose the wrong schema [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [~M]. Combining verbal and spatial representation also exploits dual coding, giving learners two retrieval routes into the content [Dual coding improves recall.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reading materials whose structure can be reliably identified in advance
- Organizer templates matched to common expository structures (compare/contrast matrix, cause/effect chain, timeline, problem/solution frame)
- Brief [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) of how to identify the structure and populate the organizer, especially for students new to the practice
- [Activation](../elements/activation.md) of prior knowledge before reading (e.g., the K and W columns of a KWL chart)
- A text whose structure is identifiable and matches the organizer chosen
- Organizer templates matched to the five common expository structures (description, sequence, compare/contrast, cause/effect, problem/solution)
- A follow-up use for the completed organizer (discussion, writing, summarizing) so it is not an end in itself

#### Constraints
- Students must be taught to identify text structures; without this training they cannot select or benefit from a structure-matched organizer [-M]
- Organizers can oversimplify complex or multi-structural texts, imposing a single schema on layered argumentation [~M]
- Pre-filled or overly detailed organizers encourage copying rather than comprehension; learners who complete organizers passively show weaker gains than those who must generate the relations themselves [~M]
- Benefits diminish for skilled readers with strong prior knowledge of the topic, for whom the organizer adds redundant processing [~M]
- Generic or mismatched organizers add extraneous load: forcing a compare/contrast frame onto a sequential text misdirects attention [~M]
- If the organizer is completed mechanically (copying facts into boxes) without discussion of *relationships*, gains shrink to note-taking benefits [~M]
- Students with strong prior knowledge of the topic may gain little, since the organizer's main work — building a structure to receive new information — is already done [~W]
- Over-reliance can leave students dependent on external frames; organizers should be faded toward student-generated structure maps [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Structure-identified:** teacher names the structure and supplies the matching organizer (strongest for novices)
- **Structure-hunted:** students infer the structure from a preview, then select an organizer (builds transferable structure awareness)
- **KWL charts:** activate prior knowledge and set purpose; weaker structure support but strong [Activation](../elements/activation.md) function
- **Partially completed organizers:** some cells pre-filled to model the format, remaining cells completed during reading (a form of [Fading](../elements/fading.md))
- **Teacher-completed partial organizers**: some components pre-filled to model the mapping, students complete the rest
- **Student-selected organizers**: after structure identification, students choose or sketch the frame themselves — higher transfer demand
- **Signal-word preview**: before reading, students hunt for structure cues ("however," "as a result," "first… then") to justify their organizer choice
- **Post-reading extension**: the same organizer becomes a writing frame for a summary or response

### Target Learners
- Elementary through secondary students reading expository text, particularly struggling readers and English learners [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]
- Students with limited prior knowledge of the topic, who benefit most from the activated schema and structural frame
- Less beneficial for advanced readers already familiar with the text structure and topic [~M]
- Elementary and middle-grade readers encountering expository text, where structure awareness is still developing [+M]
- Students with learning disabilities and struggling readers, who benefit disproportionately from explicit structure supports [Graphic organizer instruction improves recall for students with learning disabilities.](../claims/mnemonic-instruction-improves-recall-for-students-with-disabilities.md) [+M]
- English learners, for whom the visual frame makes relational language ("in contrast," "consequently") visible
- Less valuable for proficient readers with relevant background knowledge, who typically construct structure spontaneously [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension of expository and informational text
- Text-structure awareness: recognizing compare/contrast, cause/effect, and problem/solution patterns as transferable schemas
- Prior-knowledge activation and purpose-setting before reading [Activation improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Summarization and note-taking from informational sources
- Expository reading comprehension: identifying and using text macrostructure
- Content-area learning: retaining relational knowledge in science and social studies texts
- Transfer to writing: composing texts that follow the structures students have mapped

### Instructions
1. Preview the text and identify its dominant structure (or have students infer it from headings and a first-draft skim).
2. Select or have students select the organizer that matches that structure.
3. [Activate](../elements/activation.md) prior knowledge: brainstorm what students already know and what they want to learn (KWL "K" and "W" columns).
4. [Demonstrate](../elements/demonstration.md) completing one section of the organizer with a think-aloud, modeling how textual signals ("however," "as a result") map to organizer cells.
5. Students read and complete the organizer, using [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) to mark structure signals in the text.
6. Debrief: students use the completed organizer to summarize or discuss, converting the visual frame back into connected prose.

## Description
Pre-reading graphic organizers are structure-specific visual frames (e.g., a Venn diagram for compare/contrast, a flowchart for sequence, a cause-effect chain) introduced *before* reading. The teacher names the text structure, models how the organizer maps onto it, and students use the organizer as a scaffold while reading to capture the components of that structure. The organizer functions as both an advance organizer — orienting attention to the relationships that matter — and a note-taking frame that externalizes the text's macrostructure.

## Design Implications

The strategy works because expository comprehension depends heavily on detecting a text's underlying structure; readers who recognize the structure allocate attention to relational information rather than isolated facts [Relevance of emphasis directs attention during reading.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [+M]. Structure-specific organizers outperform generic ones: a Venn diagram helps only when the text actually compares and contrasts, so accurate structure identification is the critical first step. The visual frame also distributes processing across verbal and visual channels, easing the load of building a mental model of an unfamiliar topic [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M].

6. Fade support across texts: from teacher-provided organizers, to student choice among organizers, to student-drawn structure maps.
