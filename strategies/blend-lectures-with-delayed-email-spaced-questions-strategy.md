# Blend face-to-face lectures with delayed e-mail spaced education questions to activate students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's conclusion recommends combining formats: "Adding spaced education to a face-to-face theoretical radiological science course significantly activates students in a way that they spend more time on keeping busy with the learning content." Concretely, after each lecture send multiple-choice questions by e-mail after a 14-day delay, remind after 3 days, and return correct answers with explanatory material. The authors add that the combination "gives a view to a more balanced way of education that does not prefer specific learning styles."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mandatory face-to-face lectures as the base course
- E-mail infrastructure for sending questions, reminders, and feedback
- A delay of about two weeks between lecture and question e-mail, which students considered adequate
#### Constraints
- Pilot evidence from 42 dental students; the authors state results have to be confirmed in larger student populations, and exam reliability (Cronbach's α = .72) should be raised above .8 in future trials

### Target Learners
- third-year dental students
- university students in lecture-based theoretical courses

### Target Learning Goals
- increasing time spent on course content
- supporting all learning styles rather than only those suited to lectures
