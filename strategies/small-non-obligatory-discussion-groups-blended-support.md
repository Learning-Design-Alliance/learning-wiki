# Facilitate online social interaction through small, non-obligatory discussion groups and support teachers to adapt their teaching style

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structuring online collaborative activities as small groups with non-obligatory discussions, and supporting teachers to adapt their teaching style to blended formats. It states: "Our recommendation is to include small groups with non-obligatory discussions in the online learning activities." It also recommends activating prior knowledge by addressing students' posed online questions face-to-face.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An e-moderator and content experts to facilitate and stimulate interaction; small discussion groups of roughly four to six students as cited
#### Constraints
- The evaluated group was a small, highly motivated postgraduate cohort without a control group, so the experience is not immediately generalizable

### Target Learners
- postgraduate health professionals in higher and continuing education

### Target Learning Goals
- social interaction and collaborative learning
- deep learning
- patient safety knowledge and practice
