# Specific Feedback

## Description
This strategy involves providing targeted feedback on student writing that is directly tied to specific words, phrases, sentences, or paragraphs. It focuses on both the 'what' (content such as evidence, claims, and analysis) and the 'how' (writing elements such as organization, rhetorical strategies, style, voice, and conventions). Teachers can focus on the content if they are less comfortable assessing writing mechanics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires teachers to read student writing closely and provide specific comments. Digital tools like Kaizena can facilitate voice note feedback.
#### Constraints
- May require significant time if not focused. Vague feedback can be ineffective.

### Target Learners

- K-12 and higher education students at all levels.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve student writing by guiding them to enhance content, organization, and writing mechanics.

### Affordances
- Helps students understand exactly what needs improvement in their writing, guiding them in content and mechanics. Allows teachers to focus on areas they are most comfortable assessing.

### Personalization
- Teachers can adapt the focus of the feedback based on the assignment or the student's individual needs. Students can also ask for specific feedback on areas they are working on.

- Track the specificity of feedback provided and monitor improvements in student writing over time. Observe student's ability to apply feedback to future writing.

## Impact
- Specific feedback is critical to student learning by guiding them to improve both the content and mechanics of their writing.
