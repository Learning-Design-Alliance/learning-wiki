# Add real olfactory cues matched to target behaviors when designing VR safety simulations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating real-world sensory cues, specifically disinfectant scent, into VR simulation as a design element to elicit authentic safety behavior. The authors state that "olfactory cues may serve as a targeted design element to promote behaviorally realistic, safety-relevant actions in immersive simulations", particularly when the goal is eliciting clinical behavior rather than knowledge alone. They further imply that "effective sensory augmentation may need to match cues to target behaviors based on learned associations".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A real scent applied physically (e.g. to the headset facial interface), reapplied regularly to keep intensity constant
#### Constraints
- Effects were behavior-specific: overall and postcontact hand disinfection increased, but initial hand hygiene and glove use did not; evidence comes from a single VR clinical scenario

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in VR-based simulation courses

### Target Learning Goals
- hand hygiene compliance
- patient safety behavior
- behavioral authenticity in simulation
