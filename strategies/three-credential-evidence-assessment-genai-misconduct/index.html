Assess GenAI misconduct evidence against three probative credentials — relevance, credibility and inferential force — before imposing sanctions¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The study adapts a legal-scholarship evidence credentials framework for institutional misconduct decision-making, evaluating each discrete evidence item on "relevance (whether it matters to the question at issue), credibility (whether it can be trusted), and inferential force (how strongly it supports the allegation)". Ratings use ordinal scales (High/Moderate/Low; Strong/Moderate/Weak) applied to each item, with inferential force assessed as what the item establishes when taken alone. The framework is offered as a diagnostic tool revealing the gap between the evidentiary basis of cases and the formal decision architecture.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Panels must evaluate each discrete evidence item independently on all three dimensions rather than relying on unstructured professional judgement or overall narrative coherence

Constraints¶

  • The article warns reliance on any single evidentiary signal carries inherent risk as LLM capabilities shift, and that the framework's contribution is diagnostic rather than predictive of outcomes under current institutional procedures

Target Learners¶

  • Higher education staff raising and adjudicating academic misconduct allegations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Structured, defensible evaluation of evidence quality in GenAI misconduct cases under the balance of probabilities standard
