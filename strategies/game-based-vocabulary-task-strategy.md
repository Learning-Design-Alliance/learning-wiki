# Use game-based tasks in the final portion of lessons to teach and practice new vocabulary interactively

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends an implicit, task-based approach to vocabulary teaching centered on games, in which learners interact in groups to achieve an end product and use language subconsciously as a means to that end. The authors state the findings "encourage an implicit and task-based approach to teaching vocabularies centering especially on the games." Games are presented as providing "a stress free and joyful environment for learning more vocabulary and a longer retention."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Games requiring learners to interact with each other to achieve the end product
- Teacher monitoring and post-task elicitation of answers and reasons
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single small quasi-experiment with elementary-level young learners

### Target Learners
- young foreign language learners, especially at elementary levels

### Target Learning Goals
- vocabulary learning through interaction and meaningful communication
