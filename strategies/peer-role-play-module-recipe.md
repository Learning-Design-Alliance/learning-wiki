# Use peer role-play with group-developed checklists and scenario exchange as a low-cost active-learning module for counseling skills training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a concrete peer role-play recipe: students work in groups of three, alternating doctor, patient, and observer roles. "Role-play involved a ten-minute encounter followed by five minutes of feedback from the observer of each role-play episode", rated with a group-developed OSCE checklist. Groups developed their own scenario in the first round and exchanged scenarios between groups for the second round, completing three rounds in three-and-a-half hours under faculty supervision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Groups of three students with rotating doctor, patient, and observer roles
- A group-developed OSCE checklist used to rate performance and provide feedback
- Faculty supervision throughout the three-and-a-half-hour session
#### Constraints
- Students may feel a greater burden of training from developing and learning a scenario and exchanging roles
- The authors note peer role-play may not be taken seriously by students

### Target Learners
- fourth-year undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- patient counseling communication skills
- tobacco-cessation counseling performance
