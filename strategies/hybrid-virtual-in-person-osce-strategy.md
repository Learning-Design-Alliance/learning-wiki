# Adopt a hybrid model combining virtual and in-person OSCE components, assigning telehealth-suitable skills to virtual stations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that "A hybrid model combining virtual and in-person components could offer a balanced solution": virtual assessments focus on skills like telemedicine counseling and data interpretation, while in-person sessions address physical examination competencies. This follows their finding that removing physical examination was a significant limitation and that students recommended retaining virtual telehealth stations. It is an implementable assessment-design recipe for OSCE planners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional IT infrastructure and faculty commitment
- Robust SP preparation and training on the virtual platform
- Careful resource planning and support
#### Constraints
- The article notes significant financial and logistical demands in organizing virtual OSCEs
- Virtual format cannot capture physical examination competencies

### Target Learners
- medical students in clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- telemedicine counseling
- data interpretation
- physical examination skills
