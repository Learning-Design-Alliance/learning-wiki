# Stepped evaluation exercise for assessing pseudoscientific claims at increasing levels of difficulty

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes a graduated teaching strategy in which students evaluate a pseudoscientific work through steps of increasing difficulty matched to their level. "It can begin with searching the literature for information regarding the claims made by the author in question." Next, introductory-level exercises (such as the adiabatic compression derivation and collision analysis) test basic claims. Finally, a more advanced approach incorporates computational checks as part of a project, with the molecular dynamics simulation serving as the model case. The exercise should highlight sloppy reasoning, lack of knowledge of basic facts, and reliance on vague intuitions, not only that conclusions contradict established results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The exercise works through one or two concrete claims rather than attempting to refute every statement in the work
#### Constraints
- The article advises against pointing out the author by name in the teaching context and warns that conversations with authors of pseudoscientific treatises rarely lead to anything but frustration

### Target Learners
- aspiring scientists and physics students at increasing levels of difficulty

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating pseudoscientific work for oneself
- distinguishing legitimate new theories from pseudoscientific claims
