# Ask students to formulate hypotheses about the experiment's outcome before observing it, then resolve them through the demonstration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Before showing the elevator experiment, students were asked to predict what a person inside a free-falling elevator would perceive; the article identifies this as Inquiry-Based Learning, in which "effort is therefore followed in order to develop the creative thinking of pupils at the expense of a drill and memorizing." Students proposed three hypotheses (crashing into the ceiling, feeling heavier, or rising and floating), which the experiment then confirmed or refuted, prompting a collective theoretical discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An experimental demonstration whose outcome can disprove the formulated hypotheses
- A collective discussion afterward to analyze the observations theoretically
#### Constraints
- The article notes the least popular hypothesis (floating) was the correct one, so the strategy works by surfacing and then disproving common misconceptions

### Target Learners
- secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- formulating and testing scientific hypotheses
- understanding the equivalence principle
