# Use a game balancing technique when assigning students to competition-based persuasive conditions to avoid discouraging competition-averse students.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This strategy addresses the risk that leaderboard-based competition discourages vulnerable students. The article states that because "competition-averse students exposed to a leaderboard may get discouraged and frustrated," the authors employed "a game design technique called ‘game balancing’" when assigning participants to the competition strategy. Students' grades predicted by SARA were used to balance ability within the competition group, and only students with high competition susceptibility and predicted grades of 75% and above were placed in the tailored competition group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Predicted grades or equivalent ability estimates for each student
- A validated measure of each student's susceptibility to the competition strategy
#### Constraints
- The article notes previous research indicates the risk arises especially for students who are not doing well in the class

### Target Learners
- University students assigned to competitive persuasive interventions

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining motivation and engagement when competitive persuasive strategies are used
