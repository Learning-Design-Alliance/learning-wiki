# Use plan-do-study-act cycles incorporating simulation to identify and revise process, infection control, and equipment threats in new workflows

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 5 studies (3 design, 1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
During the COVID-19 pandemic, teams used simulation embedded in plan-do-study-act cycles to test and revise new patient care workflows in real time. Lie and colleagues "reported the use of plan-do-study-act cycles incorporating simulation to identify process threats, infection control threats, and equipment or PPE issues and then modified their COVID-19 patient care workflow based on their findings." Similar simulated drills tested operating room setup changes and maternity ward floor plan revisions, with findings driving protocol changes such as designating an operating room coordinator.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation sessions must feed identified threats back into revision of the workflow, as in the PDSA cycle structure
#### Constraints
- Described in the context of pandemic-era obstetric care workflow changes

### Target Learners
- Multidisciplinary obstetric and anesthesia care teams
- Health care organizations preparing new workflows

### Target Learning Goals
- Workflow and process testing
- Infection prevention and control preparedness
- Equipment and PPE issue identification
