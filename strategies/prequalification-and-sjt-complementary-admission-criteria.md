# Use professional prequalification and SJTs as complementary undergraduate admission criteria with differentiated interpersonal foci

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advises medical schools to review admission criteria in light of the two non-cognitive criteria's differentiated predictive foci. The conclusion states that professional prequalification and SJTs "can serve as valuable criteria to screen for personal characteristics in undergraduate medical admission with different foci": prequalification relates to confident, assured-dominant behavior, while HAM-SJT performance relates to considerate, warm-agreeable behavior. Schools are advised to weigh which personal characteristics are required for admission and which can be developed through the course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An admission process must administer a situational judgement test and record self-reported completion of healthcare-related vocational training before admission, as in the Hamburg procedure studied here
#### Constraints
- The article notes students without professional prequalification may develop the same level of confidence during medical tasks, so that differences are no longer present at graduation
- The article notes most medical curricula include communication skills courses through which initial differences in empathetic skills may diminish over the course of study

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical applicants
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- interpersonal behavior in social interactions with patients, relatives, and colleagues
