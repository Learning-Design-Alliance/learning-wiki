# Use a task-based pre-assessment questionnaire to stream learners by prior experience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because learners regularly under- or over-estimate their knowledge when asked directly, Software Carpentry developed a short pre-assessment questionnaire that "asks them whether they could accomplish specific tasks", such as writing a small plotting program, using Git, writing unit tests, or constructing an SQL join. Used as a sorting hat, it streams learners into parallel rooms by prior experience, which the author says simplifies teaching and improves learners' experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Running two or more rooms side by side so streamed groups can be taught separately
- Task-based self-report items rather than self-rated familiarity alone
#### Constraints
- The questionnaire is described as far from perfect, working well enough for the author's purposes
- Learners are not shuffled between rooms after day one, since this caused duplication and missed topics

### Target Learners
- Mixed-experience adult learners in computing workshops

### Target Learning Goals
- Appropriate pacing and level of instruction for learners of differing prior experience
