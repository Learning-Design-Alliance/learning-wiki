# Prioritise rigorous intervention research with standardised behavioural outcome measures to build causal evidence for communication skills training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review recommends that future research in communication skills training for medical undergraduates must be built on strong scientific evidence, starting with measures of clinical behaviour for intervention research. It states "It is important to establish measures that are able to determine meaningful change in observed behaviours, as well as demonstrating high inter-rater reliability", and that improvements in the volume and quality of intervention research are paramount to provide causal knowledge and level one evidence to inform training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires standardised measures able to determine meaningful change in observed behaviours with high inter-rater reliability
- Requires intervention trials reported in sufficient detail to allow replication in a real-world setting
#### Constraints
- The review found less than half of existing intervention studies met rigorous design criteria, indicating current evidence is insufficient

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- patient-clinician communication and interactional skills
