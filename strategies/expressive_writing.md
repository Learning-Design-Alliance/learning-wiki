# Expressive Writing

## Description
Expressive writing involves putting thoughts and feelings into words, often focusing on emotionally charged or stressful experiences. Learners write about their deepest thoughts and emotions regarding a looming test or stressful situation. Anonymity is key to encourage openness. The process helps learners offload suppressed thoughts and emotions, decreasing stress and improving health and academic outcomes. Writing interventions can also focus on values that are important to an individual and making meaning of life circumstances in the context of those values or writing about one’s “best possible future self” to improve emotional regulation and promote health and happiness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a short writing activity (15-30 minutes) before a test or when facing stressful academic situations. Requires anonymity to encourage openness.
#### Constraints
- May temporarily increase negative feelings immediately after the activity if focusing on stressful experiences. Not effective for every outcome. Eliciting negative emotions in the classroom could potentially backfire depending on an individual student’s prior experiences; thus, it is important to strike a balance between personal relevance and potentially counter-productive emotional elicitation. May not benefit those with low anxiety levels.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- K-12, higher education, and adult learners experiencing test anxiety, stereotype threat, or general stress related to academic performance

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Reduce stress, improve emotional regulation, free up working memory, enhance academic performance, reduce math anxiety and mitigate stereotype threat

### Affordances
- Helps learners manage emotions, promotes self-regulation, frees up cognitive resources, and enhances academic performance by addressing underlying anxieties and stress. Can also improve overall well-being.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted to focus on different aspects of learner experience (stressful experiences, values, or aspirations). The best future self prompt may be useful in an educational context. Prompts should be personally meaningful to students to be effective.

## Assessment Evidence
- Improved test scores, reduced math anxiety, enhanced emotional regulation, decreased stress levels, improved working memory capacity.

## Impact
- Studies show expressive writing boosts exam performance, reduces achievement gaps, and mitigates math anxiety. Benefits are often most significant for those with high anxiety levels. It is an inexpensive and time-efficient intervention.
