# Implement comprehensive nutrition education from the undergraduate level, coupled with continuing professional development and interprofessional collaboration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building nutritional competency through a layered educational model: foundational clinical nutrition content embedded in undergraduate nursing programmes, ongoing continuing professional development to keep nurses proficient in evidence‐based practice, and interprofessional collaboration among nurses, doctors and dietitians. The authors state that "implementing comprehensive nutrition education from the undergraduate level provides nurses with foundational knowledge essential for effective clinical practice". They cite evidence that a collaborative curriculum model involving dietitians and nurse educators improved nursing students' nutrition‐focused care competencies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration of nutrition content across nursing curricula with consistent teaching methods and faculty expertise
- Continuing professional development structures in employing institutions
#### Constraints
- The study's own evidence is cross‐sectional and single‐site; the recommendation rests partly on cited external studies rather than on interventions tested in this study

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students
- Early‐career and practising oncology nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- Nutritional assessment and screening
- Malnutrition risk identification
- Nutritional patient education and counselling
- Interprofessional collaboration in nutritional care
