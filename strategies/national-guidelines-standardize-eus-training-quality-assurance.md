# National guidelines to standardize EUS training and quality assurance processes across Royal College EM programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that heterogeneity in resident EUS training and practice suggests variability in level and quality of training, and states "These results suggest a potential role for national guidelines to standardize ultrasound training for all Royal College EM programs." It also identifies a key deficiency: concurrent 2011 data reported that 69% of Royal College EM programs had no formal quality assurance process for EUS while residents and faculty made clinical decisions based on EUS interpretation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs making clinical decisions based on EUS interpretation should have a quality assurance process, which CAEP and ACEP both support
#### Constraints
- The article notes the survey reflects resident perceptions and may not reflect actual training reality; 11% of respondents had external EUS fellowship participation, possibly overestimating training provided

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine residents and faculty in Canadian Royal College residency programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Standardized, quality-assured emergency ultrasound training
