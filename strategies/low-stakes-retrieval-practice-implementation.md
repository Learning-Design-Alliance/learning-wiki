# Implement retrieval practice as frequent low- or no-stakes quizzes with feedback, framed as learning opportunities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends incorporating frequent quizzes (short-answer or multiple-choice, online or face-to-face) and end-of-class summary recall into courses, ideally with feedback, since these formats improved targeted and related material in the summarized studies. It stresses that the effective interventions were "no-stakes recall practice" or "low-stakes quizzes," so implementations should avoid high-stakes framing to minimize anxiety and stereotype threat, and should be combined with other evidence-based practices such as aligned objectives and practice opportunities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Quizzes should be no- or low-stakes and articulated as learning opportunities; feedback should be provided where possible.
#### Constraints
- The review warns that a collaborative test given immediately after a high-stakes individual test showed no significant retention effect, and that stereotype threat can impair performance in high-stakes evaluation scenarios for identity-threatened groups.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science students
- community college students in introductory biology

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term retention of course content
- transfer of concepts across domains
- student metacognition about study strategies
