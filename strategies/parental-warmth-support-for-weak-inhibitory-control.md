# Support academic development of students with weaker inhibitory-control circuitry by strengthening parental warmth and supportive home environments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends parental warmth (acceptance and affection from parents) as an intervention direction for early adolescents with weaker fronto-striatal connectivity. The authors argue warmth "may help cultivate a supportive family environment for adolescents, making it easier for adolescents to engage in self‐control regardless of their fronto‐striatal connectivity", and that parents "can neutralize the negative effects by showing love to adolescents, which are feasible strategies for most parents". They also suggest school self-regulated-learning interventions should consider individual differences in students' inhibitory-control systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Family-based intervention components that increase parental acceptance and affection toward the adolescent
#### Constraints
- The moderation effect sizes are very small, so practical implications should be taken with caution
- Evidence is correlational; the buffering effect has not been tested experimentally

### Target Learners
- early adolescents with weaker fronto-striatal connectivity
- parents and caregivers of early adolescents

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement
- self-control in academic work
