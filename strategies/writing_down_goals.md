# Writing Down Goals

## Description
Writing down goals converts vague intentions into explicit, reviewable commitments. The act of articulating a goal in writing forces clarification of what one wants to achieve and why, supports prioritization by making importance and feasibility comparable across goals, and creates a durable artifact that can be revisited, monitored, and revised. In learning contexts, written goals typically specify what will be learned, by when, and by what evidence of success.

## Design Implications

Written goals function as external memory and self-regulatory anchors: they cue effort, support monitoring against a standard, and enable feedback loops that unrecorded intentions do not [Monitoring goal progress increases goal attainment.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+S]. The specificity of the written formulation matters — goals framed as processes and strategies ("complete two practice problems daily") tend to serve novices better than distant outcome goals ("master algebra") [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]. Writing goals also strengthens commitment by engaging learners in evaluating why a goal matters, which supports persistence when difficulty rises [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated time for reflection and articulation; writing tools (journal, digital document, planning template)
- Goals specific enough to judge progress against — vague statements ("do better in math") cannot be monitored
- A review routine: written goals only regulate behavior if they are revisited and progress is checked [Monitoring goal progress increases goal attainment.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+S]

#### Constraints
- Writing goals alone does not produce achievement; without monitoring and follow-through, the artifact becomes decoration [-M]
- Overly ambitious or numerous written goals can demotivate when progress stalls; goal-setting research shows performance gains depend on commitment and perceived attainability [~S]
- For novices, outcome-focused written goals can direct attention away from the strategies that actually produce learning [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Format varies: bullet lists, learning contracts, mind maps, vision boards, or structured templates (goal, rationale, deadline, success criteria)
- Social variants add accountability: sharing written goals with a peer, mentor, or cohort, or using public commitment
- Pairing written goals with implementation intentions — specifying when, where, and how the goal-directed action will occur — substantially increases follow-through [+S]

### Target Learners
- Adolescents and adults with enough self-regulatory capacity to revisit and act on written commitments [Monitoring goal progress increases goal attainment.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+S]
- Learners struggling with direction or motivation, for whom explicit written goals clarify priorities and build efficacy through visible progress [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Less effective for young children, who need externally managed goals and immediate feedback structures

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-regulation: planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's own learning
- Motivation and persistence: clarifying purpose and sustaining commitment
- Prioritization and time management: choosing among competing objectives

### Instructions
1. Prompt learners to reflect on what they want to achieve and why, using [Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md) to surface priorities before writing.
2. Have learners write specific goals with a rationale, deadline, and success criteria; guide formulation toward process-level goals for novices [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M].
3. Add implementation details: when, where, and how the work will happen.
4. Schedule regular check-ins ([Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md)) where learners compare progress against the written goal and revise as needed [Monitoring goal progress increases goal attainment.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+S].
5. Connect goals to [Practice](../elements/practice.md) — the written goal should name the recurring activities that produce progress.

## Related Strategies
- **Learning Contracts** — formalized written goals agreed with an instructor, adding accountability
- **Implementation Intentions** — the "when/where/how" complement that converts written goals into action triggers
- **Progress Monitoring** — the review routine that makes written goals effective rather than decorative

## Examples
- **[Georgia State University's GPS Advising](https://success.gsu.edu)** — students' academic goals and degree plans are recorded and tracked, with advisors reviewing progress against written milestones.
- **Learning portfolios in medical education** — residents write explicit learning goals each rotation and review them with faculty, a documented practice in graduate medical education programs.
- **Classroom goal-setting journals** — students record weekly learning goals with success criteria, then self-assess against them in Friday reflections.
