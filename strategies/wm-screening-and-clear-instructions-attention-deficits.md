# Use WM capacity measures for early identification and give clear, one-item-at-a-time instructions to children with attention deficits

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article draws practical implications from its findings: measures of WM capacity can identify children at risk of failing at school, since children with attention deficits and special needs scored lower in mathematics than children from regular classes. It also recommends that "clear instructions, without unneces sary words, focusing on one item at a time (BNST), seem to be beneficial for children with attention deficits", and underscores teaching that minimises WM overload. Teachers are advised to be familiar with WM functions because weaknesses in WM ability seem to affect school performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need to be familiar with WM functions to apply the strategy as the article describes
#### Constraints
- The article states the intervention and control groups were not randomised, so the underlying results cannot be generalised and must be considered with caution

### Target Learners
- children aged 9–12 with attention deficits and special educational needs in small-group settings

### Target Learning Goals
- basic number skills and mathematics achievement
