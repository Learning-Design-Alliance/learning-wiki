# Two Stars And A Wish

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Two Stars and a Wish is a low-stakes peer feedback protocol: each reviewer identifies two specific strengths of a peer's work and one actionable improvement, phrased as a wish ("I wish you would…") rather than a criticism. The fixed 2:1 ratio and simple sentence frames make feedback accessible to young or inexperienced reviewers while guaranteeing that every round of feedback contains both affirmation and a next step.

## Design Implications

The protocol operationalizes [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md): feedback that describes current performance against criteria and points to the next step improves achievement, especially when learners act on it [Formative feedback practices improve achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. The "stars" component is not mere cushioning — naming what works requires evaluative judgment against criteria, which develops the reviewer's own understanding of quality. The single "wish" keeps improvement focused; one prioritized suggestion is more likely to be acted on than a list of deficits.

### Context
#### Requirements
- The work under review must be visible to peers (drafts, performances, products)
- Shared criteria or a rubric so "stars" and "wishes" reference quality, not taste
- Sentence frames or modeling of specific, kind, useful comments before the first round
- Time for the author to read feedback and revise — feedback without a revision opportunity rarely changes work

#### Constraints
- Generic praise ("nice job") satisfies the format without informing learning; teachers must require evidence-linked comments tied to criteria
- The 2:1 ratio can train learners to expect praise and soften or bury the improvement; over time the "wish" should carry more weight
- Novice reviewers often lack the expertise to diagnose *why* something is weak, producing wishes that are vague or wrong [~M] — pair with exemplars or a checklist to scaffold judgment
- Online implementations with checkbox-style or rubric-only evaluation lose the diagnostic specificity that makes the protocol work [Checklist-based evaluation is less effective for online feedback.](../claims/checklist-evaluation-ineffective-online.md) [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Oral gallery walk**: work posted around the room; peers leave sticky notes with two stars and a wish
- **Written slips or digital comments**: Google Docs comments, Padlet, or Seesaw annotations
- **Two Stars and a Step**: replaces "wish" with a concrete next step, tightening actionability for older learners
- **Author-selected wish**: reviewer offers two stars and two candidate wishes; the author chooses which to act on, building evaluative autonomy
- **Reverse protocol**: authors pre-identify their own two stars and one wish before peer review, priming self-assessment

### Target Learners
- Elementary and lower-secondary students, for whom the sentence frames make peer feedback tractable [+W]
- Novice writers and creators at any age who need both encouragement and a single clear next step
- Less suitable as-is for advanced learners, who benefit from more extended critique and multiple improvement points [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving product quality through revision (writing, design, problem-solving)
- Developing evaluative judgment — recognizing quality criteria by applying them to others' work
- Building a supportive feedback culture and peer-learning norms

### Instructions
1. Share the task criteria or rubric and model the protocol on a sample work, thinking aloud about what makes a comment specific ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) modeling of the feedback itself).
2. Pair or group learners and distribute work for review.
3. Each reviewer writes two stars — strengths tied to criteria — and one wish phrased as an actionable suggestion.
4. Authors read feedback and identify one change to make; build in time for revision ([Practice](../elements/practice.md) with feedback as the scaffold).
5. Debrief: collect example stars and wishes that were most useful, refining the class's feedback quality over successive rounds.

## Examples
- **Primary writing workshops** (e.g., Units of Study classrooms): students exchange drafts and complete star/wish slips before revising.
- **[Seesaw](https://web.seesaw.me)**: classmates record audio two-stars-and-a-wish comments on posted work in elementary portfolios.
- **Design critique in maker education**: project teams present prototypes; audience members give two stars and a wish on sticky notes before the next iteration.
