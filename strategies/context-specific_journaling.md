# Context-Specific Journaling

## Description
Context-specific journaling requires individuals to reflect on specific experiences such as team assignments, internships, or professional practices. This involves writing down observations about their actions, expressing their thinking about those actions, and describing the significance of the actions. This reflective process is driven by experiential learning, catering to the preferences of adult learners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a journal (physical or digital), writing utensils, and dedicated time for journaling. Instructors or facilitators need to provide prompts and feedback to guide reflection.
#### Constraints
- May require a specific experience or context for effective implementation. Requires learners to be willing to engage in honest self-reflection.

### Target Learning Goals

### Affordances
- Connects observations, thoughts, and significance of actions. Aligns with the experiential learning preferences of adult learners. Provides a structured way to reflect on specific experiences.

### Personalization
- Allow learners to focus on experiences that are most relevant to their personal and professional goals. Provide different prompts for reflection based on the type of experience.

- Analyze journal entries to assess the depth of reflection, the quality of observations, and the understanding of the significance of actions. Evaluate changes in thinking and behavior over time.

## Impact
- Supports experiential learning and helps learners make meaning of their experiences. Develops self-awareness and critical thinking skills.
