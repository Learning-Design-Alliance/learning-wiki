# Teach Ampère-Maxwell law with unconventional curves, inverse problems, multiple surfaces, and sense-making tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that teaching-learning sequences challenge students to calculate magnetic fields using non-standard Amperian curves and problems where the field cannot be extracted from the line integral, so students reflect on the relationship between symmetry, fields and circulation. They state it is "crucial to develop teaching-learning sequences that challenge students to calculate the magnetic field from this law, using unconventional curves and solving inverse problems". They further recommend analysing magnetic field lines in configurations with conduction and displacement currents, applying the law to different surfaces bounded by the same curve, and using non-routinely formulated problems and sense-making activities such as 'Ranking Task' and 'Conflicting Contentions'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sequences must challenge students to calculate the magnetic field from the law rather than only apply symmetric-curve procedures
#### Constraints
- The small sample of students limits the generalisability of the study, per the authors

### Target Learners
- introductory electromagnetism university students

### Target Learning Goals
- deep understanding of magnetic field circulation
- recognising that Ampère-Maxwell's law states correlations, not cause-effect relationships
