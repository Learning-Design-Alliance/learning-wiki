# Balance cognitive load in digital learning by aligning design-factor costs and benefits with desired learning outcomes via constructive alignment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review proposes that designers weigh both positive and negative load effects of digital design factors rather than uniformly minimizing extraneous load. "By including aspects of constructive alignment, an approach aimed at fostering deep forms of learning in order to achieve specific learning outcomes, we devise a strategy to balance cognitive load in digital learning." Because some benefits (e.g., interactive or realistic learning gains) are detectable only with suitable assessment methods, tests should be aligned with the design factor's advantages.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment methods must be aligned with the learning outcomes and design factors so positive effects are detectable
- Designers must judge whether a design factor's extraneous load is integral or irrelevant to the target task
#### Constraints
- The strategy is offered as theory-guided and empirically testable rather than as a validated prescription; the review notes cost-benefit analysis alone cannot explain divergent retention versus transfer effects

### Target Learners
- learners in digital and online learning environments, including professional domains such as surgical training

### Target Learning Goals
- retention, transfer, motivation, and engagement in technology-enhanced learning
