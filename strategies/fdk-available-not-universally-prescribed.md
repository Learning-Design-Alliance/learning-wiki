# Make full-day kindergarten available to all children without universally prescribing it, and prioritize stronger causal designs and nonacademic outcomes in future research

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's closing recommendation is that access to FDK be universal as an option while enrollment remains a choice: "full-day kindergarten should be available to all children but not necessarily universally prescribed." This follows from findings that benefits are positive but modest and fade by third grade, and that social outcomes are mixed. The authors also call for future studies "that allow strong causal inferences and that include more nonacademic outcomes."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should attend to the content and instructional strategies used, which both proponents and skeptics agree are "paramount to its success or failure," since these may mediate whether FDK has positive, negative, or no effects.
#### Constraints
- Current evidence rests mostly on unpublished, non-randomized studies with small samples; the article notes research "would benefit from future studies that allow strong causal inferences."

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten-age children (ages 4–6) in the United States and Canada

### Target Learning Goals
- Early academic achievement and readiness
- Social development and well-being in the transition to school
