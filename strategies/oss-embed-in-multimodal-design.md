# Integrate obesity simulation suits into broader multimodal designs with structured prebriefing, guided reflection, and debriefing rather than using them as standalone interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that OSS be combined with didactic prebriefing, experiential tasks, and structured debriefing or reflection, and embedded within broader curricula on obesity, communication, or patient-centered care. It states that "structured pre-briefing, guided reflection, and debriefing appear essential to contextualize the experience and prevent misinterpretation," and that OSS should be integrated from the outset into well-structured teaching designs rather than used as isolated interventions. Complementary approaches such as standardized patients with lived experience and VR are suggested.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All studies included post-simulation debriefing or reflection; debriefing should be systematically integrated following such activities
#### Constraints
- Current evidence does not demonstrate consistent or durable effects on weight bias or empathy, so OSS appear insufficient as standalone interventions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and postgraduate healthcare students, including medicine, nursing, nutrition science, and exercise science

### Target Learning Goals
- reduce weight bias
- foster empathy and perspective-taking toward people with obesity
