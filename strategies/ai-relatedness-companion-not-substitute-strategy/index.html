Position AI chatbots for relatedness as companions that normalize help-seeking and connect students to human communities, not substitutes for them¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Because students with lower baseline relatedness perceived more relatedness support from chatbots, the authors identify a design opportunity: "AI chatbots may be most valuable for relatedness when they help students feel accompanied, normalize help-seeking, or connect them back to human learning communities, rather than becoming a substitute for those communities." This positions chatbot social support as a bridge to, not a replacement for, peer and instructor relationships, most salient for students beginning with weaker feelings of connection.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Design and user expectations that evoke rapport and social presence, which prior HCI work the article cites says relational experiences depend strongly on

Constraints¶

  • The negative baseline-relatedness path should not be interpreted as evidence that chatbots can replace peer, instructor, or classroom relationships

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students, especially those with weaker initial feelings of connection

Target Learning Goals¶

  • relatedness need support and normalized help-seeking
