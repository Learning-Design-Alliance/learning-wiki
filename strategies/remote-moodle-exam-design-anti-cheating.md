# Design remote online exams with 30–40% weekly-exercise questions, randomized algebraic tasks, added time pressure, and adjusted pass thresholds (pass at 40%, best grade at 85%)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structuring a Moodle exam taken on students' own devices at home around preventing internet searching and peer communication. Concept tasks use situational transfer and answer-explanation combinations so found explanations "must [be] understand[ed] well enough to exclude the wrong answers"; algebraic tasks use random numbers; and more complex questions create time pressure compensated by lower grading thresholds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Automatic grading via Moodle with random numbers in algebraic tasks, and essay-type questions of varying difficulty whose copied wordings can be found by comparing student texts
#### Constraints
- The authors state time pressure alone could result in a lack of time for solving questions independently, and equivalent random question variants and randomized option order were not used due to time constraints

### Target Learners
- first- and second-semester engineering and STEM students taking a remotely administered final exam

### Target Learning Goals
- valid assessment of physics understanding in a virtual open-book remote exam setting
