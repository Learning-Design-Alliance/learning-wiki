# Question-driven lab report that lets students decide which measurements to perform

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Instead of giving assembly instructions, the authors' report asks questions on the Physics involved, and students figure out which measurements are needed to answer them. Students are given much liberty on the conduct of the project and are free to spend more or less time on the different parts. The authors state that student reports analyzing differences between measurements and manufacturer specifications "ensures that the experiment has been well understood."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An experiment whose parts can be reduced or extended (the article notes a shorter project can be reduced to its first part)
- Report questions pitched at the physics rather than at procedure
#### Constraints
- Designed for two-week periods worked mainly over half-days; the authors note it could alternatively be offered as a full-time one-week project

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate Physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental design decision-making
- understanding of interferometry, FTS and optical amplification
