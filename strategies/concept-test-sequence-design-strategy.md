# Design concept test sequences that vary representation and difficulty level

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors develop concept tests through an iterative process and organize questions on a topic into sequences so students learn "the same concept from different perspectives", exploiting verbal, graphical, and mathematical representations across successive questions. Sequences are deliberately ordered as easy-moderate-difficult or easy-difficult-difficult to help students organize knowledge, and some sequences precede simulations so students predict, discuss, and then observe the phenomenon.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions developed iteratively based on prior research on student difficulties and instructor/student feedback
- A sequence covering one concept from multiple representations
#### Constraints
- In the reported sequence, the difficult graphical question (CT3) posed novel situations the students had not explicitly learned in detail

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduate quantum mechanics students

### Target Learning Goals
- building intuition about abstract quantum concepts and organizing knowledge hierarchically
