# Use a two-phase modified Delphi approach with a purposive voting panel to prioritize competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 5 studies (4 design, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study demonstrates a replicable strategy for competency development: an interdisciplinary expert panel generates initial items through group meetings and 1:1 interviews, then a separate purposively-sampled voting panel rates each competency on a 1–9 modified RAND appropriateness scale across two rounds. Items receiving "high priority scores (defined as ≥7/9) from ≥75% of the voting panel" are included; deferred items meeting a priori criteria are re-voted. Qualitative comments are adjudicated by expert consensus between rounds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A purposively assembled expert panel and a separate diverse voting panel committed to two rounds of voting
#### Constraints
- Purposive sampling may over-represent individuals with particular interest in the topic; broader representative sampling is suggested for validation

### Target Learners
- Hospice and palliative medicine physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- Standardized definition of primary mental health competencies
