# Use multidisciplinary clinician feedback on learner-generated reports to evaluate educational interventions, with informal clinician feedback where formal grading is impractical

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends assessing educational success "through the lens of the referring clinician" rather than relying on participant confidence surveys. Educational initiatives should help learners "communicate actionable findings," and outcome metrics that directly assess performance on example cases graded by clinicians provide a better assessment than surveys. Where formal blinded grading is impractical, informal feedback from non-radiologist clinicians can identify deficiencies and gauge progress.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration with referring-clinician colleagues (e.g., otolaryngology, plastic surgery)
- Assessment cases representative of the targeted reporting skill
#### Constraints
- Formal report evaluation is likely impractical in many settings
- Multidisciplinary structured report templates were suggested but not evaluated in this investigation

### Target Learners
- radiology residents and attendings
- medical trainees in reporting-intensive specialties

### Target Learning Goals
- producing accurate, clinically relevant, actionable radiology reports
- aligning reporting language with referring clinicians' expectations
