# Implement sign language-based literacy training in school as short daily sessions with teacher support discussing differences between sign language and written language syntax

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention was delivered in school over 4 weeks (20 days) with participants encouraged to work at least 10 min per day. Participants were "free to work with both Create and Test exercises to whatever extent they chose, but were encouraged to focus on the former type of exercises since these were designed to established connection between signed and written language." Teachers sat next to participants when possible and "supported training by, for example, initiating discussions on differences in syntactic structure across sign language and written language." This embeds computerized practice within ordinary school routines and pairs it with explicit sign-to-written-language comparison.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the Omega-is-d2 program in the school setting
- Teacher availability to sit with participants and discuss sign and written language syntax
#### Constraints
- Implementation succeeded only 'whenever possible without neglecting their regular duties'
- One participant received no training due to absence from school

### Target Learners
- DHH signing pupils in special schools for deaf and hard-of-hearing children

### Target Learning Goals
- Word reading
- Reading comprehension
- Understanding syntactic differences between sign language and written language
