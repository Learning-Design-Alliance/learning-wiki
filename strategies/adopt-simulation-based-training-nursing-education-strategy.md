# Adopt simulation-based training in nursing education, particularly high-fidelity simulations, to develop students' essential skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that nursing programs integrate simulation-based training into preclinical courses, with particular emphasis on high-fidelity simulation. It states that "nursing education schools should consider adopting simulation-based training methods, particularly high-fidelity simulations, to effectively develop and improve students' essential skills." The recommendation rests on the study's significant problem-solving and self-efficacy gains and its null critical thinking result under moderate fidelity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation-trained instructors, standardized scenarios, and orientation for students, as used in the study's course
#### Constraints
- The study's own moderate-fidelity, 4-week course did not significantly change critical thinking skills; the authors suggest longer duration and randomized control groups for future studies

### Target Learners
- second-year undergraduate nursing students in preclinical courses

### Target Learning Goals
- problem-solving skills
- self-efficacy
- critical thinking skills
