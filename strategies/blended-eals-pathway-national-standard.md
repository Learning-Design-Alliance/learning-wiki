# Deliver the national ALS standard through ALS, blended electronic ALS (e-ALS) or a formally recognised equivalent pathway, with local simulation reinforcing rather than replacing formal training.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that a mandatory model need not require every doctor to attend an inflexible two-day course in identical circumstances. The standard could be delivered through ALS, blended electronic ALS (e-ALS) or a formally recognised equivalent pathway, provided the endpoint is nationally defined and externally defensible. Local simulation should reinforce formal training, not replace it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- the endpoint is nationally defined and externally defensible
- access funded, protected and planned early
#### Constraints
- NHS England already routes e-ALS access for foundation doctors through employing trusts and allows reimbursement within prescribed limits, so the infrastructure for a more standardised model already partly exists

### Target Learners
- foundation doctors

### Target Learning Goals
- advanced life support
- resuscitation preparedness
