# Replace Wien's displacement law with the average photon energy in introductory blackbody instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends conveying the concept that hotter blackbodies radiate higher-energy photons by presenting the average energy of emitted photons, given analytically as ⟨Ephot⟩ = (π⁴/30ζ(3))kT = (3.7294 × 10⁻²³ J/K)·T, instead of Wien's displacement law. The authors argue this is "more concrete, and hence easier to grasp," because photons are more easily visualized and the mean is the most easily understood statistical measure of a distribution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors present the average photon energy formula and examples showing how it depends on temperature in place of Wien's-law examples
#### Constraints
- The authors suggest Wien's displacement law be introduced only in higher-level classes, once students understand the dispersion rule used to create the spectrum

### Target Learners
- introductory physics and astronomy students, including non-science majors

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding that hotter blackbodies emit higher-energy photons on average
