# Use standardized patient social history encounters, reflective writing, and a mini-OSCE practice to teach SDH history collection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 11 claims (8 for, 3 mixed) · 7 studies (3 causal, 3 review, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 7 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a concrete activity sequence: standardized patients "authentically reproduced emotional responses and healthcare-seeking dilemmas" so students had to actively obtain social histories; instructors used a social factors assessment checklist for immediate feedback; students completed one mini-OSCE practice on social history collection in a low-stakes environment; and reflective journals prompted metacognitive awareness of incorporating social context into clinical reasoning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Uniformly trained standardized patients, a social factors assessment checklist, and case backgrounds different from the formal assessment for the mini-OSCE
#### Constraints
- The strategy was evaluated within a single esophageal cancer case with locally relevant Sichuan SDH factors; other settings require contextualizing SDH factors and training SPs for local scenarios

### Target Learners
- fifth-year undergraduate medical students on surgical internship

### Target Learning Goals
- collecting and documenting social histories and responding to patients' non-medical needs
