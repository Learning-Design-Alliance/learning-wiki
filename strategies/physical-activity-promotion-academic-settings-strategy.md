# Promote physical activity within academic settings through institutional policies, flexible scheduling, and active breaks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that institutions embed physical movement in academic life: colleges and universities should "create detailed physical activity policies", establish counseling centers and health clubs, and offer flexible scheduling with accessible facilities. It also cites evidence that incorporating active breaks into lectures and study sessions provides cognitive benefits within time constraints. These strategies target demanding academic schedules of health sciences students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time-efficient programs compatible with demanding academic schedules
- Institutional policies, facilities and health clubs supporting regular exercise
#### Constraints
- Findings are cross-sectional and from a single institution, so the article calls for longitudinal and multi-institutional studies before broader applicability

### Target Learners
- health sciences university students

### Target Learning Goals
- academic engagement and resilience
- student learning and health outcomes
