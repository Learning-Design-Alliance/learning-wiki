# Instructors should attend to student gestures and inquire about early gesture occurrences rather than interrupt with content prompts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors notice students' spontaneous gestures and, when a gesture appears before an idea is verbalized, ask about it rather than redirect. In the trajectory episode, the author suggests the TA "might have inquired about the initial occurrences of the half-parabola gesture, perhaps by imitating and questioning it: 'What do you mean by this?'" This strategy treats gestures as evidence of emerging, self-generated ideas that an interjection about components can cut short.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
