# Use CRA-based small-group math tutoring with scripts, manipulatives, and computer fact practice as secondary prevention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The case study's secondary prevention is a university-developed tutoring program delivered "3 (40 minute) sessions per week delivered in a small-group format." It is "based on the concrete-representational-abstract (CRA) model of instruction," using manipulative objects, teacher scripts, and worksheets across 17 sequenced topics, with daily mastery checks, frequent cumulative review, and a final 10 minutes of game-like computer practice on addition and subtraction facts from 0-18.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher scripts and accompanying materials, manipulatives, daily mastery criteria, weekly CBM progress monitoring, and scheduling that does not interfere with general math instruction
#### Constraints
- The program is described as university-developed and shown to be effective for remediating first-grade math difficulties; the case study does not report independent outcome data for it

### Target Learners
- First-grade students unresponsive to primary prevention in math computation

### Target Learning Goals
- Number concepts, place value, and addition/subtraction computation skills
