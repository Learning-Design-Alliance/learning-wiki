# Teacher discussion prompts and reflective journaling to surface and evaluate student thinking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Across all activities the teachers used concept-focused discussion prompts and structured journaling to make students' thinking visible and support metaconceptual engagement. The article states: "Discussion prompts used by the teacher, such as 'Can you explain what you mean by …?', 'Why do you think that?' and 'Do you agree with your group's idea?', encouraged students to express their thoughts clearly, reflect on their learning processes and evaluate different perspectives." Journal prompts included "What did you do in this activity?" and "How does your opinion differ from that of your teacher, textbook, or classmates?" Journals gave insights into students' thoughts, helped introverted students practice reflection, and served as analysis data.
