# Pair high (A) with middle (B) achievement students and pair low (C) students together to maximize overall gain on conceptual tests

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a specific pairing rule for peer work on conceptual multiple-choice tests: divide students into high (A), middle (B), and low (C) thirds by initial score, then form (A B) and (C C) pairs. It states that "the pairing that helps maximize the overall gain is one that only has (A B) and (C C) pairs", based on the gain matrix showing A and B students lose gain with C partners while C students gain the same regardless of partner.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be divided into achievement thirds based on an initial individual score on the conceptual test
- Peers must be allowed or assigned to discuss responses, as in the unguided pair setting studied
#### Constraints
- The article states this holds at least for this conceptual test and explicitly calls for investigating whether the result is universal for conceptual tests
- The peer collaboration studied was unguided, with no help or facilitation from the instructor

### Target Learners
- calculus-based introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of electricity and magnetism as measured by the CSEM
