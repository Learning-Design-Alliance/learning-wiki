# Stimulate hypothesis-making and critical discussion during field measurements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
During outdoor data acquisition the article recommends that students be prompted to reason about confounding factors and expected outcomes: "the students are encouraged to make questions and assumptions, i.e., about the effects that a change in atmospheric conditions or soil humidity or about the type of ground coverage that would be the most or least abundant in natural radioactivity and to explain why, in order to support their views." This turns the measurement procedure itself into an opportunity for critical thinking about experimental conditions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A measurement procedure in which students take notes of all relevant experimental conditions and their potential impact on outcomes
#### Constraints
- The strategy addresses outdoor factors the article lists as uncontrollable, such as vegetation, buildings, morphology and soil humidity

### Target Learners
- pre-collegiate students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical understanding of unplanned factors affecting in-situ measurements
- justifying predictions with physical reasoning
