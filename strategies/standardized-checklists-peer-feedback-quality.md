# Use standardized checklists to improve the quality of peer feedback in video-based skill training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because peer evaluation is subjective and peers may show leniency bias in high-stakes assessments, the authors provided standardized checklists to all participants scoring and giving feedback. The article states this mitigation directly: "we provided standardized checklists to all participants, as evidence suggests such structured tools can improve feedback quality". This is an implementable step for any video-based peer feedback program.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standardized marking table or checklist aligned with the relevant training guidelines for each skill
- Detailed instructions on how to score and deliver feedback effectively for everyone involved
#### Constraints
- The article cites prior evidence for checklist benefits rather than testing the checklist itself as a manipulated variable

### Target Learners
- surgical residents providing peer feedback on recorded performances

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate scoring and high-quality feedback on clinical skill performance
