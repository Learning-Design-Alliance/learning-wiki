# Use easier reading texts for beginning students so they can focus on integrated reading and writing skills rather than text comprehension

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that when teaching integrated reading and writing skills to beginning students, "teachers could use easier reading texts so that the students are able to focus more on learning the integrated reading and writing skills than spending time understanding the text." This strategy addresses the finding that beginning students spent more time on vocabulary and comprehension than on paraphrasing and summarizing practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers should assess students' reading comprehension ability and vocabulary knowledge before selecting texts for integrated instruction.
#### Constraints
- This recommendation emerged from a case study at a single middle school in Seoul, Korea, and the results may not be directly applicable to other foreign language contexts.

### Target Learners
- Beginning-level EFL middle school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Summary writing
- Paraphrasing
- Integrated reading and writing skills
