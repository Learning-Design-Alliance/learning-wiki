# Run the sensor-location lab as a cumulative four-quadrant tracing procedure with a phone outline and teardown-site verification

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A step-by-step lab recipe: students record acceleration with an app, place the phone in one corner, draw the two acceleration components from the center outwards to form an acceleration vector, extend it backward as a radial line, then move the phone through the remaining quadrants tracing lines over a pre-cut phone outline. The intersecting lines estimate the sensor position, which can be checked against teardown-site circuit-board images scaled to the outline.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An accelerometer app on student smartphones; the article used the app called Physics Toolbox Accelerometer
#### Constraints
- Direct verification by dismantling phones is described as impractical, so teardown sites are the suggested check

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students working with their own smartphones

### Target Learning Goals
- applying the centrally-pointing property of centripetal acceleration to a real measurement task
