# Embed short serious-game sessions within an existing standard training curriculum to train non-routine event response

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article supports adding brief serious-game sessions to an existing standard curriculum rather than replacing it: the game-enhanced curriculum "consisted of the regular BLTC, enhanced by two separate 30-min sessions of serious gaming." Because the control group received the customary curriculum including equipment lectures, the comparison contrasted curricula rather than a trained versus non-trained group, which the authors cite as a strength increasing generalizability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standard curriculum already covering the domain basics, so the game supplements rather than substitutes instruction
#### Constraints
- Retention of learning over time was not measured in the supporting trial

### Target Learners
- surgical residents in basic laparoscopy training

### Target Learning Goals
- responding to equipment-related problems and non-routine events during surgery

## Claims

- [Surgical residents who follow a game-enhanced curriculum solve more equipment-related problems during live MIS tasks than residents following the regular curriculum](../claims/serious-game-improves-equipment-problem-solving.md) [+W]
- [The game-enhanced group's advantage was most evident in recognizing and dealing with insufflator malfunctions, problems outside the participants' direct task focus](../claims/serious-game-insufflator-subgroup-advantage.md) [+W]
- [Among game-enhanced residents, the proportion of cases solved within the game rose by 2.3% per three-minute session from earlier to later sessions.](../claims/serious-game-learning-curve-improvement.md) [+W]
- [Game-enhanced residents recognized a larger proportion of equipment problems than controls, but this difference was not statistically significant](../claims/serious-game-problem-recognition-nonsignificant.md) [~W]
- [Time required to recognize and solve equipment problems did not differ significantly between game-enhanced and regular curriculum groups](../claims/serious-game-problem-timing-null.md) [~W]
