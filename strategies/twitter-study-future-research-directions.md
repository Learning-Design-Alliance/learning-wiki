# Future research directions: control grade incentives, compare compulsory versus voluntary participation, and apply learning analytics to tweet types

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose follow-up work building on the three case studies: studies with a broader and more diverse participant profile, longitudinal monitoring of attitudes toward social media adoption, ways to control the effect of grade incentives (for example to lower or eliminate the grade bonus), making participation mandatory so compulsory and voluntary participation can be compared, and deeper analysis of intervention types using Educational Data Mining and Learning Analytics techniques.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Future designs must address the grade-incentive confound, e.g. by lowering or eliminating the bonus, and require larger, more diverse samples and longer tool use periods.
#### Constraints
- The authors state the reported results could not be considered generalizable and conclusive, positioning the study as a first step toward understanding Twitter-mediated learning.

### Target Learners
- higher education students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding determinants of participation and performance in social-media-mediated learning
