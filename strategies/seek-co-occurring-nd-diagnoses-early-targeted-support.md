# Actively seek co-occurring neurodevelopmental diagnoses and provide early diagnosis with targeted, inclusive academic supports

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools and clinicians look beyond a presenting condition: "co-occurring diagnoses should be actively sought as focusing solely on a primary condition may result in diagnostic overshadowing." It pairs this with early diagnosis and targeted intervention, and with school-based frameworks promoting "inclusive pedagogical strategies, coordinated collaboration among educators, specialists, and families, and targeted academic supports." The rationale is that unrecognised additional ND delay tailored interventions and widen achievement gaps over time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Comprehensive clinical or screening assessment capable of detecting multiple neurodevelopmental conditions, not only the presenting one
#### Constraints
- The article notes teachers often lack training and support, and evidence-based practices are not consistently implemented in classrooms, which may limit implementation

### Target Learners
- School-age children and adolescents (5–17) with neurodevelopmental disorders in general education classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance across first language, foreign language, mathematics, arts, and physical education
