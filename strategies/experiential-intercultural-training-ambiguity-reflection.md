# Experiential intercultural training: create ambiguity, behavioural experimentation and reflection rather than centralising cultural knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on Experiential Learning Theory and the study's findings, the authors recommend that intercultural training "should create opportunities to experience ambiguity and unfamiliarity, experiment with behaviour, and provide opportunity for reflection". They suggest reflection on one's own emotional stability tendencies to develop coping strategies for intercultural stress, and enhancing social initiative through roleplay exercises expanding behavioural repertoire. The study provides reason to "steer away from training approaches that centralise knowledge of cultural difference".
