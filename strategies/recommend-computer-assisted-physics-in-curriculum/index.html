Adopt computer-assisted physics instruction as compulsory in high school curricula¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that first-time use of the software was significantly more effective than traditional methods, the authors recommend that education administrators and planners incorporate computer-assisted physics teaching into the curriculum. They write that "the administrator or education planners must include this computer-assisted in teaching and learning physic into curriculum so that it becomes compulsory for any high schools", and suggest the software be recommended for high schools in Pekanbaru and across Indonesia.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • software developed with pedagogical aspects by education specialists, since previously existing software built by non-education persons did not carry the pedagogical aspects

Constraints¶

  • recommendation grounded in a single quasi-experiment in Pekanbaru schools on one physics topic

Target Learners¶

  • senior high school students in Indonesia

Target Learning Goals¶

  • physics conceptual understanding and motivation

Claims¶

  • Learning physics with the interactive Motion and Vector Analysis software produced higher conceptual understanding than traditional instruction in a quasi-experiment with XI-grade students [+M]
  • Students learning with the software showed higher learning motivation at post-test than students taught by traditional method [+M]
  • Conceptual understanding and motivation gains were similar for male and female students using the software [~M]
  • Expert panels and 31 experienced physics teachers rated the software favourably, with reliability of Cronbach's alpha .843 across 24 items [~M]
