# Prepare students for realistic placement expectations and consider combined or role-emerging placement models in occupational therapy education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators and placement coordinators choose appropriate placements and clinical supervision models, and "may consider a combination of different practice placement models", such as an interagency model combining the apprenticeship and consultative models. It recommends developing role-emerging placements to expand community practice, giving students skills to work autonomously, ensuring students have realistic expectations from supervisors and settings, and encouraging better communication between university-affiliated professionals and placement supervisors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Role-emerging practice placements require staff with the ability to devote additional time and resources to supervising students
- Better prior preparation of students, placement settings and supervisors is essential, especially in role-emerging placement
#### Constraints
- The study found role-emerging students were less satisfied with setting and supervision, attributed to supervision provided only weekly by an off-site occupational therapist

### Target Learners
- undergraduate occupational therapy students
- educators and placement coordinators

### Target Learning Goals
- professional development
- independence and self-directed learning
- realistic expectations of supervision and placement settings
