# Common Ground Group Assignments

## Description
Providing group assignments where learners can find common ground and/or interests involves designing collaborative tasks that encourage learners to identify shared interests, values, or goals. This strategy fosters a sense of connection and belonging, promoting positive relationships and teamwork. Common ground can be found through shared academic interests, hobbies, cultural backgrounds, or personal experiences. When learners work together on assignments that align with their shared interests, they are more likely to engage in meaningful discussions, support each other's learning, and develop stronger relationships.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful planning and design of group assignments that align with the learners' interests and learning objectives. Facilitation and guidance to ensure that all learners have the opportunity to participate and contribute. Assessment methods that recognize and value both individual and group contributions.
#### Constraints
- The effectiveness of this strategy depends on the learners' willingness to engage with their peers and the presence of genuine common ground. There is a risk that some learners may dominate the group or that conflicts may arise due to differences in opinions or working styles.

### Affordances
- Creates opportunities for learners to discover shared interests and values, fosters a sense of community and belonging, and promotes positive social interactions and teamwork. This can lead to increased engagement, motivation, and academic achievement.

### Personalization
- Group assignments can be tailored to the specific interests and backgrounds of the learners, ensuring that they have the opportunity to connect with peers who share their passions. Learners can also be given choices in terms of the topics, tasks, or roles within the group, allowing them to express their individuality and contribute in meaningful ways.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the learners' interactions, participation, and teamwork during group assignments. Gather feedback from the learners about their experiences and perceptions of the group dynamic. Assess the quality of the group's work, including the depth of their discussions, the level of support they provide to each other, and the overall outcomes of the assignment.

## Impact
- Research suggests that collaborative learning experiences, particularly those that focus on shared interests and goals, can effectively promote positive relationships, increase engagement, and improve academic outcomes. When learners feel connected to their peers and have the opportunity to contribute to a shared goal, they are more likely to invest in their learning and achieve success.

## Examples
- A group of learners who share an interest in environmental sustainability are assigned a project to research and propose solutions to a local environmental problem. They work together to gather data, analyze information, and develop a presentation to share their findings with the community. Through this process, they connect over their shared passion for the environment and build strong relationships based on mutual respect and collaboration.
