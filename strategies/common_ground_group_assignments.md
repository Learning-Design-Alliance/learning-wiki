# Common Ground Group Assignments

## Description
Common ground group assignments are collaborative tasks deliberately structured so learners must identify and use shared interests, values, experiences, or goals to complete the work. Rather than assigning groups and hoping cohesion emerges, the instructor builds a "finding common ground" phase into the task itself — an initial discussion, interest-mapping activity, or negotiated topic/role selection — so that the group's work is anchored in what members genuinely share. This fosters belonging and positive interdependence, which in turn sustain the discussion, mutual support, and persistence that collaborative learning requires.

## Design Implications

Belonging is a measurable lever on achievement: brief interventions that normalize the worry of not belonging and highlight common ground across group members improve academic outcomes, especially for students at risk of feeling like outsiders [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. But belonging alone is not enough — cooperative structures work when group success depends on individual accountability and shared rewards, not just proximity [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]. Common ground assignments therefore pair a social-bonding phase with task structures that make every member's contribution necessary.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured common-ground activity at the outset (interest inventory, paired interviews, negotiated topic selection) so shared ground is *discovered*, not assumed
- Positive interdependence: a single group product or goal that no member can complete alone
- Individual accountability: distinct roles, sub-tasks, or individual components assessed separately [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]
- Facilitation and norms for equitable participation so common ground does not become a clique boundary

#### Constraints
- Manufactured or superficial common ground ("we both like pizza") does not produce the engagement effects of genuine shared purpose; forcing affinity where none exists can feel inauthentic and backfire [-W]
- Groups formed purely on similarity can exclude outsiders and reduce diversity of perspective; heterogeneous grouping with a common-ground phase outperforms affinity-based grouping for learning outcomes [~M]
- Without individual accountability, shared-interest groups drift toward socializing and free-riding [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [-S]
- Dominant members can hijack the "shared" interest; rotating roles and structured turn-taking mitigate this

#### Implementation Variability
- **Interest-mapped grouping:** survey interests first, then compose groups around a genuinely shared topic
- **Negotiated topic:** fixed groups negotiate a project topic all members endorse — the negotiation *is* the common-ground activity
- **Structured disclosure:** paired interviews or [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) before task work, useful in short courses or online settings
- **Jigsaw variant:** each member owns a piece of expertise; common ground emerges from the interlocking structure rather than pre-existing similarity

### Target Learners
- Students at risk of feeling isolated — first-generation, transfer, or minoritized students — for whom belonging cues carry the largest payoff [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Learners new to a course or cohort who lack established peer relationships
- Less effective for mature groups with established norms, where the common-ground phase adds little beyond what already exists [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Collaborative and interpersonal skills: negotiation, perspective-taking, shared decision-making
- Engagement and persistence on extended projects
- Content mastery through [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) and [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) structures [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. Surface individual interests and backgrounds through a short inventory, survey, or [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md)
2. Form or confirm groups, ensuring a genuine (not forced) point of overlap; keep groups heterogeneous on ability
3. Run a common-ground phase — paired interviews, a shared-goal statement, or negotiated topic selection — before any task work
4. Assign interdependent roles and a single group product with individually assessed components ([Application](../elements/application.md) of each member's contribution)
5. Structure interaction with [Peer Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) protocols and [Collaborative Learning](../elements/collaborative-learning.md) structures such as jigsaw or think-pair-share
6. Debrief both the product and the process: what common ground helped, what conflicts arose, how the group resolved them

## Related Strategies
- **Jigsaw Method** — common ground emerges from structural interdependence rather than pre-existing similarity
- **Belonging Interventions** — the social-psychological complement: brief activities that reframe belonging uncertainty
- **Interest-Based Project Design** — extends common ground from group formation to task selection

## Related Elements

- [Application](../elements/application.md) — the task phase where shared purpose converts into joint work
- [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) — a shared case gives diverse groups an immediate common object of analysis

## Patterns That Use This Strategy
- [Cooperative Learning](../patterns/cooperative-learning.md) — common ground supplies the cohesion that positive interdependence requires
- [Collaborative Learning](../patterns/collaborative-learning.md) — the belonging phase raises the quality of peer interaction
- [Communities of Practice](../principles/communities-of-practice.md) — shared purpose and mutual engagement are the defining features of a community; this strategy seeds them deliberately

## Examples
- **PBLWorks (Buck Institute) project-based learning** (https://www.pblworks.org) — gold-standard PBL units begin with a shared driving question that groups negotiate, anchoring diverse teams in common purpose before dividing roles.
- **Jigsaw Classroom** (https://www.jigsaw.org) — Aronson's interdependent group design; each student's unique piece makes the group's shared goal unachievable alone.
- **First-year seminar interest-mapped teams** — many learning-community programs (e.g., Evergreen State College's coordinated studies) survey entering students' interests and compose project teams around genuine overlaps, pairing the common-ground phase with individually assessed contributions.
