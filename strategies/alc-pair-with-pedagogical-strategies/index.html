Pair Active Learning Classroom implementation with organisational and pedagogical strategies for using flexible space, and structured active breaks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q2–q3 · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that improving PA outcomes in ALCs "may require the implementation of specific organizational and pedagogical strategies that highlight the optimal use of flexible furniture and spaces". It further suggests including "structured active breaks, whether integrated with curriculum content or not, led by educators". Studies with zone-based routines or student-centred methodologies appeared more effective.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • teacher guidance on how to utilise flexible spaces effectively

Constraints¶

  • more studies are needed to confirm the benefit of structured active breaks

Target Learners¶

  • children and adolescents in primary and secondary schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • increasing physical activity and reducing sedentary time during lessons

Claims¶

  • Effects of ALCs on physical activity are mixed, with some studies showing lower PA in ALCs than traditional classrooms [~W]
  • Active Learning Classrooms predominantly reduce sitting time and increase standing time in children and adolescents [+W]
  • Learning Space Redesign Alone Insufficient [+W]
  • Teachers in ALCs use more student-centred and collaborative pedagogies than in traditional classrooms [+W]
  • Classroom physical activity increases children's on-task behaviour, with no consistent gain on attention or other cognitive tests [+M]
