# Implement structured cognitive tools such as PEST repeatedly across training using a spiral curriculum with spaced repetition and successive deepening

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extending one-off teaching of structured reasoning tools into a longitudinal curriculum, teaching the PEST approach at various points during the intern year. As they describe it, "The spiral curriculum involves teaching a concept, in this case the PEST approach, in a longitudinal format with spaced repetition" and involves "a successive deepening of the subject upon each encounter to allow for reinforcement, integration, and the mastery of higher-level objectives." They suggest this would let doctors in training gain familiarity with therapeutic reasoning and recognize its benefits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple teaching sessions spread throughout the academic year rather than a single intervention
- A larger and more diverse learner population to measure efficacy reliably, as the authors state
#### Constraints
- The single-session intervention reported here showed only a non-significant trend, so the longitudinal version remains untested in this article

### Target Learners
- Internal medicine interns
- Postgraduate trainees across the continuum of medical education

### Target Learning Goals
- Integrating therapeutic reasoning into antibiotic decision-making
- Comfort with antibiotic prescribing
