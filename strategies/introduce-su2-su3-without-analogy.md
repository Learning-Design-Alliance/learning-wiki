# For SU(2) and SU(3) symmetries, skip analogy-seeking and introduce the symmetries directly, stating that they give rise to forces when gauged

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that once the connection between symmetry and force has been established through the U(1) analogy, teachers should not attempt analogies for the non-abelian symmetries: 'Due to the increased mathematical complexity of the SU(2) and SU(3) symmetries, it is not realistic to ﬁnd a good analogy for them'. Instead one can simply introduce them as they are and state that they give rise to forces too when gauged, extending the standard model structure to the weak and strong interactions without new conceptual machinery.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The connection between symmetry and force must already have been established, for example through the timezone analogy for U(1), before the SU(2) and SU(3) symmetries are introduced directly.
#### Constraints
- Limited to situations where learners have met the symmetry-force connection; the article states finding a good analogy for SU(2) and SU(3) is not realistic because of their increased mathematical complexity.

### Target Learners
- high school teachers
- students without advanced physics background

### Target Learning Goals
- understand that the SU(2) and SU(3) symmetries give rise to forces
- understand the structure of the standard model
