# Combine immediate standardized-patient feedback, video review, and self-feedback diary entries as the core of OSCE remediation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends, in its conclusion, a remediation package in which "Immediate SP feedback appears to have a significant impact on performance, and we suggest incorporating video review and the use of a self-feedback diary to ensure the effectiveness of remediation." In both study arms, students watched recordings the day after the OSCE, wrote a self-feedback diary reflecting on emotions and identifying strengths and areas for improvement in medical interview, physical examination, and relationship-building skills, and had received five minutes of one-on-one SP oral feedback immediately after each station. The authors attribute the similar effects of both remediation arms partly to this shared design in which external feedback occurred immediately after the encounter and video review increased engagement with feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recorded video of each standardized patient encounter
- A self-feedback diary with prompts to reflect on emotions and identify strengths and areas for improvement
- Immediate oral feedback from standardized patients after each encounter
#### Constraints
- The authors did not evaluate long-term effects such as postgraduate clinical competence, so transferability of the remediation effect is not established

### Target Learners
- Final-year medical students in clinical clerkship

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical interview and communication skills
- Physical examination skills
- Patient–physician interaction and relationship building
