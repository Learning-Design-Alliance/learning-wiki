# Select target formulaic sequences from corpus-informed phrase lists using frequency, semantic transparency, and genre relevance criteria.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The study selected 50 target FSs from five sources (AFL, PHRASE List, ACL, OFS, Academic Phrasebank) using "frequency of occurrence and meaning or function" as criteria, following Martinez's (2013) Frequency-Transparency Framework. Spoken and general genres were excluded to focus on academic writing, and frequency thresholds (e.g., over 3.0 per million for ACL) filtered items for likely usefulness in student writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to corpus-informed academic phrase lists such as the AFL, PHRASE List, ACL, OFS, or Academic Phrasebank
- Consultation with course instructor on instructional and contextual factors
#### Constraints
- Spoken and written general genres were excluded; less common and infrequent phrases were not considered
- Frequency cut-offs alone are insufficient; semantic transparency and pedagogical relevance must also be considered

### Target Learners
- EFL college students
- English majors in academic writing courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic formulaic sequence knowledge
- Academic writing proficiency
