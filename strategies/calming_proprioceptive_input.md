# Calming Proprioceptive Input

## Description
Using proprioceptive input, also known as "heavy work," involves engaging muscles and joints to provide a calming and organizing sensory experience. Activities include squishing play dough or a stress ball, pulling against resistance bands, pushing or moving chairs or desks, climbing, holding a heavy door open, carrying books, and chewing against resistance. These activities stimulate muscles and joints, which can be calming.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Play dough or stress ball, resistance bands, chairs or desks, climbing equipment, heavy books, chewy items.
#### Constraints
- Activities should be safe and appropriate for the child's age and abilities. Supervision may be necessary.

### Affordances
- Provides a way to engage the proprioceptive system, helping to reduce anxiety and promote emotional regulation. Offers a physical way to manage sensory experiences and emotions.

### Personalization
- The type of heavy work can be tailored to individual preferences and needs. Some children may prefer pushing, while others may prefer pulling.
