# Target oral retests only to unmastered topics and maintain roughly a 20:1 student-to-instructor ratio for feasible implementation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend constraining oral exams to only the topics a student did not initially master, which kept each exam (including feedback) to no more than 15 minutes per student and rarely more than 2 hours of instructor time per week. For larger classes they suggest teaching assistants may increase throughput if properly trained, and "we suggest maintaining a student-to-instructor ratio of approximately 20:1 for feasible implementation of the exams."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained teaching assistants for larger classes
- Implicit bias training for instructors, especially in multi-instructor courses
- Clear indications of which specialized terms are required, with re-wording of non-required terms allowed
#### Constraints
- In larger classes, additional considerations are needed to administer oral exams
- The authors acknowledge implicit biases may remain despite structural mitigations

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in small- to medium-sized courses

### Target Learning Goals
- mastery of course content with equitable assessment
