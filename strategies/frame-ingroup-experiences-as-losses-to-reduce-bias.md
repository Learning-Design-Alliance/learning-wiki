# Frame ingroup experiences as losses and positive outgroup experiences as gains to reduce intergroup bias across cultural contexts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors derive a practical recommendation from their cross-cultural findings: presenting interactions with ingroup members in a loss frame, and interactions with outgroup members in a gain frame, can reduce ingroup bias in impressions. As they write, "framing ingroup experiences as losses rather than gains can effectively diminish ingroup bias by helping us see the ingroup in a more objective light." They add that gains from the outgroup can improve outgroup impressions and that the cross-cultural consistency "suggests that such strategies could be applicable across different cultural contexts." The recommendation rests on the experimentally tested frame effects in both studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must make the loss or gain framing of intergroup experiences salient, as the experimental Gain/Loss frames did.
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that individual differences such as the degree of ingroup identification may influence the effectiveness of these interventions; the loss-frame effect was limited to low-identification East Asian participants.

### Target Learners
- Adults in intergroup contact or cooperation settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing ingroup bias in intergroup impressions
