# Adapt flipped classrooms to the local context by releasing materials early, replacing MDT sessions with image-reading practice, and adding problem-solving components

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors modified a previously tried flipped radiology course based on student feedback: materials were allotted a month early so students could prepare around final examinations, and the multi-disciplinary team session was replaced with instructor-led medical image reading from the PACS system, which undergraduates found more attainable. They report that "These two modifications have improved the results of the current flipped classroom." The in-class design pairs short lectures with question answering and image-reading practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-class materials (videos, textbook sections, pre-learning assessments) distributed well before class
- Instructor access to representative cases from a PACS system
- Communication channels such as a WeChat group for pre-class questions
#### Constraints
- MDT-mode sessions requiring multidisciplinary mastery were unsuitable for undergraduates
- Course timing near final examinations reduced participation in the authors' previous attempt

### Target Learners
- fourth-year undergraduate medical imaging/radiology students

### Target Learning Goals
- radiological image interpretation
- diagnosis and differential diagnosis of malignant bone tumours
- independent learning and clinical problem-solving
