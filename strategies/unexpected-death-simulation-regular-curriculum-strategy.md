# Offer unexpected death simulation training as a regular part of health professional curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy is to incorporate simulation sessions in which the simulated patient dies unexpectedly, combined with a prebriefing that omits the death warning, an advance directive that determines the outcome, and an extended debriefing. The authors state: "we encourage schools to offer the unexpected death simulation training in other courses and to make it a regular part of the curriculum". The course used a 65-year-old metastatic colon cancer scenario, a high-fidelity manikin, and a two-hour debriefing following Lederman's three-stage model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Highly skilled and experienced trainers who can recognise and address psychological distress, as the article states the experience can be stressful or shameful without them
- A debriefing held immediately after the simulation with sufficient time, following its three phases of reflection, intensification and personalisation, and generalisation and application
- Psychosocial support offered to participants, as was done in the studied course
#### Constraints
- Carried out despite the risk of emotional overload for learners, which the authors acknowledge as discussed in the literature
- The article notes it might be unwise in some situations when it puts students under too much stress

### Target Learners
- Medical students in seventh to ninth semester
- Third-year nursing trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- Preparing to deal with patient death and termination of resuscitation
- Teamwork and communication skills in emergency situations
- Reflection on dying, death and one's own professional role
