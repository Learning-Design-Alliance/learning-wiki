# Provide learners with teacher training before AVT sessions, coaching them to guide rather than tell

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Manual evaluation of AVT teaching interactions found that several learners would try to recite the answer to novices rather than guide them to figure out the problem on their own, and learners were very hesitant when novice code was buggy, almost as if they felt personally judged. The article concludes that "while AVT has positive benefit for novices, it would be beneficial to provide learners with some form of teacher training". This strategy addresses the telling-versus-guiding concern the experts observed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A training component for learners before they host AVT sessions
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a suggestion from observed interaction weaknesses, not a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- CS1 university students acting as AVT learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Guiding novices to solve problems rather than reciting answers
- Comfort and confidence when novice code is buggy

## Claims

- [AVT learners were more motivated to prepare and found the experience more enjoyable than learners who did a practice exam](../claims/avt-motivating-and-enjoyable.md) [+W]
- [Expert instructors judged that AVT learners helped novices make educationally helpful progress in 98% of sessions](../claims/avt-novice-impact-expert-assessment.md) [+W]
