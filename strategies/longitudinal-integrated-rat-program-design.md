# Design RaT programs as longitudinal, integrated curricula with structured instruction, direct practice, formative feedback, and ongoing coaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy captures the review's core design recommendation: residents-as-teachers programs should be longitudinal and clinically integrated rather than brief stand-alone workshops. The review reports that programs "that combined structured instruction with direct practice, formative feedback, and ongoing coaching generally demonstrated more durable improvements in teaching skills than stand-alone workshops." It further notes that "effective RaT programs require ongoing support structures, not only initial training."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional support structures and protected teaching time
- Integration of structured teaching opportunities within routine clinical workflows
#### Constraints
- Short workshops produced immediate confidence gains but rarely led to lasting behavioral change
- Gains may diminish without ongoing reinforcement, requiring periodic booster sessions

### Target Learners
- Medical and surgical residents in postgraduate years 1-6

### Target Learning Goals
- Teaching skills including didactic teaching, feedback delivery, precepting, and bedside teaching
