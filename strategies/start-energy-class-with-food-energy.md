# Start an energy-and-social-policy class with food energy to give all students a shared context before introducing bespoke energy units

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends opening an energy course with several weeks of food-energy discussion before other energy types, because every student eats and the class otherwise confronts a frustrating variety of units (BTUs, barrels, Quads, kWh). As the author puts it, "Starting the class with a discussion of food energy is a nice way for everyone to start with the same context." The approach grounds unit conversion in a universal daily experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be willing to do proportional reasoning with units; the author notes asking students to reason proportionally with units is a skill that can give meaning to numbers
#### Constraints
- Designed for a 3-credit general-education class serving a largely math-averse population from across the university

### Target Learners
- undergraduate non-science majors in a general-education Science and Social Policy course

### Target Learning Goals
- unit conversion and proportional reasoning with units
- energy literacy across contexts
