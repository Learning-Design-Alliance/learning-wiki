# Assess the approximate number system with multiple tasks rather than presenting one non-symbolic task as a valid index of a unique ANS

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend that research and assessment stop relying on a single non-symbolic numerical task as the measure of ANS acuity. They state that "it is very misleading to select only one task involving non-symbolic numerical stimuli and present it as a valid index of a supposedly unique ANS", calling this a very frequent practice in the numerical cognition literature that should be avoided. Their own study demonstrates the alternative: measuring comparison, estimation and addition acuity separately and testing their unique and mediated contributions to exact calculation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Use at least the comparison, estimation and approximate addition tasks with appropriate psychophysical parameters (w and cv)
#### Constraints
- Different tasks involve very different cognitive processes, so their parameters should not be expected to be equal

### Target Learners
- School-aged children being assessed for numerical cognition
- Children with mathematical difficulties

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate characterization of approximate number processing
- Identification of specific ANS deficits underlying calculation difficulties

- Ans Multiple Instantiations Hierarchy
