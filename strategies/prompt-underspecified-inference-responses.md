# Prompt children's underspecified inference responses to reveal their comprehension potential and model the standard of coherence required

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article draws an educational implication from its finding that performance improved after prompting for all age groups on both inference types. Prompting underspecified or vague answers, by repeating the question or asking the child to be more specific, is described as "a useful means to demonstrate a child's potential (or competence)" and as a tool to indicate the standard of coherence required of comprehension. Teachers and assessors can use this during narrative comprehension activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The child must have produced an incomplete or vague answer to an inference question, which the adult then prompts with a repetition of the question or encouragement to be more specific
#### Constraints
- The article reports prompting within an individual assessment context with stories read aloud; it does not report classroom implementation

### Target Learners
- Children aged 5 to 10 years during listening comprehension of narrative text

### Target Learning Goals
- Generating local and global coherence inferences and demonstrating full comprehension potential

## Claims

- [Prompting underspecified responses improves children's performance on both local and global coherence inference questions at all ages](../claims/prompting-improves-inference-performance.md) [+M]
