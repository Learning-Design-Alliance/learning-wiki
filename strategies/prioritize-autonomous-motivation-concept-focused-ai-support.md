# Prioritize autonomous motivation and scaffold concept-focused AI support tailored to learner trajectories rather than providing uniform access alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's design recommendation is that integrating GenAI in higher education requires moving beyond simply giving every learner access to the same tool. As the authors state, "the efficacy of AI integration depends on moving beyond uniform technological access toward strategies that prioritize autonomous motivation and scaffold concept-focused AI support tailored to individual learner trajectories." This means cultivating students' self-endorsed motivation for AI use and designing supports that steer queries toward conceptual, diverse questioning — the engagement style that distinguished Growing Achievers — rather than procedural, copy-paste use. The authors tie this to ensuring shared human-AI agency so benefits do not concentrate among already advantaged learners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Voluntary-use contexts in which learner motivation shapes whether and how AI tools are adopted
- Awareness of individual learners' motivational and achievement trajectories to tailor support
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is grounded in one observational semester-long study of graduate statistics students; the article notes efficacy depends on these strategies but does not experimentally test them

### Target Learners
- graduate students in higher education using voluntary GenAI learning tools

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained meaningful engagement with AI-supported learning
- conceptual rather than purely procedural questioning
