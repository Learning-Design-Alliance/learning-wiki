# Offer CS1 course materials in multiple formats to accommodate different learner preferences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend offering introductory programming course materials in multiple formats because simulated learner responses to UDL transformations varied by profile: neurotypical learners and those with dyslexia or ADHD preferred UDL-transformed slides, while learners with ASD did not. The article states these "results highlight the potential to offer CS1 course materials in multiple formats to accomodate different learner preferences."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Maintaining both original and UDL-transformed versions of course materials so learners can access the format that suits them
#### Constraints
- Supported by simulated-student evidence from a single pilot study on one week of one course

### Target Learners
- CS1 university students with diverse accessibility needs

### Target Learning Goals
- Accessing and comprehending introductory programming content regardless of format preference
