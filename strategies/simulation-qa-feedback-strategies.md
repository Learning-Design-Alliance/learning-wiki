# Feedback strategies to maximize the effectiveness of a simulation quality assurance program

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Box 1 of the article lists eight strategies for giving feedback to simulation actors, drawn from the authors' experience that providing actor feedback resembles providing feedback to colleagues in clinical environments. The strategies include to "Set clear expectations about the nature and frequency of feedback" and to "Critique the performance, not the person," along with being self-aware while providing feedback, using inclusive pronouns, asking open-ended questions to understand barriers, establishing consistency and credibility in observation, giving actors an approved way to challenge feedback, and always explaining what approach or action is preferred rather than only what went wrong.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback providers should establish consistency and credibility in the observation approach and give actors an approved way to challenge feedback when they disagree
#### Constraints
- These strategies are proposed by the authors based on their experience; the article did not test the individual strategies experimentally

### Target Learners
- Standardized patient actors in simulation programs
- Simulation educators and study teams providing actor feedback

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective, respectful delivery of performance feedback to actors to sustain standardized simulation performances
