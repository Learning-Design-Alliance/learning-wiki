# Design PBL assignments with a conference-style paper, interdisciplinary dual mentorship, narrative framing, and skeleton code

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors' recommendations for running PBL modules in introductory quantum computing courses. They state "We recommend incorporating a conference-style paper writing component too into project-based assignments, as in our case", emphasize that "support from faculty across both domains was absolutely necessary", advise introducing the assignment early in the semester, framing it as a narrative rather than a checklist, and providing a minimal but functioning skeleton code so students spend cognitive capital on conceptual hurdles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interdisciplinary mentorship from faculty across the bridged domains, including support with academic writing
#### Constraints
- One main hurdle the authors faced was a lack of existing code, not even at small scale, motivating the skeleton-code scaffold

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students in introductory quantum computing courses

### Target Learning Goals
- technical communication in formal academic formats
- conceptual understanding of quantum computing primitives
- ownership of learning through authentic research practice
