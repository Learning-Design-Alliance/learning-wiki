# Make SEL measurement narrower in focus but broader in context and depth, choosing specific measures guided by development and skill trajectories

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's third recommendation addresses measurement practice for middle childhood: "The measurement of skills in middle childhood should grow narrower in focus but broader in context and depth." Narrower means moving away from expansive measurements of SEL outcomes toward measures that are more specific and guided by knowledge of development and skill trajectories. Broader means expanding measurement to classroom-, school-, and adult-level contexts and expanding data sources, for example by collecting data from teachers and school staff about their experiences with the program. The article also recommends caution when interpreting effects when data were analyzed at the child level but randomization occurred at the classroom or school level, since such analyses can overestimate program effects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Researchers, program developers, evaluators, and practitioners should choose measures more specific to program targets and guided by developmental knowledge
#### Constraints
- Ignoring ecological principles of development may obscure meaningful program-related changes; child-level analysis of classroom- or school-randomized trials can result in overestimating program effects

### Target Learners
- Children in middle childhood, roughly 5 to 11 years of age

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate measurement of targeted social-emotional skills
