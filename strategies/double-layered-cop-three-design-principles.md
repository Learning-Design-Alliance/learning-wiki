# Three design principles for making online learning authentically constructivist via the double-layered CoP model

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article stresses three inter-related design principles. "First, students need to be given learning opportunities to construct a clear theoretical understandings of CoP". Second, course structure and activities must explicitly guide students to choose and develop an existing community into a supportive CoP as a student-driven process in which each student projects their own identity as a core member. Third, the challenging nature of building a CoP needs to be clearly shared by all course participants, with a shared goal of developing external CoPs and supporting each other's situated learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The term CoP should not just be used as a euphonious rhetorical expression referring to any learning communities
#### Constraints
- The author notes the notion of CoP has been applied to organizational contexts with an increasing managerialist focus, losing the original insight about the authenticity and spontaneity of learning in CoP

### Target Learners
- instructional designers of online higher education courses and their adult distance learners

### Target Learning Goals
- authentically constructivist online learning situated in learners' everyday lives
