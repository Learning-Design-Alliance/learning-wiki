# Incorporate simulated clinical encounters into holistic admissions review and provide post-interview faculty interaction time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that programs consider adding simulated clinical encounters to holistic admissions review to identify applicants likely to succeed, and that programs ensure applicants can interact with faculty after interviews are completed and scored. They also suggest programs might consider competency-level feedback that does not compromise the integrity of the MMI process, and that discussing the rationale for MMI may address applicant concerns.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Space for applicants to interact with faculty once interviews are completed and scored
- Station design and communication that explain the reason a program uses MMI
#### Constraints
- Feedback during the MMI process risks compromising the integrity of the MMI process; the article notes it is unclear that feedback would improve applicants' future admission scores

### Target Learners
- physician assistant program applicants
- graduate health professions admissions programs

### Target Learning Goals
- selecting applicants with the personal attributes needed for successful study and clinical practice
