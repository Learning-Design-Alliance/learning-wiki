# Guide a teacher-led classroom discussion of concept-map differences before students answer reflection prompts individually

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
After students examine the highlighted differences between their own and an expert concept map, the teacher leads a whole-class discussion using questions such as "why would you change/add this information" and "who would (dis)agree and why", without sharing domain information. Students then answer reflection prompts individually. The article found this preparation made prompts effective for vocational students who otherwise ignored or answered them shallowly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be trained (a 60-minute scripted session in the study) to guide discussion with questions that stimulate reflection and to avoid transferring domain-related information
#### Constraints
- The discussion is a whole-class activity requiring an intact class; the study's evidence comes from secondary vocational students and may not transfer to other levels of education

### Target Learners
- secondary vocational (MBO) students in technical training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- domain knowledge of electricity and electric power transmission
