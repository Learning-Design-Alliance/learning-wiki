# Non-prescriptive GAI feedback via sequenced guiding questions calibrated to cognitive challenge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that instead of offering direct answers or evaluation outcomes, GAI should pose guiding questions prompting deeper reflection so learners independently build their summative self-assessment frameworks. Feedback is to be "structured as thought-provoking, sequenced prompts, calibrated to an optimal level of cognitive challenge, designed to facilitate guided discovery, and promote deep reflective engagement". GAI acts as facilitator, assessor, and essentially collaborator in a guided discovery process, echoing knowledge-building and spiral collaborative knowledge improvement approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback must be based on previous scoring assessments and avoid revealing GAI's evaluation or offering direct solutions
#### Constraints
- The article warns the approach presumes harmonious learner-GAI collaboration and that risks of perceived authority and externalization of judgment remain under-theorized

### Target Learners
- learners in GAI-supported self-directed learning environments

### Target Learning Goals
- reflective self-assessment
- independent framework construction
- deep reflective engagement
