# Brainstorming 'Stuff We Want to Know About'

## Description
Learners and instructors co-create an ongoing list of topics of interest and questions related to course content. These questions can drive inquiry-based projects and allow learners to pursue areas of genuine interest.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a classroom environment that values learner input and curiosity. The instructor must be willing to relinquish some control and adapt instruction based on student interests.
#### Constraints
- Some topics may not align with curriculum objectives. Requires careful planning to ensure topics are appropriate and relevant. The process can be time-consuming.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Increase learner motivation, foster curiosity, promote inquiry-based learning, connect learning to student interests

### Affordances
- Increases motivation and engagement by allowing learners to pursue topics they find interesting. Connects learning to real-world issues. Fosters a learner mindset by encouraging curiosity and inquiry.

### Personalization
- The brainstorming process allows all learners to contribute their unique interests and perspectives. Learners can choose topics that align with their strengths and interests.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe student engagement and motivation levels. Assess the quality of student inquiry projects. Collect student feedback on the brainstorming process.

## Impact
- When students have a voice in what they learn, they are more likely to be engaged and motivated (Deci & Ryan, 2000). Inquiry-based learning has been shown to promote deeper understanding and critical thinking skills (Hmelo-Silver et al., 2007).
