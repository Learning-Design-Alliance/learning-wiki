# Implement outreach surveys at the closing moments of the event, assigned to a specific person, without mandatory questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends treating evaluation implementation as seriously as design: response rates are higher when surveys do not force a response to every question, and "response rates will be much higher if the survey is administered in the closing moments of the outreach event, rather than being sent out afterward". It advises including survey administration in event plans, administering immediately after the activity, and assigning it to a particular team member when multiple collaborators facilitate.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Survey administration should be included in event plans and assigned as the responsibility of a specific team member.
#### Constraints
- Applies to paper or virtual surveys administered after outreach activities; based on the authors' own experiences rather than a controlled test.

### Target Learners
- STEM outreach practitioners and program facilitators

### Target Learning Goals
- Maximizing survey response rates in outreach evaluation
