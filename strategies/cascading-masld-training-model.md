# Cascading training model from tertiary specialists to primary care physicians and community health workers for MASLD screening

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's Way forward section recommends a cascading capacity-building model in which training content flows from tertiary specialists down to primary care physicians and community health workers, so that screening protocols and use of non-invasive tools stay consistent across tiers. It states that "A cascading training model from tertiary specialists to primary care physicians and community health workers would ensure consistent screening protocols and the appropriate use of non-invasive tools, such as the FIB-4 index". This extends the evaluated three-day training-of-trainers into a sustained, multi-level system.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardized, evidence-based training modules adapted to state contexts across all healthcare tiers
#### Constraints
- The article notes that without structural support, knowledge gains may not improve patient outcomes; FibroScan devices are rare outside tertiary centres in LMICs

### Target Learners
- Tertiary specialists, primary care physicians, and community health workers in India's public health system

### Target Learning Goals
- Consistent MASLD screening protocols and appropriate use of non-invasive tools such as the FIB-4 index
