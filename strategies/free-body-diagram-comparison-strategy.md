# Require students to draw a free body diagram and explicitly compare the free body diagrams of the analog and the quiz problem before applying the shared principles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, in addition to the analogy intervention, students be explicitly asked to draw a free body diagram before solving the problem, and that a comparison between the diagrams for the two problems be explicitly enforced. This targets the observed error where students copied the analog's equation and obtained an incorrect sign for the centripetal acceleration (T=mg-mv2/r) by failing to notice the different direction of the acceleration in the two situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have access to both the solved analog and the quiz problem during the comparison
#### Constraints
- This strategy is proposed as a possible reduction of the sign error, not as a tested intervention in this study

### Target Learners
- calculus-based and algebra-based introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- correctly applying Newton's second law with centripetal acceleration in new contexts
- noting how a new situation requires changes in the details of applying a shared principle
