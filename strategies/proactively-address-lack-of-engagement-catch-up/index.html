Proactively monitor engagement trajectories and address students who fail to improve or catch up¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that educators use longitudinal engagement monitoring rather than single snapshots, treating disengagement as a warning sign and lack of improvement as especially ominous. It states that "Lack of improvement or 'catching up' may be a more ominous sign that should be proactively addressed", while improving to higher engagement was associated with acceptable achievement and rare dropouts. This supports person-centered, trajectory-based early-alert practice rather than one-off engagement flags.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Longitudinal LMS trace data across courses so that trajectories, not single time-points, can be observed

Constraints¶

  • Disengagement at any time point was consistently associated with lower achievement, but single-time-point engagement alone is not a consistent indicator of high achievement

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students in blended or online programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustaining engagement and preventing low achievement and drop-out

Affordances¶

  • Mhmm Multichannel Sequence Engagement Achievement

Claims¶

  • For average starters, high or improving engagement was reflected in comparable achievement, while low or non-improving engagement was associated with low achievement and occasional drop-out [+W]
  • For disengaged starters, starting low and failing to improve engagement was consequential, associated with a high probability of early drop-out, though about half improved [+W]
  • Online engagement at any single time-point is not a consistent indicator of high achievement across a full program, whereas longitudinal patterns of engagement and grades forecast stable academic trajectories [+W]
  • Monitoring all students and proactively intervening on early warning signs has minimal-level evidence of improving dropout-related outcomes [+W]
  • Strong early-warning monitoring systems paired with strong norms and routines help students recover emotionally and engage academically [+W]

  • Affect-aware CPS systems should monitor local trajectories of confusion and disengagement over time rather than responding to isolated affective signals
  • Use school-level data systems to identify which students need which types of support for dropout prevention
