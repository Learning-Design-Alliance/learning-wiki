# Adopt OMP and SNAPPS in midwifery precepting with an added informed-choice step, didactic preparation, online training, and laminated cards

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adopting OMP and SNAPPS in midwifery practice by adding a step that "specifically addresses informed choice and shared‐decision‐making", teaching the methods in didactic courses before clinical rotations, offering online training with continuing education credits for busy preceptors, and using laminated cards to keep preceptors and students on track. These measures aim to ease the preceptor role, lighten its burden, and increase willingness to precept.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time and financial investment for education programs to incorporate preceptor training, including faculty training, teaching allocation, and materials
#### Constraints
- Insufficient data reassure only partially: the only midwifery study was in a gynecology outpatient setting, and data are needed for hospital inpatient and community birth settings
- Midwives may resist the models if viewed as a medical framework lacking person-centered care

### Target Learners
- Midwifery students in clinical rotations
- Midwifery preceptors

### Target Learning Goals
- Standardized, reliable, consistent precepting interactions that promote critical thinking
