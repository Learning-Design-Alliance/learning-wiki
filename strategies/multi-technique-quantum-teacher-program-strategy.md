# Use a wide range of instructional techniques—lectures, animations, readings, problem solving, discussion, and essays—over a multi-week online program

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that effective teaching of quantum concepts to a quantum-illiterate but well-educated public "requires one to use a wide range of different techniques," including "lectures, computer-based animations and tutorials, readings, numerical problem solving, development of models for physical behavior, discussion of philosophical ideas related to the content, and writing essays that synthesize different ideas together." It states this can be done online over a 4-6 week period with 7-10 hours per week of effort.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Deep enough study that the ideas will stick, requiring a shift in the way teachers think
#### Constraints
- It cannot be done in a few day bootcamp; at least 40 hours of work spread over four to six weeks is needed

### Target Learners
- Quantum-illiterate but well-educated teachers and public learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Immersion in the bizarre behavior of the quantum world to shift teachers' thinking about indeterminacy and superposition
