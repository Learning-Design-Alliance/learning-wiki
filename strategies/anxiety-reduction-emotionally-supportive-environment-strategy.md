# Future interventions should prioritise anxiety-reduction strategies and emotionally supportive learning environments informed by Control-Value Theory

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that future work develop practical strategies for managing test anxiety and enhancing mental well-being, informed by the Control-Value Theory's emphasis on creating an emotionally supportive learning environment. It states future research "should prioritise the advancement of anxiety reduction strategies to enhance academic performance, focusing on the control-value theory's emphasis on creating an emotionally supportive learning environment", and suggests designing emotionally effective learning environments with clear instructional design, fostering self-regulation of negative emotions, and teaching emotion-oriented regulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should be informed by Control-Value Theory and address students' appraisal of control and value
#### Constraints
- The retrieved literature did not provide effective solutions for managing test anxiety; the review notes the recommended course intervention tested in Green et al. was not effective at raising scores
