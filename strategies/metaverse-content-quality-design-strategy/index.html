Design high-quality simulation and metaverse content with alignment to learning objectives, interactivity, and developmentally integrated deliberate practice from basic to advanced skills¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 qualitative), q2–q4 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's Content dimension synthesis concludes that 'high-quality simulation and metaverse content require alignment with learning objectives, interactivity, and deliberate practice that should be developmentally integrated from basic to advanced skills'. It recommends structuring simulation content around prioritized skills, repetitive practice with case variations, recorded performance data for feedback, and self-paced customization, while noting curriculum requirements still play a major role in guiding simulation content.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Content must align with learning objectives and support interactivity and deliberate practice

Constraints¶

  • Curriculum requirements still play a major role in guiding simulation content, so full self-paced customization has been only partially realized

Target Learners¶

  • medical students
  • surgical and other residents
  • nursing students
  • healthcare professionals

Target Learning Goals¶

  • technical skills
  • non-technical skills such as communication and teamwork
  • diagnostic reasoning
  • knowledge retention

Claims¶

  • XR-enriched simulations were the most prevalent technology in the reviewed studies (37%, n=65), with reported effectiveness in surgical, anatomical, and procedural skills development [+W]
  • The closer training content is to authentic clinical scenarios, the smoother its translation into practice [+W]
  • Technology-enhanced simulation may result in superior retention of clinical knowledge and skills compared to traditional teaching methods alone, with durable retention for months to years [+W]
  • Simulation-based training can significantly shorten learning curves for technical skills compared to traditional clinical training alone [+W]
  • Simulation Based Education Improves Outcomes [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Position immersive technologies and virtual patients as supplementary components within blended learning rather than stand-alone replacements for established clinical communication training
  • Select technology-based learning elements intentionally across four design considerations, chosen to align with stakeholders and learning theory
  • Implement AI simulations as complements within a stepped simulation continuum, with layered interaction modes and blended feedback
  • Integrate cost-effective simulation with structured, proficiency-based curricula into pediatric MIS training
