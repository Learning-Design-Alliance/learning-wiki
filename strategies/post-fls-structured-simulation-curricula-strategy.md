# Develop structured post-FLS simulation curricula with advanced skills, regular faculty coaching, and early orientation to simulation resources

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 4 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that residency programs develop or adopt structured simulation curricula that continue after FLS completion, including "advanced laparoscopic skills not currently assessed by FLS" aligned with progressive training milestones. It also recommends incorporating regular faculty coaching and feedback into simulation sessions, and formal orientation to simulation resources early in training, such as simulation task lists tied to clinical rotations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured curricula continuing after FLS completion, aligned with progressive training milestones
- Faculty or senior-resident simulation instructors to provide coaching and feedback
- Formal early orientation to simulation resources and guidance on how and where to practice
#### Constraints
- The article notes adoption of existing advanced curricula like ATLAS is limited by attitudes toward independent simulation practice, awareness, competition for resources, and cost and availability of simulation task trainers

### Target Learners
- general surgery residents (PGY-1 through PGY-5)

### Target Learning Goals
- advanced laparoscopic technical skills beyond the five FLS core tasks
- transfer of simulation-based skills to the operating room
- self-directed simulation practice habits
