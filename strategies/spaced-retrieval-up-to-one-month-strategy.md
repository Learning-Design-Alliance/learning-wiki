# Schedule independent retrieval of target information at intervals as long as one month over several years, rather than typical 1-2 day intervals over 10-15 weeks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators 'make certain that students independently retrieve target information at intervals that are as long as 1 month' over a period of several years, rather than the much shorter intervals typical of coursework. Extended schedules slow acquisition somewhat but yield disproportional long-term retention benefits, so optimum intervals must be established empirically for the material, acquisition stage, and students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Optimal rehearsal schedules must be based on empirically established optimum intervals between sessions.
- The optimum interval is likely to be the longest interval that avoids retrieval failures.
#### Constraints
- Extended training schedules would slow down acquisition somewhat, but they would yield disproportional benefits for long-term retention.

### Target Learners
- college students
- school students learning foreign vocabulary

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term retention of learned material over years
