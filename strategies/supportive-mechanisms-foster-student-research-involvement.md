# Establish supportive mechanisms to foster student involvement in research as a pathway to cultivating competent public health professionals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that public health education programmes establish supportive mechanisms to foster student involvement in research, based on the observed positive association between research participation and job competency. The authors state it is "recommended that supportive mechanisms be established to foster student involvement in research, thereby cultivating a cohort of public health professionals equipped with specialised competencies." This strategy treats mentor-directed research participation as an experiential mastery opportunity aligned with Social Cognitive Career Theory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supportive institutional mechanisms (research platforms, mentorship, academic support) must be in place to enable broad student participation in research activities
#### Constraints
- The underlying evidence is cross-sectional and cannot establish causality; the authors note the findings "cannot establish causality but provide empirical evidence supporting the encouragement of student participation"

### Target Learners
- Public health undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- Job competency development including professional knowledge, practical skills, and professional traits
