# Pegword Strategy

## Description
The pegword strategy is a mnemonic technique that uses rhyming words to represent numbers or order. These rhyming words, or “pegwords,” provide visual images that can be associated with facts or events. This strategy is particularly useful in helping students remember ordered or numbered information by associating events with numbers that rhyme with the pegwords.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Establishment of pegwords for numbers (e.g., one = bun, two = shoe, three = tree), creation of visual aids (pictures or drawings), teaching students the association process, and regular review.
#### Constraints
- May require significant initial effort to memorize pegwords. Effectiveness depends on the clarity and memorability of the chosen pegwords and visual associations.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- K-12 and higher education learners, especially those needing to memorize sequences, dates, or numbered lists. Particularly effective for students with learning disabilities or memory challenges.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve memory of ordered information, enhance association of events with numbers, facilitate recall of sequences and lists, reinforce visual imagery for memory retention

### Affordances
- Enhances memory encoding by associating numbers with memorable rhyming words and visual images. Facilitates recall of ordered information by providing a structured framework. Can be used across various subjects to memorize dates, events, and steps in a process.

### Personalization
- Teachers can customize the pegwords and associated images to match student interests and backgrounds, making the learning experience more relevant and engaging. Students can also create their own pegwords and images to further personalize the strategy.

- Improved scores on tests involving ordered information, increased ability to recall sequences, enhanced retention of numerical data, and positive student feedback on the strategy.

- Research has shown that the pegword strategy is effective in helping students remember ordered information. Its use has proven useful in teaching students to remember historical facts and other numbered sequences.

## Examples
- To help students remember the Sugar Act of 1764 (first Act), create a picture of sugar being poured on a hamburger bun (bun is the pegword for one). For the Stamp Act (second Act), picture a pair of shoes (shoe is the pegword for two) with a stamp on it.
