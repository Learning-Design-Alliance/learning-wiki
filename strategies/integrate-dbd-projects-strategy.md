# Educators should consider integrating Debugging by Design projects to support students' self-beliefs when making projects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educators and researchers consider integrating DbD projects alongside open-ended constructionist projects, because DbD completion was uniquely correlated with programming growth mindset, which they describe as particularly important for retention, perseverance, and endurance among marginalized populations in CS. Implementation follows the printed recipe: groups discuss encountered errors, decide on bugs, get teacher approval, write a project statement with circuit and aesthetic diagrams and buggy code, then exchange and solve peers' projects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A multi-lesson block (eight 50-minute lessons were used), teacher approval of designs, and exchange of project plans between student groups.
#### Constraints
- Future research is needed on the specific roles of designing bugs for peers versus solving peers' bugs, on in-person replication, and on conceptual understanding of bug types and debugging strategies.

### Target Learners
- secondary students in introductory computing (Exploring Computer Science e-textiles unit)

### Target Learning Goals
- programming growth mindset
- debugging self-efficacy
- computing self-beliefs
