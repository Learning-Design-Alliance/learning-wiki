# Making Connections (Text-to-Self, Text-to-Text, Text-to-World)

## Description
Making Connections is a comprehension strategy that helps learners relate a text to their own lives (text-to-self), to other texts they have read (text-to-text), or to events in the real world (text-to-world). By making these connections, learners can better understand and engage with the material. It involves modeling the different types of connections, providing guiding questions, and encouraging learners to share their connections through discussion and writing. This strategy draws on prior knowledge and experiences to connect with the text and make them more engaged in the reading experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Text to be read, guiding questions for each type of connection (text-to-self, text-to-text, text-to-world), materials for recording connections (e.g., double-entry journal, connection web).
#### Constraints
- Superficial connections may not enhance understanding. Learners may need explicit instruction and modeling to make meaningful connections.
