# Discipline-tailored pedagogic practices integrating Positive Psychology micro-interventions to foster professional recognition and entrepreneurial competence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors call for "disciplined-tailored pedagogic practices that integrate Positive Psychology micro-interventions (e.g., mindfulness, gratitude) and optimize institutional support to foster professional recognition and entrepreneurial competence in different academic fields". Because the activation of professional recognition by psychological resources is stronger in applied disciplines, interventions should be calibrated to disciplinary epistemic cultures rather than applied uniformly, with institutional supports such as peer collaboration and digital tools reinforcing professional identification.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness of each discipline's epistemic culture, since the PQ→PR pathway differs between applied and theoretical fields
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a cross-sectional correlational study; causal effectiveness of the micro-interventions themselves was not tested

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in applied and theoretical disciplines at universities

### Target Learning Goals
- Professional recognition of one's major and entrepreneurial competence

### Affordances
- Universal Mediation Contextual Activation Model
