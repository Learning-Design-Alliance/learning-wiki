# Use the fractal-dimension procedure as an interdisciplinary tool across high-school subjects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend reusing the same estimation procedure to open interdisciplinary themes across the high-school curriculum: "one could use the procedure to discuss Olbers' paradox in astronomy classes, to estimate the size of coasts and watersheds in geography classes", to differentiate leaves morphologically in biology, characterize roughness in optics, investigate lightning in electricity classes, and discuss shade, light and perspective in photography and art classes. The procedure demands only a few resources and a short time.
