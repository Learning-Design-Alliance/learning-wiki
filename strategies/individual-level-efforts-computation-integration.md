# Target individual faculty rather than departments when promoting integration of computation into physics courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the meaningful predictors of teaching computation are individual experiences and beliefs rather than departmental or institutional factors, the authors conclude that deciding to teach computation is a personal choice, "suggesting that integrating com- putation into a course is a personal choice". They therefore recommend that, at this moment, efforts to increase computation use be aimed at individuals. For the early majority of faculty, they note future work should provide evidence that computation adds value to their courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Efforts should address individual faculty experiences with computation and beliefs about its value, since those were the discriminating factors
#### Constraints
- The authors frame this as holding at this moment, a snapshot of the current state; they expect the meaningful factors to change as adoption spreads

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics students taught by faculty considering integrating computation

### Target Learning Goals
- Integration of computation into undergraduate physics courses
