# Introduce integral calculus through a practical everyday problem — fairly slicing a watermelon — to motivate students and counter dropout from scientific curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes starting instruction in geometry, calculus and integrals from a concrete, familiar problem: sharing a watermelon into equal-volume slices. Students see that "geometry, calculus and in particular integrals" are not merely technical tools but solve a real question, with cut positions found by solving p(λ) = fn. The activity is designed to be reproducible at home with kitchen tools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A watermelon (or spheroid-like object), kitchen tools, and a measuring cup for the Archimedes-principle volume measurement
#### Constraints
- The relative distribution of skin (edible vs non-edible material) is not accounted for; producing equal volumes of edible pulp would require a more cumbersome computation beyond the scope of the note

### Target Learners
- young students in physics and mathematics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- familiarity with integral calculus, geometry and technical tools routinely employed in physics and mathematics
