# Use the validated high-fidelity model to determine and shorten the learning curve of laparoscopic fetal surgeons and retain operative skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using the validated rabbit model before clinical practice to establish how many simulated cases a laparoscopic fetal surgeon needs for competency, and to maintain skills. As the authors state, "We propose to use our high-fidelity model to determine and shorten the learning curve of laparoscopic fetal surgeons and retain operative skills". This follows the IDEAL recommendation that preclinical simulators are essential prior to first-in-human trials, and the model should be paired with off- and on-site clinical training and guidance from established fetal centers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Should be paired with off- and on-site clinical training and guidance from established fetal centers, as the article states
#### Constraints
- The article surmises the 33-case learning curve applies to surgeons experienced with complex multi-port laparoscopy and may be an underestimation for surgeons without that experience

### Target Learners
- laparoscopic fetal surgeons transitioning from open to fetoscopic spina bifida repair

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical competency determination and operative skill retention
