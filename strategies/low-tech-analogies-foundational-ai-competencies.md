# Teach foundational AI competencies with low-tech analogies and contextually relevant activities where technology access is limited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy responds to first-level digital divide constraints by teaching foundational AI literacy competencies without requiring full technology access. The article states that while "The SAIL framework cannot directly solve these infrastructural issues, but its design ensures some inclusivity - many of the foundational competencies can be taught with low-tech analogies and contextually relevant activities." Building AI literacy even in limited-access contexts is presented as a way to prevent the divide between those who have and those who have not from widening. Culturally relevant examples and accessible language support the same inclusivity goal.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators adapt delivery with analogies, culturally relevant examples, and accessible language suited to their learners' contexts.
#### Constraints
- Applies to foundational competencies; the article does not claim advanced levels can be taught without technology access.

### Target Learners
- Learners in contexts with limited technology access or infrastructure

### Target Learning Goals
- Foundational AI concepts and awareness despite limited access to devices and connectivity
