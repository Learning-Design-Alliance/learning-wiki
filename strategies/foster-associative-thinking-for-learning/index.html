Foster associative thinking in educational settings to support positive learning outcomes alongside creativity emphasis¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors close by proposing that as educators emphasize creativity, deliberately fostering students' capacity for goal-directed associative thinking — deliberately forming novel yet meaningful connections between concepts — could be crucial for learning outcomes. This recommendation follows from their finding that goal-directed semantic distance predicted vocabulary retention beyond intelligence and mediated the creativity–learning link.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Activities should elicit goal-directed (novel yet meaningful) association, since free spontaneous association showed no significant correlation with learning in Study 2

Constraints¶

  • The current evidence is correlational; the authors state causality should be tested experimentally via training paradigms before interventions are claimed

Target Learners¶

  • university students; extension to other ages and domains proposed as open

Target Learning Goals¶

  • vocabulary learning and associative learning of new verbal information

Claims¶

  • Associative thinking partially mediates the relationship between creativity and vocabulary learning, and fully mediates it when intelligence is controlled [+W]
  • Associative thinking (semantic distance of generated word associations) predicts paired-associate foreign-language vocabulary learning beyond general cognitive ability in undergraduates [+W]
  • Fluid intelligence predicts paired-associate word learning, but crystallized intelligence does not once associative thinking is controlled [+W]
  • Only goal-directed, not free, association correlates with vocabulary retention; the associative thinking–learning link is driven by deliberate rather than spontaneous association [+W]
  • A latent creativity factor (writing, drawing, openness) predicts paired-associate vocabulary learning, controlling for general intelligence [+W]
  • Prior learning of word pairs reduces the semantic distance of goal-directed (but not free) associations, suggesting interference from newly acquired knowledge [~W]
