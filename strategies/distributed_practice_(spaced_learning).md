# Distributed Practice (Spaced Learning)

## Description
Distributed practice, also known as spaced learning, involves reviewing information or practicing skills across multiple sessions spread out over time, rather than in one continuous session (massed practice). It leverages the spacing effect, where spaced reviews lead to superior long-term retention and learning compared to cramming. The gaps between occurrences of an item make retrieval more effortful, strengthening memory. Distributed practice enhances memory and higher-order learning by leveraging contextual variability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured review materials and tools to track spacing intervals. Computer programs can deliver assignments in a distributed fashion.
#### Constraints
- Requires advance planning to schedule review sessions. Learners may not immediately perceive the benefits compared to massed practice.

### Affordances
- Increases the durability of learning by making retrieval more effortful, encodes information with varied contextual cues, reduces habituation, and enhances retention of complex skills.

### Personalization
- Timing of review sessions can be adjusted based on individual learning needs and progress. Technology can facilitate spaced repetition schedules.
