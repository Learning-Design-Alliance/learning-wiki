# Deploy co-produced e-learning as part of a combined programme including face-to-face group training, targeted at front-line staff and newly qualified staff induction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Participants identified that the digital educational intervention would be most effective if embedded in wider workforce development: it "would be most effective if it was part of a combined programme which could include face-to-face group training allowing for skill practice and reflection". Interviewees recommended targeting dissemination at all front-line staff rather than only nurses, and suggested it would be particularly useful for newly qualified staff as a component of an induction programme. A minority also suggested adding health professional perspectives, subtitles, more interactive activities and peer support through an online community.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery alongside face-to-face group training allowing for skill practice and reflection; a quiet environment was reported as essential to enable focus when accessing the programme at work
#### Constraints
- Half of interviewed participants found it difficult to access the intervention at work due to other demands on their time; some accessed it on night shifts, which they recognised may have affected their ability to retain information

### Target Learners
- Front-line healthcare staff working with CYP
- Newly qualified staff undertaking induction

### Target Learning Goals
- Caring for children and young people admitted with self-harm
