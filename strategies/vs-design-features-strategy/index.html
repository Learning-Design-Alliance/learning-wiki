Design virtual simulations with ease-of-use features, haptic technology, moderate immersion, and postscenario feedback¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's discussion derives design recommendations from its subgroup and literature findings. For usability, it recommends features that "enhance ease of use and users' level of satisfaction (eg, step-by-step instructions, user-friendly interfaces)" so students spend time learning rather than wrestling with software. For skills, it suggests adding haptic technology for realistic hands-on experiences and warns that too much immersion can cause cognitive overload; for clinical reasoning it cites beneficial elements including training duration over 30 minutes, varied clinical scenarios, nonimmersive 2D environments, and postscenario feedback.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Designers must balance immersion against cognitive load, since 3D digital environments can increase cognitive load as students get distracted by irrelevant stimuli

Constraints¶

  • These design feature recommendations come from the authors' interpretation of subgroup findings and cited studies, not from directly tested design comparisons in this meta-analysis

Target Learners¶

  • medical and nursing students in pre- and postregistration education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • procedural skill acquisition
  • clinical reasoning
  • communication skills

Claims¶

  • Haptic simulator trials more frequently show superior accuracy outcomes when force-feedback and structured guidance are included [+W]
  • Haptic virtual reality simulation reduces procedural time for pulpotomy training compared with conventional simulators [+W]
  • Several included studies found haptic VR training equivalent rather than superior to conventional dental training [~W]
  • Immersive VR-haptic and ITS modalities are more often associated with objective psychomotor and cognitive gains, while generative AI platforms are more often associated with affective outcomes [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Progressively integrate haptic simulators from basic skills to complex surgical tasks across the curriculum
  • Prefer mixed reality with hand tracking over head-mounted-display controllers for gesture-simulation training
