# Use personal 3D-printed bone sets to augment reduced laboratory time via mock oral-examination practice at home

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
When laboratory contact time is limited, give students 3D-printed bones to keep and structure activities that directly rehearse the practical assessment: identifying landmarks, overlaying muscles, and running mock peer-to-peer examinations. Students reported that "performing mock examinations prior to the viva with the 3DP bones was instrumental in both preparing for the viva and improving their performance during the viva".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Printed models must be anatomically accurate (all models were visually assessed by the teaching team)
- An oral or practical examination to prepare for, around which mock practice is organized
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only for musculoskeletal anatomy of the upper limb in a single institution, so generalization to other curricula should be applied with caution

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate allied health students

### Target Learning Goals
- practical/oral anatomy examination skills
- independent learning and study habits
