# Implement peer-teaching-based exam training with structured curriculum integration, realistic scenarios, defined roles, and trained tutors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that structured peer teaching formats such as PT-BET can be a low-threshold yet effective way of preparing students for oral exams. For successful implementation, the authors specify that "peer teaching is not left to informal self-organization, but is integrated into a clearly structured and curriculum-embedded didactic framework," that "exam scenarios are realistic," that "the roles of students are clearly defined as either teacher or learner," and that "tutors are trained effectively in observing the students' performance as teachers/learners and in giving standardized, appreciative feedback." The authors also emphasize that peer teaching should be understood as preparatory, didactically structured learning support and not as a substitute for authentic exam situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curriculum-embedded didactic framework
- Realistic exam scenarios
- Clearly defined student roles as teacher or learner
- Effectively trained tutors providing standardized, appreciative feedback
#### Constraints
- Peer teaching should not be a substitute for authentic exam situations

### Target Learners
- Medical students preparing for oral examinations

### Target Learning Goals
- Communication skills
- Exam rhetoric
- Self-efficacy expectations
- Stress reduction
