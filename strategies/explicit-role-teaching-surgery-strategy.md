# Explicitly teach professional role patterns in surgical courses, including integration of other professions and adoption of teamwork training from high-risk industries

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend improving awareness of professional roles by explicitly teaching the corresponding patterns of Manager and Collaborator in surgical courses. They point to a Scottish surgical college running regular courses teaching how to ensure safety through good communication and teamwork, propose that integrating other relevant professions in training scenarios can improve outcomes concerning competencies in interprofessional teams, and note that teamwork training programs like Crew Resource Management may sustain the prioritization of nontechnical skills in surgery. They also cite Lafleur et al. proposing the use of clinical studies to teach professional roles explicitly.
