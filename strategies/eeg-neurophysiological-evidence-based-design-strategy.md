# Use EEG-based neurophysiological evidence to strengthen evidence-based architectural design of learning spaces

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advocates an EEG-based approach in which learners perform cognitive tasks in controlled (virtual) classroom variants while neural features are analyzed with machine learning, providing "rigorous evidence that brain activity features during cognitive tasks are affected by the design elements of window placement and room dimensions". The authors state the "ongoing development of this EEG-based approach has the potential to strengthen evidence-based design through the use of solid neurophysiological evidence", enabling precise adjustment of isolated architectural variables that would be infeasible in real buildings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Immersive VR or comparable controlled environments allowing precise manipulation of isolated architectural variables while holding temperature, noise and air quality constant
- Machine-learning feature extraction and leave-one-participant-out validation to identify generalizable neural signatures
#### Constraints
- Implications for student learning are yet to be determined; the recommended experiments use longer sessions per design condition and baseline resting EEG measured within each environment
- Prior work found no significant difference in EEG band-power between real and identical virtual classrooms, so VR is a proxy rather than a full replacement

### Target Learners
- students in classroom learning environments

### Target Learning Goals
- cognitive performance during working memory, attention and visual tasks in designed learning spaces
