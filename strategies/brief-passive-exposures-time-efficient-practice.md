# Apply brief passive exposures to classroom-learned information as a time-efficient practice tool

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend exploring whether the reactivation paradigm can be translated into daily-life educational settings: applying brief passive audio-visual exposures to information learned in the classroom, potentially as a time-efficient tool for skill acquisition and to reduce the requirement for prolonged task execution. They suggest future studies test whether incorporating spacing and testing effects into the reactivations, or adding more reactivation-reconsolidation cycles, would yield even more efficient learning, and note potential benefit for disorders caused by neurological conditions such as ADHD.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Information that has already been encoded via initial practice; reactivations performed in a distributed manner on separate days
#### Constraints
- The classroom application is proposed for future research; the article tested only arbitrary numeric facts with adult participants online

### Target Learners
- Adult learners; potentially learners with neurological conditions such as ADHD

### Target Learning Goals
- Numerical fluency and time-efficient retention of learned facts

### Affordances
