# Reserve a large enough block of problem-solving practice (about four weeks rather than two) at the end of a concepts-first course

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A scheduling strategy for implementing the concepts-first pattern: after the conceptual portion of the course, dedicate a substantial end-of-quarter block to complicated problem practice. The author, who later taught a version with only two weeks of problem solving, suggests "the problem solving time needs to be large enough (i.e. 4 weeks rather than 2) to be important to the students and to not overlap their ﬁnal exam preparation time." In that shortened version "the transfer eﬀects are still present" but the underrepresented-minority grade gap was about half rather than one-eighth of the standard-lecture gap.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A quarter long enough to separate conceptual instruction from an extended final block of complicated problem practice
- Problem-solving practice should be scheduled so it does not overlap students' final exam preparation time
#### Constraints
- The four-week recommendation is the author's stated inference from teaching the two-week version twice, with those results to be published rather than reported in this article

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- problem-solving with complicated analytic problems
- closing grade gaps for underrepresented minority students
