# Use low-cost, home-available materials for the spreading-film experiments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The experimental phase uses a transparent plastic bottle with a small orifice, a transparent plate (polymethyl methacrylate, polycarbonate or glass), rulers, a mobile phone camera recording at 30 frames per second, and common liquids. The article states "These experiments can be performed using common materials that are typically available at home", making the activity suitable for an undergraduate laboratory or even at home. Radius versus time is extracted from underneath-plate video using ImageJ, with two synchronized videos providing the time origin.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The orifice diameter must be much smaller than the bottle's diameter so the liquid level, and thus flow rate, changes very little
- The orifice-to-plate distance is kept short (~5 cm) to prevent jet breakup into droplets before impact
- Plate materials must be smooth enough that surface roughness does not influence results
#### Constraints
- Measurements start once the liquid layer forms a circle and stop when it loses its circular shape

### Target Learners
- engineering or physics undergraduates

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental design
- data analysis
- video-based measurement techniques
