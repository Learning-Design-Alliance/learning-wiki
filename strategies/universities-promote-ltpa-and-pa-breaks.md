# Universities promote physical activity both in leisure time and as short breaks during the learning process

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that LTPA related to all studied outcomes and PA breaks to functional stress and study ability, the authors recommend a two-pronged approach: universities "should offer opportunities to be physically active in leisure time" and "should support PA breaks during the learning process and in the immediate location of study". Concretely this includes university sports courses and facilities, instructor videos for PA breaks during home studying, and instructors leading in-person PA breaks in libraries, lectures and seminars, which also reduces sedentary behavior.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- University structures such as a sports department offering courses and facilities, and provision of break videos or in-person break leaders
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from a small, female-skewed daily-diary sample during COVID-19 home studying; replication is needed

### Target Learners
- university students

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining health, stress regulation, recovery, and perceived academic functioning during studying
