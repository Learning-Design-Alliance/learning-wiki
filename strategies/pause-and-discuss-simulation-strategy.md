# Pause & Discuss: student-controlled pausing of simulation for team clinical reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
During the simulation scenario, teams may stop the simulation at will to confer with peers about next assessment or intervention steps, using a think-aloud protocol to verbalise clinical reasoning, with peer-driven feedback during the scenario and formal feedback reserved for debriefing. The article states "Students were permitted to 'stop' the simulation at their discretion to confer with their peers regarding the subsequent steps of assessment or intervention", and links this to cultivating metacognitive skills for clinical judgement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A team-based (rather than individual clinician) simulation format
- An instructor able to control the simulation and narrate clinical signs
#### Constraints
- The article identifies this feature as addressing psychological stress during the scenario phase, per Bae et al.'s identified impediment

### Target Learners
- final-year speech-language pathology master's students in clinical simulation

### Target Learning Goals
- collaborative clinical reasoning
- shared decision-making
- metacognitive clinical judgement
