# Use the log of the prevalence odds ratio as the target of estimation in meta-analytic modeling

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that, for meta-analytic modeling, analysts target the log of the POR rather than the ratio itself, because the POR's unbounded range makes modeling awkward. The author writes that 'the POR ranges from 0 to positive infinity, it is therefore useful to use the log of the prevalence odds ratio rather than the ratio itself,' defining log-POR as the target of estimation and inference. This strategy applies when synthesizing single-case studies whose behavioral outcomes were recorded with different procedures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A behavior-stream model under which the POR is defined for each study's recording procedure
#### Constraints
- The log-POR is undefined or requires strong assumptions for event counting and partial interval recording data unless average event duration is known

### Target Learners
- individual cases in single-case intervention studies (e.g., students with problem behavior or task dis-engagement)

### Target Learning Goals
- quantifying and synthesizing changes in observed behavioral outcomes across intervention phases
