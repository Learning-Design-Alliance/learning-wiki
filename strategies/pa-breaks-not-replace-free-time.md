# Schedule classroom physical activity breaks so they do not replace students' free time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In this study the physical activity breaks replaced homeroom free time rather than instructional time, and adherence was low: on average students participated fewer than 14 times out of 20 potential chances. The authors recommend that future programs avoid displacing free time, stating "student participation adherence rate will be better if PA programs do not replace students' free time." They suggest the assumption that students will be motivated to participate is more likely to hold when breaks replace traditionally sedentary instructional time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A daily slot in the school schedule, ideally within instructional or traditionally sedentary time rather than free time
#### Constraints
- The article notes there are neither consensus nor recommendations about the modality, frequency, or intensity of physical activity breaks needed to achieve intended health benefits

### Target Learners
- middle school students

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained participation in classroom physical activity breaks to achieve fitness benefits
