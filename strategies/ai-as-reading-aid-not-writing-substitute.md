# Frame generative AI as an optional comprehension aid during reading rather than a substitute for producing the summary

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the experiment's Active condition, participants read the paper and could optionally question an AI chatbot to enhance comprehension rather than delegate the writing. This preserved more engagement than full AI writing, though accuracy still fell on average; benefits accrued mainly to students with strong prior knowledge. The design suggests positioning AI as a question-answering aid during reading instead of a summary generator.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants/students must still engage directly with the reading material and answer comprehension checks themselves
#### Constraints
- Average accuracy in the guided active-reading condition still declined by 12% relative to manual writing, and the distribution of outcomes was bimodal

### Target Learners
- college-educated students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- comprehension of academic reading materials
