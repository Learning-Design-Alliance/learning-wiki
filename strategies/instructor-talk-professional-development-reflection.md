# Use the Instructor Talk framework in professional development to help instructors reflect on the learning environments they create

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that the Instructor Talk framework "holds promise in professional development settings to assist instructors in reflecting on the learning environments they create." Because the framework categorizes the noncontent language instructors use—from building relationships to explaining pedagogical choices—it gives faculty a concrete vocabulary for examining what they say beyond content. The strategy is to introduce instructors to the five categories and 17 subcategories and have them analyze or reflect on their own classroom talk against them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to recordings or transcripts of an instructor's own class sessions for reflection
- Familiarity with the framework's categories and subcategories
#### Constraints
- The framework was developed in one cotaught introductory biology course, and the authors note further characterization is required to determine impacts of different levels, types, and timing of Instructor Talk

### Target Learners
- College instructors, particularly those implementing student-centered teaching in undergraduate STEM courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving classroom learning environments, reducing student resistance to active learning, and promoting inclusive teaching
