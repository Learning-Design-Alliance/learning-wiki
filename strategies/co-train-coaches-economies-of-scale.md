# Co-train coaches across schools to capture economies of scale in training and equipping coaching programmes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report notes that training and equipping coaches carries economies of scale when schools pool the activity: "there are clearly economies of scale in training and equipping groups of coaches". It contrasts the cost of training just one or two coaches in a school with linking with other schools to co-train five or six coaches, and reports minimum and maximum per-pupil costs for a five-coach, 60-pupil deployment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A school employing five coaches working with 12 pupils each over one academic year, with core costs covering coach salaries, administrative support, training and resources
#### Constraints
- The report notes the cost of the programme was high compared to other literacy catch-up approaches, including those delivered one to one, due to coach salary costs and the intensity of support

### Target Learners
- Year 7 pupils with low attainment in English at secondary school

### Target Learning Goals
- literacy catch-up in reading and writing
