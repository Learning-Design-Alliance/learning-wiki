# Paired-question technique: matching questions so students get one right and one wrong, then guiding refinement of raw intuition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
In epistemologized tutorials, instead of opening questions research shows most students get wrong, a pair of matched questions is created "so that most students will get one right and one wrong." Elaborating the pair reveals that students' intuitions lead them into conflict; they are then guided to find the essentially correct raw intuition underlying both answers and to refine it toward results consistent with the physics being learned. This convinces students their intuitions are valuable when properly refined, avoiding the negative epistemological side effect of cognitive-conflict approaches.
