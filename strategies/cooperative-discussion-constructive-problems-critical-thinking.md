# Use cooperative group discussion with constructive problems to train critical thinking in mathematics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that mathematics learning be focused on developing critical thinking by giving students freedom to try their own solutions through constructive problems and classroom discussion. "Critical thinking ability can be trained when that ability is applied in situations in classroom discu ssions that discuss certain mathematical concepts." In discussions, students argue rationally, and if a teacher consistently trains critical thinking, the learning output produces students with better critical thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must provide constructive problems and facilitate classroom discussion where students argue rationally
#### Constraints
- Conventional teaching methods (telling students what to do without exploration) should be avoided as they produce rote learning without deep understanding

### Target Learners
- Secondary school mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Mathematical critical thinking, including comparing, categorizing, explaining, sequencing, evaluating evidence, predicting, and decision-making
