Diagnose self-regulation at semester start and provide scaffolding or self-regulation instruction for online learners¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on findings that self-regulation is required to benefit from learner autonomy online, the authors recommend that instructors "diagnose their self-regulation level at the beginning of semester and provide appropriate instructional assistance or scaffolding, if necessary". They further suggest instructors "teach self-regulation strategies and provide opportunities to practice self-regulation as an orientation program near the beginning of a semester or when entering an online learning degree program".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructor capacity to assess students' self-regulatory skill levels early in a course

Constraints¶

  • Recommended for students with low self-regulatory skill or experience in online learning environments

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in online and blended courses
  • students with low self-regulatory skill

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-regulated learning
  • learning engagement

Claims¶

  • Self-regulation was positively related to both cognitive presence and learning engagement [+W]
  • In a cross-sectional survey analysed with SEM, self-efficacy's direct path to learning engagement was non-significant, and its association with engagement ran indirectly through self-regulation, teaching presence and cognitive presence (statistical mediation, which cannot show cause) [+W]
  • Cognitive presence was positively related to learning engagement and mediated relationships of teaching presence (full) and self-regulation (partial) with engagement [+W]
  • Self-efficacy for learning was positively related to teaching presence, self-regulation, and cognitive presence among Korean undergraduates during COVID-19 [+W]
