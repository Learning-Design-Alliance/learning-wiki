Use SMS as a complementary device for vocabulary instruction and assessment, including reciprocal testing¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that language teachers and institutes incorporate mobile learning into language courses as a readily available complement to face-to-face instruction, moving toward a learner-centered classroom in which students are more responsible for their own learning. Beyond one-way delivery, the authors suggest reciprocal use: "they can send some tests to students and ask them to send back the answers via SMS." Students can likewise use mobile vocabulary learning as self-study inside and outside the classroom.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students already use mobiles and send and receive SMS regularly

Constraints¶

  • SMS character limits restrict the number of words and the length of example sentences per message

Target Learners¶

  • university EFL students
  • language institute learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • vocabulary learning and long-term retention

Affordances¶

  • Mall Mobile Assisted Language Learning Definition

Claims¶

  • Both SMS and dictionary groups gained higher mean scores on the delayed post-test than on the immediate post-test [+W]
  • The SMS group's vocabulary scores rose significantly from post-test to delayed post-test four weeks later [+W]
  • Vocabulary taught via SMS is retained significantly better in the long term than vocabulary learned with a dictionary [+W]
  • University freshmen learning academic vocabulary via SMS show no significant short-term advantage over freshmen learning the same words with a dictionary [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use SMS as a low-requirement support tool in learning processes
  • Integrate e-learning as a supplement to rather than replacement of traditional teaching (blended learning)
