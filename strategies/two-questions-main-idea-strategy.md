# Teach main idea identification with two repeated questions: who or what is the paragraph about, and what happens in the paragraph

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the intervention's third activity, the assistant reminded students what a main idea is and repeated the two questions that must be asked to identify one: "Who or what is the paragraph about?" and "What happens in the paragraph?" Students took turns rereading a paragraph, answering the questions, and formulating a sentence describing the main idea, with a drawing shown once the idea was identified. This routine was repeated for all paragraphs of the text.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A text left within view of students and turn-taking so each student participates; the assistant offers help when required
#### Constraints
- The article reports only moderate mastery of this strategy and suggests some main ideas in the instructional texts were too implicit or too abstract

### Target Learners
- students with high-functioning autism spectrum disorder in small groups

### Target Learning Goals
- main idea identification
- reading comprehension
