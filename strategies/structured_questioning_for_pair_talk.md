# Structured Questioning for Pair Talk

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Structured questioning for pair talk means giving pairs of learners a specific, well-formed question — often sequenced, scaffolded, or tiered — together with a clear goal and time frame, before they begin talking. Rather than asking students to "discuss with your partner," the instructor supplies the prompt, the expected product of the talk (an answer, a decision, an explanation), and a time limit, then monitors and harvests responses. The structure converts unguided conversation into a focused cognitive task.

## Design Implications

Pair talk is a form of [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md), but its benefits depend on the quality of the prompt: vague prompts produce social chat, while structured questions direct attention to the target content and elicit elaborative explanation [Active learning improves exam performance relative to lecture alone.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. Requiring learners to explain and justify answers to a partner produces elaboration and self-explanation effects that strengthen retrieval and understanding [~S]. Structure also manages [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md): a well-posed question defines the problem space so pairs spend working memory on reasoning rather than on figuring out what they are supposed to do.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A question that is open enough to require reasoning but bounded enough to be answerable in the allotted time
- An explicit product for the talk ("agree on the best answer," "each explain your reasoning, then reconcile")
- A defined time frame, typically 1–5 minutes, announced before talk begins
- An accountability mechanism — random reporting, pair sharing out, or written capture — so talk is not optional in practice
- Instructor circulation to monitor talk quality and select pairs to report

#### Constraints
- Unstructured or vague prompts ("discuss this topic") reliably degrade into off-task social talk, especially among novices who lack the knowledge to sustain content-focused dialogue [-M]
- Questions that are too difficult for the pair's current knowledge produce floundering and shared misconceptions rather than productive struggle [-M]; pairs can reinforce each other's errors when neither partner has relevant knowledge [~S]
- Overly long pair-talk episodes lose focus; effectiveness drops sharply beyond roughly 5–7 minutes without a new prompt [~W]
- If reporting is never required, accountability collapses and participation becomes uneven [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Think-Pair-Share**: individual thinking time before pair talk, which raises answer quality and widens participation [~S]
- **Sequenced question ladders**: a series of questions escalating from recall to inference to evaluation, so talk builds
- **Role assignment**: one partner explains, the other questions or checks, then roles swap — a lightweight version of [Learning by Teaching](../claims/learning-by-teaching-improves-tutor-learning.md) [+S]
- **Sentence starters and talk frames**: scaffold the *form* of academic talk for language learners or novices [~M]
- **Error-analysis prompts**: pairs diagnose a flawed solution, leveraging [Erroneous examples build conceptual knowledge.](../claims/erroneous-examples-build-conceptual-knowledge.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Novices and mixed-ability groups, provided the question is calibrated to what they can attempt with a partner — structure substitutes for the guidance an expert would need less of [~M]
- Multilingual learners, who benefit from sentence frames and a low-stakes rehearsal space before whole-class [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) [~M]
- Less beneficial for learners with strong prior knowledge on easy questions, where structured talk adds little beyond what solo work achieves [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: articulating and reconciling explanations
- Reasoning and justification: [Argumentation](../elements/argumentation.md) in miniature before formal argument construction
- Retrieval practice: answering and explaining from memory strengthens retention [~S]
- Preparation for whole-class discourse: pairs rehearse ideas before public sharing

### Instructions
1. Pose the structured question aloud and in writing, with the goal and time frame stated explicitly.
2. Optionally insert individual think time (30–60 seconds) before talk begins.
3. Launch pair talk; circulate to listen, prompt with follow-up questions, and note pairs with strong or contrasting answers.
4. Close the talk on time and require a visible product — a pair reports, a vote is taken, or answers are written.
5. Debrief the *reasoning*, not just the answer, connecting pair ideas to the target concept; use [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) routines to keep the reporting phase fast and inclusive.

## Related Strategies
- [Think-Pair-Share](../patterns/think-pair-share.md) — the canonical protocol this strategy generalizes; structured questioning is what supplies its "think" and "share" phases with substance
- [Peer Instruction](peer-instruction.md) — structured pair talk embedded in a vote–discuss–revote cycle around a conceptually targeted question
- [Reciprocal Questioning](reciprocal-questioning.md) — extends structure by having learners generate the questions themselves

## Examples
- **Peer Instruction (Eric Mazur, Harvard University)** — students answer a conceptually targeted clicker question individually, then convince a neighbor before revoting; the question design is the structure that makes the pair talk productive. [https://www.per-central.org](https://www.per-central.org)
- **Kagan Structures** — published cooperative-learning protocols (e.g., RallyCoach, Timed Pair Share) that specify question type, roles, and timing for pair interaction. [https://www.kaganonline.com](https://www.kaganonline.com)
- **Talk Science / Accountable Talk routines (Institute for Learning, University of Pittsburgh)** — question and talk stems that structure academically productive peer and whole-class discourse. [https://ifl.pitt.edu](https://ifl.pitt.edu)
