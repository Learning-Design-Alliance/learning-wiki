# Structured Questioning for Pair Talk

## Description
Using structured questions with clear goals and timeframes provides focus and scaffolding for pair talk activities. These questions should be designed to elicit specific types of responses, such as identifying key factors, comparing advantages and disadvantages, or explaining processes. Announcing the timeframe in advance helps students manage their time and stay on task.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires careful planning of questions that align with learning objectives. A visible timer can be useful for managing time.
#### Constraints
- Overly structured questions may limit creativity and exploration. Questions must be clear and easily understood by all students.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Focus pair discussions, provide structure and scaffolding for students, encourage in-depth exploration of topics, and improve time management during collaborative activities.

### Affordances
- Reduces aimless discussions, promotes focused thinking, helps students structure their responses, and improves their ability to stay on task. Acts as a scaffold.

### Personalization
- Questions can be differentiated to match students' readiness levels. Timeframes can be adjusted based on the complexity of the question and students' needs.

- Structured questioning can improve the focus and effectiveness of pair talk activities, leading to deeper understanding and better learning outcomes.
