# Use a single-question free-response final exam to reveal which topics, explanations, and visual representations students privilege

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advocates the single-question free-response exam format, in which students are asked to discuss everything they learned, including formulas, definitions, drawings, and concept discussions, with spelling and grammar ungraded. The authors state this format "has been historically under-utilized as a research tool in probing the nuances in students' comprehension of science, and its potential use in teachers' self-assessment has also gone unexplored." It allows a unique view into which aspects of the course were privileged by students and can reveal subtle teaching and learning deficiencies not apparent on traditional exam questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should have prior experience answering open-ended questions posed during lessons
- Instructions permitting use of the maternal language were employed to help students feel more at ease with the format
#### Constraints
- The article reports trade-offs will always be present between closed and open-ended question formats, citing Martinez

### Target Learners
- Lower secondary (6th grade) science students

### Target Learning Goals
- Comprehensive recall and sensemaking of a year's physics content, communicated through written and visual representations
