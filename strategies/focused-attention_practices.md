# Focused-Attention Practices

## Description
Focused-attention practice is a brain exercise for quieting the thousands of thoughts that distract and frustrate us each day. When the mind is quiet and focused, learners are able to be present with a specific sound, sight, or taste. Quieting our minds ignites the parasympathetic nervous system, reducing heart rate and blood pressure while enhancing coping strategies to effectively handle the day-to-day challenges. Thinking improves and emotions begin to regulate so learners can approach an experience with variable options.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a quiet space and brief periods of time (e.g., 1-5 minutes). May use guided audio, videos, or instructor-led exercises.
#### Constraints
- Some learners may find it difficult to focus or may be skeptical of the practice. Requires consistent implementation to see benefits.

## Examples
- Breathing: Use the breath as a focus point. Have students place one hand close to but not touching their nose and the other on their belly. Colors: Visualize colors while focusing on the breath. Inhale a deep green, and exhale a smoky gray. Movement: Direct students to stand and, as they inhale, lift an arm or leg and wiggle it, exhaling it back to its original position. The Deep-Dive Breath: Have students inhale for four counts, hold for four, and exhale for four counts. Energizing Breath: Have students pant like a dog with their mouths open and their tongues out for 30 seconds, and then continue for another 30 seconds with their mouths closed, taking short breaths with one hand on the belly. Sound: The use of sound is very powerful for engaging a calm response. Rise and Fall: Learners can lie on the floor and place an object on their stomachs, enhancing their focus by watching the rising and falling of their bellies.
