# Spelling Retrieval Practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size

## Description
Spelling retrieval practice asks learners to produce the correct spelling of a word from memory — through dictation, spelling tests, fill-from-memory tasks, or self-testing — instead of copying the word or studying it passively. The act of reconstructing the orthographic form strengthens the memory representation that fluent writing depends on. It is the spelling-specific application of the broader [retrieval practice](../principles/retrieval-practice.md) effect, in which attempting to recall information produces more durable learning than re-exposure.

## Design Implications

Retrieval attempts strengthen orthographic representations more effectively than repeated copying or visual study, because generation forces learners to reconstruct the word's letter sequence rather than merely recognize it [+S]. The benefit is largest when retrieval is effortful but successful: feedback should follow each attempt so errors are corrected before they consolidate [~S]. Because spelling is a production skill that must eventually operate under time pressure during writing, retrieval practice also builds the [automaticity](../elements/automaticity.md) that frees working memory for composing [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A word list targeted at each learner's current instructional level, not a one-size-fits-all list
- A retrieval prompt (dictation, cloze-from-memory, or self-test) that requires production, not recognition
- Immediate corrective [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) showing the correct spelling after each attempt
- Spaced re-testing of missed words until mastery ([Spaced Practice](../principles/spaced-learning.md))
- A word set appropriate to learners' current orthographic knowledge
- A production task (writing from dictation, spelling from a definition or sentence context) that requires full retrieval, not recognition
- Immediate corrective feedback showing the correct spelling after each attempt
- A schedule that revisits words at expanding intervals ([Spaced Repetition](../elements/spaced-repetition.md))

#### Constraints
- Testing before instruction on entirely unfamiliar words can entrench errors; retrieval works best on words learners have some orthographic knowledge of [~S]
- Whole-class lists waste time for learners far above or below list difficulty; effectiveness drops sharply when success rates are very low [~M]
- Copying words multiple times — the most common classroom spelling activity — produces far weaker retention than a single retrieval attempt with feedback [-S]
- High-stakes grading of spelling tests can shift attention to performance rather than learning and discourage risk-taking in writing [~W]
- Copying and re-reading word lists produce familiarity without production ability [-S] — learners and teachers often mistake this fluency for mastery
- Retrieval before any initial instruction produces high error rates and can entrench misspellings in learners with no orthographic foundation [~S]; an initial study phase must precede testing
- Pure whole-word retrieval without attention to morphological or phonological patterns limits transfer to untaught words [~M]
- High-stakes spelling tests with public error can create anxiety that displaces working memory resources needed for retrieval [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Dictation sentences** embed words in meaningful context, adding semantic retrieval cues
- **Self-testing with flashcards or apps** (e.g., Spelling Shed, Quizlet) enables individualized pacing and spacing
- **Cover-copy-compare** has learners study a word, cover it, write it from memory, and compare — a self-managed retrieval loop suitable for independent work
- **Spelling-focused writing** — composing sentences that require the target words — combines retrieval with authentic use
- **Look–Say–Cover–Write–Check**: a self-managed retrieval cycle widely used in UK primary classrooms; the "cover" step forces retrieval, though the "check" step is often skipped, degrading the feedback loop
- **Spaced digital drills**: tools such as [Spellzone](https://www.spellzone.com) or [Spelling Shed](https://www.spellingshed.com) schedule word retrieval at expanding intervals with immediate feedback
- **Sentence-dictation**: retrieval embedded in meaningful writing context, adding the load of simultaneous composition
- **Morphology-focused retrieval**: retrieving spellings of word families (*sign → signal → signature*) to build generalizable orthographic knowledge

### Target Learners
- Beginning readers and spellers who have some alphabetic knowledge and can attempt plausible spellings [+M]
- Struggling spellers, who benefit most from the combination of retrieval, immediate feedback, and spaced review [+M]
- Less effective for learners with no phonemic awareness or letter knowledge, who need explicit phonics instruction first
- Struggling spellers, who benefit most from the explicit feedback loop that retrieval provides, provided errors are corrected immediately
- Less effective as unguided testing for learners with no prior exposure to the word forms; an initial study phase is required [~S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate, automatic spelling of high-frequency and curriculum words
- Orthographic mapping: bonding spellings to pronunciations and meanings in long-term memory
- Transfer to spelling accuracy in independent writing, not just test performance [+M]
- Automatic, accurate production of high-frequency and curriculum word spellings
- Retention of irregular spellings that cannot be derived from phonological rules
- Transfer of orthographic patterns (morphological, etymological) to new words

### Instructions
1. Select 5–10 words at the learner's instructional level, prioritizing high-frequency and pattern-consistent words.
2. Briefly study each word (read it, segment its sounds, note the tricky part) — study is the setup, not the practice.
3. Cover the word and write it from memory, or take it under dictation ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
4. Compare against the correct spelling and give immediate corrective feedback; have the learner rewrite the corrected form.
5. Re-test missed words after a delay, expanding the interval on successive successes ([Spaced Practice](../principles/spaced-learning.md)).
6. Periodically assess in authentic writing, not just isolated tests ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)).

## Description
Spelling retrieval practice asks learners to produce a word's spelling from memory — via dictated spelling tests, spaced quizzes, or write-from-prompt activities — instead of passively studying word lists or copying words. Each retrieval attempt strengthens the orthographic memory trace and provides feedback about which spellings have and have not been mastered. It is the application of [retrieval practice](../elements/practice.md) to the orthographic domain.

## Design Implications

Retrieval — attempting to recall spelling before seeing the correct form — produces stronger long-term retention than restudying the same words, an effect well established across verbal learning tasks [+S]. The act of generating the letter sequence, even when the attempt is imperfect, engages deeper processing than reading or copying, which can create fluency illusions in which words "look familiar" but cannot be produced accurately [-S]. Feedback after each attempt is essential: without correction, errors may be rehearsed and consolidated [~S]. Spacing retrieval sessions across days rather than massing them in one sitting substantially improves retention [~S].

### Instructions
1. **Initial study**: present the word with its meaning and a note on any tricky part (e.g., "hear the *-able*, remember it's *-able* not *-ible*") — [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) the word into pronounceable or morphological units reduces load [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
2. **Cover and retrieve**: hide the word and have learners write it from memory — the retrieval attempt, not the copying, drives learning
3. **Immediate feedback**: show the correct spelling; learners compare and self-correct, rewriting the word once
4. **Space retrieval**: retest the same words after 1 day, 3 days, then a week, dropping words only after consistent correct retrieval ([Spaced Repetition](../elements/spaced-repetition.md))
5. **Embed in writing**: transfer check via dictation sentences or authentic writing conferences, since isolated test accuracy does not guarantee use in composition [~M]
