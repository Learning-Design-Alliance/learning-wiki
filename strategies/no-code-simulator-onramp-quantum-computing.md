# Use no-code interactive circuit simulators as an on-ramp to quantum computing frameworks and hardware

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends browser-based no-code quantum circuit interfaces, like TQF and similar simulators, as gateways for on-boarding users without formal quantum backgrounds. A key feature is letting users process a circuit step-by-step and examine intermediate states, facilitating understanding and rapid iteration on designs. The article states such interfaces "could play an important role in on-boarding users without a formal quantum background into quantum computing" and could lead users toward sophisticated quantum software frameworks and simulations on actual quantum hardware such as Amazon Braket.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An interface that lets users process circuits step-by-step and examine intermediate states
- An approachable, user-friendly environment for experimenting with circuits and algorithms
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as potential usefulness, especially for technically skilled engineers/scientists entering quantum computing from other fields; it reports no evaluation of the gateway pathway

### Target Learners
- Users without a formal quantum background
- Technically skilled engineers/scientists from other fields entering quantum computing

### Target Learning Goals
- Exploring circuit configurations that would otherwise require non-trivial mathematical analysis
- Transitioning to professional quantum software frameworks and hardware
