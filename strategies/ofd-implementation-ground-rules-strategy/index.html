Implement synchronous online forum discussions in EFL writing with clear ground rules, communicated evaluation criteria, and relevant challenging topics¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends incorporating online forum discussions as a best practice in ELT if implemented effectively. Teachers should "make clear the ground rules for the discussion including strict use of L2, refraining from silly jokes and insults", communicate evaluation criteria such as number of posts, and assign topics of interest and relevance that challenge learners' thinking in a stress-free atmosphere.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Clear ground rules including strict use of L2 and refraining from insults
  • Evaluation criteria clearly communicated to students
  • Topics of interest and relevance assigned in a stress-free atmosphere

Constraints¶

  • The study's null quantitative result means the recommendation rests on qualitative perceptions, not measured writing gains

Target Learners¶

  • tertiary EFL diploma students in the Middle East

Target Learning Goals¶

  • EFL argumentative writing
  • L2 interaction and collaboration

Claims¶

  • Synchronous online forum discussion produces no statistically significant difference in lexical density compared with asynchronous blog writing [~W]
  • Learners perceive synchronous online forum discussions positively across seven themes: feeling, motivation, interaction, writing skills, autonomy, challenges, and cultural issues [+W]
  • Synchronous online forum discussion produces no statistically significant difference in syntactic complexity compared with asynchronous blog writing, though the OFD mean was slightly higher [~W]
  • Synchronous online forum discussion produces no statistically significant difference in lexical diversity compared with asynchronous blog writing in EFL post-test writing [~W]
