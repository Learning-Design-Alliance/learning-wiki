# Just-in-time training: stop-and-go simulation debriefing and over-the-shoulder clinical coaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For time-constrained settings, the article recommends just-in-time methods. In the "stop and go" method, "instructors can pause the scenario and correct individual or team errors in real-time, preventing consolidation of incorrect skills". In clinical environments, "over-the-shoulder" guidance provides real-time coaching during task performance with immediate correction and reinforcement, and a brief pre-procedure topic revision (e.g., a video before bronchoscopy) improves procedural fluency and decreases overload.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must retain flexibility to adjust debrief length and be present during task performance to correct errors in real time
#### Constraints
- Lengthy simulation sessions are not always feasible in ICU teaching due to time constraints and limited availability of simulation equipment or facilitators

### Target Learners
- ICU physicians, nurses, and other healthcare professionals performing procedures and team-based care

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural skill performance, teamwork, and prevention of incorrect skill consolidation
