# Use multi-format follow-up sub-items (free-text and law-selection MRQ) alongside FCI-style MCQs to probe student reasoning more deeply

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends combining follow-up questions in different formats with FCI-style items in one multi-modal instrument. Type 1 sub-items ask variations on the main item, while Type 2 sub-items ask students to identify which Newtonian law(s) they used, letting educators check whether correct answers rest on correct reasoning. The article states these investigations "highlight that free-text responses oﬀer extra dimensions, such as word count or word con tent, to measure student understanding beyond the dichotomous correct/incorrect classiﬁcation".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Free-text items require expert human marking with a shared marking guide and majority ruling; Type 2 sub-items are formative and ungraded
#### Constraints
- Post-test analyses in the article drew only on one institution's data; wider administration would be needed to strengthen the findings

### Target Learners
- first- and second-year undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnosing student reasoning and misconceptions in Newtonian mechanics
