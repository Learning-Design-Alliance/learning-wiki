# Build multiple-choice distractors by categorizing students' open-ended written responses into groups of similar ideas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy converts open-ended assessment items into MC format by mining students' own written answers: responses are grouped by similar ideas, and those idea-groups become the distractors, with literature on documented student difficulties checked for congruence. The article reports that "Student responses from Cal Poly Pomona (CPP; N=19) and CU Boulder (CUB; N=53) were used in the construction of distractors." In the first version students could select more than one correct response to widen the distractor pool; later versions pruned unpopular distractors and merged correct responses into a single choice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A prior open-ended instrument with archived student written responses
- Access to literature on known student difficulties for congruence checking
#### Constraints
- In the first version, allowing multiple correct responses complicated grading and raised difficulty; standard single-key format required pruning distractors

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnosing common student difficulties in quantum mechanics concepts
