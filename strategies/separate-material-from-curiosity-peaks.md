# Separate challenging material in time from peak-curiosity moments, or build a sustained curiosity state before presenting less-interesting material

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests that piquing strong curiosity can interfere with encoding of other material presented too close in time, so curiosity-based classroom techniques should avoid placing difficult-to-learn material immediately adjacent to high-curiosity moments. The authors propose that "in real-world applications, curiosity would likely be a more sustained (tonic) state" and that "Waiting some amount of time to pass after a tonic curiosity state might then prevent overshadowing of the target information, whilst retaining the neuromodulatory benefits of the state".
