# Temperature Monitoring for Learning Environments

## Description
This strategy involves monitoring the temperature of a learning environment and making adjustments to maintain optimal conditions for learners. Research suggests that environments that are too cold can lead to distractibility and decreased attention, while environments that are too warm can result in decreased concentration due to discomfort and drowsiness. This strategy can be implemented through product design or direct intervention, such as adjusting thermostats, opening windows, using fans, or dimming lights.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires monitoring devices (thermometers, sensors) and adjustable environmental controls (thermostats, fans, air conditioning). Regular monitoring and adjustment are needed to respond to changing conditions and learner feedback. Implementation might involve policy changes regarding temperature control in learning spaces.
#### Constraints
- Individual preferences for temperature can vary, making it challenging to satisfy everyone. Energy costs associated with maintaining optimal temperatures may be a limiting factor. Overemphasis on temperature control may distract from other important aspects of the learning environment.

### Target Learners

- All learner levels, including K–12, higher education, and adult learners in various learning environments, including classrooms, offices, and training facilities.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Maintain an optimal temperature range to maximize learner attention, engagement, and productivity; mitigate the negative impacts of uncomfortable temperatures on learning outcomes.

### Affordances
- Optimizing temperature supports cognitive function by minimizing distractions and discomfort, thereby enhancing focus and concentration. Creating a comfortable learning environment fosters positive attitudes and improves motivation. This approach addresses the physical well-being of learners, leading to increased engagement and productivity.

### Personalization
- Different individuals may have different temperature preferences. The strategy can be personalized by allowing learners some control over their immediate environment, such as adjusting individual thermostats or using personal fans or heaters. Consider factors such as clothing, activity level, and individual differences in thermal sensitivity.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track learner performance metrics (e.g., test scores, task completion rates) in relation to temperature levels. Monitor learner feedback regarding thermal comfort and its impact on concentration. Observe changes in learner behavior (e.g., fidgeting, drowsiness) as indicators of temperature-related discomfort.

## Impact
- Research indicates that maintaining an optimal temperature range (68º-74ºF) can significantly improve engagement levels and overall productivity, including student achievement. Air conditioning in schools can mitigate the adverse effects of high temperatures on test scores. Studies have found that temperature effects account for a notable percentage of the U.S. racial achievement gap due to differences in environmental conditions and school resources.
