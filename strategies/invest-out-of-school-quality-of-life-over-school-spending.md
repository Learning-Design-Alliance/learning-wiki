# Direct public investment toward unemployment reduction and quality-of-life factors rather than increased school spending to improve graduation outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (4 for, 4 against) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, because unemployment and community risk factors correlate with graduation rates more strongly than per-pupil spending, policymakers should fund programs addressing out-of-school conditions. It states that "investment in programs that address unemployment and other quality of life factors may have a greater impact on improving educational outcomes for students than increased spending on schools", and lists further variables to analyze such as income-to-poverty ratio, living conditions, and access to internet.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements

#### Constraints
- Based on observational correlations in Maryland, Virginia, and DC from 2015–2018; the authors note the models should not be used for making predictions due to the spread of the data

### Target Learners
- public high school students in Maryland, Virginia, and the District of Columbia

### Target Learning Goals
- improving 4-year high school graduation rates through public funding allocation
