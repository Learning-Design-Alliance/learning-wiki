# Use combination immersive and procedural simulation for interconnected topics when resident education time is limited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend pairing an immersive case on a procedure's dangerous complication with hands-on procedural training on the procedure itself, so that one session covers recognition, management, and technical skill. They argue "Combination cases may provide a chance for a deeper grasp of integrated topics as well as a unique opportunity for residents to practice in an immersive simulation environment," and that limited resident education time makes covering multiple aspects of care in one day valuable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interconnected topics where one simulation's content (e.g., a complication) reinforces the other (e.g., the procedure), plus faculty expertise in both domains
#### Constraints
- The article states further studies would be required to elucidate the effects of combination simulation as a learning opportunity

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine residents and clinicians

### Target Learning Goals
- Integrate procedural skill with recognition and management of the procedure's complications
