Teacher educators adjust technology-integration instruction using moderator-aligned effect sizes from prior meta-analyses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that mathematics teacher preparation guided by TPACK draw directly on meta-analytic evidence: "teacher educators could review the effect sizes in these categories and adjust instructional practices based on the strength and directionality of the moderator variables aligned to each TCK or TPK construct." Concretely, educators would attend to the five identified moderators — duration, assessment, instructional modality, grade level, and mathematics subject matter — when preparing teachers to integrate technology in mathematics classrooms.

Requirements¶

  • Access to the moderator-aligned effect size findings of prior meta-analyses for each technology category

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on a review of meta-analyses whose moderator data largely reside at the individual study level, limiting precise estimation of moderator influences

Claims¶

  • CBI/CAI effects on mathematics achievement vary from small to medium, with duration and instructional modality as statistically significant moderators [+W]
  • Calculator effects on mathematics achievement tend to converge at moderate effectiveness, with grade level and assessment as statistically significant moderators [+W]
  • Mean effect sizes of technology-enhanced mathematics learning vary by didactical functionality from small to medium, with the largest variation for developing conceptual understanding [+W]
  • Five TPACK-related moderators are most salient across meta-analyses: grade level, assessment, duration, instructional modality, and mathematics subject matter [+W]
  • Mean effect sizes across the 13 reviewed meta-analyses ranged from -.11 to .57 [+W]
  • Relational-understanding technologies show the most divergent effects (.09 to 1.02), moderated by grade level, duration, and mathematics subject matter [+W]
