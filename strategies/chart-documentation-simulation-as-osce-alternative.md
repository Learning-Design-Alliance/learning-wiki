# Use simulation-based medical chart documentation as an alternative OSCE component, including during pandemics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adding simulation-based chart documentation components to the OSCE. Because the format "does not require human-to-human contact, it may be effective for summative evaluation during pandemic situations such as COVID-19." The authors conclude that "medical chart documentation can be one possible alternative component in the OSCE." This was implemented as three original components in the 2020 Post-CC OSCE when mandatory CATO components could not be performed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation videos and scenario images must be created in advance
- Original checklists with defined essential documentation elements
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a single institution and may not generalize to other medical schools, though the authors believe they apply to most Japanese medical schools

### Target Learners
- Medical students completing clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- Medical chart documentation quality as a summative clinical competency assessment
