# Onboard new project-team members through small, low-cost practice projects done in groups of 2-3 that build skills without carrying mission-critical deliverables

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
PULSE-A guides new members through small projects that build the skills they will need on the team. The paper states that "new members are guided through small projects that do not usually directly contribute to the mission but allow them to practice skills they will use on the team." Projects are deliberately straightforward and low-cost so participants can focus on skill-building rather than logistics, and are done in groups of 2-3 so members collaborate while each remains individually accountable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Projects must be relatively straightforward and low-cost so participants focus on key skills rather than logistical hurdles
- Groups of 2-3 students, sized to allow collaboration while each person is pushed to take accountability for their work
- New members first become acquainted with the mission's documentation before starting these projects
#### Constraints
- The projects do not usually directly contribute to the mission's deliverables

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students newly joining a student engineering project team

### Target Learning Goals
- Practice discipline-specific engineering skills needed on the team
- Develop individual accountability alongside collaboration
