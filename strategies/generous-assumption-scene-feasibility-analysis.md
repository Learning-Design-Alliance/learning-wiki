# Analyze a film scene quantitatively with generous assumptions in favor of the film to evaluate whether its depicted plan is physically feasible

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the Armageddon example, the class estimates the asteroid's mass from its stated size, assumes a conservative density, and computes the deflection speed of the two fragments given a deliberately generous bomb yield, finding each fragment would move only about 7 cm per second sideways. The calculation ends with an evaluation of the feasibility of the film's plan, showing the fragments would be only a few city blocks apart when they collided with Earth. The same scene segment is discussed twice, once with momentum concepts and once with energy concepts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicitly stated simplifying assumptions (e.g., the asteroid splits into exactly two equal fragments, all bomb energy becomes sideways kinetic energy)
- access to the specific scene segment and basic physical data such as densities and TNT energy equivalents
#### Constraints
- The analyses are often more sophisticated than expected of Physical Science students, though the authors report no complaints because the analysis is strongly correlated to the film

### Target Learners
- non-science majors in introductory physical science

### Target Learning Goals
- applying momentum conservation and energy concepts
- critically evaluating scientifically implausible proposals
