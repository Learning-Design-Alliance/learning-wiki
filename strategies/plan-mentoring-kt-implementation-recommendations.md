# When planning mentoring to support evidence uptake, plan regular meetings over time, choose an appropriate delivery approach, establish a mentor-mentee matching process, and consider combining mentoring with other KT interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
In its Linking Evidence to Action section, the review recommends that clinical educators, advanced practice nurses, and others positioned to be mentors should "Plan regular meetings with mentees over a period of time", "Deliver mentoring using the most appropriate approach (e.g., individual and/or group meetings, telephone, e-mail)", and "Establish a selection process to match mentees and mentors". It further advises that "Mentoring as a KT intervention may be combined with other KT interventions (e.g., educational meetings and materials, audit, and feedback)", reflecting that nine of ten included studies used mentoring within multifaceted interventions. The review also calls for research to determine the impact of mentoring on professional and patient outcomes and the influence of the mentor-mentee relationship.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentors positioned to support evidence uptake (clinical educators, advanced practice nurses)
- A selection process to match mentees and mentors
#### Constraints
- The review states it is difficult to determine the effect of mentoring specifically on the uptake of evidence into practice, since only one study evaluated mentoring alone

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals responsible for patient care in clinical settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Uptake of empirical evidence into clinical practice

### Affordances
- Mentoring As Kt Intervention Definition
