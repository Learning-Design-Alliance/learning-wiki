# Match teaching style to student preparation: studio-type and inquiry-based settings for algebra-based physics, modeling methods for teacher training, traditional problem-solving for calculus-based courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article holds that different courses and teaching styles attract students of relevant aptitude: "studio type courses, inquiry based method, traditional lectures and labs and problem solving strategies are all different adoptable methods. None of them is either perfect or obsolete." Studio-type settings teach concepts alongside experimental testing and suit algebra-based students not yet prepared to learn physics through equations, developing critical thinking and revealing potential majors among undeclared science students. Modeling methods are called excellent for physics teachers' training and are used in professional development workshops, while traditional chalk-board problem-solving prepares calculus-based students for intermediate courses.
