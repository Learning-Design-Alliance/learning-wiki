# Four-part policy package for institutionalizing e-learning in Ghanaian health training institutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes policy recommendations for institutions of higher learning in Ghana and countries with similar conditions: initiating "policy dialogues on developing e-learning statutory policies", negotiating public-private-partnership internet packages with telecommunication companies, institutionalizing capacity-building workshops and trainings for students and faculty, and creating ring-fenced e-learning budgets. These follow directly from the survey's preparedness and cost/connectivity findings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions should ring-fence budgets for e-learning to improve ICT infrastructure and human resource capacity for sustainability
#### Constraints
- Findings are largely descriptive and locally situated to one Ghanaian institution and region

### Target Learners
- Nursing and midwifery trainees in Ghanaian public universities

### Target Learning Goals
- Quality pre-service training of health professionals via e-learning and m-learning innovations
