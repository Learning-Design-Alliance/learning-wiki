# Using Manipulatives

## Description
Manipulatives are physical objects used as teaching tools to engage students in hands-on learning, especially in mathematics. They introduce, practice, or remediate concepts across all math areas (number and operations, algebra, geometry, measurement, data analysis, probability) and grade levels. Examples include counters, base-10 blocks, fraction strips, pattern blocks, geoboards, and rulers. Effective use of manipulatives involves making their mathematical structure transparent, ensuring learners are familiar with the resource, and allowing learners to use them to support their own arguments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a variety of physical manipulatives relevant to the mathematical concepts being taught, instructor guidance on how to use the manipulatives effectively, and time for exploration and practice.
#### Constraints
- If used without proper understanding, manipulatives can become a crutch rather than a tool. Learners may focus on the manipulative itself rather than the underlying mathematical concepts. Requires careful planning and instruction to ensure effectiveness.

### Target Learning Goals

### Affordances
- Provides a concrete way to understand abstract mathematical concepts, supports active learning and engagement, allows learners to build understanding and internalize math processes through manipulation of objects, and facilitates the transition from concrete to pictorial to abstract understanding.

### Personalization
- Manipulatives can be adapted to suit different learning styles and content areas. Learners can choose from a variety of manipulatives based on their individual preferences and the specific mathematical concept being taught. Tasks can be adapted for individual students to meet their specific needs.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learners' ability to explain mathematical concepts using manipulatives, assess their ability to solve problems using manipulatives, and evaluate their transition from using manipulatives to abstract representations.

## Impact
- Research indicates that manipulatives have a positive effect on learning, particularly in retention of mathematical concepts. Studies show manipulatives contribute to well-grounded, interconnected understandings of mathematical ideas.
