# Sampled oral code interviews over accepted solutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because accepted code does not establish understanding or authorship, the course randomly samples roughly ten percent of students weekly for short interviews over one accepted solution, conducted without viewing the submitted code. The article states "CP1 therefore samples approximately ten students each week (10%) for a 5-15 minute interview over one solution accepted during the preceding week." Four core prompts cover the problem's request, a brute-force approach, the accepted approach with its decisive insight, and complexity bounds, with an optional prompt about initial errors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Every student must be interviewed at least once; neutral follow-up probes and a published rubric with calibrated interviewers are recommended to keep the protocol consistent across staff.
#### Constraints
- A weak interview alone does not establish misconduct; insufficient evidence causes the sampled solution to lose credit and triggers review under ordinary academic-integrity process. The instructor's judgment of effectiveness is not presented as measured evidence.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in an introductory competitive programming course of roughly 100 students

### Target Learning Goals
- reconstructable reasoning behind accepted solutions
- authorship verification
- complexity justification
