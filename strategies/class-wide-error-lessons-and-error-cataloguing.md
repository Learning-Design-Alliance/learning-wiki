# Design class-wide grammar lessons from common journal errors, and let students catalogue their own errors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The author suggests two further corrective approaches. Teachers can provide feedback to the entire class based on common student errors: for repetitive errors in count and non-count nouns, a grammar lesson is designed on the topic, either inductively (analyzing modified journal examples as a class to infer the rule) or deductively (highlighting the rule, then practicing and revising). Keen students may catalogue error types with their corresponding rules on dedicated pages at the back of the journal, becoming more aware of the errors they routinely make and monitoring them in future entries.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Journal examples must be modified to maintain confidentiality when analyzed as a class
#### Constraints
- The teacher must balance attention to form with the overall intention of meaningful dialogue and communication

### Target Learners
- adult ESL students

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing and self-monitoring recurring error types such as verb tense, word usage, and article usage
