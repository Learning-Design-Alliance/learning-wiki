Strategies

Design and implement VS teaching with simplified interfaces, multimodal feedback, multilingual diverse case libraries, pre-training, and controlled VR session lengths¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's forward-looking recommendations for VS teaching design: future devices should "prioritize simplifying interactive designs to lower operational barriers and explore multimodal feedback mechanisms to improve the realism of virtual patients," alongside "developing case libraries with diverse scenarios and multilingual support." Supporting practices include giving students without prior VS experience appropriate pre-training, fragmenting instructional tasks with dynamically adjusted difficulty based on Cognitive Load Theory, controlling VR session lengths to reduce physical side effects, and having instructors provide supplementary immediate verbal feedback to offset computer-based platforms' feedback lag.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • VS software and hardware development capacity; instructor time for supplementary verbal feedback; pre-training sessions for VS-naive students

Constraints¶

  • High cost of VS equipment poses barriers to adoption, though the review notes hardware expenses are gradually declining; recommendations arise from a qualitative synthesis without effect-size evidence

Target Learners¶

  • nursing students in higher education
  • students with limited English proficiency
  • students without prior VS experience

Target Learning Goals¶

  • clinical practice skills
  • operational adaptation to VS technology
  • reduced cognitive load and simulator sickness

Claims¶

  • The most frequently reported challenge of virtual simulation teaching is its lack of realism, alongside operational complexity, English-language demands, cultural mismatch, and adverse physical reactions [+W]
  • Computer-based simulation and immersive VR platforms differ in their primary limitations: feedback timeliness versus physiological discomfort [+W]
  • Nursing students express needs for optimized VS technology and cases: more diverse scenarios, greater realism, multilingual support, flexible training content, and culturally contextualized cases [+W]
