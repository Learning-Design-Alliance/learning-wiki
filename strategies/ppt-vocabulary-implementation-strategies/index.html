Practical strategies for implementing PPT vocabulary lessons¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's implications section proposes concrete teacher actions for PPT vocabulary teaching: prepare the lesson carefully with clear aims and sufficient materials; balance time between vocabulary presentation and other activities, since "vocabulary lesson should not take up more than 15 minutes for each period"; consider students' interests, learning styles and attention spans before choosing techniques; manage student noise tactfully (closing doors and windows, reminders, individual work); and adapt textbook skill-practice tasks to save time for vocabulary presentation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher preparation time and effort: searching for visual materials and converting notes into slides is described as time consuming

Constraints¶

  • Students' making noise might be a problem of PPT teaching; teachers are advised to handle it tactfully

Target Learners¶

  • High school EFL teachers in Vietnamese secondary schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Effective presentation of new English vocabulary within limited class time

Claims¶

  • Students held positive attitudes toward the use of PowerPoint in vocabulary lessons [+W]
  • Grade-10 EFL students taught vocabulary with PowerPoint-enhanced lessons retained more vocabulary than students in traditional lessons [+W]
  • Both groups improved vocabulary retention from pre-test to post-test, with a larger gain in the PowerPoint group [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Extend study duration and add a delayed post-test to measure vocabulary retention
  • Implement GO-based vocabulary instruction through four steps: preparation, presentation, practice and evaluation
