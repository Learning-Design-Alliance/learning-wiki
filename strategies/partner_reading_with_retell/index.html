Partner Reading with Retell¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 11 studies (5 quant-synthesis, 2 causal, 2 review, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 5 of 11 report an effect size

Description¶

Partner Reading with Retell is the core fluency activity of Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS) for reading. Students work in pairs, taking turns reading aloud from a shared text; after each segment, the reader retells or summarizes what was read while the partner listens, corrects errors, and asks prompting questions. The stronger reader reads first, providing a fluent model, before roles reverse.

Design Implications¶

The strategy combines two well-supported mechanisms: repeated and modeled oral reading builds automatic word recognition, which frees working-memory resources for comprehension Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension. [+S], and requiring a retell forces generative processing of the text rather than passive decoding Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding. [+M]. Peer feedback is most effective when it targets the task and the process (specific errors, missed main ideas) rather than the person Feedback most effective at task and process levels. [+S].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Texts at an instructional level for the weaker reader, readable in 5–10 minute segments
  • Explicit training in partner behaviors: error correction routines, retell prompts ("Who was this about? What happened first?"), and constructive feedback language
  • A predictable structure: timer, role cards, and a quiet space
  • Teacher monitoring and periodic re-pairing based on running records or fluency data

Constraints¶

  • Untrained pairs drift into simple turn-taking without error correction or retell, collapsing the activity into shared reading with minimal benefit [-M]
  • Pairing two very weak readers removes the fluent model; pairing too wide a gap can make the stronger reader dominate or disengage [~M]
  • Texts too difficult for the weaker reader produce word-by-word reading that consumes all capacity and leaves nothing for summarization [-S]
  • Retell quality depends on prior instruction in story structure or main-idea identification; without it, retells degenerate into listing details [~M]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Kindergarten–Grade 1 PALS uses a modified version with letter-sound and sight-word games instead of full retells
  • Retells can be oral, or replaced with "paragraph shrinking" (stating the main idea in 10 words or fewer) in the upper-elementary version
  • English learners benefit from pre-teaching retell sentence frames ("First…, then…, in the end…")
  • Can be run as a classwide routine 2–3 times per week for 25–35 minutes, or as a small-group intervention

Target Learners¶

  • Students in Grades 2–6 who read at an instructional level but below grade-level fluency benchmarks
  • Struggling readers who benefit from a fluent peer model before practicing themselves [~M]
  • Less appropriate for students still decoding at a frustration level, who need explicit phonics first Phonics instruction outperforms whole-word for generalization. [+S]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Oral reading fluency: accuracy, rate, and prosody through modeled and repeated reading
  • Summarization and main-idea identification through structured retell
  • Comprehension monitoring, since the partner's questions surface breakdowns
  • Peer feedback and collaborative learning routines

Instructions¶

  1. Pair students and assign the stronger reader to read first, providing a fluent model (Modeling).
  2. Set a timer (typically 5 minutes per reader) and have Reader 1 read aloud while Reader 2 follows the text and corrects errors using a trained routine (Practice).
  3. Reader 2 retells what was read, prompted by question cards; Reader 1 gives process-level feedback (Provide Feedback).
  4. Reverse roles with the next text segment, so the weaker reader practices with a fresh passage (Peer Teaching).
  5. Close with a brief whole-class or pair reflection on one retell, connecting the summary to story structure.

Related Strategies¶

  • Reciprocal Teaching — shares the summarizing-and-dialogue structure but uses adult-led group roles rather than pairs
  • Repeated Reading — isolates the fluency mechanism without the comprehension retell
  • Think-Pair-Share — a general discussion structure that Partner Reading with Retell embeds within a reading task

Examples¶

  • PALS Reading (Vanderbilt University) — the source program, classwide and validated across Grades K–6: https://iris.peabody.vanderbilt.edu/modules/pals/
  • Peer-Assisted Literacy Strategies in Title I schools — Fuchs et al.'s field trials showed effects on fluency and comprehension across achievement strata
  • Reading Partners (US nonprofit tutoring program) — community volunteers use a structured partner-read-and-summarize protocol with struggling readers: https://readingpartners.org
