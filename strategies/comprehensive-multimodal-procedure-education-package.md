# Combine preprocedural online didactics, video demonstration, case-based learning, and simulation to address both cognitive and psychomotor procedural learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues single-modal resources are insufficient for procedure education: the authors created "a comprehensive curricular package encompassing not only a simulation experience using deliberate practice to address psychomotor procedural skills but also didactics, cases and assessments to address cognitive skills, and an entrustment-based assessment tool to judge competence". Learners complete online modules and quizzes before arriving, watch a gold-standard procedural video, practice and are assessed in simulation, and later review diagnostic-interpretation and spontaneous bacterial peritonitis cases in small groups. The authors attribute the curriculum's strengths to this comprehensiveness and to offering education through a variety of media.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online curriculum site to distribute materials and notify learners 2 weeks before their session
- Quiz passing threshold of >70% before simulation scheduling
#### Constraints
- About 20% of learners did not complete the mandatory online modules and could not be rescheduled due to faculty and lab time constraints, which the authors state may have affected simulation performance

### Target Learners
- Internal medicine and medicine-pediatric residents
- Potentially emergency medicine residents and gastroenterology fellows

### Target Learning Goals
- Diagnosis of ascites etiology from laboratory values
- Diagnosis and treatment of spontaneous bacterial peritonitis
- Ultrasound-guided paracentesis technique
