# Teach the sonification mapping process explicitly, as audiences unfamiliar with sonification as data representation misunderstand it

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Open-ended survey responses revealed frequent misunderstanding of sonification, both conceptually (e.g., expecting actual sound from space) and in interpretation (e.g., not understanding the relationship between sounds and images). The authors argue that because "We teach students to read graphs and charts visually, so education in soniﬁcation might likewise be necessary", creators should provide background on how data are mapped to sound, and improve accompanying descriptions of the techniques used.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Descriptions of the goals and the creation process should be central and involve careful and considerate communication
#### Constraints
- Misunderstanding how data are represented may contribute to feelings of mistrust, remedied through more exposure

### Target Learners
- general public audiences
- blind or low-vision (BLV) audiences

### Target Learning Goals
- interpreting sonified data representations
- trust in scientific data representations
