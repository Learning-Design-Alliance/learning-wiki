# Offer a sophomore-year mathematical physics course preparing students for the mathematics of quantum physics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that physics departments add a dedicated mathematical physics course before students take quantum physics, because students struggle to connect algebra and calculus training with the complex algebra and partial differential equations quantum physics requires. The author states that "at least one mathematical course intended for quantum physics needs to be offered for students in their sophomore year so they would have one year to digest them", noting only two of five universities offered such a course and that junior-year offerings come too late.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Physics department curriculum must have room for an additional mathematical physics course offered before the quantum physics sequence
#### Constraints
- The article notes junior-level offerings are too late because students take quantum physics the same year

### Target Learners
- sophomore-level undergraduate physics majors preparing for quantum physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- command of the mathematical tools (complex algebra, partial differential equations) needed for quantum physics
