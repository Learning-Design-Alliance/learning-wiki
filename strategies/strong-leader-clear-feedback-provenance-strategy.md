# Assure strong local leadership and clear feedback provenance when implementing peer review audit-and-feedback collaboratives

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors state that problems with fidelity of the feedback and uptake of the intervention "could best be handled by assuring that a strong leader picks up the group and lead them forward". This responds to process-evaluation findings that the source of the feedback was often unclear to participants and that many groups failed to set achievable, measurable working agreements. Moderators were trained to discuss feedback validity and establish working agreements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Moderators trained in discussing test ordering and prescribing behaviour, setting working agreements, and handling questions on feedback data validity
#### Constraints
- Providing key messages on individual feedback would have impaired or influenced the peer review process, an essential part of LQIC work

### Target Learners
- General practitioners and local moderators (pharmacists, laboratory specialists) in local quality improvement collaboratives

### Target Learning Goals
- Improve guideline adherence in test ordering and prescribing through peer review of comparative feedback
