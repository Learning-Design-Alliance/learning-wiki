# Structure a simulation-based mastery learning program as sequential steps: video tutorial, MCQ with feedback, supervised practice, then repeated self-directed practice with video-based assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's training protocol provides a replicable sequence for simulation-based procedural training. Residents begin with a video tutorial covering foundational knowledge, complete a nine-item MCQ examination with investigator-reviewed feedback, perform the procedure on a simulation model under direct supervision with immediate feedback, then engage in repeated self-directed practice over time until they perceive acceptable proficiency. The final performance is video-recorded and scored by two independent pediatric ophthalmologists using a standardized rubric, with confidence rated at each stage on a 0–4 Likert scale.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supervision by an expert pediatric ophthalmologist during the guided practice session
- Video recordings capturing only residents' hands to maintain anonymity during scoring
#### Constraints
- The number of self-directed practice sessions was not standardized or systematically recorded, which may have influenced individual learning trajectories

### Target Learners
- ophthalmology residents

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural skills training
- standardized competency assessment
