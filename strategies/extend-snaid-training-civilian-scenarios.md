# Extend Snaid® extrication device training to civilian emergency settings and diverse rescue scenarios

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests the training intervention using the Snaid® extrication device could be applied beyond the military sphere, particularly in civilian emergency settings such as search and rescue teams, out-of-hospital emergency services, or specialized units. Future research should evaluate the device in water rescues, complex urban environments, and mass casualty situations, and analyze its impact on clinical, operational, and safety variables.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Transfer of interventions to military units in other countries or local and regional teams to promote standardization of procedures and improve interoperability
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted with participants from a Tank Infantry Battalion, which may limit generalizability to non-military personnel
- The long-term impact of the training was not assessed, leaving open the question of the sustainability of the observed benefits

### Target Learners
- Search and rescue teams
- Out-of-hospital emergency services personnel
- Military units in other countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective use of the Snaid® extrication device across diverse operational rescue contexts
