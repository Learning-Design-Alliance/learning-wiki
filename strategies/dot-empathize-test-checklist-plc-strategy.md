# Use the DOT Framework's Empathize and Test/Feedback stages as practical checklists, and build professional learning communities around shared prompting and critique practices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that instructors treat the design thinking stages, particularly Empathize and Test/Feedback, as "practical checklists for AI integration, moving beyond ad hoc experimentation toward structured, needs-driven, iteratively evaluated AI use." Because practitioners already use progressive prompt refinement (82%) and cross-checking (79%), the authors recommend professional learning communities centered on sharing and critiquing these practices, with deliberate attention to the missing beginning-of-cycle needs assessment and end-of-cycle feedback collection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Peer-learning formats where faculty share AI outputs, prompts, and design processes together, to build shared norms that self-directed learning cannot provide
#### Constraints
- The data show these stages are currently the least-developed dimensions of practice, and the recommendation's effect on instructional outcomes is untested

### Target Learners
- higher education faculty
- instructional designers

### Target Learning Goals
- structured needs assessment before AI use
- structured feedback collection and iteration on AI-generated outputs

### Affordances
- Dot Framework Ai Enhanced Teaching
