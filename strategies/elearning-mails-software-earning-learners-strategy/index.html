Use an e-learning strategy of mails and software to build English competency in working in-service teachers¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's e-learning strategy is defined operationally as "using mails and software" and was executed through a five-step procedure: pre-test both groups to identify problems in the conventional method, prepare the e-learning strategy, treat, and administer the post-test. Because e-learning's flexibility lets working teachers study at convenient times without dramatically altering their schedules, the author recommends it for earning learners and suggests extending it to primary and higher-secondary teachers, other subjects, adult education, other distance teacher-education programmes, and Diploma in Teacher Education.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Identification of learning obstacles by administering a pre-test to both groups before treatment
  • Preparation of e-learning activities suited to self-paced, application-oriented use

Constraints¶

  • Studied only with low-scoring (below 30%) in-service B.Ed teachers at one distance-education centre over one week

Target Learners¶

  • In-service school teachers studying B.Ed in distance mode

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English competency and lifelong learning in methods of teaching English

Claims¶

  • Control-group teachers showed no significant pre-test to post-test achievement change under conventional methods [+W]
  • E-learning strategy produced higher post-test achievement in English than conventional methods among low-scoring in-service B.Ed distance teachers [+W]
  • Post-test performance differed sharply between groups, with the experimental group scoring 80% and the control group 20% [+W]
