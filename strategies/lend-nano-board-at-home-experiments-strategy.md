# Lend students a sensor-equipped nano board to expand at-home physics experiments while protecting their smartphones

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends lending nano boards to students for at-home experiments, which became possible "now that smartphones are ubiquitous", whether as a pedagogical exercise or imposed by a pandemic situation. Lending a board increases the number of possible experiments by adding sensors such as a thermometer and by substituting for the student's phone in risky activities. Example activities include monitoring temperature and pressure of a sealed jar cooled in a freezer, and measuring the thermal relaxation of hot water.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need a smartphone with the phyphox app and the board's experiment programs, distributed via QRcodes; the board must be pre-loaded and battery-powered.
#### Constraints
- For the freezer experiment, qualitative analysis works well, while precise quantitative analysis requires care because the thermometer thermal inertia is larger than the barometer response time.

### Target Learners
- high-school students
- university students working from home

### Target Learning Goals
- thermodynamics
- ideal gas law
- thermal relaxation and heat loss
