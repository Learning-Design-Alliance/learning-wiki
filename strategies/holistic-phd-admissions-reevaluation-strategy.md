# Reevaluate graduate admissions toward holistic assessment of research potential instead of decontextualized test-score cutoffs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 4 studies (2 associational, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that physics communities reevaluate admissions criteria so selection is both equitable and effective. It argues that "Discriminating on GRE implied preparation, rather than a holistic assessment of potential to earn the PhD and conduct research, overlooks applicants who could become strong research physicists", and urges assessing research mentoring experience and socio-emotional, non-cognitive competencies alongside academic preparation, plus matching prospective students to programs where they will thrive.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identifying a broader set of applicant characteristics that predict graduate student outcomes, and understanding characteristics of mentoring and PhD programs that create healthy learning environments
#### Constraints
- The article notes disciplinary preparation may be necessary but insufficient to identify completers, and that further research on student and program-level factors is needed

### Target Learners
- Prospective and admitted physics PhD students, especially those from underrepresented groups

### Target Learning Goals
- Equitable and effective selection of students who can complete the PhD and conduct research
