# Disaggregate student outcome data by demographic variables when evaluating educational interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that "The disaggregation of student data is an important practice in evaluating educational interventions, including active learning," because aggregate course-level effects can hide who benefits. It recommends examining outcomes at the student level disaggregated by demographic variables, and calls for future work with "stronger controls, deeper disaggregation, and more robust replication" across different undergraduate TBL contexts, plus investigation of outcomes such as motivation, engagement, and self-efficacy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to student demographic data (registrar records or surveys) with IRB approval
- outcome measures that can be analyzed at the student level
#### Constraints
- The article notes privacy concerns prevented identifying transgender and nonbinary students in the data, and institutional routine collection limited which identities could be modeled

### Target Learners
- undergraduate STEM students

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable evaluation of active learning interventions
