# Apply three design principles for self-guided embodied training: feedback as calibration reference, modality granularity matched to behaviour's temporal structure, and autonomous interpretability as a deployment prerequisite

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
From trajectory data, qualitative accounts, and usability outcomes, the authors synthesise "three transferable design principles for self-guided embodied training: feedback as a calibration reference, not an immediate corrector; modality temporal granularity matched to behaviour's temporal structure; and autonomous interpretability as a deployment prerequisite, not an after-effect of usability." In TibetCPR this is realised as a continuous visual rhythm channel for the continuously-regulated compression rate and a discrete electrotactile depth channel that fires only outside the recommended range, with deterministic rule-based mappings whose semantics are inferable through bodily action alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback signals must be interpretable through the user's own bodily action without external mediation, supported by deterministic, rule-based mappings
#### Constraints
- The principles were synthesised from a single formative evaluation in high-altitude, resource-limited Tibetan settings with CPR-naive lay learners

### Target Learners
- Lay bystanders in self-guided, instructor-limited training contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- Embodied motor-skill acquisition and stabilisation, such as CPR compression depth and rhythm
