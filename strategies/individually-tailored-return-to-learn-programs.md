# Provide individually tailored Return-to-Learn programs that match academic accommodations to symptom severity and predicted recovery length

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An individually tailored Return-to-Learn (RTL) program is a physician-outlined set of academic accommodations for a concussed student returning to school. The article reports that such programs "included accommodations such as limiting time reading and using computers, allowing students to take breaks if they experienced worsening concussion symptoms, and allowing students extra time to complete tests and assignments." The authors recommend that "individually tailored Return-to-Learn programs can be an important resource to help facilitate communication between all stakeholders involved in a student-athlete's recovery" and ensure accommodations match symptom nature, severity and predicted recovery length. In this study 56 (49.6%) of SRC students received an RTL program.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Physician assessment of the nature and severity of concussion symptoms and the predicted length of clinical recovery to tailor the accommodations
- Communication between all stakeholders involved in the student-athlete's recovery, including the school
#### Constraints
- In this study RTL programs were provided at the discretion of the treating neurosurgeon and not based on any standardized criteria, and no established guidelines existed to identify which students should receive one
- It remains unknown whether teachers received the Return-to-Learn programs or incorporated the outlined recommendations

### Target Learners
- Adolescent high-school students (grades 8-12) recovering from sport-related concussion

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe gradual return to school activities and coursework during concussion recovery
- Maintaining academic performance while concussion symptoms resolve
