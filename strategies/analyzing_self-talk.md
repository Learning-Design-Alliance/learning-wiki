# Analyzing Self-Talk

## Description
Students identify patterns or themes in their self-talk, such as recurring thoughts or feelings related to specific situations or times of day. This analysis helps students understand the triggers and underlying beliefs that drive their negative self-talk.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students' self-talk logs or journals. Time for reflection and analysis. A supportive environment for sharing insights and discoveries.
#### Constraints
- Students may find it challenging to identify patterns and themes on their own. Some students may be resistant to analyzing their thoughts and feelings. The accuracy of the analysis depends on students' honesty and self-awareness.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- K-12 students who are developing self-awareness and emotional intelligence.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- To help students recognize recurring patterns and themes in their self-talk.

### Affordances
- Enhances self-awareness by identifying triggers and underlying beliefs. Supports emotional regulation by understanding the roots of negative thoughts. Promotes critical thinking by analyzing patterns and themes.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to different age groups and learning styles. Students can focus on specific situations or emotions that are relevant to their lives. Educators can provide different types of prompts and questions to guide the analysis.

## Assessment Evidence
- Review students' analyses for accuracy and depth of insight. Assess students' ability to articulate patterns and themes in their self-talk. Observe students' engagement and participation in discussions about self-talk.

## Impact
- Research supports that self-analysis can improve self-regulation and self-efficacy. By analyzing their self-talk, students can gain insights into the drivers of their behavior and develop strategies to manage it.
