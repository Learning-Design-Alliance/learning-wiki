# Recommendations for applying the five-step conceptual framework technique

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
From the case study's errors and limitations — uniqueness, availability, less is more, meaningfulness, approach, and space — the article derives recommendations for researchers. These include regard for the complexity of the research subject, a preliminary search of "the most up-to-date academic papers" before committing to a subject, including "only the necessary number of sources" and classes, applying the steps so they support sense-making, and adapting the technique to longer essays, theses, dissertations, or as a foundation for research proposals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A preliminary search to confirm enough information is available on the subject before in-depth research
#### Constraints
- The detailed framework development is recommended mainly for longer essays, master theses, and doctorate dissertations; the steps were determined for deductive research

### Target Learners
- researchers, graduate students, and authors of research proposals

### Target Learning Goals
- avoiding methodological errors when constructing a conceptual framework
