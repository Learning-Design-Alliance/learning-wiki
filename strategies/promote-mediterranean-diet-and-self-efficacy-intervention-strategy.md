# Education intervention programs should promote healthy dietary patterns and increase self-efficacy perceptions to support adolescent achievement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the longitudinal findings, the authors conclude that "education intervention programs should promote the adoption of a healthy dietary pattern and increase perceptions of self-efficacy in order to enhance adolescents' chances of achieving higher". The strategy combines a nutrition component (Mediterranean diet adherence) with a motivational component (academic self-efficacy), targeting both in multicomponent, culture-specific school programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must address both dietary pattern adoption and self-efficacy perceptions together
#### Constraints
- The evidence is associational; the authors call for intervention studies testing whether experimental manipulation produces corresponding achievement change

### Target Learners
- high school adolescents aged 15-18

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement
- healthy dietary habits
- academic self-efficacy
