# Offer open certification for passing qualifying examinations as a low-overhead credential that can credit generalist preparation outside degree programs.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Responding to PhD overproduction relative to faculty positions and the difficulty of mobilizing a lengthy PhD variant, the article proposes "an open certiﬁcation for anyone who is able to pass a qualifying examination." Just as universities and private companies offer certificates for coursework completed outside degree programs, this credential would demonstrate competency at the beginning graduate level, and making it available across all disciplines would support educational innovation including generalist training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participating institutions would need to administer and credit qualifying examinations in an open, non-degree-granting context.
#### Constraints
- The strategy is framed as one possibility to balance competing factors in graduate education, not as a tested intervention.

### Target Learners
- prospective graduate students and independent learners seeking graduate-level credentials

### Target Learning Goals
- demonstrating beginning-graduate-level competency across multiple disciplines
