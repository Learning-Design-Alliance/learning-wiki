Use technology-mediated learning to shift the trainer's role from knowledge provider to facilitator and foster reflection¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends computer-facilitated learning and activities to foster reflection in pre-service ELT teacher education. Citing Strampel & Oliver, it argues "use of the computer is significant in terms of changing the role of the course instructor from a knowledge provider and controller to a facilitator and helper", giving student teachers access to multiple worldviews about ELT and creating an environment conducive to reflection leading to conceptual change, knowledge transfer and action. Computer-mediated technologies support reflection through the multiple tools and activities they provide, especially when student teachers are given scaffolds and social tasks.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Student teachers should be given 'scaffolds' and 'social' tasks that encourage, nurture and stimulate further reflection

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is grounded in the cited literature rather than tested in this study

Target Learners¶

  • pre-service ELT student teachers in higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • autonomous reflective practice and conceptual change about ELT

Claims¶

  • Teachers perceive their role shifting from knowledge transmitters to facilitators, co-investigators, and ethical supervisors of AI use [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Engage trainees in focused observation tasks, collaborative learning tasks, and peer communities of practice to develop reflective practitioners
  • Adopt a facilitator role and prepare students for active participation in sociocultural L2 writing instruction
  • Use technology to facilitate active participation rather than replace face-to-face learning, potentially creating greater synchronicity between modes
  • Focus staff development on the judgement work of AI integration rather than tool training
  • Provide teachers with training periods on how, when, and where to use language-learning technologies
