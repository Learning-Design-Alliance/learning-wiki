# Financial support programs improve workforce diversity most when specifically targeted to underrepresented minoritized entrants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (1 review, 1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Loan repayment and service-contingent scholarship programs reduce financial barriers to health professional education, which disproportionately affect underrepresented minority students. The article reports that "loan repayment programs for early‐career health professionals are most impactful for improving workforce diversity and representativeness if they are specifically targeted to underrepresented minoritized entrants," and similarly that scholarship programs are most impactful when designed to specifically appeal to and reach underrepresented minoritized students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Loan repayment and scholarship programs must be specifically targeted to underrepresented minoritized entrants to maximize diversity impact
#### Constraints
- Loan repayment programs are often linked to a service commitment in a defined geographic area or care setting

### Target Learners
- Underrepresented minoritized students and early-career health professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- Access to and completion of health professional education
