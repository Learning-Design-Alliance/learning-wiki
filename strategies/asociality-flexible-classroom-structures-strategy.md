# Remove normative social demands: flexible attendance, autonomy, and no forced disclosure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Under the asociality theme, the article recommends concrete classroom moves: "Remove strict attendance requirements", support learner autonomy in research and lab experiences, allow students to work alone or choose partners, provide flexible seating, include breaks within long lectures once every hour, and avoid pressuring students to justify why they think differently or to disclose a diagnosis. The strategy treats learning outside expected social norms as legitimate knowledge production rather than deviation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors engage students with compassion and maintain open dialogue, since they may not have the answers to every student's situation
#### Constraints
- Some class purposes legitimately require attendance; the article notes a laboratory class may crucially require perfect attendance, so the recommendation may be better interpreted as allowing make-up labs throughout the week

### Target Learners
- neurodivergent physics students
- high school physics students, about one-third of whom have an IEP or 504 plan in the co-author's course

### Target Learning Goals
- participation and knowledge creation in physics courses
- student well-being and autonomy

### Affordances
- Neuroqueer Literacies Framework Physics
