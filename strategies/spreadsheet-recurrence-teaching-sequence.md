# Teaching sequence: discretize the evolution equations, implement the recurrences in a spreadsheet, and compare classical, stochastic and quantum solutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy runs across the paper's three sections: students discretize the evolution equations into recurrence relations, implement them in a spreadsheet, compute and graphically represent solutions for given initial conditions, and examine how solutions depend on initial conditions and dynamical parameters. The article states that 'the possibility to compute and analyze approximate solutions of the evolution laws allows to make a ﬁrst comparison between classical, stochastic and quantum world, to highlight the distinctive role played by probability in diﬀerent contexts'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A spreadsheet work environment available to students
- For the stochastic part, previous knowledge of basic elements of probability theory
#### Constraints
- The authors state their aim is not to counterpose a discrete mathematics to a continuous one; the rigorous continuous limit should be discussed as requiring new mathematics

### Target Learners
- high school students
- second-year physics and mathematics students
- high school science teachers in training

### Target Learning Goals
- analyzing dynamical features of system evolution
- comparing the role of probability in classical, stochastic and quantum contexts
