# Use generative AI to prototype design alternatives within research-informed boundaries and simulate learners to narrow the design space before classroom testing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the RIGID design phase, generative AI acts as a coordination layer: prompts combine micro-, meso-, and macro-level considerations, and AI generates multiple design alternatives within research-informed boundaries for instructors to compare. The article states that "RIGID addresses this challenge by positioning generative AI as a coordination layer that operationalizes research-based constraints while enabling scalable exploration of the design space." Simulated learners serve as exploratory evaluation tools for prototypes before human testing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured, context-rich inputs and carefully designed prompting strategies so outputs align with user intent and pedagogical objectives
- Instructors retain the central role in interpreting outputs and deciding which elements to adopt, discard, or refine
#### Constraints
- Simulated learners cannot substitute for validation with real students
- AI-generated resources are draft materials, not prescriptive solutions

### Target Learners
- instructional designers
- instructors

### Target Learning Goals
- generating lesson plans, assessment items, and adaptive tutoring content
- anticipating learner misunderstandings before deployment
