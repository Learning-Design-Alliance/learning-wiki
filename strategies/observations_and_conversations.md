# Observations and Conversations

## Description
Monitoring student learning through observations and conversations helps determine understanding of concepts and problem-solving abilities. Instructors can ask key questions to gauge understanding, observe student interactions, and document progress through anecdotal notes. Listening in on student dialogue allows the instructor to hear how learners are engaging with one another and what their problem-solving process is like.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires skilled instructors who can effectively observe and question students. Instructors must be able to take detailed notes and synthesize information quickly to inform instruction. Time is needed for observation and reflection.
#### Constraints
- Can be subjective and influenced by instructor bias. May not provide a comprehensive measure of all learning outcomes. Time-consuming to implement effectively, especially in large classes.

### Target Learners

- All learner levels, including K–12, higher education, and adult learners. Particularly useful in hands-on, exploratory learning environments such as STEM and STEAM.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Measure student growth, assess conceptual understanding, evaluate problem-solving processes, gather qualitative data

### Affordances
- Provides real-time insights into student learning, allows for immediate feedback and instructional adjustments, and captures qualitative data on student thinking and collaboration. Enables instructors to assess skills not easily measured by traditional tests.

### Personalization
- Observations and conversations can be tailored to individual learners by focusing on their specific strengths, weaknesses, and learning styles. Open-ended questions can be adapted to different levels of understanding.

## Assessment Evidence
- Collect anecdotal notes on student understanding and problem-solving processes. Analyze responses to key questions to assess conceptual understanding. Track changes in student performance based on observational data.

## Impact
- Enhanced learning: Frequent observation and conversation helps learners to retain information and instructors make timely instructional adjustments. Increased engagement: Studies indicate regular monitoring of student engagement improves learners’ self-regulation strategies.
