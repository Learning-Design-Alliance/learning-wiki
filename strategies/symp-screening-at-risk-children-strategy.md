# Use the SYMP Test as a quick screening measure to identify children at risk for mathematical difficulties or dyscalculia

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends administering the SYMP Test as an early screening tool: because symbolic magnitude comparison is robustly associated with mathematics achievement and children with MLD score lower on the test, "the test has the potential to be used as a screening measure to identify children who are at risk to develop mathematical difficulties at an early age." Its group administration takes about 10–15 minutes and requires little teacher training, making it usable by practitioners and in large-scale intervention research.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administration by a trained test administrator in a classroom group session with stopwatch timing
- Use of both subtests together, since overall-score reliability exceeds single-subtest reliability
#### Constraints
- The article notes the data do not establish causal or predictive associations, so screening identifies risk rather than causes of difficulty
- No group differences between MLD and typically achieving children were found in Grade 6, limiting screening sensitivity in the oldest grade

### Target Learners
- Primary school children in Grades 1–6, especially those at risk for mathematical difficulties or dyscalculia

### Target Learning Goals
- Early identification of children at risk for mathematical difficulties to enable timely remedial intervention targeting numerical skills
