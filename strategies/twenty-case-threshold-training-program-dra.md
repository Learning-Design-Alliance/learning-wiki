# Use a 20-case CUSUM-derived threshold to structure training programs for US-guided distal radial artery cannulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating its 20-case learning-curve threshold into training programs for advanced trainees and interventionalists, stating that "The threshold of 20 cases proved valuable for incorporating training programs for advanced trainees and interventionalists". The threshold comes from the study's pooled mean CUSUM turning point, after which times and first-attempt success improved. The authors say the data "may provide a theoretical basis for training for this technique in various institutions".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Novice operators with limited experience, supervised by an experienced attending who takes over after 2 failed skin punctures or complications
- Recording of per-case times, attempts, and complications to support CUSUM monitoring
#### Constraints
- Derived from a small-sample, single-center study in China with only 3 residents; patients with BMI > 30 kg/m² were excluded; residents had prior radial artery cannulation experience, so the curve may differ for other operators or settings

### Target Learners
- novice anesthesiology residents

### Target Learning Goals
- achieving proficiency in ultrasound-guided distal radial artery cannulation
