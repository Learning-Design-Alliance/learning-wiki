# Incorporate TBL, or increase its percentage, in the undergraduate medical curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adding team-based learning to the curriculum, or raising its share, because students in their randomized crossover study achieved learning outcomes and clinical reasoning skills better with TBL and preferred it over interactive lecture. The authors write, "It is suggested to include TBL, or increase its percentage, in the curriculum", and note the recommendation rests on students' affinity for team application and feedback and on clinical reasoning being a mandatory competency. Implementation requires the full TBL sequence (advance assignment, iRAT, tRAT, instructor clarification, tAPP, peer feedback).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Planning to conduct the TBL session is described by the authors as an important factor for better implementation
- The structured TBL sequence of advance assignment, iRAT, tRAT, instructor clarification review, tAPP, appeal, and peer feedback must be followed
#### Constraints
- The study was confined to two modules on an all-male campus and the authors state it cannot be generalized
- Results come from an integrated modular system and might differ in colleges with a traditional curriculum

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students (3rd-year preclinical and 6th-year clinical)

### Target Learning Goals
- Achieving module learning outcomes
- Clinical reasoning skills
- Teamwork and collaboration
