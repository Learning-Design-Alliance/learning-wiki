# Target individual epistemological resources, rather than general instructions, as the lever for shifting students' epistemological frames

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because observed frame shifts occurred at the level of a single epistemological resource, the authors argue that "trying to shift an individual epis- temological resource may be an eﬀective way to shift a student’s epistemological frame." This means designing interventions or assessment features that interrupt or redirect a specific resource—such as epistemic form—rather than simply telling students to solve problems conceptually or algorithmically, which the authors did not observe to be effective.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identifying which specific resource (e.g., epistemic form, knowledge source) is maintaining the current frame so the intervention can target it directly
#### Constraints
- Observed in only three frame-shift instances within introductory physics assessment interviews; the authors note they did not observe simply telling students to solve problems conceptually or algorithmically to be effective

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- shifting students toward conceptual engagement with physics problems
- flexible epistemological framing during problem solving
