# Simulated Clinical Experience program with standardized patients and three-perspective feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
After the 24 weeks of clinical skills education, a one-week Simulated Clinical Experience (SCE) program uses "simulated clinical setting and standardized patient" across 15 modules of common primary-care presentations such as dizziness, coughing, and low back pain. Each student has 6 occurrences of the 15 modules in 10-minute sessions at the Clinical Skill Simulation Room. Students "receive multidimensional feedback on their clinical performance from fellow students (peer perspective), standardized patients (doctor-patient perspective), and academic staffs (teaching/learning perspective)".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained standardized personnel prepared through 15 hours of scenario training, simulation rooms with one-way mirror and closed-circuit recording, and a matching guidebook with 15 chapters
#### Constraints
- The discussion notes "written feedback can be difficult to immediately improve clinical performance" and recommends video-based debriefing in a dedicated room

### Target Learners
- medical students preparing for a clinical performance test

### Target Learning Goals
- history taking, physical examination, differential diagnosis, syndrome identification, and patient education
