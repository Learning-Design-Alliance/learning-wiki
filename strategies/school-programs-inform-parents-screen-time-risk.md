# School-based health programs and physical education courses should inform parents and guardians about the risk of excess leisure screen time for academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that school-based health programs and physical education courses deliver information to guardians or parents about how excess leisure screen time may harm children's academic performance. It notes that "some Western countries such as Australia hold classroom lessons specifically focused on reducing screen time", and that parental engagement via take-home materials, newsletters, meetings, and regular contact with teachers or sports organizations can be effective for reducing screen time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must reach guardians or parents with information about excess leisure screen time risk
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is a single-school observational study; the authors state results may lack representativeness and may not be generalizable

### Target Learners
- elementary school children aged 7–10 and their parents or guardians

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining children's academic performance by reducing leisure screen time and increasing physical activity
