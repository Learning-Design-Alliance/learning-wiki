# How Spelling Supports Reading

## Description
Instruction on the relationship between spelling and reading. Research shows that learning to spell and learning to read rely on much of the same underlying knowledge, such as the relationships between letters and sounds, and that spelling instruction can be designed to help children better understand that key knowledge, resulting in better reading.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured spelling instruction, materials for phoneme-grapheme mapping, activities for exploring morphology, access to lists and word walls.
#### Constraints
- May not be effective if spelling instruction is not integrated with reading instruction. Also, it is critical to focus on individual learning styles.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- To improve reading fluency and comprehension by enhancing spelling skills and understanding of phoneme-grapheme relationships.

### Affordances
- Enhances reading skills by strengthening spelling proficiency, improves writing by reducing cognitive load, and builds overall literacy skills.

### Personalization
- Spelling instruction can be tailored to individual student needs by focusing on specific phoneme-grapheme correspondences or morphology. Explicit, systematic instruction is provided.
