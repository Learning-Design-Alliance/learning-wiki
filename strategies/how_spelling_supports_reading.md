# How Spelling Supports Reading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Spelling instruction that explicitly teaches phoneme–grapheme correspondences, orthographic patterns, and morphology strengthens reading by building the same word-knowledge network used for word recognition. Rather than treating spelling as a memorization subject, this strategy treats encoding (spelling) as the reciprocal of decoding (reading): analyzing *why* a word is spelled the way it is — sound-by-sound, syllable by syllable, morpheme by morpheme — creates durable orthographic representations that support fluent reading [Ehri's phases show word reading and spelling draw on shared orthographic knowledge.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [+S].

## Design Implications

Spelling and reading rely on shared orthographic representations, so systematic spelling instruction produces measurable gains in word reading, and the effect is strongest when instruction is explicit rather than incidental [Graham & Santangelo's meta-analysis found explicit spelling instruction improves both spelling and reading performance.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [+S]. Instruction should move from phoneme–grapheme mapping to syllable patterns to morphological analysis, mirroring how orthographic knowledge develops. Automatic word recognition — the payoff of well-encoded spellings — frees working memory for comprehension [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A planned scope and sequence of phoneme–grapheme correspondences and morphemes, not ad-hoc word lists
- Explicit teaching of the *reasoning* behind spellings (why *jumped* ends in *-ed*, not *-t*), often via [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)-style teacher modeling
- Dictation and encoding practice, not just visual copying — retrieving spellings from memory builds stronger representations than copying
- Word study that invites [Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md) and comparison of patterns

#### Constraints
- Rote memorization of unrelated word lists produces weak transfer to reading [Explicit spelling instruction outperforms rote or incidental approaches.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [-M]
- Whole-word visual memorization as the primary method generalizes poorly compared with phoneme–grapheme teaching [Phonics-based instruction outperforms whole-word approaches for generalization.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [+S]
- Weekly test-only cycles (study Friday, test Monday, forget by Wednesday) without distributed review undermine retention
- For highly irregular words, sound-based strategies alone fail; instruction must shift to etymology and morphology [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Word study / developmental spelling** (e.g., Words Their Way): students sort words by pattern, comparing and contrasting — a form of [Comparing Cases](../elements/comparing-cases.md)
- **Structured literacy programs** (e.g., Orton-Gillingham–based approaches): simultaneous oral spelling, tracing, and dictation in a multisensory sequence
- **Morphological word inquiry** (upper grades): investigating Latin/Greek roots and etymology to explain spellings that phonology alone cannot

### Target Learners
- Beginning readers in the full-alphabetic phase, who benefit most from systematic phoneme–grapheme instruction [Phonics-based instruction outperforms whole-word approaches for generalization.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [+S]
- Struggling readers and students with dyslexia, for whom explicit encoding instruction is a core component of structured literacy interventions [+S]
- Upper-elementary and adolescent readers, who gain comprehension-relevant vocabulary depth from morphological spelling study [+M]
- Less beneficial as isolated drill for already-fluent spellers, who consolidate orthographic knowledge through volume of reading [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Orthographic mapping: forming automatic letter–sound–word representations
- Decoding fluency and word recognition accuracy
- Vocabulary morphology: using spelling patterns to unlock meaning of unfamiliar words

### Instructions
1. **Diagnose** — assess students' spelling of feature-based words to place them in a developmental stage (letter-name, within-word, syllable-juncture, derivational).
2. **Teach the pattern explicitly** — model segmenting a word into phonemes and mapping each to its grapheme, narrating choices aloud ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)).
3. **Sort and compare** — students sort word cards by pattern, articulating why each word belongs where it does ([Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md)); contrasting correct and incorrect spellings sharpens discrimination ([Non-Examples](../elements/non-examples.md)).
4. **Encode from memory** — dictation and spelling-from-memory practice, with immediate feedback; [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) multisyllabic words by syllable or morpheme reduces load [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S].
5. **Connect to reading** — have students find the week's pattern in connected text and apply it to read new words, closing the encoding–decoding loop.
6. **Distribute review** — revisit earlier patterns cumulatively rather than testing once and moving on.

## Examples
- **Words Their Way** (Bear, Invernizzi, Templeton, & Johnston) — developmental word study with pattern sorts; widely used in K–8 classrooms.
- **Orton-Gillingham–based programs** (e.g., [Wilson Reading System](https://www.wilsonlanguage.com)) — explicit, sequential encoding instruction with simultaneous oral spelling for struggling readers.
- **Spelling Through Morphographs** (Engelmann) — Direct Instruction program teaching prefixes, suffixes, and bases as spellable units, with documented reading gains [+M].
