# Integrate VR with traditional training methods and validate with large-scale multicenter trials before wide promotion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (4 for, 5 mixed) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on findings that NIVR matched conventional training on knowledge and drill scores while speeding some tasks, the authors recommend that "policy makers should proceed cautiously when promoting VR training and consider integrating various training methods to ensure comprehensive skill improvement for nurses." They call for validation "through large-scale, multicenter, randomized controlled trials", task efficiency assessment mechanisms, and exploration of long-term and cross-disciplinary applications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Policy-level decision making informed by evidence on VR training effectiveness before promotion
- Integration of multiple training methods to ensure comprehensive skill improvement
#### Constraints
- The authors note ongoing debates: some studies find VR does not significantly outperform traditional methods in skills, satisfaction, confidence, and performance time

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses in infectious disease and isolation ward settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Comprehensive pandemic preparedness and clinical skill improvement
