Use TAPPS as a listening-partner procedure in which the solver verbalizes thoughts while the listener prompts without guiding¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

TAPPS pairs one student solving a problem aloud with a listener who prompts verbalization. The listener "prompts the problem solver to explain what actions or thoughts are taking place and why," challenging silences with prompts such as "Tell me what you are thinking now." Listeners may not solve the problem or guide the solver toward a solution. In this study, listening partners received oral instructions and a list of probing questions, and TAPPS students practiced with two dissimilar word problems before the task.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The listener must ensure the solver explains his or her reasoning and continues talking
  • Listeners are not allowed to solve the problem or ask questions or make statements that guide the problem solver toward a solution
  • Practice tasks should be sufficiently dissimilar so as not to introduce bias into verbal reports

Constraints¶

  • Verbalizing thoughts can interfere with task execution and may slow progress
  • Students' motivation or comfort with discussing thoughts may inhibit or slow their success rate

Target Learners¶

  • secondary-level career and technical education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • metacognitive self-regulation during problem solving
  • troubleshooting technical faults

Claims¶

  • Metacognitive processes contributing to problem solving include identifying the problem, defining the problem space, representing it, planning, and evaluating performance [+M]
  • The effect of TAPPS may depend on student maturity, experience, and domain-specific knowledge [~M]
  • TAPPS produced no significant difference in troubleshooting completion time compared with working silently among successful secondary-level students [-M]
  • TAPPS produced no significant difference in troubleshooting success rate compared with working silently among secondary-level CTE students [-M]
