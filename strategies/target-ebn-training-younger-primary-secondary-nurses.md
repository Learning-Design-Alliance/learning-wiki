# Target EBN education and training at younger nurses and nurses in primary or secondary hospitals, and emphasize knowledge use in practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends prioritizing younger nurses (30 years or below), who were among the first generation to receive school EBN education in China and show higher EBP readiness, and nurses from primary or secondary hospitals, who have fewer opportunities to learn and practice EBN. It also stresses that "it is not enough to acquire knowledge alone in EBN education and training, but also essential to use and demonstrate EBN knowledge in clinical practice," since knowledge alone showed limited explanatory power.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- EBN education and training programs that enrich perceived EBN knowledge and provide opportunities to use and demonstrate knowledge in clinical practice.
#### Constraints
- Short-term EBN training may not produce obvious effects for evidence searching, critical appraisal, and synthesis, which the authors say requires systematic, long-term theoretical education and practical training.

### Target Learners
- younger nurses (30 years or below)
- nurses in primary or secondary hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- evidence-based practice competency
- evidence-based nursing knowledge
