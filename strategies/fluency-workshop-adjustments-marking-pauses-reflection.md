# Adjust the fluency workshop by removing the marking-pauses activity for conversational dialogues and allocating structured class time for reflection on role-play recordings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on learner feedback, the article recommends two adjustments to the fluency workshop: dropping marking pauses when materials are short-turn conversational dialogue, and scheduling in-class reflection time after the role-play recording. The authors write that "Removal of the marking pauses activity would allow time to be allocated to structured reflection after the role-play recording during class time," ensuring learners actually listen to and reflect on their recordings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Class time must be allocated and structured for learners to listen to and reflect on their role-play recordings
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from learner feedback in this specific low-proficiency Japanese EFL context, not from a tested manipulation of the adjustments themselves

### Target Learners
- low-proficiency EFL university students in Japan

### Target Learning Goals
- speaking fluency
- self-reflection on oral performance
