# Develop a dedicated OOOR airway curriculum focused on DAA comprehension and communication with higher-level physicians

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the gap between residents' self-reported algorithm familiarity and their observed simulation performance, the authors developed and recommend a curriculum for out-of-the-operating-room emergent airway management. They state: "we have since developed a curriculum designed to improve resident comprehension of the difficult airway algorithm and its use OOOR, as well as enhance communication during stressful and often unorganized events." The curriculum should also consider other algorithms such as the Difficult Airway Society algorithm and instruct residents on thresholds for involving faculty.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A focused curriculum in advanced airway management with defined objectives, which programs offering such training suggest is crucial to increase airway management expertise.
#### Constraints
- Efficacy of the new curriculum has not yet been tested; the authors anticipate future studies comparing questionnaire responses and simulation performance.

### Target Learners
- anesthesiology residents (post-graduate years 2–4)

### Target Learning Goals
- comprehension and application of the ASA Difficult Airway Algorithm during emergent out-of-operating-room intubation
- communication with faculty and specialists during airway emergencies
