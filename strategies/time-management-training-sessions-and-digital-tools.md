# Time management training can be delivered as structured sessions or daily digital goal-setting and monitoring tools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review describes two intervention formats for improving students' time management: weekly in-person training sessions and web-based tools for daily goal setting and monitoring. In one study, students "received six weekly 50-minute time management training sessions" and improved both time management and self-efficacy. In another, students used "a web-based intervention tool for setting goals for the next day and monitoring their time management daily over 2 weeks," improving time management strategy use. These offer implementable recipes for practitioners targeting study habits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained engagement is needed: one format involved six weekly sessions, the other daily use of a digital tool over two weeks
#### Constraints
- Effects on self-efficacy were inconsistent across the two interventions

### Target Learners
- fifth-grade students with learning disabilities; undergraduate and graduate students in online courses

### Target Learning Goals
- improved academic time management and study engagement
