# Incorporate conceptually-rich, engaging real-world and astronomical contexts into introductory physics problems and instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that introductory physics instructors incorporate problems with exciting, realistic contexts and astronomical objects, precede problem-solving with interactive demonstrations, and use real images rather than cartoons or sketches. It argues that "more captivating instruction of physics classes, including more engaging contexts such as those used in astronomy classes, may improve physics students’ attitudes and approaches to problem solving."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems and instruction must retain conceptual and quantitative rigor; the article frames this as tailoring instruction to motivate students "without sacrificing rigor in learning."
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is motivated by a post-test group comparison and interviews, not a direct test of the intervention; the authors call for future pre/post AAPS administration to test the motivational hypothesis.

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students (science and engineering majors)

### Target Learning Goals
- favorable attitudes and approaches to problem solving
- conceptual understanding of physics principles
