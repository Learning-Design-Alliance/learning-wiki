# Run an error-detection examination: distribute written procedures with planted errors, have students identify and correct them, present corrections to peers, then close with teacher summary and short lecture

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This is a specific implementable activity sequence the article developed for fifth-year dental students in a 1-hr didactic slot after clinical training. Error-detection examination sheets "presented detailed written treatment procedures for typical cases, but some steps were purposefully written incorrectly". Students identify and amend the incorrect steps, each student presents corrections to the group, and a teacher then delivers the ideal answers and a short lecture.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small-group seminar setting after clinical training, with examination sheets presenting detailed written treatment procedures for typical cases containing purposefully incorrect steps
#### Constraints
- The article notes only a 1-hr slot was available for supportive didactic teaching, and feedback in this pilot was brief; the authors plan to allocate more time for feedback next year

### Target Learners
- fifth-year undergraduate dental students in clinical training

### Target Learning Goals
- retention and deep understanding of conservative dentistry treatment procedures, such as composite resin filling, amalgam removal, and ceramic inlay restoration
