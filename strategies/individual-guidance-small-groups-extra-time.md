# Provide one-to-one guidance, small groups and flexible pacing for low-grade pupils

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because pupils' skills to accomplish different tasks vary, "The one-to-one guidance is needed", and the teacher must deliberate whether a pupil experiences extra teaching as a sanction or as a positive thing. The article questions whether the standard 45-minute Finnish lesson is optimal for slow learners needing concretising, and suggests educational extra-time or class repetition, even classlessness at primary level, with attention to cognitive skills rather than only subject-specific goals. It also reports that "The low -grade pupils perform the best when working in a small group", since smaller groups let the teacher observe pupils more individually.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must know whether the pupil is committed and intrinsically motivated before arranging private teaching
#### Constraints
- Individualised teaching is complicated by age-group progression, where all pupils move to the next grade at the same pace

### Target Learners
- low-grade primary pupils whose learning is slow and requires concretising

### Target Learning Goals
- control of cognitive skills
- successful completion of tasks
