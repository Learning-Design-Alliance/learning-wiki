# Emphasize concepts, hands-on experiments and emotion-raising stories in science teaching to ease conceptual change

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (6 for, 2 mixed) · 4 studies (3 causal, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, since misconceptions are seemingly founded on false preconceptions, "more emphasis in Science teaching should be on hands-on experiments, blackboard drawings without formulas and concept oriented teaching in sciences." It further recommends that "suspense stories like „How Galileo overcame superstitious believes“ raise emotions improving memory and attendance thus easing concept change," citing psychological and neurological research on emotional events and memory (Cahill 1994; Erk 2003).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Involving emotional events in the process of teaching improves memory and eases overcoming misconceptions (Cahill 1994)
#### Constraints
- The article notes "Emotional events improve memory but they seem not to prevent misconceptions": the same misconceptions appeared whether the rocket question was described by a picture with emotion-raising words or in linear form

### Target Learners
- first-semester engineering students
- university science students

### Target Learning Goals
- overcoming science misconceptions
- conceptual change in physics and mathematics

### Affordances
- Raca Four Step Misconception Model
