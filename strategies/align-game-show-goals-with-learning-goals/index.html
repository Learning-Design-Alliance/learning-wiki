Align competitive game goals with learning goals by restricting opponent rating differences and requiring tutoring after losses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors propose design improvements to the competitive Game Show so that winning requires SimStudent competency. "One such idea is to restrict the range of available opponents so that the difference in ratings is within a desired zone." They also suggest requiring tutoring after a loss and embedding virtual contestants of varied competency, which is technically feasible since SimStudent can be pre-trained.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Winners' SimStudents must demonstrate high competency on target problems; students must tutor after a loss

Constraints¶

  • The current matching schema allowed easy wins that misaligned performance and learning goals

Target Learners¶

  • middle and high school algebra students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • solving linear equations with variables on both sides

Claims¶

  • The competitive Game Show increased students' tutoring engagement relative to baseline [+W]
  • Students with higher desire to win strategically selected lower-rated opponents for an easy win [+W]
