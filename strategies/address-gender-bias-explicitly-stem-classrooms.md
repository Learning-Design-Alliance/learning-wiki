# Address gender bias in STEM classrooms through interventions and explicit discussion rather than assuming representation gains suffice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation for university educators: because women can "still be subject to gender bias even when they outperform and outnumber their male counterparts," institutions should not treat performance disparities or representation as the primary problem. Instead, educators should confront biased perceptions directly through interventions and open discussion, and publicly recognize women's performance and accomplishments so that misperceptions of reduced capability are corrected and women's awareness of other women's success can support retention in STEM.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment to diversifying undergraduate STEM populations
- Instructors willing to surface and discuss gender bias with students
#### Constraints
- The article notes intergroup contact alone is not sufficient: interactive educational environments may not reduce the gender gap, and women in group work can face diminished benefits such as having suggestions adopted less often

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate STEM students
- University STEM instructors

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing gender bias in peer perceptions of ability
- Retaining women in STEM majors and careers
