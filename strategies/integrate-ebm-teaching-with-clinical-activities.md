# Integrate EBM teaching with clinical activities and bedside application rather than standalone didactic teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends teaching EBM during students' clinical years, integrated with bedside and problem-based or blended-learning approaches, so students apply evidence to real clinical scenarios. It argues this provides "an opportunity to improve competence in both their EBM and clinical skills", a nexus the authors call essential if EBM is to be applied in clinical settings, and helps bridge the gap between EBM theory and practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to clinical environments where students encounter patients
- Assessment tasks requiring application of appraised evidence to a clinical scenario
#### Constraints
- The article states it remains uncertain whether barriers such as lack of time, resources, patient-related factors or peer influence negate transfer of EBM skills into clinical practice after graduation

### Target Learners
- medical students in their clinical years

### Target Learning Goals
- applying critically appraised evidence to clinical decision making
