# Use immersive VR headset training as a feasible, accessible option for robotic set-up training in resource-limited settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends IVR headset training as an adjunct for teaching operating room configuration where hands-on robot access is scarce: "Adjuncts such as VR headsets can provide realistic and interactive learning scenarios in a virtual environment that closely mimics real-world situations... accessible and feasible for implementation in resource-limited settings". This addresses limited opportunities for in-theatre training and scarcity of robotic equipment shared across specialties, and suits a growing pool of less experienced team members with variable staff retention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need access to the headset hardware and the platform's online portal; training should be followed by ongoing deliberate practice.
#### Constraints
- The article states IVR does not replace the benefits of ongoing deliberate practice over time, which is still necessary for achieving optimal performance.

### Target Learners
- Robot-naive novices
- Theatre nursing staff
- Surgical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- Robotic operating room set-up and configuration skills
