# Introduce antimatter through the historical path, using non-relativistic quantum formalisms the student already possesses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The note outlines a university teaching path for antimatter based on the history of knowledge, with two stated design features: it draws on the historical steps, and it avoids a purely late-course formal introduction that leaves ideas and mathematics entangled. The author writes that the goal is "un percorso didattico di livello universitario basato sulla storia della conoscenza", one that "ricorre per quanto possibile ai formalismi e linguaggi della meccanica quantistica non relativistica già nota allo studente", moving gradually toward relativistic quantum mechanics. The sequence is: wave-function viewpoint first, then Dirac's hole theory, then Majorana's canonical quantization.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students have already met spin, the Pauli exclusion principle, and the Dirac equation as presented in university atomic-physics and quantum-mechanics courses, as the note assumes the Dirac equation "sia stata già esposta, come avviene nei corsi universitari di fisica"
#### Constraints
- The note addresses only university particle/theory courses; the author flags that history-centred paths must repeat laborious steps, and that concepts of anti-particle in general are distinguished from the specific notions of anti-electron and anti-matter treated here

### Target Learners
- university students in theoretical particle physics who have completed quantum mechanics and atomic physics

### Target Learning Goals
- understand the concept of antimatter and its origin in the theory of the relativistic electron
