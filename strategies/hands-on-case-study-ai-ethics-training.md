# Embed ethics in AI capacity building using hands-on approaches, case studies, and role reversal

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review reports that AI ethics education pilots recommend active, experiential delivery. The University of North Carolina pilot "suggested the value of prototyping/hands-on approaches and challenging students to employ diverse ethical approaches", and the University of Oulu pilot recommended future implementations incorporate unexpected ethical issues through methods like case study analyses and role reversal between defenders and opponents. Delivery methods across the 41% of capacity-building studies include seminars, summer schools, challenge-based learning engineering ethics courses, and constructivist hands/minds-on experiences with robots.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit guidance is needed, since US Information and Computer Science students often failed to consider ethical implications of AI design for privacy and well-being without it
#### Constraints
- Few programmes report training on ethical issues around AI, and even fewer on best practices for ethical integration of AI resources

### Target Learners
- Future AI professionals especially in Engineering
- K-12, TVET and higher education students
- Teachers, school deans and researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding ethical implications of AI design and use
- AI literacy and AI ethics competence
- Informed choices about AI applications
