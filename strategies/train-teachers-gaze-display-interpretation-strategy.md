# Train teachers in gaze-display interpretation before deploying live gaze displays in teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the null results and teacher debriefings, the authors recommend that "Future research should therefore investigate the effects of training teachers in gaze display interpretation and the longitudinal effects of working with (live) gaze displays." Some teachers reported that having to interpret and react almost simultaneously was cognitively demanding, and teachers still expressed doubt in their interpretations. Training and extended use are proposed before expecting live gaze displays to improve adaptive teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need opportunities to get used to the gaze display and practice interpreting it before live use
#### Constraints
- This is a recommendation for further research; the article did not itself test training or longitudinal effects

### Target Learners
- teachers and supervisors in one-to-one health-sciences teaching

### Target Learning Goals
- adaptive instructional support in teaching complex visual tasks
