# Replace standardized test cutoffs with structured interviews and bridge master's programs in graduate admissions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 5 studies (4 associational, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends moving away from GRE cutoffs in graduate admissions, citing evidence that standardized tests are "better predictors of gender and color than professional success." It points to the Fisk-Vanderbilt program's use of a 30-minute interview instead of a GRE cutoff with over 80% retention, and to Cornell's policy since the late 1990s of ignoring low test scores when GPA and research credentials are outstanding. It also suggests programs connecting education levels could in time replace standardized tests.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained interviewers able to assess communication and interaction ability
- Alternative evaluation criteria such as GPA and research credentials
#### Constraints
- The article notes that at Cornell, low GRE scores are accepted only when other credentials are outstanding, and most admitted students still have high GRE scores

### Target Learners
- graduate school applicants
- women and minority students

### Target Learning Goals
- access to graduate STEM education
- retention through PhD completion

## Claims

- [Cornell University's physics program, which has not applied a GRE cutoff since the late 1990s, graduated 73% of its women PhD students between 1993 and 2011, above the NSF-reported average of 59%](../claims/cornell-physics-no-gre-cutoff-retention.md) [+W]
- [Standardized admission tests introduced since the 1940s were found to be better predictors of gender and color than of professional success, keeping women and minorities out of top schools](../claims/standardized-tests-predict-demographics-not-success.md) [+W]
- [A 700 QGRE cutoff would reject nearly three quarters of Hispanic physical-sciences test takers, more for other minority groups, and filter out women at a higher rate than men](../claims/qgre-cutoff-700-diversity-filter.md) [+W]
- [GRE Verbal is the only GRE subtest to predict graduate GPA after controlling for other admissions variables](../claims/gre-verbal-weakly-predicts-graduate-gpa.md) [+W]
- [QGRE correlates only weakly with graduate GPA in the author's program, where undergraduate GPA is a better predictor of graduate GPA and correlates with all three GRE sections](../claims/qgre-weak-predictor-ugpa-better.md) [+W]
- [PGRE score shows no statistically significant correlation with first-author papers published during or exiting graduate school among prize fellows](../claims/pgre-no-correlation-graduate-publications.md) [+W]
- [The Fisk-Vanderbilt master's-to-PhD bridge program, using a 30-minute interview instead of a GRE cutoff, achieved over 80% retention of women and minorities toward the PhD](../claims/fisk-vanderbilt-bridge-program-retention.md) [+W]
- [Women enrolled in STEM PhD programs had GRE Q scores averaging about 40 points lower than men at each institution while completing at similar rates](../claims/gre-q-gender-gap-similar-completion.md) [+W]
- [GRE V and GRE Q scores do not predict STEM PhD completion for women](../claims/gre-scores-no-prediction-women-stem-phd.md) [+W]
