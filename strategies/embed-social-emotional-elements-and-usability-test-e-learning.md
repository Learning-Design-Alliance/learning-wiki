# Improve e-learning presentations by embedding social and emotional elements such as patient-case videos and reflection-triggering questions, and usability-test the tool with a few users first

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that "The presentation could be improved by including social and emotional elements, for example embedding videos with patient cases connected to the different antibiotics, or including questions that trigger increased reflection." They also state that, as e-learning novices unfamiliar with usability testing, testing the technology with just a few users would most likely have discovered the difficulties before students encountered them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Patient-case videos or reflection-triggering questions embedded in the presentation
- Usability testing with a small number of users before deployment
#### Constraints
- Technical problems partly stemmed from educators' and students' lack of technical skills; some issues were resolved post hoc by advising a different web browser and explaining the software's functions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- placing antibiotic knowledge in a larger, meaningful clinical context
- increased reflection during e-learning
