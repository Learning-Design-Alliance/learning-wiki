# Use a distracter task between matched question sets to probe consistency of solution method without memory of prior methods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the interviews, five vector questions (all gridded or all non-gridded) were followed by a half-hour distracter task on sound waves or integration, then the other five questions. The article states "The intent of adding a distracter question was to cause the students to approach the second set of vector tasks without definite memory of the methods used in the first set." This design let the authors observe whether students re-derived or retained their solution method across representation changes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A distracter task of comparable length and unrelated content to plausibly disrupt memory of the first question set.
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as an interview design choice, not a validated procedure; its effectiveness at erasing method memory was not independently measured.

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- probing stability of problem-solving methods across representational contexts
