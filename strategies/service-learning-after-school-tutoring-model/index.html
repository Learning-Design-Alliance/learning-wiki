Run an after-school literacy tutoring program pairing an existing after-school site, a structured scripted curriculum, and service-learning college student tutors¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's implementable model combines four components, as the authors state: "The current study used a combination of an existing after-school program; a structured, scripted curriculum; minimally trained tutors; and tutor support to provide targeted reading instruction." College students enrolled in an elective service-learning course on illiteracy, received four 1-hour training sessions, and tutored twice weekly across one school year. The authors suggest this approach would be more feasible and cost effective than hiring all certified teachers for one-to-one instruction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tutors enroll in a course with an academic service-learning component and receive course credit
  • Sessions occur twice weekly across a school year, with missed sessions made up

Constraints¶

  • The program was not coordinated with each child's school instruction; the authors suggest alignment with classroom instruction would likely yield even stronger results
  • Sample sizes per grade were small, making effectiveness across all grade levels difficult to determine conclusively

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary students in Grades K–6 at risk for reading failure from low-income families

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Word recognition, decoding, fluency, and comprehension for struggling readers

Claims¶

  • Minimally trained, non-education-major college tutors implemented the structured program with high fidelity (average 96.3%) [+W]
  • Struggling K–6 readers tutored twice weekly by minimally trained college students made significantly greater gains than controls in letter-word identification, word attack, and passage comprehension [+W]
  • Gains were achieved with a relatively small tutoring dose: about 27 sessions averaging 25–45 minutes, roughly 15–19 hours across one school year [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Pair program adoption with two days of initial training and weekly coaching meetings
  • Intensive trainer-led observation and feedback model for tutoring professional development
  • Provide LLI interventionists with 3-day formal training including follow-up on-site coaching
