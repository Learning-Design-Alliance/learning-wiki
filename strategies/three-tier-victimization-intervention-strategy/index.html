Coordinate universal, selected, and indicated interventions to reduce chronic peer victimization involvement of students with disabilities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Building from the study's findings, the authors propose comprehensive, multi-factored programs with three coordinated layers. First, "universal strategies that focus on managing the general social dynamics of the classroom including peer norms, peer group processes, and social reinforcement for peer victimization and bullying behavior." Second, selected interventions enhancing the social competencies and skills of students with disabilities to reduce marginalization. Third, indicated interventions targeting the social functions of peer interactions for students chronically engaged in peer victimization. They recommend these operate as an integrative program designed in collaboration with teachers and school support personnel.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interventions should be developed in a coordinated manner and designed in collaboration with teachers, with strong linkage between teachers and school support personnel

Constraints¶

  • The authors state the SEALS universal approach alone is not fully sufficient for students with disabilities' chronic involvement

Target Learners¶

  • students with disabilities chronically involved in peer victimization
  • middle school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reducing and preventing peer victimization involvement
  • reducing social marginalization and vulnerability

Claims¶

  • Social marginalization in the peer network is associated with stable peer victimization involvement, and this association is amplified for students with disabilities [+W]
  • Students with disabilities and academically gifted students report perceiving more peer encouragement of bullying than other students, but within SEALS intervention schools they report perceiving less than nondisabled peers [~W]
  • The SEALS intervention showed no significant effect on peer victimization subtype placement, but students in intervention schools showed less variability in involvement over time [~W]
  • Students with disabilities are more likely than nondisabled peers to be nominated as victims and bully-victims and less likely to be noninvolved in peer victimization [+M]
  • Students with disabilities are predominantly socially marginalized in the sixth-grade peer network, while academically gifted students are mostly well integrated [+W]
  • Students with disabilities show more stable peer victimization involvement across fifth and sixth grade, whereas academically gifted students show less stable involvement [+W]
