# Avoid asking participants to arrive with 'clean' hair worn down; recommend against references to cleanliness in participant communications

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review explains that common pre-visit requests — hair clean and worn down — pose unanticipated burdens for Black and Latinx participants: washing, detangling, and styling can take hours, be uncomfortable or painful, and straightened or removed styles hold value and cost. Because of the racist history and stigma surrounding Black and Latinx hair, the authors state “we recommend against references to cleanliness in participant communications,” and urge offering styling options that work for both EEG and family confidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants should be offered options around how to style their hair that make them feel confident as well as work well for EEG collection.
#### Constraints
- Families will likely need to wash hair twice — before the recording and after to remove conductive media such as saline or gel — which may be particularly burdensome for Black and Latinx participants.

### Target Learners
- Black and/or Latinx children and families in pediatric EEG research

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing participant burden and avoiding race-related bias in who consents to and completes EEG data collection
