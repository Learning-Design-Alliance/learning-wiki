# Quantum mechanics instruction should explicitly address experimental limitations in quantum measurement as distinct from uncertainty arising from quantum principles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the better-data results, the authors recommend that theoretical quantum mechanics instruction "more explicitly address the experimental limitations in quantum mechanical measurement as distinct from the uncertainty resulting from the principles of quantum mechanics." Students who expected distributions to stay the same largely did so based on physical principles with little consideration of experimental limitations. The authors add that while an experiment may reach a "quantum limit", students should be provided with experience in analyzing when that limit might be reached.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction that separates principle-based uncertainty from limitation-based uncertainty in quantum measurement
- Student experience analyzing when a quantum limit might be reached
#### Constraints
- Motivated by the prevalence of Principles-coded reasoning in this study's better-data results; the recommendation is further supported by the authors' companion work on sources of uncertainty, not by a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduate physics students in quantum mechanics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguishing sources of measurement uncertainty in quantum experiments
