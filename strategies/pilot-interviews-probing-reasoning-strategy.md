# Use pilot interviews probing final, second-best, and considered responses to build evidentiary arguments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The SPRUCE team's piloting strategy combined three interview rounds with full-class beta pilots. Interviewers prompted students "to provide reasoning supporting their final responses as well as other answers they considered," and later rounds explicitly asked for reasoning behind a "'second-best' response as well." This approach gathered evidence of student reasoning to link responses to reasoning for each answer option of each item. Interviewers also asked targeted follow-up questions about why students did or did not select specific answer options.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to students who have completed an introductory physics lab with a measurement uncertainty component
- Video- and audio-recorded sessions with screen-sharing for observing item completion
#### Constraints
- Interview rounds had low participant numbers (9, 3, and 27 students), which the authors note but found provided valuable reasoning data

### Target Learners
- introductory physics lab students

### Target Learning Goals
- measurement uncertainty concepts and practices
