# Use standardized licensure-style examinations (e.g., NAPLEX, MPJE, or Chinese Pharmacy License Examination) to evaluate PBL effectiveness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (8 for, 1 against) · 5 studies (5 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 5 of 5 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adopting existing standardized examinations as outcome measures for PBL research, since the included trials lacked uniform theoretical tests. It notes "existing standard examinations, such as the North American Pharmacist Licensure Examination (NAPLEX) and the Multistate Pharmacy Jurisprudence Examination (MPJE) in the United States, or corresponding examinations in other countries, could be used as evaluation tests", and specifically suggests "a standard examination such as the Chinese Pharmacy License Examination could be adopted as the standard examination for PBL pedagogy evaluation" in China.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to standardized examination instruments or comparable licensure-style tests
- Alignment of examination content with PBL curriculum objectives
#### Constraints
- Before a standard evaluation system of PBL pedagogy is established, existing examinations serve as interim measures; the article notes no unified criteria currently exist

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate pharmacy students in Chinese pharmacy programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Theoretical pharmacy knowledge assessment
- Standardized evaluation of PBL pedagogy effectiveness
