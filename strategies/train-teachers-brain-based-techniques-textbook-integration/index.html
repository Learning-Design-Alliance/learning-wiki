Train science teachers to teach brain-based learning techniques and integrate them into science textbooks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the mental group's longer-lasting retention advantage, the authors recommend that science teachers be trained in the four brain-based learning techniques and that these methods be integrated into science textbooks. The stated aim is "helping students to make more connections with different aspects of the scientific concepts under study and to construct more associations with their everyday experiences". The recommendation rests on the postponed-test advantage of the mental group over the control group.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher training in the four brain-based learning techniques adopted by the current study
  • Integration of the recommended teaching methods into science textbooks

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is grounded in one quasi-experimental study with 197 grade-eight students in Muscat; the technology group's short-term-only advantage suggests the no-technology delivery drove the lasting effect

Target Learners¶

  • grade eight science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • science achievement
  • postponed retention
  • making connections between science concepts and everyday experience

Affordances¶

  • Brain Based Learning Techniques Cardellichio Field

Claims¶

  • The mental group outperformed the technology group in reasoning on the postponed test, with no other significant postponed differences [+W]
  • Brain-based learning without technology produced longer-lasting retention: the mental group outperformed the control group on the postponed test [+W]
  • The short-lived advantage of technology-delivered instruction may reflect a novelty effect, though further research is needed [+W]
