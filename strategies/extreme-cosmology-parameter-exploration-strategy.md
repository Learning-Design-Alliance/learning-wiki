# Explore extreme cosmologies by varying energy-density parameters in the visualization

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
AHaH lets students form an approximate image of any cosmology by varying parameters such as ΩM, ΩR, ΩΛ, and ΩK instead of relying on "verbal and written descriptions (which can be obtuse)". The article's example raises the vacuum energy density ΩΛ from 0.763 to 2.0, "which of course results in a Universe rapidly pushed outwards until just a few galaxies hypothetically remain in Hubble's field-of-view", chosen because higher values make all galaxies disappear from view. This targets the established difficulty students have with energy-density parameters and how different portions dominate cosmic behavior over time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The tool must compute angular sizes and comoving distances under the user-selected cosmological parameters
#### Constraints
- The article states this change in parameters "does not produce a perfect representation of a Universe with these parameters, and what is simulated by the software has no physical analogue"

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory astronomy and cosmology students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding cosmological energy-density parameters and their effects on cosmic structure
