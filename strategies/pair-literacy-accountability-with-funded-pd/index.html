Pair literacy accountability indicators with funded professional development and aligned mandates¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

From the Ohio case study, the paper recommends treating literacy accountability as part of a broader ecosystem: indicators are most effective when paired with aligned mandates such as dyslexia screening, funding, and instructional guidance. Its policy implication is explicit: "Fund and require literacy-focused PD. Ohio's success was catalyzed by a state policy that not only required PD but also paid teachers to complete it." It further recommends prioritizing data literacy and infrastructure, balancing rigor with flexibility in screener selection, leveraging public reporting, and evaluating for impact across subgroups.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • State policy that both requires and pays for literacy-focused PD
  • Tools and training such as data warehouses, visualization platforms, and designated literacy/data coaches

Constraints¶

  • Some Ohio educators worried proposed budget cuts might limit ongoing access to PD and instructional materials, threatening the durability of current gains

Target Learners¶

  • elementary educators
  • state education agencies

Target Learning Goals¶

  • early literacy outcomes and instructional change in K-3 reading

Related Strategies¶

  • Pair technology access with targeted investments in infrastructure, staffing, and support, and pilot initiatives with dedicated budgets
  • Invest in educator professional learning on experiential AI pedagogies using existing federal funding streams
  • Invest in dedicated instructional technology leadership roles and role-specific professional learning for AI literacy
