# Adaptive feedback designs that tailor feedback elaboration and representational format to learner profiles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that ITSs and adaptive learning environments should adjust both the elaboration level of feedback and the external-representation formats offered, based on learner profiles such as representational competence. The authors state the findings "motivate adaptive feedback designs and inform intelligent tutoring systems capable of tailoring feedback elaboration and representational format to learner profiles". This builds on their observation that elaborated multirepresentational feedback use related to higher post-test scores and that the benefit of diverse representations varied with competence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A platform capable of delivering verification and elaborated feedback in multiple representational formats, with logging of learner feedback-selection behavior
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is observational, so the authors state no causal relation between feedback use and learning gains can be inferred

### Target Learners
- High-school physics students (grades 10-11)

### Target Learning Goals
- Kinematics conceptual understanding
- Representational competence across verbal, graphical and mathematical formats
