# Do not expect interventions targeting self-control and grit alone to produce large gains in school performance, given the small causal effect

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article argues that interventions designed to enhance self-control and grit to improve school performance rest on a causal assumption that the twin analysis only weakly supports: the causal effect size is "4.4% of variance, a multiple correlation of 0.21", suggesting "an intervention is likely to have a relatively small effect in the population studied here". The article recommends well-designed intervention research, "including, where possible, randomized controlled trials", and notes that observational causal studies and intervention studies answer distinct questions ("what is" versus "what could be"), so findings may contrast.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Well-designed intervention research, including randomized controlled trials where possible, to establish what works in educational programmes
#### Constraints
- The effect estimate applies to 11.5–12.5-year-old children in the Netherlands; randomized controlled trials are in practice partially compromised by volunteer bias and selective dropout

### Target Learners
- Primary-school-age children (around 12 years old)

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving school performance (grades) via non-cognitive skills
