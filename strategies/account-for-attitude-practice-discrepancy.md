# Account for the attitude–practice discrepancy when supporting students' transition from traditional teaching to peer-to-peer learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy addresses the transition from traditional lecture-based teaching to peer-to-peer learning by explicitly taking into account the gap between what students say about collaboration and what they actually do. The authors recommend it directly: "In order to help with the transition from traditional teaching to peer-to-peer learning this discrepancy should be taken into account." Because students may value teamwork without practicing it, instructors should not assume stated attitudes will translate into collaborative behavior, particularly during students' first semester in an active-engagement curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A curriculum in which collaborative work is promoted or incorporated, so that the attitude–practice gap can be observed and addressed
#### Constraints
- The authors' evidence comes from one institution's introductory Modeling Instruction physics sequence; they note it may take basically an entire semester before students develop the practice of reaching out to peers

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory physics students new to active-engagement, peer-to-peer curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- developing the practice of out-of-class collaboration with peers
