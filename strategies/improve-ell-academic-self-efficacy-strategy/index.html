Find ways to improve academic self-efficacy among ELLs to support faster academic growth and gap closing¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's forward-looking recommendation is that interventions targeting ELLs' academic self-efficacy—their confidence in attaining educational goals—may accelerate math and reading growth and help close achievement gaps with non-ELLs. It grounds this in its finding that 5th-grade self-efficacy is associated with achievement growth and that ELLs' low self-efficacy indirectly reduces growth. The authors state that "finding ways to improve academic self-efficacy among ELLs could also lead to greater academic growth for those students, including closing achievement gaps with non-ELLs."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires valid measures of academic self-efficacy that can be administered longitudinally; the article used a four-item Likert-scale survey with demonstrated longitudinal partial strong measurement invariance.

Constraints¶

  • The supporting analyses are not causal: the article states the models are likelihood-equivalent rescalings and that findings are commensurate with theory rather than causal proof.
  • Evidence comes from one large urban California district serving a high proportion of low-income ELL students; the article says findings should be replicated in other settings and with other students, including younger elementary grades.

Target Learners¶

  • English language learners in middle school grades 5-8

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Academic self-efficacy
  • Math and reading achievement growth
  • Closing achievement gaps between ELLs and non-ELLs
