# Use expert teachers' gaze sequences, tailored to cultural context, as a template in professional development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that professional development programmes attend to the cognitive models of expert teachers as revealed by their gaze sequences. Because "the order in which teachers look at differing regions of the classroom matters", novices can go beyond increasing eye contact to consider "the order in which they do things in classroom teaching". The article also stresses that the optimal model must take culture into consideration, since expertise manifests differently across cultural settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to expert teachers' gaze sequences or sequential patterns from the same cultural setting as the novices being trained
#### Constraints
- Expert teachers do not develop a universally uniform cognitive model regardless of culture; the model must take culture into consideration
- Post hoc power for some analyses was below the conventional standard

### Target Learners
- novice secondary school teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- developing expert-like, culture-appropriate classroom gaze and attentional strategies

### Affordances
- Scanpath Theory Teacher Cognitive Models
