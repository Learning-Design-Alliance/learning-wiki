# Build shared adult capacity across disciplines, systems, and settings to support whole child development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends empowering the adults who work with children and youth across systems and settings by giving them a deeper understanding of young people's assets and communities, a common understanding of what good conditions for learning look like, appreciation of role and setting variation, and "Regular opportunities to connect across disciplines, systems and settings." It argues adults are the climate-setters who create identity-safe, developmentally rich experiences in every setting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Shared accountability for student well-being across all adults connected to school
- Regular cross-discipline and cross-setting connection opportunities
#### Constraints
- The article notes increased staff numbers alone are insufficient: better-integrated support takes more than increased numbers

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in school, afterschool, and community settings

### Target Learning Goals
- student health and well-being
- social, emotional, cognitive, and physical development
