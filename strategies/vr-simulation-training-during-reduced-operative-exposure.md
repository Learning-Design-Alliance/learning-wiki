# Use VR surgical simulators to expand training opportunities when operative exposure is reduced, as during the COVID-19 pandemic

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends VR simulators as a substitute training venue when residents' operative experience is curtailed. It states that the pandemic forced surgical residents to see fewer cases through redeployment and reduced elective surgery, and that "surgical simulators, such as the VR simulator, have the enormous potential to increase the opportunity for training instead of an actual operating theater." The recommendation rests on the simulator's demonstrated ability to discriminate skill levels.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated simulator that can detect the operator's dissection ability
- Familiarization time with instruments and simulator before assessed performance
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is grounded in a single-center validation of one procedural scene, and the article recommends a larger sample size for further confirmation

### Target Learners
- surgical residents
- laparoscopic surgery trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining and improving surgical dissection skills when operating-room exposure is limited
