# Combine knowledge-based BLS instruction with hands-on and simulation-based training.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article states that 'These findings support combining knowledge-based instruction with hands-on and simulation-based training.' This recommendation arises because knowledge-based competency was assessed by questionnaire alone, and the authors note that formal education alone may not ensure effective practical BLS performance. The strategy addresses the multidimensional nature of overall clinical BLS competency that a knowledge assessment alone cannot fully capture.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation-based education and objective performance assessment, consistent with resuscitation education guidelines cited by the authors
#### Constraints
- The article notes that psychomotor skills, simulation-based performance, and actual clinical resuscitation performance were not assessed in this study

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals with direct clinical or patient-care responsibilities

### Target Learning Goals
- BLS knowledge and practical resuscitation performance
