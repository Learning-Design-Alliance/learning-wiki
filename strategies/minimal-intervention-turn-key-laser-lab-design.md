# Design the ultrafast lab for minimal intervention: pre-aligned optics, magnetic switching, automation, and layered safety enclosures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends making an advanced laser experiment implementable in a teaching lab by automating measurements, pre-aligning optics, and enclosing the beam path. Students "simply turn on the electronics, execute the LabVIEW control software, and move a single magnetically mounted mirror from one position to another while the laser beam is blocked to access the different measurements." Layered engineered safety controls (a primary enclosure with an interlocked shutter and a secondary enclosure) plus a no-alignment procedure increase safety and repeatability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires an existing mode-locked femtosecond laser, or purchase of a Ti:Sapphire laser with adequate specifications; additional startup costs kept under $20,000.
- Experiments must be considerably robust and turn-key because the instructor oversees multiple simultaneous experimental options.
#### Constraints
- Student alignment of the intense optical pulses would require close supervision due to potential retinal damage and skin burns; the experiment as configured involves minimal student intervention, though it can be adapted to involve increased assembly and alignment where time allows.

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate laboratory students

### Target Learning Goals
- performing and interpreting ultrafast pump-probe and autocorrelation measurements
