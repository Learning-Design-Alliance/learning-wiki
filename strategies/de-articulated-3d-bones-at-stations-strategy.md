# Provide small groups with de-articulated 3D printed bone models at each learning station during clinical skills practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes placing a 3D printed clavicle, scapula, and humerus beside each massage table so student pairs could pick up, rotate, and place models on themselves or partners during palpation practice. Students reported that "having a set by your side is more time efficient, instead of waiting for the instructor to come by". The models were printed in ASA plastic from high-resolution digitized files shared by the Parametric Human Project.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- One set of de-articulated 3D printed bones per learning station; no prior training was given in this case, though the authors suggest explicit guidance would improve use
#### Constraints
- Effects were likely dampened by lack of orientation and preparation; findings come from one cohort and one instructor and are not intended for generalization

### Target Learners
- Massage Therapy students
- health professions students in clinical skills courses

### Target Learning Goals
- palpation of bony landmarks
- anatomy visualization
- cognitive integration
