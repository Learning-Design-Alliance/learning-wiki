# Use near-peer guides rather than faculty to demonstrate that authoritative bias arises even with limited authority

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends selecting guides whose authority is limited but real. Senior medical students were chosen "due to their relative similarity to the cohort of participants", so that participants would see that "authoritative bias, and halo effect could occur even if the authority of the person's power is significantly limited". This makes the bias experience more transferable to situations with modest power gradients.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Guides who hold some authority over participants but are similar in status and experience
#### Constraints
- Demonstrated with senior medical students guiding first-year students; generalization to other guide types is not tested

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing authoritative bias and the halo effect in hierarchical relationships
