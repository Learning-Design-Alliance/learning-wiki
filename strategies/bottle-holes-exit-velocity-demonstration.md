# Bottle-with-holes demonstration showing exit velocity depends on pressure, not hole cross-section

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A quick physical demonstration to address the student question of whether partial blocking (as with a hose) contradicts Bernoulli's principle. The article instructs: "fill a plastic container (for example, a 5 L family-size detergent bottle) and drill several holes with different cross-sections at the same height". The range of the jets — hence the exit velocity — is the same for all holes, and may even be shorter for smaller holes due to friction, while range decreases with water height, i.e. pressure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A plastic container with holes drilled at the same height
#### Constraints
- In constant-flow-rate systems, mass conservation must be considered alongside energy conservation; reducing the exit cross-section raises base pressure and thus exit velocity, which does not contradict Bernoulli's principle

### Target Learners
- third-year Bachelor physics students
- secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguishing constant-pressure from constant-flow-rate systems
- applying energy and mass conservation to incompressible fluids
