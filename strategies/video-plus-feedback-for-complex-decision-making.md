# Pair e-learning video with embedded feedback and debriefing to target complex decision-making knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because recall and comprehension were well served by one-way video but complex decision-making topics (treatment, transport) were not, the authors recommend platforms that assess understanding and collect immediate feedback, and future inclusion of debriefing in a synchronous mode. They suggest "An e-learning video platform that can assess the participants' understanding and collect their immediate feedback", naming ED-Puzzle or Common Ground scholar, the latter supporting recursive feedback from peers and instructors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A platform able to assess understanding and collect immediate feedback while viewing
- Synchronous mode for debriefing, since debriefing was not suitable for asynchronous teaching
#### Constraints
- The study's one-way Screencast-O-Matic videos could not provide these higher-order supports; the recommendation is forward-looking, not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- higher-order decision-making in disaster response medicine (treatment and transport decisions)

## Claims

- [DRM knowledge scores rose from pre-test to post-test after e-learning in one group with no comparison group; recall and comprehension were reported as comparatively stronger than complex decision-making knowledge afterward](../claims/e-learning-serves-recall-not-complex-decisions.md) [+W]
- [Knowledge scores rose from pre-test to post-test after the videos for principles, decontamination and triage, but did not show a statistically significant change for treatment (p = 0.029) or transport (p = 0.180), in one uncontrolled pre-post study](../claims/drm-complex-decision-knowledge-not-improved.md) [~W]
- [Students endorsed e-learning as an adjunct to face-to-face DRM teaching (PS +1.12) but disagreed that it should replace F2F sessions (PS −0.36)](../claims/e-learning-adjunct-not-replacement-drm.md) [~W]
