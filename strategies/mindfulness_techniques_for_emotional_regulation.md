# Mindfulness Techniques for Emotional Regulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 7 studies (4 review, 2 theoretical, 1 quant-synthesis), `q1`–`q4` · 1 of 7 report an effect size

## Description
Mindfulness techniques for emotional regulation teach students to notice their emotional and physiological states non-judgmentally in the moment, creating a pause between stimulus and reaction. Practices include focused breathing, body scans, brief meditation, and mindful observation of thoughts. The mechanism is improved attentional control and interoceptive awareness, which weakens automatic reactivity and supports deliberate self-regulation [~S].

## Design Implications

Mindfulness training functions as a self-regulation support: by strengthening attention monitoring and awareness of present-moment states, it gives learners the raw material for [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) — you cannot regulate an emotion you have not noticed [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]. Effects are dose-dependent: brief one-off exercises produce short-lived calm, while sustained practice over weeks is what shifts trait-level reactivity [~S].

### Instructions
1. Establish the rationale briefly — mindfulness as a trainable skill, not a compliance tool ([Direct Instruction](../elements/direct-instruction.md))
2. Model a short practice (2–5 minutes of breath-focused attention), narrating what to do when the mind wanders ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))
3. Guide repeated practice in low-stakes settings, gradually shortening prompts as students internalize the routine ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
4. Prompt application at the point of need — before assessments, after conflict, during transitions — so the skill transfers to genuine emotional situations ([Application](../elements/application.md))
5. Debrief with brief reflection or [check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md): what did you notice, when did you use it, what changed?

## Description
This strategy embeds short, structured mindfulness practices — focused breathing, body scans, brief meditative pauses before demanding tasks — into instruction to help students notice their emotional and attentional states and regulate initial impulses. The mechanism is improved metacognitive awareness: students learn to observe reactivity as it arises rather than being carried by it, creating a pause in which a deliberate regulatory response becomes possible.
