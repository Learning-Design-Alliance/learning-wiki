# Have a physician present during peer-led simulation to monitor accuracy and correct mistakes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
During student-led sessions, one physician remains present to safeguard learning quality while peer teachers lead. The article states "a physician was present during the student-led simulations to answer questions, monitor what was being taught, correct mistakes, and to provide additional explanations." This monitoring role is credited with ensuring standardized delivery of basic instructional components and addressing the risk that peer teachers provide incomplete information, at one-third the physician time of fully physician-led sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Physician volunteers willing to serve in a monitoring rather than leading role
#### Constraints
- The article notes students may still perceive peer teaching as less good than physician-led, which could affect their general perception of simulation

### Target Learners
- fourth-year medical students in emergency medicine clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate acquisition of emergency management content
- comfort asking questions in a low-threat environment
