# Co-develop classroom mental health activities with both teachers and young people before embedding them in routine lessons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's development process shows a strategy of co-designing intervention activities through sequential workshops: teachers identify priorities and co-create activities, young people review and adapt them, teachers refine for feasibility, and a new teacher group validates the refined programme. The study "brought together teachers and young people to co-develop activities that can be integrated directly into routine teaching". This addresses acceptability barriers for teacher-led interventions and aligns with young people's preferences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sequential workshops with both teacher and young person groups
- Advisory panel input on workshop design and measures
#### Constraints
- Developed and evaluated in a Scottish secondary school context

### Target Learners
- secondary school pupils
- secondary school teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- mental health and wellbeing support
- emotion regulation

- [Flexible thinking and problem-solving activities were perceived as feasible and beneficial through co-regulation, playfulness, characterisation and low-stakes whole-class design](../claims/flexible-thinking-low-stakes-design.md) [+W]
