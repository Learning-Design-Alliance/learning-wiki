Automatic enrollment into advanced coursework with an opt-out option¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The brief recommends automatic enrollment policies with an opt-out option, "so students identified through any measure can be enrolled without having to opt in." This removes the opt-in barrier that can keep eligible Black and Latino students out of advanced courses, and pairs with multiple-measures identification rather than reliance on a single test score or teacher recommendation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students identified through any measure can be enrolled without opting in; identification should use multiple measures rather than one measure only

Constraints¶

  • The brief warns that enrollment alone is insufficient — students need support and high-quality teaching to be successful and feel they belong

Target Learners¶

  • Black and Latino students eligible for advanced coursework

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Equitable enrollment in AP, IB, dual-enrollment, and other advanced courses

Related Strategies¶

  • Adopt automatic (opt-out) enrollment policies placing qualifying students in advanced coursework
  • Adopt automatic enrollment and open enrollment policies for advanced courses to expand equitable access
  • State policy package to expand equitable access to advanced coursework: credit transparency, automatic enrollment with opt-out, fee removal, disaggregated data reporting, and belonging supports
  • Adopt automatic-enrollment policies that default students into higher-level math based on prior achievement or demonstrated proficiency
