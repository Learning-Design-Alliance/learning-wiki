# Adopt gamification-based training as a cost-effective approach to preventing adolescent addiction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that policymakers and social and health planners "use gamification-based training as an attractive, valuable, and cost-effective approach to preventing adolescent addiction". The strategy applies game elements (points, badges, rewards, feedback) to health-prevention content delivered via mobile application, with facilitator support through a messaging group. The authors argue prevention training during adolescence is cost-effective and superior to treatment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adolescents need smartphone access
- Content should cover addiction definitions, risks, drug types, internet addiction, and prevention activities
#### Constraints
- The study's follow-up was short, so long-term effects require further investigation
- Cultural considerations limited inclusion to one gender

### Target Learners
- male adolescents aged 13–15

### Target Learning Goals
- prevention of substance and internet addiction
- knowledge and attitudes about addiction
