# Teach non-technical skills formally in surgical oncology education using structured modules, checklists, briefings, and bad-news communication training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that surgical oncology education formally incorporate non-technical skills training, arguing these skills "are now known to have the same magnitude of impact on a surgical outcome as surgical technique" yet were traditionally omitted. It points to existing models: the ACS-APDS Surgical Skills Curriculum, ACGME Core Competencies, preoperative checklists and team briefings, and structured bad-news training such as S-P-I-K-E-S. The authors conclude that evidence-based training of non-technical skills will produce surgeons competent in the skills necessary for effective patient treatment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formal curricular slots and dedicated training modules, as in the ACS-APDS Surgical Skills Curriculum and ACGME competencies
#### Constraints
- Copying aviation solutions by simply changing the word pilot into surgeon is not sufficient, per the authors; non-technical skills training must be built on surgical-specific evidence

### Target Learners
- surgical oncologists and surgical trainees/residents

### Target Learning Goals
- communication with patients and colleagues
- teamwork
- situation awareness
- decision-making
- leadership
- coping with stress
- disclosure of medical errors
