Develop high-retention pathways — Grow Your Own programs and teacher residencies — to build and diversify local teacher supply¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report describes GYO programs that "recruit local community members into teaching, including high school students, paraprofessionals, after-school program staff, and others," often using state funding for tuition reimbursement as classified staff earn credentials. Teacher residencies are "1-year intensive apprenticeships modeled on medical residency programs" in which residents apprentice with an expert teacher for a full academic year, then receive 2 years of mentoring and typically a stipend and tuition assistance in exchange for a 3- to 4-year teaching commitment in a high-need subject and location. District leaders noted both approaches were important for recruiting more teachers of color.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • State funding such as the Classified School Employee Credentialing Program for tuition reimbursement; district–university partnerships for residencies

Constraints¶

  • Well-established programs are needed to anticipate new-teacher supply; at least one district was still working to launch a new residency program

Target Learners¶

  • Classified school employees, substitutes, paraprofessionals, and local community members becoming teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Full teaching credentials; retention in high-need subjects and schools; a more diverse teacher workforce

Related Strategies¶

  • Grow-your-own teacher training programs targeting community members facing certification and career-change barriers
  • Build high-retention, supportive pathways into teaching for candidates of color
  • Establish high-retention preparation pathways—residencies, Grow Your Own models, service scholarships, and mentoring/induction—to stem turnover in high-need subjects and schools
  • Grow-your-own and recruitment initiatives: alternative programs, Minority Serving Institutions, high-school pipeline programs, and district human-capital efforts to diversify the teacher workforce.
  • Offer targeted service scholarships, loan forgiveness, and 1-year postbaccalaureate residency programs to rapidly expand the supply of well-prepared teachers in shortage fields and locations
  • Deploy service scholarships, loan forgiveness, and teacher residencies to staff high-need fields and schools
  • State and federal policy recommendations: recruit and prepare more candidates, retain new teachers through early-career mentoring, and improve working conditions
  • Build an after-school/out-of-school-time pipeline into teaching to diversify the teacher workforce
  • Recruit classified school employees into teacher credentialing with braided funds plus individualized financial and non-financial supports
  • Build a diverse teacher pipeline through Grow Your Own grants, teacher academies, scholarships, MSI investment, and residency pathways
  • Diversify the educator workforce through Grow Your Own programs, scholarships, and retention supports
  • Build diverse school leader pipelines through HBCU/HSI preparation partnerships, transparent diversity data, equitable hiring supports, affinity networks, and Grow Your Own teacher pathways
  • Form partnerships with community organizations, higher education, and CTE programs to expand emergency staffing and build the talent pipeline
  • Retain teachers of color through leadership pipelines, multi-year induction and mentoring, and culturally responsive school climates
  • Districts respond to shortages with teacher preparation and pathway strategies, especially partnerships coordinating student teaching and residency placements
  • Use Teacher Residency Grant Program funds, braided with other sources, to recruit and retain a diverse teaching workforce
