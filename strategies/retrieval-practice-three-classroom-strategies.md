# Implement retrieval practice in class through everything-you-know recall, multi-representation flashcards, and practice test questions that differ from the actual test

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes three concrete retrieval-practice routines a math teacher used: a student tells a peer or teacher everything they know about an area to be assessed, flashcards containing multiple representations such as a definition and an image, and practice test packets. Practice questions were deliberately "structured in a similar way to each upcoming test, but not exactly the same" so students still had to apply knowledge in new situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must engage in the effortful retrieval the article calls desirable difficulties, which the article notes students may initially resist
#### Constraints
- The article notes some students wanted extra credit for participating, suggesting an incentive challenge during adoption

### Target Learners
- high school students in academic courses

### Target Learning Goals
- retention and recall of assessed material
- applying knowledge in different types of situations
