# Use residency program prescribing data formatively, with audit and feedback, communication coaching, or simulation-based antibiotic prescribing training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that residency programs use their graduates' prescribing data as formative self-assessment rather than summative evaluation. It states that "Programs whose graduates prescribe at higher rates may find prescribing data useful for formative self-assessment and program improvement, for example by incorporating audit and feedback, communication coaching, or simulation-based training in antibiotic prescribing." This targets stewardship-relevant prescribing habits during the formative residency window.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Program-level prescribing data linked to graduates, as the study constructed from Medicare Part D claims and residency records
#### Constraints
- The authors state these data are not intended for summative program evaluation or accreditation given the absence of appropriateness data and potential case-mix confounding
- Claims data capture prescribing rates rather than clinical appropriateness

### Target Learners
- Internal medicine and family medicine residents
- Early-career primary care physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- Guideline-concordant antibiotic prescribing
- Evidence-based decision-making
- Patient communication
- System navigation
