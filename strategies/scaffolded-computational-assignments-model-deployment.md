# Replace some Model Deployment worksheets with scaffolded computational assignments that follow in-class experiments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Leveraging MI's two-stage design, the instructor "replaced some of the worksheet activities that would take place during the 'Model Deployment' stage with computational assignments" that followed in-class experiments and problem-solving sessions. Students were not asked to write programs from scratch: "They were given scaffolded code" with computational components like class declarations and imports pre-written, so students focused on the missing physics, such as the Euler-Cromer integration of Newton's 2nd law. Students worked collaboratively in small groups and presented problems on whiteboards.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need prior experience or scaffolding; given the cohort's computing background, scaffolded code with pre-written computational components is used and students fill in the physics parts.
#### Constraints
- Data collection in the revised assignments is done exclusively via video cameras or smartphone cameras and analyzed in Tracker.

### Target Learners
- ninth-grade high school physics students with little or no programming background

### Target Learning Goals
- constructing computational models of constant velocity and other force and motion models
- connecting computational models to the physics they represent
