# Design recommendations for longitudinal neuroimaging studies of reading development and dyslexia

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review proposes concrete practices to improve longitudinal neuroimaging research on dyslexia. To counter attrition, "families should receive feedback on children's performance whenever possible and should be informed about the outcomes of the study". It recommends collecting data on instruction amount and type, print exposure, and interventions, and urges multi-site collaboration: "Combining data from different laboratories in a multi-site approach is a good solution for increasing sample sizes and, as a result, statistical power and reliability of findings", with attention to generalization across languages and instructional strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recruitment enriched with children at familial risk of dyslexia, since only about 10% of children are eventually diagnosed
- Following children for at least 2 or 3 years until a dyslexia diagnosis is possible
#### Constraints
- Familial-risk enrichment only raises the chance of a diagnosed case to about 40%; neuroimaging of young pre-readers imposes recruitment, compliance and head-movement constraints; multi-site pooling must address language-generalizability

### Target Learners
- pre-readers and beginning readers
- children at familial risk for dyslexia

### Target Learning Goals
- early identification of dyslexia risk
- understanding typical and atypical reading neurodevelopment
