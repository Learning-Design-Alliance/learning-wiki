Prioritise randomised controlled trials of existing promising hernia repair models and involve educationalists in simulation development¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's authors recommend shifting research effort from describing novel unvalidated models to robust evaluation of existing ones. They state: "The recommendation of the authors of this review is that future research is focused on randomised controlled trials assessing existing promising hernia repair models for their educational value, and transferability of skills to the operating room." They further cite frameworks stressing the importance of involving educationalists in developing and assessing simulation programmes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Future studies should use robust methodologies such as the Schout framework for developing and assessing simulation models

Constraints¶

  • Further studies solely describing novel but unvalidated simulation models are unlikely to benefit trainees or patients, per the review

Target Learners¶

  • surgical trainees in open and laparoscopic hernia repair

Target Learning Goals¶

  • demonstrating educational impact and transfer of simulated skills to the operating room

Claims¶

  • A simulation-based mastery-learning curriculum using the Guildford MATTU TEP model produced faster TEP repair, higher GOALS scores and fewer complications than standard apprenticeship training [+W]
  • Simulation-based training with the McGill Laparoscopic Inguinal Hernia Simulator improved operating-room GOALS-GH scores by a mean of 3.4 points in a randomised trial [+W]
  • A moulded rubber TEP box trainer programme produced higher composite operative scores (P = 0.01) and suggested transfer of skills to real operations [+W]
