Use lightweight transparency cues about AI fallibility to encourage more cautious, help-seeking interaction strategies¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends carefully designing how uncertainty is communicated in AI-supported learning environments, concluding that "even minimal transparency cues about AI fallibility can meaningfully shape learners' interaction strategies." A simple popup warning that a pedagogical agent may make mistakes led students to request more hints, potentially fostering verification and reflection, without impairing error rate or time on task.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A warning message displayed at introduction points in the tutoring system
  • a pedagogical agent whose guidance learners read

Constraints¶

  • The tested system contained no actual errors
  • the study was conducted in a specific setting in Japan and findings may not generalize across contexts
  • increased help-seeking alone does not necessarily reflect metacognitive engagement

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school students using AI-based tutoring systems

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical and metacognitive engagement with AI-provided guidance

Related Strategies¶

  • Use predicted hint-taking likelihood and hint effects to adaptively decide whether to withhold or provide hints
