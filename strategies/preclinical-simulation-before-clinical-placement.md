# Schedule preclinical simulation sessions in the first weeks of the semester, before clinical placement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that simulation precede real clinical practice: the majority of students agreed that "Simulations should always precede the real clinical practice (m = 3.95, SD = 0.95)", and the course placed simulation sessions "during the first 3 weeks of each semester prior to the clinical placement". The strategy is to teach core procedures in the simulation lab before students encounter real patients, so they enter placements with practiced basic skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must be trained on simulation delivery, and clinical scenarios and learning resources must be prepared before sessions
#### Constraints
- The authors note the simulated maternity experience was part of a required course, so there was no concurrent control group in the study design

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students beginning clinical placements

### Target Learning Goals
- basic maternity nursing skills prior to real clinical practice
