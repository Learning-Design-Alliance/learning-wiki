# Progressively integrate haptic simulators from basic skills to complex surgical tasks across the curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends a staged curricular integration of haptic virtual simulators so learners gain familiarity with the technology over time before tackling complex procedures. It proposes starting "with basic skill (eg, knot tying) at early stages of curricular training followed by progression to complex surgical tasks (eg, tumor resection in a haptic-enabled VS) at later stages". Curricular integration should be complemented with hands-on simulator practice prior to training sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hands-on practice with the simulators prior to training sessions, as the review states curricular integration should always be complemented with hands-on practice
#### Constraints
- The review grounds this in LeBlanc and colleagues' finding that residents found the virtual simulator needed further improvement and took longer to complete tasks due to its novelty

### Target Learners
- medical residents
- medical students
- novices in surgical training

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical technical skills
- familiarity with haptic virtual simulation technology
