# Use focus groups with current students to triangulate and verify suspected curriculum weaknesses identified from graduate interviews

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study used a two-phase design in which suspected curriculum weaknesses identified from interviews with newly qualified nurses were checked with volunteering fourth-year students in focus groups, performed by student collaborators rather than the researchers "in order to minimize bias". This triangulation strategy verifies whether perceived weaknesses still occur for current students before recommending curricular change.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to both recent graduates and current late-stage students in the same program
- Group facilitators independent of the participants' teachers to minimize bias
#### Constraints
- The authors note participants' views on their own responsibility for learning were not explicitly explored and could generate biased results

### Target Learners
- newly qualified nurses
- fourth-year undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating competency achievement of a university professional program
