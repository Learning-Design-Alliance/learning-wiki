# Use paired typical and atypical assessment questions to diagnose knowledge integration levels

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes assessments containing a mixture of typical questions, solvable with memorization in familiar coursework contexts, and atypical questions requiring integrated thinking around the central idea in unfamiliar contexts. Comparing performance across the two question types allows students' levels of knowledge integration to be empirically modeled, and results can inform instruction targeting specific conceptual pathways and missing links.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions must be designed from the conceptual framework so that atypical items require use of the central idea rather than memorized procedures.
#### Constraints
- The article states that assigning students to levels usually requires careful interviews in addition to the assessments.
