Strategies

Incorporate gardening and entrepreneurship education into secondary curricula with the teacher acting at a supervisory, guided-inquiry level¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that gardening exposure improved entrepreneurial attitudes, the authors recommend that entrepreneurship education be incorporated fully into secondary school curricula as a core course rather than only as crosscutting concepts, and that teaching be designed so the teacher works at the supervisory or guidance level. They describe a guided inquiry method in which "the teacher goes round the class to ask if they need help", encouraging listening, participation, respect for others' opinions, team spirit and trust toward business productivity.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training and retraining of science teachers in Entrepreneurship Education through short and long term courses, seminars, and workshops

Constraints¶

  • Findings come from a non-randomized two-class quasi-experiment in one Nigerian locality; the article states conclusions concern attitudes, not measured skill outcomes

Target Learners¶

  • Senior secondary school science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Entrepreneurial skills acquisition attitudes
  • self-reliance and job creation

Claims¶

  • Students reported that gardening teaching skills promoted entrepreneurial skills acquisition to a large extent (53.30%) [+W]
  • Teaching gardening produced more positive attitudes toward entrepreneurial skills acquisition than conventional teaching of the same concepts among Nigerian senior secondary science students [+W]
  • Gender was not a significant factor in students' attitudes toward entrepreneurial skills acquisition after gardening instruction [+W]
  • Students' attitudinal dispositions toward entrepreneurial skills acquisition were highly positive, with 76.00% positively disposed [+W]
  • The study's theoretical framework anchored gardening-based entrepreneurship instruction in Vygotsky's theory of social influence on cognitive development [+W]
