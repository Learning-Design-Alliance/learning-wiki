# Permit chatbots selectively within structured activities, e.g. a chatbot-free consolidation phase followed by a resource-supported inquiry phase

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that where structured guidance is needed, educators permit chatbot use in some phases only: "a chatbot‐free consolidation phase could be implemented, followed by a resource‐supported inquiry phase, where students fill gaps in knowledge and check understanding". Compared with blanket prohibition this leverages chatbot strengths and may be easier to enforce. Guidelines should take cultural and activity factors into account.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning outcomes that justify some structured guidance
- awareness of the learning approaches and student motivations in the local context
#### Constraints
- Derived from one qualitative study of a single CBL curriculum; the phased design itself was not tested

### Target Learners
- preclinical undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- consolidation of lecture content
- inquiry and gap-filling with resources
