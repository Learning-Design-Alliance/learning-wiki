# Use the 6-microskills method as a short, concrete clinical reasoning exercise in which learners verbalize evidence against their own diagnosis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators "provide clinical reasoning education using the 6-microskills method in a short time and in a concrete manner, by utilizing self-reflection". Concretely, after a learner states a predicted diagnosis and its supporting evidence, the instructor prompts the learner to articulate the points that do not fit the hypothesis, with confidence ratings at each step. The authors also suggest instructing students early in clinical training "encouraging them to apply Steps 2 and 3 through self-questioning".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator to pose the does-not-fit question one-on-one
- A case or patient presentation on which the learner has stated a predicted hypothesis
#### Constraints
- The study found no diagnostic-accuracy improvement among students on difficult vignettes; the authors propose testing with residents and objective indicators such as frequency of diagnostic changes.

### Target Learners
- medical students
- residents

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- critical reflection on diagnostic hypotheses
- debiasing

## Claims

- [Confidence in a predicted diagnosis rose slightly from Step 1 to Step 2 and fell slightly from Step 2 to Step 3 among medical students reasoning on vignettes, in one group with no comparison group](../claims/verbalizing-discordant-evidence-lowers-diagnostic-confidence.md) [+W]
- [Misdiagnosis students showed significant confidence increases at Step 2 and decreases at Step 3, while correct-diagnosis students showed no significant confidence changes](../claims/confidence-steps-significant-only-in-misdiagnosis-group.md) [+W]
- [In one group with no comparison group, a small number of misdiagnosed students changed their diagnosis at Step 2 or Step 3 after verbalizing evidence for and against the hypothesis; no student changed to the correct diagnosis at Step 3](../claims/diagnosis-changes-rare-single-question.md) [~W]
- [Confidence change across the three steps did not differ between correct-diagnosis and misdiagnosis students (no interaction)](../claims/no-interaction-correct-vs-misdiagnosis-confidence-steps.md) [~W]
