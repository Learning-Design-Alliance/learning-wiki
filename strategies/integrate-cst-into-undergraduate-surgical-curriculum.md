# Integrate communication simulation workshops into undergraduate surgical curricula as an alternative to passive observational learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that undergraduate curricula incorporate targeted consent-communication simulation rather than relying on didactic instruction and passive observation during rotations, reporting that "these results have resulted in the formal integration of communication skills simulation training into our surgical undergraduate education programme from 2025". Curriculum leads are advised that such sessions also cultivate interpersonal skills extending beyond consent into daily interactions with patients and colleagues.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An established workshop design (tutor-led good/poor roleplay with an actor and CCCM framework) that can be embedded in a surgical tutor's regular workload
#### Constraints
- Evidence currently covers Levels 1 and 2 of Kirkpatrick's model; behaviour change (Level 3) assessment was not feasible and long-term retention was not evaluated

### Target Learners
- senior undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- informed consent communication skills
- patient-centred consultation structure
