# Modify non-STEM curriculums with technology courses and teacher training to raise technology integration and self-efficacy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, because non-STEM students show lower computer self-efficacy and lower intention to use e-learning, "educational policymakers can modify non-STEM curriculums to reflect courses and trainings" that improve learning experiences and ensure students can "effortlessly integrating new technologies into the ir learning practices". It adds that teacher training supporting students' technology use and self-efficacy increase would also be beneficial.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curriculum-level courses and trainings that build technology skills in non-STEM majors
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is directed at Chinese educational policymakers in a context where students are streamed into STEM and non-STEM majors from high school

### Target Learners
- non-STEM university students

### Target Learning Goals
- computer self-efficacy
- integration of new technologies into learning practices
