Replace exclusionary discipline for low-level offenses with social-emotional skill teaching, educator supports, implicit-bias training, and relationship-centered schools¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends a package of evidence-based alternatives to zero-tolerance discipline: "Replacing zero-tolerance policies and the use of suspensions and expulsions for low-level offenses with strategies that teach social-emotional skills," targeted educator supports including caring teacher-student relationships and proactive classroom management, implicit-bias training for all adults working with youth, memoranda of understanding separating educator from law-enforcement discipline, and relationship-centered schools with family and community engagement. States such as California, Michigan, Ohio, and Colorado are presented as implementing these approaches.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Staff who lack behavior-management strategies may need focused supports to develop these skills and strategies

Constraints¶

  • Addressing disproportionality requires distinct strategies and interventions in addition to broader efforts to reduce exclusionary discipline

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students
  • preschool children
  • historically underserved students including students of color and students with disabilities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved school climate and safety
  • Positive behavior and social-emotional skills
  • Equal access to educational opportunity

Related Strategies¶

  • Use school climate surveys, suspension rates, and chronic absenteeism as accountability indicators of school supports for SEL
  • Six-part state and local policy strategy for reducing exclusionary discipline and suspension gaps
  • Replace zero tolerance discipline policies with explicit social-emotional teaching and restorative discipline practices
  • Replace exclusionary discipline with restorative practices and implement data-driven attendance monitoring
  • Build safety and belonging by detracking, offering low-barrier extracurriculars, and implementing restorative approaches in place of exclusionary discipline
  • Use preventive disciplinary and restorative justice approaches rather than exclusionary discipline for students experiencing homelessness
