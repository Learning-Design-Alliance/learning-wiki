# Physics departments can intentionally leverage informal programs, which require no significant curricular changes, to enhance female students' identity development and persistence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that departments maximize the impact of their informal physics programs as a route to a more diverse discipline. Informal programs "are less structured and do not require signiﬁcant changes to departmental curricula to implement", and "By intentionally leveraging informal experiences, departments may enhance the identity development and persistence of female students". Facilitation gives students rich teaching opportunities explaining concepts to audiences unfamiliar with physics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Informal physics programs run by the department, in which students facilitate rather than merely attend
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on self-reported data from volunteers at a single large, land-grant university; the authors note generalizability is not a goal of qualitative interview analysis

### Target Learners
- female undergraduate and graduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- physics identity development
- persistence in physics

## Claims

- [Female students were less confident in their choice of major than male students before participating in informal physics programs, and this difference disappeared after participation](../claims/female-confidence-choice-major-gap-closed-by-outreach.md) [+M]
- [All interviewed female students reported characteristics indicative of a growth mindset, which appeared as a central theme in the semantic network map](../claims/outreach-facilitation-growth-mindset-central.md) [+W]
