# Offer a flexible primary language of instruction with a secondary support language in multilingual EdTech

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that EdTech for multilingual marginalized regions deliver content in both a colonial and a local language, letting learners "choose a primary language of instruction while leveraging an additional language of support when needed". This respects learner preferences, offers translanguaging support, and scales multilingual instruction through EdTech, while developers should avoid marginalizing local languages and raise awareness of their benefits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content and instruction must be available in both a local and a colonial language
- Learners must be able to flexibly switch or choose languages during the course
#### Constraints
- The article notes the persistence association does not imply causality
- Learners reported difficulty switching languages at scheduled switch points

### Target Learners
- Remote learners in multilingual marginalized communities in rural Uganda

### Target Learning Goals
- STEM learning via the Engineering Design Process
- Comprehension of course content across two languages
