# Train pre-service teachers in epistemic verification protocols for AI-generated content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 5 studies (3 qualitative, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding verification behaviors into teacher preparation so that AI outputs are treated with scrutiny. Recommended practices include "Triangulating AI-generated content with textbooks, academic sources, or expert opinions", identifying oversimplification or symbolic inaccuracy, and encouraging students to question, revise, or reject AI outputs that fail disciplinary standards. The authors argue these behaviors are pedagogically productive, promoting critical thinking, scientific reasoning, and metacognitive awareness while cultivating epistemically safe classrooms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher education programs must explicitly train verification habits; AI should be framed as a supportive scaffold, not an authoritative source
#### Constraints
- The article notes chatbot responses often appear fluent and persuasive, making errors difficult for novice users to detect

### Target Learners
- pre-service physics teachers
- university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- epistemic responsibility
- critical evaluation of AI-generated content
- information literacy
