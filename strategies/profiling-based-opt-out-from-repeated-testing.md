# Offer high-performing students an opt-out from repeated testing based on a routine initial profiling test

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because students with high initial numeracy and statistical-literacy profiling scores did not profit equally from repeated testing, the authors propose administering the five-item profiling test routinely and then "allowing high performers to opt out of repeated testing". This targets the additional workload of repeated tests at students who benefit, while sparing top performers, given the considerable workload the test sequence entails.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A routine initial profiling test covering basic numeracy and awareness of probability and uncertainty to identify high performers
#### Constraints
- The article states this applies to students with profiling scores above 80% and that the benefit for this subgroup is unproven rather than harmful

### Target Learners
- medical students with high initial numeracy scores

### Target Learning Goals
- efficient allocation of test-enhanced learning effort
- risk estimation and communication competence
