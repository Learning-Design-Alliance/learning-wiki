# Integrate EI into health sciences curricula through structured experiential methods such as reflective journaling, simulation, peer mentoring, and structured debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends weaving EI training into existing courses with objectives covering emotional awareness, regulation, empathy, communication, teamwork, and leadership, using experiential techniques. It states: "Techniques such as reflective journaling, guided self-assessment, simulation-based learning, case discussions, peer mentoring, and structured debriefing following clinical or academic experiences enable students to critically evaluate their emotional responses and develop effective coping strategies." Faculty development and authentic assessment methods are also recommended.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty development opportunities so instructors can teach these competencies effectively
- Embedding EI into competency-based curricular frameworks rather than separate courses
#### Constraints
- Effects of such interventions are described as context-dependent and inconsistently sustained over time, per the systematic reviews the article cites

### Target Learners
- undergraduate health sciences students
- health sciences educators

### Target Learning Goals
- emotional awareness and regulation
- empathy
- teamwork
- communication
- leadership
