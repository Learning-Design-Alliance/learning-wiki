Use foam-projectile embodied metaphors to teach four key concepts of neural communication¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A four-part classroom activity sequence in which students in pairs or small groups use foam projectiles such as Nerf guns to act out neural processes: firing threshold, all-or-none principle and refractory period (action potentials); exocytosis and ionotropic vs metabotropic receptor action (neurotransmission); EPSPs and IPSPs; and reuptake, degradation, diffusion and glial clearance (neurotransmitter inactivation). The article presents it as "an easy and low-budget embodied-learning activity" that "can be easily completed within 30 min."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructor first provides a basic overview of the chemical and electric aspects of neural communication
  • Students divided into groups of a minimum of three people, with foam projectiles supplied or brought by students
  • Instructor introduces and guides each activity, then follows up with discussion linking it back to the key concept

Constraints¶

  • May work better in a relatively small classroom setting; modifiable by class size, resources and space
  • For larger classes where not all students can participate, passive observation will not encourage active learning in those observing
  • Toy weapons may not be appropriate in some contexts, such as locations where gun-related violence is more common

Target Learners¶

  • introductory university students in psychology or biopsychology/neuroscience

Target Learning Goals¶

  • action potentials
  • neurotransmission and receptor action
  • excitatory and inhibitory post-synaptic potentials
  • neurotransmitter inactivation

Claims¶

  • Embodied Nerf-gun activities increased students' self-reported understanding of, and ability to explain, key neural communication concepts in a pre-post seminar design [+W]
  • In an initial feedback survey, students strongly agreed the embodied activities were memorable, helped learning beyond lecture alone, and were recommendable to future groups [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Mitigate reluctance to participate in group activities with clearly defined roles and an icebreaker interaction with the materials
