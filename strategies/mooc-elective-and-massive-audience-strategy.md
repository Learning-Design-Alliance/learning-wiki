# Use MOOCs mainly as elective courses and for educating massive audiences, with multidisciplinary design and implementation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review concludes that MOOCs "can be useful to educate students, mainly as elective courses, and to educate a massive audience, thus embodying the third mission of the university". Because "the complexity of factors increasing effectiveness suggests the need for a multidisciplinary approach both in their design and implementation", institutions adopting MOOCs should combine course-level supports (forums, multiple resources, interactivity, teacher feedback) with attention to participant-side factors such as motivation and time management.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Promoting discussion and collaboration through forums, collective tasks, and social media groups
- Using multiple resources and delivery systems
- Facilitating interactivity with face-to-face sessions or tools as virtual patients
- Ensuring constructive communication, feedback, and support by teachers and staff
#### Constraints
- High dropout rates (>90%) reported in several MOOCs; difficulty accessing technology and internet connection issues, especially in developing countries, limit completion

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and postgraduate nursing and healthcare sciences students
- healthcare professionals and lay citizens in global audiences

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical subjects such as emergency medicine and chronic pain
- research and statistics methods
- continuing professional education
