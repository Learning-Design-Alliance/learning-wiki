# Combine adolescent alcohol interventions with parental educational programs and refusal-skills training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that school-based alcohol prevention pair adolescent sessions with parent-focused education and explicit refusal-skills training. It states that "the delivery of parental educational programs in conjunction with adolescent interventions may maximize the effectiveness of preventive efforts", and that parental monitoring and modelling moderate drinking shape teens' attitudes. It also suggests role-plays to practise refusal skills and booster sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Active parental involvement and honest family discussions about alcohol, since parents' own drinking can undermine school-based messages
#### Constraints
- Malta's strong social acceptance of alcohol and cultural embeddedness make change difficult; the authors note changing trends "requires an overall change in the cultural mentality of Maltese people"

### Target Learners
- secondary school students aged 14-16
- parents of adolescents

### Target Learning Goals
- reduce or prevent alcohol misuse
- build drinking refusal self-efficacy
- dispel misconceptions about alcohol expectancies
