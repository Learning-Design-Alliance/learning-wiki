# Increase English contact hours and emphasize EMP over EAP, including medical terminology, for medical undergraduates

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's concluding recommendations, agreed by both faculty and student populations, are that more time should be assigned to the English language course, reading and speaking skills should be emphasized, medical terminology should be taught in the English course, and "EMP should be emphasized more instead of EAP" to prepare learners for medical studies. Faculty additionally recommended teaching pronunciation of important medical terminology and arranging special evening English courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- English course design should be grounded in a triangulated needs analysis of the specific institutional context, as the study executed at CMMS
#### Constraints
- Findings come from one medical college (CMMS, Taif University) during the 2008 academic year; the author notes some questionnaire findings were contradicted by the other three tools

### Target Learners
- freshmen medical undergraduates in English-medium medical colleges

### Target Learning Goals
- English for medical purposes
- reading and speaking skills for medical study
- medical terminology
