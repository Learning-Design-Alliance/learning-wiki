# Use skills-focused instructional laboratories, including project-based and open-ended labs, to develop experimental skills aligned with quantum workforce needs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 4 studies (3 qualitative, 1 review), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends skills-focused instructional laboratories as a key environment for developing experimentally relevant skills, since many identified skills overlap with those already emphasized in undergraduate physics laboratories and transfer across career paths. The authors state that "skills-focused lab courses" are key in preparing students for QISE careers, pointing to labs where students build their own apparatus, choose analysis methods, troubleshoot setups, and engage in multi-week projects with iteration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lab courses include instrumentation and computation/data analysis objectives, multi-week project structures foregrounding planning and persistence, and open-ended activities rather than purely prescriptive guides.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the suggested implications do not require complete curricular overhaul or very expensive specialized quantum hardware, making the approach feasible across institutions with differing resources.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in first-year and beyond-first-year lab courses

### Target Learning Goals
- instrumentation skills, computation and data analysis, planning and carrying out experiments, persisting through setbacks, working in open-ended contexts
