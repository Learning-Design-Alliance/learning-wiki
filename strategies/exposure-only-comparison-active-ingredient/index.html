Use an exposure-only storybook comparison to isolate the active ingredient of embedded instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article argues that when testing embedded lessons, a comparison group hearing the same stories with the same words but without lessons is more appropriate than a business-as-usual control. It makes the vocabulary outcome measures fair to the comparison group, because comparison children are exposed to the tested words, and it elucidates "the role of our hypothesized active ingredient (explicit, embedded instruction) in the intervention."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Both conditions must hear the same stories containing the same vocabulary words in the same small-group format

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • preschool children with limited vocabulary knowledge

Target Learning Goals¶

  • isolating the effect of explicit embedded instruction from story exposure alone

Claims¶

  • Embedded vocabulary lessons in prerecorded storybooks produce large vocabulary learning gains relative to hearing the same stories without lessons [+W]
  • Dosage and preintervention language skills showed little moderation of vocabulary treatment effects [+W]
  • Embedded comprehension lessons did not improve story comprehension relative to story exposure alone [+W]
  • Story Friends produced no group differences on distal standardized language measures (PPVT-IV, CELF-P) [+W]
  • Preschoolers learn almost no challenging vocabulary from hearing stories without embedded lessons [+W]
