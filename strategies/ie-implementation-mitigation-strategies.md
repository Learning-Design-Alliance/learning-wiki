# Mitigate low-gain IE courses through instructor apprenticeship, grade incentives, conceptual exams, and full-course integration of IE methods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on case histories of seven low-gain IE courses, the article lists six implementation practices that might mitigate low gains: apprenticeship education of instructors new to IE methods, emphasis on the nature of science and learning, attention to motivation and "grade incentives for taking IE-activities seriously", intervention for potential low-gain students, exams probing conceptual understanding, and use of IE methods in all course components with tight integration. It is offered as practical advice grounded in the survey's failure cases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors new to IE methods receive apprenticeship education, and IE methods are used in all components of a course and tightly integrated.
#### Constraints
- The article presents these as mitigations suggested by case histories of low-<g> courses, not as practices tested experimentally.

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students in high school, college, and university courses

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of Newtonian mechanics
