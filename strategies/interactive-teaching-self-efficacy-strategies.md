# Implement interactive teaching strategies and self-efficacy-supportive practices in student-centered higher education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's recommendations section directs educators and institutions toward concrete practices: "educational institutions should implement interactive teaching strategies such as collaborative projects, problem-based learning, and peer-led discussions". It further recommends building confidence through "regular constructive feedback, personalized learning goals, and the recognition of student achievements", investing in digital tools such as learning management systems and virtual collaboration platforms, and tailoring strategies to learners' cultural and contextual characteristics. Professional development programs should equip educators with interactive teaching skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators need skills to design and facilitate interactive learning experiences, which the article says professional development programs should provide.
#### Constraints
- The recommendations are grounded in a cross-sectional survey of education students at Chinese application-oriented universities; the article itself notes limited generalizability to other educational contexts.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in application-oriented universities
- pre-service teachers in faculties of education

### Target Learning Goals
- learning achievement
- self-efficacy
- engagement with content and peers
