# Provide sonification of astronomical data with sufficient context, since sounds without context are hard to interpret

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Sonification — presenting astronomical data as sound — is recommended for outreach, education and research analysis. Interviewee EPM cautions that "sounds without context are hard to interpret" and that "If you play a soniﬁcation of astronomical data without any context it is meaning- less"; he supplies context through an article or voice description. Interviewee NB adds that for researchers the sonification "might not need to sound nice but needs to be useful", while for public audiences the emotional response matters more.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sonification must be accompanied by context, for example an article or voice description, to be interpretable.
#### Constraints
- For public and education audiences, unpleasant or frightening sounds may put people off, so aesthetic considerations apply there but not necessarily for researchers.

### Target Learners
- astronomy researchers
- public and education audiences in astronomy outreach

### Target Learning Goals
- interpreting astrophysical data through sound
- presenting research results accessibly
