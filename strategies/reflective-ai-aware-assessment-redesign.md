# Redesign assessment reflectively for possible AI use, using supervised tasks for lower-order skills and intentional AI use with reflection for higher-order work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The chapter recommends adapting or redesigning assessment to account for intentional or incidental AI use, explicitly not relying on GenAI detection software, which has "proven to be inaccurate and unreliable." Student suggestions include in-person essays, oral exams, and presentations with personalized questions. The chapter also describes the AAA (Against, Avoid, Adopt) framework by Lye and Lim (2024): supervise assessment of lower-order skills AI can complete, contextualize higher-order tasks, and design some assessments that use AI intentionally with student reflection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment methods adapted to account for possible AI (mis)use
- For Adopt-style tasks, students submit their own work plus an evaluation of their AI use
#### Constraints
- Detection software is rejected as inaccurate, unreliable, and risk of instilling a policing dynamic

### Target Learners
- higher-education students, predominantly engineering

### Target Learning Goals
- critical thinking and self-reflection about AI use
- authentic demonstration of higher-order skills
