# Monitor working memory and processing speed in girls with ADHD and provide accommodations such as breaking down instructions into manageable pieces

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Although WM and PS did not predict math growth, they predicted both initial and adult math scores. The authors therefore recommend "monitoring WM and PS in girls with ADHD and providing relevant accommodations (e.g., breaking down instructions into manageable pieces) for girls with WM and PS difficulties" to enhance math performance. This is an accommodation strategy, not a remediation of growth.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of girls with WM or PS difficulties via childhood cognitive assessment (WISC-III Backward Digit Span, Processing Speed Factor in the study).
#### Constraints
- The article states these cognitive measures were more predictive of math performance at testing than of math learning over time, so accommodations target performance rather than long-term growth.

### Target Learners
- Girls with ADHD with working-memory or processing-speed difficulties

### Target Learning Goals
- Math problem solving performance
