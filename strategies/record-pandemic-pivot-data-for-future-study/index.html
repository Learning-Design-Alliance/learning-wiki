Use the pandemic disruption as an opportunity to record data on shifting to online student-centered learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Tabor recommends that researchers and districts take advantage of the COVID-19 disruption to study the effectiveness of the framework and of the shift to online, student-centered learning. He argues the pandemic is fundamentally changing how education is delivered and there has never been a better time to record data for future study, since desire for an online learning option will remain or possibly increase.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Schools and districts will need to continue improving their online education methods and course designs through continuous training and research

Constraints¶

  • Teachers are overwhelmed with adapting lesson plans, supporting students' social/emotional needs, learning many online tools, and stress of possibly becoming infected with COVID-19

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in online learning

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Effectiveness of student-centered online learning and teacher adaptation to digital formats

Claims¶

  • Tabor argues the Own it, Learn it, Share it framework can be applied immediately to support districts' rapid pivot to online learning [+W]
  • The pandemic made professional learning about online and blended learning immediately relevant and highly sought after by teachers who had previously been difficult to persuade to prioritise it [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Combine quick response research approaches with meta-aggregative review methodology to capture educator experiences of rapid digital shifts and extend the TPD-for-OBL framework
