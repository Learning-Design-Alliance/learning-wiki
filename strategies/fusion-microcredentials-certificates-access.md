# Offer core fusion topics as micro-credentials, certificates, and publicly available courses to broaden access

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (2 review, 2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
To improve accessibility to fusion education, the report proposes packaging core introductory topics — plasma physics, reactor design, materials science, nuclear engineering — as micro-credentials and certification programs. Such programs "can provide foundational knowledge in a cost-effective and flexible manner, which will help broaden participation in the fusion workforce", and are designed for working professionals, adult learners, and those seeking to upskill or re-skill quickly from neighboring industries. The report also points to Europe's FuEL hub as a model for on-demand online course access.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration with universities and research centers to develop and deliver the programs
#### Constraints
- The FuEL initiative does not provide accreditation or certification at its early stages

### Target Learners
- working professionals
- adult learners
- students transitioning from neighboring industries
- undergraduate and graduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational knowledge of plasma physics, reactor design, materials science, and nuclear engineering
- rapid upskilling or re-skilling to enter the fusion sector
