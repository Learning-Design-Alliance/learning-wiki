# Embed structured BLS/AED training early in the medical curriculum with spaced simulation-based reinforcement and competency-based assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend a structured curriculum approach in which BLS/AED concepts are introduced in the pre-clinical years, reinforced through spaced repetition and simulation, and verified through objective assessment. They propose using the study's item-level gap profile as a targeted needs assessment, applying Kern's six-step approach to define competency-based outcomes, and aligning assessment with Miller's framework combining knowledge testing, OSCE performance, and supervised drills. They also note peer-led BLS training may mitigate resource constraints in Syrian settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires curriculum redesign toward competency-based, standardised emergency-skills training supported by governance and accreditation mechanisms, as the authors state
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a cross-sectional knowledge survey without practical skill assessment; the authors note future studies should incorporate simulation-based assessment/OSCE-style scenarios

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students, particularly those in pre-clinical years

### Target Learning Goals
- Guideline-critical CPR and AED knowledge
- Recognition of myocardial infarction symptoms
- First-response readiness in cardiovascular emergencies
