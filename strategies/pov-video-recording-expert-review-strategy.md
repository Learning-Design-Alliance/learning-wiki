# Use first-person point-of-view video recording of student technique attempts followed by one-on-one expert review as a formative feedback activity in laboratory skills courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A specific implementable activity: on a designated formative attempt, the student wears a chest-mounted camera (GoPro) or uses a tripod-mounted phone to record their own first-person performance of a laboratory technique, supervised by a peer rather than an instructor. The recording is saved under a non-identifiable code, made available through the student's online portfolio, and later reviewed one-on-one with the assessor for technical skills, suggestions for improvement, and self-reflection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video recording equipment (chest mounted GoPro or iPhone 6s with tripod)
- A student peer to supervise the recording rather than an Instructor
- Non-identifiable code numbers and password-protected online portfolio access
- Instructor availability for blind written rubric assessment of recordings followed by one-on-one review
#### Constraints
- The video must not contain identifiable features of the student such as face, hands, or skin
- Students did not receive oral feedback at the time of the recording
- Approximately 15 min of assessor time per student (5 min oral feedback plus 10 min video review and written feedback)

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in laboratory-based practical courses
- Medical laboratory science students learning histology techniques

### Target Learning Goals
- Technical skill development in laboratory procedures
- Self-reflection on technique quality
- Preparation for summative practical examination

## Claims

- [No students reported anxiety or avoidance when watching expert video techniques or receiving peer feedback, but 28% of open-ended comments expressed anxiety about being recorded themselves](../claims/anxiety-limited-to-self-recording.md) [+W]
- [Most students responded positively to video recording and feedback: 66% of open-ended survey comments were positive, and three quarters agreed the video provided an authentic picture of their skills (M = 4.0, SD = 1.47)](../claims/positive-student-perceptions-video-feedback.md) [+W]
- [Students reported that their self-perception of completing the histology activity differed from what the video recording showed (M = 3.53, SD = 1.51), and that video feedback was an alternative way to receive feedback that enhanced learning (M = 4.15, SD = 1.21)](../claims/self-perception-differs-from-video-recording.md) [+W]
- [Medical laboratory science students recorded performing histology techniques and reviewing the video with expert feedback achieved significantly higher summative practical examination scores and final grades than control students receiving peer feedback](../claims/video-feedback-higher-practical-scores-final-grades.md) [+M]
- [Students who viewed expert histology technique demonstration videos or completed the online e-Learning module had significantly higher final grades than students who did not](../claims/expert-videos-elearning-module-higher-final-grades.md) [+W]
