# Design school-based substance use interventions combining theoretical grounding, multi-stakeholder involvement, and developmental-stage-calibrated dosage

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's stated implication for practitioners and policymakers is to design interventions that combine theoretical grounding, involvement of parents, community and experts, and dosage calibrated to children's developmental stage. It advises this "rather than relying on content volume alone," because its synthesis found theory-grounded and multi-stakeholder models produced more consistent and sustained reductions, while dosage and developmental timing interacted with outcomes across age groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Active involvement of school administration, teachers, trained staff, parents, and community members as the review describes for integrated school settings.
#### Constraints
- The review notes programme effectiveness is not uniform across development, with specific intervention characteristics working for some age groups but not others.

### Target Learners
- School-going children and adolescents (roughly ages 10-18 across the reviewed programmes)

### Target Learning Goals
- Prevention of tobacco, alcohol, cannabis and other substance use initiation; refusal and resistance skills; negative attitudes toward substance use
