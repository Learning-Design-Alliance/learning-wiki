# Build spiritual care competency through team learning and multidisciplinary collaboration with religious/spiritual care professionals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's conclusions recommend a team-based route to spiritual care competency: nurses should collaborate with doctors, social workers, chaplains, psychologists, and counselors, engage in team learning, and involve professionals in providing religious/spiritual care. This recommendation is supported by the regression finding that professional involvement predicted higher competency, and the authors state that integrating patients' religion/spirituality "requires a team approach involving various professionals like doctors, nurses, social workers, chaplains, psychologists, and counselors."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Organizational support, training opportunities, and reduced workload, which the authors state can help nurses incorporate patients' religion/spirituality into care
#### Constraints
- Recommended in the context of moderate baseline competency and the finding that most nurses (90% and 85% in the two hospitals) did not rely on specialists for spiritual/religious care

### Target Learners
- Practicing nurses in general and psychiatric hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- Collaborative delivery of holistic, spiritually integrated patient care
