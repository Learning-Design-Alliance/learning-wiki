# Design, content and communicative-style recommendations for training doctors to communicate with adolescents

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
From their evaluation the authors draw three sets of implications for training doctors to be more effective helping agents. Design implications are "use of authentic simulation; positioning the client as coach as well as simulated patient; and provision of an experimental learning space in which immediate formative feedback is provided together with the opportunity for rehearsal and re-play". Content implications stress proactive opportunistic screening with a tool, alerts to adolescents' sensitivity about mental health disclosure, and explaining confidentiality. Communicative-style implications cover explaining the reason for screening, non-judgemental questioning, and smorgasbord questions that name, guide and invite responses rather than sole reliance on open-ended questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires school students prepared by teachers in authentic patient portrayal and formative feedback techniques, and co-facilitation by a classroom teacher and medical educator
#### Constraints
- The authors state further longitudinal research is required to explore whether participating adolescents show increased help-seeking and whether qualified doctors conduct opportunistic screening

### Target Learners
- Fifth-year medical students
- Medical educators training doctors for adolescent health care

### Target Learning Goals
- Initiating proactive screening conversations with adolescents
- Non-judgemental communicative style and confidentiality explanation
