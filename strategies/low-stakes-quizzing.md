# Low Stakes Quizzing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Low stakes quizzing embeds frequent, brief tests into instruction with minimal or no consequences for grades. Learners actively retrieve information — via multiple choice, short answer, or free recall — rather than rereading it. The act of retrieval itself strengthens memory, and quiz results give both learner and instructor diagnostic information while the material can still be corrected.

## Design Implications

Retrieval practice produces substantially larger and more durable learning gains than restudying, with effects that persist over delays of days to months [Rowland, 2014 meta-analysis] [+S]. The benefit holds across formats, ages, and subject areas, though free-recall and short-answer formats tend to outperform simple recognition [Adesope et al., 2017] [+S]. Because stakes are low, quizzes function as learning events rather than assessments of record — the goal is error correction, not sorting. Feedback after each quiz is essential: retrieval without corrective feedback can entrench errors [~M]. Quizzing also pairs naturally with spacing; distributing quizzes across weeks compounds the retention benefit [Distributed practice improves retention.](../claims/distributed-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Brief, frequent quizzes (typically 2–10 items) scheduled throughout a unit, not clustered before exams
- Low or zero grade weight so learners treat errors as information, not failure
- Timely corrective [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) following each attempt
- Items that require retrieval, not recognition of just-seen material

#### Constraints
- Quizzing on material learners have not yet encountered produces mostly guessing and can implant errors, especially with multiple-choice formats [~M]
- Excessive quiz frequency can crowd out other learning activities and raise anxiety for some learners [~W]
- If stakes creep upward, learners shift from learning to impression management and the formative benefit collapses [~M]
- Poorly aligned items (trivia-level recall of facts when the goal is reasoning) train the wrong retrieval routes [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Pre-class quizzes** (flipped settings) to ensure exposure before discussion
- **In-class clicker or poll questions** with peer discussion between attempts
- **Post-class online quizzes** with immediate automated feedback
- **Cumulative quizzes** that revisit earlier material, converting each quiz into spaced retrieval
- **Pre-questions** placed before instruction as advance organizers — effective for directing attention even when learners answer incorrectly [~M]

### Target Learners
- Learners of all ability levels benefit, though effects are often largest for those with weaker prior knowledge [Rowland, 2014] [+S]
- Younger learners and those with low metacognitive skill benefit especially, since they otherwise over-rely on rereading
- Anxious learners need genuinely low stakes; high-stakes testing conditions can impair retrieval [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of factual and conceptual knowledge over long delays [+S]
- Building fluency and automaticity in foundational skills
- Formative diagnosis: identifying gaps while instruction can still respond [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. Identify the core knowledge and skills learners must retain; write 2–10 retrieval items per quiz targeting those.
2. Schedule quizzes at spaced intervals across the unit, including earlier material in later quizzes ([Distributed Practice](distributed-practice.md)).
3. Present the quiz with explicit framing that it is for learning, not grading.
4. Require actual retrieval — close notes, answer before discussion.
5. Provide immediate corrective feedback; have learners restudy only what they missed.
6. Follow with application or [practice](../elements/practice.md) that uses the retrieved knowledge in a new context.

## Examples
- **[Retrieval Practice](https://www.retrievalpractice.org)** (Agarwal & Bain) — classroom guides and research summaries for implementing low stakes quizzing across K–12 subjects.
- **[Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net)** — spaced-repetition flashcard app that operationalizes low stakes quizzing through scheduled retrieval with immediate feedback.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — short mastery quizzes with hints and instant feedback embedded between instructional videos.
- **Clicker/Poll Everywhere questions** in large lectures — individual answer, peer discussion, re-answer, then instructor explanation (peer instruction sequence).
