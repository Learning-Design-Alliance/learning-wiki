# Train all OSCE tutors to systematically challenge students' clinical reasoning and address communication and professionalism in feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that feedback training for OSCE tutors go beyond teaching skills to include conceptual frameworks that help tutors facilitate rather than direct learning. They state that "All tutors should be trained to systematically challenge students' clinical reasoning and to address communication and professionalism during formative OSCEs." Faculty programs should target both skills and frameworks so tutors can help students revisit assumptions and develop broader responses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors should attend training on feedback provision; the article notes faculty development programs have been effective in improving teaching skills and encouraging a more learner-centered approach.
#### Constraints
- The authors note communication and professionalism require a frame of reference often lacking without training and can feel more challenging than history taking or physical examination.

### Target Learners
- pre-clinical medical students (2nd and 3rd year) in formative OSCEs

### Target Learning Goals
- history taking, physical examination, communication skills, clinical reasoning, professionalism
