Incorporate theories from other fields into graphic design course content¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends importing theories or principles from other disciplines, such as narrative theory from linguistics, into graphic design curricula as a route to innovation in design education. It argues this helps students value the intrinsic nature of visual communication design, move beyond conventional thinking, and explore creative visual concepts, and concludes that "The application of theories or principles from other fields to graphic design course content will definitely improve students’ learning outcomes."

Claims¶

  • Narrative theory instruction improved students' image creativity scores for poster design [+W]
  • Narrative theory instruction improved students' thematic concept scores for poster design [+W]
  • Narrative theory instruction improved students' visual aesthetic scores for poster design [+W]
  • Narrative theory instruction improved overall public welfare poster design performance in a 4-week university graphic design course [+W]
  • Narrative theory instruction did not significantly change expressive technique scores for poster design [~W]
  • Control group students without narrative instruction tended to imitate famous designers' works instead of generating original concepts [+W]
