# Break large cohorts into small groups for one-hour bifurcated CT sessions combining problem-based learning with phantom setup

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
To manage a large cohort of 50 students, one-hour CT sessions are bifurcated into two parts within the hour, each involving five students: one group engages in a problem-based learning scenario concerning CT scanning and localization while the other sets up the anthropomorphic phantom for subsequent image acquisition. This responds to the timetabling challenge of breaking larger cohorts into smaller groups, particularly for CT sessions, to ensure effective hands-on learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A flexible timetable layout facilitating well-organised scheduling of theoretical and practical sessions
- Staff availability for year 2 small-group CT hands-on teaching
#### Constraints
- The timetable requires alterations to accommodate one-hour CT sessions for the large group

### Target Learners
- Large Year 2 undergraduate cohorts (approximately 50 students)

### Target Learning Goals
- Hands-on CT scanning and localization skills
- Phantom setup for image acquisition
