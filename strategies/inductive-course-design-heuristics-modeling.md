# Inductive course-design heuristics for teaching modeling content and process together: induce predictable mistakes, pair simple models with complex scenarios, omit key information, and plan for frustration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article offers curriculum heuristics developed iteratively over four semesters for instructors prototyping courses that combine content and process learning. Key moves include mapping a general learning path, then deliberately choosing "teaching examples and assignment scenarios specifically so students could make the mistakes I wanted them to make" in a low-stakes environment; assigning simple models with complex, uncertain scenarios; "[b]y omitting key information," forcing students to determine what they knew and did not know; requiring short papers on short deadlines; using rubrics that include process criteria; and planning for frustration, which peaked around the fifth week.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course objectives emphasizing both content and process mastery must drive content and exercise choices
- Rubrics must explicitly include process criteria and standards, since students are accustomed to content lists
#### Constraints
- Process learning can be frustrating for students even when structured well; instructors should not let students wander into detours that will not help understanding

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology majors

### Target Learning Goals
- modeling process skills
- cooperative skills
- confronting and replacing misconceptions
