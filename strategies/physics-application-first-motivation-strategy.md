# Motivate each mathematical tool by its physics application before formal development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Each chapter opens by anchoring the mathematics in physics use. The vectors chapter states: "The purpose of this chapter is twofold. Firstly, and most importantly, I want to help you learn how to use vectors for implementation in your study of the natural world." Maxwell's equations are shown as motivation for vector notation, and vector subtraction is justified because it describes "the change in a system," such as displacement. Coordinate systems are motivated by physics cases from electrons in metals to black holes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The instructor or text must supply a genuine physics context in which the mathematical tool does work.
#### Constraints
- The author notes some motivating details, such as the meaning of Maxwell's equations, are explicitly left to later coursework.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting mathematical formalism to physical phenomena
