# Support students at the peer-evaluation stage with training and tutoring so all can participate and benefit

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q1`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
From the study's findings the authors recommend that "training, tutoring and support from teachers should be provided to students at the peer-evaluation stage" so that all students are willing and capable of participating and benefiting. This responds directly to interviewed students who could tick checklist items but not suggest corrections, and to a high achiever who received only general comments. Teachers should also provide time for writing and revising and teach metacognitive strategies for pre-writing, drafting, evaluating and revising.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher preparation in process-based writing pedagogy and time allocated for writing and revising
#### Constraints
- Peer feedback may be inaccurate or non-constructive without training, per the article's discussion

### Target Learners
- Secondary school EFL students of mixed proficiency

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective peer evaluation and revision of English writing
