# Concept cartoon discussion routine: present, debate viewpoints, then student research without teacher explanation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes a specific classroom routine for using concept cartoons: the teacher projects the cartoon, introduces the characters and their advocated thoughts, asks students which character they agree with and why, and lets all students express ideas openly. Following discussion, the teacher invited the students to suggest a method to research the correctness of the thoughts they advocated, and while students researched, the teacher offered no scientific explanations about the concept. Students then discussed research results and reached a conclusion together.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A concept cartoon with multiple characters advocating different ways of thinking
- A teacher who withholds scientific explanation during the student research phase
#### Constraints
- Studied over a three-week (12 hours of class) teaching process in one unit; the authors recommend longer-term and blended-method studies

### Target Learners
- seventh grade science students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical thinking skills
- evaluating the correctness of asserted views
