# Future scalpel-skills intervention research should use more engaged training, supervision, and larger participant numbers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future studies of sensory or supplementary motor training for scalpel skills use "more engaged training, provision of supervision and greater participant numbers". They suggest self-reported practice data and unsupervised audio-recorded training may have lacked fidelity, and that supervised practice could improve engagement. The recommendation follows directly from the trial's null between-group findings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be feasible within existing curricula; the trial used approximately 40-minute sessions plus home practice over a minimum of two weeks
#### Constraints
- The article states two weeks may not have been sufficient time to see benefits of extra training, and that usual teaching at both sites likely had high fidelity, providing increased motivation and high levels of feedback

### Target Learners
- second-year podiatry students learning scalpel skills

### Target Learning Goals
- dexterous scalpel skill acquisition
- self-efficacy and anxiety during psychomotor skill learning
