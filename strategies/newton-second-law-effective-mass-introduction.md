# Introduce bare and effective mass through standard applications of Newton's second law in a junior-level classical mechanics course.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy is to make a stimulating digression during the review of Newton's laws: derive the added-mass equation for a body in a fluid and the sliding-block equation with friction, then let temperature-dependent parameters (fluid density, friction coefficient) show how effective mass runs with temperature. The author proposes "that these topics be discussed in a more formal wa y through standard applications of Newton's second law of motion," giving students a mathematical rather than purely conceptual view of modern-theory ideas.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students attend a classical mechanics course where Newton's laws of motion are reviewed; at an introductory level, sections II.A and III suffice.
#### Constraints
- Section II.B must be presented with care to avoid confusions regarding Newton's second law of motion, since M*(Λ) is not a true effective mass.

### Target Learners
- junior-level undergraduate physics majors

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguish bare and effective mass
- understand how coupling parameters and temperature affect effective mass
- prepare for perturbation theory and renormalization
