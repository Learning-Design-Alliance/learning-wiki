# Apply a developmental perspective when investigating or designing around ability self-concept formation in young learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that findings on ability self-concept formation from secondary school cannot be assumed to generalize to younger students. Educational researchers and practitioners should consider developmental changes in children's reasoning about competence and in their learning environments when interpreting or supporting the formation of ability self-concepts in the early school years. As the article states, "Findings underline the importance of considering developmental differences to better understand ability self-concept formation."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of students' developmental stage alongside measures of ability self-concept and achievement
#### Constraints
- Evidence derives from German elementary school students in Grades 2–4; generalizability to other educational systems is not directly tested

### Target Learners
- elementary school students
- students in transition between elementary and secondary school

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate formation of domain-specific ability self-concepts
