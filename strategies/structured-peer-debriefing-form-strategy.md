# Structure peer debriefing around observed actions, good judgement, and closing performance gaps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Peer debriefers in the trial used a debriefing form "developed from the best practice guide" structured around three aspects: (i) actions observed, (ii) use of good judgement, and (iii) exploring and closing the gaps observed. This gave inexperienced student debriefers a concrete scaffold for facilitating reflection without an expert instructor. Pairings were changed between scenarios so participants were not debriefed twice by the same peer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured debriefing form and a 30-minute orientation covering simulation concepts, crisis resource management, and non-technical skills.
#### Constraints
- The study did not examine debriefing content, so qualitative differences between peer and instructor debriefing styles remain unknown.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students acting as peer debriefers

### Target Learning Goals
- facilitating reflective feedback on non-technical crisis management performance
