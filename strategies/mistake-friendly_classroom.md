# Mistake-Friendly Classroom

## Description
Creating a mistake-friendly classroom involves fostering an environment where mistakes are seen as a fundamental part of learning, rather than a source of embarrassment or failure. This approach encourages students to take risks, ask questions, and engage deeply with the material without fear of negative consequences. Key strategies include acknowledging the reality of fear, working within the zone of proximal development, tapping into passion and curiosity, actively modeling mistakes, encouraging 'rough-draft thinking,' and grading fewer things.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a shift in mindset from instructors to view mistakes as valuable learning opportunities. Implementation of specific strategies to promote a mistake-friendly environment. Ongoing modeling and reinforcement of positive attitudes toward mistakes. Creation of a supportive and collaborative classroom culture.
#### Constraints
- Some students may initially resist the idea of embracing mistakes due to prior experiences or ingrained beliefs. Requires consistent effort and reinforcement from instructors to maintain a mistake-friendly environment. May be challenging to implement in high-stakes testing situations or highly competitive environments.

## Examples
- Teachers actively model mistakes by writing in front of their class, showing the confusion, messiness, stopping and starting, and hesitation that comes with composing. The teacher is the best writer in the room and therefore it is critical that the best writer in the room models the confusion, the messiness, the stopping and starting, the hesitation that comes with trying to compose.
