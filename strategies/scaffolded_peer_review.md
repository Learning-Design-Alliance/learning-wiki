# Scaffolded Peer Review

## Description
Scaffolded peer review involves providing learners with structured support and guidance to improve the quality and effectiveness of their feedback. This can include using student-produced samples as exemplars, limiting the scope of feedback to one specific area, and fostering a culture of constructive criticism. This technique is especially helpful when learners feel uncomfortable or unconfident providing feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Exemplars of high-quality work, structured prompts and questions, and a supportive classroom environment.
#### Constraints
- Requires careful planning and preparation to ensure the scaffolding is effective. May be time-consuming, especially in the initial stages.

- Improve the quality and effectiveness of peer feedback, build confidence in providing critique, and foster a culture of constructive criticism

### Affordances
- Provides structured support and guidance, helping learners overcome their fears of inadequacy and providing unhelpful feedback. Fosters a classroom environment where criticism is embraced and learners are comfortable providing and receiving critique.

### Personalization
- The level of scaffolding can be adjusted based on the learners' needs and abilities. The specific focus of feedback can be tailored to the subject matter and learning objectives.

## Assessment Evidence
- Review of feedback provided by learners to assess the depth and quality of their critique. Observation of group interactions to assess learners' confidence and engagement in the process.

## Impact
- Scaffolding matters: Using more student-produced samples to look at as a class and giving the students models to compare their own writing to can improve the level of peer review.
