# Promote interprofessional cooperation during undergraduate medical studies through combined courses with trainees and students of other medical care professions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because delegating work to non-medical personnel is expected to increase and learning goals not exclusive to physicians were rated relevant, the authors recommend starting interprofessional cooperation during undergraduate studies. They state that "combined courses with trainees or students of other medical care professions, could be one method of implementing the interpersonal aspect of care more intensively during medical education."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordination between medical faculties and other medical care professions' training institutions to run combined courses
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is derived from expert thesis assessments, not from a tested intervention in this study

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- trainees and students of other medical care professions

### Target Learning Goals
- interprofessional teamwork and cooperation
- interpersonal aspects of patient care
