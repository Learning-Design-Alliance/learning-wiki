Offer entrepreneurial simulation board games in workshops for young adults to enhance basic entrepreneurial traits¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend offering entrepreneurial games such as TBG in entrepreneurship workshops for young adults to enhance basic traits of entrepreneurial factors. They state "it is also suggested that entrepreneurial games, such as TBG are offered in entrepreneurial workshops for young adults in order to enhance their basic traits of entrepreneurial factors." This follows from the finding that moderate-frequency play improved resilience.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Structured gameplay sessions with facilitator guidance in early games

Constraints¶

  • Evidence base is a small-sample three-week study; optimal frequency appears to be once weekly rather than more often

Target Learners¶

  • young adults in entrepreneurship workshops

Target Learning Goals¶

  • resilience
  • entrepreneurial factors

Claims¶

  • Playing TBG in moderation (once weekly) yields better resilience improvement than playing more frequently (twice weekly) [+W]
  • Frequency of playing TBG did not significantly affect self-esteem improvement over three weeks [+W]
  • Playing the TRADERS board game once a week for three weeks significantly improves resilience among university students compared to not playing [+W]
  • Control group resilience decreased over the three-week period, suggesting TBG protects against resilience decline [+W]
  • Bao gameplay is linked to economic decision-making and entrepreneurial skills [+W]
