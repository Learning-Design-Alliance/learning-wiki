Strategies

Design adapted recreation programming with SWD input, undergraduate assistants interested in therapy careers, adequate equipment, and academic credit¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors' recommendations for disability services educators designing adapted recreational programs: gather SWD input through focus groups and program committees, use programming strategies such as Benefits Based Programming, staff classes with undergraduate assistants with career goals in physical or occupational therapy, ensure sufficient equipment, and advocate for academic course credit. Participants "suggested that for future programming, assistants remain undergraduate students with career goals in physical and occupational therapy," and the authors argue credit "may affect the participation rates and success of adapted recreation programs."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • SWD included on committees and panels for program design and renovation
  • Instructors able to modify activities for multiple levels of ability
  • Assistants provided by the program to prevent need for external provision

Constraints¶

  • Some participants wanted more independence during activities than safety regulations allowed

Target Learners¶

  • university students with physical disabilities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • participation in recreational physical activity
  • social inclusion
  • confidence and independence

Claims¶

  • An inclusive aquatic exercise class produced no significant pre/post survey differences between SWD intervention and control groups except in recreational physical activity levels [+W]
  • Thematic analysis indicates intervention participants perceived numerous positive physical, psychological, and social outcomes from the inclusive aquatic exercise class [+W]
  • Participants reported positive social relationships primarily with their assigned undergraduate exercise personal assistants rather than with other class participants [+W]
