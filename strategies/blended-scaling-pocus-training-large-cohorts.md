# Scale simulator-based POCUS training to large cohorts through blended strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A scaling recipe the authors propose for delivering simulator-based POCUS training to whole graduating classes: "Scaling to larger cohorts is feasible through blended strategies, including asynchronous e-learning, peer-led group practice, and increased simulator availability", adaptations the authors say "could reduce instructor time while maintaining educational efficacy".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Asynchronous e-learning, peer-led group practice, and increased simulator availability
#### Constraints
- Scaling this training to a graduating class (170 students) presents logistical challenges, primarily since it would require three trainers working 3 days per week throughout the academic year

### Target Learners
- large cohorts of undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- abdominal POCUS procedural skills at scale
