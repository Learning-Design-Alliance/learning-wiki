# Use probe questions during a trigger phase to direct attention to causal variables

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Teachers pose open-ended probe questions during or after free exploration to focus children's attention on causal and non-causal variables without providing direct instructions. The article provides a full table of probe questions for each of the 15 activities, such as "Can you find out what makes the ball rolling faster?" and "How would you investigate this?". Finding the correct answer is explicitly not the goal; the questions serve to provoke systematic investigation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need guidelines and pre-prepared probe questions for each activity, as the intervention teacher received all probe questions in advance.
#### Constraints
- The article notes that no children answered the probe question 'Do large objects always float?' correctly, yet children still engaged in productive experimentation.

### Target Learners
- preschool children aged 4-6 years

### Target Learning Goals
- scientific inquiry skills
- variable isolation
- hypothesis testing
