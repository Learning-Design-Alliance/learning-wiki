# Teach students AI literacy and critical evaluation skills for productive genAI use in STEM coursework

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that students need to be taught how to use genAI tools effectively for learning rather than as shortcuts. It states "there is a need to measure and teach students AI literacy for more effective use of these tools to enhance their learning", and recommends developing critical evaluation mindsets, for example by cross-referencing AI-generated explanations with textbooks and instructor-provided materials. Institutions are also urged to provide free access to reliable, education-focused genAI tools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors and institutions provide guidance and dialogue about when genAI supports learning versus enables cheating
#### Constraints
- The article notes institutional guidance on genAI in STEM courses is often limited and superficial, focused on coding and writing tasks

### Target Learners
- US undergraduate STEM students

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective, learning-enhancing use of generative AI tools
- Critical evaluation of AI-generated content
