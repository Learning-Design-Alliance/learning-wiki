# Combine self-efficacy-building interventions with regular opportunities to experience performance success, in a cyclical fashion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, because self-efficacy and performance reciprocally influence each other, "students would benefit when interventions to enhance self-efficacy are combined with regular opportunities to experience performance success". The authors suggest guided mastery, graded tasks, explicit progress feedback, and framing performance in terms of progress, used together cyclically so each construct feeds the other.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should target both self-efficacy and performance concurrently, since targeting either construct alone risks ignoring half of the reciprocal relationship.
#### Constraints
- The authors caution there is no single guaranteed way to enhance self-efficacy beliefs or performance outcomes, and approaches should be explored with contextual factors and individual students in mind.

### Target Learners
- adult students in tertiary education
- school-aged students

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance
- academic self-efficacy
