# Create structured team learning spaces combining case discussion, expert backup and emotional debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A concrete team-learning recipe in which a recurring meeting on work time follows a 'clinical case' discussion structure applied to IPV cases: one professional presents a case and her response, the group comments and suggests, and external experts or the IPV coordinator present new initiatives. The malaise group doubled as a debriefing space where 'emotions, including anxiety and frustrations, were shared', helping staff 'cope with the emotionally intense consultations'. Around 20 professionals from a total of 35 participated regularly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A committed, credible internal group to lead sessions
- Management respect for professional autonomy and protected time
- Willing voluntary participation
#### Constraints
- Participation varied across professionals; some were overtly resistant and the mechanisms did not trigger for everyone

### Target Learners
- interdisciplinary primary health care teams

### Target Learning Goals
- improving IPV case management skills
- coping with emotionally intense cases
- building self-efficacy in responding to IPV

### Affordances
- Pt2 Team Learning Ipv Programme Theory
