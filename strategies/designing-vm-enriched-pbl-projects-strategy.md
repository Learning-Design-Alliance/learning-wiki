# Design VM-enriched PBL projects aligned with the curriculum, avoiding repetitive activities, tailoring project duration, ensuring adequate VM quality and quantity, and providing well-designed technological infrastructure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's concluding recommendations for implementing VM-enriched PBL in mathematics. The author states that "The VMs in parallel with the curricula make it possible to develop projects and use them in PBL settings" and that to develop good projects, "repetitions in activities should not be allowed, and the duration of the projects should be tailored". Teachers should be encouraged to use VMs since preparing projects with them is not easy, and infrastructure matters for success.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both the quality and the quantity of VMs must be adequate.
- Well-designed technological infrastructure is important for the success of ePBL environments.
- Teachers should be encouraged to use VMs, since it is not easy to prepare projects with VMs.
#### Constraints
- Repetitions in activities should not be allowed, and the duration of the projects should be tailored.

### Target Learners
- 10th-grade mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding of polynomials and quadratic equations
- Academic achievement in mathematics
