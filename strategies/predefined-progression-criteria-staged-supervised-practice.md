# Use predefined progression criteria and staged supervised practice to standardize training in invasive dental procedures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Organize undergraduate training of invasive dental procedures by deconstructing the procedure into teachable microskills, defining transparent criteria for progression, and requiring documented supervised performance before advancement. The article reports this supports more structured supervision and more transparent entrustment decisions while protecting patient safety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Direct observation and documentation of each clinical attempt as 'Pass' or 'Fail' according to predefined criteria.
- Supervisors must verify progression through multiple stages.
#### Constraints
- Should be adapted to local case availability and supervisor workload.
- Predefined progression criteria may increase administrative workload and reduce flexibility.
- Unpredictable patient case-mix can delay progression regardless of student mastery.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate dental students in clinical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- Standardized acquisition of invasive procedural skills
- Transparent entrustment decisions
