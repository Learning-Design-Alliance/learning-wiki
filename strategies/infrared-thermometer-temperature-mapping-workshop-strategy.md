# Run a temperature-mapping field workshop in which student groups measure surface temperatures with infrared thermometers and construct color thermal maps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementable investigative activity: students receive an introductory workshop on heat, temperature and heat islands, then work in groups of up to three in fixed roles (marking points, operating the infrared thermometer, recording values) to measure natural and artificial surfaces, and finally build a two-dimensional color-scale temperature map in SciDAVis. The article states the workshop "proved to be an effective strategy for promoting scientific literacy and engaging participants in the study of environmental impact in urban contexts."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Monitors trained beforehand by a professor on equipment use, activity execution and the analysis software
- A measurement protocol excluding painted or sand-covered surfaces and self-shadow, with standardized step-based spacing
#### Constraints
- Conducted during morning hours (9:00–10:00 a.m.) on three days in November 2024; weather varied across days
- Focused exclusively on surface temperature during a specific period, without soil moisture, precipitation, wind speed or seasonal variables

### Target Learners
- public-school students from the municipal and state systems of the Médio Mearim region

### Target Learning Goals
- scientific literacy
- heat and temperature concepts
- understanding urban heat islands and environmental impact

## Claims

- [Students displayed scientific literacy indicators — hypothesis formulation, argumentation, explanation, organization and information sequencing — without explicit instruction](../claims/literacy-indicators-emerge-without-explicit-instruction.md) [+W]
- [Students articulated a measured temperature gradient from 22°C to 40°C across the courtyard and attributed it to the presence or absence of trees](../claims/student-observed-22-to-40-degree-gradient-trees.md) [+W]
- [After the activity, students proposed planting trees as an intervention to reduce urban heat and predicted that continued deforestation would worsen it](../claims/students-propose-tree-planting-predict-worse-heat.md) [+W]
- [Student-built thermal maps show tree-shaded and vegetated areas with lower temperatures and sun-exposed concrete with higher temperatures, reaching a printed maximum of 43.1°C on the second day](../claims/thermal-map-shade-lower-concrete-higher-temperatures.md) [+W]
