# Teachers can combine educational technologies, or use non-technology-based exploratory learning activities before an ITS, to leverage the combination effect

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the study's findings, the authors recommend that classroom teachers exploit the combination effect: "Teachers can take advantage of the combination effect in their classrooms by combining educational technologies or even engage their students with non-technology-based exploratory learning activities before using an ITS to consolidate what the students have learned." The strategy interleaves exploratory activities that build conceptual understanding with structured practice that consolidates procedures. The authors also suggest the EdTech industry develop standalone ELE technologies or incorporate ELE features into existing products.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to an exploratory learning activity (technology-based or not) followed by structured practice such as an ITS
#### Constraints
- The authors state this possibility warrants further research; effectiveness of classroom technology depends on classroom pedagogy and integration

### Target Learners
- Primary and secondary school mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Joint development of conceptual and procedural mathematical knowledge
