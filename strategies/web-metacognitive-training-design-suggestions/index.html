Design web-based metacognitive training with structural reflection, social interaction, and self-awareness activities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on their findings, the authors recommend that e-learning materials for metacognitive skills integrate "more training on structural reflection, social interaction, and self-awareness". They suggest exploiting computer graphics for deeper reflective training in self-monitor, embedding dialogues and interactions between students and instructors since transfer of metacognitive skills relies on social interaction, and emphasizing the self-observation, self-judgment, and self-reaction processes in self-evaluate design. They also advise allocating time for content review and developing online tests of learning transfer.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors should follow the principles for designing metacognitive instructions and integrate metacognitive strategies such as scaffolding, problem-solving, inquiry, summarization, concept map, and visualization

Constraints¶

  • The authors did not evaluate the effect of learning transfer due to limitation of time, so these suggestions extend beyond their tested results

Target Learners¶

  • college students in web-based self-learning environments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-monitoring, self-modification, and self-evaluation skills; self-learning competence

Claims¶

  • Web-based metacognitive skills training significantly improved college students' self-plan, self-monitor, and total metacognitive scores from pretest to posttest [+W]
  • Only self-plan showed significantly greater gains in the web-trained group compared to the control group [+W]
  • Web-based SRL training interventions produced statistically significant group by time interactions on the SRL subscales planning, self-motivation, volition, elaboration and metacognition, but not on goal-setting or reflection [+W]
  • Web-based SRL training combined with a learning diary (Group TD) produced statistically significant pre-post gains in SRL knowledge, the SRL overall score and self-efficacy, but not in mathematics scores, among prospective university students in an online preparation course [+W]
  • In a randomized trial of SRL interventions in an online mathematics preparation course, group and time interacted significantly on SRL knowledge, self-efficacy and the SRL overall score, with Group TDP (training + diary + peer feedback) showing the most prominent gains [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide explicit teaching of SRL methods as structured guidance for regulating learning in online and blended settings
