# Clinicians develop AI literacy and invite client disclosure of AI use as clinical material to explore in session

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that clinicians and supervisors develop AI literacy so they can explain to clients what AI therapy tools do and where they tend to fail. Rather than ignoring clients' AI use outside of session, clinicians are encouraged to invite disclosure and treat it as clinical material to be explored together, while remaining alert to signs of dependency, social isolation, and inadequate crisis response. This strategy responds to the study's finding that AI agents can function as supplements to, not substitutes for, human therapy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clinicians and supervisors first develop their own AI literacy regarding what these tools do and where they fail
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a pilot study of one trained GPT model in a single mock session; the authors state findings do not reflect other AI models or a variety of clients

### Target Learners
- CFT clinicians and clinical supervisors
- Clients using or considering AI for relational advice

### Target Learning Goals
- Ethical integration of AI into clinical practice
- Recognizing risks of AI dependency and inadequate crisis response
