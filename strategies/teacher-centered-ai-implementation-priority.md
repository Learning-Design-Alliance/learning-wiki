# Prioritize teacher-centered AI implementations that enhance rather than replace human expertise

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The review's forward-looking agenda calls for AI deployments designed around teachers rather than around the technology. It identifies teacher marginalization as one of eight critical gaps and recommends "teacher-centered implementations that enhance rather than replace human expertise" as a future priority. Concretely, the review argues professional development should move beyond tool operation toward pedagogical integration, and that co-design with classroom teachers ensures AI activities fit instructional sequences rather than disrupting established routines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Professional development addressing both content knowledge and pedagogical practice, not just tool operation
- Teachers treated as essential partners in co-designing activities and classroom routines before deployment
#### Constraints
- Elementary teachers are typically generalists rather than STEM specialists with variable content knowledge and pedagogical confidence, and the rapid pace of AI advancement creates a perpetual catch-up cycle

### Target Learners
- Elementary students ages 5-12, taught by generalist elementary teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Integrated STEM learning supported by AI without displacing teacher expertise
