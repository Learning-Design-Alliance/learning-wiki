# Blocks and Legos: Maker Spaces and Fab Labs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Maker spaces and fab labs are shared workshops — physical or embedded in classrooms — where learners design, prototype, and build tangible artifacts, from robots and musical instruments to sewn garments and upcycled sculptures. The strategy treats construction as the engine of learning: learners apply concepts to real materials, iterate on physical feedback, and share what they make. It descends from constructionist theory, which holds that learning is most durable when learners build artifacts that are personally meaningful and publicly shareable.

## Design Implications

Making is a form of [active learning](../principles/active-learning.md) grounded in authentic production, and hands-on construction of concrete artifacts supports engagement and persistence [active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]. Learner choice of project supports autonomy, which strengthens intrinsic motivation [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S], and visible, personally meaningful products raise perceived task value [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]. However, open-ended making without structure can leave novices floundering; effective maker programs pair freedom with [coaching](../elements/coaching.md), just-in-time instruction, and critique routines.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated space with tools and materials (from hot glue and scrap wood to 3D printers and laser cutters)
- Instructor or mentor guidance and safety training; unstructured access alone rarely produces learning
- Collaboration structures and a culture of sharing work-in-progress, not just finished products
- A driving project or prompt that connects the build to target concepts ([Application](../elements/application.md))

#### Constraints
- High setup and maintenance costs and staffing demands; under-resourced spaces often devolve into unguided play [-W]
- Open-ended building without scaffolds overwhelms novices, who lack the prior knowledge to direct their own inquiry [~M] — the expertise-reversal pattern applies to making as to other minimally guided approaches
- Physical artifact production can consume time and attention, crowding out the conceptual reflection that consolidates learning [-W]
- Assessment is difficult: polished products can mask shallow understanding of underlying principles [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Low-tech maker corners (cardboard, blocks, Legos) vs. digital fab labs (3D printing, CNC, electronics)
- Structured challenges with fixed constraints vs. open studio time with learner-chosen projects
- Embedded in a course (e.g., engineering design) vs. extracurricular club or community space
- Individual builds vs. team projects with assigned roles ([Collaboration](../elements/collaboration.md))

### Target Learners
- K–12 through adult learners; particularly strong for middle and secondary students, where identity-relevant making builds confidence and persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Learners underrepresented in STEM, when spaces are deliberately designed for equity rather than access alone [~W]
- Less effective for complete novices without mentorship; guided entry points matter more as prior knowledge decreases [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Applying theoretical knowledge to authentic problems ([whole-task performance improves transfer.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [+M])
- Design thinking, iteration, and troubleshooting through physical feedback
- Collaboration, communication, and critique through shared making
- Creativity and self-directed exploration

### Instructions
1. Frame a driving challenge or invite project proposals tied to target concepts ([Challenge Identification](../elements/challenge-identification.md)).
2. Have learners plan and sketch designs before touching materials ([Application](../elements/application.md)).
3. Support building with just-in-time mini-lessons and [coaching](../elements/coaching.md) rather than front-loaded lectures.
4. Build in structured iteration: test, get [feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md), revise.
5. Close with public sharing and reflection that makes the underlying concepts explicit ([Articulation](../elements/articulation.md)).

## Examples
- **Fab Foundation fab labs** ([https://www.fabfoundation.org](https://www.fabfoundation.org)) — a global network of digital fabrication labs offering K–12 and community programs with standard toolsets (laser cutters, 3D printers, electronics).
- **Maker Ed** ([https://makered.org](https://makered.org)) — supports educator-run maker programs in schools; its "maker education" framework pairs open building with structured reflection routines.
- **MIT Fab Lab program** — the original fab lab model, connecting design software to rapid prototyping equipment for community learners.
- A high school physics class prototyping energy-efficient devices in a fab lab, testing designs and revising based on measured output.
