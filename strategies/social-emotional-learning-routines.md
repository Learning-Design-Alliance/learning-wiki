# Social Emotional Learning Routines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Social Emotional Learning (SEL) routines are brief, repeated, predictable practices — such as daily check-ins, emotion-naming rituals, cooperative norms review, or structured reflection — embedded into the regular flow of instruction. Rather than treating social-emotional competence as a one-off curriculum unit, routines distribute its development across many small, low-stakes repetitions, making self-regulation and interpersonal skills habitual alongside academic work.

## Design Implications

SEL routines work through repetition and predictability: consistent rituals lower the cognitive and emotional cost of self-regulation by making it automatic, freeing attention for academic content [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Universal, school-wide SEL programming produces measurable gains in both social-emotional skills and academic achievement [Universal school-based SEL programs improve skills, attitudes, and academic achievement.](../claims/sel-programs-improve-academic-achievement.md) [+S], and effects persist years after programming ends [SEL benefits persist at follow-up.](../claims/sel-benefits-persist-follow-up.md) [+M]. Routines also function as belonging and climate interventions: predictable relational rituals signal safety and membership, which supports engagement [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Predictable timing and placement (e.g., opening of class, transitions, closing reflection) so the routine becomes automatic
- Explicit naming of the skill being practiced (e.g., "we are labeling emotions," "we are taking perspective") so students can transfer it
- A psychologically safe climate; routines that require emotional disclosure fail without trust and clear norms
- Teacher modeling of the target skills — routines are [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) opportunities for adult self-regulation [Social learning theory.](../theories/social-learning-theory.md)

#### Constraints
- Effects are weaker when SEL is delivered as isolated lessons disconnected from academic content rather than embedded in daily practice [~M]
- Poorly facilitated disclosure-based routines (e.g., forced sharing of emotions) can harm students with trauma histories or heighten social risk in low-trust classrooms [-W]
- Implementation quality drives outcomes; under-trained teachers delivering scripted SEL content show substantially attenuated effects [~S]
- Routines consume instructional time; benefits depend on the self-regulation gains offsetting that cost, which is less likely in tightly time-pressured test-preparation contexts [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Opening/closing rituals**: brief check-ins or mood meters (e.g., RULER's Charter and Mood Meter) at class boundaries
- **Transition routines**: breathing or attention resets between activities, common in mindfulness-based programs
- **Academic-embedded routines**: [Think-Pair-Share](../patterns/think-pair-share.md) with explicit listening norms, or [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) protocols that require perspective-taking
- **Restorative routines**: circles and repair conversations replacing purely punitive responses [Brief empathic-discipline interventions cut suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Elementary and early secondary students, where self-regulation is developing most rapidly [Universal school-based SEL programs improve skills, attitudes, and academic achievement.](../claims/sel-programs-improve-academic-achievement.md) [+S]
- Students from contexts of chronic stress, for whom predictable routines provide external regulation supports [~M]
- Students experiencing belonging uncertainty; relational rituals signal membership [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Older students benefit more from routines framed as autonomy-supportive choices than from compliance-oriented rituals [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-management: emotion labeling, attention regulation, impulse control
- Social awareness and empathy: perspective-taking and intergroup understanding [Building empathy improves intergroup attitudes.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M]
- Classroom climate prerequisites: the relational foundation that [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) and discussion-based formats depend on

### Instructions
1. **Choose a small, repeatable anchor practice** — e.g., a one-minute [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) at the start of class using an emotion scale or mood meter.
2. **Model the skill explicitly** — the teacher demonstrates naming and managing their own state, enacting [Social Learning Theory](../theories/social-learning-theory.md) through adult modeling.
3. **Name the skill and its purpose** — brief labeling ("this helps us notice when we're distracted") supports transfer beyond the routine.
4. **Embed the routine in academic activity** — pair it with cooperative structures such as [Think-Pair-Share](../patterns/think-pair-share.md) or [Jigsaw](../elements/jigsaw.md) so social skills are practiced on real content.
5. **Keep it brief and fade scaffolds** — as self-management becomes automatic, reduce teacher prompting, consistent with [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) and fading.
6. **Close the loop with reflection** — periodic brief reflection or [Check-Ins](../principles/check-ins.md) on how the class's norms are working.

## Examples
- **[RULER](https://medicine.yale.edu/childstudy/services/community-and-schools-programs/ruler/)** (Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence) — the Mood Meter check-in and class Charter are routines for emotion labeling and norm-setting; evaluated in randomized school trials.
- **[Responsive Classroom](https://www.responsiveclassroom.org)** — the Morning Meeting is a structured daily routine combining greeting, sharing, and activity to build community and self-management.
- **[Second Step](https://www.secondstep.org)** — weekly lessons paired with daily "moment to practice" routines that reinforce emotion-regulation strategies between lessons.
