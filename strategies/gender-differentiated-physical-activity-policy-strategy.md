# Differentiate physical activity promotion policies by gender for adolescents experiencing depressive emotions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the indirect effect of depression on academic performance through peer relationships differed by gender and activity level, the authors recommend gender-sensitive policy. They write that "for adolescents experiencing depressive emotions, it may be important to differentiate policies to promote physical activity according to gender to enhance policy efficiency." They also call for research on sports emphasizing femininity, noting that physical education classes in China favor masculine sports such as soccer and basketball, which may explain why activity did not help female students' peer relationships.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Policy design must account for gender differences in how sports participation relates to peer acceptance
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on one secondary longitudinal analysis; the authors note gender identity regarding physical activity was not considered and self-report measures may carry social bias

### Target Learners
- male and female middle school adolescents with depressive emotions

### Target Learning Goals
- supporting academic performance via peer relationships and physical activity policy
