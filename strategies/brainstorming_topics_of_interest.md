# Brainstorming Topics of Interest

## Description
Brainstorming an ongoing class list of stuff learners want to know about and are interested in—a phenomenon, an event, or a law, for example. If teaching science, math, or history, require topics to be about the field, but also encourage general topics (and then find connections to content later).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires teachers to provide clear guidelines and boundaries for topic selection. Classroom environment needs to support open sharing and respect for diverse interests.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging for learners who struggle to identify personal interests or connect them to academic content. Requires careful facilitation to ensure all voices are heard and that the discussion stays focused and productive.

### Affordances
- Increases motivation and engagement by allowing learners to explore topics of personal interest. Connects learning to real-world events and phenomena. Supports active participation and knowledge-sharing.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted by allowing learners to propose topics in various formats (written, verbal, digital). Topics can be tailored to learner interests and backgrounds. Flexibility in topic selection can support diverse learning preferences.
