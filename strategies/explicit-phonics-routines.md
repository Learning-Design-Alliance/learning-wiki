# Explicit Phonics Routines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Explicit phonics routines are structured, teacher-directed sequences for teaching grapheme–phoneme correspondences and how to blend them into words. A typical routine moves from teacher modeling ("this letter says /m/"), to guided practice with immediate correction, to independent reading and spelling of words containing only taught patterns. Instruction follows a planned scope and sequence from simple to complex, with new content introduced in small steps and previously taught patterns reviewed cumulatively.

## Design Implications

Systematic, explicit phonics produces stronger word-reading and generalization outcomes than incidental or whole-word approaches, because it teaches learners the alphabetic code rather than asking them to infer it [Systematic phonics instruction outperforms whole-word and incidental approaches for generalization to new words.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [+S]. As letter patterns become automatized, decoding consumes fewer working-memory resources, freeing capacity for comprehension [Automatic word recognition frees cognitive resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]. Routines work because they manage intrinsic load: each lesson introduces one or two correspondences at a time [Chunking reduces working-memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M], and cumulative review distributes retrieval of earlier patterns over time [Spaced repetition improves retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A logical scope and sequence: high-utility correspondences first, decodable text matched to what has been taught
- Clear teacher modeling with precise articulation of sounds (unambiguous pronunciation, no schwa added)
- Immediate, corrective feedback during guided practice, with error correction routines (model → lead → test)
- Cumulative review built into every lesson, not left to end-of-unit assessment
- Decodable practice materials so learners apply the taught code, not guess from pictures or context

#### Constraints
- Effectiveness drops sharply when instruction is unsystematic — teaching correspondences as they happen to appear in texts — or when phonics is taught in isolation without application to real reading and writing [Systematic phonics instruction outperforms whole-word and incidental approaches for generalization to new words.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [~S]
- Overly long, decontextualized drill displaces time for connected text and vocabulary, with no compensating benefit; phonics is a means to comprehension, not an end
- Rigid pacing that ignores assessment data leaves some learners practicing unmastered patterns while others are held back; routines must be paired with quick checks and regrouping
- For learners who have already automatized the taught patterns, continued explicit drill adds little and can reduce engagement [Guidance that helps novices can be redundant or counterproductive for experts.](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Synthetic phonics** (blend sounds into words from the start, as in [Jolly Phonics](https://www.jollylearning.co.uk) or [Sounds-Write](https://www.sounds-write.co.uk)) versus **analytic phonics** (analyze sounds within whole words already known); synthetic approaches show stronger effects in most comparisons [+M]
- **Embedded in a full lesson arc** (review → new content → word work → dictation → decodable text) versus delivered as a short standalone drill block
- **Small-group, data-matched grouping** versus whole-class delivery; both work, but grouping allows tighter pacing and more response opportunities

### Target Learners
- Beginning readers in kindergarten through grade 2, who are still building the alphabetic principle [Systematic phonics instruction outperforms whole-word and incidental approaches for generalization to new words.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [+S]
- Older struggling readers and students with dyslexia, who typically need more repetitions, smaller steps, and longer-lasting cumulative review than typical readers
- English learners, who benefit from explicit teaching of correspondences that differ from their first language's orthography
- Least beneficial for fluent readers who have already consolidated the code [Guidance that helps novices can be redundant or counterproductive for experts.](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate, automatic decoding of unfamiliar words [Automatic word recognition frees cognitive resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Encoding (spelling) aligned with taught patterns
- Generalization: reading new words independently rather than memorizing a sight vocabulary [Systematic phonics instruction outperforms whole-word and incidental approaches for generalization to new words.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. **Open with cumulative review.** Flash previously taught graphemes and words for rapid retrieval; distribute older items across days [Spaced repetition improves retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S]
2. **Model the new correspondence.** Present the grapheme, articulate the phoneme cleanly, and give a key-word anchor; keep the step small and isolated [Chunking reduces working-memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
3. **Guide blending practice.** Lead students through sound-by-sound blending into whole words containing only taught patterns, using an error-correction routine (model → lead → test) drawn from [Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md)
4. **Move to independent application.** Students read decodable words, sentences, and connected text, then spell (dictate) words with the same patterns — reading and spelling reinforce each other [Practice](../elements/practice.md)
5. **Assess and regroup.** Use brief checks to confirm mastery before adding new correspondences; reteach or regroup students who have not mastered current patterns

## Related Strategies

## Examples
- **[Sounds-Write](https://www.sounds-write.co.uk)** — a UK synthetic phonics program with a scripted four-part lesson routine (code, word-building, word-reading, sound swapping) and a fixed scope and sequence
- **[Jolly Phonics](https://www.jollylearning.co.uk)** — teaches 42 letter sounds with actions and songs, then moves to blending and segmenting routines
- **[University of Florida Literacy Institute (UFLI) Foundations](https://ufli.education.ufl.edu/foundations/)** — an explicit, systematic routine with daily review, new-concept teaching, word work, dictation, and decodable reading; freely available lesson materials
- **[Reading Simplified](https://readingsimplified.com)** — a streamlined blending routine ("Switch It," "Read It") that compresses the modeling–guided–independent arc into short small-group sessions
