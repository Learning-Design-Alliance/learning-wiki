# Teachers should intervene to distribute help relations more equally, potentially through peer tutoring interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that inequality in help networks is associated with lower achievement, the authors recommend that "teachers should intervene in a classroom in such a way that help relations become more equally distributed". They suggest integrating this idea into peer tutoring interventions where students are grouped to mentor each other. The authors also caution that teacher attempts to foster relations through grouping may produce adverse outcomes, and suggest network information may be most useful when teachers cooperate with students in interpreting it.

## Design Implications
