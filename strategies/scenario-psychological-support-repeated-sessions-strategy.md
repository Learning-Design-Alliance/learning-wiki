# Design simulation scenarios that explicitly address psychological support and system knowledge, and use repeated sessions to affect stress

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendations from the discussion and conclusion: future simulation training should "incorporate structured scenarios that systematically address psychological support and health care system knowledge", and "repeated simulation sessions may be necessary to impact stress", since a single session did not change perceived stress levels. They also recommend longitudinal follow-up to assess long-term retention of self-confidence gained through simulation-based education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios must be structured to systematically address psychological support and health care system knowledge
- Repeated simulation sessions rather than a single session to affect stress
#### Constraints
- In this trial a single simulation session did not produce significant short-term changes in perceived stress levels
- The scenario's focus on clinical shock interventions rather than systematic psychological support may explain the null between-group psychological support difference

### Target Learners
- Senior undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-confidence in patient care interventions
- Psychological support skills
- Stress management during patient interventions
