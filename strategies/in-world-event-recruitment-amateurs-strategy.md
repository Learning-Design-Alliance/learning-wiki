# Recruit citizen science contributors through in-world events targeting genuinely interested amateurs rather than relying on online media or forced participation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends reaching genuinely interested amateurs in person: "genuinely interested amateurs reached in -world seem to be the most fertile ground for recruiting intrinsically motivated citizen cyberscientists". The beta used four recruitment strategies — forced classroom sign-ups, public talks, online/media coverage, and unknown community buzz — plus featured challenges with badges and prizes (books, mugs, t-shirts, a lab visit) to bring existing players back. The authors frame motivational devices in a two-dimensional space of in-game versus in-world situation and intrinsic versus extrinsic flavor, arguing engagement is a question of balancing design strategies rather than relying on a single approach such as vulgar points-badges-leaderboards gamification.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Public talks and events with real physicists, plus a game attractive enough that even non-contributing visitors add network value
#### Constraints
- Forced participation yields players who play little and mostly drop out quickly; extrinsic reward has been found to exact a detrimental effect on intrinsic motivations (as the article cites)

### Target Learners
- adult amateurs interested in science
- high-school students
- public lecture audiences

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained participation in citizen science gameplay
- skill development toward scientific contribution
