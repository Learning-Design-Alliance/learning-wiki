# When planning simulation-based medical education, prioritise authentic roles, realistic teamwork and debriefing over the physical simulation site, using off-site simulation where convenient

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's design recommendation from the focus groups is that simulation planners should secure authenticity elements other than location: authentic roles in participants' own professions, realistic multidisciplinary teamwork, well-organised practical details and structured debriefing. The authors conclude that "OSS can be used provided that all other authenticity elements are taken into consideration and respected". This lets institutions use training rooms instead of clinical areas without sacrificing perceived learning value, while reserving in situ simulation when organisational insights are the goal.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation must include participants working in their authentic professional roles with their usual colleagues
- Debriefing must be included as a central part of the simulation activity
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on participant perceptions in one hospital's obstetric and anaesthesia departments and needs confirmation in other institutions, specialities and less simulation-experienced groups

### Target Learners
- healthcare professionals in obstetric and anaesthesia emergency teams
- interprofessional simulation participants

### Target Learning Goals
- interprofessional teamwork and communication
- emergency scenario management
- transfer of learning to clinical practice

- Context Dimensions Fidelity Framework Simulation
