State policy actions across the career continuum: attract, prepare, and develop/support/retain educators¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The document enumerates concrete state policy actions organized in three sections with numbered components and subcomponents describing practices used by states and districts. Attract strategies include grow-your-own programs, university-district partnerships, licensure reciprocity, and career path approaches; prepare strategies include aligned certification standards and program approval; develop/support/retain strategies include comprehensive induction, job-embedded professional learning, tiered licensure, competitive compensation, and working-conditions supports. Components were identified based on research summarized in the Educator Talent Management Framework (Meyer, Nelson, & Mann, 2019) and created with content experts.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Coordination among state, district, and school levels, since neither states nor districts and schools can achieve success on their own

Constraints¶

  • State influence on some components, such as recruitment and hiring and working conditions, is more limited than that of local decision makers

Target Learners¶

  • State education agencies
  • District leaders
  • Educator preparation programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Equitable student access to effective teachers and leaders through strategic educator talent management

Affordances¶

  • Talent Development Framework Three Sections

Related Strategies¶

  • CEO Forum recommendations: prepare and certify teachers for technology integration across pre-service, licensure, and in-service career stages
  • Districts should map virtual-learning and equity actions onto the seven domains of a comprehensive, aligned principal pipeline
  • Grow-your-own teacher training programs targeting community members facing certification and career-change barriers
  • Grow-your-own and recruitment initiatives: alternative programs, Minority Serving Institutions, high-school pipeline programs, and district human-capital efforts to diversify the teacher workforce.
  • Grow-your-own teacher pipelines and institutionalized peer-based professional learning to build a qualified newcomer-school workforce
