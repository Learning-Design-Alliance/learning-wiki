Start simple: relationships-first, low-tech student success systems can suffice in small, tight-knit communities¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Drawing on Demopolis High School's experience, the report recommends that districts "Focus on relationships first," design systems that let staff see student history across grade levels and years, and adopt a "Just Start" mentality of beginning with a manual solution rather than waiting for the ideal system. It concludes that "Effective student success systems don't require sophisticated technology, particularly in a tight-knit community where people know and have access to each other," and that simpler solutions may be better and more cost effective where feedback loops are small and local and the team shares a commitment to keeping every student on track.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Shared staff commitment to keeping every student on track
  • Reasonably small, local feedback loops

Constraints¶

  • The report frames this as applying where feedback loops are reasonably small and local, not as a universal prescription

Target Learners¶

  • students in small, rural schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • staying on track for graduation through early identification and mentoring

Related Strategies¶

  • Small school reformers should intentionally build professional community, principal leadership, and teacher influence rather than relying on size reduction
  • Conduct ed-tech pilots by articulating the instructional need, setting clear product-review criteria, involving educators in planning, supporting educators throughout, gathering data from all users, deciding from data, negotiating purchasing options, and sharing results
  • Know every student well through many adults, multi-year teacher loops, and flexible small-group instruction
