Implement ABSL lessons through structured argumentation activities sequenced within the 5E instructional model¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (3 causal), q1–q2 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's implementation strategy organizes ABSL teaching as a weekly plan of argumentation activities — concept cartoons, competing theories, predict-observe-explain, expressions tables, news, and a migration story — delivered within the "5E model steps (i.e., Engage, Explore, Explain, Extend, and Evaluate)". Materials and the implementation process were planned with the ADDIE instructional design model, students worked in groups of five, and a mysterious event activity opened lessons to raise interest before subject teaching began.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A teacher guideline and weekly lesson plan specifying subjects and activities for each topic
  • An initial familiarization period; the authors assisted implementation in the first three weeks so students understood the activities
  • A pilot implementation to identify and correct deficiencies in activities before actual implementation

Constraints¶

  • Students reported initial difficulties due to inexperience with the approach and problems in the discussion process

Target Learners¶

  • seventh-grade middle school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • social studies unit content mastery
  • argument construction and justification skills

Affordances¶

  • Absl Argumentation Based Science Learning Approach

Claims¶

  • Students most liked the competing theories, news, and concept cartoon activities because these made creating and defending arguments easier [+W]
  • Seventh-grade students taught social studies with the ABSL approach scored significantly higher on post-test academic achievement than students in two traditionally taught control groups, with a large effect size [+M]
  • Before implementation, the experimental and control groups showed no statistically significant difference in academic achievement pre-test scores [+W]
  • Students reported difficulties with the ABSL implementation, most commonly problems in the discussion process and difficulties creating arguments in concept cartoon and migration story activities [~W]
  • Students reported that ABSL activities helped them understand subjects better, learn permanently, become more active, and increased their interest and attitudes towards social studies [+W]
  • Students' group communication during concept cartoon© lessons depended mainly on the teaching topic, and scientific terms were never used [~W]
  • Students' written opinions indicated they enjoyed concept-cartoon lessons, attended more, and came to believe they could understand mathematics [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use Toulmin-based argumentation activities (developing arguments, competing theories, predict-observe-explain, expression tables, concept maps) with small-group techniques to teach science units.
