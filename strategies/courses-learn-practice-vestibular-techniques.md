# Organise courses in which GPs and GP trainees learn and regularly practice repositioning manoeuvres and VR to build and maintain confidence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends hands-on training as the response to the skill gap it documents: "courses should be organized to learn and practice these techniques so GPs and GP trainees get and maintain confidence in the ability to perform the treatment." Because the skills "should not only be trained but also practiced on a regular basis," courses must support repeated practice, not one-off instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must include regular practice opportunities so GPs gain and maintain confidence in executing the techniques
#### Constraints
- Skills decay without regular practice; GPs who perform the manoeuvres infrequently may not gain confidence in their execution

### Target Learners
- GPs and GP trainees in primary care

### Target Learning Goals
- Performing canalith repositioning manoeuvres and vestibular rehabilitation competently and confidently
