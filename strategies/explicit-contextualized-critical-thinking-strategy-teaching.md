# Teach critical thinking strategies explicitly, within subject matter, with staged practice and fading cues

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the evidence that critical thinking must be nested in content, the article recommends that "Thinking critically should be taught in the context of subject matter," with strategies made explicit and practiced in stages: explain a strategy with at least two examples, label it, apply it to course content, and then progressively reduce cueing until students identify the applicable strategy on their own. It also recommends drawing on student experiences to introduce complex concepts when domain knowledge is thin, and rejects standalone critical thinking programs as poor value given their opportunity costs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Subject-matter context for each strategy
- Opportunities for repeated practice with feedback from a skilled teacher
#### Constraints
- Standalone special programs show very limited success, especially for novel situations; gains shrink as transfer materials become dissimilar from program materials

### Target Learners
- K-12 students, including struggling students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical thinking
- scientific reasoning
- historical source evaluation
