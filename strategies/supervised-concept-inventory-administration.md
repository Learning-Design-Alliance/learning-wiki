# Administer concept inventories in supervised, in-person settings rather than as take-home assignments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because chatbots achieve near-perfect scores on most CRCI items, the authors recommend that instructors using concept inventories for formative or summative assessment administer them in supervised, in-person settings so responses reflect genuine student reasoning. They also advise communicating to students that chatbot accuracy is item-dependent, since high average performance does not guarantee reliability on any specific question, and that correct answers may come with flawed reasoning (false positives).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must know which specific items current chatbots fail on, since failures are severe and unpredictable
#### Constraints
- Based on a snapshot of December 2025–January 2026 models; future models may overcome the documented visual and reasoning difficulties

### Target Learners
- university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- valid measurement of conceptual understanding of physics

## Claims

- [Frontier LLMs outperform the student average on most CRCI items, with mean accuracies of 97.0% (Gemini 3 Flash), 88.6% (Gemini 3 Pro) and 73.3% (GPT-5.2) versus 61.5% for students](../claims/llms-outperform-students-on-crcl.md) [+W]
