# Use baseline questionnaires prior to educational interventions to identify problem areas for targeted CME

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The PRIMAIR study used an author-constructed, patient-case-based questionnaire administered before a CME intervention to map which guideline recommendations GPs poorly recognised or carried out. The questionnaire "was designed as an examination and followed the educational principle of constructive alignment developed by John Biggs", aligning assessment with the intended learning outcomes of the planned intervention. The authors suggest this approach lets educators concentrate efforts on weak areas such as spirometry interpretation and multimorbidity management.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questionnaire items must be constructed on the basis of national guidelines and known real-life problems in the target clinicians' practice
- A predefined, detailed correction template with scored responses (0–2 points) is needed before administration
#### Constraints
- Participants were unaware of scoring methods and received no feedback, to avoid influencing later recall and post-intervention scores
- The questionnaire revealed only theoretical knowledge and self-reported preferred actions, not actual clinical practice

### Target Learners
- practising general practitioners in primary care

### Target Learning Goals
- evidence-based COPD diagnosis and management
- adherence to clinical guidelines
