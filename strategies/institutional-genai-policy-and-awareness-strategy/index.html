Develop clear institutional GenAI policies, communicate them, raise awareness, and promote digital literacy¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that institutions respond to student confusion about GenAI by developing clear institutional policies, communicating and adhering to them, creating awareness, and promoting digital literacy. It states that "developing clear institutional policies, communicating and adhering to the policies, creating awareness, and promoting digital literacy seem to be essential." It further cautions that having a policy is not sufficient: policies must be comprehensive, clear, and well communicated, and rules must be reinforced and their effectiveness continuously assessed.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Policies that are comprehensive, clear, and well communicated to all stakeholders
  • Continuous reinforcement and assessment of policy effectiveness

Constraints¶

  • The article notes that with existing policies students found the rules vague and faculty lacked training in enforcing them

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in higher education
  • higher education instructors and institutions

Target Learning Goals¶

  • ethical use of generative AI
  • academic integrity

Related Strategies¶

  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
  • Institutions should respond to GenAI with clear governance frameworks and structured professional training programs for staff.
  • Build ethical AI integration on four coordinated levels: faculty professional development, ethics curricula, clear institutional guidelines, and interdisciplinary updating
