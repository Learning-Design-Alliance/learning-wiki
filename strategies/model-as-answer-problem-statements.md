# Model-as-answer problem solving: assign few rich modeling problems and grade the model, not the numeric answer

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A problem-design and grading strategy in which standard textbook problems are rewritten so the required answer is a complete model rather than numeric results, and few problems are assigned because rich models take substantial time. The article states "By assigning smaller numbers of problems (on the order of 2-3/week) students see that the emphasis is on quality and richness of the model rather than right answers." Figure 1 contrasts a standard block-drawn-by-rope problem with a modeling version asking students to "Construct a complete Model of the following situation." The article adds that "the grade must reflect whether or not the student actually created a model, rather than whether an answer is achieved."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Carefully chosen problems whose answer is a model; revised grading practices; acceptance of fewer assigned problems
#### Constraints
- The article notes "it takes significantly more time to create a rich model than to answer a standard physics problem" and that changing grading "is the most challenging for instructors new to modeling"

### Target Learners
- university introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- model construction as the goal of problem solving
- rich multi-representational reasoning
