# Vary physical contexts and practice reflection skills when teaching boundary conditions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the observed difficulties, the authors recommend that instruction shift boundary-condition practice beyond the E&M-wave context and vary problem setups. They note that in their physics class "students often only practice on simple situations (e.g. boundary z=0) and this may cause them to over-generalize the results to other situations", so it is likely worthwhile to present both simple and messier situations. They also recommend practice on recognizing unphysical limits and taking limits with appropriate independent variables to develop reflection skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need access to a variety of physical situations, including non-wave (e.g. static) contexts and boundaries not located at z=0
#### Constraints
- The recommendations are based on a preliminary investigation at CU and affiliated institutions; the authors present them as potential pedagogical implications rather than tested interventions

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students in electrodynamics

### Target Learning Goals
- activating boundary conditions across contexts
- reflecting on results by checking units and limiting behaviors
