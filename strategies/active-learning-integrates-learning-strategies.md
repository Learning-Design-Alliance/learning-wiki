# Use active learning in introductory college science courses as an instrument for teaching transferable study skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors interpret their findings—the higher A rates in subsequent traditional-format courses and the higher graduation rate—by suggesting that "one of the most important functions of introductory college science classes may be to teach students transferable study skills." They recommend active learning as "a good instrument for integrating learning strategies into a curriculum," and note other community colleges may benefit from examining how changing teaching paradigms affects future-course performance and graduation rates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor buy-in to implement active-learning methods in introductory courses
#### Constraints
- The improvement observed was associated with one first-semester course at a single community college; component contributions could not be teased apart

### Target Learners
- Community college students, particularly those beginning in STEM fields of study

### Target Learning Goals
- Transferable study skills, persistence, and graduation from associate-degree programs
