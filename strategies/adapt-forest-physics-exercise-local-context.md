# Adapt the forest physics exercise to other settings and disciplines by preserving its salient points

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose transposing the exercise to other universities by keeping its salient points — low-cost experiments outside labs, letting students figure out an interesting problem to investigate, autonomy and freedom over the project, and creative presentation of results. They stress that “Nature is not limited to the forest, and this exercise could easily be adapted to other places: a meadow, an urban park, or the streets of a city.”

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adaptations to the local context; the two consecutive-day format can become a short one-day version, a longer version with more elaborate projects, or a hybrid mixing classical environmental-physics sessions with outdoor sessions.
#### Constraints
- Plan ahead for different teaching scenarios depending on the conditions, since weather can impact an outdoor exercise.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students at other universities
- students in other experimental disciplines, particularly life sciences

### Target Learning Goals
- using physics to explore environmental issues
- applying freshly learned knowledge in a natural setting
