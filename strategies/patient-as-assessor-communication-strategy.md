# Use patients as assessors of trainee communication to lessen resource demands of standardized patients and OSCEs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using the patient as the assessor of communication skills: "Using the patient as the assessor may lessen the resource utilization and funding needs often required of these more traditional modalities." Unlike standardized patients and direct observation, patient ratings come from the primary participants of the doctor–patient relationship and may yield more distinct evaluative encounters and increased feedback. The authors note an ED attending, nurse, or tech could administer the five-minute survey instead of research assistants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mechanism to notify staff when a student takes a patient, eligible discharged patients, and a staff member to administer the CAT and deliver feedback
#### Constraints
- Only discharged patients were surveyed, skewing the population to lower acuity; the Hawthorne effect may have influenced student performance and patient responses

### Target Learners
- medical students and trainees in clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- communication and interpersonal skills assessment and feedback
