# Integrate anxiety management and psychological-safety practices into simulation planning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that anxiety in simulation contexts be treated as a design concern: educators should establish clear learning objectives, encourage learning rather than perfection, allow time-outs when needed, minimize unconscious bias, and conduct constructive debriefings focused on strengths and opportunities for improvement. Pre-briefing and debriefing are described as strategic opportunities to build trust, and careful calibration of scenario difficulty is described as essential to sustain motivation while avoiding overwhelm.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators establish clear learning objectives and conduct constructive debriefings focused on strengths and opportunities for improvement
#### Constraints
- The article states excessive anxiety and distress may compromise the attainment of learning objectives, and that some students perceive simulation scenarios as even more stressful than real clinical settings

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students in simulation-based curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical and communication skills development
- psychological safety and adaptive coping in simulated patient encounters
