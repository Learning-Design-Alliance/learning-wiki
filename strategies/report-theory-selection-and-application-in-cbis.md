# Report selection and application of theory explicitly when designing computer-based behavior-change interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that developers of computer-based interventions give greater emphasis to selecting and applying theories of behavior change and to reporting how theory informed design and evaluation. It argues that clearly articulating theory use helps explain not just what interventions work but why, and states that "greater emphasis on the selection and application of theory is needed". The classification system it used is offered as a reporting framework for this purpose.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Intervention articles should name the broad theory, describe how it was applied, and include measures of theoretical constructs in evaluations
#### Constraints
- Based on a review of alcohol-use CBIs for youth aged 12-21; the authors note the field's reporting practices currently fall short of this standard

### Target Learners
- adolescents and young adults aged 12-21
