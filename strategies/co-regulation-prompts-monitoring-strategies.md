# Use co-regulated learning prompts and multimodal scaffolds to explicitly teach monitoring strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on a co-regulated learning model, the authors recommend that instructors or curriculum developers share the cognitive and metacognitive thinking load with learners to develop monitoring strategies, since these strategies "can be explicitly taught with opportunities for repeated practice." The article offers sample educator prompts, such as "What is the relationship between this content and other content?" and "How can you visually organize these concepts into a chart, graph, diagram?", and suggests scaffolding through multimodal representations such as a concept map, flow chart, or graphic organizer integrated into learning activities. The support is an interpretation of this study's findings rather than a tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators or curriculum developers with expertise willing to scaffold students; opportunities for repeated practice of monitoring strategies
#### Constraints
- The prompts are proposed as potential aids, not validated in the study; the finding base is 26 students in one curriculum

### Target Learners
- medical students transitioning from lecture-based to flipped, competency-based curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- monitoring-phase self-regulated learning strategies such as organizing, summarizing, and applying content
