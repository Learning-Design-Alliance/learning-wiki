# Develop and share international teach-the-teacher courses on problem-based learning to expand CPT teaching capacity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends an international teach-the-teacher course on problem-based learning as a means to expand CPT teaching capacity. Participants who experienced a shortage of teaching colleagues "agreed that an international teach-the-teacher course on problem-based learning may help to attract more teachers or enable pharmacists and other paramedical professionals to qualify to teach CPT." The platform should also keep an overview of new teaching innovations and offer a discussion board where teachers can ask each other for help.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Attention to good practice in faculty development
- Appeal to the intrinsic motivation of teachers rather than external motivators
#### Constraints
- Whether high-quality teach-the-teacher courses will actually help attract new CPT teachers remains to be seen, according to the authors

### Target Learners
- CPT teachers and faculty
- Pharmacists and other paramedical professionals who could qualify to teach CPT

### Target Learning Goals
- Problem-based teaching of clinical pharmacology and therapeutics
- Faculty development in prescribing education
