# Classroom strategies to develop metacognition: reflective questions, decision diagrams, thinking aloud, reflective dialogue, and metacognitive argumentation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article enumerates strategies for promoting metacognitive work at individual and interactional levels. Individually, students apply meta-knowledge questions (e.g., "Do I know how much I know about this subject?"), use decision diagrams that associate YES/NO alternatives to each step of a task, and think aloud to make thoughts explicit and conscious. Interactionally, teachers use reflective dialogue, reflective feedback, and metacognitive argumentation, where questioning reasons and identifying counterarguments increases depth and validity of argumentative structure.
