# Use Kirkpatrick's Four-Level Model to evaluate simulation-based training programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 mixed) · 6 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 3 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 6 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends evaluating SBT programs with Kirkpatrick's Four-Level Training Evaluation Model, which "assesses training programs at four levels: reaction, learning, behavior, and results". Reaction is measured through satisfaction surveys, learning through pre- and post-tests, behavior through follow-up assessments in real clinical settings, and results through impact on patient outcomes and organizational performance. Studies applying the model showed improved skills and patient care quality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment tools such as checklists and rating scales must be rigorously developed and validated
- Inter-rater reliability must be established for consistent scoring across evaluators
#### Constraints
- Assessment bias from instructors' familiarity with learners can be mitigated by blind assessments and multiple evaluators
- High-fidelity simulations and OSCEs require significant time, equipment, and personnel investment

### Target Learners
- Medical students and residents undergoing simulation training

### Target Learning Goals
- Valid and reliable measurement of clinical competencies acquired through simulation training
