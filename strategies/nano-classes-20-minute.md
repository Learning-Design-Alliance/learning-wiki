# Use nano-classes (20-minute teaching modules followed by 10-minute Q&A) as the guided instruction unit in teaching hackathons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Nano-classes are short instructional modules of 20 minute teaching followed by a 10 minute Q&A, used to deliver only the necessary support while leaving most time free for independent work. The first nano-class overviews the scientific topic for a non-expert or heterogeneous audience; a second demonstrates basic tool usage with a practical walk-through; a later one covers analysis. Optional nano-classes can provide complementary theory and attract applicants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A demonstration script or example simulation to walk through practically
- Expert support on-hand during subsequent free-play time
#### Constraints
- The article notes optional nano-classes were grouped under guided courses in the exit poll, making it difficult to distinguish their impact from core mandatory classes
- Participants accustomed to guided courses may have attributed them greater importance

### Target Learners
- heterogeneous audiences including non-experts
- students from B.Sc to Ph.D

### Target Learning Goals
- basic usage competence with a computational tool
- awareness of relevant scientific concepts for self-directed exploration

### Affordances
- Teaching Hackathon Modality
