# Deliver 360-video/VR orientation resources before first clinical exposure, timed to the pre-clinical years and in concise, accessible formats

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's findings indicate that 360-video and VR resources should be introduced at the optimal point in training — before students start treating patients — because once students have already experienced practically what a video shows, they deem its content redundant. Students recommended "bite sized" amounts or week-by-week delivery via easily accessible channels such as email or messaging groups, and videos of procedures made available prior to live demonstrations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Resources must be delivered prior to students' first clinical exposure to have impact
- Content should be concise and easily accessible, e.g. via mobile-viewable channels
#### Constraints
- Students who have already started treating patients found these exercises less valuable
- Headset setup effort and expense may deter students; the video alone may suffice

### Target Learners
- pre-clinical undergraduate dental students approaching their first specialty clinics

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing anxiety about entering clinical environments
- preparation and expectation-setting for clinical sessions
- safe rehearsal of clinical and IT-based skills
