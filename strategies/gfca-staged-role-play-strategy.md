# Structure role-play lessons as sequenced generalization, formulation, correction and appreciation stages with intergroup corrective feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy implements the AI-GFCA stages in a role-play lesson: students first comprehend task goals and success criteria (generalization), then script and rehearse within groups (formulation), then watch another group's recorded performance and report target-language use and L2 errors (correction), and finally give immediate and delayed positive appraisal (appreciation). The article states that "Each group was assigned to give corrective feedback on another group's performance" and that students watched videos noticing "the use of target lan-guage and ... the L2 errors which occurred during the role-play performance."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An AI speaking app for rehearsal feedback and a platform for sharing recordings and collecting error reports
- Rubric criteria made explicit to students in the generalization stage
#### Constraints
- Students reported that role-play preparation time was too short, and some preferred immediate rather than delayed corrective feedback

### Target Learners
- First-year university EFL students with A1-B1 CEFR English proficiency

### Target Learning Goals
- L2 role-play performance
- Peer corrective feedback provision and reception
- Reduced foreign-language anxiety
