# Elby paired questions: reconcile spontaneous stories with physics-accurate ones

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paired-question technique developed by Elby creates a pair of matched questions so that most students get one right and one wrong, producing a conflict. Elaboration and analysis of the pair shows students that their intuitions lead them into conflict, and they are guided to find and refine their "raw intuition" so it yields results consistent with the physics they are learning — for example reconciling the p-prim "more means more" with Newton's 3rd law.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions must be matched so most students get one right and one wrong, producing a conflict; the instructor then guides elaboration and refinement of the underlying intuition.
#### Constraints
- The article contrasts this with the traditional elicit-confront-resolve approach, which it says tends to put students down and undermine trust in their physical intuition.

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students (undergraduate)

### Target Learning Goals
- reconciling everyday intuition with Newton's 3rd law
- valuing and refining physical intuition
