# Physicist-mediated explanation strategy: teach quantum ideas in lay terms before introducing the graphical argument

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article prescribes an explanation sequence for physicists addressing non-scientists: first explain the probabilistic nature of quantum physics using quantum coins, then present the box diagrams and measurements, and only then let teleportation "arise naturally" from the graphical description. The paper's pedagogical sections are explicitly "written as if addressed to the layperson", so the physicist can relay them. This ordering is intended to avoid the mathematics that "has a well-known tendency to scare people away".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The explainer should possess a solid understanding of quantum physics, including graduate students, since the method is addressed to them rather than directly to learners.
#### Constraints
- Aimed at audiences without much mathematical skill; the treatment does not entirely avoid mathematical expressions and cannot convey the amplitude-probability distinction.

### Target Learners
- general public
- non-scientists without strong mathematics background

### Target Learning Goals
- correcting public misconceptions about teleportation
- building intuitive understanding of quantum mechanics
