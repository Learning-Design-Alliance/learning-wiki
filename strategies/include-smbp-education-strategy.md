# Include self-measured blood pressure (SMBP) education in BPM curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues BPM education should cover self-measured blood pressure and home monitoring so graduates can support evidence-based hypertension care. It notes the USPSTF now recommends out-of-office measurements and that "BP measured via HBPM exhibits a stronger correlation with cardiovascular disease outcomes than office‐based readings." The authors recommend "increasing emphasis on SMBP education, including patient instruction, positioning, and home monitoring strategies during clinical rotations."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coverage of correct patient positioning, home monitoring protocols, and patient instruction within BPM education
#### Constraints
- Some studies confirm patients frequently fail to adhere to proper protocols for home BPM and reporting

### Target Learners
- health professional students during clinical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- self-measured blood pressure technique and patient education for out-of-office hypertension assessment
