# Model Reflection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Students need to encounter reflective role models. Modeling reflection can involve reflecting on a workshop session: teachers share journal excerpts that model evidence of good listening, probing for clarity, asking thoughtful questions, and voicing metacognitive thinking. Through experience and continuous modeling, the class learns to use habits of mind in reflective conversations, strengthening transfer to the internal voice of reflection.

## Design Implications

Reflection is a skill that must be demonstrated before it can be internalized; observing an expert's reflective process gives learners a template for their own metacognitive self-talk [Social modeling is a primary route through which cognitive and metacognitive strategies are acquired.](../theories/social-learning-theory.md) [+M]. The model must make reasoning visible — not just conclusions — so learners can see *how* an expert notices, questions, and revises their thinking [Modeling in cognitive apprenticeship makes tacit expert processes observable and learnable.](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time for reflection and a willingness to share experiences and insights
- Authentic artifacts — real journal excerpts, decision points, mistakes — rather than polished exemplars
- A follow-on structure that invites learners to practice the modeled moves ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), journals, or [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))

#### Constraints
- Requires vulnerability; a teacher unwilling to share genuine uncertainty or error produces models students read as performance, not reflection [-W]
- Overly polished or idealized models can imply that reflection is a talent rather than a practice, discouraging novices [~W]
- If modeling is never faded into student-led reflection, learners remain dependent on external prompts [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Live think-aloud reflection after a lesson or workshop versus pre-written shared journal excerpts
- Peer models: advanced students modeling reflection can be more attainable than expert models for some learners [~M]
- Written, audio, or video formats; video preserves tone and hesitation, which are themselves part of reflective practice

### Target Learners
- Novices who have no internal template for what reflection "sounds like"
- Pre-service and in-service teachers learning reflective practice as part of professional growth
- Students developing self-regulated learning habits [Self-regulated learners benefit from explicit modeling of planning, monitoring, and evaluation strategies.](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Metacognitive skill development: noticing one's own understanding, confusion, and strategy use
- Professional dispositions: habits of mind such as listening, probing, and revising positions
- Transfer of reflective practice from guided conversation to independent internal dialogue

### Instructions
1. Select an authentic recent experience (a lesson, workshop, or project) with genuine uncertainty or a mistake worth examining.
2. Model the reflection aloud or in writing, making each move explicit — evidence of listening, probing for clarity, questioning assumptions, and metacognitive commentary (a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) applied to reflection itself).
3. Share artifacts such as journal excerpts so students can revisit and compare reflective moves.
4. Invite students to practice the same moves in structured [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or journals, with the teacher [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and giving feedback.
5. Gradually fade the model: shift from teacher-modeled to student-led reflective conversations, then to private reflection.

## Related Strategies

## Examples
- A teacher educator reflects on a workshop session and shares journal excerpts with teacher candidates, annotating where she noticed a missed question or revised an assumption.
- Medical education uses chart-review sessions in which an attending physician verbalizes diagnostic reasoning and self-correction, modeling clinical reflection for residents.
- Writing conferences in which the teacher rereads their own draft aloud, questioning word choices, model revision as reflective practice.
