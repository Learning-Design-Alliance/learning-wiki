# Assess individual surgeon sleepiness before night-shift procedures and evaluate whether specific operations should be postponed until the surgeon is rested

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using simple, validated subjective sleepiness assessments in clinical settings and studying whether fatigued surgeons' procedures should be deferred. It states that "individual assessment of surgeon sleepiness may be warranted to mitigate fatigue-related alterations of surgical performance", and that future studies should test whether specific procedures show comparable or better results when performed by rested surgeons the following day, as demonstrated for appendectomies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simple subjective sleepiness and tiredness assessments that require relatively little effort and can be carried out independently during clinical shifts
#### Constraints
- The clinical relevance of the measured motion-efficiency alterations is described as speculative; the question of postponing procedures is raised for evaluation in randomized controlled trials, not settled

### Target Learners
- surgeons
- surgical trainees and medical students in laparoscopic training

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining laparoscopic psychomotor skill and tissue-handling quality under fatigue
