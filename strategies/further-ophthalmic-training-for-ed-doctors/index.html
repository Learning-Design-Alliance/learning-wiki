Develop further ophthalmic training for doctors working in EDs, including funduscopy training for medical students and foundation doctors¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 9 claims (9 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends building targeted ophthalmic training for ED doctors because confidence in eye emergencies, funduscopy and slit-lamp use is low and formal ED training is widely absent. It states "we believe further funduscopy training for medical students and foundation doctors is vital" and suggests anterior segment examination is an achievable aim for foundation doctors. It also calls for assessing why foundation doctors feel ill-prepared.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training must fit the ED context, addressing the lack of formal ophthalmology training the review documents in the ED

Constraints¶

  • The review notes slit-lamp proficiency takes time and is not a compulsory part of the medical school curriculum

Target Learners¶

  • medical students
  • foundation doctors
  • Emergency Medicine physicians

Target Learning Goals¶

  • managing ophthalmic emergencies
  • performing funduscopy
  • using the slit lamp
  • performing lateral canthotomy and cantholysis for retrobulbar haemorrhage

Claims¶

  • Confidence of SHO/FY2 doctors in managing ophthalmic emergencies declined between 1993 and 2018 [+W]
  • ED doctors lack confidence in performing funduscopy despite widespread ophthalmoscope access [+W]
  • Most surveyed EM doctors were not confident performing lateral canthotomy and cantholysis for retrobulbar haemorrhage [+W]
  • Foundation and SHO doctors working in UK general EDs lack confidence in managing ophthalmic emergencies [+W]
  • Introduction of local guidelines was followed by improved SHO confidence in managing corneal abrasions [+W]
  • Most ED doctors receive no formal ophthalmology training in the ED and many believe more teaching would improve their confidence [+W]
  • Confidence in slit-lamp use among junior ED doctors declined between 1993 and 2018 [+W]
  • Undergraduate ophthalmology teaching is widely perceived as inadequate preparation for ED work [+W]
  • Included studies report consistent weakness in advanced examination skills such as slit-lamp use and ophthalmoscopy [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Combine emergency department exposure with structured trauma X-ray teaching programmes for junior doctors
