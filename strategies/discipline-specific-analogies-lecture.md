# Use discipline-specific analogies during lecture so students from each background can connect new qubit concepts to prior knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
When lecturing on manipulating qubits, Educator EE1 makes discipline-specific connections so "people who know it can make a relationship, and people who don't know it, feel comfortable that they don't have that part of the picture" — e.g., framing the concept as "a Hamiltonian" for physics students or "an LC oscillator" for engineering students. She also points students to a textbook if they want the rest of the story.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The instructor must know which prior concepts each disciplinary audience already knows and can connect the new material to.
#### Constraints
- EE1 notes that "one course in quantum computing is not enough to cover the whole area" and points students to textbooks for the remainder.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students from physics, engineering and other departments in interdisciplinary QISE courses

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting new qubit-manipulation concepts to each student's prior disciplinary knowledge
