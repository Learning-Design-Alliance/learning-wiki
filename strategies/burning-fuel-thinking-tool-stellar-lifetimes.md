# Use a burning-fuel analogy thinking tool to bridge data trends to stellar lifetimes and mass-to-energy conversion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A demonstration in which liquid fuel is ignited in two metal dishes of drastically different diameters helps students connect observed trends to stellar evolution. The article states: "The purpose of the thinking tool is to give students a way to think about stellar lifetimes, characteristics of stars, and the idea of converting mass into energy." Students observe that the fire in the larger dish burns brighter and for less time, and connect that larger stars burn brighter and for less time than smaller stars. It was introduced to make the inquiry less facilitator driven.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two metal dishes of drastically different diameters, liquid fuel, and ignition setup
- The analogy's limits (nuclear fusion versus chemical reaction) should be directly told to students
#### Constraints
- The analogy of burning dishes and stars is not perfect because stars convert matter into energy via nuclear fusion while burning fuel is a chemical reaction

### Target Learners
- advanced high-school students
- college students

### Target Learning Goals
- stellar lifetimes
- mass-luminosity relation
- mass-to-energy conversion
