# Measure the LED turn-on voltage U* visually through a straw waveguide using at least five repeated readings and the median

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
To find the smallest voltage U* at which an LED emits light, participants rotate the potentiometer and watch through a black plastic straw used as a light guide. The problem instructs: "We recommend at least 5 different measurements and taking the median (or middle) value for U∗." This is repeated for every working LED and recorded in a table with columns for LED number, color and U*. The paper acknowledges the human eye is a very good sensor but the results are subjective, which later university tasks address with numerical I-V-curve methods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A darkened room, a plastic straw as a light waveguide, and an adjustable voltage source with a voltmeter are needed.
#### Constraints
- The visual method inserts subjective uncertainties, which the paper asks university students to estimate by order of magnitude from the repeated measurements.

### Target Learners
- high school students
- university students

### Target Learning Goals
- careful threshold measurement
- repeated-measurement error awareness
