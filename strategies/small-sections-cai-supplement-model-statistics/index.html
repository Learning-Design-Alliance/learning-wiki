Deliver CAI in small class sections as a supplement using tutorials, simulations, computation, and drill-and-practice¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study, q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the meta-analysis's moderator findings, the authors propose a formula or model for optimal statistics instruction: "Such a formula or model would include small class sections that use CAI to enhance or supplement activities, and using the computer for the purposes of tutorial, simulation, computation and drill and practice." Smaller classes with cooperative learning groups are described as a bridge for computer novices. The strategy integrates the moderator variables that showed the largest effects.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • CAI used to enhance or supplement activities rather than replace instruction
  • small class sections

Constraints¶

  • CAI applied haphazardly may create statistical technicians who cannot reliably interpret results; CAI is not a panacea for statistics education

Target Learners¶

  • postsecondary students in introductory statistics courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • conceptual understanding of statistics
  • student achievement in statistics courses

Claims¶

  • CAI supplements instruction effectively but shows no benefit as a complete replacement for instruction [+W]
  • CAI mode matters: tutorials show the largest effects while strictly online CAI shows negative effects [+W]
  • Face-to-face course delivery with CAI outperforms online delivery of CAI in statistics education [+W]
  • CAI has a moderate positive effect on student achievement in postsecondary statistics education (d = 0.566) [+W]
  • CAI effects are largest when used across several class meetings rather than an entire semester [+W]
  • CAI benefits introductory statistics students more than intermediate/advanced students [+W]
  • CAI effects vary by student discipline, with the largest effects for psychology students [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use computer-delivered practice to free teacher time for explicit instruction and enable smaller instructional groupings
