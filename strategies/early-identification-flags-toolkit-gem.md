# Use a toolkit of academic and non-academic flags for early identification of struggling graduate-entry medicine students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 10 claims (8 for, 2 against) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article applies the flag toolkit developed for the Nottingham undergraduate medicine course to a graduate-entry programme. Flags include "missed attendance, unsatisfactory attitude or behaviour, health problems, social/family problems, failure to complete immunity status checks, and attendance at academic progress committee". Counting two or more flags in the first 18 months identified over half of eventual strugglers and most early leavers, so support and interventions can be targeted.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to course progress files and records of attendance, behaviour, health, social circumstances, immunity status compliance, and Academic Progress Committee attendance
#### Constraints
- A substantial minority of struggling students could not be identified early, attributed to lack of disclosure, lack of insight, and shame or perceived stigma

### Target Learners
- graduate-entry medicine students

### Target Learning Goals
- early identification of students at risk of academic difficulty or attrition to enable targeted support and intervention
