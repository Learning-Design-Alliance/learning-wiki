# Combine cognitive training with direct da Vinci simulator training, using larger samples and higher numbers of monitored training trials, in future robotic surgery curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that future robotic surgical training research should pair computer-based cognitive training with direct da Vinci simulator training and increase training trial numbers. They state that "Further investigation with a larger number of subjects on the one hand and a higher numbers of training trials on the other hand should be performed" and that the combination "could be an interesting question." This follows their finding that the cognitive training group's sessions were not carried out as instructed and that prior DLR analysis showed many trials are needed to stabilize gains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tighter controlled training program with monitoring, since lack of monitoring in the cognitive training group was identified as a problem
- Cost-effective designs with higher numbers of training trials
#### Constraints
- In this pilot the cognitive training was implemented in a significantly reduced manner compared to the necessary measure, and the small sample limits assessment of cognitive training efficacy

### Target Learners
- Medical students learning robotic surgery basics
- Novice robotic surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- Robotic-assisted surgical psychomotor skills
- Cognitive skills supporting robotic surgery performance
