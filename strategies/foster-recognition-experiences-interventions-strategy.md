# Design K-12 and out-of-school interventions that deliberately foster the three significant recognition experiences for underrepresented students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that stakeholders in K-12 and out-of-school learning settings develop interventions centering the recognition experiences that predicted aspirations and identity: getting called upon to answer questions, being recommended by a teacher for an advanced STEM class, and receiving awards in STEM activities. They suggest NARST members "adopt collaborative efforts to research with teachers in determining effective approaches and co-develop interventions" acknowledging STEM recognition, particularly for women and ethnic minorities, and extend research on reforming existing recognition approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher participation in co-developing and acknowledging student recognition in STEM tasks
#### Constraints
- The authors describe the supporting results as preliminary due to high missingness

### Target Learners
- students who identify as female and/or with historically underrepresented racial and ethnic groups

### Target Learning Goals
- increase STEM career aspirations
- support STEM identity formation
