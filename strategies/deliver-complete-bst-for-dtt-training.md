# Deliver complete behavioral skills training (feedback, instruction, modeling, and rehearsal together or sequentially) to train DTT implementers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study, `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends training DTT implementers with the full BST package rather than isolated components, because complete BST was the only configuration shown to be statistically significantly effective. The authors write that "The results from this meta-analysis serve as supporting evidence that training methods that utilize BST are effective and recommended for use." BST delivered feedback, instruction, modeling, and rehearsal simultaneously or sequentially, and worked even for the 69% of participants with no prior DTT experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All four BST components (feedback, instruction, modeling, rehearsal) delivered together and/or sequentially
- DTT implementation fidelity directly observed and scored as percentage of steps correct
#### Constraints
- Instruction and feedback could not be isolated as separate variables in this meta-analysis (instruction present in 96% of studies; feedback separate from BST in only 11%), so their individual impacts remain unknown
- More research is needed on BST effectiveness in home settings and for parents, who were only 13% of participants

### Target Learners
- Novice DTT implementers, including teachers, paraprofessionals, clinicians, and parents of children with autism spectrum disorders

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate, high-fidelity implementation of discrete trial training
