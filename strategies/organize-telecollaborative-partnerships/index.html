Organize telecollaborative partnership environments linking EFL students with university students in English speaking countries¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that universities in EFL contexts arrange institutionalized, electronically mediated intercultural exchanges so learners gain frequent contact with target language speakers at low cost. It states that "university deans in English as Foreign Language contexts should make arrangements" for intercultural interactions through telecollaborative partnerships, as a cost-effective substitute for educational sojourns.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to internet-mediated communication tools such as synchronous chat, e-mail, Facebook and Skype
  • A languacultural expert (teacher) guiding the institutionalized exchange

Constraints¶

  • Educational sojourns, the face-to-face alternative, cannot be afforded by the majority of language learners, motivating the telecollaborative option

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate EFL students in foreign language contexts

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Pragmatic comprehension of implied meanings
  • Intercultural competence

Claims¶

  • Telecollaboration with native English speakers significantly improves EFL students' pragmatic comprehension beyond pragmatic instruction alone [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Organize self-regulated collaborative learning environments in higher education where students plan, work with peers and self-evaluate
  • Include pragmatic issues in ESL/EFL classes and in syllabus design
  • Recruit real virtual-world interlocutors from target cultures via language-exchange platforms before SL tasks
