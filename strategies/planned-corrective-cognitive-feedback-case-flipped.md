# Plan multifaceted feedback combining corrective and cognitive feedback, expert worksheet models, and peer observation under rubrics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because clinician availability constrains feedback delivery, the article recommends planning feedback in advance to be time-efficient and educationally effective. Feedback spans two types: "corrective feedback, which addresses specific errors or misconceptions, and cognitive feedback, which prompts learners to reflect on their reasoning processes and outcomes". Practical moves include instructors sharing their own completed worksheets after group presentations so students compare their reasoning with an expert example, peer observation of other teams' approaches, and rubric-based instructor feedback to each group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback must be planned in advance; clear rubrics are needed so instructors can deliver both corrective and cognitive feedback on group performance
#### Constraints
- Delivering sufficient feedback is often constrained by clinicians' limited availability in practice

### Target Learners
- Preclinical medical students working in case-discussion groups

### Target Learning Goals
- Recognizing mistakes, refining reasoning, and developing stronger illness scripts
