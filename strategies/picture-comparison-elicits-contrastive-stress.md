# Use picture-comparison tasks with limited, noticeable differences to elicit contrastive stress in spontaneous speech practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 4 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The instructional strategy pairs pictures whose differences are limited and noticeable with prompts to compare them, so that learners naturally produce contrastive stress in unscripted speech. The article reports that "These pictures prompted comparisons that naturally elicited contrastive stress and could be used with multiple students," and the same pictures served both instruction and the pretest-posttest spontaneous speech tasks. Feedback was given as students produced comparisons in class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pictures must have limited and noticeable differences so comparisons naturally require contrastive stress
- Instructors coordinate lessons and give feedback on learners' comparison sentences
#### Constraints
- The article notes a general spontaneous speech task such as a picture narrative may not reveal improvement because it would not easily target contrastive stress

### Target Learners
- intermediate-level ESL learners in an Intensive English Program

### Target Learning Goals
- producing contrastive stress in spontaneous speech
- improving comprehensibility of L2 speech
