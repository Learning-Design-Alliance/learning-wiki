Strategies

Open the course with a psychic-powers demonstration that students critically test and eventually debunk¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Each semester began with a demonstration of psychic powers tied to a unit of introductory scientific reasoning. One instructor performed while the other "served as a confidant in these tricks", encouraging students to challenge the evidence and list changes they could make. The goal was critical thinking while the "psychic" adapted to overcome their tests; in each year students "eventually found a controlled change in the demonstration which negated the abilities". Students often reported this first-day activity made a considerable difference in their ability to think critically.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A second instructor acting as confidant who coaches students to propose tests and changes to the demonstration

Constraints¶

  • The article reports the demonstration as concrete preparation within a cognitive acceleration structure; its reported benefit is student self-report

Target Learners¶

  • first-year undergraduate science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical thinking
  • scientific reasoning
  • evaluating evidence

Claims¶

  • The article reports that without explicitly applying critical thinking to pseudoscientific content, students may develop strong scientific reasoning while still holding uncritical paranormal beliefs. [+W]
  • LC students scored significantly lower on the full Paranormal Beliefs scale than other SJFC students, but only four of seven subscale comparisons were significant. [+M]
  • Students in the interdisciplinary learning community had significantly lower paranormal beliefs at the end of the semester than at the beginning (Cohen's d = .74 on the full scale). [+M]
  • Without prompting, 14 students wrote on course evaluations that the course improved critical thinking, with other unprompted comments citing skepticism and scientific reasoning. [+W]
  • The extraordinary life forms subscale showed no significant pre-test to post-test decrease (d = .03). [~M]
