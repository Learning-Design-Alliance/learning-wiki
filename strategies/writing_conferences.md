# Writing Conferences

## Description
Writing conferences are brief, individualized meetings between a teacher and a student focused on the student's writing. The primary purpose is to provide targeted feedback, foster self-assessment, and encourage goal setting to improve the student's writing skills. During the conference, the teacher listens to the student, reviews their work, asks introspective questions to stimulate reflection, and offers clear, positive, and specific feedback and suggestions. The goal is to unlock the student’s potential as a writer and help them understand the writing process more effectively. Conferences can be used at various stages of the writing process and should be structured, focused, and encouraging, helping students find joy in writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires dedicated time for one-on-one meetings (3-7 minutes per student). Teacher preparation includes reviewing student work and preparing targeted questions and feedback. Independent writing activities for other students during conferences. A checklist or record-keeping system to track conferences and addressed topics.
#### Constraints
- Can be time-consuming, especially in large classes. Requires strong classroom management skills to ensure productive independent work for other students. Success depends on the teacher’s ability to provide clear, specific, and positive feedback quickly.

### Affordances
- Provides immediate, targeted feedback, encourages self-reflection and critical thinking about writing, allows for individualized instruction tailored to the student's needs, and fosters a positive attitude towards writing.

### Personalization
- Questions and feedback are tailored to each student's individual needs and skill level. Can be adapted to focus on specific writing skills or phases of the writing process. Flexible structure allows for addressing various writing challenges.

## Examples
- A high school English teacher conducts 5-minute writing conferences with each student during class, focusing on specific aspects of their essays identified beforehand. After the conferences, students revise their essays based on the feedback.
