# Integrate validated study techniques such as spaced repetition and wellness strategies (sleep, physical activity) into university support programs for entrance examinations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that because success appeared multifactorial, universities should "integrat[e] validated study techniques and wellness strategies into university support programs for entrance examinations." Concretely, this means supporting spaced repetition as a primary revision method alongside guidance on sleep duration and regular weekly physical activity, since each was independently associated with higher odds of admission in their survey. The strategy addresses the transition from small-group secondary instruction to autonomous university learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be preparing for a high-stakes selective examination with a large memorization load, and support programs must be able to reach them during the preparation year
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is a retrospective observational survey; the article notes reporting bias from a non-standardized questionnaire and recommends prospective, validated measurement of study strategies

### Target Learners
- first-year university candidates preparing for medical school entrance examinations

### Target Learning Goals
- effective long-term retention of foundational disciplines and admission success
