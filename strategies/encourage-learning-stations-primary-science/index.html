Encourage use of learning stations in primary-level science courses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the meta-analytic findings, the authors recommend that practitioners encourage the learning stations technique where it showed the largest effects: primary education, 3-4 week applications, and science and technology lessons. They write that "teachers should be encouraged to use the learning stations technique in appropriate subject areas" and that the technique "can be used especially in science courses". They further recommend more research on the technique in physics, chemistry, and mathematics and at different educational levels.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Appropriate subject areas for the technique
  • Pre-prepared stations and heterogeneous student groups

Constraints¶

  • The moderator differences by duration were not statistically significant
  • Few studies exist at secondary and university levels and in physics, chemistry and mathematics

Target Learners¶

  • primary education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic achievement in science and technology courses

Claims¶

  • The learning stations technique has a large positive effect on academic achievement compared to traditional teaching [+W]
  • The effect of learning stations differs significantly by education level, largest at primary level [+W]
  • Effect of learning stations differs significantly by course type, highest in science and technology [+W]
  • Effect of learning stations does not differ significantly by application duration [~W]
