Keep video materials used for learning under 30 minutes to sustain learner attention¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 review), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article observed that a few students lost interest while watching the second cartoon, attributed to the cartoon's length of nearly 40 minutes in total. Given students' concentration span, "it would have been better if the movies had been kept under 30 minutes", and it recommends that in further research cartoons be trimmed to include only the parts targeted in the study.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Video material length should fit learners' concentration span

Constraints¶

  • Based on the researcher's observation of a few students in one treatment session, not a measured outcome

Target Learners¶

  • university ELT students watching video in class

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustained engagement with video-based language learning material

Claims¶

  • Median student engagement with MOOC videos falls sharply as video length increases beyond 6 minutes [+M]
  • MOOC video engagement declines with video length, with median engagement under half the duration for videos longer than nine minutes [+M]
  • Viewing of the personalized AI videos declined sharply across the quarter, with views of the first week-9 video set suggesting fewer than 10% of the course population was watching voluntarily by the end of the course [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Keep online lecture videos under twenty minutes and segment by topic with speed control
  • Limit flipped classroom video duration to around 20 minutes and use opening quizzes
  • Limit pre-class video length to 20 minutes to reduce student burden
  • Keep pre-class teaching videos short (10–20 minutes), goal-targeted, and followed by checking homework
