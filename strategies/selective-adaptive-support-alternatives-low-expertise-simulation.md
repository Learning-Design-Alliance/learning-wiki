# Use selective instructional supports (examples, prompts) combined with adaptive instructional support as alternatives to reflection phases for low-expertise learners in simulation-based learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In their conclusions the authors recommend that, if the null effect of reflection phases is replicated, "other instructional supports might be more beneficial in this context and similar settings", specifically "Combinations of selective instructional support (such as examples and prompts) and adaptive instructional support". They justify this by citing evidence that both have been found beneficial in simulation-based learning and for learners with relatively little expertise.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulation-based learning environment capable of delivering examples, prompts, and adaptive support
#### Constraints
- Recommended as an alternative for learners with low to medium expertise in simulation-based learning; contingent on replication of the null finding

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students with low to medium expertise

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnostic competence in simulation-based learning
