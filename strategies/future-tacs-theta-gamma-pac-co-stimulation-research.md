# Future research should test theta-gamma phase-amplitude coupling co-stimulation protocols and extend tACS benefits to cognitively impaired populations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on evidence that cross-frequency coupling supports cognitive processing, the authors propose that "tACS protocols with co-stimulation at theta and gamma frequency bands may be a viable option to increase cognitive improvements by inducing theta-gamma PAC." They also recommend testing tACS in older adults and patients with neurological diseases, and providing "individualized current intensity thresholds and electrode montage positions" to minimize side effects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simultaneous theta and gamma frequency delivery over multifocal areas
#### Constraints
- The PAC co-stimulation benefit is a proposition supported by prior work the review cites, not tested within this meta-analysis

### Target Learners
- older adults
- patients with neurological diseases

### Target Learning Goals
- working memory
- cognitive functions impaired by decreased theta PAC
