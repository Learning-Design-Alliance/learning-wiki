Replace passive theoretical IT instruction with hands-on active technological creation and algorithmic troubleshooting¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that IT curricula move away from passive software navigation toward practical, creation-centered activity. It argues that "Fostering true digital resilience requires curricula centered on algorithmic trial-and-error, logical troubleshooting, and active technological creation", grounded in the confidence deficits and 76.5% student demand for hands-on practice its survey documented.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Formal, specialized instruction rather than mere exposure to digital devices, which the article says is insufficient

Constraints¶

  • Evidence base is a convenience sample from three schools in Spain and Ireland; the authors say results should be interpreted cautiously pending broader replication

Target Learners¶

  • secondary students aged 12-16

Target Learning Goals¶

  • digital literacy
  • algorithmic thinking and troubleshooting
  • programming and CAD creation skills
  • critical AI awareness

Claims¶

  • Secondary students report near-maximum confidence in passive digital tasks but sharply lower confidence in active creation and algorithmic logic [+W]
  • 76.5% of students demand a shift toward hands-on practical computer activities over theoretical instruction [+W]
  • Technology-pathway students report higher active-creation self-efficacy than Arts and Humanities students (p < 0.001) [+W]
  • Embedded AI assistance enabled a novice student to troubleshoot in real time and stay in creative flow [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide systematic educator training on playlist content, activity implementation practices, technology troubleshooting, and content modification for age groups
