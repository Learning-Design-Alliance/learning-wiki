# Adopt peer-coaching and faculty learning community models in which instructors regularly observe and support one another

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The essay argues that "models of peer feedback or coaching rather than peer observation and review could encourage the adoption and effective use of evidence-based teaching strategies in science." Unlike a one-time classroom observation, a peer-feedback model is all-encompassing, covering learning objectives and assessment strategies, with instructors regularly observing one another. Establishing faculty learning communities for willing participants is offered as an avenue for regular feedback beyond student evaluations and drop-in peer evaluations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Voluntary participation, since volunteers are more receptive to receiving feedback
- A defined agenda so faculty learn and reflect together on specific problems
- Pre-observation meetings to discuss class goals, challenges, and areas for specific suggestions
#### Constraints
- The essay notes barriers including lack of in-house experts, reward structures that undervalue teaching, and few formal mechanisms for peer feedback beyond promotion and tenure evaluations

### Target Learners
- STEM faculty, including tenure-track and non-tenure-track instructors and change agents

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained adoption of evidence-based, learner-centered instructional practices

### Affordances
- Three Characteristics Effective Feedback
