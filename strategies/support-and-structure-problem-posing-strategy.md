# Support inexperienced and low-achiever students and structure problem posing with innovative strategies and technologies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers implementing the problem posing learning model provide support to students, especially those inexperienced or low-achieving, and structure the model with innovative teaching strategies and technologies. It states teachers "may structure the problem posing learning model with innovative teaching strategies and technologies", naming worked examples, worksheet scaffolds, game-based problem-solving, the "what if not?" strategy, and Desmos activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need to attend to learning components that affect the effectiveness of problem posing, including providing support for inexperienced and low-achiever students
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a study in which the unsupported model showed no significant achievement effect, so effectiveness depends on the added support and structuring

### Target Learners
- eighth-grade middle school mathematics students, especially inexperienced and low-achiever students

### Target Learning Goals
- mathematics learning achievement
- learning motivation
