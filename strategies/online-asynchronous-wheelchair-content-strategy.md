# Leverage online and asynchronous learning strategies to supplement lecture and lab components of wheelchair-related content

## Description
The article identifies underuse of online delivery: "Only six programs utilized on-line modules, primarily for the Assessment step." It states "There appears to be considerable opportunity yet for programs to leverage on-line and asynchronous learning strategies to supplement or support the lecture and lab components of wheelchair-related content", as proposed in several publications it cites.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online modules must supplement or support existing lecture and lab components rather than replace them.
#### Constraints
- The article notes programs may have begun developing such resources during the COVID pandemic, which occurred subsequent to most survey responses.

### Target Learners
- Occupational therapy entry-to-practice students in Canadian university programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Flexible delivery of wheelchair service provision content across the WHO 8-step process
