# Enrich patient presentation, embed provoked errors with feedback, and individualize management decisions in VPs; assess clinical reasoning as a multi-step process with varied methods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the Patient-centeredness and Teaching/Assessment categories, the authors recommend providing at least a basic patient description including name, age and contextual information plus an image in all VPs, enriching some with additional media, since a minimalistic presentation "does not adequately represent the patient in the clinical reasoning process". They suggest including errors into VP scenarios, provoking errors and explicitly including potential causes such as biases, with immediate feedback, elaboration of the error, and avoidance strategies. They also suggest assessing clinical reasoning as a multi-step process using a variety of assessment and feedback methods including qualitative methods such as concept mapping, and more explicitly covering the individual nature of management decisions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- More complex scoring algorithms that take into account the diversity of concept maps; elaboration of factors influencing individualized management decisions
#### Constraints
- Media-rich VPs are more time-consuming for learners, contradicting the concept of working through many cases to develop illness scripts

### Target Learners
- Medical and healthcare students

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning including error awareness, bias recognition, communication, and individualized management decision-making
