# Adopt low-cost, non-digital board games as adjuncts for complex biomedical topics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating game-based learning into medical curricula “particularly in courses involving complex topics such as diabetes mellitus” and providing faculty training for implementation. It argues board games are “cost effective,” need no advanced technological infrastructure, and foster face-to-face collaborative interaction, making them practical where digital tools face technological or economic barriers. It also advises students to incorporate such gamified tools into self-study routines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty support and training to address concerns about curriculum overload and time constraints when incorporating these tools
#### Constraints
- Board games may not suit students preferring didactic teaching; lack of standardized games hinders comparison and integration across institutions

### Target Learners
- Preclinical undergraduate medical students
- Medical educators and institutions adopting gamified teaching

### Target Learning Goals
- Engagement, knowledge retention, and critical self-assessment in complex biomedical coursework
