# Differentiate e-module design by achievement level: add spaced repetition and adaptive quizzes for low achievers and advanced case-based challenges for high achievers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the subgroup findings, the authors recommend tailoring future e-modules to achievement levels. For low achievers they suggest that "strategies, such as spaced repetition and adaptive quizzes, could be incorporated in e-modules to reinforce knowledge retention," while for high achievers "future iterations of the module should integrate advanced case–based learning and real-world problem-solving tasks." They also recommend hybrid models combining e-modules with case-based learning and simulations, and adding gamified elements and competitive assessments to sustain motivation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- E-modules already structured around Bloom taxonomy and the Miller pyramid
- Assessment data able to identify learner achievement subgroups
#### Constraints
- These are authors' recommendations for future iterations, not manipulations tested in this study

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduate medical (MBBS) students across high, medium, and low achievement levels

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge retention
- Clinical reasoning and problem-solving
- Sustained engagement and motivation
