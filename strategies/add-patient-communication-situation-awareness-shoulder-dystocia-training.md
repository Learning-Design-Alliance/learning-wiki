# Include patient communication and situation awareness alongside technical skills in shoulder dystocia simulation training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors advocate that future simulation training programs for shoulder dystocia add specific non-technical skills to their technical curricula. Based on their video findings, they recommend training that emphasizes "promptly recognizing the emergency and mobilizing all essential personnel, and effective patient communication, calming the mother and guiding her to collaborate during internal rotational maneuvers." Their own facility's training had so far covered technical skills plus verbalizing the diagnosis and calling for help.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training should combine technical maneuvers with the two non-technical skills the study associated with high technical performance
#### Constraints
- The associations were observational and not proof of causality; the article notes barriers to implementing annual simulation-based training as it is costly and time consuming

### Target Learners
- obstetric delivery ward teams (midwives, registrars, consultants)

### Target Learning Goals
- correct management of shoulder dystocia, including technical maneuvers, patient communication, and situation awareness
