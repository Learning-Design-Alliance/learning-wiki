# Use validated self-efficacy and thanatophobia scales to evaluate palliative care programmes and tailor pedagogical approaches

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educators use instruments such as SEPC-Br and TS-Br for ongoing evaluation of palliative care educational programmes, since "there is no gold standard programme in PC education". Evaluations using these instruments "may help educators in shaping the best methods and curriculum composition for their students' needs", allowing medical educators to follow students' development and tailor pedagogical approaches across the many approaches used to teach palliative care.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Validated instruments grounded in a solid theoretical basis, applied repeatedly to track development over a course
#### Constraints
- Self-assessment alone is insufficient; external evaluation methods should be used in addition for a thorough evaluation of learning outcomes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in Brazil and Latin America

### Target Learning Goals
- preparedness for palliative and end-of-life care
