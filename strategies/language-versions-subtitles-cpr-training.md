# Offer CPR training materials in different language versions or with subtitles so students with other native languages receive instruction in their own language

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adapting film-based CPR training to students' native languages, since training delivered in Swedish was associated with slightly lower skill scores for ONL students. It states that "The addition of subtitles or different language versions could facilitate learning for students with other native languages" and that whole-class teaching can continue with ONL students receiving instruction via headphones. This is an authors' design recommendation from the discussion, not a tested manipulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires producing subtitle tracks or alternative language versions of the training film, and headphones if language versions are used within whole-class teaching
#### Constraints
- The article did not test subtitles or language versions; it states further research is needed on how CPR educational material should be designed and simplified

### Target Learners
- 13-year-old seventh-grade school students, including students whose native language differs from the language of instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- Practical CPR skills (compressions, ventilations, recovery position) and willingness to intervene in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest
