# Design future encouragement interventions to target self-efficacy, use a familiar sender, and encourage systematically rather than only before exams

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article derives two design recommendations from its finding that self-efficacy, not grades, responded to a light-touch message. It states that “Future encouragement interventions should therefore use innovative and effective encouragement messages that target students’ self-efficacy,” and offers that “a personalized encouragement message sent by a sender to whom students have contact might be worth considering,” plus encouraging students “systematically and not just shortly before their exams.” The intervention it evaluated was an automated e-mail and SMS message praising past achievements and signaling trust, sent by the Head of the Directorate of Education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A delivery channel that reaches students shortly before the target situation (e-mail the evening before, SMS the morning of the exam, as used in the study)
#### Constraints
- The article found no grade effect, so the strategy is justified by the self-efficacy mechanism, not by achievement gains
- The secondary-outcome subsample was more advantaged, and the article warns against generalizing those results to the entire analytic sample

### Target Learners
- university students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-efficacy beliefs about academic performance
- engagement with the academic aspect of school life

## Claims

- [Automated encouragement messages sent shortly before exams do not raise university students’ end-of-semester exam grades, and the effect does not differ between first and second exams](../claims/encouragement-message-no-exam-grade-effect.md) [-M]
- [Encouragement messages increase university students’ self-efficacy before exams (Cohen’s d = 0.12), with no difference between first- and second-exam delivery](../claims/encouragement-raises-self-efficacy.md) [+M]
- [In the baseline-survey subsample, encouragement raised self-efficacy more for anxious students and for students with low baseline self-confidence](../claims/encouragement-self-efficacy-anxious-low-confidence.md) [+M]
- [Encouragement messages produce a small increase in students’ exam motivation (Cohen’s d = 0.066)](../claims/encouragement-small-motivation-increase.md) [+M]
