# Open-ended kinematics lab in which students judge small-versus-big push differences from StatKey box plots

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (4 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the 'Slowing Down' lab, students measure local g with the iOLab accelerometer, then observe acceleration graphs of pushed and freely sliding motion and investigate differences between small and big pushes of student-chosen magnitude. Rather than running statistical tests, students "make a judgment based on looking at the box plot graph for average sliding acceleration for big versus small pushes", uploaded to StatKey as a .csv file. The lab is open-ended: students are not told how many measurements to make or how to measure average sliding acceleration, and it is graded partly with the ISLE scientific ability rubric for an observing experiment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- iOLab device, a computer, a flat surface with felt pads, and the StatKey web application with a student-created .csv data file
#### Constraints
- The article notes a common conceptual difficulty: students think the push ends at time II even though acceleration is still positive at that time

### Target Learners
- introductory mechanics students in online courses

### Target Learning Goals
- data sense-making with box plots
- identifying patterns in data
- experimental design and variable identification
