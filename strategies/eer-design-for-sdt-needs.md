# Structure EER design around the three basic psychological needs of self-determination theory

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends designing EERs to support competence, autonomy, and relatedness, since the reviewed articles "explained how the implementation of EERs can support and enhance these needs, thus fostering intrinsic motivation among participants". Concretely, designers create multiple solution pathways and allow players to set their own sub-goals (autonomy), make progress visible through unlocking puzzles (competence), and establish a common escape goal pursued as a team, optionally with inter-team competition (relatedness).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple pathways must exist to reach the final goal to support autonomy
- Team collaboration around a shared goal is needed to support relatedness
#### Constraints
- Grounded in two included articles (a narrative review and an empirical study) rather than validated experimentation

### Target Learners
- higher education students in medical/health fields

### Target Learning Goals
- intrinsic motivation
- collaboration
- competence development
