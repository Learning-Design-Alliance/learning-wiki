# Routinely monitor combined academic and non-academic markers to trigger early targeted support for struggling students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (5 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that medical schools routinely record and total simple academic and non-academic markers so that potential strugglers are identified "at an early stage, enabling additional support and guidance to be given to these students". Support may include study-skills advice, time out for recovery after illness, group academic remediation to reduce stigma, tailored individual support, or guidance towards alternative non-clinical careers. The author suggests the marker combination could suit other schools and may predict better than academic criteria alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standardised recording system for adverse health, social and behavioural events, such as the new Concerns Form
- Pastoral infrastructure (personal tutors, Senior Tutors, Clinical Sub-Deans) able to deliver targeted support
#### Constraints
- Reaching students who resist help remains difficult; support delivery is labour-intensive for the Faculty and must be delivered carefully
- Confidentiality of student disclosures must be preserved in any searchable recording system

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- timely remediation of academic difficulty and prevention of course failure or attrition
