Improve rural school teaching facilities and encourage wide reading irrespective of location and gender¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 causal), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's recommendations respond directly to its location findings: rural schools should receive the teaching and learning facilities their urban counterparts enjoy, and students should be encouraged to read widely regardless of school location or gender. The author states "rural schools should be improved u pon in terms of social facilities that could aid te aching" and that "students should be encouraged to read wide irrespective of school location and gender". These recommendations rest on the significant location-by-treatment effects and the null gender effects reported in the study.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Rural schools identified as lacking facilities that aid teaching relative to urban counterparts

Constraints¶

  • Recommendation derives from a single quasi-experiment in Ekiti State secondary schools

Target Learners¶

  • Senior Secondary class two students in Nigerian public secondary schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading comprehension in English language
  • Inferring word meanings

Claims¶

  • Urban-school students significantly outperformed rural-school students in reading comprehension between experimental and control groups [+W]
  • Rural school settings' distance from urban areas and long commutes can exacerbate the challenges struggling rural schools face [+W]
  • Rural SIG schools reported additional challenges from remote locations and large catchment areas beyond those reported by nonrural SIG schools [+W]
  • Administrators in eight of nine rural SIG schools reported rural-setting factors, chiefly long teacher commutes (six schools) or isolated communities (four), that hampered teacher recruitment or retention [+W]
