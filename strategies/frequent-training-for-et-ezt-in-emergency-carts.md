# Schedule frequent hands-on training for ET and EasyTube if these devices are kept in pediatric emergency carts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because endotracheal intubation and the EasyTube showed the longest insertion times and the largest training-related gains, the article recommends that carts carrying them be paired with regular practice. It states: "If using these two devices in an emergency cart, frequent training is indispensable for proper performance, more than in the other tested devices." The 6-month retest showed significant success-rate improvement for exactly these two devices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a manikin or similar practice setup allowing repeated insertion attempts, as the article notes a manikin can be used to make similar practice sessions more realistic
#### Constraints
- The recommendation derives from a manikin study with a single insertion attempt per condition in the first run; the article notes manikin airway securing times are generally shorter than in actual patients

### Target Learners
- Pediatricians and other medical professionals who infrequently perform ETI

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining insertion skill and success rate for complex airway devices over time
