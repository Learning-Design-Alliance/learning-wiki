# Open apparatus design plus a harmonic-then-parametric teaching sequence for the spring-mass laboratory

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends letting students assemble the spring-mass system themselves, describing a laboratory where "students can assemble their own apparatus as they see ﬁt (picking components from a pile)", because such systems often show complex dynamics rather than idealized harmonic motion. The proposed teaching sequence treats the system in class first as a harmonic oscillator and then develops it towards the parametric oscillator, allowing students to approach the challenging, complex physics of parametric behavior. Students must deal with the gap between theory and practice, treat data statistically, and manage unexpected experimental observations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students design the experiment setup themselves rather than being given predefined apparatus
- Guidance towards larger masses for more regular oscillations during the harmonic phase
- Initial smooth, precise mass displacement and small oscillation amplitudes to minimize spurious motion
#### Constraints
- Forced oscillations were not covered and are left as the subject of further research
- The detailed parametric analysis in the paper is beyond first-year undergraduate teaching level

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics students
- beginning laboratory students
- more advanced students exploring complex aspects

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental procedure and physics thinking
- managing unexpected experimental observations
- statistical data treatment
- understanding harmonic and parametric oscillation

### Affordances
- Coupled Spring Pendulum Parametric Oscillator Model
