# Prepare video-feedback sessions with facilitators attentive to learners' psychological needs, skilled at linking feedback to communication theory

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (3 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's design recommendations for video-feedback sessions emphasize the facilitator's role in preserving "a pleasant and trustful atmosphere in VF". It advises a mindful facilitator attentive to students' psychological needs who can associate feedback with previously addressed communication theories, and local supervisors who give continuous constructive feedback in real settings. For research, it recommends inter-institutional collaborations to circumvent sample-size limitations and a more extended longitudinal follow-up, since the complex skills targeted take a long time to develop.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators able to quickly establish connections between the given feedback and pertinent theoretical frameworks
- A facilitator attentive to students' psychological needs to preserve a pleasant and trustful atmosphere
#### Constraints
- The first video recording experience tends to be more stressful, with learners' stress decreasing gradually over time
- Complex skills targeted by VF take a long time to develop, requiring longer follow-up to measure change

### Target Learners
- First-year medical residents in primary care residency programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical communication skills
- Acceptance of receiving feedback
- Reflective practice
