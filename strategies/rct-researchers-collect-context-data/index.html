Strategies

RCT researchers should collect qualitative data on context alongside implementation and impact¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that researchers conducting randomized controlled trials collect data not only on implementation and impact but also on context. In this study, some of the most compelling data on context was qualitative, which many researchers collect sparsely or not at all in RCTs. Context data was essential to understanding why the PD did not affect student outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • RCT designs should include qualitative context data collection

Constraints¶

  • Context-related constraints may make it impossible for PD alone to measurably impact student outcomes

Target Learners¶

  • education researchers conducting intervention evaluations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding how contextual factors moderate intervention effects

Related Strategies¶

  • Conduct large-scale, well-designed RCTs with multiple outcome indicators to evaluate new teaching methods
  • Prioritize rigorous RCTs with retention tests and transfer measures before expanding distance simulation programs
  • Rigorously compare PBL with traditional methods before broad PGME implementation, using larger samples, controls, and follow-up
  • Conduct well-designed long-term randomized controlled trials of PBL in oral radiology
  • Future MBI trials for youth should use scaled-up definitive designs with active intervention controls and embedded mechanism measures
