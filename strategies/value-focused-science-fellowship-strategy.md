# Year-long moderated value-focused fellowship for scientists

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention convened fourteen science faculty from different disciplines in ten 1.5-hour sessions across an academic year, discussing readings on topics such as inductive risk, choice of statistical methods, reproducibility, advocacy, and diversity. The fellowship "did not explicitly teach the participants about ethics, but was designed to foster directed exploration and discussion of the role that values play in decisions made within science." Sessions combined open-ended and directed discussion questions with written follow-up reflections.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A moderator to run sessions and guide discussion
- pre-readings provided before each session
- participants aware they are addressing value questions
#### Constraints
- Meetings moved to Zoom for the last three sessions due to the COVID-19 pandemic; participants came from a single midwestern research university

### Target Learners
- basic-science faculty across disciplines

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing value judgements in scientific decision-making
- improving moral sensitivity

### Affordances
- Four Component Model Ethical Decision Making
