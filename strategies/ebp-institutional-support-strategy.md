# Support EBP education with mentorship, protected time, and continuity of initiatives rather than isolated courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that EBP educational interventions be embedded in institutional support structures. It states that "institutions should endeavor to provide follow‐up support for EBP projects, such as mentorship offered by qualified professionals to EBP groups", and that institutions should "allocate protected time for professionals to engage in EBP project development". Continuity of educational initiatives is described as crucial to maintaining motivation, knowledge, and competencies, since isolated programs may not sustain change.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment including leadership support, mentorship programs pairing experienced EBP practitioners with novices, and protected time for project development.
#### Constraints
- Barriers such as job changes, excessive workload, and institutional priorities can limit the impact of educational strategies.

### Target Learners
- healthcare professionals in hospital settings
- hospital leaders and managers

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained integration of evidence-based practice into clinical decision-making and patient care
