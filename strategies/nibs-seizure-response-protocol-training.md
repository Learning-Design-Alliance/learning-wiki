# Train all NIBS trainees in a seven-step seizure response protocol and in distinguishing seizures from syncope

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because seizure is the most serious, albeit rare, adverse effect of TMS, the guidelines require all trainees to identify early signs of seizure and of syncope, which can resemble seizure, and to be practiced in a seven-step response protocol: remaining calm, calling for help, protecting the individual from harm, timing the seizure, remaining with the individual until help arrives, loosening restrictive clothing when appropriate, and preventing aspiration from vomiting. Trainees should also review common misconceptions to avoid restraining the individual or placing something in the mouth.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainees should observe different types of seizures in person or by video, and institutions typically require basic life support training including CPR
#### Constraints
- The article notes syncope can often resemble a seizure, complicating recognition

### Target Learners
- all NIBS trainees: Technicians, Clinicians, and Scientists

### Target Learning Goals
- recognition and management of seizures and syncopal episodes during TMS/tES administration
