# Take participants' gender into account when designing simulations for SBL

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
From the finding that women reported more hindrances than men, the authors draw a design implication: "this finding indicates the need to take into account the participants' gender when designing a simulation for SBL." They note prior work arguing that gender can affect how learners experience the simulated conflict, the group conversation and its efficacy, and the potential simulation-related hindrances, and that teaching is a predominantly female field in Israel.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designers must know or consider the gender composition of the participant group when planning scenarios and group discussion
#### Constraints
- The sample was 92% female, which the authors state may limit generalizability of the findings across genders

### Target Learners
- preservice teachers
- in-service teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- managing simulation-related hindrances
- experiencing simulated conflict productively
