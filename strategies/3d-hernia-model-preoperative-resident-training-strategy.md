# Use the reusable low-cost 3D-printed inguinal hernia model for resident training before operating-room participation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends positioning the 3D-printed model as a training supplement before residents enter the operating room: respondents gave complete agreement (median 5) that it is "valuable for training residents before entering the operating room", and the authors conclude it "could be a good option, particularly beneficial for training surgeons at the beginning of their careers". It supports practicing the full sequence of simulated TAPP steps in a safe environment, with EVA structures and replaceable peritoneum enabling reuse across sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a 3D printer and the released printing file
- Surgical video equipment and instruments at a simulation center, as used in the study
#### Constraints
- Electrosurgery cannot be used with the model
- Further studies are needed on cost-benefit, reproducibility, required training time, and learning curve impact

### Target Learners
- Surgical residents at the beginning of their careers
- Surgeons with limited TAPP experience

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning the steps of laparoscopic transabdominal preperitoneal inguinal hernia repair
- Recognizing inguinal anatomical structures in the laparoscopic view
