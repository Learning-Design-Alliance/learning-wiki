# Choose a journal by scope, readership and impact, and follow its author instructions through peer review

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The guide gives general guidelines for deciding where to submit: consider the field, scope and readership of the journal and check the author's instructions; prefer refereed professional journals for career development; aim as high as is reasonable, since a famous or quartile-1 journal is better than a less-known one; ask advice from more experienced researchers; and beware predatory journals that aggressively pursue submissions to collect publication fees. It also explains the roles of editor, referee and copy editor, and recommends registering with ORCID for a persistent digital identifier.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Following the chosen journal's author instructions on style, length and American or British English
#### Constraints
- Some journals ask for page charges, which can often be waived if requested before submitting; if a paper is rejected, analyse why and consider a revised submission

### Target Learners
- master's and doctoral students in astronomy and astrophysics

### Target Learning Goals
- selecting a publication venue
- navigating peer review and the publishing process
