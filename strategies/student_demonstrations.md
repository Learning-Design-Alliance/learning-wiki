# Student Demonstrations

## Description
Student demonstrations involve learners showcasing their understanding of a topic or skill through a performance, presentation, or hands-on activity. These demonstrations are incorporated as components of an assessment plan to evaluate what learners have learned.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires clear learning objectives, well-defined assessment criteria, and sufficient time for learners to prepare and present. Instructors must provide constructive feedback and create a supportive environment.
#### Constraints
- Assessment can be subjective and influenced by presentation skills. May not comprehensively assess all aspects of learning. Requires careful planning and scaffolding to ensure all learners can participate effectively.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learner levels, adaptable for K–12, higher education, and professional training. Particularly useful in subjects involving practical skills, performance, and creative expression.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Assess knowledge application, evaluate practical skills, promote active learning, encourage self-reflection

### Affordances
- Enables learners to actively apply knowledge, promotes deeper understanding, provides instructors with direct evidence of learning, fosters confidence and communication skills. Showcases practical skills and creativity, and offers an opportunity for self-reflection and feedback.

### Personalization
- Demonstrations can be tailored to learner interests, learning styles, and skill levels. Flexible formats allow learners to choose presentation methods, topics, and assessment criteria.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track learner performance against predefined criteria. Analyze depth and accuracy of presented content. Measure audience engagement and understanding of the demonstration.

## Impact
- Increased engagement: Research indicates active learning and demonstrations enhance student interest and motivation. Enhanced comprehension: Studies show learners retain information more effectively through application and demonstration. Improved communication skills: Practicing presentations bolsters learners’ communication abilities.
