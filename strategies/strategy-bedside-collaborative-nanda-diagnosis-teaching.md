# Student-recommended improvements to nursing diagnosis teaching: bedside instruction, collaborative care planning, smaller groups, consistent feedback, and broader domain exposure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Participants proposed concrete changes to nursing diagnosis instruction: extending teaching "beyond theoretical transfer, incorporating practical bedside instruction with instructors during clinical practice," joint care plan preparation with nurses, uniform face-to-face constructive feedback, reduced clinical group sizes, training on NANDA-I resources, and integrating at least one diagnosis from each of the 13 domains or 47 classes into theoretical and clinical education. The article's conclusion formalizes these as recommendations including promoting interactive bedside discussions and integrating innovative approaches such as simulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clinical placements with adequate instructor availability and manageable student-to-instructor ratios
#### Constraints
- The article notes the subject "cannot be adequately mastered within the confines of a single course" and suggests dedicated or elective curriculum placement

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students in clinical placements

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate identification and application of NANDA-I nursing diagnoses across diverse domains
- Diagnostic reasoning and care planning skills
