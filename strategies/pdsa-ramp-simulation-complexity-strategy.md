# Use a Plan-Do-Study-Act ramp to build simulation complexity stepwise, repeating a cycle when objectives are unmet before escalating.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates a PDSA ramp as "a framework for creating increasingly complex simulations in a stepwise, iterative fashion." Each cycle follows Plan (tailor scenario to learning objectives), Do (simulation plus facilitated debriefing with checklists), Study (assess performance, objectives, and latent safety threats), and Act (closed-loop debriefing with email summary; LSTs escalated to leadership). Progression is gated: "If the main learning objectives were fulfilled and no critical clinical issues were identified, we progressed up the PDSA ramp," otherwise the current simulation was repeated.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- High levels of coordination and facilitation; the article states in situ simulation "should not be initiated without serious pre-work and planning."
- A performance observation checklist and debriefing discussion points to evaluate each cycle before progressing.
#### Constraints
- The authors note "trauma systems are individualized across institutions and that these specific PDSA ramps reflect our local environment," and cite limitations including buy-in of stakeholders and barriers to implementation.

### Target Learners
- Multidisciplinary trauma team members: emergency physicians, ED nurses, surgery, anesthesiology, respiratory therapy, registrars, techs, police

### Target Learning Goals
- Trauma team leadership
- team roles
- communication
- clinical care decision-making
- systems assessment and identification of latent safety threats
