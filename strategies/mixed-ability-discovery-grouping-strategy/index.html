Compose discovery groups mixing high, medium, and low ability so peers support lower-ability students¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that before running discovery learning, teachers form groups containing high, medium, and low ability students, because higher-ability peers help lower-ability ones and guidance becomes more effective. The author links this to Vygotskian scaffolding and social learning, observing that grouped low-ability students could exchange ideas with moderate and high ability peers so obstacles were overcome. Teachers should first identify students' ability to create these conditions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers identify students' ability levels before forming groups
  • Groups contain a mix of high, medium, and low ability members

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • junior high school mathematics students of mixed ability

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mathematical analogical ability
  • collaborative problem solving in geometry

Claims¶

  • No significant interaction between learning method and initial ability category on mathematical analogical gain [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Assign small mixed-ability groups and manage group functioning and rotation for collaborative feedback tasks
  • Structure mutual learning teams with heterogeneous ability within teams and equality across teams
  • Use concrete props and algebraic patterns to scaffold analogical skill in geometry discovery lessons
