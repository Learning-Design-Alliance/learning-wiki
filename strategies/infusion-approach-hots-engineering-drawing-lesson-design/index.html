Strategies

Infuse specific higher-order thinking skills into each phase of Engineering Drawing lessons through real-world problem assignments, questioning, discussion and student presentation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Infusion Approach embeds named higher-order thinking skills (analytical and logic, reasoning, identifying/making assumption) into the introduction, main and closing sections of each Engineering Drawing lesson, using assignments with "problems related to the applications of Engineering Drawing based in real-world scenarios". Students present solutions, reason about their judgments, and defend ideas against questions from peers and the lecturer. The treatment teacher received three days of training covering HOTS definitions, teaching steps, problem-based learning, and the facilitator role before the intervention.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher training before implementation, as the treatment teacher received training to ensure the intervention would go smoothly

Constraints¶

  • Specific HOTS infused into particular topics were only used for the treatment group; the approach was practiced two hours a week over eight weeks

Target Learners¶

  • Technical Matriculation College students with prior Engineering Drawing knowledge

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Critical thinking disposition
  • higher-order thinking skills in engineering drawing

Claims¶

  • Infusing higher-order thinking skills into Engineering Drawing instruction produced significantly higher critical thinking disposition than conventional drill-based teaching after 8 weeks [+W]
  • Drill-based, exam-oriented teaching environments are associated with negative thinking dispositions such as indifference, bias and negligence [~W]
  • Descriptive means moved in opposite directions: the treatment group's critical thinking disposition mean rose while the control group's fell [+W]
  • Effectiveness of CT instruction varies with year level: first-year students benefited most under general or infusion approaches [~W]
