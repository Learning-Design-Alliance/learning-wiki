# Iteratively refine virtual laboratory teaching using student feedback: add group discussions, advance reading materials, and clearer in-experiment guidance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article implemented targeted improvements based on student feedback, which "included incorporating collaborative group discussions, distributing labreadingmaterialsinadvance to allow students sufficient time to prepare, and providing clearer guidance during experiment." Student suggestions for increased interactivity and self-exploration in the first two years were converted into group discussions on QA, QB, and QC in the third round. By 2024–2025 no students reported understanding issues, which the author presents as evidence the adjustments were effective.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mechanism for collecting structured open-ended student feedback each semester
- Willingness to revise instructional approach between iterations
#### Constraints
- Feedback noted software bugs, clunky operation, slow lab computers, and desire for video tutorials and more self-exploration opportunities

### Target Learners
- undergraduate general education students from science and arts backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- effective engagement with virtual laboratory experiments on quantum entanglement
