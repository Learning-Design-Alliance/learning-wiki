# Help GenAI users articulate their use as an efficiency-enhancing practice to reduce negative peer evaluations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on the finding that perceived AI literacy buffers the negative trust association, the article recommends that users' GenAI use be framed and communicated as deliberate, competent practice. The authors write that the findings "underscore the value of helping users articulate their GenAI use as an efficiency-enhancing practice, which may reduce the risk of being perceived as inauthentic or uncaring". They also suggest signaling active, collaborative use rather than passive reliance may support more favorable competence perceptions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Observers must be able to perceive the user's AI literacy or the rationale for the user's GenAI use
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional and correlational; the article calls for experimental and longitudinal replication

### Target Learners
- university students using generative AI in academic work

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining interpersonal trust and positive peer perceptions while using GenAI
