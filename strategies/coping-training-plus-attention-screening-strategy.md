# Train university students in stress-coping techniques alongside early screening for attention or learning difficulties

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that low-resource universities support achievement through psychosocial skill-building and cognitive support together. It suggests that "universities might develop programs to train students in effective stress-coping techniques, time management, and emotional resilience", and separately that institutions "Implement screening for attention or learning difficulties early in the academic journey", offering targeted support such as study skills coaching, cognitive strategy training, or quiet study spaces. The strategy pairs coping workshops with early identification of attentional weaknesses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional capacity to run coping and study-skills workshops and to screen students early in their academic careers
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence comes from a small single-institution cross-sectional sample, and the cognitive measures showed only modest effects

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in low-resource higher education settings

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement (GPA)
- stress management and self-regulation
