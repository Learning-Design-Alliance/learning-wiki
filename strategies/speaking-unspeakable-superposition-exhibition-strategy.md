# Use the single-electron interference experiment with bistable-image analogy to introduce quantum superposition without classical analogies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Speaking the Unspeakable exhibition introduced quantum superposition to the general public using the single-electron interference experiment proposed by Richard Feynman, which has no classical analogues. The organisers "used only the concept of quantum state and decided never to talk about wave-particle dualism so as not to suggest classical analogies that confuse rather than clarify". To support intuition, they used the analogy with the perception of bistable images.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A recording of the experiment performed in laboratories
- Written panels, installations, and interactive exhibits
- Guided tours by physics researchers, PhD and university students
#### Constraints
- Quantum superposition, having no classical analogue, is extremely difficult to explain using categories and concepts derived from everyday experience and without resorting to the language of mathematics

### Target Learners
- general public
- school groups

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of quantum superposition
- probabilistic nature of quantum measurements
- difference between quantum superposition states and classical mixed states
