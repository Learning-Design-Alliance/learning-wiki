# Use LLMs in medical education as support tools requiring human review, not as independent decision-makers or authoritative instructors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the demonstrated consistency gap, the authors recommend that AI systems in medical education be viewed "not as authoritative instructors but as support tools whose outputs require continuous human review and judgment." They caution that smaller LLMs with fewer than 7B parameters showed large consistency gaps and low CPR and "are not suitable for independent teaching tasks," potentially encouraging fragmented learning. Students should be trained to critically evaluate AI-generated answers across the full clinical context rather than accepting individual answers without verification.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Medical education programs should train students to critically evaluate AI-generated answers across the full clinical context
#### Constraints
- Even advanced models such as GPT-4o may answer some questions within a clinical case incorrectly despite strong overall question-level performance
- Smaller LLMs with fewer than 7B parameters showed large consistency gaps and low CPR

### Target Learners
- medical students
- medical educators

### Target Learning Goals
- safe and critical integration of LLMs into medical education and clinical training
