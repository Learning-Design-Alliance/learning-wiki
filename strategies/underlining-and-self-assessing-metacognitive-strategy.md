# Add underlining during reading and self-assessing at the end of lessons as metacognitive strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's specific recipe: underlining is used "in the phase of search" as a monitoring strategy to select important ideas from reading passages, and self-assessing is added "at the end of share phase" as a self-evaluation strategy in which students reflect on and evaluate the quality of their work, identify strengths and weaknesses, and revise. The authors judge both strategies especially effective for low academic ability students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Underlining must be attached to reading passages in the search phase; self-assessing questions must monitor what will be done, what has been accomplished, and what needs improvement
#### Constraints
- The authors' attribution of effectiveness to these strategies is interpretive; the study compared whole learning models, not the strategies in isolation

### Target Learners
- low academic ability students in senior high school

### Target Learning Goals
- comprehension monitoring
- self-awareness of strengths and weaknesses
- creative thinking
