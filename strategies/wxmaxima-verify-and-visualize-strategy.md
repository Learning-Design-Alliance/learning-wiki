# Embed wxMaxima in Calculus teaching so students verify hand computations and freed time goes to deeper conceptual understanding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 against) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding wxMaxima so the software serves as assistance to students and instructors "in verifying hand-computed results, obtaining numerical values of tedious expressions, and providing rich-quality graphical plots". Time saved on manual calculation "can be spent for a deeper understanding of key ideas, theoretical concepts, and problem-solving methods". Students connect visual plots of cubic functions with increasing/decreasing and concavity tests, and examine linear approximation near a point of interest.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors and students must learn to adopt and adapt the CAS, which the authors note can be challenging for beginners
#### Constraints
- Embedding technology adds burden in a 14-week Single-Variable Calculus course covering nine chapters; the authors suggest teaching with less material coverage, and some students resist CAS sessions

### Target Learners
- tertiary Calculus students
- current and future calculus teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding Calculus concepts such as limits, derivatives, integrals, extrema, concavity and linear approximation
