# Run compared simulation conditions on separate days with debriefing to limit carryover of stress between sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because the same students performed both simulations, the authors note this "could have inevitably modified their primary state after performing the first simulation". To minimize this effect they guaranteed an optimal debriefing and performed the simulations on two different days with several days of rest between the two events. As a future improvement they suggest dividing students into two groups with opposite simulation orders and comparing the results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Several days of rest between the two simulation events, with a debriefing session after each
#### Constraints
- The counterbalanced two-group design is proposed as a possible future improvement, not something implemented in this study

### Target Learners
- medical students participating in simulation-based team training

### Target Learning Goals
- valid measurement of stress responses across different simulation training formats
