# Graduated undergraduate periodontal curriculum: plaque-control and lifestyle counselling training, longitudinal patient assignments, and graded surgical integration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (2 for, 2 mixed, 3 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes dental curriculum reforms derived from its findings on student-performed therapy: integrated nutrition and lifestyle education with structured communication seminars for patient counselling, longitudinal patient-student assignments to relax rigid semester structures and support risk-based SPC adherence, guideline-compliant antibiotic use, and graduated clinical training in which early-semester students treat mild-moderate cases (PD ≤ 5 mm) while advanced students manage complex cases (PD ≥ 6 mm) with surgical integration. The authors state that "offering low-threshold surgical training already at the undergraduate level would be a meaningful step."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supervised treatment settings with licensed dentists or periodontists adjusting treatment steps, as in the studied student courses
#### Constraints
- The article notes this study did not directly assess educational interventions, so the curricular aspects warrant further investigation
- Postgraduate training for technically demanding techniques such as grafts and membrane techniques remains unaffected

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students

### Target Learning Goals
- periodontal diagnosis and non-surgical therapy
- plaque control and lifestyle risk-factor counselling
- surgical periodontal competence
