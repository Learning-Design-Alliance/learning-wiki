# Use active-learning activities — peer interaction, interactive engagement, writing tasks, and student-tutor dialogue videos — to teach quantum concepts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's section on instructional strategies reports several implemented and evaluated activities built on student activity: weekly peer interaction, interactive engagement methods such as quizzes, simulations and clicker questions, letter-writing about the photoelectric effect, PowerPoint versus summary-report writing tasks, and watching a student-tutor dialogue video on tunneling. The review concludes that "These results all suggest that active learning can contribute to better understanding of quantum concepts." Each activity was evaluated against a comparison group or alternative task in the cited studies.

## Design Implications

- The review notes several alternative ways to introduce quantum mechanics have not been properly evaluated and compared, so strategy choice beyond these evaluated activities remains under-researched

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of wave-particle duality, atomic models, the photoelectric effect, and quantum tunneling
