# Facilitators allowed ontologically incorrect argumentation to continue without interjection to support authentic scientific argumentation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy, drawn from the facilitators' actual moves in the workshop, is to let learners' ontologically incorrect but productive argumentation continue rather than correcting it mid-discussion. In the focal episode, "the facilitators let the conversation continue without interjection," because Daniel's insistence at reconciling Lynn's idea pushed her to articulate her reasoning better — argumentation the authors call "an authentic science practice." The strategy presumes facilitators monitor discussion for productivity rather than correctness of ontology.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators must monitor the discussion so that the argumentation remains productive, since the article notes a tension between supporting argumentation and nudging toward correct ontology

### Target Learners

### Target Learning Goals
- constructing a correct causal explanation for why objects of different masses fall with the same acceleration
