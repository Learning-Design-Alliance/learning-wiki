# Principle-based instruction telling children the goal is to make both sides of the problem equal

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
During training, the experimenter told children in the instruction conditions that "the goal in solving these problems is to make both sides of the problem equal", provided a principle-based procedure (find the total on the left, compare it to the right side, find a number that makes both sides the same), and gave feedback after each problem that recapped the child's reasoning and re-explained that the equal sign means 'is the same as'. This instruction, previously shown effective by Perry et al. (1988), was given after each of the training problems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction is delivered per problem with feedback tied to the child's own explanation
#### Constraints
- Only the procedure 'make both sides of the problem equal' can solve all 12 addition and 6 multiplication training problems; the problem set was designed to force reevaluation of narrower procedures

### Target Learners
- Fourth-grade students who do not yet understand mathematical equivalence

### Target Learning Goals
- Correct understanding of the equal sign and of mathematical equivalence
