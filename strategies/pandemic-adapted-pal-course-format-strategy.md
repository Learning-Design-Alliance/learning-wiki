# Adapt an existing PAL skill course to pandemic hygiene requirements by reducing group size, compacting sessions, and enforcing hygiene measures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a concrete adjustment recipe for hands-on skill teaching under hygiene restrictions: reduce course size from 5 to 4 persons, hold courses as "half day compact courses on two days with 6h sessions/day", require facemasks, regular hand washing, and disinfecting of transducers, equipment surfaces and hands, hold all courses within a two-week window with an OSCE at the end, register students online, and submit a hygienic concept to the deanery. Students were asked upfront whether they wanted to join the more intense format.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A hygienic concept submitted to the deanery before courses are held
- Upfront agreement of registered students to the more intense compact format
- Online registration with agreement to be contacted in case of infection
#### Constraints
- The authors report the two half-day format was felt to be too dense for many students, and they plan to return to weekly teaching over 8 weeks

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in skills training

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical skill acquisition (ultrasound) under infection-control restrictions
