# Pair initial newborn-care coaching with complementary quality-improvement and refresher strategies to sustain resuscitation skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because knowledge and skills declined significantly within about two years of coaching, especially resuscitation skills and among health workers who infrequently attend deliveries, the authors recommend complementary strategies beyond one-off training. These include periodic refreshers and regular practice with portable simulators for infrequent attenders, requirements for continuing education, strengthening local coordinator roles, and quality-improvement approaches such as daily bag and mask skills practice, peer review and weekly skills checklists.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ongoing facilitation, mentoring and support capacity after the initial coaching event
#### Constraints
- Strategies must fit the local context and be feasible within geographic and financial limitations of small-island, low-resource settings

### Target Learners
- Healthcare workers in low-resource facilities who infrequently attend deliveries

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustaining neonatal resuscitation and immediate newborn care skills over time
