Maximise self-assessment as an alternative teaching and learning strategy in writing classes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the significant effects found, the authors recommend that "teachers can maximize the use of self -assessment as an alternative teaching and learning strategy to be implemented in writing classes." Students are advised to continuously practise self-assessment to develop metacognitive skills in reflecting on their learning, being responsible for it, and staying motivated. The strategy positions self-assessment not merely for assessment purposes but as a teaching strategy and process of metacognition in which teachers facilitate and students take an active role in the whole writing process.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers provide time for decision making, drafting, reflecting, discussing, reading, and responding
  • Collaboration between teacher and student in using self-assessment techniques

Constraints¶

  • Evidence base is one post-test-only control-group study with 68 seventh-grade students in a single Indonesian school

Target Learners¶

  • junior secondary EFL students in writing classes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • writing competence
  • learning independence
  • metacognitive reflection

Affordances¶

  • Self Assessment Three Step Process

Claims¶

  • Self-assessment has a significant positive effect on seventh-grade EFL students' independence compared with conventional teacher assessment [+W]
  • Self-assessment has a significant positive effect on seventh-grade EFL students' writing competence compared with conventional teacher assessment [+W]
  • Self-assessment has a significant simultaneous effect on students' independence and writing competence together [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Teachers introduce portfolios in language classes so students regularly self-assess and become aware of their learning process
  • Train faculty and students in rubric use with calibration strategies to maximize agreement and self-assessment accuracy
