Strategies

Adopt Polya problem-solving teaching in senior secondary physics and train teachers in it¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that the Polya model produced better PTCE performance "irrespective of the scoring level", the authors recommend that use of the Polya Model be encouraged in senior secondary physics teaching, that pre-service physics teachers be exposed to the model during training, and that refresher training be organized for practicing teachers. They further recommend that textbook authors incorporate problem-solving models of teaching in new editions so that both teachers and students are encouraged to use them.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher training: pre-service exposure and in-service training and re-training programmes on the Polya Problem-Solving Model

Constraints¶

  • Recommendation is grounded in one quasi-experimental study in current electricity with 120 students in two Nigerian schools

Target Learners¶

  • Senior Secondary School physics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Physics problem solving and achievement

Affordances¶

  • Polya Four Stage Problem Solving Model

Claims¶

  • Senior secondary students taught current electricity with the Polya Problem-Solving Model performed better on the PTCE than students taught by lecture method [+W]
  • No significant performance difference between male and female students taught with the Polya Problem-Solving Model [~W]
  • Performance under the Polya Problem-Solving Model differed significantly by scoring level, with high scorers gaining most [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Shift physics teaching from lecture methods to activity-based problem-solving models
