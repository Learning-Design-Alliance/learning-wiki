# Intentional recruitment and retention efforts centering student assets and addressing stereotypes, especially in early introductory courses

## Description
The article recommends that increasing physics majors, especially women, "would require intentional recruitment and retention efforts that center student assets and focus on addressing cultural factors such as biases and stereotypes". It urges making introductory physics courses equitable and inclusive, since students declare majors in their second year and early courses show problematic gender differences. Faculty should be given incentives, support, and collective opportunities such as faculty retreats to reflect on recruitment and retention of majors with varying career interests as a departmental goal.
