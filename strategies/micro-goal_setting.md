# Micro-Goal Setting

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Micro-goal setting decomposes a large learning task into small, specific, near-term goals that a learner can complete in a single session or sitting. Each micro-goal is concrete enough that completion is unambiguous ("outline section 2," "learn five vocabulary items"), giving the learner frequent, verifiable success experiences. The strategy draws on goal-setting theory — specific, challenging goals outperform vague "do your best" intentions [Specific difficult goals lead to higher performance.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md) [+S] — and on self-efficacy research, since mastery experiences are the strongest source of efficacy beliefs [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M].

## Design Implications

Micro-goals work because they convert a distant, abstract outcome into proximal subgoals whose attainment supplies regular evidence of progress. Proximal subgoals are especially effective for novices, who lack the knowledge to translate distal goals into actions [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]. The design task is calibration: each goal must be attainable with effort but not trivial, and the sequence must visibly accumulate toward the larger objective.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-defined overall objective that can be decomposed into a coherent sequence of subgoals ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) of the task structure)
- Goals phrased in specific, measurable terms — completion criteria the learner can verify independently
- A visible progress-tracking mechanism (checklist, streak, progress bar) so attainment is salient
- Brief feedback or self-check after each micro-goal ([Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md), [Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md))

#### Constraints
- Goals set too low produce completion without challenge, undermining the sense of accomplishment that drives the effect [Specific difficult goals lead to higher performance.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md) [-S]
- Over-fragmenting a task can destroy its coherence — learners complete many micro-goals but never integrate them into the whole performance; each micro-goal should map onto a meaningful subtask, not an arbitrary slice
- Excessive extrinsic framing of micro-goals (points, streaks) can displace intrinsic motivation for learners who already value the task [~M]
- Micro-goals focused purely on completion quantity ("finish 10 items") can encourage shallow, speed-driven engagement rather than mastery

#### Implementation Variability
- **Instructor-assigned vs. learner-set:** learner-set goals build self-regulatory skill; novice learners may need instructor-scaffolded goal templates [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- **Time-boxed vs. scope-boxed:** "20 minutes on problem set" vs. "complete problems 1–5"; scope-boxed goals give clearer completion signals, time-boxed goals suit open-ended work
- **Digital tracking (Duolingo daily goals, Khan Academy mastery progress) vs. analog (learning logs, checklists)**

### Target Learners
- Novices facing large, complex tasks who cannot yet decompose them themselves [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]
- Learners with low self-efficacy or a history of failure, for whom frequent attainable wins rebuild mastery beliefs [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Less necessary for experts, who already set their own effective subgoals; imposed micro-goals may feel constraining [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained engagement and persistence on long or effortful tasks
- Self-regulated learning: planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's own progress ([Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md))
- Skill and knowledge acquisition that depends on consistent practice over time (e.g., vocabulary, coding fluency, instrument practice)

### Instructions
1. Define the overall objective and verify the learner understands it ([State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md))
2. Decompose the objective into micro-goals achievable in one session, sequenced so each builds on the last ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md))
3. Have the learner commit to the next micro-goal explicitly — written, spoken, or logged
4. Learner completes the micro-goal through deliberate [Practice](../elements/practice.md)
5. Check completion and give brief feedback before setting the next goal ([Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md))
6. Periodically review accumulated progress against the overall objective so micro-goals remain means, not ends

## Examples
- **[Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com)** — daily XP goals and streaks decompose language acquisition into daily micro-goals with visible progress tracking.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — mastery progress bars break a course into skill-level micro-goals; each skill exercise is a completable unit.
- **Writing instruction** — a research paper assignment scaffolded as micro-goals (topic approval → outline → annotated sources → draft section 1 → …), each with its own deadline and feedback checkpoint.
