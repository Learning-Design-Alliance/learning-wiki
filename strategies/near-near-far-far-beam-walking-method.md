# Teach beam steering with the iterative near-near far-far alignment method using near and far mirror adjustments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The package's core reusable alignment technique is the near-near far-far method: a beam is steered onto a desired path by alternately adjusting a near mirror to center the beam at a near reference point and a far mirror to center it at a far reference point, repeating until both points are centered. In Tutorial 2 the student should "Repeat the near-near and far-far alignment process until the beam exactly follows the desired bolt-hole line and is parallel to the table." The same method is reused in Tutorials 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7 for walking beams and centering beams through cage systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two adjustable mirrors (or a mirror pair) in kinematic mounts and reference points (ruler positions, irises, or cage centering targets) at near and far locations along the desired path
#### Constraints
- Only one direction and one mirror should be adjusted at a time when walking a beam both horizontally and vertically

### Target Learners
- students in an experimental optics laboratory course

### Target Learning Goals
- steering a laser beam onto a desired path
- centering a beam through irises or a cage system
