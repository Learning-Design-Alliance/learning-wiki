# Build confidence and early mastery experiences to address self-efficacy gaps and miscalibrated difficulty perceptions in introductory programming

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's discussion recommends instructional supports targeting students' beliefs in introductory programming: "interventions that target confidence-building may help address persistent self-efficacy gaps across gender and experience groups", and "designing early course activities that provide mastery experiences could reduce miscalibrated perceptions of difficulty". It names peer instruction, pair programming, and flipped classrooms as techniques shown to support self-efficacy and engagement, and advises instructors to scaffold engagement in flipped contexts with structured opportunities to build confidence and programming experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be situated in introductory programming contexts where students perceive programming as intimidating or lack prior experience
#### Constraints
- Findings and recommendations are scoped to a flipped CS1 course for engineering students at one research-intensive university and may differ in other courses, majors, or settings

### Target Learners
- Engineering students in introductory programming (CS1) courses, including non-CS majors without prior programming experience

### Target Learning Goals
- Programming self-efficacy and confidence
- Accurate perceptions of programming difficulty
- Engagement and persistence in introductory programming
