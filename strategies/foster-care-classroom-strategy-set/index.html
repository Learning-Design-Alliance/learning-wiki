Classroom strategy set for supporting students in foster care: buddies, helper tasks, structure, earned rewards, small successes, sincere praise, kept promises, high expectations, clean slates, and assumption-free assignments¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Teachers in the discussion groups shared a set of classroom strategies for students in foster care: buddy systems and hand-picked helpful groups, matching each child with a caring adult at school, giving the child a helper task and choices, providing a predictable structured environment, token economies with low-cost rewards, small do-able assignments with praise, keeping promises, keeping expectations high, starting each day with a clean slate, and avoiding assignments like family trees that set foster children apart. Teachers said a structured environment "may feel particularly safe — and thus be more conducive to learning."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must know or learn which students are in foster care, or apply the strategies to any new student, since many do not know which of their students are in the system

Constraints¶

  • Praise must reflect a true accomplishment, however small, because teachers noted foster children often have particularly acute radar for insincerity

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in the foster care system in elementary, middle, high, and alternative school settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Classroom engagement and behavior
  • Academic confidence and success
  • Trust and relationships with adults at school

Related Strategies¶

  • Welcome new students in foster care with buddy support and quick, low-burden informal assessments, while treating late records with caution
