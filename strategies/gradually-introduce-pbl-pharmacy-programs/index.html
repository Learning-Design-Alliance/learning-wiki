Pharmacy course directors should consider gradually introducing PBL methods into their programs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (3 quant-synthesis), q3 · 3 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review concludes that directors and teachers of pharmacy graduate and undergraduate courses should consider gradually introducing PBL methods into their programs. This recommendation follows the meta-analytic finding that PBL students performed better on objective examinations, while subjective evaluations did not differ. The authors also state that pharmaceutical education courses should consider implementing PBL.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher and staff training
  • A necessary reduction in class size

Constraints¶

  • The review notes PBL could increase the cost of pharmacy education, though it also reports research showing PBL and traditional curriculum costs are comparable

Target Learners¶

  • directors and teachers of pharmacy graduate and undergraduate courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving pharmacy students' knowledge and academic performance

Affordances¶

  • Pbl Method Pharmacy Education

Claims¶

  • PBL pharmacy students perform better on midterm examinations than students in traditional learning groups [+W]
  • PBL produces higher examination scores than LBL in undergraduate pharmacology education (SMD = 2.03) [+W]
  • PBL yields higher course examination scores than LBL in introductory Chinese undergraduate medical courses [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Introduce and promote PBL pedagogy in undergraduate pharmacology education
  • Exercise caution when selecting a single teaching method; consider disciplinary characteristics and student demographics, and weigh combined approaches
  • Scaffold method choice by student progression: CBL early, transitioning to PBL and TBL
