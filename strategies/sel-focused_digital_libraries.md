# SEL-Focused Digital Libraries

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy involves curating a digital library to include a dedicated section of books addressing Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) topics — self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, and responsible decision-making. Titles are tagged by SEL competency so teachers and students can search for books relevant to a specific skill, making it easy to weave SEL into reading activities rather than treating it as a separate program.

## Design Implications

Literature-based SEL works because narrative provides safe, vicarious contexts for reasoning about emotions and social situations; reading fiction is associated with improved empathy and social cognition [Reading fiction improves empathy and social cognition.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M]. A curated digital collection lowers the search cost that otherwise prevents teachers from using SEL-relevant texts, and tagging by competency supports targeted instruction. The library itself is only access infrastructure — effects depend on how texts are used in instruction, e.g., through [Dialogic Reading](../elements/act-it-out.md)-style discussion or [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) role-play, not on mere availability.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A digital library platform with a curated, metadata-tagged SEL collection (by competency, age band, theme)
- Teacher awareness of the collection and models for integrating it into literacy lessons
- Discussion prompts or activities that connect book content to students' own social-emotional experience
- Attention to diversity of characters and situations so students see themselves and others in the collection

#### Constraints
- Access alone produces little change; an uncurated or unused collection has no measurable effect [-W]
- Digital access gaps (devices, connectivity, home use rights) can exclude the students who most need the resources [-M]
- Collections that present SEL themes only through didactic, message-heavy stories reduce engagement and discussion quality [~W]
- If books depict only one cultural perspective, students from other backgrounds may experience lower belonging rather than greater [Belonging supports engagement and outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Whole-class [Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md) tied to a current SEL focus vs. student self-selection from the collection via [Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md)
- Teacher-curated "theme shelves" (e.g., managing frustration, friendship conflicts) refreshed to match class needs
- Audiobook and read-aloud formats to widen access for emerging readers
- Family-facing portals extending the collection into home reading

### Target Learners
- K–12 students, with the strongest evidence for elementary populations in universal SEL programming [Universal SEL programming improves social-emotional skills, attitudes, and achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]
- Students who benefit from vicarious, low-stakes contexts for exploring emotions before discussing their own
- Emerging readers, when the collection includes audio and read-aloud formats

### Target Learning Goals
- SEL competency development: emotion vocabulary, self-regulation strategies, perspective-taking
- Empathy and intergroup understanding through diverse character perspectives [Reading fiction improves empathy and social cognition.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M]
- Literacy goals pursued simultaneously — comprehension, discussion, and motivation to read
- Belonging and identity: seeing one's own experience represented in texts [Belonging supports engagement and outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Audit the existing digital library and identify gaps across the five SEL competencies and the student population's backgrounds.
2. Curate and tag the SEL collection by competency, theme, and reading level, using accessible language for the tags ([Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md)).
3. Publish the collection to teachers and students with guidance on how it maps to SEL goals.
4. Design integration activities: assign or offer titles, then structure discussion, journaling, or role-play that connects the story to students' lives ([Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md), [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md)).
5. Offer student choice within the collection to support ownership and motivation [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S].
6. Review usage data and teacher feedback each term; refresh titles and tags accordingly.

## Examples
- **[Epic!](https://www.getepic.com)** — K–6 digital library with curated collections and search by topic, including emotion- and friendship-themed shelves teachers can assign.
- **[Unite for Literacy](https://www.uniteforliteracy.com)** — free digital picture books with narration in multiple languages, usable for early-years SEL and family reading.
- **[Sora (OverDrive Education)](https://soraapp.com)** — school digital library platform that districts use to build curated SEL collections with class assignment features.
- **[CASEL](https://casel.org)** — publishes book lists and guidance aligned to the five SEL competencies that districts use to guide curation.
