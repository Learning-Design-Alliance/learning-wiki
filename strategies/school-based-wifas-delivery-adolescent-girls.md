# Deliver iron supplementation to adolescent girls through school-based platforms such as weekly iron and folic acid supplementation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review describes schools as the primary delivery platform for adolescent iron interventions, since adolescents are the least likely to access health systems for preventative services. In areas where anemia prevalence is ≥20%, WHO recommends WIFAS containing 60 mg elemental iron and 2.8 mg folic acid once weekly for three consecutive months, aligned with the school semester. Teachers and ministries of education are key partners, and defining academic and cognitive benefits of WIFAS is of keen interest to schools investing time in delivery. The review notes out-of-school girls will miss these benefits, so additional platforms are needed for vulnerable groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Partnerships with the education sector, including teachers and ministries of education, for delivery and reporting of the health intervention
- Continuous once-weekly consumption of the WIFAS supplement, aligned with the school semester
#### Constraints
- Out-of-school girls will miss out on the benefits of school-based supplementation programs, so additional platforms are needed for vulnerable groups

### Target Learners
- adolescent girls in areas where anemia prevalence is ≥20%
- school-aged children in areas where anemia is highly prevalent (≥40%)

### Target Learning Goals
- improved school performance, attention, and concentration alongside anemia reduction
