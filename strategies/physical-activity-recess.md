# Physical Activity & Recess

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Physical activity and recess strategies embed movement into the school day rather than treating it as time taken from instruction. Forms include scheduled recess, short classroom movement breaks (e.g., "Energizers"), physically active academic lessons, and active transitions. The rationale is bidirectional: activity supports the attentional and executive systems that learning depends on, and active formats can deliver academic content directly.

## Design Implications

Aerobic activity acutely improves executive function and attention in children, which are the cognitive resources classroom tasks draw on [Hillman et al. found better reading and math performance following acute exercise.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0909437106) [+S]. Reviews find that adding physical activity to the school day does not harm academic achievement and often benefits it, particularly in mathematics [Rasberry et al.'s CDC systematic review found positive or neutral effects on academic outcomes.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ypmed.2011.01.027) [+M]. Physically enacting content can itself support learning, consistent with evidence that embodied experience enhances science learning [Physical experience enhances science learning.](../claims/physical-experience-enhances-science-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Protected time: recess and movement breaks that are not withheld as punishment or displaced by test preparation
- Moderate-to-vigorous activity options, since dose matters — brief sedentary "brain breaks" show weaker effects than sustained aerobic activity
- For active lessons, content-accurate movement tasks (e.g., jumping while counting, acting out a story sequence) rather than generic exercise

#### Constraints
- Withholding recess as a behavioral sanction is counterproductive: it removes the very activity that supports self-regulation and attention [-M]
- Generic movement breaks without aerobic intensity or content linkage show small or null academic effects [~W]
- Poorly structured active lessons can add extraneous load — novelty and physical management demands compete with the target content [~M]
- Benefits are acute and transient; single sessions do not substitute for daily activity

#### Implementation Variability
- **Recess** — unstructured play; primary value is social-emotional and attentional restoration
- **In-class movement breaks** — 5–10 minute structured activity (e.g., [Take 10!](https://www.take10.net), GoNoodle)
- **Physically active academic lessons** — movement carries the content itself (e.g., the [PAAC curriculum](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ypmed.2011.01.027) delivered math and reading through movement)
- **Active transitions** — walking routes, standing discussions, [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) dramatization of texts

### Target Learners
- Elementary students, who show the most consistent attention and executive-function benefits from acute exercise [+S]
- Students with ADHD and low self-regulation, who benefit disproportionately from activity opportunities [~M]
- Students who are kinesthetically engaged by embodied tasks [Physical experience enhances science learning.](../claims/physical-experience-enhances-science-learning.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Attention and executive function as prerequisites for any academic task
- Procedural and conceptual content that can be embodied (counting, sequences, spatial relations, story structure)
- Social skills and self-regulation developed through unstructured play

### Instructions
1. Schedule recess daily and protect it from being withheld or displaced.
2. Insert 5–10 minute aerobic movement breaks between cognitively demanding blocks, using structured routines ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) can pair with a brief stretch or walk).
3. Where feasible, convert review or practice into active formats — [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) for narratives, movement-based counting or sorting for early math.
4. Keep active lessons content-focused: the movement should encode the target concept, not merely occupy energy.
5. Monitor dose — aim for moderate-to-vigorous intensity, not just "on your feet."

## Examples
- **[Physical Activity Across the Curriculum (PAAC)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ypmed.2011.01.027)** — a 3-year cluster-randomized trial in 17 elementary schools delivering math and reading through physically active lessons; academic achievement was maintained or improved.
- **[Energizers](https://www.eatsmartmovemorenc.com)** — free North Carolina classroom-based movement activities tied to academic content.
- **[GoNoodle](https://www.gonoodle.com)** — short video movement breaks widely used in elementary classrooms.
- **Finland's model** — 15 minutes of outdoor play after every 45-minute lesson, paired with strong international academic performance.
