# Design GenAI learning support to withhold direct answers and prompt students to articulate reasoning and generate solution ideas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 6 studies (2 causal, 2 theoretical, 1 review, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 6 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In this RCT, all GPTs across conditions were given a common design feature: "All GPTs were explicitly instructed not to provide correct answers, even if requested". The authors justify this by evidence that students often request complete solutions prematurely and that direct answer provision can reduce generative cognitive engagement, sometimes described as "metacognitive laziness". The design deliberately prioritized cognitive activation by prompting students to articulate reasoning and generate solution ideas, with correct solutions provided on a subsequent page for corrective feedback and self-correction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prompts must be developed through iterative prompt-engineering with a structured knowledge base covering tasks, solutions, and intervention principles; GPTs need access to task materials and solutions to reduce hallucinations
#### Constraints
- Withholding solutions may appear pedagogically inauthentic, as the authors acknowledge; the approach targets SRL processes rather than optimizing performance or providing direct tutoring through answer provision

### Target Learners
- secondary school students (Grades 7–9) in regular physics and English lessons

### Target Learning Goals
- cognitive activation, generative engagement with learning tasks, and self-regulated learning processes
