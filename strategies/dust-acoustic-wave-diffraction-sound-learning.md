# Use diffraction of dust-acoustic waves around a cylindrical object as a model system for learning diffraction of sound waves

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because dust-acoustic waves and sound waves obey a similar set of equations, the article proposes using a dusty plasma medium to demonstrate how waves bend around an obstacle, paralleling ripple-tank demonstrations. The wavelength of DAW can be changed by altering discharge parameters, helping students understand the amount of diffraction around differently sized cylindrical objects. The article concludes that "a dusty plasma medium can be considered as an excellent model system to learn the diﬀraction of sound waves at the graduate level in the physics laboratory", citing Kim et al.'s published diffraction experiment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dusty plasma experimental setup with a larger sized cathode (> 10 cm) and smaller sized anode (< 2 cm) configuration in DC discharge
- A cylindrical or spherical object placed in the dust grain medium as an obstacle
#### Constraints
- The diffraction results the article describes are reproduced from Kim et al.'s prior published experiment, not newly reported here

### Target Learners
- graduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding diffraction of sound waves around obstacles and its dependence on object size and wavelength
