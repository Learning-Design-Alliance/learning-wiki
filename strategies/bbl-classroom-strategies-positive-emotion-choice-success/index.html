Classroom strategies from brain-based learning: positive emotion, student choice and responsibility, and the joy of success¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends concrete teaching practices derived from its brain-based account: teachers should not threaten students or say learning is difficult, because negative feeling blocks information from reaching long-term memory; teachers should build positive emotion, gradually increase student responsibility by letting students "make their own decisions, evaluate themselves, design their own solution", and start from what students can do to give them the joy of success. Wrong answers are treated as opportunities for instruction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must express confidence that students can learn, stay close during assignments, and use leading questions

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • primary school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • positive emotion toward learning; long-term memory of content; student ownership of learning

Affordances¶

  • Brain Based Learning Framework Waree

Related Strategies¶

  • Teachers up-regulating positive facial expressivity during teaching, now supported by evidence that teachers emotionally benefit from student reciprocation of joy expressions
  • Use indirect correction and self-correction to avoid face-threatening error treatment
  • Build autonomy-supportive learning contexts through perspective-taking, choice, self-initiation, and non-controlling language
  • Design feedback conditions in which students feel understood, respected, and invited to actively process feedback, supporting emotion regulation
