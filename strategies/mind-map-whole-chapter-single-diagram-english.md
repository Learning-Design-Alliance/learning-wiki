# Replace lecture-only English instruction with mind-map-based presentation of the whole chapter in a single diagram

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends presenting an entire English chapter in one diagram to make it interesting and learner-centred: "English can be made interesting, if we present the whole chapter in a single diagram. Mind map makes it possible." It argues that traditional methods leave learners as passive listeners and that mind maps introduce an overall topic, get thoughts down quickly and cut across ability levels and subject matters. The conclusion states the mind map method "could be of much help to the teacher to teach English more effectively".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher-created mind maps for the selected English grammar content
- Willingness to move from traditional instruction to a learner-centred approach
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single small study in one boys' higher secondary school; the authors call for validation in other areas of English and at higher secondary and college levels

### Target Learners
- VIII standard high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved achievement in English grammar across knowledge, understanding and application

## Claims
