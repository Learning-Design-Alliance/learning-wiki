# Run a low-cost school lunar research project combining telescope field work with open-source data analysis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a project model in which school students observe the Moon with an affordable reflector telescope using a structured observation metric, then analyze assigned lunar zones with open-access LROC quickmap data using a data analysis metric, and share experiences through podcasts. The authors state "This kind of project has high impact for school science education with minimal costs" and that the affordable telescope "makes this model accessible to schools with limited resources", and suggest organizations use it as a science outreach program to popularise planetary sciences at school level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A relatively affordable reflector telescope with eyepieces
- Open-access data such as LROC quickmap
- A structured observation metric and data analysis metric
#### Constraints
- The authors note it would be beneficial to assess improvements in academic performance and sustained enthusiasm in the long term

### Target Learners
- secondary school students in grades 8-10

### Target Learning Goals
- astronomy and geology understanding
- data analysis skills
- critical thinking
