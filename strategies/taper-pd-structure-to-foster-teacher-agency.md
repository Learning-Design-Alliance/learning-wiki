# Taper researcher-provided structure and leadership over time so teachers take over design, direction, and agenda setting of their professional community

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (3 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that leaders of learner-driven PD deliberately reduce the structure, leadership, and mentoring they provide so that participants can take increasing responsibility for tasks and for the group's vision and mission. As the authors note, "the structures, leadership, and mentoring provided by the principle investigator and two graduate students was deliberately tapered over time," and teachers subsequently took control of the group's direction. This strategy balances the need for initial norm-setting with the philosophy that teachers know their own needs best.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Initial structure is needed to establish norms for supporting learning before responsibility is handed over.
#### Constraints
- Participants reported struggling with the apparent lack of structure of the program since its inception.

### Target Learners
- practicing K-12 science teachers in professional learning communities

### Target Learning Goals
- developing teacher agency and leadership
- designing and enacting learning experiences for themselves and colleagues
