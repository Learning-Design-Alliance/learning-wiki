# Use freely re-watchable training videos as a fast, resource-efficient method for large-scale PPE donning and doffing training during infectious-disease emergencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends video training as an alternative to face-to-face instruction when urgent, large-scale training is needed: instructor-led training is "time-consuming for both trainees and instructors," and gathering people in person "increases the risk of exposing staff to infection." In this trial, video-trained participants performed as well as instructor-trained participants while videos allowed unlimited re-watching from home, supporting social distancing and minimizing PPE used in training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Videos must demonstrate the same procedures and use the same equipment as live training would, and must be tested for clarity before deployment.
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a small pilot trial with large confidence intervals; a larger study might show a significant difference between methods.

### Target Learners
- health care providers
- medical students
- junior doctors
- personnel deployed during infectious-disease outbreaks

### Target Learning Goals
- correct donning and doffing of personal protective equipment
