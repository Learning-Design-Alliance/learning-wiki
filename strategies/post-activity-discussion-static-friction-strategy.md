# Follow analogical reasoning activities with an instructor-led post-activity discussion targeting the misconception

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that after students complete the analogical reasoning quiz, the instructor carry out a discussion using the quiz as a concrete example of why static friction need not equal its maximum value. The authors state the activity may serve as a good starting point and that sustained similar activities and post-activity discussions throughout an introductory physics course are likely to help students develop expertise and become better problem solvers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must first complete the analogical reasoning quiz activity involving the solved isomorphic problem and the friction problem
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as advantageous following the quiz activity, noting the maximum-value notion remained common even after the scaffolding interventions

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students in calculus-based and algebra-based courses

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding that static friction is not always equal to its maximum value
- applying Newton's second law in equilibrium problems
