# Aggregate instructor and student measures at the institutional level when individual-level matching is not feasible.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
When educators and students are recruited from multiple universities across different cities, direct individual-level matching between specific instructors and specific students is not feasible. Analyses can instead be conducted at the institutional level, aggregating educators' quality-of-life scores and students' clinical education quality scores within each institution before performing correlation analyses on the aggregated scores.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All educators included must be confirmed to be actively involved in clinical teaching of the participating final-year students within their respective universities.
#### Constraints
- Individual instructor–student dyads are not analyzed, so the observed associations should be interpreted as contextual relationships within educational settings rather than direct individual-level effects.

### Target Learners
- medical education researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- examine relationships between educator variables and perceived educational quality

## Claims

- [Midwifery educators' quality of life is positively and significantly correlated with students' perceived clinical education quality at the institutional level (r = 0.219, p = 0.042).](../claims/educator-qol-institutional-clinical-training-correlation.md) [+W]
- [Physical functioning is the strongest predictor of clinical education quality among the eight SF-36 dimensions (β = 0.807, p < 0.01).](../claims/physical-functioning-strongest-sf36-predictor.md) [+W]
- [Students' age is inversely and significantly correlated with their perception of clinical education quality (r = −0.259, p = 0.001).](../claims/student-age-inverse-clinical-quality-correlation.md) [+W]
