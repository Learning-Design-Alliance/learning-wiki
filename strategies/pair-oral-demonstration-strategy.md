# Oral mastery demonstrations in student pairs, with a pitch and course-assignment evidence requirement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Demonstrations are oral examinations at a computer terminal with one examiner and a pair of students, both of whom must individually demonstrate mastery. Students pitch the demonstration by stating the achievements, explaining why they fit together, and presenting the evidence — artefacts from the course's own assignments. The evidence requirement was added after the first instalment, when allowing any code "prompting students to search for code rather than write their own". Pairing reduces stress, enables discussion, and halves examiner time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examiners must be prepared to assess any part of the course material at any time
- Evidence must come from course assignments to prevent code searching
#### Constraints
- Some students complain they prefer explaining in writing; the authors provide no alternative means of demonstration

### Target Learners
- second-year university programming students

### Target Learning Goals
- programming skill mastery
- technical communication
- reflection on course concepts
