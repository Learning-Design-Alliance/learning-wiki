# Combine examiner training, scoring standardization, multiple examiners, and feedback mechanisms to improve oral examination quality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review groups proposed solutions into examiner training and calibration, question and scoring standardization, use of multiple examiners, integration of technological tools, implementation of feedback mechanisms, and structural or format adjustments. Examiner training was the most frequently recommended intervention (62 studies; 60.8%), "typically involving orientations, workshops, or peer review sessions to mitigate bias and improve scoring consistency", while standardization through structured checklists, rubrics, or predefined case scenarios was suggested in 58 studies (56.9%). Solutions were frequently tailored to the specific challenge domain.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examiner training and calibration sessions; standardized questions and scoring rubrics; multiple examiners where feasible
#### Constraints
- Technological adoption in traditional formats was limited (4 of 24 cases; 16.7%); time requirements (25–37.5 hours for 150 students) posed feasibility challenges

### Target Learners
- Medical and paramedical students and postgraduate trainees undergoing oral assessments

### Target Learning Goals
- Fair, reliable, and valid assessment of clinical competencies via oral examination

### Affordances
- Osve Optimized Oral Exam Framework
