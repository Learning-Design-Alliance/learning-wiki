# Train an interprofessional workforce for dementia care coordination and care transitions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that research show how to "improve the delivery of dementia care and services, increase satisfaction with care, and reduce costs to improve care coordination and care transitions". It cites evidence that care coordination interventions can reduce caregiver burden, delay institutionalization, and improve quality of life, and notes that trained dementia care navigators are growing in both for-profit and not-for-profit sectors. Training a workforce to coordinate multiple needs and services "requires specialized training".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interprofessional collaboration and reciprocal working partnerships between community-based providers, clinicians, and team members
#### Constraints
- Medicaid long-term services and supports are constrained by state budget limits; Medicare does not cover LTSSs needed as dementia progresses

### Target Learners
- interprofessional dementia care teams
- dementia care navigators
- clinicians

### Target Learning Goals
- care coordination and management
- care transitions
- integrating caregivers into interprofessional teams
