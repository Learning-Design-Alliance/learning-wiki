Use progress monitoring to tailor math instruction to each child's position on a developmental progression¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The guide's third recommendation is a concrete assessment routine: "Use introductory activities, observations, and assessments to determine each child's existing math knowledge, or the level of understanding or skill he or she has reached on a developmental progression." Teachers then tailor instruction to each child's needs, relate new ideas to existing knowledge, and assess, record, and monitor each child's progress throughout the year so instructional goals and methods can be adjusted as needed.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Understanding developmental progressions is necessary to employ progress monitoring
  • Teachers must assess, record, and monitor each child's progress so goals and methods can be adjusted

Constraints¶

  • The panel could not find research that isolated the impact of progress monitoring on children's math knowledge; the rating is minimal evidence

Target Learners¶

  • children ages 3 to 6 in preschool, prekindergarten, and kindergarten

Target Learning Goals¶

  • individualized progression through early math skills
