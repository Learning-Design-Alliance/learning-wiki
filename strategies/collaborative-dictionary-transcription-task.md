# Have learners look up highlighted words, write their phonetic transcriptions, and compare them collaboratively while the teacher explicitly emphasizes stress location

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the experimental condition, learners used Longman pocket dictionaries to look up highlighted words and write their phonetic transcriptions down, working collaboratively to compare transcriptions. After the teacher explained the articulation of specific phonemes, students pronounced words while looking at their transcription and could ask for assistance. This explicit, task-based transcription work replaced equivalent time spent on listen-and-repeat practice in the control class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need access to dictionaries containing phonetic transcriptions
- The teacher must explicitly emphasize the right location of primary stress and explain articulation of specific phonemes
#### Constraints
- Tested with adult university EFL learners; the author suggests the method will probably have different results with young learners

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate EFL students
- adult learners whose L1 has a different stress system

### Target Learning Goals
- Correct location of English lexical stress
- explicit knowledge of L2 phonology

### Affordances
- Slm Perception Precedes Production
