# Low-cost, schoolwide book distribution without teacher training or incentives as a scalable literacy strategy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends simply distributing high-interest, culturally relevant books schoolwide for students to keep and read at home, without bundling in training, incentives, or accountability. The authors state that "unlike many book distribution programs, this initiative required no teacher training and minimal staff involvement," and that at approximately $2 per book the full-treatment impact was achieved for roughly $68 per student. This isolates the effect of access alone and makes the strategy easily scalable in high-poverty settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A book sourcing and distribution partner; school-level delivery to maximize student reach
#### Constraints
- Effects depended on cumulative and sustained exposure; students in the most severe book deserts showed descriptively smaller effects, and the authors suggest more intensive services may be needed there

### Target Learners
- Students in high-poverty elementary schools, particularly those lacking books at home

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved reading achievement and literacy outcomes
