Reach out to families with low-cost SMS socio-emotional support during remote or disrupted schooling¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 8 claims (6 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that, beyond curricular remediation, public school systems reach out to families to provide support and encouragement during challenging times, using "simple technologies such as text messages" that "do not require smartphones or internet access" and work "under much lower complexity and costs" than trained counselors. The strategy targets students' motivation, emotion regulation, growth mindset and grit via twice-weekly thematic message sequences, and can complement school counselors when specialists are unavailable. Schools should also verify phone-number accuracy and consider distributing SIM cards with earmarked connectivity to reach low-income families.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Valid phone numbers for the students or caregivers to be targeted
  • Students or caregivers reachable via SMS; in low-literacy settings the article notes text may need accompaniment by audio calls or in-person help interpreting messages

Constraints¶

  • Effects documented under remote learning in a middle-income setting; generalization to other settings is stated as requiring additional research
  • Findings based on the ~40% of students for whom the Education Secretariat had valid phone numbers

Target Learners¶

  • High-school students (grades 10-12, ages 15-18) in public full-time programs
  • Their primary caregivers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Maintaining engagement and motivation with school activities during remote learning
  • Mitigating learning losses in math and Portuguese

Claims¶

  • Targeting students directly (versus caregivers) produced significantly larger nudge effects on test scores (0.31 vs. 0.08 s.d.) [~W]
  • Message content mattered: loss framing and avoiding social pressure produced significantly larger test-score effects, ruling out experimenter-demand explanations [+W]
  • Treatment effects were larger for previously higher-performing students and for schools with prior online activities, consistent with complementarity between curricular content and socio-emotional skills [~W]
  • Text-message nudges achieving impacts of the same order as remote remedial instruction indicates motivation itself carries much of remote-learning effectiveness [+W]
  • Nudges significantly decreased the share of schools with sub-proficient average test scores, from 17.1% to 8.4% [+W]
  • The intervention increased student engagement with remote activities and motivation to return to in-person classes [+W]
  • SMS nudges targeting socio-emotional skills increased student-level standardized test scores during remote learning, by 0.21 s.d. in Portuguese and 0.19 s.d. on the summary measure [+W]
  • Nudges prevented 24.2% of learning losses in Portuguese and 7.5% in math at school level, though imprecisely estimated [+W]
