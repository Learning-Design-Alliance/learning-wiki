# Use pre-recorded sub-lectures of roughly 10 minutes with interspersed graded assessments, and use synchronous time for active engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends asynchronous pre-recorded lectures so synchronous meeting time can be devoted to interactions, discussions and reflections, in the spirit of flipped and Just-in-Time Teaching approaches. Instructors pre-recording their own lectures are told to "break your lecture into roughly 10 -minute long sub-lectures and intersperse online assessments that you can grade students on", e.g., multiple-choice, between sub-lectures, a design the author says helps maintain attention and lets students assess their learning between modules.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tools for recording voice-over slides or writing-capable devices (laptop or iPad) and an online assessment mechanism
#### Constraints
- Existing resources such as FlipIt Physics may cost money; engaging students in peer interaction via polling in large classes may be more difficult

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in large or small online lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained attention to lecture content
- self-assessment of learning between modules
- active engagement during synchronous sessions
