# Consider alternative and multi-modal approaches to promote OSCE data review, including peer-assisted review, grade weighting, and extended SP feedback time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Beyond faculty debriefing, the article's discussion enumerates options to encourage student review of F-OSCE data: adopting a video-review and informed self-assessment process with administrator tracking, peer assisted learning where students "meet with a peer and review their data together", making all OSCEs contribute to the course grade, and scheduling more SP feedback time during exams. The authors conclude "A multi-modal approach that involves several of these strategies might be the best approach to reach more of these students."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation recording software able to track student review of data
- A process of video review, informed self-assessment and reflection such as that demonstrated in prior work
#### Constraints
- The authors note it is unclear how much feedback a learner can absorb in real time when extending SP feedback
- Whether grade weighting would encourage review is posed as a consideration, not a tested result

### Target Learners
- first- and second-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement with formative performance feedback
- self-assessment and reflection on clinical skills
