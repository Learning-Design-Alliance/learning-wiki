# Explicitly discuss the rubber sheet analogy's limitations with students to prevent gravity misconceptions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers know and react to misconceptions the RSA can reinforce, such as thinking space-time is two-dimensional and that gravity always points downward, or that black holes are holes into which matter falls. The approach used, which "proved to be effective", is to make the limitation explicit and use students' own considerations (such as a marble leaping over the hole) to make them reflect. The same applies to the intrinsic limitation that the sheet shows only spatial deformation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be prepared to know and react to RSA-related misconceptions, dedicating time to defining the model, its simplifications and how to guide discussion
#### Constraints
- The limitation that the rubber sheet shows only spatial deformation is intrinsic to the model and cannot be overcome, only explicitly addressed

### Target Learners
- high school students
- high school teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- correct understanding of gravity and space-time deformation
- avoiding misconceptions about gravity direction and black holes
