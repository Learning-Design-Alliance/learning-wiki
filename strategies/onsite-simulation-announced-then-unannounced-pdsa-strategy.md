# Conduct on-site simulation sessions with a first announced session and a second unannounced session within a PDSA cycle

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable training recipe is simulation training using Plan-Do-Study-Act: "In the first session, the protocol was explained in advance, and the scenario was made known to all participants", allowing protocol problems to be examined and contents disseminated; "In the second session, the scenario and implementation date were not given in advance, to ensure a more realistic situation and evaluate the staff understanding and proficiency." Debriefing sessions after each simulation promoted the PDSA cycle and identified problems to fix.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A multidisciplinary core group
- informed core staff to play the patient and recorders
- a preprepared recording sheet
#### Constraints
- Staff playing the patient found their personal background pervaded the role, and the paper-based scenario was difficult to understand because the patient's situation was not reflected on the vital monitor

### Target Learners
- hospital staff at facilities distant from emergency departments, including staff with little direct patient contact

### Target Learning Goals
- emergency response proficiency
- protocol familiarity
- sustained awareness and preparedness
