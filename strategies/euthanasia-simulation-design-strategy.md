# Design euthanasia simulation training with expert-validated content, small role-play groups, realistic simulated environments, and reflective DML debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article offers its findings to support teachers in designing and evaluating euthanasia simulation training programs. Students indicated that "a realistic environment should be created", whether a simulated hospital room or a simulated home environment, and that debriefing should use the reflective Debriefing for Meaningful Learning method rather than CRM principles. Modules should be tailored to different geographical and legal contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content validated by experts in end-of-life care
- A preparatory online learning module covering legislation and conversation techniques
- A skilled lecturer/facilitator leads the debriefing
#### Constraints
- Conclusions drawn only from nursing students in the Belgian legal context
- Participation was voluntary, so participants may have been already interested in the topic

### Target Learners
- final-year nursing students
- nursing program designers

### Target Learning Goals
- handling euthanasia requests
- end-of-life communication skills
- reflective attitude towards euthanasia
