# Use paired self-survey and scenario-based competence assessment results to build need-based emergency medicine curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends administering both a self-survey and an objective scenario-based assessment, then directing curriculum development at the domains where competence is weakest. It states that "those domains which showed poor competence should be of particular interest for the development of emergency medicine curriculum" and that periodic monitoring can evaluate future curriculum effects. The strategy is grounded in the tools' moderate correlation and feasibility in a developing-country setting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated self-survey and an objective assessment method combined for more valid and practical results
- Periodic re-administration to monitor competence and curriculum effects over time
#### Constraints
- Self-survey results may be subjective; over-estimation exceeded under-estimation in this study
- Supplementary assessment such as clinical behavior observation may be needed to improve validity

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals in developing countries preparing to staff emergency care facilities

### Target Learning Goals
- Emergency medicine clinical knowledge and procedural competence aligned to local needs
