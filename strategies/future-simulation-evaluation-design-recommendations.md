# Future simulation evaluations should use rigorous designs, reduce attrition, and add booster practice, team dynamics, and distraction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future evaluations of clinical simulation use rigorous experimental designs (suggesting a stepped wedge design randomized by location), reduce attrition by creating quarantined off-road participation time or rewarding participation, and ensure representative samples. They also recommend exploring "best-practice methods to maximize the impact of the advanced technology clinical simulation manikins", citing prior research suggesting "incorporating booster practice, team or group dynamics, and distraction (eg, placing the manikins in real-life situations)", and collecting paramedic OHCA case volume data or validating self-report as a proxy measure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Future evaluations should collect paramedic OHCA case volume data or a validated proxy measure
#### Constraints
- The authors note that in their current form the manikins are an overt assessment tool and it remains unknown how manikin ECC performance translates to in-the-field behavior and patient outcomes

### Target Learners
- registered paramedics

### Target Learning Goals
- resuscitation skills maintenance and broader advanced life support, trauma, and nonclinical team skills
