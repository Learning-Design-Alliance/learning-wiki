# Plan the second language course situationally rather than grammatically

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends organizing an adult second language course around real-life situations in which speakers of the target language actually find themselves, rather than around a grammatical syllabus. It reports that "Planning a second language course situationally rather than grammatically... proved to be sufficient for significant target language improvement," for example introducing people before asking them to interview each other rather than presenting a lesson on the present tense before one on the past tense. Teachers may require students to develop reports, make interviews, and produce dialogues and skits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful planning so that students have some contact with and participation in the target language every day.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation derives from limited experimental research with thirty adult multi-national students over fifteen weeks.

### Target Learners
- adult second language students
- immigrant learners of English

### Target Learning Goals
- target language improvement in structural and communication skills
