# Positive Behavior Support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Positive Behavior Support (PBS, often implemented as Schoolwide Positive Behavior Interventions and Supports, PBIS) is a proactive framework that reduces problem behavior by (a) defining and explicitly teaching behavioral expectations, (b) reinforcing them consistently, and (c) using functional behavioral assessment to understand what problem behavior accomplishes for the student and replacing it with functionally equivalent, appropriate behavior. It operates at three tiers: universal supports for all students, targeted group supports for some, and individualized intensive supports for a few.

## Design Implications

PBS treats behavior as a learned, communicative act shaped by its environment, applying the logic of [Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md) with an emphasis on antecedent prevention and skill teaching rather than reactive punishment. Its central empirical finding is that explicitly teaching expectations and reinforcing them reduces office discipline referrals and improves school climate [~S], with effects strongest when implementation fidelity is high and sustained over multiple years [~M]. Punitive exclusionary discipline (suspension, expulsion) does not reduce future problem behavior and disproportionately harms marginalized students [-S]; brief empathic-discipline interventions that reframe misbehavior as developmental rather than willful reduce suspensions [Brief empathic discipline interventions reduce suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A small set of positively stated, schoolwide behavioral expectations (e.g., "be safe, be respectful, be responsible")
- Explicit instruction of what each expectation looks like in each setting ([Modeling](../elements/modeling.md) and [Practice](../elements/practice.md) of routines, not just posters)
- A consistent, low-effort reinforcement system (acknowledgment tokens, praise ratios heavily favoring positive over corrective statements)
- Functional behavioral assessment and behavior intervention planning capacity for Tier 3 cases
- Leadership commitment and staff-wide implementation fidelity; effects degrade sharply when only some staff participate [~M]

#### Constraints
- Punishment-based responses (reprimands, exclusion) suppress behavior temporarily but do not teach replacement skills and increase avoidance, disengagement, and dropout risk [-S]
- Token economies implemented without fading plans can undermine intrinsic motivation and produce dependence on external rewards [~M] — reinforcement should shift toward natural and social contingencies
- PBS implemented as a compliance or discipline program (tracking points, public displays of earned status) can shame students and damage belonging [~M]; public leaderboards and point loss are common implementation failures
- Effects on academic achievement are indirect and inconsistent; PBS alone does not improve instruction [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Tier 1 (universal):** schoolwide expectations, routines, acknowledgment systems
- **Tier 2 (targeted):** check-in/check-out mentoring, small-group social skills instruction ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))
- **Tier 3 (individualized):** person-centered planning, functional communication training, wraparound supports
- Classroom-level adaptations: [Classroom Management](classroom-management.md) routines, precorrection of predictable problem contexts, choice-making opportunities to build autonomy [Autonomy support improves motivation and engagement.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- All students benefit from predictable, explicitly taught behavioral routines [~S]
- Students with emotional and behavioral disabilities and those with a history of exclusionary discipline benefit most from Tier 2/3 function-based supports [+M]
- Students whose problem behavior is maintained by escape from aversive tasks need instructional accommodations ([Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md)) alongside behavior plans — punishing escape-motivated behavior without fixing the task escalates it [~S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Behavioral: acquisition of prosocial and self-regulation skills
- Climate: sense of safety and [Belonging](../elements/belonging.md), which supports achievement [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Self-management: students monitoring and evaluating their own behavior ([Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md))

### Instructions
1. Define 3–5 positively stated expectations with staff and, where possible, student input.
2. Teach expectations directly in every setting using [Modeling](../elements/modeling.md), rehearsal, and feedback — treat behavior like any other skill ([Explicit Instruction](../principles/explicit-instruction.md)).
3. Establish a high-frequency acknowledgment system with at least a 4:1 positive-to-corrective interaction ratio; plan for fading toward natural reinforcement.
4. Respond to minor problem behavior with brief, calm, instructional corrections; reserve exclusionary discipline for safety-critical situations.
5. For persistent cases, conduct a functional behavioral assessment, hypothesize the behavior's function, and teach a functionally equivalent replacement behavior with [Fading](../elements/fading.md) of supports.
6. Monitor implementation fidelity and outcome data (referrals, attendance, climate surveys) at least quarterly and adjust.

## Examples
- **[PBIS](https://www.pbis.org)** — the OSEP-funded national technical assistance center; provides the standard Tier 1–3 implementation guides used in over 25,000 U.S. schools.
- **Check-In/Check-Out (CICO)** — a Tier 2 intervention in which a mentor greets the student each morning, sets daily point goals, and reviews progress each afternoon; meta-analytic evidence supports moderate reductions in problem behavior for escape- and attention-maintained behavior [~M].
- **Functional communication training** — teaching a nonverbal student to request a break with a card instead of throwing materials, directly replacing escape-motivated aggression.
