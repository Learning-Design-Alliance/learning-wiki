Four ways teachers can administer and score the vocabulary lists, from raw scores to weighted scale values¶

Description¶

The article prescribes four practical uses of the 100-word lists: giving them as they stand and comparing raw scores with the ten-school norms; assigning the scientifically determined scale weights to each word in scoring, which the article argues is "far more equitable than to give each word the same value"; selecting a ten-word list differing in difficulty by equal steps for quick large-scale comparison; and using a twenty-word sublist of nearly equal difficulty to simplify scoring. A perfect weighted score on the Latin-English list is 125.5 points.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to the printed word lists and their Table IV/V scale values for weighted scoring

Constraints¶

  • Weighted scoring requires the year-appropriate scale values; raw-score comparison is only valid against the reported school norms

Target Learners¶

  • first-year high school Latin pupils

Target Learning Goals¶

  • measuring vocabulary knowledge for rating pupils, comparing schools and teachers, and studying methods

Claims¶

  • Vocabulary test scores correlate moderately with teachers' final Latin grades, averaging .56 for Latin-English and .66 for English-Latin [+W]
  • The Latin-English and English-Latin vocabulary tests correlate very highly (average .82), so either test could be used alone without much error [+W]
  • First-year Latin vocabulary scores vary widely across ten good high schools, from 90 percent correct to 42 percent correct on the Latin-English list [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Score sentence tests by summing each correctly translated sentence's scale value rather than raw number correct
