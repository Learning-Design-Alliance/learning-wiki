# Support low-DEC students by sustaining engagement and prompting strategic reading behaviors such as related-slide recommendations and slower pacing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends targeted support for students with low trait-level flow, whose disadvantage "can be mitigated through higher engagement," and for skimming-prone clusters, where instructors could prompt students to slow down and recommend slides related to the current one. These operationalizations translate the moderation findings into concrete, implementable classroom and analytics actions for personalized reading support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reading log sequence metrics and DEC questionnaire scores to identify low-DEC students and their reading profiles
#### Constraints
- Trait-level flow itself cannot be changed; support works by mitigating its disadvantage through engagement and strategy

### Target Learners
- higher-education engineering students with low DEC profiles

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable learning outcomes
- more strategic reading behaviors

## Claims

- [DEC and reading-strategy metrics jointly explain an additional 21.3% of grade variance beyond a log-traced engagement baseline of 25.5%, totaling 46.8% of variance explained](../claims/dec-and-strategies-add-grade-variance-beyond-engagement.md) [+W]
- [DEC improves grade prediction beyond engagement (ΔR² = 7.8%), and DECI moderates the engagement–grade relationship, with the engagement effect reduced for high-DECI students](../claims/dec-moderates-engagement-grade-effect.md) [+W]
- [The slope of DECI on final grades differs across reading-strategy clusters (omnibus interaction significant), while the DECE moderation is not significant](../claims/dec-moderates-strategy-clusters.md) [+W]
- [Trait-level flow (DECI/DECE) shows no significant correlation with the log-derived engagement indicator, suggesting the two capture independent aspects of learning](../claims/dec-uncorrelated-with-log-engagement.md) [+W]
