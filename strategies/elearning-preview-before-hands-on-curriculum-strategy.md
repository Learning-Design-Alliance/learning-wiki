# Assign e-learning access before the hands-on ultrasound curriculum to improve OSCE performance of novice sonographers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends having novices preview the e-learning materials before the in-person curriculum: "using e-learning before the curriculum had a significant impact on the OSCE scores", which the authors say indicates "previewing the learning materials played an important role in better understanding sonographic techniques and standard images". In the study, 48 of 70 users accessed the platform before the day-7 curriculum and outperformed the 22 who accessed it after. The strategy preserves flexibility, since e-learning could occur at any time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An e-learning platform covering the target application's core knowledge and standard images
- A hands-on curriculum following the preview period
#### Constraints
- Clinical FAST performance (number, image quality, accuracy) showed no significant differences between before- and after-curriculum users; the OSCE benefit was the measured difference
- The optimal timing for e-learning use was described by the authors as still uncertain, citing prior randomized studies with comparable outcomes

### Target Learners
- PGY-1 residents and other novice sonographers in short, focused training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- sonographic technique and standard image recognition
- OSCE performance in point-of-care ultrasound
