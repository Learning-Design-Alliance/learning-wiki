Implement PSU-reduction and screening actions to protect academic achievement, prioritizing elementary and middle school students¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q4 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends raising awareness of PSU's potential negative impact on achievement and targeting two objectives: identifying at-risk students via "psychometric screening tests that could be applied to both students and external informants such as parents", and designing educational initiatives instructing students in PSU-reduction strategies such as replacing smartphone time with physical activity, disabling non-essential notifications, keeping the phone silent or leaving it at home. It also suggests extending initiatives to parents through supervisory and restrictive mediation, particularly for younger students.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • initiatives should be designed, implemented, and tested for effectiveness, per the article

Constraints¶

  • the article states the supporting evidence is cross-sectional, so causal benefits of PSU reduction on achievement are not yet established

Claims¶

  • Problematic smartphone use shows a small negative pooled correlation with academic achievement (r = −0.110) [+M]
  • All available evidence on the PSU–achievement relationship is cross-sectional, preventing causal conclusions [~M]
  • The PSU–achievement relationship is not significantly moderated by sex, region, age, year of publication, study quality, achievement indicator, PSU assessment format, or publication status [~M]
