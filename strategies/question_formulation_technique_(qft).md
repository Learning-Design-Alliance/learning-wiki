# Question Formulation Technique (QFT)

## Description
The Question Formulation Technique (QFT) is a structured method for generating and improving questions to develop critical thinking skills, increase self-efficacy, and encourage participation in civic life. It involves divergent, convergent, and metacognitive thinking. Participants are presented with a Question Focus (QFocus), which can be a statement, phrase, image, video, or other stimulus. They then follow four rules: Ask as many questions as possible, do not discuss or judge the questions, write down every question exactly as stated, and change statements into questions. Participants categorize questions as open- or closed-ended, discuss the value of each type, and change questions from one type to another. They prioritize questions based on specific instructions related to the central objective, such as identifying the most important questions or those that will aid research. The process concludes with a discussion of next steps and a reflection on what was learned and how it can be applied. The facilitator guides participants through the steps, validating contributions equally and avoiding examples that might impede independent thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator, a Question Focus (QFocus), writing materials, and a structured environment following the rules of the technique. Time is needed for each step of the QFT process.
#### Constraints
- Requires a structured environment and adherence to specific rules, which may feel restrictive to some learners. The effectiveness depends on the facilitator's ability to guide the process without influencing the content of the questions. Some participants may struggle with generating questions or prioritizing them effectively.

- The QFocus can be tailored to the individual's or group's specific interests or needs. Individual question generation allows for personalized exploration, and prioritization can be aligned with personal goals.

## Assessment Evidence
- The quality and quantity of questions generated, the ability to categorize and prioritize questions, and the depth of reflection on the learning process can be assessed. Participants' feedback on the usefulness of the technique and their plans for applying what they learned can also be evaluated.

## Impact
- The QFT has been shown to be transformative for both students and professionals, helping them identify key goals, clarify objectives, and develop a mindset of inquiry. It enhances motivation, promotes self-directed learning, and fosters active solution-seeking.
