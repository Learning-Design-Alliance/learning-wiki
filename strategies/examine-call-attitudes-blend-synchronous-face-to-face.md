# Examine learners' attitudes toward CALL and blend synchronous with face-to-face speaking tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that negative perceptions may have dampened VT's impact, the authors urge instructors to assess learner attitudes toward CALL and to combine task modes: "language teachers need to blend synchronous and face-to-face tasks ... rather than using merely asynchronous ones in speaking classes to develop friendly bond between learners". They also recommend helping students make their voices heard in teacher-centered systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must actively examine learners' attitudes toward their learning context and toward CALL tools
#### Constraints
- In contexts like Iran's, teacher-centered traditions, cultural reservations about CALL, and logistical and financial issues can impede implementation

### Target Learners
- EFL university students in CALL-peripheral contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- Speaking accuracy and willingness to communicate in English
