Staff game jams with tutors who have pedagogical, technological, collaborative and creative competencies, plus volunteers for materials and technical support¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends tutors familiarized with video-game development who hold "pedagogical, technological, collaborative, and creative competencies", create a safe space, and remain neutral and impartial. Volunteers handle idea-generation techniques, extra materials, and technical problems such as software installation and Internet connection issues.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tutors with game-development or theme knowledge and the four competency areas
  • Voluntary workers for materials and technical problems
  • A venue with electricity, Internet, tables, eating area and quiet resting areas

Constraints¶

  • Tutors must not display preferences or prejudices to foster a truly creative session

Target Learners¶

  • adolescent students in game jams

Target Learning Goals¶

  • 4C skill development through supported creative design

Claims¶

  • Participating in game jams develops 4C skills (critical thinking, creativity, communication, collaboration) in adolescent students, though skill gains are difficult to verify [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use an intermediate game-jam duration of about 6 hours, long enough for teams to pass through the first four development phases
