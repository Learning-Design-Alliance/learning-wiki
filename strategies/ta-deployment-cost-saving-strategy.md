# Deploy teaching associates for patient-based clinical skills training to combine effectiveness with cost savings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using teaching associates to transfer complex practical skills, anticipating the method could be easily translated to nearly every patient-based clinical skill, particularly for competence-based education of future doctors. It also argues the economic case: in the local remuneration structure two student TAs at 10€/h each versus a faculty member at 50€/h make it possible to save up to 60% of costs while assuring a high training standard, and it creates time for the medical faculty.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- TAs must be trained in feedback giving and the target examination beforehand
- a training manual describing all examination items must be available
#### Constraints
- Single-center design; other universities might have different conditions that would prevent the findings from generalizing to their student populations

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- complex practical clinical examination skills

### Affordances
- Teaching Associates Visuohaptic Feedback
