# Use in situ high-fidelity simulation with an integrated lung simulator as an affordable training and assessment tool for mechanical ventilation, targeting frequently missed skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends its high-fidelity simulation model, combining a mannequin with a lung simulator that can change compliance and resistance, as a training and assessment experience "at a relatively affordable cost for university departments and clinical facilities". Because two key items were missed frequently by the computer group (troubleshooting patient-ventilator dys-synchrony and performing recruitment maneuvers/prone positioning for severe hypoxemia), the authors suggest more specific teaching of these skills, and note HFS could evidence errors in clinical practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mannequin with an integrated lung simulator connected to a ventilator, plus instructors experienced in mechanical ventilation and simulator programming
#### Constraints
- The study was not designed to evidence specific gaps; whether simulation training translates into better real clinical performance remains an open question for future studies

### Target Learners
- anesthesiology and critical care residents

### Target Learning Goals
- mechanical ventilation management skills, including lung-protective ventilation in ARDS
