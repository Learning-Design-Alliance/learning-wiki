# Fade explicit comparison-cycle support across the course (instructions first, then marks, then instructor support)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementation strategy is to begin with explicit instructions and grading rubric items requiring students to make and act on quantitative comparisons, then progressively withdraw this support. As the supplementary fading map shows, "The explicit instructions in the text were the ﬁrst to be removed, followed by assigned marks, and eventually instructor support was also removed." By weeks 16 and 17 instructions given to both groups were identical, so the persistent group differences reflect behaviour maintained without support. New statistical tools were introduced as the instructions faded.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Repeated experiments each week offering data-model and data-data comparison opportunities
- A marking scheme that initially enforces comparing and iterating
#### Constraints
- The printed fading schedule is specific to this year-long physics lab course; the article does not test alternative fading schedules

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory science laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- autonomous critical thinking behaviours about data and models
