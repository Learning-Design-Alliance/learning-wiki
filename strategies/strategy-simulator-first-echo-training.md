# Use an echocardiography training simulator for initial skill acquisition before scanning real patients

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
NPE and TNE guidelines recommend that learners first acquire basic echocardiography skills on a simulator: "how to acquire basic echocardiography views, how to learn hand-eye movements, how to manipulate probe to acquire a particular or sweep views". Once these skills are acquired on the simulator, learners can rapidly adapt to perform echocardiography on real patients. This addresses the scarcity of training opportunities on vulnerable sick neonates and the difficulty of encountering the required number of abnormal cases, especially in smaller neonatal units.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a simulator with a substantial case database of congenital heart defects and different functional/hemodynamic states
#### Constraints
- High acquisition costs for high-fidelity simulators and running costs such as dedicated experts, space and time; limited data on skills transfer from the simulated environment to real patients

### Target Learners
- neonatology fellows
- neonatologists without prior echocardiography skills

### Target Learning Goals
- basic view acquisition
- hand-eye coordination
- probe manipulation
- pattern recognition of uncommon congenital heart defects
