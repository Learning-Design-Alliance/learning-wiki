# Introduce an openly accessible rating system for training hospitals to raise teaching standards

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose "an openly accessible assessment system for training hospitals" in which "a visible rating system would allow students and residents to identify teaching hospitals more suited toward their needs and motivate other departments to raise their training standards to combat a shortage of doctors." It leverages the survey finding that training location strongly shapes student ratings, with Vienna rated worst, to create competitive pressure on departments. It also feeds into supervisor accountability, since supervisors receiving multiple negative reviews may be replaced.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Systematic collection of student ratings of clinical training per hospital and department
- Open accessibility of the ratings to students and residents
#### Constraints
- The article links the system to combating physician shortage but does not report any test of the rating system itself

### Target Learners
- Medical students and residents choosing clinical training placements

### Target Learning Goals
- Selecting clinical training environments suited to students' needs
- Improving supervision and teaching quality in hospitals
