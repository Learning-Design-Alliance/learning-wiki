# Post-activity reflection questions linking classroom parallax accuracy to Hipparcos and Gaia precision

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (3 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
After pupils complete either activity, the article recommends having them work through questions connecting their own measurements to real astronomy. The article suggests pupils be asked how large their parallax angle was, how accurately they could read angles, what distances they could determine at 10% accuracy, and how that compares to astronomical accuracies, so that "Simple calculations involving ratios can serve to link these accuracies in a classroom setting to actual parallax measurements", letting pupils appreciate the precision of Hipparcos and Gaia.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Completion of one of the two parallax activities, so pupils have their own measurements and accuracy estimates to reflect on
#### Constraints
- The article notes pupils "are likely to need some guidance in order to ﬁnd this solution for the longer baseline" (Earth's orbit) when extending the discussion to larger baselines

### Target Learners
- students grade 7 and older
- students grade 10 and older

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting classroom measurement accuracy to the precision of real astronomical parallax missions
