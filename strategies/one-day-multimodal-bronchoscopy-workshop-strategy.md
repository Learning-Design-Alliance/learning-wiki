# Use a one-day multimodal simulation workshop combining lectures, manikins, virtual simulation and a 3D-printed model to train basic bronchoscopy to multidisciplinary teams

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a workshop combining "didactic lectures, small group skill stations, virtual bronchoscopy simulations and hands-on training using manikins and a novel, cost-effective, 3D-printed airway model". The design was based on Peyton's four-step teaching approach and Gagne's model of instruction. The authors report this multimodal format produced consistent knowledge gains across professional backgrounds and high satisfaction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty consultants in pulmonology, anaesthesia and radiology plus experienced endoscopy nurses
- Single-use flexible bronchoscopes with monitors and anatomically accurate airway manikins
#### Constraints
- The authors state simulation-based training can provide fundamental bronchoscopy skills but cannot replace learning bronchoscopy on real patients under supervision

### Target Learners
- Intensivists and critical care team members without structured bronchoscopy training

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic bronchoscopy knowledge and procedural skills including anatomy, technique, sampling and critical care practices
