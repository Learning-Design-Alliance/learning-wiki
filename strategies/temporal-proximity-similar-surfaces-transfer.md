# Administer isomorphic transfer questions in close temporal proximity to the tutorial and keep surface features of the two contexts similar

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article hypothesizes that transfer from the MZI to the DSE context was aided by the close temporal proximity of the DSE transfer questions to the tutorial and by the shared surface features of both instruments, which relate to single photons and polarizers in front of various paths or slits. Specifically, it proposes that proximity in timing "may make it more likely for students to be able to discern the similarity between the two contexts". The strategy is to sequence analogous questions soon after the instructional context that teaches the target reasoning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instructional context (the MZI tutorial) that teaches the target reasoning, such as WPI, before the analogous questions are administered.
#### Constraints
- Transfer of learning from one context to another is not guaranteed a priori even if students understand the underlying physics principles in the MZI context.
- The authors present the temporal-proximity and surface-similarity factors as hypotheses, while noting introductory students may not discern similarity between isomorphic questions even when asked back to back.

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate quantum mechanics students
- first-year physics graduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- transfer of quantum interference reasoning between isomorphic contexts
