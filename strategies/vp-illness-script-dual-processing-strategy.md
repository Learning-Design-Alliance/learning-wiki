# Support illness-script construction and dual processing in VPs by linking scenarios to underlying illness scripts, offering large pools of short focused cases, and allowing learners to skip steps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on the Psychological Theories category, the authors recommend uncovering the implicit link between VPs and the illness scripts they are based on, engaging learners in actively and explicitly building their own illness scripts, which form stand-alone learning achievements that can be enriched outside a VP environment. They also recommend creating a large pool of short and focused VPs covering a variety of problems and clinical presentations, and envision a dynamic representation of reasoning allowing learners to apply analytical reasoning, pattern recognition, or a combination depending on VP complexity, skill level and goals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- VP scenarios authored with explicit illness-script components such as a problem list, differential diagnoses or a final diagnosis; a large pool of short and focused VPs at varying levels of complexity
#### Constraints
- The authors note there are "still many open questions concerning the exact nature of dual processing"

### Target Learners
- Novice healthcare students developing illness scripts and pattern recognition

### Target Learning Goals
- Analytical reasoning, pattern recognition, illness-script construction and knowledge encapsulation
