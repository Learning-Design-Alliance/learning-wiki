Design synchronous online sessions as interactive lessons rather than recorded-style lectures¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's discussion recommends that synchronous online time be used for interactivity rather than one-way lecturing, because students who schedule time for live sessions expect interaction. It reasons that "Students scheduling time to participate in synchronous sessions would prefer to have an interactive session (Martin et al., 2012) rather than listen to a lecture which could have been recorded and delivered asynchronously." This strategy follows from the moderator result that interactive lessons outperformed lectures on affective outcomes in the synchronous-versus-asynchronous condition.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Synchronous sessions must be scheduled with real-time two-way communication so interactive formats (rather than lecture) are feasible

Constraints¶

  • The supporting moderator evidence comes from the synchronous-versus-asynchronous affective condition only; method could not be examined as a moderator in the face-to-face comparisons
  • Moderator findings rest on few studies and are subject to the detected publication bias

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students
  • graduate and professional learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • affective outcomes such as learner satisfaction, engagement, and attitudes

Claims¶

  • Interactive synchronous lessons yielded larger effects than lectures for affective outcomes versus asynchronous learning [+W]
  • Synchronous online learning did not significantly affect affective outcomes compared with asynchronous online learning (g = 0.32, p = .051) [~W]
  • Synchronous online learning significantly and positively impacts cognitive outcomes compared with asynchronous online learning (g = 0.37) [+W]

  • Enrich live online lectures with visuals, varied instructor communication, and interactive structures to sustain attention and positive emotion
