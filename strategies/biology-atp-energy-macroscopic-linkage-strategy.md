# In biology classes, add explicit focus on how energy stored via ATP relates to macroscopic processes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article notes that biology classes spend considerable time on how photosynthesis and respiration result in the synthesis of ATP, "but less time on how the energy stored via ATP is used." It recommends biology classes "include a more explicit focus on how these microscopic mechanisms relate to macroscopic processes," helping students connect microscopic and macroscopic energy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Biology instruction must already cover ATP synthesis via photosynthesis and respiration, so the strategy extends existing content rather than replacing it.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a single-student case study and is framed as a direction for the authors' new integrated physics-biology curriculum development.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life science majors and pre-health-care students

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting microscopic energy mechanisms (ATP) to macroscopic biological processes
