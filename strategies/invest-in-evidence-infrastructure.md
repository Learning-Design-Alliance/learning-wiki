# Invest in technological infrastructure for evidence generation, including synthetic data, shared platforms, and privacy-enhancing technologies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article argues that 'infrastructure-less evaluations are becoming less tenable' as funding shrinks, and identifies growing infrastructure opportunities: synthetic data sets for early-stage innovation testing, platform data made research-accessible (e.g., AIMS EduData making data from Curriculum Associates and Khan Academy available), generalized rapid-cycle evaluation toolkits, a potential registry of district rapid-cycle evaluations, and privacy-enhancing technologies that permit research on detailed learning data without revealing personally identifying information.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Any new evidence ladder should be steeped in evolving capabilities of infrastructure for data gathering, research methods, and rapid-cycle evidence gathering
#### Constraints
- Participants offered the strong caveat that synthetic data are unlikely to convince a district decision maker

### Target Learners
- Researchers
- edtech developers
- district leaders

### Target Learning Goals
- Lower-cost, faster evaluation and evidence building for emerging technologies
