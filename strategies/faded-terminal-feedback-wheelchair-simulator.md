# Provide terminal augmented feedback on outcome (time and collisions) plus trajectory review on a faded schedule

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The feedback group received terminal feedback after each trial: task completion time, number of collisions, a 2D overhead trajectory overlay marking collisions and near-collisions, and snapshots of their three worst moments. To avoid overwhelming novices, trajectory feedback was faded — given after each of the first three trials, then every three trials — while time and collision feedback was given every trial. The article suggests alternative feedback methods could include concurrent warning sounds near obstacles or feedback on joystick movement patterns.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback must be informative enough to guide future movement, with level matched to learner skill and task difficulty
#### Constraints
- In this study the terminal outcome feedback was not informative enough to produce significant group differences on time or collisions; its effect appeared only in speed-accuracy distributions during and immediately after training

### Target Learners
- novice powered wheelchair trainees in simulator-based rehabilitation

### Target Learning Goals
- driving safely by avoiding collisions and efficiently by not driving too slowly
