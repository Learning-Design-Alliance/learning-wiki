Integrate the discourse analysis method into language and literature lessons as the primary textual analysis instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends integrating the discourse analysis method into language and literature lessons so students analyse texts as wholes, in surface and deep structure, rather than paraphrasing or repeating content. The author concludes that "integration of discourse analysis method to language and literature lessons makes these lessons more interesting and attractive" and would "bring a multidirectional success". In the study it was applied to the experimental group for four months while the control group kept routine practices.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers and students learn and apply the discourse analysis method to texts over an extended period (four months in the study)
  • Texts are treated as wholes with all interrelated constituents, not reduced to content, form, context, author or reader alone

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from one eleventh-grade class of 20 in a single foundation laboratory school in Erzurum, compared with one control class of 24, chosen by convenience and purposive sampling

Target Learners¶

  • eleventh-grade secondary students in language and literature courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading comprehension
  • textual analysis
  • making discourse
  • use of language
  • critical and evaluative thinking
  • language awareness

Claims¶

  • Eleventh-grade students taught with the discourse analysis method showed larger pretest-to-posttest gains in making discourse skill than the traditionally taught control group [+W]
  • Eleventh-grade students taught literature with the discourse analysis method showed larger pretest-to-posttest gains in reading comprehension than the traditionally taught control group [+W]
  • Eleventh-grade students taught with the discourse analysis method showed larger pretest-to-posttest gains in textual analysis skill than the traditionally taught control group [+W]
  • Eleventh-grade students taught with the discourse analysis method showed larger pretest-to-posttest gains in use of language skill than the traditionally taught control group [+W]
  • Experimental-group students described literature lessons using the discourse analysis method as enjoyable and confidence-building, reporting increased interest and participation [+W]
  • Experimental-group students reported that the discourse analysis method improved their thinking skills, including analysis, synthesis, inference and critical thinking [+W]
