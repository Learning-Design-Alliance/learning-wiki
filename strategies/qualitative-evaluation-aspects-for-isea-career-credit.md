# Evaluate informal education efforts for career advancement through recommendation letters and a statement of informal education efforts, using qualitative aspects instead of metrics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that institutions evaluate ISEAs for hiring and career advancement the way they evaluate service work: via letters of recommendation detailing initiative, commitment, time, and impact, supplemented by a candidate-written statement of informal education efforts. Because ISEAs vary too widely in scale and effort, it argues "it is not practical to establish metrics," and instead provides a representative list of evaluation aspects: Initiative, Creativity, Audience size, Duration, Interactivity, Impact, Funding, Publicity, and Longevity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Letter writers must be instructed to comment, when appropriate, on candidates' informal education efforts
- Candidates may include a statement of Informal Education Efforts in their career advancement portfolio
#### Constraints
- The article states metrics are impractical because ISEAs vary widely in initiative, commitment, and time; some reach thousands of participants and others just a few

### Target Learners
- physics researchers and faculty whose ISEA participation is being evaluated

### Target Learning Goals
- institutional recognition of informal science education efforts in career advancement
