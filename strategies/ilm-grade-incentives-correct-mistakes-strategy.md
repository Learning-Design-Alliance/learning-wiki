# Incentives for learning from mistakes (ILM): grade incentives for correcting exam mistakes before seeing solutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (7 for, 2 mixed) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 review, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The ILM pedagogy gives students grade incentives to correct their own mistakes on graded assessments before receiving correct solutions. "The ILM pedagogy is based on the tenet that instructors can explicitly incentivize students to learn from their mistakes by rewarding them for correcting their mistakes." In this study the instructor returned posttests with incorrect parts marked but no explanations and asked students to correct their mistakes as homework for up to half of the lost quiz points, creating productive struggle outside class without extra class time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Returning assessments with incorrect parts marked but without explanations, so students diagnose mistakes themselves using resources such as class notes and the textbook
#### Constraints
- Uptake was voluntary and incomplete when the assessment was low stakes (worth less than 1% of the final grade), unlike a prior midterm implementation where all students corrected

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- learning from problem-solving mistakes
- developing problem-solving skills and reflective habits
