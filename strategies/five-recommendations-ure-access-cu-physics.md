# Five departmental recommendations to improve access to UREs: FAQ page, fall symposium, spring poster session, sophomore Research Methods course, and national-lab partnerships

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Responding to survey-identified areas for improvement, the authors crafted five recommendations for the CU Physics Department: "(1) create a “Frequently-Asked-Questions (FAQ) About UREs” page on the department’s website, (2) host a symposium on access to UREs each fall, (3) host a poster session for UREs each spring", (4) develop a sophomore-level Research Methods course, and (5) explore partnerships with local national labs for off-campus UREs. The FAQ page and symposium use official centralized channels to raise awareness; the poster session and course raise awareness of research practices; the fall symposium targets first-year students to encourage early help-seeking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Departmental infrastructure and support for implementation; a Committee on Undergraduate Research was created to oversee implementation of the recommendations.
#### Constraints
- Recommendations should avoid changing the nature of the mentor-student relationship, including the number of students mentored by each faculty member.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics and engineering physics majors at large research universities

### Target Learning Goals
- awareness of research-related programs, practices, and pathways to UREs
- selection among on- and off-campus research opportunities
