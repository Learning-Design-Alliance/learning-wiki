# Use priming tools, examples of practice, and artifacts of practice to support video-based reflection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article identifies three types of materials and resources as essential for supporting video-based reflection. Priming tools are text-based materials supporting activation of prior knowledge; in combination with video they might be beneficial in light of a possible pre-training effect. Examples of practice are text-based or videotaped examples that "help bridge the gap between theory and application" and can automate identification of effective practices. Artifacts of practice are products of teaching that serve as a vehicle for reflection across RoA, RfA, and RiA tasks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials aligned with coaching objectives and the collective frameworks or noticing literature
- Guiding questions to support comparison of framework with practice
#### Constraints
- The article notes pre-training before multimedia learning is not always effective, and learners who already have knowledge on the subject seem less likely to benefit from pre-training

### Target Learners
- Early career teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Identifying effective teaching practices
- Noticing salient classroom moments
- Reasoning about cause and effect in teaching
