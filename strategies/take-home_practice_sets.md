# Take Home_Practice_Sets

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Take-home practice sets are curated collections of problems, exercises, or tasks assigned for completion outside of instructional sessions. They extend the [Practice](../elements/practice.md) cycle beyond limited class time, typically pairing items at varied difficulty with feedback mechanisms such as answer keys, worked solutions, or automated grading.

## Design Implications

Practice outside class multiplies opportunities for retrieval and skill consolidation, but only when items require active retrieval rather than passive review [Testing effect: retrieval practice produces durable learning.](../claims/retrieval-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S]. Sets should be sequenced so that items are spaced across sessions and mixed in type, since blocked, same-type repetition inflates short-term performance while undermining retention [Spacing and interleaving improve long-term retention relative to massed practice.](../claims/spaced-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S]. Because take-home work occurs without instructor support, early items should include scaffolds ([Worked Examples](../principles/worked-examples.md), hints) that fade across the set.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Items aligned to specific learning goals, not generic "chapter problems"
- A feedback path: worked solutions, answer keys, or automated feedback ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)) so errors are corrected before they consolidate
- Sufficient class-time modeling first — learners must know the target performance before practicing alone ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md))
- Realistic time estimates; sets that exceed available time get skimmed or copied

#### Constraints
- Without feedback, errors practiced at home consolidate and must later be unlearned [Feedback is necessary for practice to improve performance.](../claims/feedback-improves-learning.md) [-S]
- Unmonitored settings invite copying or answer-key lookup, producing an illusion of fluency; low-stakes accountability (in-class spot checks, warm-up quizzes using set items) mitigates this
- Excessive volume triggers fatigue and diminishing returns; quality and spacing beat quantity [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-M]
- Novices attempting items far beyond current competence engage in unproductive search; provide worked examples for the hardest items [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Interleaved sets**: mix item types within a set rather than blocking by topic [Comparing and contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- **Faded sets**: begin with worked examples, move to completion problems, end with independent items
- **Adaptive sets**: platforms such as [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org) or [ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org) select next items based on performance
- **Retrieval-oriented sets**: free-recall prompts and flashcard-style items rather than recognition or rereading

### Target Learners
- Learners with basic familiarity who can attempt items independently; pure novices need in-class scaffolding first [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]
- Learners preparing for cumulative or delayed assessment, where spacing pays off most [Spacing and interleaving improve long-term retention relative to massed practice.](../claims/spaced-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S]
- Less effective for learners who lack the self-regulation to initiate unsupervised work; pair with [Check-ins](../elements/check-in.md) or accountability structures

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency: automating skills through distributed repetition
- Retention and cumulative knowledge: durable memory for previously taught content
- Discrimination: interleaved sets build skill at *selecting* the right method, not just executing it

### Instructions
1. Model the target skill in class with [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) and [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) before assigning independent work.
2. Curate 8–15 items spanning difficulty levels, including at least one [Worked Examples](../principles/worked-examples.md) item and one challenge item.
3. Interleave item types and connect to earlier material so the set doubles as spaced review.
4. Attach worked solutions or an automated feedback channel ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)); require learners to check and correct before submission.
5. Open the next session with a brief in-class quiz or warm-up drawn from the set to verify genuine practice and add a retrieval event.

## Examples
- **[ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org)** — free online math problem sets with immediate automated feedback; randomized controlled trials in Maine middle schools showed improved achievement when homework included instant feedback.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — mastery-based practice sets with hints and spaced skill review across units.
- **Physics course homework redesign** — replacing blocked end-of-chapter problems with mixed sets drawn from all prior units, converting homework into distributed retrieval practice.
