# Use tiled game floors and grind rails for hands-on coordinate-system and kinematics activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
In Shaun White Skateboarding, students use a regular grid of floor tiles as a unit of length to build their own coordinate systems, measure object locations, and verify distance invariance under relabeling. Grind rails that evolve into three-dimensional paths let students assign coordinates to points along a path, note crossing times, and distinguish instantaneous from average speed. The authors note this can be done "without explicit reference to calculus."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A game with a regular spatial grid (tiles) and persistent, controllable three-dimensional paths, plus a controllable camera
#### Constraints
- Precise measurement is difficult; the authors suggest video tracking software on gameplay recordings as a possible remedy

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Cartesian coordinates, measurement units, distance invariance, paths, average and instantaneous velocity
