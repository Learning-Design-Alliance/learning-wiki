# Open with ink-drop diffusion and plasticine-mixing experiences to raise reversibility and homogeneity questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Before the model, the article proposes simple classroom experiences: ink drops diffusing in water glasses at different temperatures, and two students randomly exchanging plasticine between differently coloured balls about every minute. These raise the questions 'is the process reversible?' and 'why randomness produced homogeneity?', with students generating their own hypotheses. The plasticine exchange shows perfectly identical colours after about half an hour, shocking supporters of the hypothesis that colours never become identical.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements

#### Constraints

### Target Learning Goals
- recognising irreversibility of everyday processes
- understanding that randomness can produce homogeneity
