Train students to serve as discussion leaders within small online discussion groups¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends preparing students to lead their small-group online discussions so they become empowered and explicitly trained in critical thinking engagement and strategies. Each of the twelve groups in the study had a designated leader, and leaders in both conditions provided their groups with a brief summary of the discussion trend at the end of week one. The authors state, "They also recommend training students in being the discussion leaders within the groups."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A designated leader per small group who provides a summary of the discussion trend at the end of the first week

Constraints¶

  • The authors note there may be a difference between student-led and instructor-led discussion since students interact differently with their peers, and more research would be needed

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate pre-service teachers in online courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical thinking engagement and strategies
  • peer-led argumentation in online discussion

Claims¶

  • Treatment group discourse followed a seven-step developmental process while control group discourse followed only three steps and rarely settled on a position [+W]
  • Pre-service teachers given specific discussion instructions and instructor feedback produced better argumentation and interactivity than control groups in both weeks of online discussion [+W]
