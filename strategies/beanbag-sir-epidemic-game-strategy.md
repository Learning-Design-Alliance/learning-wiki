# Beanbag epidemic game in which students simulate disease spread with beans, then vary vaccination, recovery, and transmission rules

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Students in groups of two to four use cups of brown and white beans to simulate an epidemic: drawing two beans blind, replacing a brown bean with two white beans when colors differ, and recording events each time step. The article states "This simple game quickly demonstrates to students how to build a mathematical model for the spread of an infectious disease by understanding the basic epidemiology of the disease." Instructors then discuss the model's assumptions and introduce variations such as coin-flip transmission, vaccination with a third bean color, and recovery, before moving to computer simulations and the formal SIR equations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Beans of two colors of approximately the same shape, cups, and groups of two to four students; discussion of the model's underlying assumptions (e.g., no one recovers in the basic version)
#### Constraints
- The basic game models a disease from which no one recovers; variations are needed to represent vaccination, recovery, or reduced spread rate

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology students

### Target Learning Goals
- building and understanding mathematical models of infectious disease spread
- reasoning about model assumptions
