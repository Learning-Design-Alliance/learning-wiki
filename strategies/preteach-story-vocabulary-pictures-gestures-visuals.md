# Teach key story vocabulary through pictures and gestures before telling the story with visual support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy sequences vocabulary work before and during storytelling: learners first "learned the key vocabulary via pictures and gestures", and once the words were identified the teacher told the story while directing attention to a PowerPoint visual representation of it. The article reports this combination supported comprehension of the story and was followed by significant vocabulary gains and increased interest in the experimental group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stories prepared or adopted from the learners' course books, a PowerPoint presentation with visual representations, and a data-show projector in the classroom
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only with young upper-beginner EFL learners in Iranian language-center classes; the article does not claim storytelling is the only effective vocabulary strategy

### Target Learners
- Young EFL learners (upper-beginner)

### Target Learning Goals
- vocabulary knowledge
- story comprehension
- motivation for learning English
