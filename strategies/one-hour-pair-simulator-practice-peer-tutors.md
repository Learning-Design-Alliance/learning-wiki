# One-hour simulator practice in pairs supported by trained peer tutors, with pre-test deficits discussed

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The implementable teaching recipe the article used: after a baseline pre-test, "the students practiced inserting PVCs in groups of two on simulators for a total of 1 hour (training period of 30 minutes per student)" and the deficits noted in the pre-test were discussed. Training sessions were supported by peer-teachers, trained student tutors from higher semesters who had received about one hour of instruction and schooling on the checklist. Students were asked to imagine caring for a real patient and to comment each step of their actions, making self-reflection and active articulation part of the practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- simulators (IV-arms) and a simulation center setting
- peer tutors trained for about one hour and schooled on the checklist
- a checklist-based test serving as both assessment and guide for correct insertion
#### Constraints
- the intervention was one hour long and delivered in a simulation center; the article does not claim effectiveness beyond this setting without further controlled study

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate medical students without formal PVC training

### Target Learning Goals
- peripheral venous catheter insertion skill to a mastery standard
