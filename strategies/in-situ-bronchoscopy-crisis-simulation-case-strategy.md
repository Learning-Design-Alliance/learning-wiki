# In-situ simulation case with three consecutive crisis elements and GAS-model debriefing for bronchoscopy team training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a case-based in-situ simulation in which "The case included three consecutive elements that each needed to be completed to proceed in the case": sedation-requiring agitation, respiratory depression from added sedation, and severe endobronchial bleeding during biopsy. Training occurs in the real bronchoscopy suite with real equipment in normal places, a mannequin patient, real-time interventions, and a fifteen-minute debriefing based on the GAS model (Gathering, Analysis, Summarization).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants fulfil their professional roles in a standard team composition of one physician and two nurses
- Instructors judge whether the team reacted satisfactory before proceeding to the next element
- Debriefing follows the GAS model to discuss the case, performance, communication, and transferability
#### Constraints
- The mannequin could not exhibit coughing or agitation autonomously, so condition changes were verbally communicated by the instructor
- The bleeding management followed local standards; alternatives such as balloon catheters or double lumen intubation were not included in the scenario

### Target Learners
- bronchoscopy nurses
- respiratory physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- detecting and managing adverse events during bronchoscopy
- team communication
- clinical decision-making during crises
