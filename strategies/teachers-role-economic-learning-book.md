# Teacher's ten-part role in economic learning: prevent interfering associations, time effort and rest, and supply incentives at critical stages

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article closes with a summary of the teacher's specific functions in economic learning, derived from the typewriting studies. The teacher should know how the subject is naturally learned and the difficulties arising at each stage, since "By carefully directing the activities of his students in advance, he not only prevents the formation of many hindering associations but makes sure of the perfection of the elemental habits." The teacher should guide acquisition of right methods of study, ensure the natural economic method is used, prevent learners from pushing too fast at critical stages, regulate work and rest, provide incentives to effort and emotional helps at critical stages, urge learners on when conditions favor new adaptations, keep conditions favorable, and cultivate interest and pleasant feeling tone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must know the specific difficulties learners encounter, at what stages they occur, and the best ways of meeting them
- The teacher must understand the psychological importance of prompt and complete elimination of difficulties
#### Constraints
- The most economic periods of work and rest in different fields of learning had not yet been determined and required further investigation

### Target Learners
- Students acquiring complex skills in school or laboratory settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Economic, interference-minimizing acquisition of skill hierarchies
- Elimination of plateaus from learning curves
- Development of permanent interest and helpful feeling tone
