Use an active foreign-language control condition when testing auditory-cognitive training efficacy¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article addresses methodological shortcomings of prior auditory-cognitive training studies — lacking active control groups, non-naturalistic outcomes, and untrained transfer tasks — by assigning the control group to computer-based Duolingo foreign language training, chosen because prior work showed it to be "cognitively engaging yet orthogonal to the auditory and attentional mechanisms targeted" by the experimental training. Both groups trained at least 20 minutes a day, five days a week for four weeks. The article presents this design as offering stronger evidence for real-world applicability.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A control activity matched in time-on-task and engagement but orthogonal to the targeted mechanisms.

Constraints¶

  • Control-group training adherence was self-reported in paper-based learning diaries, which the authors list as a limitation.

Target Learners¶

  • older adults with hearing loss participating in auditory rehabilitation research

Target Learning Goals¶

  • valid estimation of auditory-cognitive training effects on speech-in-noise comprehension

Claims¶

  • Gamified personalized auditory-cognitive training improves naturalistic speech-in-noise comprehension in older adults with hearing loss relative to an active control group [+W]
  • Cognitive training gains are maintained or further enhanced at two-month follow-up [+W]
  • ACT does not change speech-in-noise intelligibility, subjective hearing, or listening effort, dissociating low-level fidelity from higher-order comprehension [+W]
  • On-training cognitive performance improves across all four trained domains after ACT, with large effect sizes [~W]
  • Baseline cognitive abilities do not significantly predict the size of ACT-related speech-in-noise improvement [+W]
  • Noise level moderates training-related SIN comprehension gains, with attenuated improvements under high- and low-noise conditions relative to no-noise trials [~W]
  • Off-training cognitive transfer shows gains in working memory and focused attention for the ACT group only, with no significant group-by-session interactions [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • SLA researchers should make future research more contextualised, theoretically broader, methodologically wider, and involve teachers
