# Implementation guidelines for PAR: weekly cycles, varied partners, completion grading, framing, and discussion of sample work and feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article offers practical guidelines for teachers implementing PAR. Students complete one PAR problem per week, turning in revised solutions a later day to avoid simply getting the problem done; partners should vary, e.g., by assigning random partners each week; completion points for self-reflection, peer feedback, and revision provide accountability. Framing is crucial because students may feel they lack expertise to give feedback; emphasizing communication helps. Weekly discussions of sample student work train feedback quality, and class time discussing the feedback students actually give signals that the teacher values it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students turn in revised solutions at a later date to avoid turning the work in at the end of the same class period
- Framing is crucial: emphasize the importance of communication and the skills PAR aims to develop
#### Constraints
- Students can resist assessing peers' work because they feel they do not have the expertise to provide feedback

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in mathematics and the sciences
- physics teachers implementing PAR

### Target Learning Goals
- giving helpful peer feedback
- communication, collaboration, and persistence
