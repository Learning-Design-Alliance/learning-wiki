# Use quantile regression to examine whether an intervention has uniform or differential effects across the achievement distribution

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates quantile regression as an estimation strategy for detecting differential intervention effects across the achievement distribution. As the authors state, "An appropriate estimation procedure to investigate empirically whether interim assessments have uniform or differential effects across the achievement distribution is quantile regression." The method produces estimates at the middle and tails of the distribution, providing a more complete picture of treatment effects than mean-based analysis alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standard errors of the regression estimates should be corrected for potential clustering effects
#### Constraints
- Quantiles are like percentiles and can be interpreted as such; the method was applied to student achievement data nested within schools

### Target Learners
- students across the achievement distribution, including low-achievers

### Target Learning Goals
- estimating differential intervention effects across the achievement distribution
