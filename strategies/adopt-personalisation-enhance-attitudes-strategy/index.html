Senior secondary mathematics teachers should adopt personalisation of instruction to enhance students' attitudes toward word problems¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teachers personalise word-problem instruction by incorporating students' familiar contexts and preferences into problem content, because the study found personalised students showed more favourable attitudes on all seven dimensions, including lower anxiety. As the article states, "it is suggested that the senior secondary school mathematics teach ers adopt personalisation of instruction as a strategy to enhance students’ attitudes toward mathematics word problems and by implication improve students’ academic performance in the subject". The mechanism offered is that a meaningful, interest-based problem context places students at the centre and may catalyse motivation and comprehension.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need students' interest and preference data (e.g., via an interest inventory) to build familiar contexts

Constraints¶

  • The article notes time constraints in preparing individualised materials could reduce classroom use, and that personalised contexts risk sex-stereotyping

Target Learners¶

  • senior secondary school mathematics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • positive attitudes toward mathematics word problems
  • reduced anxiety in word problem solving

Affordances¶

  • Personalisation Of Instruction Framework

Claims¶

  • Personalised print-based instruction produces statistically significant multivariate differences in attitudes toward mathematics word problems compared with non-personalised instruction [+M]
  • Personalised instruction students differed significantly from non-personalised students on every individual attitude subscale, including lower anxiety and higher perceived male domain [+M]
  • Achievement in mathematics word problems correlates positively with five attitude dimensions and negatively with anxiety [+W]
