# Make student oral presentations of laboratory results the central activity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Each laboratory group prepares a brief oral talk sharing what it learned with all other students; students choose the form and means (speaker delegation, blackboard, slides, showing devices), followed by a brief discussion with the public. The authors report that although students fear public speaking, most respond to the challenge, and that over the years 'communication is became the central activity in which students are involved and it encourages and increases active and collaborative behavior in the school'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough laboratory groups that results must be shared rather than all experiments performed by everyone
- Teacher support available for preparing the communication
#### Constraints
- Originally introduced as a necessity because there was not enough time and space for all groups to perform all experiments

### Target Learners
- high-school students in inquiry-based laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- communicating physics results
- active and collaborative behavior
