# Use blended learning for hands-on fields such as sports, medicine, and nursing where fully distance education is not viable

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review recommends blended learning for fields whose curricula require hands-on skills, where the possibility of distance education is very low. Blended learning is said to "reduce the presence of students in universities and promote the hands-on skills and experiences of students". It cites Al-Balas et al., whose Jordanian medical students found e-learning unfulfilling and considered blended learning more suitable for medical education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Fields must have hands-on or experimental skill components that fully online delivery cannot support.
#### Constraints
- The review presents this as a recommendation during the health emergency, not a tested intervention; it cites Jordanian medical students' perceptions as supporting evidence.

### Target Learners
- University students in sports, medicine, and nursing programs in developing countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Hands-on clinical and practical skills development
