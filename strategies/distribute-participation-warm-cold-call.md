# Consistently distribute whole-class participation opportunities throughout a course sequence, e.g., via warm or cold calling and even group time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because outspokenness is the most prominent driver of peer recognition and its association with peer interactions diminishes over time, the authors recommend instructors "consistently offer opportunities for a diverse set of students to participate in whole-class discussions". Specifically, instructors "may use \"warm\" or cold call when choosing students to present ideas" so outspokenness is spread across many students, and may allot roughly even time with each group during small-group activities so all students can ask questions in front of groupmates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must teach in settings with whole-class discussions and small-group activities where participation is visible to peers.
#### Constraints
- The article states these practices are likely more important than diversifying peer interaction networks due to the diminishing association between peer interactions and recognition over time; recommendations rest on a small single-institution study.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory physics course sequences

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable peer recognition as a science student
- science identity development and persistence
