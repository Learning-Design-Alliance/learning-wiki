# In situ inter-professional simulation training programme with scenarios adapted from MMC cases

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The active feedback intervention develops an in situ simulation training programme whose scenarios are adapted from cases analysed in Morbidity and Mortality conferences. The in situ simulation training "will be conducted in the ward during working hours in order to make the experience as close as possible to reality". Each session has specific objectives promoting technical and nontechnical skills (stress management, situational awareness, teamwork, and briefing strategies), uses a standardised briefing and debriefing limited to a maximum of 5 key messages, is filmed and broadcast live to ward staff, and aims to involve at least a quarter of the department's staff as participants or observers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios written by the principal investigator according to pedagogical objectives agreed by a pedagogical team; a trainer from the local simulation centre runs each inter-professional session
#### Constraints
- The minimum number of sessions was set at 6 over 2 years, justified by technical and logistical constraints of simulating in the clinical environment; the article states it seems illusory to reach all or even half of a ward's staff

### Target Learners
- inter-professional ward teams (medical and paramedical) in at-risk specialties

### Target Learning Goals
- technical and nontechnical skills including stress management, situational awareness, teamwork, briefing strategies, and communication skills
