# Design multimodal biometric sensing deployments to be minimally intrusive and validated against educational outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (1 review, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's guidance for future multimodal sensing work is to prioritize minimally intrusive approaches: remote eye-tracking for screen-based tasks, wrist-worn sensors for physiological data, and optional EEG only when research questions justify complexity. Validation must link sensor-derived metrics to meaningful educational outcomes through careful triangulation with performance data and teacher observations. The review frames this as the condition under which multimodal sensing can fulfill its potential for understanding and supporting elementary STEM learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stringent consent procedures, privacy protections, and careful avoidance of stigmatizing inferences for biometric data from minors
- Triangulation of sensor-derived metrics with performance data and teacher observations
#### Constraints
- Systematic deployments in elementary classrooms remain limited, revealing a critical gap between laboratory validation and educational implementation; device comfort, classroom movement artifacts, and workflow integration constrain feasibility

### Target Learners
- Elementary students ages 5-12 participating in STEM activities

### Target Learning Goals
- Measurement of attention, cognitive load, and engagement during STEM learning activities
