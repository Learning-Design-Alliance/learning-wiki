# Working Memory Support Strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Working memory support strategies are instructional design techniques that manage the severe capacity limits of working memory — roughly three to five novel elements for most adults [Cowan's embedded-processes model.](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922) [+S]. They work through three mechanisms: reducing intrinsic load (segmenting, [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md), pretraining), minimizing extraneous load (removing redundancy, integrating sources, clear structure), and externalizing content (graphics, organizers, worked examples) so learners need not hold intermediate states in mind. The approach is grounded in [Cognitive Load Theory](../theories/cognitive-load-theory.md) [+S].

## Design Implications

Every instructional decision should be evaluated against the question: what must the learner hold in mind right now? Material that exceeds working-memory capacity produces failure to encode regardless of motivation or quality of explanation [Cognitive load theory: instructional consequences of limited working memory.](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4) [+S]. Supports should be faded as schemas form and load is freed [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Task analysis identifying which elements are essential (intrinsic) and which are design artifacts (extraneous)
- Segmentation of complex material into learner-paced units
- Integration of text and graphics rather than split sources ([Dual Coding Theory](../theories/dual-coding-theory.md)) [+M]
- A plan for fading support as expertise develops

#### Constraints
- Support becomes harmful for knowledgeable learners: redundant guidance imposes extra load on those who can self-manage, the [expertise reversal effect](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [-S]
- Over-segmenting can fragment a coherent schema; learners may process pieces without integrating them [~M]
- Offloading content to graphics backfires when the graphic is decorative rather than relevant [Decorative illustrations do not improve learning.](../claims/decorative-illustrations-do-not-improve-learning.md) [-M]
- Learners with very low prior knowledge may not benefit from learner-controlled pacing, since they cannot judge when they have understood a segment [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Segmenting and pacing**: break animation or narration into learner-controlled segments [Clark & Mayer's segmenting principle.](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119239086) [+S]
- **Pretraining**: teach names and characteristics of key concepts before the main task
- **Worked examples**: [Example-problem sequences reduce cognitive load.](../claims/example-problem-sequences-reduce-cognitive-load.md) [+S]
- **External representations**: [Graphic organizers support novice comprehension.](../claims/graphic-organizers-support-novice-comprehension.md) [+M]; note that learner-constructed organizers can outperform provided ones once learners are capable [Learner-constructed graphic organizers outperform provided.](../claims/learner-constructed-graphic-organizers-outperform-provided.md) [~M]
- **Language simplification**: [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md) reduces decoding load for language learners

### Target Learners
- Novices, who lack schemas to chunk incoming information and therefore face the highest element interactivity [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]
- Learners with working-memory limitations, including many learners with ADHD or specific learning disabilities; see [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md)
- Less beneficial — potentially harmful — for experts, per the [expertise reversal effect](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [-S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Complex procedural and conceptual learning with high element interactivity
- Schema construction: consolidating elements into single retrievable units
- Foundational knowledge that later learning will build on

### Instructions
1. Analyze the task to estimate element interactivity; identify what is essential vs. design-induced load.
2. Segment the material into coherent, learner-paced units ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md)).
3. Remove extraneous load: eliminate redundancy, integrate text with graphics, apply [Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md).
4. Provide external supports — [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md), worked examples, [Analogies](analogies.md) — to hold structure the learner cannot yet.
5. Fade supports as performance improves, shifting from examples to problems to independent work [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

## Related Strategies
- [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) — the core unit-of-analysis move; grouping elements so they occupy one slot rather than several

- [Pretraining](pretraining.md) — front-loading vocabulary and concepts to lower intrinsic load of the main task

## Examples
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — short, segmented videos with learner-controlled pacing and immediate practice, embodying segmenting and example-problem sequencing.
- **[Worked Examples](../principles/worked-examples.md) in algebra instruction** — Sweller & Cooper's original studies replaced early problem solving with studied solutions, improving novice performance [doi:10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3).
- **Mayer's multimedia principles** — narrated animation with integrated labels rather than on-screen text plus narration, reducing split attention [doi:10.1002/9781119239086](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119239086).
