# Facilitate heterogeneous within-class grouping to avoid profuse upward or downward comparisons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that high-performing students in high-performing classes report lower life satisfaction, the authors recommend that schools and teachers arrange within-class learning groups that mix students of different performance levels rather than grouping strictly by similar performance. The article states teachers should facilitate "mixing students with different performance-levels in heterogeneous learning groups within classes", so that high performers can offer help and support to poorer performing peers while noticing other high performers in their group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers able to compose and moderate mixed-performance learning groups within classes
#### Constraints
- The article notes class composition heterogeneity means not all low-performing students can simply be placed in high-performing classes; the underlying evidence is non-experimental and cross-sectional

### Target Learners
- seventh-grade secondary school students of mixed performance levels

### Target Learning Goals
- life satisfaction and well-being
- peer interaction and support across performance levels

### Affordances
- Bflpe Hypothesis Social Comparison Classroom
