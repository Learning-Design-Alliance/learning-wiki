# Administer CQIPP pairs back to back with the quantitative question preceding the conceptual question, without telling students the questions are isomorphic

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete administration recipe used in the study: five multiple-choice CQIPPs in mechanics with different contexts, given after instruction and related homework, with the quantitative question preceding its conceptual partner and about 2.5 minutes per question on average. Students were free to move between the two questions and could revise earlier answers, but were not told the questions were isomorphic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions must be isomorphic, requiring the same physics principle, and asked after instruction on the relevant concepts
#### Constraints
- The author notes the order and proximity of the paired questions may be major factors in whether students recognize the similarity, and future research should explore spacing and changing the order

### Target Learners
- college calculus-based introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing isomorphism between conceptual and quantitative problems
- leveraging quantitative solutions for conceptual inference

## Claims

- [Students who answer both questions of a conceptual and quantitative isomorphic problem pair back to back often perform better on the conceptual question than students given the conceptual question alone](../claims/conceptual-questions-better-when-paired-with-quantitative.md) [+M]
- [Introductory students answered a conceptual momentum question at roughly half the correct rate of an isomorphic quantitative problem, but performed equally on both when the quantitative question came first](../claims/conceptual-quantitative-momentum-gap-introductory.md) [+W]
- [Pairing a conceptual question with a quantitative question did not significantly improve student performance on the quantitative question relative to answering it alone](../claims/quantitative-performance-not-improved-by-pairing.md) [~M]
- [For the rolling-hoop problem pair, no significant difference between paired and single presentation, attributed to failure to use conservation of energy correctly](../claims/hoop-pair-no-difference-energy-errors.md) [~W]
