Design large, contiguous, heterogeneous attendance zones with equivalent programs and limited transport time¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The guide specifies zone design rules: "Zones should be large enough to encompass heterogeneous population groups but small enough to limit student transportation time toand from school within the zone to not more than thirty, thirty-five, or forty minutes." Zones should aggregate contiguous neighborhoods of different population groups to avoid the appearance of gerrymandering, encompass equivalent educational programs, and reflect districtwide racial composition, with a minority critical mass of at least one-fifth.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Zones built from contiguous neighborhoods; equivalent range of programs and services across zones

Constraints¶

  • Modest variation from districtwide racial composition is permitted, but the minority population may not fall below one-fifth of the zonewide student body

Target Learners¶

  • all students in a district using zone-based controlled choice

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • proportional access to all schools and programs

Claims¶

  • Neighborhood-based desegregation plans are short-term and eventually defeated by shifting residential patterns [-W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Districts supporting neighborhood schools should plan for continually shifting student populations and mobility-driven capacity demands

Related Principles¶

  • Use choice data as a referendum to identify and improve least-chosen schools rather than magnetizing only the best
  • Implement desegregation, choice, and school improvement simultaneously rather than incrementally
