# Theatre improvisation method for communication training in circular steps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A communication training activity in which GPs practise listening, asking questions that facilitate dialogue, and helping patients formulate problems, needs and resources "using the method of theatre improvisation". It "allows going back and forth between the following circular steps: improvising a scene, reflecting together on what happened, playing the scene anew incorporating the feed-back and sharing reflections in plenum". Two professional actors initiate scenes and interact moment-to-moment with the audience, after which GPs take the roles of patient or GP with audience feedback moderated by a facilitator.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two professional actors to initiate and interact in the scenes
- A facilitator to moderate audience feedback
#### Constraints
- Participants in the pilot commented that more time should be spent on this method and less on theoretical knowledge

### Target Learners
- Practising general practitioners
- Residents in training to become GPs

### Target Learning Goals
- Verbal and non-verbal existential communication skills
- Communicative confidence with patients about existential and religious/spiritual issues
