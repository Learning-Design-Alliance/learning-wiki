# Use a planetarium set to historical sky conditions to let students learn by experience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
In response to student disappointment at not using the planetarium, the team developed a worksheet-based activity incorporating Sydney Aboriginal astronomy and the interactions between Lieutenant William Dawes and Patyegarang. Students learn Aboriginal names of astronomical objects, then enter the planetarium, which is set to 1788 conditions so the Milky Way is seen clearly as Dawes and Patyegarang would have seen it, giving students an opportunity to learn by experience, with visual cues including images of animals, plants, and ancestors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A planetarium dome with digitally projected night sky and the ability to set historical sky conditions
- Worksheet incorporating local Aboriginal astronomy content
#### Constraints
- The activity relies on historical records: Dawes's journals contain local Dharug names of the Sun, Moon, Milky Way, and Magellanic Clouds

### Target Learners
- Primary school students, Year 2 to Year 4

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning Dharug names of astronomical objects
- Experiencing the pre-light-pollution night sky of 1788 Sydney
