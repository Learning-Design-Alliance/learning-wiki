# Design resident training modules explicitly targeting the teaching strategies students identified, and integrate resident teaching behaviors into core competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the implications section the authors recommend that residency programs "design training modules explicitly targeting these teaching strategies" and that medical schools and residency programs consider integrating these specific teaching behaviors into core competencies for resident educators. The identified strategies include case debriefings, baseline knowledge assessment, in-depth explanation, psychological safety practices, and inclusive team integration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Needs assessment to identify which teaching strategies matter locally; structured educational intervention such as the six-module workshop
#### Constraints
- The article recommends objective measures and formal program evaluation frameworks to guide future implementation in different contexts

### Target Learners
- Residents teaching medical students during clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective clinical teaching behaviors, active learning, and student engagement

## Claims

- [The percentage of students identifying effective resident teaching behaviors rose from 63% (49 of 77) pre-intervention to 96% (53 of 55) post-intervention, with themes of teacher characteristics and teaching strategies across cognitive, affective, and psychomotor domains](../claims/effective-teaching-behaviors-identification-rose-63-to-96.md) [+W]
- [Post-intervention, residents more often used scaffolding for procedures, demonstrating a technique first and then allowing supervised student attempts with real-time feedback](../claims/post-intervention-scaffolding-hands-on-supervised-practice.md) [+W]
- [After a residents-as-teachers workshop, medical students rated the overall quality of anesthesiology resident teaching significantly higher (2.98 ± 0.53 pre vs. 3.40 ± 0.46 post, p < 0.001)](../claims/rat-workshop-improves-perceived-teaching-quality-anesthesiology.md) [+W]
- [Resident knowledge of key clinical teaching concepts, including Kolb's Experiential Learning theory and Miller's Pyramid, improved significantly after the workshop](../claims/resident-teaching-concept-knowledge-improved-post-workshop.md) [+W]
- [Students' self-perceived knowledge acquisition, technical skills development, career interest in anesthesia, and overall rotation satisfaction all rose from pre-test to post-test in one uncontrolled pre-post study (p < 0.001 for all categories)](../claims/student-learning-outcomes-career-interest-satisfaction-improved.md) [+W]
- [Eight of nine supervision domains rose significantly from pre-test to post-test, while 'residents were present during critical moments' did not change (p = 0.161), in one single-group pre-post study](../claims/supervision-domains-improved-except-presence-critical-moments.md) [+W]
