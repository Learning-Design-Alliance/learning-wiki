# Incorporate varied exercise modalities, including resistance exercise, into school settings to support academic performance and health

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools increase the prescription of varied exercise modalities, specifically adding resistance exercise alongside traditional aerobic activity. The authors state such results "may support an increase in the prescription of varied exercise modalities within school settings for the purposes of improving academic performance and student health," and that findings "should be used to encourage greater amounts and varieties of physical activity in the modern school system." The tested protocol was 20 min of resistance exercise (six exercises, two sets of 15 repetitions) completed within a school schedule, with testing 5–20 min afterward to mimic authentic classroom use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Resistance exercise for adolescents should be supervised, which the article notes is established as safe under supervision.
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single study with 63 students, predominantly boys (57 boys, 6 girls), and without a precise measure of exercise intensity.

### Target Learners
- middle school students (eighth grade)

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement in mathematics
- executive function and cognition
- physical health
