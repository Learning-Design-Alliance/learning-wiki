# Build CPC programmes around blended, practically oriented activities: practical training scenarios, cardiac re-certification, e-learning followed by related practice, and simulation manikin training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that CPC for Irish paramedics and APs emphasise "a compulsory ‘mixed’ model approach of CPC which includes evidence of patient contact and CPC activities that are practically orientated". Blended learning involving evidence of patient contact, team-based learning and practical skills is the preferred CPC activity format, while e-learning alone, project work and journal reviews attract least interest.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- CPC activities should include evidence of patient contact and practical, hands-on components rather than passive individual learning
#### Constraints
- E-learning modules without related practice were rated not relevant by 36% of respondents, so e-learning should be paired with practice

### Target Learners
- registered paramedics and advanced paramedics in pre-hospital emergency care

### Target Learning Goals
- maintenance of clinical competence including practical skills and patient care
