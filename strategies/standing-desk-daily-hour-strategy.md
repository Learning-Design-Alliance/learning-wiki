# Use standing desks for at least 1 hour per school day with teacher reminders and direct-feedback self-monitoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends supplying classrooms with standing desks (with unstable floor mats) and asking teachers to encourage pupils to work about 60 min per day at them, children managing their own standing time. The authors "would recommend using a direct feedback method for the purpose of self-monitoring to initiate readjustments of physical activity and higher self-motivation." In the pilot this achieved 60.5 daily standing minutes, about 39% of available desk time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standing desks with height adjustment and non-slip floor mats must be available in the classroom
- Teachers must remind children frequently to use the desks
#### Constraints
- Standing desks involve high financial and organizational challenges
- Long-term maintenance of behavior likely requires intrinsic motivation rather than external prompts

### Target Learners
- primary school children aged 10–12 years

### Target Learning Goals
- increase daily standing time and reduce classroom sitting
- support cognitive function (working memory, inhibitory control)
