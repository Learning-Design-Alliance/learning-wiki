# Promote students' emotional vocabulary and emotion understanding (e.g., RULER-style interventions) to support achievement in language arts and humanities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend theory-driven interventions that develop students' abilities to label and understand emotions, citing RULER as an example. They suggest that "interventions designed to promote students’ emotional vocabulary and understanding of “feeling words” might be an effective means to improve their academic performance" in areas requiring emotional language, i.e., the language arts and humanities. They also recommend interventions teaching students to handle school situations in an emotionally intelligent rather than impulsive manner.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be theory-driven and target students' abilities to label and understand emotions
#### Constraints
- The incremental-prediction evidence concerns strategic EI, particularly EU; EM did not contribute to GPA in this adolescent sample, though the authors do not take this as undermining the branch's importance

### Target Learners
- Adolescents in secondary education

### Target Learning Goals
- Emotion understanding and emotional vocabulary
- Academic achievement in language arts and humanities
