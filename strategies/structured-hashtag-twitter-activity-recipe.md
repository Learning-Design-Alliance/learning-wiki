# Structured weekly Twitter activity recipe: hashtagged questions, tweet limits, peer commenting and question quotas with a participation incentive

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy organises Twitter use in a course around one question per laboratory session posted under a course hashtag (#websci), with students answering within 24 hours under three rules: respond "with no more than two tweets", "comment to the replies of at least two fellow students each week", and "submit at least two questions related to the course during the semester". Participation was optional, supported by a small grade incentive for the most active students. The article used this design to structure four weeks of activities whose participation levels were then analysed against grades and engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A course hashtag and weekly questions tied to laboratory sessions, with a 24-hour answering window
#### Constraints
- Participation was optional, and the incentive was one additional grade point for the highest-activity student and half a point for the next two

### Target Learners
- university students in an elective course

### Target Learning Goals
- course content discussion and peer interaction in laboratory sessions
- academic engagement and sense of belonging to the academic community
