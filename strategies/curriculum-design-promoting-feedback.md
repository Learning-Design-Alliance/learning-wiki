# Design the curriculum to position feedback as a key attribute, orienting students to feedback and building in practice giving it

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "The curriculum should be deliberately designed to inspire students to engage in feedback", with interventions that permeate the curriculum and organisational culture. Its tips include orientating students to the purpose and methods of feedback, promoting multiple tasks with formative assessment and feedback, developing incremental challenges for tasks, and providing opportunities for students to not only receive, but practice giving feedback. Comparisons of performance should be encouraged early in the curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback should be viewed as a required element of any curriculum, and central to student learning
- Systematically building self-analysis as an expectation within the curriculum
#### Constraints
- Interventions need to permeate the curriculum and the culture of organisations to ensure learners can identify appropriate standards

### Target Learners
- health professional students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-regulated learning
- making judgements about their own work
- planning their own learning needs
