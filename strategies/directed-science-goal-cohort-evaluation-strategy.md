# Design projects with a directed science goal over a defined parameter space, work as a cohort, and engage an external evaluator to measure program impact

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's recommendations for project design and program evaluation: design a project with a relatively directed science goal so novice researchers have defined parameter space with room for agency; have students work together as a cohort, since learning through teaching promotes ownership of learning and makes topics accessible; and engage an external evaluator to design a robust evaluation, which could include validated surveys and focus groups. The authors recommend, in the future, robust measurement of impact on students' science identities, ownership of learning, self efficacy and sense of belonging.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A project that, once students mastered the analysis technique, could be deployed on a large source list
- Periodic informal check-ins with students plus an external evaluator collecting feedback
#### Constraints
- The authors state they did not yet conduct robust impact measurement themselves; the robust evaluation is a recommendation for the future

### Target Learners
- novice undergraduate researchers at community colleges

### Target Learning Goals
- science identity, self efficacy, sense of belonging, ownership of learning
