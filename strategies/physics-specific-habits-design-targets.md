# Furnish the model with physics-specific habits of mind and practice as concrete design targets for a preparation program

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
To make the generic model usable, the article furnishes it with physics-specific habits that program designers should target. Physics habits of mind include seeing physics everywhere, approaching problem solving as a physicist, treating physics as a process rather than a set of rules, and using mathematics in a physics-specific way. Physics teacher habits of mind and practice include starting by connecting new ideas to students' existing ideas, attending to student thinking about physical mechanism, encouraging students to test ideas experimentally instead of waiting for validation from authority, listening to student conversations and improvising instruction, and treating all students as capable of learning physics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- None of these habits can be developed without deep connections to physics as an experimental science and to physics education research, which provides PSTs with tools that help build these habits
#### Constraints
- The authors note some listed habits are not exclusively physics-specific but are crucial for a physics teacher, and generic habits such as careful lesson planning are assumed but not the focus

### Target Learners
- pre-service physics teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- spontaneous physics-expert reasoning and responsive in-class teaching decisions

### Affordances
- Teacher Habits Taxonomy Mind Practice Maintenance
