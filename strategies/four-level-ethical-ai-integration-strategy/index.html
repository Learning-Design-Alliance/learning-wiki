Build ethical AI integration on four coordinated levels: faculty professional development, ethics curricula, clear institutional guidelines, and interdisciplinary updating¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The study's concluding recommendation is that ethical AI use in higher education requires action at several levels simultaneously: "professional development support should be provided to faculty members, courses covering ethical dimensions should be added to curricula, clear ethical usage guidelines should be established at universities, and these guidelines should be updated through interdisciplinary collaboration." This responds to the study's findings that faculty lack institutional guidelines and support, students receive inconsistent guidance, and both groups rely on individual effort. The strategy treats ethical AI integration as an institutional design task rather than an individual one.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional commitment to establish and maintain ethical usage guidelines, since participants reported that institutions and policies are not ready.

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation arises from a study situated in one public university context and based on participants' reported views.

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students
  • faculty members

Target Learning Goals¶

  • ethical and responsible use of AI in learning and teaching
  • ethical awareness and ethical literacy in higher education

Affordances¶

  • Six Theme Ethical Structure Ai Higher Education

Related Strategies¶

  • Ground AI integration in higher education on core principles of human agency, academic freedom, transparency, ethics, inclusivity, and critical thinking
  • Foster communities of practice for AI governance instead of rigid top-down policies
  • Higher education leaders should develop more inclusive and future-oriented GenAI policies integrating social equity, interdisciplinary experimentation, and sustainability considerations
  • Prepare teachers to use AI expeditiously and provide AI-text detectors for evaluation, alongside updated academic-integrity rules
  • Embed GenAI literacy and ethical use directly in nursing curricula rather than prohibiting it
  • Institutions should respond to GenAI with clear governance frameworks and structured professional training programs for staff.
  • Foster interdisciplinary collaboration and actively include student perspectives in institutional GenAI responses.
  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
