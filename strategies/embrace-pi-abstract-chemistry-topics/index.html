Strategies

Chemistry teachers should embrace programmed instruction alongside conventional approaches when teaching abstract topics¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

On the basis of the study's findings, the authors recommend that teachers of Chemistry embrace programmed instruction, especially in all abstract topics they teach, so as to solve the attitude crisis currently plaguing Chemistry education in Kenya. The recommendation rests on the finding that incorporating PI with conventional methods improved form two students' attitudes towards SAPT, attributed to PI's student-centeredness: content is sub-divided into small parts in sequential order and students advance only after answering correctly, allowing them to progress at their own rate.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Schools need computer laboratories and sufficient computers, since the PI software in this study was delivered on CD-ROM during normal Chemistry lessons

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from one quasi-experimental study of the SAPT topic in four mixed secondary schools in Butere district, Kenya

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school Chemistry students, particularly those studying abstract topics

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving students' attitudes towards abstract Chemistry topics and Chemistry as a subject

Claims¶

  • Descriptive gains show the experimental group's attitude change exceeded the control group's, and pretesting had no effect on the intervention [+W]
  • Students taught Structure of the Atom and the Periodic Table with Programmed Instruction obtained significantly higher attitude mean scores than control groups taught conventionally [+W]
  • The null hypothesis of no attitude difference between PI-taught and conventionally taught students was rejected [+W]
  • Before intervention, attitude scores of the pretested experimental and control groups were statistically the same [~W]
