# Shadowing for Behavior Adjustment

## Description
Shadowing is a strategy used to address harmful behaviors in children, such as biting or hitting. An adult closely follows the child at all times, maintaining an arm's reach distance, to prevent the behavior before it occurs. The goal is to provide constant supervision without drawing special attention to the child or making them feel punished. This approach is typically used after a couple of incidents and continues until the child is incident-free for two weeks, aiming to break the cycle of negative behavior and protect classmates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated adult to shadow the child, clear understanding of the behavior goals, and consistency in implementation. The adult must maintain close proximity and be ready to intervene immediately.
#### Constraints
- Requires significant adult resources and attention. The shadowed child must not feel singled out or punished. Success depends on consistent application and the child's ability to learn new strategies.
