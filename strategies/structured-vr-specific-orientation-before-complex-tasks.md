# Provide structured, VR-specific orientation and training sessions before learners engage in complex clinical tasks in VR

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because certain actions are not replicated one-to-one from real-life procedures, participants must allocate cognitive load to learning the VR mechanics. The article underscores "the importance of structured, VR-specific training sessions before learners engage in complex clinical tasks, and considering these extraneous loads when targeting specific learning objectives." In the study, each dyad received a 5-minute orientation to navigation and the functionality of the virtual patient and equipment from a trained VR simulation facilitator.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trained VR simulation facilitator to deliver the orientation and remain available to address questions and provide feedback
#### Constraints
- Interface-based burden can divert attention from core clinical objectives even with orientation; the study used a single scenario with a 5-minute orientation

### Target Learners
- interprofessional clinician dyads (nurse and physician) new to VR simulation

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing, describing, and beginning to treat a patient with developing shock
