# Use an experienced peer family-carer facilitator with structured group review sessions to support self-learning programs for carers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends replacing professional therapists with a trained experienced family carer who facilitates group review sessions around a self-help manual. The authors state that "The presence/use of an experienced peer facilitator in psychosocial interventions, especially in self-learning or community mental health care programs, not only better engaged/empowered participants in group sessions but also overcame the weakness of employing a family therapist who would require extensive training and subsequent resources." The facilitator reviews module progress with standardised questions, shares personal caring experiences and problem-solving strategies, and addresses cultural considerations such as stigma.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator experienced in psychosis care, trained in three full-day sessions by an experienced psychiatric nurse and the researchers
- A structured self-help manual whose content the review sessions follow
- Fidelity monitoring of sessions (the trial audio-recorded sessions and rated adherence around 92%)
#### Constraints
- The article notes there was no opportunity for families to clarify module content outside the group sessions, so individualised needs might not have been fully addressed

### Target Learners
- Adult family carers of people with early psychosis in community mental health settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Independent self-learning of illness management and problem-solving
- Peer empowerment and motivation through shared caregiving experience
