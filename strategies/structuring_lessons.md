# Structuring Lessons

## Description
A lesson structure maps the teaching and learning that occurs in class. Sound lesson structures reinforce routines, scaffold learning via specific steps and activities, optimize time on task and classroom climate through smooth transitions, and stimulate and maintain engagement by linking lesson and unit learning through planned sequencing of teaching and learning activities. Structure operates at two levels: the macro-sequence of activities within a lesson (opening, development, consolidation) and the micro-decisions about how each activity connects to the next.

## Design Implications

Structured lessons reduce extraneous cognitive load by making the organization of content explicit, freeing working memory for the material itself [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Predictable routines and clear activity sequences also support learner attention and on-task behavior, particularly when each phase of the lesson builds on the previous one rather than treating activities as isolated events [Teaching effectiveness research links structured, coherent instruction to achievement.](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543073003453) [+M]. The strongest structures begin with review and activation of prior knowledge, present new material in small steps with practice after each step, and end with independent application and consolidation [Rosenshine's principles of instruction.](https://www.aft.org/sites/default/files/periodicals/Rosenshine.pdf) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A planned sequence of activities with explicit transitions between phases
- An opening that activates prior knowledge and states lesson goals ([Activation](../elements/activation.md), [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
- New content presented in manageable segments with [Practice](../elements/practice.md) distributed between segments rather than batched at the end
- Guided practice with [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) before independent work
- Closure that consolidates the lesson's key ideas and connects them to the unit

#### Constraints
- Overly rigid structures may limit flexibility and student autonomy; highly scripted pacing can prevent teachers from responding to learner confusion in the moment
- Over-structuring for learners with strong prior knowledge can produce the expertise-reversal effect, where support that helps novices actively harms more expert learners [Expertise reverses the benefit of instructional support.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Structure without cognitive demand — well-organized but passive lessons — does not improve achievement; engagement must be cognitive, not merely behavioral [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]
- Rigid time-boxed structures can force transitions before understanding is reached, particularly in [Mastery Learning](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md) contexts where pacing should follow competence, not the clock

#### Implementation Variability
- **Direct instruction models** (e.g., [Explicit Teaching](../patterns/explicit-teaching.md), [Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md)) use teacher-led, tightly sequenced structures: review, model, guided practice, independent practice
- **Inquiry-oriented structures** (e.g., [Case-Based Learning](../patterns/case-based-learning.md)) open with a problem or case, then structure the lesson around investigation and debriefing rather than presentation-first sequencing
- **Flipped structures** ([Flipped Classroom](../patterns/flipped-classroom.md)) move first exposure outside class so in-class structure can emphasize application and feedback
- **Event-based templates** such as [Gagné's 9 Events of Instruction](../patterns/gagnés-9-events-of-instruction.md) provide a generic nine-phase skeleton adaptable to any content

### Target Learners
- Novices benefit most from explicit sequencing and small-step presentation, which reduce unguided search and load [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Younger learners and learners with attention or executive-function challenges depend on predictable routines and clear transitions
- Learners with substantial prior knowledge may need less internal structure and more open, problem-centered sequencing [Expertise reverses the benefit of instructional support.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural and skill acquisition: stepwise sequencing with practice after each step
- Conceptual understanding: sequencing that moves from simple to complex and connects new ideas to prior knowledge
- Self-regulation: predictable structures model planning and monitoring routines learners can internalize [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Open with review and goals.** Begin with a short review of prerequisite knowledge and state what the lesson will accomplish ([Activation](../elements/activation.md), [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md)).
2. **Present new material in small steps.** Segment content into chunks sized to working memory limits ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md), [Cognitive Load Management](../elements/cognitive-load-management.md)).
3. **Model before practice.** Demonstrate the target skill or reasoning before asking learners to perform ([Practice](../elements/practice.md) follows modeling, not replaces it).
4. **Insert guided practice between segments.** Check understanding with questions and [Check-Ins](../principles/check-ins.md) after each chunk rather than only at lesson end.
5. **Fade support toward independent work.** Move from guided to independent practice as accuracy grows, releasing responsibility progressively [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].
6. **Close with consolidation.** End with summarization, application, or a preview linking to the next lesson ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) for formative checking).

## Examples
- **Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction** — a research-derived lesson template (daily review, small-step presentation, guided practice, checks for understanding) widely used in UK and US school improvement programs ([Rosenshine, 2012](https://www.aft.org/sites/default/files/periodicals/Rosenshine.pdf)).
- **[Gagné's 9 Events of Instruction](../patterns/gagnés-9-events-of-instruction.md)** — a nine-phase lesson template (gain attention → inform objectives → … → enhance retention and transfer) used as the structural backbone of many corporate and higher-education course designs.
- **Success for All** — a whole-school cooperative learning program whose tightly scripted lesson structures produced consistent reading gains in randomized trials (https://www.successforall.org).
