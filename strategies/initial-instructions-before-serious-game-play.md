# Provide initial instructions on the adequate sequence of actions before self-training game play

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors propose that serious-game self-training should be preceded by explicit instruction on the correct action sequence, because in their game players were "interrupted by instructions, which could be skipped", allowing trial-and-error play without an initial overview. They believe initial instructions and a brief preparatory video may increase knowledge acquisition, while acknowledging this may decrease interest in playing by breaking game continuity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The game's step-by-step instructions must be skippable or easily ignored, so upfront instruction must be delivered outside or before the game
#### Constraints
- May decrease the student's interest in playing the game due to breaking game continuity, as the authors state

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students
- health professionals
- lay rescuers

### Target Learning Goals
- CPR knowledge and sequence of cardiac-arrest management actions
