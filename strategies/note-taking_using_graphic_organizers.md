# Note-Taking Using Graphic Organizers

## Description
Learners use graphic organizers to take notes on their reading, research, films, or podcasts, visually structuring information and relationships between concepts to enhance comprehension and retention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Graphic organizers, learning materials (e.g., readings, videos), and instructor modeling.
#### Constraints
- May require explicit instruction on how to use different types of organizers effectively. Avoid complete sentences to maximize efficiency.

- <!-- TODO -->

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learners, particularly effective for complex or dense material.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance comprehension and retention by visually organizing information and showing relationships between concepts.

### Affordances
- Improves focus and organization, supports dual coding by combining visual and verbal information, and facilitates review and recall.

### Personalization
- Teachers can provide pre-designed organizers or allow students to create their own. Adaptable for different content areas and learning styles.

- Quality and completeness of notes, ability to summarize and synthesize information, and performance on related assessments.

## Impact
- Decades of research show that incorporating graphic organizers into instruction improves student learning. Teacher-created organizers can be more effective than student-generated ones.
