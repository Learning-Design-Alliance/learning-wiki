# Invest in strengthening the academic skills of adolescent mothers, in addition to increasing educational attainment and school quality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Beyond expanding enrollment and improving school quality, the article recommends that "investments are needed to strengthen the academic skills of adolescent mothers" in low- and middle-income countries. The rationale is that adolescent childbearing erodes English literacy and numeracy among low-attainment girls, and that losing these skills may undercut the demographic and economic benefits expected from girls' education, including labor market access and fertility-related pathways.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs that reach adolescent mothers after they leave school and begin childbearing
#### Constraints
- The evidence base is three LMIC longitudinal samples; the article notes analytical samples exclude the most vulnerable girls who left school before baseline

### Target Learners
- Adolescent mothers with low levels of grade attainment in low- and middle-income countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintenance or restoration of English literacy and numeracy skills after first birth

### Affordances
- Skill Retention Threshold Theory

## Claims

- [Among girls with low grade attainment (below grade 6), a first birth has a significant negative effect on English literacy, while among those reaching the last year of primary or secondary school the effect reverses or disappears](../claims/first-birth-english-literacy-low-attainment-negative.md) [+W]
- [Childbearing has little or no effect on local language literacy, except a negative effect at low grade attainment in the rural Zambia sample](../claims/first-birth-local-language-literacy-null.md) [+W]
- [At low grade attainment, childbearing has a significant negative effect on numeracy; the effect is null at higher attainment, with the joint interaction significant only in Malawi](../claims/first-birth-numeracy-low-attainment-negative.md) [+W]
- [Across all three studies, the article finds consistently significant interactions between grade attainment and childbearing on academic skills, with negative effects concentrated at grade 7 or lower](../claims/grade-attainment-childbearing-interaction-consistent.md) [+W]
- [Literacy levels are low overall, rise with grade attainment particularly at the transition to secondary school, and are lower among girls out of school longer](../claims/literacy-low-rises-with-grade-lower-out-of-school.md) [+W]
- [Time since school leaving is significantly associated with English literacy scores — negatively in all three rural samples but positively in the urban Zambia sample](../claims/time-since-school-leaving-english-literacy-rural-negative.md) [+W]
