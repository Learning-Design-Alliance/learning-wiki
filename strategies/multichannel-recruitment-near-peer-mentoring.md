# Recruit mentors and mentees through many channels, since different channels reach different students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The guide recommends combining recruitment methods — email listservs, physical letters on university letterhead, phone banking with scripts, referrals from faculty and advisors, word of mouth, in-class presentations, new student orientations, and flyers and social media — because "you're likely to catch different students through different channels." It highlights in-person information sessions with free food and returning participants as among the most effective tools, and advises recruiting future leaders from active site members by giving them bite-sized projects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Advertising should include a link to a contact email or an application so interested people can stay connected
- An application through Google forms is usually the best approach for collecting contact and demographic information
#### Constraints
- Some methods reach certain groups better than others; the guide notes, for example, that high schoolers never pick up the phone, so call their parents instead

### Target Learners
- undergraduate STEM students, including incoming freshmen and transfer students
- graduate students recruited as mentors

### Target Learning Goals
- participation in a near-peer mentoring community
- recruitment of future student leaders and organizers
