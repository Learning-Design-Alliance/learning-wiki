# Use a real-life forensic physics case as problem-based inquiry material in undergraduate physics courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article builds a complete problem-based learning case around a historically significant real event, the 1994 shooting of the Rwandan presidential aircraft, solvable with undergraduate-level physics. Students translate qualitative observations into quantitative equations and reconcile incomplete data: "Daily-life phenomena constitute a rich source of problem-based inquiry in physics, as they require students to construct models, formulate and test hypotheses, and critically assess the reliability and uncertainty of their results." Each section of the analysis provides at least one exercise idea for students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Publicly available source material (expert reports, witness statements, recordings) that students can analyse quantitatively
#### Constraints
- The witness accounts do not allow one to determine what actually happened, in particular whether the missile hit the aircraft or exploded nearby

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- model construction and hypothesis testing
- inference from incomplete and uncertain data
- critical assessment of reliability and uncertainty
