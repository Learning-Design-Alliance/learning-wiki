Teach a grammar structure by pausing and replaying subtitled short segments of an authentic animated sitcom and having students produce sentences¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's experimental treatment: show short segments of an authentic animated sitcom with English subtitles, pause and replay each time the target structure occurs to draw learners' attention to it, then have students practice and produce sentences using the structure. The authors state this was in line with Focus on Form and Consciousness-raising approaches, which direct learners' attention to formal properties of the target language.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Episodes should be shown as shorter segments rather than in full, to avoid overwhelming students with unfamiliar information
  • English subtitles are used to make content and the target structure tangible

Constraints¶

  • The article recommends the approach based on one small convenience-sample study of a single structure (conditional sentences) and calls for future studies on other structures

Target Learners¶

  • intermediate EFL learners in language institutes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • learning grammatical structures communicatively, e.g. conditional sentences

Affordances¶

  • Three Dimensional Grammar Framework Form Meaning Use

Claims¶

  • Experimental-group EFL learners taught conditional sentences via an animated sitcom improved significantly from pretest to posttest (p=.003) [+W]
  • Explicit instruction alone produced no statistically significant pretest-to-posttest gain in conditional sentences (p=.18) [+W]
  • Experimental-group students held predominantly positive attitudes toward using the animated sitcom for grammar learning [+W]
  • Two students found the sitcom characters' speech too fast and some suggested showing short episodes, which the authors read as support for short subtitled video segments to avoid overload [+W]
  • The animated-sitcom group outperformed the explicit-instruction control group on the posttest (p=.03) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use pre-task subtitled videos of similar (not identical) tasks to build familiarity while preserving cognitive complexity
