# Adopt a step-wise approach to technology integration in clinical skills teaching, starting with simple familiar tools matched to learning outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that post-pandemic technology adoption in practical teaching should be iterative and stepped: educators first consider whether learning outcomes are better facilitated by digital tools, then introduce simple and familiar technologies, and only later adopt advanced technologies when confident. Participants warned against adopting technology because it is available rather than outcome-driven, so selection must be "appropriately mapped onto specific learning outcomes".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning outcomes must be identified first to judge whether digital tools better facilitate them
- Institutional support and digital skills training for staff are needed
#### Constraints
- Poorly integrated technology risks frustration and feelings of being undervalued among staff and students

### Target Learners
- health science educators

### Target Learning Goals
- practical clinical skill acquisition
