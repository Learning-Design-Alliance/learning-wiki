# Use long-term school-scientist collaborations with shared detector hardware to sustain cosmic ray engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Beyond one-off events, the article describes sustained models in which schools and scientists collaborate over the long term around shared detector hardware. QuarkNet organizes multiple cosmic ray activities through "long-term collaborations between high school teachers and scientists", and during International Muon Week paired schools collect and analyse data and share their results. Italy's OCRA project involves 24 INFN divisions offering activities for both students and teachers, and French institutes share detector suitcases with schools and training programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Detector hardware (e.g. scintillator-based setups or MRPC telescopes) available to schools or shared as portable kits
- A network of scientists and teachers committed beyond a single event, with data-sharing occasions such as International Muon Week
#### Constraints
- Examples documented in the US, France and Italy; implementation depends on national infrastructure and volunteer networks

### Target Learners
- High-school students
- High-school teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Collecting and analysing authentic cosmic ray data
- Collaborative data comparison across schools
- Sustained engagement with astroparticle physics
