# Design physics tasks that have students critique, compare, and revise AI-generated explanations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends shifting the pedagogical focus "from locating explanations to evaluating their quality" as students increasingly access explanations through generative systems. Students analyze an AI-generated explanation, identify omissions or oversimplifications, and revise it against physical principles. Comparative tasks ask students to compare their own reasoning with a generated response and a formal solution or representation, distinguishing plausible-sounding from robust explanations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional tasks must engage students directly with generated explanations and make evaluation skills — conceptual coherence checking, consistency with physical principles, identifying omissions — explicit.
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as an extension of an existing syllabus rather than a fundamental curricular shift, and does not require teaching prompt construction as a separate technical skill.

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating the quality and conceptual coherence of explanations
- metacognitive judgment about physical understanding
