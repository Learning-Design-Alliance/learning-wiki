# Scaffolding storytelling: guiding a child to create their own story about new material through open-ended questions, hints, and validation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
In this strategy the instructor prompts the child to create their own story about newly learned material rather than telling a scripted story. The article describes scaffolding behaviors as "(1) open-ended questioning", "(2) hinting", and "(3) validation/feedback", with scaffolding "unscripted and adapted to the child's narrative". In the study, first-grade children created stories about novel objects with an instructor who asked guiding questions, gave hints, and validated responses, and this strategy improved novel word learning in person.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Face-to-face interaction between child and instructor; the advantage did not emerge in remote sessions
#### Constraints
- The word-learning advantage was demonstrated for first-grade-age children learning novel object names with a single instructor; it disappeared in the remote version of the task

### Target Learners
- first-grade-age children (about 6.9 years)

### Target Learning Goals
- novel word learning (object names and functions)
- narrative skills
