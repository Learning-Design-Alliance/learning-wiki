Use web-assisted activities and simulations in an integrated way with real experiments rather than as a replacement¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's discussion recommends that web-assisted activities and simulations be combined with traditional, hands-on teaching rather than substituting for it. Citing Hennessy et al. (2007), it notes that "by using simulations, teachers moved away from using 'real' experiments", and concludes integrated teaching techniques are preferable. It suggests such activities are most suitable when students have conceptual difficulties.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must retain real experiments alongside the digital activities

Constraints¶

  • Suggested for use especially when there are conceptual disagreements or students have difficulty understanding the subject

Target Learners¶

  • 6th grade science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Meaningful and permanent learning of science concepts

Claims¶

  • A gain- and activity-based web-assisted virtual material produced significantly higher 6th-grade achievement on the 'Systems in our body' unit than usual teaching [+W]
  • The virtual material did not significantly improve knowledge permanency three weeks after the unit, while the control group's scores declined significantly [~W]
  • Students reported that the virtual-lab lesson made learning fun, helped them figure out concepts themselves, and supported thinking like real scientists [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Blend web-based and traditional approaches rather than replacing classroom instruction with web-based instruction
  • Integrate virtual and augmented reality as supplementary tools rather than full replacements for traditional learning methods
  • Use virtual simulations to enhance cognitive learning while prioritizing in-person laboratory sessions for psychomotor and technical skills
  • Integrate simulation technologies with traditional teaching through blended approaches tailored to specific clinical tasks
  • Implement AI simulations as complements within a stepped simulation continuum, with layered interaction modes and blended feedback
