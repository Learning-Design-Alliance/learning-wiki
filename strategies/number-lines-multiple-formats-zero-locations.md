# Present number lines in multiple formats with zero in varied locations and discuss numbers on either side of reference points

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on students' error patterns, the authors recommend that when introducing and using number lines, "teachers should present numbers in multiple formats (not always starting at the left of the paper) and talk about numbers on either side of key reference points, such as zero." They further suggest that presenting number lines with different numbers marked and with different scales may help students attend to the relevant features of number lines and the placement of numbers. This addresses the observed tendencies to span the whole line and to count from the left edge of the page.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classroom use of visual aids such as number lines
#### Constraints
- Recommendation derives from observed placement errors, not from a tested manipulation of number line format

### Target Learners
- kindergarteners
- first graders

### Target Learning Goals
- attending to relevant features of number lines
- placing integers accurately relative to zero

## Claims

- [Kindergarteners did not improve on placing positive integers, possibly because zero was marked in the middle rather than at the edge of the line](../claims/kindergarten-positives-zero-placement-hypothesis.md) [+W]
- [Kindergarteners frequently placed integers on the wrong side of zero, and less successful students either spanned the whole line or clustered placements](../claims/wrong-side-of-zero-error-patterns.md) [+W]
