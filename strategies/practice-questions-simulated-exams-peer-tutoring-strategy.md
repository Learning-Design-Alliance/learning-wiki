# Increase practice questions, simulated examinations, and peer tutoring to strengthen test strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because Test Strategies was the largest contributor to performance variation for all students, the article recommends enhancing examination preparation through increased practice questions and simulated examination environments, interleaved and post-lecture questions, and peer tutoring by high-performing students. It cites studies where "increased practice problem completion, greater than at least 2000 in one study and 3500 in another, had significantly resulted in higher Step 1 scores".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classes offering multiple opportunities for practice with lecture-linked questions and reading resources
- High-performing students who have taken the examinations available as tutors
#### Constraints
- The practice-volume figures are cited from prior studies, not tested in this article's own data

### Target Learners
- medical students preparing for USMLE Step 1
- preclinical medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- improved examination preparation and test strategies
- higher USMLE Step 1 scores
