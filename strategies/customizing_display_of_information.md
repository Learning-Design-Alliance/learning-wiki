# Customizing Display of Information

## Description
Customizing the display of information involves modifying the appearance of digital content to reduce reading effort and improve comprehension. This includes adjusting text size, font, colors, spacing, margins, and justification. Web browsers and e-reading apps often provide options to remove distractions and personalize the reading experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A digital device (computer, smartphone, tablet) with web browser or e-reading app. Digital text content in a customizable format (e.g., EPUB, HTML).
#### Constraints
- Customization options may vary depending on the software or app used. Some file formats (e.g., PDF) may not allow for text resizing or reflowing. Over-customization can sometimes create new distractions.

- Individuals with visual impairments, dyslexia, attention deficits, or reading difficulties. Useful across all age groups and educational levels.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Reduce visual distractions, improve readability, accommodate visual impairments, enhance focus, support personalized learning

### Affordances
- Reduces cognitive load by optimizing visual presentation. Improves reading comfort and reduces eye strain. Supports diverse learning preferences and needs. Allows for a more engaging and accessible reading experience.

### Personalization
- Users can select font styles, text sizes, color themes, spacing, margins, and justification settings that best suit their individual needs. Reading views can be used to remove distractions and simplify the layout of web pages.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe improvements in reading speed, accuracy, and comprehension. Collect user feedback on the effectiveness of different customization settings. Measure the time spent on reading tasks with and without customized display settings.

## Impact
- Research shows that customizing the display of information can improve reading fluency, comprehension, and motivation, particularly for students with dyslexia and visual impairments.
