# Grade open-ended assignments generously with flexible test weighting as a safety net against anxiety

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors designed grading to buffer the anxiety that open-ended problems produce: assignments and gists were "graded more generously with significant weight placed on effort", and term tests used a flexible weighting scheme in which the lowest mark counted less than the higher one (e.g., 10%/20% weighting in some years). Assignments were graded on choice of physical approaches, realistic assumptions, effort, and analytical insight rather than a single correct answer. This addresses student reservations about open-ended assessment while maintaining academic standards.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Open-ended assignments and tests with no definitive correct answer, graded on approach, assumptions, effort, and insight.
#### Constraints
- Students still found open-ended questions daunting; 77% of commenting students (n=31) found them hard to answer.

### Target Learners
- Second-year undergraduates encountering open-ended interdisciplinary problems for the first time

### Target Learning Goals
- Applying physical approaches to novel biological problems
- Managing assessment anxiety in open-ended problem solving
