# Provide immediate individual written and oral corrective feedback the day after a writing task

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 2 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
In this study, each experimental-group student was called individually, revised her mistakes with the researcher, and received both written and oral feedback the day after the pre-test, while control students "did not see or revise their mistakes and received no feedback". The authors conclude that "teachers may consider the power and effectiveness of immediate well-given feedback". Case examples suggest single-session feedback was sometimes not retained, with one student's weak effect "most likely because it was given just once".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback must be given immediately after the writing task and accompanied by individual revision of mistakes with the teacher
#### Constraints
- Feedback given only once may fail to be retained at a four-week delay, as several case examples show

### Target Learners
- female foundation-level EFL students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- grammatical accuracy in L2 writing
- lexical accuracy in L2 writing
