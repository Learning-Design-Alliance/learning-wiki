# Visual Representations in Mathematics

## Description
This evidence-based strategy involves using visual representations, such as schematic diagrams, to help learners understand abstract mathematics concepts and solve problems. These visual aids accurately depict a problem’s mathematical quantities and relationships, reflecting a learner's understanding of the problem and aiding in its correct solution. Visual representations can take various forms, including number lines, strip diagrams, pictures, graphs/charts, and graphic organizers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials for creating visual representations, instruction on different types of visual representations, and opportunities for practice.
#### Constraints
- Students may need explicit instruction on how to create and use visual representations effectively. Some may require additional support to avoid disorganized or inaccurate visuals.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the accuracy and clarity of the visual representations. Assess the learners' ability to use the diagrams to solve problems correctly. Analyze the explanations of the solutions.

## Impact
- Studies show using visual representations significantly improves problem-solving accuracy. Visuals help students with and without disabilities understand and solve math problems effectively.
