# Educative and restorative discipline implemented through restorative deans, circles, peer mediation, and youth court

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report describes Bronxdale's approach to behavior as educative and restorative rather than punitive: staffing decisions such as "hiring two restorative deans, a counselor, and two social workers" brought "restorative practices to every facet of school life, emphasizing learning and self- and community repair over punishment." Mechanisms including advisory, Peer Group Connections, restorative circles, peer mediation, and youth court give students roles in resolving conflict and sustaining a safe community, positioning students as "an active and critical part of the solution." The NYC DOE designated Bronxdale a model site for restorative practices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated restorative staffing (deans, counselors, social workers) and schoolwide mechanisms that amplify student voice and shared responsibility for norms.
#### Constraints
- The report notes some students are assigned to the school without having chosen it, which poses a challenge in orienting those students to the school culture.

### Target Learners
- High school students, including those experiencing adversity and conflict in and out of school

### Target Learning Goals
- Constructive emotion and conflict management, collaboration, conflict resolution, community contribution, peer leadership
