# Use simulation, case-based learning and hospital partnerships to increase practical DMT training exposure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Interviewed academics proposed methodological strategies to compensate for limited hands-on DMT practice. They suggested "the use of simulation technologies and partnerships with hospitals for practical training," alongside hybrid approaches combining lectures with "case‐based learning (CBL) and seminars and simulations." Simulation lets students practise in a controlled setting while hospital collaborations provide real-world exposure; observation of postgraduate clinicians treating DMT patients was suggested where postgraduate courses exist.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to simulation technologies or hospital partnerships; observation of postgraduate clinicians is possible only in schools with postgraduate courses covering DMT
#### Constraints
- Observation-based learning is limited to schools that have postgraduate courses covering DMT

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students in Southeast Asian dental schools

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical skills and competencies for managing dental and maxillofacial trauma
