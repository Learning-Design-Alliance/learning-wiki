Implement the 7E model through unit-wise lesson plans with active participation, peer interaction and group work¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes implementing the 7E model as unit-wise lesson plans supported by teaching aids, with teachers acting as facilitators who encourage "peer interaction, group discussion, experimentation, field visiting". This two-way mode of learning is presented as giving learners scope for active participation and creating a joyful learning environment, particularly benefiting low-intelligence students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need to understand the constructivist approach and act as facilitators; school administration should provide required learning resources

Constraints¶

  • The strategy was trialled only in physical science with Class IX students at one school over two lessons

Target Learners¶

  • Class IX secondary school science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • constructing science knowledge meaningfully through active participation and interaction

Affordances¶

  • Constructivist 7E Model

Claims¶

  • Class IX physical science students taught with the constructivist 7E model achieved significantly higher post-test and gain scores than students taught with traditional methods [+W]
  • Within the 7E experimental group, post-test achievement scores did not differ significantly among high, average, and low intelligence students [+W]
  • Experimental and control groups did not differ significantly in pre-test science achievement before the intervention [+W]
  • Gains from the 7E model were significant for low- and average-intelligence students but not for high-intelligence students, with gain magnitude decreasing across intelligence levels [+W]
