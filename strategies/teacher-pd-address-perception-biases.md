# Address teacher perception biases through teacher training and professional development focused on scaffolding and identifying children needing extra support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implication section recommends targeting teacher perceptions in professional development as an avenue for addressing the achievement gap. "Further investigation into whether and how these perceptions could be addressed in teacher training and professional development is also recommended (Kilday et al., 2012), with particular attention to assisting teachers in learning how to appropriately scaffold children's learning." It also recommends continued research into teacher background, training, and contextual factors such as the preschool work environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need accurate understanding of preschoolers' knowledge and skills to provide differentiated instruction matched to children's needs
#### Constraints
- The article recommends further investigation of how perceptions could be addressed; it did not itself evaluate any training intervention

### Target Learners
- low-income, ethnically diverse preschoolers aged 3 to 5

### Target Learning Goals
- language and pre-literacy skills
- mathematics skills
