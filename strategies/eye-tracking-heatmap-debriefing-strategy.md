# Embed eye-tracking heatmap review into simulation debriefing to give situation-awareness-focused feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's intervention used eye-tracking heatmaps during debriefing: "Eye-tracking heatmaps were used during debriefing to review attention to critical cues (e.g., the vital signs monitor and bleeding source) and to provide SA–focused feedback." This lets instructors surface missed cues and attentional gaps that conventional verbal feedback may not reveal. The authors suggest future work compare instructor-led, data-driven, and self-review feedback approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Eye-tracking hardware with validated calibration
- Trained instructors able to interpret heatmaps during debriefing
#### Constraints
- In this pilot the feedback was qualitative instructor interpretation of heatmaps rather than quantitative gaze metrics
- Authors caution there is no single universal gaze pattern for effective SA, cautioning against overinterpreting any single metric

### Target Learners
- medical interns

### Target Learning Goals
- situational awareness
- attention to critical clinical cues
