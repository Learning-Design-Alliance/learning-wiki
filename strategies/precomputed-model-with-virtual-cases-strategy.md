# Pair pre-computed physiological model outputs with virtual clinical cases and embedded questions to ground pathophysiology in clinical context

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable recipe: use a simple, clinician-familiar model whose outputs (blood pressure, stroke volume, heart rate) are matched to guideline-defined disease stages, display results in tabular and graphical formats such as pressure-volume loops, and anchor them in virtual cases comprising "a brief history, examination findings and previous investigations where relevant" with questions on diagnosis, treatment, pathophysiology and severity. This lets students classify disease severity and study physiological processes interactively rather than reading about them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-compute model outputs before exposing the environment to minimise difficulties in using the model and ensure timeliness
#### Constraints
- A delicate balance was needed between adding model detail (risking bewildering students with little physics background) and embedding the model so deeply its purpose was lost

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- classifying disease severity and understanding abnormal cardiovascular physiology in clinical context

### Affordances
- Lumped Parameter Cardiovascular Model Theory
