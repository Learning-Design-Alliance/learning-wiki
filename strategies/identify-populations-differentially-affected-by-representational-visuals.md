# Future research should identify student populations and contexts differentially affected by representational visuals in mathematics materials

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that future work on representational visuals in mathematics investigate which students benefit or are distracted, proposing to identify "populations of students who may be differentially affected by representational visuals (e.g., English Language Learners and special education students)" and contexts such as challenging or unfamiliar contexts. The authors argue conflicting effects of representational visuals could explain previously reported null effects on mathematics performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies need to distinguish student subgroups and context difficulty when measuring effects of representational visuals
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a possibility to investigate, not an established effect

### Target Learners
- middle school mathematics students, including English Language Learners and special education students

### Target Learning Goals
- mathematics performance with contextual visuals in word problems
