# Adapt AGREE II instruction to learners' prior knowledge: offer e-learning to true novices, streamlined materials to experienced learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the null trial, the authors recommend targeting the online interventions at "truly novice" participants such as trainees and students in health services research, and note the literature suggests good instructional design matters more for low than high prior-knowledge learners. They observe that redundant content should usually be eliminated for more experienced learners and invoke expertise reversal effects, where a method that helps novices may be less effective or even detrimental for experts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of learners' prior knowledge of health research methodology and practice guidelines to route them to appropriate training
#### Constraints
- The authors state further investigation is required to assess whether efficient instruction for advanced learners requires different methods; the novice-benefit hypothesis is supported only by a pass-rate comparison (86% vs 73%) and requires further study

### Target Learners
- Trainees and students new to practice guidelines and health research methodology; experienced guideline stakeholders

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective, efficient training in applying the AGREE II to appraise guideline quality
