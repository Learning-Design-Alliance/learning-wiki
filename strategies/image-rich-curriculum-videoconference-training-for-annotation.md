# Train novices for image-annotation tasks via an image-rich anatomy curriculum, an SOP with a short demonstration video, and recorded interactive videoconferencing sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a training package for technologists unfamiliar with annotation tools: a curriculum document with infographics covering "an image-rich multiplanar depiction of pancreatic anatomy on CT, common anatomic variations" and relevant CT artifacts, an SOP and a 20-min training video, plus four 1-hour radiologist-led interactive virtual sessions that were recorded for future reuse. Trainees signed an online verification form and had optional access to an e-anatomy atlas.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standard operating procedure document and a training video demonstrating the software's annotation workflow
- Subject-matter experts to author the image-rich curriculum and lead the interactive sessions
- Recorded sessions and shared training materials accessible to trainees
#### Constraints
- The duration of training was relatively short, a limitation the authors state
- Sessions were delivered during a COVID-19 downtime period; segmentations were done amid clinical duties without extra remuneration

### Target Learners
- CT and MRI technologists (working adults with imaging workflow experience but no annotation training)

### Target Learning Goals
- Volumetric organ segmentation on cross-sectional imaging
- Recognition of anatomic variations and CT artifacts affecting organ boundaries
