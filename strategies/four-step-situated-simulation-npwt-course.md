# Four-step situated simulation teaching course for NPWT continuing education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article implements situated simulation teaching as a 3-hour course in four steps: course design, inventory of human and material resources, simulation, and learning outcome evaluation. The simulation step comprises "briefing (60 minutes), where lecturers explain the session conduct and objectives and assign roles; ... simulation and cues (90 minutes), where participants gain experiential or observational simulator/scenario experience; and ... debriefing (30 minutes)". Low-fidelity wound simulators, NPWT dressings, and loaned NPWT devices support hands-on operation practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two negative-pressure wound care experts to administer the course, plus environmental equipment such as group discussion tables, computer projection equipment, and space for wound care practice
#### Constraints
- The study used a single stationary NPWT device model as the primary simulator because of schedule variability and time constraints, which the authors note could introduce bias given varying operational scope of other models

### Target Learners
- surgical nurses in regional hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of NPWT indications, device operation, patient assessment and care, and anomaly response
