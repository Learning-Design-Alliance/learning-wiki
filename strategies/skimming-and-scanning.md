# Skimming And Scanning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Skimming is rapid reading to extract the gist of a text — typically by reading titles, headings, topic sentences, and summary paragraphs. Scanning is rapid visual search for a specific piece of information (a name, date, figure, keyword) without processing surrounding text. Both are goal-directed reading behaviors that deliberately trade completeness for speed, and both are teachable skills rather than innate talents.

## Design Implications

Skimming and scanning depend on efficient eye-movement control and on text features that signal where relevant information lives; instruction that makes these signals explicit improves learners' search efficiency [Disciplinary literacy instruction improves comprehension by teaching text-specific reading moves.](../claims/disciplinary-literacy-instruction-improves-comprehension.md) [+M]. Because both techniques suppress deep processing, they should be positioned as *preparatory or search* phases — orienting to a text or locating a target — not as substitutes for careful reading when comprehension is the goal. Fluent word recognition is a prerequisite: learners who still decode effortfully cannot free capacity for rapid search [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Texts with navigable structure (headings, lists, typographic cues) that support selective attention
- A clear, specific reading purpose stated before reading — what to find or what gist is needed
- Explicit modeling of the technique ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) demonstration of where the eyes go and why)
- Timed practice with feedback on both speed and hit rate

#### Constraints
- Skimming produces systematically poorer comprehension and inference than full reading [-S] — learners who skim before an exam often mistake familiarity for knowledge
- Scanning fails when the target is conceptual rather than lexical; searching for "the author's argument" cannot be done by keyword match
- Poor readers do not automatically transfer the skill; without instruction they skim the same way they read — slowly and completely [-M]
- Dense, poorly signposted texts (or screen layouts without headings) defeat scanning [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Preview skimming** before full reading to build a schema for the content ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) serve a similar function)
- **Search scanning** under time pressure (e.g., finding a citation, a price, a protocol step)
- **Digital scanning**: Ctrl-F and database filters change the skill from visual search to query formulation; instruction should cover both
- **Discipline-specific variants**: skimming an empirical article (abstract → figures → conclusion) differs from skimming a narrative or a legal document

### Target Learners
- Intermediate and advanced readers who already decode fluently [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Second-language learners preparing for academic reading loads, once decoding is automatic
- Less appropriate for beginning readers, who need full-text reading practice to build fluency [-M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Study skills: efficiently triaging large reading loads
- Information literacy: locating evidence and evaluating relevance before committing to close reading
- Test-taking: managing time on reading-heavy assessments

### Instructions
1. State the reading purpose explicitly ("You have 90 seconds to find the sample size") — purpose drives where attention is allocated.
2. Model the technique with a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md): show how headings, first sentences, and typographic cues guide the eye.
3. Have learners preview-skim the text and predict its content, then verify by close reading — the skim functions as an [Advance Organizer](../elements/advance-organizers.md).
4. Run timed scanning drills with immediate feedback on accuracy, not just speed.
5. Follow with [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) to mark located targets, converting the search into a durable artifact.

## Examples
- **IELTS/TOEFL preparation** — timed scanning tasks (locating names, dates, figures in passages) are a core tested skill; official practice materials from [IELTS](https://ielts.org) train it directly.
- **Empirical-article reading protocol** — abstract → headings → figures → conclusion, a skimming sequence taught in many graduate research-methods courses.
- **SQ3R study method** — the "Survey" step is a structured skim before reading, questioning, and reciting.
