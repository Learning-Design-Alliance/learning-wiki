# Sequence VR haptic simulator training before students' first artificial-tooth access cavity preparation in preclinical endodontic curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 against) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's central design recommendation is to place VR haptic simulator sessions before hands-on practice with artificial teeth, because this ordering "has a positive effect on students' manual dexterity along with reduced stress and increased self‐confidence in clinical skills". In the study, the VR-first group completed three training sessions plus a recorded evaluation session before a single artificial-tooth attempt, after standardized lectures and demonstrations for both groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardized lectures and demonstrations on access cavity preparation delivered to all students before training
- Multiple VR simulator practice sessions before the artificial-tooth attempt
- Blinded, standardized assessment of artificial-tooth preparations
#### Constraints
- Evidence is from a single institution and a single first attempt on artificial teeth
- Long-term skill retention and clinical transferability were not evaluated
- Unequal VR exposure between groups (four sessions vs one artificial-tooth attempt) could have influenced skill acquisition

### Target Learners
- third-semester undergraduate dental students in preclinical endodontic training

### Target Learning Goals
- manual dexterity in access cavity preparation
- stress and anxiety management during preclinical training
- self-confidence in clinical skills
