# Use blended learning—combining lectures, digital modules, and limited in-person simulation—as a scalable compromise for communication training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes blended learning as a response to the logistical challenges of simulation-based training: "Structured debriefing, emotional safety, and skilled facilitation are essential but not always feasible on a large scale." Combining lectures, digital modules, and limited in-person simulation is presented as a scalable, sustainable compromise. This is a forward-looking proposal, not a tested design in this study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Combination of lecture-based content, digital modules, and limited in-person simulation sessions
#### Constraints
- Proposed, not evaluated in this study; full-scale simulation's resource-intensive nature is the stated motivation

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical communication skills including breaking bad news
