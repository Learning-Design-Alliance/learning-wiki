# Design GenAI tools with modular offloading control and meta-cognition support for learning contexts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
To preserve informal learning with general-purpose GenAI tools, the article suggests two areas of new affordances beyond cognitive scaffolding. First, "provide affordances for users to understand and control which processes or subprocesses—e.g., which of Ellis' model's information-seeking stages—are offloaded to AI," allowing flexible configuration for learning versus productivity goals. Second, add "additional meta-cognition support": improving users' awareness of their own goals, processes, and self-regulation capabilities to take charge of learning goals, monitor progress, and manage motivation, emotions, and behaviors, including their use of AI.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- GenAI tools would need to expose which information-seeking subprocesses are offloaded and support user configuration of them
#### Constraints
- The article notes development and adoption barriers for structured educational GenAI tools remain steep compared with easily accessible general-purpose tools

### Target Learners
- Adult informal learners using general-purpose GenAI tools for knowledge and skill acquisition

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-regulated learning and preservation of agency while offloading cognitive tasks to AI

### Affordances
- Ellis Information Seeking Stage Model Lens
