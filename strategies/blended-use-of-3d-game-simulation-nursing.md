# Use 3D game-based simulation in blended learning environments alongside traditional teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the 3D animation-supported game simulation yielded comparable academic achievement and self-confidence but no advantage over traditional teaching, the authors recommend combining the two approaches rather than substituting one for the other: "it is recommended that such technology-supported methods be used in blended learning environments alongside traditional teaching methods in nursing education." The discussion adds that simulation tools offer advantages in interaction, scalability, and potential long-term retention, so integration into curricula through blended or supplementary models warrants consideration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the web-based simulation platform and devices compatible with it
- Traditional laboratory or demonstration instruction retained as the companion component
#### Constraints
- The trial evaluated only short-term cognitive outcomes with no long-term follow-up of knowledge retention
- Findings come from first-year nursing students at a single state university, limiting generalisability

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of vital signs measurement knowledge and skills
