# Invite student-generated questions from the problem context to launch additional learning cycles in which students identify variables, hypothesize, design experiments, and test predictions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Beyond the core problem, the article recommends letting student questions extend the activity into further learning cycles. It notes that questions such as whether planted clove size influences the eventual bulb's clove number "provide opportunities for students to take more responsibility for their learning by challenging them to identify variables, form hypotheses, design experiments, and make predictions based upon their hypotheses." A suggested example has students sort cloves into healthy, diseased, and questionable groups, plant them in a school garden, and test their germination predictions the following spring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A context rich enough to generate testable student questions
- Time or a setting (e.g., a school garden) where predictions can be checked over an extended period
#### Constraints
- Some predictions, such as germination as a function of clove mass or mulch depth, require a growing season to test

### Target Learners
- preparatory chemistry students
- high school science students

### Target Learning Goals
- hypothesis formulation and experimental design
- control and exclusion of variables
- prediction testing
