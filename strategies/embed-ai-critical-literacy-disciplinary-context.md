# Embed AI tools into disciplinary teaching to develop critical literacies and produce 'maximally intelligent users' rather than uncritical acceptors of AI output.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a second path alongside robust assessment: integrating AI tools into disciplinary teaching. The authors state that "these tools have the potential to be powerful learning tools, if embedded into teaching in an authentic manner" and that "Core to a strategy where AI tools are embedded is the need to support students with critical literacies around their use." This transforms the 'maximally intelligent cheat' into a 'maximally intelligent user.'

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have sound disciplinary understanding so that prompt engineering skills alone are insufficient; AI use must be embedded in discipline knowledge.
#### Constraints
- The authors note AI tools present both challenge and opportunity depending on discipline; in narrative-heavy disciplines rote learning may be less central.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students
- higher education students across disciplines

### Target Learning Goals
- critical AI literacy
- ethical AI use
- disciplinary mastery with AI augmentation
