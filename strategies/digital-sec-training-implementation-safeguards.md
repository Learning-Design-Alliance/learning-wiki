# Implement home-based digital SEC trainings with screen-time limits and family-participation options

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q4` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article draws implementation remarks for digital trainings for young children: because delivering trainings online might contribute to children's daily screen time, "parental guidance and access restrictions provided by the training should be in place to monitor and control for excessive use". Additionally, since socio-emotional skills must transfer into daily interactions, "the SEC training should provide options to facilitate family participation" so parents can model effective social and learning interactions outside the app. Digital trainings are positioned as low-cost, flexible prevention strategies reachable where in-person services are unavailable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Parental guidance and built-in access restrictions to monitor and control excessive use
- Options to facilitate family participation for transfer into daily interactions
#### Constraints
- Training effects were partly based on parental evaluations prone to rater bias; sustainable transfer from short 6-week home-based training was not demonstrated for most outcomes

### Target Learners
- Preschool children (4–6 years) learning at home
- Children in health care-deprived areas

### Target Learning Goals
- Socio-emotional competence
- Transfer of trained skills into daily family interactions
