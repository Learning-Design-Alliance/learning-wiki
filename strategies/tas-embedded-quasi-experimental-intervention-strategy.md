# Deliver TAS as an embedded classroom intervention with teacher training, matched control, and withdrawal phase to test motivation effects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's treatment procedure provides a replicable framework for implementing teacher autonomy support as student-centered pedagogy. The TAS intervention instructions were "embedded in the regular lesson plan", the experimental teacher received three intensive training sessions of approximately 60-80 minutes modeled on Reeve et al. (2004), and teachers were observed regularly to establish consistency and accuracy in intervention and withdrawal. The design paired a matched control section and an A-B-A withdrawal phase so motivation changes could be attributed to the autonomy supportive environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Selection of the intervention teacher via a personality orientation and motivating styles scale, with the higher scorer delivering TAS
- Regular observation of teachers in both classes to establish consistency and accuracy in intervention and withdrawal procedure
#### Constraints
- The article states replications of this design would be required to refine ways to apply TAS as part of regular teaching style or incorporate it into the basic school curriculum

### Target Learners
- grade-6 students in public school English classes

### Target Learning Goals
- motivation for learning English
- perceived autonomy support
- self-regulation
