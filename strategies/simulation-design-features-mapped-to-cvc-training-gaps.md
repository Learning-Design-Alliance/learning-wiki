# Map identified procedural-training gaps to targeted simulation design features, including timed scenarios, embedded consent tasks, ethical scenarios, modular practice, feedback loops, and milestone-based benchmarks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Building on participants' perspectives, the article's Table 1 pairs each identified CVC training gap with a simulation design consideration: timed scenarios with escalating difficulty, embedded consent and communication tasks, ethically ambiguous or emotionally charged scenarios, repeatable modular training with varied case presentations, live or automated feedback loops within the simulation interface, and structure using established benchmarks and milestones. The strategy operationalizes participant desires for feedback, standardization, and realism in simulation design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation resources capable of implementing timed scenarios, feedback loops, and varied case presentations, which participants noted are costly
#### Constraints
- The article notes access to high-quality tools "is inconsistent" across institutions, creating disparities in training opportunities

### Target Learners
- physicians and residents across specialties performing central venous catheter placement

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural pacing, patient communication and informed consent, ethical decision-making, and standardized procedural competency in CVC placement
