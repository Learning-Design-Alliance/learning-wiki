# Direct future rubric-equity research toward underrepresented groups, admissions drivers, and student outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The authors lay out a research agenda for rubric-based admissions: study impacts on historically underrepresented groups, examine which application components drive decisions under the rubric, and test whether rubric admissions change the type of applicant admitted and student outcomes. They write that "future work should ensure that rubric-based admissions do increase equity rather than just use a new tool to perpetuate existing inequities," citing undergraduate evidence that subjective additions can widen existing inequalities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies need to examine groups the present dataset could not cover, including Black, Latinx, Indigenous, first-generation, low-income, disabled, and veteran applicants.
#### Constraints
- The application system does not allow applicants to enter their race or ethnicity, limiting which comparisons the present study could make.

### Target Learners
- prospective and admitted physics graduate students from historically underrepresented groups

### Target Learning Goals
- increasing equity in graduate admissions evaluation
- understanding what drives admissions decisions
