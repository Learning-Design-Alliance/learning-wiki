# Assess troubleshooting with process-based measures of students' troubleshooting strategies rather than outcomes-based measures of circuit completion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A process-based assessment gathers evidence about how students troubleshoot—their strategies—rather than only whether the final circuit works. The article recommends this after finding that completing a lab activity does not guarantee troubleshooting proficiency: "Process-based assessments that focus on students' troubleshooting strategies may be more appropriate." It is intended for undergraduate electronics laboratory courses where circuit building is central.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment tasks must allow observation of students' troubleshooting strategies, not only whether the final circuit functions
#### Constraints
- The article states that factors aside from troubleshooting contribute to student efficiency and successful completion of lab activities

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in electronics laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing students' ability to troubleshoot electric circuits
