# Use a set of critical-reading questions to skim and evaluate literature efficiently

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The guide adapts recommendations from Wallace & Wray's book on critical reading into a list of questions new astronomers can use when reading the literature: why you are reading the paper, why the authors wrote it, what story the figures tell, whether the conclusions are warranted by the evidence, and what use the paper is to your own work. Following these questions helps readers skim efficiently, decide which articles are relevant, and organise notes for future reuse.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient knowledge or expertise about the subject in question, since readers can only be critical of an idea if they have it
#### Constraints
- The guide notes many first-year doctoral students struggle to read scientific papers, and it is often not the fault of the reader if a piece of writing seems unclear

### Target Learners
- master's and doctoral students in astronomy and astrophysics

### Target Learning Goals
- critical reading of scientific papers
- managing and organising literature notes
