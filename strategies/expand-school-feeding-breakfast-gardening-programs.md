# Expand school feeding programs, school breakfast programs, and school food gardening to address food insecurity among students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a multi-sectoral response linking nutrition policy to educational outcomes: strengthening "school feeding programs that are currently specific to a small number of schools", introducing school gardening in the curriculum, and initiating School Breakfast and Afterschool Nutrition Programs. It is grounded in the study's finding that food insecurity and breakfast habits were strongly associated with academic performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must reach food-insecure students beyond the limited set of schools currently served, since the WFP program covers only public schools for Internally Displaced Populations.
#### Constraints
- The cross-sectional design cannot establish a causal relationship between variables, as the authors state.

### Target Learners
- primary school adolescents in low-income and food-insecure households

### Target Learning Goals
- improving academic performance by reducing food insecurity
