# Use virtual patients to replace passive instruction and train clinical reasoning before bedside learning, without diminishing bedside teaching hours

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends deploying virtual patients where they show the greatest benefit: replacing passive forms of traditional education and training clinical reasoning and critical thinking before direct patient contact. The authors state virtual patients "can have greater impact when applied where knowledge is combined with skills and applied in problem solving, and when direct patient contact is not yet possible". They also warn against using the results to justify reduced bedside teaching, which was investigated in only one study without positive outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning needs, not technological objectives, should drive teaching; curriculum time resources should be considered when adding virtual patients
#### Constraints
- Introduction of virtual patients will not always lead to detectable positive outcomes without consideration of needs and implementation details; evidence for replacing bedside teaching is limited to one study

### Target Learners
- Preregistration health professions students
- Postregistration health professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning
- Problem solving
- Procedural skills
