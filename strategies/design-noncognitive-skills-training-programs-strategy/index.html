Design training programs to nurture non-cognitive skills, especially for students with low GPA scores¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's recommendations call on universities and teaching staff to act on non-cognitive skills: "Design training programs for students to nurture their various non-cognitive skills which help improve their academic performance, especially in students with low GPA scores." Other recommendations include holding training sessions for teaching staff on the importance of these skills, researching staff attitudes toward them, identifying students with academic problems to provide assistance, and studying the most efficient development strategies.

Claims¶

  • Acquisition of non-cognitive skills showed only a weak, statistically insignificant correlation with GPA (r = 0.064) [+W]
  • The experimental group had the largest share of students at high levels of non-cognitive skills (80.7%), ahead of the traditional (62.1%) and control (59.3%) groups [+W]
  • An interactive training program produced statistically significant differences among three teaching methods on overall non-cognitive skills and on empathy, growth mindset and grit; post hoc tests found it beat traditional teaching, and the article does not report its comparison with a no-training control [~W]
  • Skill acquisition in the experimental group did not differ significantly by sex, academic year, or university major [+W]
  • The interactive training program did not develop planning, locus of control, or self-confidence, which the authors attribute to prior knowledge and insufficient activities [~W]
  • Post hoc comparisons showed the experimental group outperformed the traditional group, and the control group outperformed the traditional group [+W]
