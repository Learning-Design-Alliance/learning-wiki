# Design gamified activities with progressive sequenced levels through which learners advance at their own pace

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review identifies a design condition for producing motivating gamified experiences: structuring activities as a progression of levels that learners work through individually. As the authors state, "gamified activities should follow a progressive system with sequenced levels through which players can advance at their own pace". This design supports the sense of progress, achievement and self-efficacy that the review's theoretical framework links to sustained intrinsic motivation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Gamified systems must provide sequenced levels and allow self-paced advancement to produce motivating experiences
#### Constraints
- The review links this design recommendation to theoretical frameworks rather than to a directly tested design manipulation

### Target Learners
- higher education students learning a second language

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement, sense of accomplishment and intrinsic motivation

### Affordances
- Flow Theory Engagement Loop Gamification
