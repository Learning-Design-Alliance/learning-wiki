# Establish a viable simulation training site by first identifying trainee groups and course lists, then designing the environment conservatively within budget

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article lays out a stepwise site-establishment recipe: "The first step to establish a viable simulation-based airway management training site is to identify immediate and future trainees" such as medical students, residents, or attending physicians in anesthesiology, emergency, or intensive care. The second step is creating a potential course list and deciding course types and contents; the next is designing the training environment, including audio and video needs, with architects creating facility blueprints. Because budgets are usually tight, the use of funds must be very conservative, with most funds allocated for task training devices and high-fidelity simulators.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of immediate and future trainee groups before course design
- Conservative use of funds with priority allocation to task training devices and high-fidelity simulators
#### Constraints
- Budgets are usually tight; sites may need to obtain outdated or opened-but-unused supplies from operating room supply managers to maximize cost-effectiveness

### Target Learners
- medical students
- residents
- attending physicians in anesthesiology, emergency, or intensive care

### Target Learning Goals
- basic and advanced airway management skills
- fiberoptic and surgical airway training
