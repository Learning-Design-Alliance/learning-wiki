# Emotion Identification and Labeling

## Description
Learners identify, label, and match facial expressions and feelings. Categorizing feeling words by type, analyzing characters’ feelings in literature, and connecting actions with emotions help build emotional literacy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to visual aids (e.g., emotion face cards), books, and drawing materials. Facilitator guidance is needed to prompt learners to connect feelings with events and behaviors.
#### Constraints
- May oversimplify complex emotions. Requires careful facilitation to ensure learners feel safe and supported in expressing feelings. Might be challenging for learners with cognitive or communication difficulties.

### Affordances
- Enhances emotional literacy and self-awareness. Provides a foundation for higher-level social-emotional skills such as empathy and self-regulation. Supports understanding how emotions drive behavior.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted using diverse materials (pictures, books, real-life scenarios). Learners can select from a range of activities aligned with their interests and skill levels.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track accuracy in matching emotions to facial expressions and situations. Monitor the use of emotion words in daily conversations. Assess the depth and complexity of emotion analysis in literature.

## Impact
- Improved emotional literacy: Research indicates that identifying and labeling emotions enhances self-awareness and emotional regulation. Enhanced social skills: Studies show that emotional literacy is linked to better relationships and social interactions.
