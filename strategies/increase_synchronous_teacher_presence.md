# Increase Synchronous Teacher Presence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Teacher presence in synchronous (live) online sessions is the deliberate use of instructor visibility, immediacy, and interaction — greeting learners by name, narrating decisions, responding in real time, and structuring the session — so that learners experience the course as personally guided rather than self-serve. It is carried out through live video/audio, chat, polls, and shared workspaces, combined with tight session structure to limit distraction and cognitive overload.

## Design Implications

Teacher presence is one of three legs of the [Community of Inquiry](../principles/community-of-inquiry.md) framework (teaching, social, and cognitive presence), and perceived instructor presence and social presence are consistently associated with higher student satisfaction and, more moderately, with perceived learning in online courses [Richardson et al. meta-analysis](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. Presence works partly by restoring the relational cues (voice, facial expression, immediate feedback) that asynchronous formats strip away, which supports learner belonging and persistence [+M]. However, live delivery also adds extraneous load and distraction risk, so presence must be paired with [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — clear agendas, chunked segments, and purposeful media use [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reliable synchronous technology (video conference, chat, shared whiteboard) and a fallback for connectivity failures
- A visible session structure: agenda, segment timing, and interaction points ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure-presentation.md))
- Instructor willingness to show face and voice; camera-on instruction supports immediacy [+W]
- Active participation structures — polls, cold-calling with warmth, breakout tasks — rather than lecture-only delivery [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]

#### Constraints
- Lecture-heavy live sessions with no interaction produce no presence benefit and add scheduling cost [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [-S]
- Large enrollments make individual recognition impractical; presence techniques must shift to group-level (naming cohorts, public chat acknowledgment)
- Poor audio, screen-share sprawl, and multitasking invitations (long unbroken slides) increase extraneous load [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-S]
- Mandatory camera-on policies can disadvantage learners with bandwidth limits, caregiving duties, or social anxiety; presence should be instructor-led, not universally enforced [-W]
- Time-zone spread can make "synchronous" a burden for some learners; record sessions and pair with asynchronous equivalents [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **High-presence openings:** a brief [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) or personal greeting at session start builds social presence cheaply
- **Modeled sessions:** instructor uses [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) and [Modeling](../elements/modeling.md) with live narration, enacting [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) in real time
- **Interactive segments:** [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), chat-storms, and polls interleaved every 10–15 minutes
- **Coaching formats:** live [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) or office-hours blocks where the instructor works problems with individuals or small groups
- **Flipped pairing:** content delivery moved to pre-work so synchronous time is spent on interaction ([Flipped Classroom](../patterns/flipped-classroom.md))

### Target Learners
- Fully online learners at risk of isolation — first-year students, part-time and adult learners, and students new to online study [+M]
- Learners in discussion-heavy or skills-based courses where immediate feedback changes performance
- Less critical for self-regulated, experienced online learners who prefer asynchronous pacing [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Belonging and persistence: reducing attrition in online programs [+M]
- Procedural and skills instruction where live modeling and immediate corrective feedback matter
- Discussion-based and case-based objectives requiring real-time dialogue

### Instructions
1. Open with a structured [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) — one-question pulse or roll-call greeting — to establish social presence in the first five minutes.
2. Present a visible agenda and chunk the session into 10–15 minute segments alternating instructor input and learner activity ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure-presentation.md), [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md)).
3. [Model](../elements/modeling.md) the target skill live with narration, thinking aloud through decisions rather than presenting finished results ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)).
4. Interleave interaction — polls, chat responses, breakout tasks, [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) — and name learners when responding to their contributions.
5. Close with a live [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) moment or Q&A that addresses the session's sticking points, and post a brief follow-up message referencing learner contributions to extend presence beyond the live hour.

## Related Strategies
- Establish instructor presence in asynchronous channels (announcements, video feedback) so synchronous presence lands on an existing relationship
- Pair synchronous sessions with structured pre-work so live time is spent interacting, not transmitting

## Examples
- **Zoom-based seminar courses** — instructor opens camera-on, runs a two-minute check-in, alternates mini-lectures with breakout discussions, and closes by naming student contributions.
- **[Flipped Classroom](../patterns/flipped-classroom.md) online delivery** — recorded content before class; synchronous time devoted to problem-solving with the instructor circulating among breakout rooms.
- **Live office hours / coaching blocks** — scheduled small-group sessions where the instructor works through student-submitted problems in real time.
