# Design simulated-death sessions with student-non-fault scenarios, experienced instructors, and an extended debriefing that releases guilt and gives practical advice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
To guarantee a positive learning situation around manikin death, the article describes following literature recommendations: "avoiding scenarios in which death results from an action or lack of action by the student, and using an experienced instructor", plus careful prebriefing and debriefing. The debriefing includes an emotion phase, analysis, a guilt-releasing message, and concrete advice for announcing death to families.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Debriefing of not less than 30 min with an experienced instructor (more than 2 years experience)
#### Constraints
- Death should not result from an action or lack of action by the student

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in simulation sessions

### Target Learning Goals
- Managing unexpected patient death and announcing bad news
