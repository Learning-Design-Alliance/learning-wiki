# Embed the navigation activity in a historical and multimedia context using documentaries, oral presentations and cross-curricular links

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends preparing the field through history and media before the hands-on work. It suggests three named documentaries on Harrison and longitude, and states that "This activity could be combined with other forms of acquiring knowledge like giving oral presentations in history, literature or geography highlighting navigation", adding that this prepares the topic more interactively than a teacher summary alone. Guided question-and-answer prompts connect students' everyday wayfinding to open-sea navigation and the 1707 Scilly naval disaster that triggered the Longitude Act.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A computer, tablet or smartphone with internet connection to show the suggested documentaries, ideally during a preparatory lesson or at home.
#### Constraints
- The article notes it would be beneficial to embed the activity in a larger context of seafaring within geography, history or literature, and that the topic is also well suited for acting classes.

### Target Learners
- Students aged 14–19 in middle or secondary school

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding the historical longitude problem
- Explaining why determining longitude at sea was difficult for centuries
- Describing basic navigational skills of the Age of Exploration
