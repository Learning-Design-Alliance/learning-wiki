# Implement object-control and stability-skill physical programs, prioritizing lower-grade primary schoolchildren, to support Chinese and mathematics performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators and parents deliver engaging physical programs that develop specific fundamental motor skill components tied to subject outcomes. It states that "Programs focused on object control (e.g., throwing, catching, and kicking during a ball game) are necessary to boost children's performance in Chinese and mathematics", while stability-skill programs such as balance practice target mathematics specifically. Because age weakens the EF-to-mathematics path, the article advises concentrating these interventions on lower-grade children. The strategy rests on cross-sectional associations, not tested interventions in this study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should be effective, interesting, and motivating, and should target the specific FMS components linked to each subject (object control for both subjects; stability skills for mathematics).
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional, so causal benefits of the programs are not established by this study; the article recommends focusing on lower-grade children given the age moderation.

### Target Learners
- Primary schoolchildren aged 6–12 in China

### Target Learning Goals
- Chinese language achievement
- Mathematics achievement
