Use visual-spatial (diagramming) strategies rather than verbal strategies when training word problem solving in children with math difficulties¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that the visual-only diagramming condition produced the largest benefits for high-WMC children with math difficulties, the authors recommend visual strategies as the important route for strategy training. The article states: "Our findings do suggest, however, that visual strategies rather than verbal strategies are an important route for strategy training." The visual strategy taught two diagram types, one representing parts making a whole and one comparing quantities with two boxes and a question-mark placeholder for the missing number.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Strategy instruction delivered in small groups of 3 to 5 children via scripted lessons with warm-up, instruction, guided practice, and independent practice phases

Constraints¶

  • Benefits appeared for children with relatively higher WMC; "No advantages were found for children with low WMC in the strategy conditions"

Target Learners¶

  • third-grade children with math difficulties

Target Learning Goals¶

  • word problem solving accuracy
  • calculation accuracy

Claims¶

  • At high pretest WMC, the visual-only condition outperformed verbal-only, verbal+visual, and control on solution accuracy [+W]
  • At low pretest WMC, control children outperformed the strategy conditions on solution accuracy [-W]
  • Strategy training outcomes on word problem accuracy interact with working memory capacity, favoring children with higher WMC [+W]
  • The treatment x WMC interaction persists after controlling reading, calculation, and fluid intelligence [+W]
