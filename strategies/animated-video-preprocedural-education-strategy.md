# Integrate animated video education into pre-procedural patient education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that healthcare providers convert conventional pre-procedural verbal education into short simulated animated videos combining images, text, and audio-visual elements. It states providers "can consider integrating visual and interactive educational content into their patient education strategies to address uncertainty, alleviate anxiety, and improve sleep quality among individuals scheduled for medical procedures". The video is watched before the procedure alongside conventional information.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content organized into an engaging animation covering the procedure, preparation, and risks
- A viewing opportunity before the examination
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only in a specific digestive endoscopy population at one hospital with a small sample; the article notes cost-effectiveness and cultural or regional differences require further investigation

### Target Learners
- Adult patients scheduled for medical procedures, particularly digestive endoscopy

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing pre-procedural uncertainty, anxiety, and sleep disturbance through better procedural understanding
