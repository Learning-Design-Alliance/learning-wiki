# Universities combine responsible AI use guidelines, accessible technical support, AI literacy training, and assessment-embedded critical evaluation activities to address AI learning anxiety

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on the observed associations, the article recommends that "universities could operationalize these forms of support through concrete institutional strategies, particularly by developing responsible AI use guidelines and establishing accessible technical-support mechanisms". Guidelines should cover appropriate uses, disclosure, verification of AI-generated content, privacy, and academic integrity. Universities could also offer AI literacy courses, train faculty, and embed support in learning activities where students evaluate AI-generated content against assessment criteria, correct inaccuracies, and apply feedback, giving repeated practice in judging AI outputs and managing anxiety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment to guidelines, visible technical-support mechanisms, and faculty capacity to guide AI-supported learning
#### Constraints
- The article frames these as implications of observational associations; it states the design cannot establish causal effects, and findings are most directly applicable to students with prior AI-tool experience in Chinese universities

### Target Learners
- undergraduates with AI-tool experience at universities

### Target Learning Goals
- confidence in using AI, recognition of AI's educational value, and lower AI learning anxiety
