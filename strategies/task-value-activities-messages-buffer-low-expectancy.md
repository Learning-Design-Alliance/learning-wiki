# Raise attainment value for low-expectancy students and foster expectancy for low-value students, using task-value activities and messages with caution

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors advise practitioners that "there are potential gains to be achieved for students who are low in their attainment value or expectancy". They recommend efforts to raise attainment value for students whose expectancy is low and resistant to change, and efforts to foster expectancy for students whose attainment value is low, achieved through task-value activities with explicit links to achievement outcomes and teacher task-value messages.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Efforts should be directed at students low in expectancy or attainment value, especially to foster on-task engagement
#### Constraints
- Approaches helping students link achievement to esteem and self-worth "could be a risky strategy that ultimately could backfire if students employ avoidance strategies as a means of self-worth protection" and should be used with caution

### Target Learners
- Primary school students low in expectancy of success or attainment value

### Target Learning Goals
- Mathematics test performance
- On-task behavioural engagement
