# Sequence learning tasks from simple structure relaxation to substitution reactions in an interactive quantum chemistry setting

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents a progression of example tasks typical for an undergraduate chemistry curriculum: first a squeezed water molecule whose structure relaxes and whose bonds are derived from quantum calculations, then a prototypical SN2 reaction, then electrophilic aromatic substitution. Students without prior haptic experience "can use this simple example system to get acquainted with the interactive exploration of a chemical system with force feedback" before tackling reactions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- SCINE Interactive software and, optionally, a haptic pointer device; a standard laptop is sufficient
#### Constraints
- Correct overall charge must be set in the quantum chemical calculations, otherwise spurious effects occur

### Target Learners
- undergraduate chemistry students

### Target Learning Goals
- molecular equilibrium structure
- bond perception
- reaction mechanisms
- directing group effects
