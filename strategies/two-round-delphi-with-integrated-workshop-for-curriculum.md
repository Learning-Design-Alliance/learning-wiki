# Use a two-stage modified Delphi survey with an integrated workshop between the rating rounds to reach curriculum consensus

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's procedure for consensus-based curriculum development: experts rate competencies on a four-point relevance scale, discuss non-consensual or commented competencies in a facilitated online workshop, and re-rate revised items. The article states: "Our two-stage Delphi survey included two rating rounds and a workshop that occurred between them." The workshop was intended to let experts "reach a consensus more quickly"; revised competencies such as the delirium/dementia/differentiation item reached 100% agreement in round two.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experts with knowledge and experience in the subject area (here, delirium in nursing home residents)
- An a priori defined evaluation scheme with cut-off values (80% consensus; 75–80% discussed; below 75% removed)
#### Constraints
- Integrating a workshop removes expert anonymity and gave dominant personalities more weight in discussion, mitigated only by neutral moderation
- Only two rating rounds were conducted due to limited resources

### Target Learners
- Health care professionals in nursing homes (nurses and GPs)

### Target Learning Goals
- Consensus on competence-based content for delirium-specific e-learning
