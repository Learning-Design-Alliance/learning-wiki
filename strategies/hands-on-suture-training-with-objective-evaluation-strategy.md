# Combine brief hands-on suture training with objective skill evaluation during clerkship to boost surgical career motivation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-hour clerkship session in which students receive in-person instruction on single-ligature suturing, perform the task on a simulator, and receive objective performance scores, with pre/post VAS surveys of aspiration. The article concludes that "skill-assessed suture training may stimulate students' desire to become surgeons". The approach is described as "an easily implementable educational approach".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A board-certified surgeon instructor
- An objective assessment simulator such as the A-LAP mini
- Pre- and post-training VAS questionnaires
#### Constraints
- Effects on actual career choice require long-term follow-up; no control group in the supporting study

### Target Learners
- fifth-year medical students in hospital-based clerkship

### Target Learning Goals
- basic suturing skill
- motivation toward surgical careers
