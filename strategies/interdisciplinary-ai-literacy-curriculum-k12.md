# Design AI literacy curricula for K-12 as interdisciplinary short-term courses grounded in learning theory and developed with teachers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that AI literacy curricula for K-12 adopt an interdisciplinary approach, incorporating elements from computer science, mathematics, ethics, sociology, and other pertinent fields, and encompass essential AI principles, applications, and ethical considerations and security measures. It observes that existing AI education teaching units are predominantly in-person and extracurricular, often condensed into short-term courses of approximately 15 hours, and that a brief yet focused lesson may be adequate for children to grasp fundamental AI concepts. The authors stress adopting learning principles or theories to guide curriculum design and involving PreK-12 teachers in curriculum development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Grounding in an educational theoretical framework
- Involvement of PreK-12 teachers in the curriculum development process
#### Constraints
- AI education is still not a standard component in regular classrooms, including technology and computing-related courses; most units are implemented in informal settings such as labs, workshops, or after-school sessions

### Target Learners
- PreK-12 students

### Target Learning Goals
- AI literacy: knowing and understanding fundamental AI concepts, skills, and ethics; using and applying AI; evaluating and designing/co-creating with AI applications
