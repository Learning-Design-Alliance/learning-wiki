# Supported Independent Reading

## Description
Independent reading involves students selecting and reading texts at their independent reading level or texts easy enough to decode and understand without much effort. This practice allows students to apply reading strategies learned in more structured instructional contexts and fosters a love of reading. Effective independent reading includes guided choices, teaching students how to select books at an appropriate reading level, and providing time for teachers to confer with students. To maximize impact, balance student choice with appropriate text demands and align independent reading activities with skills practiced during read-aloud, shared reading, and guided reading sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A classroom library with a diverse collection of books, dedicated time for independent reading (15-30 minutes), explicit instruction on book selection and reading procedures, tools for conferencing with students, and activities to engage students with their books.
#### Constraints
- May be ineffective if there is little or no structure. Students may not make optimal book choices without guidance. Struggling readers may not improve if challenged with difficult texts. Requires careful planning and implementation to balance student choice and text demands.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Practice reading skills, develop reading fluency and comprehension, exercise choice in reading material, cultivate reading interests, build background knowledge

### Affordances
- Provides opportunities to practice reading strategies independently, fosters a love of reading through choice, increases reading fluency and comprehension, builds background knowledge across various topics, and encourages self-regulation in reading habits.

### Personalization
- Offer a variety of reading options including multicultural books of various genres and reading levels. Guide students in selecting appropriate books based on their reading level and interests. Offer a read-aloud option for non-fluent readers. Implement book talks and sharing sessions to maintain accountability and engagement.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor the quantity and quality of books read. Observe student engagement and participation in book-related activities. Track reading fluency and comprehension improvements over time. Collect feedback from students on their reading experiences and interests.

## Impact
- Studies show a correlation between time spent reading and reading achievement. Provides ready access to books, allows for autonomy, increases engagement and motivation, reduces summer reading loss, and prevents diminished reading habits at the secondary level.
