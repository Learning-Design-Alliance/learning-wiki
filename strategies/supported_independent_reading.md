# Supported Independent Reading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Supported Independent Reading (SIR) is a classroom structure in which students read self-selected texts at their independent reading level — texts they can decode and comprehend with minimal effort — during a protected block of instructional time (typically 15–30 minutes). The "supported" component distinguishes it from unstructured silent reading: teachers explicitly teach book-selection strategies, confer individually with students about their reading, and connect independent reading to strategies modeled during read-aloud, shared reading, and guided reading. The goal is voluminous, engaged reading practice that consolidates skills taught in more structured contexts.

## Design Implications

Independent reading works as a strategy only when it functions as *supported practice*, not as a substitute for instruction. Wide reading builds fluency and background knowledge, and reading volume correlates with achievement [~M]; but the correlation is largely driven by engagement and access, and unstructured "drop everything and read" time without instruction or conferring shows weak or null effects on comprehension growth [-M]. Text difficulty is the critical lever: texts at students' independent level (roughly 95%+ word recognition accuracy) allow fluent, meaning-making practice, while frustration-level texts suppress volume and comprehension [~M]. Because fluent word recognition frees cognitive resources for comprehension, easy texts are pedagogically appropriate — not "too easy" [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A diverse, well-organized classroom library spanning genres, cultures, and reading levels, with ready access during reading time
- Protected time (15–30 minutes daily or near-daily) that is not repurposed for other activities
- Explicit instruction in book selection ("five-finger rule," previewing, abandoning books strategically) and reading procedures
- A conferring routine: brief one-on-one conversations in which the teacher listens to the student read, discusses the text, and sets a goal
- Accountability structures that do not punish reading itself — book talks, reading logs, or sharing sessions

#### Constraints
- Unstructured silent reading with no conferring, feedback, or connection to instruction produces little measurable comprehension growth [-M] — the National Reading Panel (2000) found insufficient evidence for sustained silent reading alone
- Students who choose frustration-level texts (or are assigned them) read less, avoid reading, and show no fluency gains [-M]
- Conferring is skill-intensive: poorly run conferences that focus on compliance (pages read, logs signed) rather than comprehension and strategy use consume time without benefit [-W]
- Replacing explicit phonics or comprehension instruction with independent reading time harms struggling readers, who need teacher-led instruction first [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Reading Workshop** (Fountas & Pinnell; Calkins' Units of Study): mini-lesson → independent reading with conferring → mid-workshop teaching → share
- **Concept-Oriented Reading Instruction (CORI)**: independent reading embedded in a thematic science/social studies unit with explicit strategy instruction and autonomy supports [+S]
- **Scaffolded silent reading (ScSR)** (Reutzel et al.): structured silent reading with teacher-student conferences and goal setting, replacing round-robin accountability
- **Audiobook-supported reading**: non-fluent readers listen to an [audiobook](../principles/audiobooks.md) while following text, extending access to age-appropriate content

### Target Learners
- Developing readers in K–8 who have decoding skills but need volume to build fluency and stamina
- Struggling readers — *provided* texts are truly at their independent level; frustration-level choice is actively harmful [~M]
- Disengaged adolescent readers, for whom autonomy over text choice is a primary motivator [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]
- Less appropriate as a primary approach for pre-decoding students or students with severe reading disabilities without [accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md)

### Target Learning Goals
- Fluency and automaticity: volumetric practice consolidates word recognition [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M]
- Comprehension strategy application: applying skills modeled during guided reading to self-chosen texts
- Background knowledge building: wide reading across topics accumulates the knowledge base that later comprehension depends on
- Motivation and reading identity: developing intrinsic interest in reading and self-regulated reading habits [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Model selection and set expectations.** Teach students how to choose and preview books; use [choice boards](../elements/choice-boards.md) or curated bins to guide choice without eliminating it.
2. **Deliver a brief mini-lesson.** Connect the day's focus strategy to what students will do in their own books, following a [direct instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) arc: model, then release.
3. **Protect reading time.** Students read self-selected independent-level texts; the teacher confers with 3–5 students per session, using [coaching](../elements/coaching.md) moves — listening to a snippet, discussing meaning, setting one goal.
4. **Provide feedback and accountability.** Use [check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md), reading logs, or book talks so students discuss books with peers; feedback should target comprehension and strategy use, not compliance.
5. **Apply and share.** Close with a brief share session where students [apply](../elements/application-of-knowledge.md) the mini-lesson strategy and recommend books to classmates.

## Related Strategies
- Guided reading — the instructional counterpart; SIR is where guided-reading strategies are independently applied
- Read-aloud and shared reading — provide the modeled comprehension talk that SIR practice consolidates
- Reading workshop — the larger instructional structure that houses SIR

## Related Elements
- [Practice](../elements/practice.md) — SIR is distributed, self-selected reading practice; volume is the mechanism
- [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) — conferring is one-on-one coaching embedded in reading time
- [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) — sticky-note or journal responses can deepen engagement for readers ready for it
- [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md) — audiobooks, partner reading, and leveled text access for striving readers

## Examples
- **Concept-Oriented Reading Instruction (CORI)** — a research-validated program embedding independent reading in thematic inquiry units; multiple studies show gains in comprehension and engagement over traditional instruction [+S]. See [http://www.corilearning.com](http://www.corilearning.com)
- **Reading Workshop (Teachers College, Columbia University)** — [Units of Study for Teaching Reading](https://www.unitsofstudy.com/tcrr_reading.asp) structure independent reading with mini-lessons, conferring, and share sessions
- **Scaffolded Silent Reading (ScSR)** — Reutzel, Fawson, & Smith's structured alternative to SSR, with book tubs, goal setting, and scheduled conferences
