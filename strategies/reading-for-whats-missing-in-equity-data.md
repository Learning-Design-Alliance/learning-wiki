# Read equity data for what is missing: notice erased populations, non-disaggregated data, and imposed binaries

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors invite readers to pay attention to what the data doesn't tell us in charts and documents about underrepresented minorities in STEM. They give examples: Indigenous Americans are often left off statistical accounts entirely, datasets about women often do not disaggregate by race, and analyses about gender often assume a gender binary excluding nonbinary and gender expansive people. They frame this as being curious about whose stories are told and which ones are missing, and why they might be hidden from view.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The strategy requires readers to actively notice absences, because not all researchers have committed to the visibility of severe underrepresentation and inclusion
#### Constraints
- The authors note the stated argument for omitting Indigenous Americans is that they do not participate in STEM at high enough rates for statistical analyses to be possible, which the authors counter by saying that fact alone is data

### Target Learners
- physics and STEM community members reading demographic reports
- researchers and educators interpreting equity data

### Target Learning Goals
- critical reading of demographic and equity data
- recognizing intersectional erasure in statistical accounts
