# Instill the ability to recognize qualitatively treatable problems by working through examples

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A topic-selection and practice strategy: instructors favor 'taste-based topics allowing qualitative methods' and use examples to instill the skill of recognizing which problems admit qualitative treatment — what the authors call recognizing 'a smell' of qualitatively treatable problems. The article implements this by demonstrating multiple qualitative analyses across condensed matter subfields.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A curated set of worked qualitative examples spanning the target field.
#### Constraints
- Many condensed matter problems 'would not allow qualitative methods, such as e. g. energy analyses of particular microscopic conﬁgurations in solids'.

### Target Learners
- physics students
- physics professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing which problems admit qualitative treatment
- problem-selection and approach skills
