# Commission more ADHD-specific GP training, clearer pathways and adult services, and better integration between primary and secondary care

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implication-for-practice section recommends specific actions for UK primary care: increasing ADHD awareness training, commissioning clearer pathways and services with a focus on adult services and transition, better integrating primary and secondary care, and supporting patients during diagnosis. The authors state there is "a strong need for more commissioned pathways and GP specific educational programs" and that further training "could also reduce GPs' uncertainties about ADHD".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Commissioning bodies must fund ADHD-specific pathways and GP training for the recommendation to apply as described.
#### Constraints
- The article notes some barriers such as pathway complexity in the UK are harder to address than awareness and education.
- Findings are based on individual stakeholder experiences that may not map onto other stakeholders' experiences.

### Target Learning Goals
- improved identification and awareness of ADHD in primary care
- understanding ADHD referral pathways and management roles
