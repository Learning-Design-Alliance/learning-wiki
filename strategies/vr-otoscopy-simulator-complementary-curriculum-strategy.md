# Implement the validated otoscopy simulator as a complementary tool alongside traditional teaching in medical curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding the validated virtual reality otoscopy simulator into undergraduate medical education as an addition to, not a replacement for, conventional instruction. The authors state the results "encourage the use of the otoscopy simulator as a complementary tool to traditional teaching methods in a curriculum for medical students," motivated by reported training deficits and low diagnostic accuracy in ear assessment among primary care physicians. Simulator-based training offers a risk-free, error-forgiving environment with immediate objective feedback after each examination.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Face, content, and construct validity evidence should be established before implementation of the simulator into a training program, as the article states
#### Constraints
- The article notes the influence of realism on learning is discussed controversially in the literature, with studies reporting better, worse, or similar outcomes for low- versus high-fidelity simulators

### Target Learners
- medical students in their otolaryngology rotation
- otolaryngology residents

### Target Learning Goals
- correct otoscope handling and otoscopy examination technique
- diagnostic assessment of eardrum pathologies
