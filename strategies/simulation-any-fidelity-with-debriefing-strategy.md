# Use simulation at any fidelity level, with debriefing, to build beginner nursing students' communication skills and satisfaction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "It seems reasonable to recommend its use, in all degrees of fidelity (MFS, HFS), for the acquisition of competencies", since satisfaction with one's own performance favours involvement in learning. Both interventions in the study paired scenario practice with a debriefing phase — approximately 20 minutes after each 12-minute HFS case, and report-based error review in the MFS setting — and communication was the highest-rated domain in both groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each simulation session should include a debriefing phase, which the article describes as what any simulation should include.
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from second-year students in two Spanish universities; the authors note setting particularities could affect the differences found.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students in simulation subjects

### Target Learning Goals
- communication skills, team-working, decision-making and psychomotor skills in critical-care scenarios
