# Explicitly incorporate informal number skills content into early mathematics curricula and intervention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that informal number skills gains related to first-grade achievement for intervention participants, the authors recommend "creating interventions that emphasize the development and monitoring of growth in informal number skills through the explicit incorporation of informal number skills content in early mathematics curricula." They argue this may help at-risk students and potential nonresponders bridge gaps in their numerical understandings and support sustained learning gains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Combining informal number skills activities with formal number skills activities
- Informal number skills content (nonnumeral, verbal and relational tasks) embedded alongside formal number skills activities
#### Constraints
- The informal-skills association emerged only for intervention participants, not the at-risk control sample
- The supporting moderation finding appeared for first-grade achievement among intervention participants but not for the at-risk control sample, and the authors note it bears further exploration

### Target Learners
- at-risk kindergarten students and potential intervention nonresponders
- at-risk kindergarten students, including potential intervention nonresponders

### Target Learning Goals
- early number skills and long-term mathematics achievement
- informal early number skills: relational phrases, counting processes, quantity comparisons, and number words
