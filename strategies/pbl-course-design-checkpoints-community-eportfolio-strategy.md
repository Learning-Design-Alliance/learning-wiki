# Design CBR courses with progressive checkpoints, small-group tutoring, community-based learning, and e-portfolio documentation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the evaluation, the article distils effective course design incorporating PBL: arrange course content into progressive submodules (checkpoints) so students consolidate knowledge before continuing; use small group tutoring, which the study showed facilitated learning through interaction, exchange and peer feedback; make learning real and community-based through on-site visits and experiential activities beyond the classroom; and require an effective tool such as e-portfolios to report, document, and reflect on process and outcomes. The article states this protocol "offers a practical and concrete guide for course instructors to lead the students through the learning process".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators must be trained to skilfully and sensitively give feedback, and both students and instructors must be well oriented and prepared for the new mode of learning
#### Constraints
- Students may overlook essential information because of the unspecific focus of learning in PBL; institutions must be well-prepared with technical support

### Target Learners
- Future CBR practitioners in higher education health science programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning, decision-making, case conceptualisation, and transfer of theory to practice
