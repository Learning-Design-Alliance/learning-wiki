# Prediction-then-verification demonstration sequence with optional flat tuning and student-brought instruments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
When presenting the open-string spectrum as a laboratory or demonstration, the authors have "students predict the position of each successive peak before zooming in on the graph and verifying the expected frequency of the peak". For audiences less adept at math, the A string can be tuned a bit flat to 50 Hz so harmonic frequencies can be computed quickly. The authors also invite students to bring other musical instruments, which stimulates discussions of timbre, acoustics, effects pedals, synthesizers, standing waves in pipes, and voice spectrogram identification.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A sampled frequency spectrum display that can be zoomed in on during the demonstration
#### Constraints
- Tuning the A string flat to 50 Hz is offered specifically for audiences less adept at math

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students
- audiences less adept at math

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting spectrum peaks to resonant modes
- computing harmonic frequencies
