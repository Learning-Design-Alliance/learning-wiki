# Use multiple complementary simulations (a 'dose' of two) to maximize knowledge and attitude gains

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's dose-effect design shows that assigning students two different, complementary simulations produced greater knowledge and attitude gains than a single simulation. Teachers with adequate time can sequence two related simulations on the same topic area to reinforce experimental design concepts and clinical trials attitudes, exploiting repeated exposure to content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient class time for two approximately 45-minute simulation sessions plus testing sessions
- Two simulations covering different but complementary clinical trial contexts
#### Constraints
- Two simulations conferred no extra benefit for scientific possible selves or career interest, only for knowledge and clinical trials attitudes

### Target Learners
- high school Anatomy and Physiology students

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental design knowledge
- attitudes toward clinical trials
