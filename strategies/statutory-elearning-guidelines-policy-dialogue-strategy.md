# Develop statutory e-Learning guidelines through multi-stakeholder policy dialogues and build staff capacity in e-Learning content development before scaling up

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends institutionalising e-Learning through policy rather than ad hoc arrangements. "There is the need to engage relevant stakeholders at the University, ministries of health and education through policy dialogues to develop statutory e-Learning guidelines for health educational institutions of higher learning to complement existing traditional teaching and learning approaches." Supporting measures include ring-fenced ICT budgets, dedicated internet bandwidth with telecom companies, consistent training in e-Learning content development for faculty and ICT staff, and clear reporting lines for e-Learning management.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Commitment of university resources for physical and human ICT capacity, and consultation with relevant stakeholders
#### Constraints
- Limited budget space for additional ICT-related expenditure has been a major challenge of many public institutions of higher learning

### Target Learners
- healthcare trainees in public universities in resource-constrained settings

### Target Learning Goals
- quality pre-service health professional training complementing traditional teaching
