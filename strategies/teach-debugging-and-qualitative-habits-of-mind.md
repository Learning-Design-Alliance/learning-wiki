# Focus computational-modeling instruction on debugging practice, including qualitative analysis, not only correcting procedural mistakes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that instructional efforts target both procedural errors and qualitative habits of mind: "Students would be best served by learning the practice of debugging. Here, debugging includes identifying syntax errors and, more importantly, performing the type of qualitative analysis that is typically taught for solving analytic problems." Students who synthesize analytic and computational skills would be better prepared for open-ended problems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course contexts where students write and execute programs whose errors can be examined
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on post-hoc analysis of erroneous programs; the authors note this limited their ability to assess students' challenges contextualizing problems

### Target Learners
- introductory STEM undergraduates

### Target Learning Goals
- debugging programs
- qualitative analysis of physical systems
- integrating analytic and computational problem solving
