# Use responsive teaching in lab instruction: attend closely to and engage with student thinking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
All three curricula rely on instructors' close attention to student thinking during labs. The authors write that "all of our designs depend to some extent on “responsive teaching” [35], that is the lab instructors’ close attention to and engagement with student thinking, in individual and small group interactions, as well as in classroom community-level idea sharing and discussions." In practice this includes TAs listening to and supporting the substance of student ideas, treating confusion as productive, and building whole-class discussions around student findings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors or TAs with the disposition and time to attend to student ideas in individual and small-group interactions and in class-wide discussions.
#### Constraints
- The article does not report a test of this strategy's effectiveness; it is described as a shared design dependence of the three curricula.

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students working in lab groups

### Target Learning Goals
- framing lab as genuine inquiry rather than following prescribed steps
- taking up epistemic agency in experimental work
