# Systematically introduce combined 3D and 2D training, with real clinical cases, early in surgical residency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "combined 3D and 2D training, especially with real clinical cases, be systematically introduced into surgical residency programs early during training". The rationale is that 3D images improved junior residents' accuracy and speed on imaging reasoning, and residents themselves encouraged introducing case-based learning into 3D training because systematic training with real clinical cases can "aid in memorizing and understanding material and reduce the gap between theory and practice".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to 3D visualization technology (e.g., the multi-touch visualization touchscreen table with built-in cases) and attending-doctor-reviewed 3D reconstructions of real clinical CT cases
#### Constraints
- The study recommends early introduction based on a single-center trial with a limited number of participants and few female residents; the authors call for larger samples and validation with different standardized tests

### Target Learners
- junior surgical residents at the start of residency training

### Target Learning Goals
- imaging reasoning
- anatomy learning
- clinical thinking in the anatomy-imaging-surgery system
