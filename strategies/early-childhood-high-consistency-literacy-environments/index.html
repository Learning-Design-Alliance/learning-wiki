Provide rich linguistic environments with high-consistency orthographic patterns in early childhood before introducing subtler regularities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's pedagogical recommendation is to provide "rich linguistic environments during early childhood, particularly around ages 4 –6, to support statistical learning development". It suggests curricula and literacy instruction could emphasize "high-consistency patterns for younger children before introducing more subtle regularities", and that interventions for learning difficulties may be most effective when implemented early, while continued improvement into adulthood implies it is never too late to enhance these skills.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instruction must be matched to the child's developmental stage, with high-consistency input offered to the youngest learners before less predictable regularities

Constraints¶

  • The article notes its findings come from Chinese orthography and may not generalize to alphabetic writing systems; it also measured only immediate, short-term performance

Target Learners¶

  • kindergarten children aged 4–6 years
  • school-age children
  • adult literacy and second-language learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • acquisition of orthographic positional regularities
  • statistical learning of visual linguistic patterns

Claims¶

  • Rates of improvement in statistical learning differ by consistency level, with high consistency developing most rapidly, and a marked enhancement between ages 4–5 and 5–6 followed by stabilization after 8–9 years [+M]
  • Children from age 5–6 onward and adults acquired positional regularities above chance, while 4–5-year-olds extracted only high and moderate levels [+M]
  • Recognition accuracy is higher for high-consistency than moderate- and low-consistency pseudocharacters, with no significant difference between moderate and low [+M]
  • Power-function models fit the developmental trajectory better than linear models, with significant developmental change for high and moderate consistency only [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Support parents in providing a rich home literacy environment during the early years to foster emergent reading skills
