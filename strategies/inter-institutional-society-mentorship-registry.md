# Establish an inter-institutional, society-level mentorship registry providing continuous support from undergraduate entry through faculty appointment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The editorial proposes a mentorship registry, potentially housed within ACS or RSC, to fill the gap left by institution-bound programs whose "benefits do not transfer when a first-generation student moves to a new institution." It recommends formalized, accessible mentorship networks rather than "leaving guidance to chance or informal relationships," and suggests peer-led orientation programs in which senior first-generation researchers guide incoming students as an implementable step requiring no external funding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Support must persist across institutional transitions, from undergraduate entry through faculty appointment, unlike single-institution models
#### Constraints
- The authors note many existing programs share the structural limitation of being institution-bound; the University of Rochester's alumni-anchored model is described as only a partial exception

### Target Learners
- first-generation chemistry students, postdocs, and early career faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- navigating institutional expectations and professional norms
- sustained mentorship across career transitions
