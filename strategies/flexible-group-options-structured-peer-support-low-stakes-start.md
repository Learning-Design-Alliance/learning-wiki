# Offer flexible group and individual work options, structured peer-support opportunities, and low-stakes group activities to build confidence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (1 for, 5 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's teaching implications recommend that instructors support peer collaboration without forcing it: "Science teachers might consider offering flexible options for both group and individual work, recognizing that different students thrive in different settings." It further suggests that "Teachers can create structured opportunities for peer support that don't require students to initiate collaboration themselves," and that "Beginning with low -stakes group activities can help build student confidence before tackling more challenging problems." Virtual collaboration tools are also suggested as potentially valuable even in face-to-face classes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration should emphasize positive interdependence and individual accountability for effectiveness, per the article's framework discussion.
#### Constraints
- The authors note group dynamics might affect underrepresented students and that inclusive environments are needed so all students feel valued.

### Target Learners
- students in calculus-based introductory physics courses
- undergraduate STEM students

### Target Learning Goals
- physics self-efficacy
- academic performance in introductory physics
- effective peer collaboration

## Claims

- [Students who collaborated during COVID had higher grades, PISE, and physics self-efficacy than pre-COVID TWG students, while TWA students improved only in PISE](../claims/covid-twg-students-higher-than-pre-covid-twg-peers.md) [+M]
- [A greater percentage of women than men typically worked in groups, and during the pandemic women working in groups increased to 66% while men decreased to 46%](../claims/gender-composition-twa-twg-shifted-during-covid.md) [~M]
- [Men reported higher PISE and higher physics self-efficacy than women across all TWA/TWG groups and both periods, with the self-efficacy gap widest for TWA students during COVID (d ~ 0.66)](../claims/men-higher-pise-and-self-efficacy-across-groups.md) [~M]
- [No statistically significant difference in physics self-efficacy between TWA and TWG students before or during the pandemic](../claims/no-self-efficacy-difference-twa-twg.md) [~M]
- [In regression models, working alone predicted higher grades before COVID but not during COVID, while prior preparation (HS GPA, SAT Math) and physics self-efficacy were the strongest grade predictors in both periods](../claims/regression-prior-preparation-and-self-efficacy-strongest-grade-predictors.md) [~M]
- [Before COVID, students who typically worked alone earned significantly higher grades than those who worked in groups; during the pandemic the grade gap between TWA and TWG students was small and not statistically significant](../claims/twa-higher-grades-pre-covid-gap-gone-during-covid-calculus-physics.md) [~M]
