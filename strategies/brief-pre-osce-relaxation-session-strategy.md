# Offer a brief pre-assessment relaxation, breathing and positive-reinforcement session to reduce student anxiety before high-stakes performance examinations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a 30-minute preparation session combining an educational briefing, an imaginary sensory walk, mental pre-activation, normal and relaxing breathing exercises, and between-station breathing with positive reinforcement. It reduced pre-exam anxiety but did not improve grades, so it should be offered for anxiety management rather than score improvement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 30-minute slot before the examination circuit; students learn breathing and positive-reinforcement exercises to use between stations
#### Constraints
- The article found no improvement in exam scores; the full HPOT program could not be used, which the authors say may explain the null grade effect

### Target Learners
- fourth-year medical students naïve to OSCEs

### Target Learning Goals
- anxiety management before clinical performance assessment
