# Exclude cognate-containing questions from subject competence tests and ask test-takers for the most frequent translation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend two adjustments to AI-generated subject competence tests: removing cognate items from assessment use and reframing answer instructions toward usage frequency. "To mitigate the impact of cognates, we suggest excluding questions containing them from the subject assessment tests." Cognate-heavy questions may instead serve as instructional tools introducing generalist post-editors to specialized vocabulary. They also suggest instructing test-takers "to select not just the correct translation, but the best and most frequent translation, considering usage frequency", aligning tests with real-world translation decision-making.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Subject competence tests intended to prevent guessing without genuine domain knowledge
#### Constraints
- Cognate frequency in tests may reflect genuine characteristics of specialized engineering vocabulary, so blanket exclusion needs balancing against domain realism

### Target Learners
- External linguists modeled by language service providers; translation students in situated learning contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate assessment of subject-area terminology competence; awareness of usage-frequency variation among valid translations
