# Implementation practices for adopting preclass reading guides in college science courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors offer four recommendations for instructors adopting reading guides: expect significant development time and align guides with intended reading; change class sessions to hold students accountable by quizzing definitions rather than restating content; do not post completed guides, since "we fear that students may choose to simply study the completed document without actually reading the textbook"; and incorporate guides into in-class activities, such as having students compare their guide work with each other.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A significant time commitment to develop guides matched to the chosen textbook
- Class time reallocated away from defining terms and basic content delivery toward application activities such as problem-based learning, case studies, or primary literature analysis
#### Constraints
- Results come from a single large R1 university, a majors course, one textbook, and a 10-week quarter system; replication at other institution types is needed

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in large-enrollment college science courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Preclass preparation through textbook reading and daily accountability for course content

## Claims

- [Most students in a large-enrollment introductory biology course completed at least some of the optional preclass reading guides, with about half completing the entire guide before class each day](../claims/high-preclass-reading-guide-completion-rates.md) [+M]
- [Full completion of preclass reading guides before class is significantly positively correlated with exam performance in a large-enrollment introductory biology course, controlling for demographic and aptitude factors](../claims/reading-guide-completion-exam-performance-correlation.md) [+M]
- [About 70% of students accurately self-reported reading guide completion via daily clicker questions, with roughly 15% overreporting and 15% underreporting, and the headline correlation survives the projected 30% error](../claims/reading-guide-self-report-accuracy-validation.md) [+M]
- [Reading guides were used similarly across student groups by gender, ethnicity, and aptitude, with demographics contributing to completion in few cases and inconsistently](../claims/reading-guide-use-equitable-across-student-groups.md) [+M]
