# Improve routine IPV inquiry processes and enforce disability-specific routines through targeted training and practical tools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that formal support institutions strengthen provider capacity through two linked actions: improving routine IPV inquiry so women with disabilities are routinely asked about IPV, and training providers to actually implement existing disability-specific routines. It states that "Formal support institutions need to improve routine IPV inquiry processes to ensure that women with disabilities are routinely asked whether they have been subjected to IPV." The recommendation follows directly from the survey findings of low inquiry rates and low routine use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Service providers need targeted training and practical tools that focus on implementing specific routines, as the article's conclusion states
#### Constraints
- The article notes the relatively low response rate (32.9%) may affect generalizability of the underlying findings

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals
- Social services professionals
- Police officers

### Target Learning Goals
- Routinely asking about IPV
- Identifying signs of IPV
- Applying disability-specific IPV support routines
