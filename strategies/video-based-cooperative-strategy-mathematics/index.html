Employ video-based cooperative instructional strategy to improve secondary mathematics performance¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that the cooperative video group significantly outperformed competitive, individualized, and conventional groups, the article recommends that mathematics teachers use video-based cooperative instruction. It states: "Teachers at secondary school level should expose mathematics students to video -based cooperative instructional strategy so as to improve their performance in mathematics." Supporting recommendations include government and stakeholder workshops on the strategy, improved teacher education programmes for innovative technology use, and development of relevant instructional packages for Nigerian schools.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need training; the article recommends workshops on video-based cooperative learning and improved teacher education programmes

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from a single six-week study in four purposively selected computer-equipped schools in Minna, Nigeria

Target Learners¶

  • senior secondary school mathematics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving students' performance in mathematics, especially geometry

Claims¶

  • The video-based cooperative group outperformed competitive, individualized, and conventional groups on geometry post-test scores [+W]
  • Video-based instructional strategy produces significant post-test performance differences among cooperative, competitive, individualized, and conventional groups in geometry [+W]
  • Gender has no influence on performance of students taught geometry with the video-based cooperative strategy [+W]
