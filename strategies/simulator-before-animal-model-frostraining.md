# Train for fetoscopic OSB repair on a high-fidelity simulator before animal models to reduce animals needed and improve efficiency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend simulator-based training as a step preceding animal-model and clinical training: 'We expect that training on a simulator before using animal models will reduce the number of animals needed to achieve surgical competency, thereby enhancing ethical practices and improving efficiency in skill development and clinical translation.' The strategy uses the validated silicone FROST simulator with CUSUM-monitored progress against composite success criteria.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Use in well-established foetal centres already proficient in open OSB surgery, as the authors strongly advocate
- CUSUM control limits and success criteria must be preset (failure probabilities of 18% and 30%, acceptable deviation of 5%)
#### Constraints
- The article states the superiority of a highly realistic model over a low-realistic model is not definitive
- Direct comparison between fetoscopic and robotic approaches was impossible with this training model

### Target Learners
- foetal surgery fellows and residents
- maternal-fetal medicine and neurosurgery consultants

### Target Learning Goals
- robot-assisted and fetoscopic open spina bifida repair skills
- surgical competency verification before live-tissue or clinical cases
