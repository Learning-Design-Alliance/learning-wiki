# Use participatory action research with a commitment step to develop and sustain preceptor education programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends developing preceptor education through PAR, in which researchers solve problems with participants rather than on them, and having participants make a commitment before training. The authors state that having participants make a commitment before receiving training could help them perform assigned tasks more responsibly, and that PAR enabled problem-solving through fundamentally criticizing habitual work methods via reflection. In this study, preceptors pledged commitment within the PAR team and with fellow preceptors, an experience they had not had in existing preceptor education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators to support participants' reflective activity
- Weekly meetings where participants reflect on and discuss their experiences
#### Constraints
- The authors note it was difficult to reach consensus in the PAR planning stage because nurse educators were accustomed to top-down educational content approaches

### Target Learners
- preceptor clinical nurses
- clinical nurse educators

### Target Learning Goals
- preceptor role competence
- responsible engagement in training
- reflective practice
