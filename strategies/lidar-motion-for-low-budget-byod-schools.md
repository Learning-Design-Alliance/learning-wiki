# Adopt LiDAR Motion as a low-cost motion-graphs option for schools with iPad or personal-device policies and for home learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends placing LiDAR Motion among the viable technology options for teaching motion graphs because it is "particularly applicable to schools that have policies that include iPads or permit the use of personal devices, but may not have the budget for specialized science equipment." The authors also present it as applicable where students need to learn from home, supported by the randomized comparison against ultrasonic rangers and by students' stated preference for the app.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to LiDAR-equipped iPads or iPhones and a 45-60 minute paired, inquiry-based laboratory protocol with graph-matching tasks
#### Constraints
- Study participants had substantial prior graph-reading knowledge; the authors recommend future work with a more general audience or students around ages 12-14, or more difficult questions for upper-level students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate non-major physics students
- secondary students just learning algebra (suggested by the authors for future studies)

### Target Learning Goals
- graphing and mathematical modeling of linear motion
