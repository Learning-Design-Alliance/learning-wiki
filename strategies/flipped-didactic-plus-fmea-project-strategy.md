# Couple a flipped-classroom didactic rotation with a practice-based FMEA project and ongoing incident learning participation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The program delivers didactic content through recorded lectures reviewed in advance — "A “flipped classroom” arrangement was used whereby the lectures were recorded, and the resident reviewed them in advance" — enabling richer in-person discussion. This is coupled with project-based learning: "During the one‐month rotation each resident is expected to complete one project in one of the topic areas in Table 1," often an FMEA of a clinical process involving dosimetrists, physicians, and therapists. Learning is assessed via quizzes, faculty presentations, written reports, and an RCA presentation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recorded lectures or borrowed content (e.g., AAPM virtual library, low-cost screen-capture software) are needed to deliver the didactic component; at least three physics faculty must attend end-of-rotation presentations.
#### Constraints
- The one-month rotation was found to be "a very compressed time scale" and will be extended to 1.5 months; some programs may struggle to set aside rotation time from a 24-month program.

### Target Learners
- medical physics residents

### Target Learning Goals
- patient safety concepts, FMEA competency, incident learning and root-cause analysis
