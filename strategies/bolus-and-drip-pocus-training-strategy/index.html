Adopt a 'bolus and drip' longitudinal training paradigm combining structured didactic material with frequent practical scanning sessions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review describes "a 'bolus and drip' approach" that "effectively integrat[es] structured didactic materials ('bolus') with practical scanning sessions ('drip') to facilitate long‐term competency". It aligns with established ALS training practices where brief, frequent educational interventions have demonstrated enhanced efficacy in CPR performance and skill retention.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Ongoing access to scanning practice opportunities after the initial didactic 'bolus'

Constraints¶

  • The review states comparative effectiveness studies between this approach and traditional extended-duration training are needed to establish definitive evidence-based recommendations

Target Learners¶

  • novice practitioners and healthcare trainees in PoCUS programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustained point-of-care ultrasound competency over time

Claims¶

  • Hands-on practice integration is associated with better retention in Indication and Medical decision-making domains, with a less pronounced difference in Interpretation [+W]
  • PoCUS competency declines across all I-AIM domains over 1–12 months, with Acquisition showing the most pronounced decline (median −11.8%) [+W]
  • Short-course PoCUS programs (≤4 h) show greater competency decline (median −11.8%) than long courses (>4 h; median −2.6%) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Brief initial training with extensive follow-up coaching and on-site facilitation for whole-school program implementation
  • Implement Classtime with a kick-off training day plus ongoing grade-specific follow-up sessions
