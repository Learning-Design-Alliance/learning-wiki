# Support engagement particularly in academic subjects by incorporating exploration and learning by doing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' conclusion states that "engagement should be supported particularly in academic subjects which do not lend themselves as easily to exploration and learning by doing." Because lesson-to-lesson variation was largest within students and tied to subject, and higher engagement occurred in subjects emphasizing learning by doing (home economics) or exploring and experimenting (physics), teachers should foster active participation and hands-on, exploratory tasks in academic lessons, and actively support lower achieving students' engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional designs that allow active participation and effort, such as exploration, experimenting, and learning by doing
#### Constraints
- The article's support is correlational evidence from subject contrasts, not a test of an intervention

### Target Learners
- lower secondary school students, especially lower achieving students

### Target Learning Goals
- situational engagement and reduced disaffection in academic lessons
