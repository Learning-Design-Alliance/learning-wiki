# Incorporate support groups into intervention programmes for latent-phase children affected by HIV/AIDS

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that structured support groups for parents and children be built into psychosocial intervention programmes for HIV-affected families. "The findings suggest that the use of support groups should be incorporated into intervention programmes dealing with latent-phase children affected by HIV/AIDS to enhance adaptive behaviour." They argue that "Support groups for parents, group sessions for children and group activities are the most powerful interventions in a non -medical context", addressing communication, coping and problem-solving skills for children displaying non-adaptive behaviour, and providing strong adult and peer attachments reported in resilience research as a protective factor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Group sessions for children and parents, ideally with trained facilitators and culturally tailored content.
#### Constraints
- Evidence base in this article is a single-site quasi-experimental study relying on mother-reported outcomes.

### Target Learners
- Latency-phase children (6–10 years) affected by maternal HIV/AIDS and their mothers

### Target Learning Goals
- Enhance adaptive behaviour and resilience
- Communication, coping and problem-solving skills

### Affordances
- [Forsyth Resilience Conceptual Model](../designs/forsyth-resilience-conceptual-model.md)
