Strategies

Use universal teacher training that targets teacher perceptions and skills with parents to promote parent involvement at scale¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends training teachers to assess their perceptions of parents and students and equipping them with skills for parent interactions, because teachers interact with entire classrooms of students and parents each year. It argues this "has the potential to foster positive youth development more efficiently than working with one parent at a time," and that universal teacher training programs like IY TCM may complement efforts to improve academic and social outcomes for all students, including those with the greatest need.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training content that addresses teacher bias and builds empathy for children and parents
  • Explicit instruction and practice to support improvements in teacher-parent interactions

Constraints¶

  • The study design did not allow testing mechanisms between intervention, teacher perceptions, and student outcomes
  • For youth at highest risk, working with parents alone may not be enough to improve school success

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary school teachers
  • Students with academic and behavior problems and their families

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Promote parent involvement in education
  • Foster adaptive teacher expectations of students and parents

Claims¶

  • Four parent involvement profiles emerged, and the Low Contact/Low Comfort profile had the worst academic and behavioral correlates [+W]
  • IY TCM intervention teachers were more likely to rate parents in the most adaptive involvement profiles at follow-up compared to control teachers [+W]
  • Progression to more adaptive involvement profiles was more likely in the intervention group and digression more likely in the control group [+W]
  • Low contact alone was not associated with adverse student outcomes; the Low Contact/High Comfort profile had the most favorable correlates [+W]
  • The Low Contact/Low Comfort profile was more likely to contain parents of FRL and African American students [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Support teachers' relational pedagogy through reflection and corrective experiences to prevent conflict escalation and emotional exhaustion
