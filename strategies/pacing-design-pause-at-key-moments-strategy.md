# Future pacing design should explore pausing at key moments rather than evenly slowing visuals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article identifies pacing as an open design question for accessible video: its D-Slowdown idea (extending on-screen time of fast-moving visuals) produced no significant TLX changes, yet participants still requested slower visuals in other clips. The authors conclude that "further research is needed on pacing design; for example, pausing at key moments may yield different effects than evenly slowing down", and one participant suggested speed should correspond to the video's content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pacing adjustments should be tied to content type rather than applied uniformly
#### Constraints
- Evidence for pacing effects is limited to three clip pairs in one AR video with 16 participants; the null result does not establish equivalence

### Target Learners
- Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading captions and viewing visuals with sufficient time
- Reducing temporal pressure in video learning
