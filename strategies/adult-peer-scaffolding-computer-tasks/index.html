Adult and peer scaffolding to sustain young children's interest and persistence with computer tasks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teachers and parents actively support children's computer use by designing meaningful activities, helping solve technical problems, and scaffolding persistence. It states that "Having an adult (or a peer) to scaffold their learning and show them ways to persist o n a task will enhance children's academic and socioemotional development". This addresses the finding that access alone does not sustain interest, since very difficult tasks discourage young children.

Claims¶

  • Mere home access to computers did not sustain young children's interest, which declined for home-only users [+W]
  • Children in the seven-month computer intervention reported more positive computer attitudes post-intervention, including doubled confidence [+W]
  • Playful embodied robot sessions sustained young children's attention well beyond the planned session length, and children voluntarily invited peers into one-on-one play [+W]
