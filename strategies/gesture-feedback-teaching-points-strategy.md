# Use gesture-level feedback as specific, actionable teaching points in surgical trainee instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating gesture assessment into surgical education: "gestures can provide immediate explainable, and actionable teaching points that can be imparted to trainees," and "understanding how expert surgeons select certain gestures can guide the development of training protocols for novice surgeons and trainees." Because gestures deconstruct operations into moment-to-moment instrument–tissue interactions, instructors can give trainees concrete, discrete feedback rather than only broad procedural ratings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video recording of the dissection step and trained annotators applying the gesture classification system
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from apical dissection of RARP with a relatively small sample; the authors call for future work linking gestures to machine learning-based assessment and real-time teaching points

### Target Learners
- novice surgeons and trainees in robotic surgery

### Target Learning Goals
