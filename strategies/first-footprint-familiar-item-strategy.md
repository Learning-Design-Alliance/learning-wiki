# Anchor a first product footprint in an item students know well, with a published study to draw on, and break the product into staged categories

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For the first full product footprint, the article recommends starting with an item students know well so they can apply their understanding of the processes involved, and working from a high quality published study with plenty of direct measurements. The product is broken down by asking "How is the product made? Where to the parts or inputs come from? What do we include in the footprint?", and computations are done in teams focused on individual processes in a cloud-shared spreadsheet with comments and citations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a high quality published study with plenty of direct measurements for the chosen item
- Cloud-shared documents and spreadsheets so students can quickly share tips, work, and sources
#### Constraints
- The process is highly customized to the product or process, which the author calls a strength of process-based assessment

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in an energy course

### Target Learning Goals
- performing a complex process-based carbon footprint calculation
- identifying significant contributions to a product's carbon emissions
