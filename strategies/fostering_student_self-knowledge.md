# Fostering Student Self-Knowledge

## Description
Fostering student self-knowledge involves implementing measures that help students understand themselves as learners. By reflecting on their strengths, areas for growth, and learning preferences, students can become more effective self-advocates. Strategies include using learning interest inventories, exit tickets, progress monitoring, and peer/self-assessments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to self-assessment tools, a supportive classroom environment, and strategies to help students articulate their needs.
#### Constraints
- Requires time and effort from both students and educators to cultivate self-awareness and effective communication skills. Students may initially resist taking responsibility for their learning or feel uncomfortable sharing their needs.

### Affordances
- Enhances students' ability to recognize their strengths and needs, encourages them to take responsibility for their learning, and prepares them for success in various environments by promoting independence and problem-solving skills.

### Personalization
- IEPs and transition meetings can become more student-centered, and students can be supported in participating in real-world learning opportunities where they can practice self-advocacy skills.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe students' willingness to participate in discussions, request help when needed, and articulate their learning preferences. Measure students' ability to set goals, solve problems, and advocate for themselves.

## Impact
- Research indicates that self-knowledge leads to improved academic outcomes and success in post-secondary education, the workforce, and civic life. Students who self-advocate are better equipped to navigate challenges and make informed decisions about their learning and lives.
