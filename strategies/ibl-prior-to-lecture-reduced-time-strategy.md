# Use IBL material prior to traditional lecture to reduce lecture time and increase learner satisfaction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend deploying well-designed Internet-based material before or alongside classroom instruction: "Use of IBL material prior to TCL could result in reduced lecture time, increased learner satisfaction to participate during instruction." They also suggest exploring blended approaches combining asynchronous and synchronous formats for Internet-based CME delivery, and note learners can set their own time and place to learn, saving learners and institutions time and money.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The IBL material must be well designed and tailored to the job profile of the health-care professionals using it.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from an exploratory study of one clinical topic in one country, and the authors call for research on effects on subsequent physician practices and behavior.

### Target Learners
- postgraduate physicians and medical trainees in CME programs

### Target Learning Goals
- efficient acquisition of clinical guidelines knowledge
