# Refine expert-consensus mnemonics with objective methods such as the Delphi technique and evaluate them on process and outcome measures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future refinements of consensus-developed mnemonics use more objective development methods and that the tool be evaluated empirically. They write that "future refinements of this mnemonic would benefit from more objective development methods (i.e. Delphi technique)" and that "Future studies will assess whether CPADS-30 can improve important process measures such as time-to-defibrillation or important outcome measures such as ROSC or survival to hospital discharge". This pairs a development recommendation with an evaluation plan.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- access to a panel of clinical content experts for structured consensus methods
- resuscitation process and outcome data to evaluate whether the mnemonic improves time-to-defibrillation, ROSC, or survival to hospital discharge
#### Constraints
- the mnemonic itself has not yet been empirically evaluated

### Target Learners
- developers of resuscitation training tools and mnemonics

### Target Learning Goals
- improving the design and empirical validation of cognitive aids for resuscitation task prioritization
