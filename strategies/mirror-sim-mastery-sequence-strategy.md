# Sequence mirror simulation within a mastery learning curriculum to reinforce flexible decision-making after fixed ACLS skills are established

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend incorporating in situ mirror simulation into a broader mastery learning curriculum: it "initially introduces learners to assume team leadership, and later utilizes in situ mirror simulation to enhance and reinforce flexible decision-making capabilities." Because confidence gains concentrated in flexible, patient-tailored skills rather than fixed protocol skills, the strategy positions mirror simulation as a later-stage adjunct rather than a replacement for initial ACLS instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must first have baseline team-leadership and ACLS protocol training
- Access to a vacant unit room and mirrored patient data
#### Constraints
- Confidence gains were limited to flexible skills; fixed skills showed no significant change in this program

### Target Learners
- PGY-3 internal medicine residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Flexible patient-tailored decision-making during cardiac arrest
- Team leadership reinforcement

## Claims

- [In situ mirror simulation significantly increases PGY-3 resident confidence in flexible, patient-tailored cardiopulmonary arrest skills such as interpreting end-tidal CO2, ruling out reversible causes, and post-resuscitative care](../claims/mirror-sim-confidence-flexible-skills.md) [+W]
- [In situ mirror simulation significantly increases confidence in the non-technical skill of performing a debrief, but not in fixed skills such as ACLS protocol knowledge or team-leader role assignment](../claims/mirror-sim-debrief-gain-fixed-skills-null.md) [+W]
- [Most PGY-3 residents (83% of 2023–2024 participants) endorsed practice changes, and free-text feedback highlighted debriefing, patient mirroring, and the in situ location as most valuable](../claims/mirror-sim-practice-change-qualitative.md) [+W]
- [Residents rated the mirror simulation program highly for perceived benefit and interest, averaging 3.87 and 4.08 out of 5](../claims/mirror-sim-benefit-interest-ratings.md) [+W]
- [PGY-3 team leaders averaged 65% on a graded ACLS checklist, with most leaders assigning compressions and airway roles but fewer assigning IV access or medication administration](../claims/mirror-sim-checklist-performance.md) [~W]
