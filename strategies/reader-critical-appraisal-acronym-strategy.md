# Use the READER acronym to structure critical appraisal of a research article and its applicability to the reader's own practice context

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A critical-reading examination task teaches appraisal through the READER acronym: 'Use the acronym READER (Relevance, Education, Applicability, Discrimination, Evaluation and Reaction) to analyse this article's applicability to your own context (take-home message)'. The model answer demonstrates each step on a real cross-sectional study of COVID-19 intubation practices, judging relevance (the selection bias 'skews the findings towards an urban, tertiary, specialist-care environment'), education, applicability to district resources, discrimination (internal validity compromised by selection and measurement bias), evaluation (unlikely to change policy but useful for arguing further research) and reaction ('this is of some interest', prompting local resource review and context-specific guideline development). The strategy trains evidence-based practice judgement tied to one's own setting rather than abstract critique.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An article to appraise and a mark allocation guiding response length
- Candidates instructed to use their own words rather than copy from the article
#### Constraints
- The reaction step is explicitly subjective, dependent on the reader's own perspective and practice scenario; the worked example is written from the perspective of a family physician in the South African district health system

### Target Learners
- Family medicine registrars preparing for the critical-reading component of a fellowship examination

### Target Learning Goals
- Critical appraisal of observational studies
- Judging the transferability of research findings to one's own practice context
