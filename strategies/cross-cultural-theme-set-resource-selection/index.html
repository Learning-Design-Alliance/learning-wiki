Select theme-set resources that appeal cross-culturally and present content on students' terms¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends choosing supplemental resources that students can relate to and that appeal across cultures. Readings reflecting only Western rhetorical ideals can alienate students familiar with other communication strategies, so instructors should present "texts that reflect the demographics and psychographics of the various cultures that comprise each class." Including popular media such as YouTube presents content on students' terms, since web resources are a common denominator for most audiences.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors must know the cultural composition of their class to select relevant resources

Constraints¶

  • Resources reflecting only Western rhetorical ideals may alienate students from other communication traditions

Target Learners¶

  • linguistically diverse first-year composition students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • equitable access to course readings
  • personal connection with course content

Claims¶

  • Multimodal theme-sets improve L2 students' comprehension of core concepts in unit surveys [+W]
  • Multimodal theme-sets increase L2 student engagement with assigned readings in mainstream composition courses [+W]
  • Native speakers in theme-set courses tend to ignore the primary text while engaging supplemental multimodal materials [-W]
  • Multimodal theme-sets foster more balanced, multicultural small-group and whole-class discussion [+W]
  • In theme-set courses, L2 students outperform native-speaking peers on unit surveys, reversing the control-group gap [+W]
