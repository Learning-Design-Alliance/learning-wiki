# Integrate AI-based teaching platforms with human instructors and rigorous validation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's ethical and social analysis argues that AI-based teaching platforms cannot replicate the affective component of feedback and "struggle to understand the current emotional and cognitive state of the learner". It recommends that such platforms "should be integrated into multifactorial educational systems" and undergo rigorous validation through expert opinion and multi-institutional studies assessing transfer of expertise to real-life scenarios, with optimal participation of human instructors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Expert-opinion validation, multi-institutional studies of transfer to real operative settings, and human instructor involvement
#### Constraints
- Automated feedback can be ineffective or detrimental if the learner feels disconnected from the instructor; the optimal frequency of algorithm updating is unknown

### Target Learners
- Surgical and medical trainees using AI-assisted simulation training

### Target Learning Goals
- Transfer of simulated psychomotor expertise to real-life operative performance
