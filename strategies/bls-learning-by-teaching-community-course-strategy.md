# Use learning-by-teaching of BLS to community health professionals as a curricular activity combining student learning with knowledge dissemination

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy embeds a learning-by-teaching activity in the medical curriculum: after BLS training, students in groups of four to nine "taught BLS to groups of 10 to 25 people in the community in two-shift courses of four hours each", preparing posters and booklets and having autonomy in course preparation. This simultaneously deepened students' knowledge and skills and disseminated BLS to underserved health professionals, serving the university's social accountability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students first receive structured BLS training (26 h of theoretical classes and simulations) and an initial practical assessment; community participants volunteer and work where students practice.
#### Constraints
- Non-randomized class-level assignment was used because randomization within curricular classes was not possible; community attendance oscillated across stages due to work and holiday factors.

### Target Learners
- second-term medical students
- community health professionals and laymen

### Target Learning Goals
- basic life support and cardiopulmonary resuscitation knowledge and skills
- teaching competence
