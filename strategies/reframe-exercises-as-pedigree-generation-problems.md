# Reframe typical biology exercises as complex problems by prompting students to generate and compare possible scenarios, using clickers and Peer Instruction to foster discussion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends transforming typical exercises into complex problems: instead of calculating the probability a son will have hemophilia, students are asked to "Generate a possible pedigree for three generations showing unaffected, affected, and carrier individuals in a pedigree for hemophilia. Share your pedigree with your neighbor. How are the two pedigrees different?" This invites students to generate possible scenarios by applying knowledge of a generalized system to a specific application. Such problems can be presented as in-class clicker questions, and "multiple-choice clicker questions that require higher-order thinking" can engage students in complex problem solving.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clickers are easy to incorporate, especially in large classes, and provide immediate feedback to both students and instructor
#### Constraints
- Some may view multiple-choice clicker questions as limited, since the potential answers are defined

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology students, including those in large introductory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- complex problem solving, applying generalized knowledge to specific applications, formative assessment
