# Open-ended team project with hardware criteria, week-by-week task allocation, and a public project show with industry evaluators

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy implements the open-ended phase: student groups of 4-5 propose and build an embedded/IoT system solving a self-defined real-life problem, subject to criteria such as using at least two sensors, a wireless data transfer system, at least one motor, and multiple communication protocols. The article states students "embarked on a project-based learning experience (intended outcomes of CO2 and CO3)", and that criteria include that they "Must use at least two sensors (analog or digital) to develop the system, a wireless data transferring system... and at least one motor". Work is allocated week by week (literature review and proposal in week 7, weekly updates in weeks 8-11, finalization in week 12), and the project culminates in a project show where "industry experts and faculty members participate as evaluators".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Every group member must contribute to hardware assembling and their respective code
- The project must have at least four major features matching the 4-5 member group size
- Projects must be budget-constrained and cost-effective, with microcontroller selection justified by estimated I/O needs
#### Constraints
- The project accounts for 50% of the final grade, with the final week carrying 30 marks (60% of project marks)
- Use of RPi is optional due to its high cost

### Target Learners
- 8th-semester undergraduate Computer Science and Engineering students working in teams of 4-5

### Target Learning Goals
- Solving self-defined real-life complex engineering problems
- Literature review, critical analysis and project management
- Interpersonal communication and presenting to industry and society
