# Learner-Centric Approach

## Description
A learning approach where the problem is presented as an open-ended one, so there are several ways to solve it, with each solution leading to a different set of outcomes. Learners must understand the problem and try to find the best solution. In the process, they develop a strong learning foundation, develop, build, and apply newly-acquired skills, experience both success and failure, learn to think in more ways than one, and find innovative ways to counteract existing problems

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An open-ended problem, access to resources, and a supportive environment that encourages exploration and risk-taking.
#### Constraints
- May require more time and resources compared to traditional methods. Requires careful facilitation to ensure learners stay on track and achieve learning outcomes.

### Affordances
- Promotes deeper understanding, critical thinking, and real-world application of knowledge. Increases engagement and motivation by allowing learners to explore different solutions and learn from both success and failure.

### Personalization
- The open-ended nature of the problem allows learners to tailor their approach and solutions based on their unique skills, knowledge, and interests. This can also be adapted based on the learners' skill levels.
