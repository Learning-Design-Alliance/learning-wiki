# Self-Monitoring of Performance (SMP)

## Description
Self-monitoring of performance is a strategy where learners monitor an aspect of their academic performance, such as the rate at which they correctly complete class work or the overall accuracy of their performance. It is useful for building fluency and encourages learners to self-assess their behavior and record the results. It increases or decreases the frequency, intensity, or duration of existing behavior.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a defined academic behavior to monitor, a method for recording performance (e.g., a tally sheet), and a clear understanding of performance expectations.
#### Constraints
- May not create new academic skills, but rather improves the application of existing skills. Requires willing participation from the learner for successful implementation.

### Affordances
- Provides immediate feedback to learners, facilitates self-regulation, and promotes learner engagement. Saves instructors time by shifting monitoring responsibility to the learner.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted with personalized self-monitoring forms to reflect the learner's age, developmental level, and individual needs.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track rate of work completion and accuracy of performance. Compare data before and after implementation to gauge effectiveness. Monitor consistency of self-monitoring procedures.

## Impact
- Studies demonstrate positive changes in completion of written classroom work, academic engagement, and math fluency. (Gumpel & Shlomit, 2000; Hughes et al., 2002; McDougall & Brady, 1998; Rock, 2005; Wehmeyer et al., 2003)
