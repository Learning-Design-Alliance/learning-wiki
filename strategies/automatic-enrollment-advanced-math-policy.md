# Adopt automatic-enrollment policies that default students into higher-level math based on prior achievement or demonstrated proficiency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report recommends that states address disparities in advanced math participation through default placement programs. It states that "forward-thinking states have implemented default placement programs that automatically enroll students in higher-level math courses based on prior achievement or demonstrated proficiency on standardized assessments." After North Carolina passed House Bill 986, approximately 8,000 students gained access to advanced math and were placed up from regular or remedial math, and Illinois introduced similar legislation for 2024-25 including automatic enrollment and teacher diversity goals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prior achievement or demonstrated proficiency on standardized assessments to determine automatic placement
- State-level legislation or policy authorizing default placement into higher-level math courses
#### Constraints
- The report presents these as policy approaches adopted by states, not as evaluated interventions with measured causal effects

### Target Learners
- High-achieving Black and Latino high school students
- Students from low-income backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- Access to and success in advanced mathematics coursework leading to college and STEM opportunity
