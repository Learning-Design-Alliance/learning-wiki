# Direct Instruction: Patterning

## Description
Direct instruction of patterning skills involves teaching learners to identify and create patterns using various mediums such as colors, sizes, shapes, sounds, and numbers. This includes copying and extending patterns, abstracting patterns by creating the same pattern with different objects, and identifying the pattern's rule (the repeating part). Explicit instruction with alphabet labels (e.g., 'ABB pattern') helps learners generalize pattern rules.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials such as colored blocks, shapes, sounds, or numbers. Explicit instruction time to introduce pattern concepts, practice activities, and feedback.
#### Constraints
- Without proper guidance, learners might focus on surface-level characteristics of patterns rather than understanding underlying rules. May not be as effective if learners are not engaged in creating patterns themselves.

### Target Learners

- Primarily young learners (preschool to elementary school) but adaptable to all ages as a foundation for mathematical and logical thinking.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Develop skills in identifying, copying, extending, and abstracting patterns. Enhance understanding of pattern rules and regularity, fostering mathematical reasoning.

### Affordances
- Enhances mathematical reasoning, supports development of problem-solving skills, and improves general math knowledge by identifying rules and regularities. Early pattern knowledge can predict later math achievement.

### Personalization
- Can be personalized by using different types of patterns (color, shape, number) and adjusting complexity based on learner level. Scaffolding can include alphabet labels to explain pattern rules.
