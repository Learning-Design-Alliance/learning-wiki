# Guide peer cooperation so children assist each other in fine motor operations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
During activities children who could not finish an operation alone invited more capable peers to help, for example one opening tape while another cut or wrapped the paper ball. The article states that "in later period of teaching practice, in addition to children’s free combination, teachers would consciously guide children helping each other and improve their fine motor skills level in mutual assistance", so cooperation became a deliberate part of the intervention rather than only spontaneous.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks must contain operations genuinely difficult for one child (e.g., cutting and handling wrapped clear tape) so that cooperation is functional
#### Constraints
- The article notes some children would go to teachers for help rather than peers, so teachers consciously guided mutual assistance in the later period

### Target Learners
- children aged 4 to 5 in kindergarten middle classes

### Target Learning Goals
- fine motor operation skills developed through mutual assistance, plus cooperation and friendship
