# Position music education as a supplementary, not primary, method for enhancing clinical auditory skills in medical training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on weak direct relationships between musical aptitude and percussion competency, the authors recommend incorporating music as an elective subject within the medical curriculum while cautioning that it should be "a supplementary, rather than primary, method for enhancing clinical auditory skills". They suggest structured music training could complement traditional clinical training by developing auditory perception abilities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration as an elective alongside traditional clinical training, not as a replacement
#### Constraints
- The limited explanatory power of MBEA scores and the modest effect of prior musical training; a pilot study to evaluate impact on clinical competencies is recommended before integration

### Target Learners
- pre-clinical undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- auditory perception and percussion note discrimination in clinical examination
