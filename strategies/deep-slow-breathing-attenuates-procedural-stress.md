# Deep slow breathing (including the 4-7-8 method) as a pre-procedure stress-management technique for trainees performing time-critical procedures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article includes deep slow breathing (DSB) in its educational program as an evidence-based strategy to attenuate trainees' acute stress response during procedures like ETI. It explains that acute stress involves "hyperventilation leading to hypocarbia and respiratory alkalosis" and that reducing respiration rate through DSB restores blood pH and reduces uncomfortable physical sensations. A low inhale/exhale ratio increases vagal activity, producing a relaxed state. The program teaches the "4-7-8 breathing" method, which "can be practised anywhere and at any time".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainees learn the DSB method in advance and can apply it immediately before and during the procedure
#### Constraints
- The article cites studies of DSB for stress reduction but reports no trial of DSB specifically during neonatal ETI

### Target Learners
- medical trainees performing neonatal intubation
- senior fellows, consultants and nursing staff maintaining skills

### Target Learning Goals
- managing acute psychological stress during time-critical procedures
