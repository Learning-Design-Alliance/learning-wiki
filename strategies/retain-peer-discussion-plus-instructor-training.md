# Retain peer discussion in active learning for collaboration and communication goals while adding individual writing, and support instructor training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (1 for, 5 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Although writing outperformed discussion-only on exam essays, the article does not recommend removing peer discussion, because "Collaboration and verbal communication skills are often objectives for introductory biology courses, and these objectives will not be met by student writing alone." The article also recommends that instructors "may need training and practice to become effective with active learning," given the significant instructor effect, and calls for rigorous testing and publication of specific active-learning exercises.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need opportunities for training and practice with active-learning techniques; weekly debriefing and planning meetings were used to standardize delivery in the study
#### Constraints
- Further research is needed to determine the most effective mix of discussion and writing, per the article

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology students
- instructors implementing active learning in large introductory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- collaboration and verbal communication skills
- conceptual understanding
