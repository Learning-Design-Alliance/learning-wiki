# Classroom Physical Activity Breaks

## Description
Classroom physical activity breaks are short (typically 3–10 minute) episodes of movement embedded within seated instructional time. They range from structured curricular activities (e.g., movement-based math drills) to brief aerobic or stretching routines, and are carried out at the whole-class level without special equipment or leaving the room.

## Design Implications

Brief in-class movement can restore attention and on-task behavior after sustained sitting, with the largest observed effects on time-on-task for younger students [~M]. The strongest designs integrate academic content into the movement itself, so instructional time is recovered rather than traded away [~M]. Breaks function as an attention-management tool within [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md): they reset the attentional system between demanding segments rather than reducing load within a task.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A predictable routine and clear start/stop signals so transitions do not consume the time saved
- Movement that is safe in the available space and inclusive of varied fitness levels and mobility
- A direct link back to the learning task (a transition sentence or prompt) so the break does not fragment the lesson

#### Constraints
- Poorly managed breaks increase off-task behavior and transition time, particularly in classes without established routines [-M]
- Effects on academic achievement are small and inconsistent; breaks are not a substitute for effective instruction [-W]
- Overly stimulating or competitive breaks can leave some learners dysregulated rather than refocused [~W]
- Benefits attenuate when breaks are used as rewards or punishments rather than as a neutral instructional routine [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Content-integrated breaks** (e.g., TAKE 10!®): movement doubles as review or practice of academic content
- **Energizing breaks**: aerobic movement to raise arousal during post-lunch or mid-morning dips
- **Calming breaks**: stretching, breathing, or yoga-style movement to de-escalate and settle a class
- **Transition breaks**: movement used as a deliberate boundary between activity types, supporting [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) routines

### Target Learners
- Elementary students, who show the most consistent gains in on-task behavior after movement breaks [~M]
- Students with attention difficulties, for whom brief movement can support sustained engagement with seated tasks [~W]
- Adolescents and adults benefit less reliably; effects on attention and learning are weaker and more mixed [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Attention and self-regulation: re-establishing conditions for learning rather than teaching content directly
- Procedural fluency when breaks embed retrieval or counting tasks into movement
- Classroom community and behavioral routines

### Instructions
1. Establish a routine and signals before the first break; teach the transition the way any other procedure is taught.
2. Schedule breaks at predictable attention dips (mid-morning, post-lunch) or immediately before demanding new content, consistent with [Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md).
3. Keep breaks short (3–7 minutes) and end with a settling activity that returns arousal to baseline.
4. Where possible, integrate academic content — e.g., jumping while skip-counting, acting out vocabulary — connecting to [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) and [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md).
5. Use a brief [Check-In](../principles/check-ins.md) or observation to judge whether the break improved readiness, and adjust intensity accordingly.

## Examples
- **TAKE 10!®** (https://www.take10.net) — a published curriculum of 10-minute classroom activities that pair movement with grade-level academic content in language arts, math, and science.
- **GoNoodle** (https://www.gonoodle.com) — short video-led movement and mindfulness breaks widely used in elementary classrooms.
- **The Morning Mile / classroom walking breaks** — brief in-room or hallway walking routines used as low-arousal energizers in secondary settings.
