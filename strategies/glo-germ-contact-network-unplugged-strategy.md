# Use hands-on unplugged simulations (Glo Germ and contact-network cards) to connect BFS to virus transmission and contact tracing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The curriculum extended BFS learning into science instruction through unplugged activities. In the "Glo Germ" simulation, "a small amount of fluorescent powder was placed on a student's hands to represent an infectious agent", spreading through contact and revealed under UV light. A card-based task then modeled the class as a contact network, with green and red dot markers indicating infection states and students discussing infection likelihood in terms of network distance between individuals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A classroom context where students can interact physically and a health science unit on disease transmission to anchor the activity
#### Constraints
- Activities were designed to be feasible within 45-minute class periods and existing instructional time

### Target Learners
- middle school students in health science classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding transmission through contact networks
- connecting BFS shortest-path reasoning to contact tracing
