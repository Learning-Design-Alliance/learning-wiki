# Collective Data Analysis

## Description
Collective data analysis is a systematic, iterative process of making sense of data through group collaboration, most prominently in participatory action research (PAR) and youth participatory action research (YPAR). Participants — students, teachers, community members — jointly "dive" into datasets (survey results, interview transcripts, observation notes) using structured techniques such as data dives, grounded coding, affinity mapping, and gallery walks to surface themes, outliers, and tensions. The group, not a designated analyst, owns the interpretive work, which both improves the credibility of findings and builds participants' analytical capability.

## Design Implications

Collective analysis converts interpretation from a private expert task into a [collaborative learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) activity, which distributes cognitive load across the group and exposes learners to multiple valid readings of the same evidence [~M]. Because participants analyze data about their own context, the process affirms lived experience and increases ownership of resulting action plans — a core mechanism in PAR epistemology. Structure matters: unstructured discussion of data tends to be dominated by confident voices and to produce confirmation of pre-existing beliefs, so facilitators must scaffold the interpretive moves (describe → pattern → inference → implication) rather than asking "what do you see?" [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A dataset that is genuinely shared and meaningful to participants (their survey, their community, their classroom)
- Data simplified or "cleaned" to a form participants can actually read in the time available (charts, excerpted transcripts, tallies)
- Skilled facilitation with explicit norms ensuring all voices are heard and no single interpretation is treated as final
- A protocol that separates observation from inference before jumping to conclusions

#### Constraints
- Time-intensive; groups without adequate time default to superficial "spot the biggest number" readings [-W]
- Dominant voices can steer analysis toward their framing, undermining the participatory claim [-M]
- Groups with no prior experience interpreting data (e.g., reading a Likert distribution or a qualitative excerpt) need prior instruction in basic data literacy or they will conflate anecdote with pattern [-M]
- Analysis of data participants feel personally implicated in (e.g., their own survey responses) can trigger defensiveness that blocks honest interpretation [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Data dives**: small groups work through structured worksheets on segments of a dataset, then jigsaw back together
- **Grounded/affinity coding**: participants code transcript excerpts and cluster codes into emergent themes
- **Gallery walks**: analyses or visualizations are posted; participants rotate, annotate, and question each other's readings
- **Data-driven dialogue**: facilitator-led phases of predicting, observing, and inferring from a shared display (Lipton & Wellman's protocol)
- Scale ranges from a single class period analyzing a class survey to multi-month YPAR cycles

### Target Learners
- Students and youth in participatory action research projects, who gain both analytical skill and a sense of epistemic authority over their own data [+W]
- Teacher teams and professional learning communities analyzing student outcome data
- Community members in community-based research partnerships
- Less suitable as an introduction to data analysis for complete novices without prior scaffolding in how to read the data type being used [-M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Interpretive skill: distinguishing observation from inference, identifying themes and outliers
- Critical data literacy: questioning how data were produced and what they leave out
- Collaborative meaning-making and productive disagreement about evidence
- Translating findings into action — the bridge from analysis to intervention in inquiry cycles

### Instructions
1. **Prepare the data**: clean, simplify, and format the dataset so participants can read it unaided; remove identifying information where sensitive.
2. **Predict before looking**: have participants state expectations before seeing results, so surprises become visible.
3. **Describe first**: groups record only what they literally see (counts, quotes, patterns) — no interpretation yet.
4. **Analyze in small groups**: use a structured protocol (data dive worksheet, affinity coding, or gallery walk) to generate inferences and candidate themes; incorporate [class discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) to surface disagreements between groups.
5. **Synthesize**: converge on shared themes and tensions, distinguishing well-supported findings from speculation.
6. **Act**: connect conclusions to an action plan, completing the inquiry cycle; revisit the data after action to close the loop.

## Examples
- **YPAR projects** (e.g., the University of California's [YPAR Hub](https://yparhub.berkeley.edu)): youth analyze survey and interview data they collected about school or community issues, then design advocacy actions from their findings.
- **Data-driven dialogue in PLCs**: teacher teams use Lipton & Wellman's observe–infer–predict protocol to analyze student assessment data before planning instruction.
- **Classroom data dives**: students analyze their own class survey on study habits using structured worksheets, then jigsaw-share themes before proposing changes to class routines.
