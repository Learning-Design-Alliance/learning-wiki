# Instructor practices to support women in physics: prevent isolation, recognize students, monitor social dynamics, and advocate for smaller classes and affinity groups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article distills its interviews into four instructor suggestions: ensure students are not isolated by assigning partners equitably, recognize students for successes including simply making progress and take interest in their well-being, take responsibility for classroom social dynamics with check-ins, and advocate for smaller classes plus study or affinity groups. Each suggestion is anchored in a student's own words, such as "making the teachers assign partners, as uncool as that is, would definitely help with that kind of thing."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must actively structure group work and monitor which students lack partners rather than leaving pairing to students
#### Constraints
- Suggestions arise from two interviews at one large public university and are offered as instructor reflections rather than tested interventions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate women in physics and physical science courses

### Target Learning Goals
- sense of belonging in physics
- physics self-efficacy
- persistence toward a physical science degree
