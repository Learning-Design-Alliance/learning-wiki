# Include stories in the teaching methods of the first three grades of basic education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that pictorial stories significantly raised second-grade Arabic achievement, the researcher recommends that the Ministry of Education instruct curriculum teachers "to include the stories in the basic stage of the lower stage (the first three grades)" and to include short stories in teaching methods, with further studies on pictorial stories in the first three grades.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers trained to implement story-based lessons in the lower basic stage
#### Constraints
- Recommendation extrapolates from a two-school second-grade quasi-experiment in Irbid

### Target Learners
- Students in the first three grades of basic education

### Target Learning Goals
- Raising achievement in Arabic language

## Claims

- [Second-grade students taught Arabic with pictorial stories significantly outperformed traditionally taught controls on an achievement test, with a large group effect](../claims/pictorial-story-raises-arabic-achievement-second-grade.md) [+W]
- [Posttest speaking and writing achievement was significantly higher for students taught through short stories than for control-group students](../claims/short-stories-posttest-mean-difference.md) [+W]
- [Achievement gains from pictorial-story instruction showed no significant differences due to gender or the group-by-gender interaction](../claims/pictorial-story-effect-no-gender-or-interaction-difference.md) [~W]
