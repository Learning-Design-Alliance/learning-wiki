# Targeted in-service support programs for non-specialist science teachers, prioritising physics and building on teachers' existing degrees

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose support programs for serving non-specialist teachers, arguing that acquiring content knowledge in a foreign subject "may have a significant impact on the quality of education while requiring a relative low investment of time and effort". Priorities follow the data: physics deserves primary attention, programs can build on non-specialist physics teachers' maths or engineering degrees, and primary science teachers without STEM backgrounds may need far more comprehensive programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must be tailored to the actual needs of non-specialist teacher to achieve increased acceptance and participation.
#### Constraints
- The existing Segunda Licenciatura program includes no career-related incentives such as salary increases, and the demand by teachers has been very low.
- Primary science teachers very often have no background in any STEM related field and may require a far more comprehensive program to build up pedagogical content knowledge.

### Target Learners
- non-specialist teachers of physics and chemistry at secondary schools in Alagoas
- teachers giving science classes at primary schools in Alagoas

### Target Learning Goals
- science content knowledge in the taught subject
- pedagogical content knowledge
- subject-specific didactics
