# Establish university-led exercise services as an alternative source of authentic clinical learning when placements are scarce

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that universities run their own clinical exercise services, overseen by qualified CEPs under university governance, to provide students authentic patient-facing problem-based learning. Such practice "was identified by stakeholders as a potential solution to the difficulties in securing clinical placements through the provision of authentic PBL in the UK setting." Reported benefits include increased student-patient engagement, hands-on experience that removes the need for less effective peer-to-peer role play, and in situ assessment opportunities with real patients.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Qualified CEPs (or equivalent) employed under university governance who retain registration with the accrediting body
- University facilities able to deliver exercise interventions for the general public
#### Constraints
- Described as requiring consideration and exploration rather than an established UK practice; placement scarcity is the motivating condition

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate clinical exercise physiology students

### Target Learning Goals
- Real-world clinical competency development in exercise delivery with patients across multiple long-term conditions
