# Integrate GAI-based teaching into medical curricula with robust curricular integration, technological support, and systematic training for educators and students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that GAI-based teaching be adopted as a complement to, not a replacement for, traditional medical teaching, and that implementation succeed only with deliberate support structures. It states that "successful implementation requires careful consideration of several factors: robust curricular integration, adequate technological support, and systematic training for both educators and students." It further suggests pairing GAI with pedagogical approaches such as flipped classrooms, case-based learning, and gamified instruction, and establishing a practical framework for AI literacy in medical education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Robust curricular integration of GAI within existing educational frameworks
- Adequate technological support
- Systematic training for both students and educators
#### Constraints
- The article states GAI currently functions primarily as a complementary educational tool rather than a replacement for traditional teaching; without robust curricular integration its impact on knowledge acquisition and skill development may be constrained

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- medical educators

### Target Learning Goals
- practical clinical skill development
- knowledge acquisition
- AI literacy
