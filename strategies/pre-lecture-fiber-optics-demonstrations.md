# Open a fiber optics lecture with a simple demonstration that raises curiosity questions before covering the underlying theory

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that the teacher "demonstrate some simple yet revealing fiber optics experiments right before the lectures", because these demonstrations "bring out the curiosity in the stud ents and help them to focus" on the lecture material. The teacher poses questions the demonstration raises (e.g., can light be bent?), links the demo to real-life communication systems, and only then introduces the physics needed to answer them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Low-cost equipment such as a laser pointer, a portable light source with fiber-coupled output, a screen, and a fiber optic cable with connectors, purchasable from vendors
#### Constraints
- The demonstrations are designed for college fiber optics lectures and assume students will subsequently learn reflection, refraction, and diffraction

### Target Learners
- college science majors
- college non-science majors

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding physical principles of fiber optics such as total internal reflection
- connecting physics concepts to real-life communication technology
