Make social justice issues explicit by assigning critical literature and guiding questions alongside experiential activities in teacher education¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that teacher educators explicitly focus students' thinking on social justice issues, since without critical readings and guiding questions many students are unlikely to encounter them. They suggest incorporating "social justice issues related specifically to the politics of language" into curriculum and using a beginning-of-course survey plus the end-of-course linguistic activity to gauge shifts in students' thinking.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Critical literature on the politics of language must be assigned and discussed
  • Reflection must be guided by specific questions targeting the politics of language

Constraints¶

  • Without explicit focus, students may reinterpret the activity in ways antithetical to its purpose, such as seeing it as a way to teach grammar rules

Target Learners¶

  • pre-service teachers, particularly predominantly white cohorts with little cross-cultural experience

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical awareness of the politics of language
  • reflection on social justice in literacy education

Claims¶

  • Pre-service teachers required to read critical literature and reflect through guiding questions show greater awareness of the political aspects of language than those who are not [+M]
  • Critical awareness of the politics of language did not necessarily carry over into pre-service teachers' thinking about literacy education for language minority students [~M]
