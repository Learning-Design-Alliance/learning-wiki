# Teachers can use friendly eye contact to convey communion and encourage reciprocal gaze interaction during group work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their case study, the authors recommend that teachers use nonverbal eye contact deliberately during small-group scaffolding. They state that "Through behaviors of communion, the teachers can capture students' attention" and that "With friendly eye contacts, the teachers can convey communion and give the agency to the engaged students". Especially simultaneous high communion and modest agency is described as teacher behavior that encourages students' reciprocal eye contact interaction. Teachers are also advised to acknowledge students' gaze initiatives and gaze aversion as nonverbal messages relevant to the teacher-student relationship.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be able to observe and interpret students' momentary gaze direction or aversion during instruction interactions.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation derives from a small case study of three teachers; the article states its results are not necessarily generalizable to other contexts.

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students working in small groups

### Target Learning Goals
- Building fruitful teacher-student relationships and encouraging student participation and interaction during collaborative problem solving
