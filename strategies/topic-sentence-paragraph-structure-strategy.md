# Structure paragraphs around an explicit topic sentence, with three to seven sentences each

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For inexperienced writers whose paragraphs wander, the guide teaches that 'Well-structured paragraphs begin by expressing the main idea in the first sentence (topic sentence), then develop that main idea with facts, examples, or analysis'. Paragraphs should contain three to seven sentences, neither too long nor too short, and cover just one idea; a measurement drifting into a second topic should be split into its own paragraph with its own topic sentence. Results paragraphs can open by referring to a figure or table and can end with a conclusion or transition sentence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each paragraph develops exactly the idea announced in its topic sentence
- Split paragraphs longer than about half a page into two smaller ideas
#### Constraints
- Paragraph structure in English differs from some other languages, e.g. Chinese, where the main idea comes at the end

### Target Learners
- beginning writers of scientific papers, especially non-native English speakers

### Target Learning Goals
- write well-structured academic paragraphs
