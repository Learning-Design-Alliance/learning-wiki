# Use human facilitation — guided discussion, structured reflection, and peer interaction — as a core equity mechanism in AI education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that equitable participation in AI education depends on human facilitation rather than self-paced or credential-gated formats. Guided discussion, structured reflection, and peer interaction "created space for questioning and ethical reasoning without penalizing technical uncertainty". The authors conclude that "facilitation is not ancillary but a core equity mechanism in AI education", contrasting this model with self-paced online courses, coding-first bootcamps, and credential-gated programs, and recommend that programs seeking to broaden participation adopt mixed-cohort structures supported by intentional scaffolding and facilitation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors or facilitators able to run guided discussion and structured reflection
- Mixed-cohort structures with intentional scaffolding
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from early signals in one program's first cohort, not completed outcome data

### Target Learners
- Non-traditional learners without technical backgrounds
- Adult professionals and career-transitioning learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Ethical reasoning about AI
- Academic agency and confidence engaging with AI concepts
