# Use AR overlays aligned with multimedia learning principles: pair words with nearby, simultaneous images and segment lessons into self-paced chunks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends designing AR physics applications in line with Mayer's cognitive theory of multimedia learning, highlighting the multimedia principle, spatial contiguity, temporal contiguity and the segmenting principle. Concretely, AR tools can "place words and images near animated or physical objects", show words and images simultaneously, and be designed as "multiple mutually related, conceptual chunks". Examples include overlaying computer-generated textual descriptions on real-world car engine parts and displaying live measurement data on AR glasses during a science experiment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- AR applications must combine words with corresponding images close together and simultaneously, and be divided into self-paced segments.
#### Constraints
- The review presents these as design opportunities grounded in multimedia theory, not as effects tested within this review.

### Target Learners
- school and university physics students using tablet- or glasses-based AR

### Target Learning Goals
- constructing target physics knowledge from overlaid textual, graphical and 3D content without overload
