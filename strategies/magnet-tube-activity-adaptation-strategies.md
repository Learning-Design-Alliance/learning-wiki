# Adapt the magnet-tube activity by audience: limit materials for younger students, add sound-based digital timing, run it after formal electromagnetism study, and add peer-review and consensus dynamics for non-epistemic NOS

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author offers six adaptations: for younger students, limit materials to the copper coil, rubber, and neodymium magnets and possibly provide a procedural guide changing one variable at a time; use the free Audacity app to measure fall times from sound spikes; run the activity after formal electromagnetism study with students explaining Faraday's and Lenz's laws; assign readings questioning whether laws derive from data; require written justification before students access materials, mirroring research practice; and run an anonymous peer-review consensus game in Stage 5 to show how scientific consensus is constructed and revised.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials matched to student level and learning goals
- For the timing adaptation, a device running the free Audacity app and a brief introductory lesson on time measurement
#### Constraints
- Any implementation must take into account the students' level, learning goals, and available resources

### Target Learners
- First-year high school students
- Advanced high school classes
- Undergraduate courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding how scientific consensus is constructed, evaluated, and revised
- Distinguishing epistemic and non-epistemic dimensions of NOS

## Claims

- [A magnet-in-tube NOS activity led high school students to assimilate consensual epistemic aspects of the nature of science, including that there is no universal scientific method and that observation is theory-laden.](../claims/nos-magnet-tube-activity-epistemic-assimilation.md) [+W]
- [During the activity students improved group communication, exercised creativity in problem-solving, and took ownership of the collective investigative process.](../claims/nos-activity-communication-creativity-ownership.md) [+W]
- [Without prompting, students repeated their experiments several times to average fall times, and later proposed more precise tools and more objects as ways to increase confidence in conclusions.](../claims/students-spontaneous-repetition-and-tool-proposals.md) [+W]
