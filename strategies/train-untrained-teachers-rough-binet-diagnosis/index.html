Train untrained school teachers to apply the Binet tests for rough diagnosis¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The bibliography records the position that school teachers untrained in clinical psychology can be taught to apply the Binet tests and obtain accurate results, and separately that the inexperienced teacher may use the tests for rough diagnosis whose results, 'although not wholly accurate, would still possess sufficient educational significance to be of great value to her.' Together these entries support a strategy of extending Binet testing beyond expert clinicians to ordinary teachers, with accuracy limits acknowledged.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training sufficient for teachers 'untrained in clinical psychology' to apply the tests, and acceptance that results are rough rather than wholly accurate.

Constraints¶

  • The annotation limits the inexperienced teacher's use to 'a rough diagnosis' and concedes the results are not wholly accurate.

Target Learners¶

  • school teachers learning to administer the tests

Target Learning Goals¶

  • administering the Binet tests for rough diagnosis of pupils

Claims¶

  • The Binet-Simon scale is a valuable diagnostic tool for classifying defective children when used by a trained examiner but dangerous in untrained hands [~W]
