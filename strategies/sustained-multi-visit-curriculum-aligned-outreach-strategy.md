# Design sustained public engagement as multiple structured visits across a school year, aligned with the curriculum and built for evidence gathering from the start

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article outlines an approach to designing and delivering sustained public engagement: five one-hour sessions delivered to the same student group across an academic year (October to June), each split into two 20-minute presentations with question intervals, planned with teachers so each presentation acts "in synergy with the AS -level material". The entire activity was "built specifically with evidence gathering in mind", following NCCPE guidance, with registers taken to confirm full attendance before questionnaires were issued. Five sessions were chosen as a compromise between wanting multiple interactive sessions and available college time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must agree to allocate time for five individual sessions across the academic year
- Presentations must be aligned with when the related syllabus material is delivered
- Registers must be taken at each session so questionnaire respondents can be confirmed as full attendees
#### Constraints
- Five sessions were a compromise against the college academic calendar; the authors note they did not analyse whether benefits differ for students attending only some sessions

### Target Learners
- Sixth-form students aged 16-17 studying AS-level Physics

### Target Learning Goals
- Stimulated interest and engagement with science
- Enhanced science-related education and awareness of research
