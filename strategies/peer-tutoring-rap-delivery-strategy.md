# Deliver RAP strategy instruction through peer tutoring with trained high-achieving classmates as one-on-one tutors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 causal), `q2`–`q4` · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Pair low-achieving tutees with high-achieving tutors from the same classroom, provide tutors with a detailed script and two two-hour training sessions, and have teams meet three times per week for 20-30 minutes. The article states that peer-tutoring "provides more opportunities to respond for every student because of the one-on-one-setting of tutor and tutee" and is "more resource-friendly than instructor-directed one-on-one or small-group settings." Sessions follow an explicit instruction pattern of modeling, scaffolding, and independent practice with progressively longer and more difficult texts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors require prior training (two two-hour sessions with a detailed script) and ongoing observation to ensure fidelity
- A sociometric inquiry should inform team composition to avoid pairing children who do not get along
- Teachers or research staff must attend sessions to monitor adherence and intervene when teams deviate from the script
#### Constraints
- More sessions are necessary for strategy instruction and practicing compared with instructor-directed one-on-one settings
- Tutors need supplementary help handling disruptive and off-task behavior, particularly from tutees with or at risk for EBD

### Target Learners
- struggling fourth-grade comprehenders in inclusive elementary classrooms
- high-achieving fourth graders serving as tutors

### Target Learning Goals
- reading comprehension strategy use (RAP: Read, Ask, Put)
- paraphrasing main ideas of expository text paragraphs
