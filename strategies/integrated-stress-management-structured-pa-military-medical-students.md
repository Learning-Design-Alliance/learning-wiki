# Implement integrated stress-management strategies and structured physical activity programmes for medical students in military institutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends pairing stress-management support with structured physical activity programmes to protect cognitive function and academic performance in high-stress medical training. It states its findings "support the implementation of integrated stress-management strategies and structured PA programmes to enhance cognitive function and academic performance among medical students in military institutions." PA is treated as a moderator that partially mitigates, but does not remove, stress-related academic risk.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programmes should target students with combined high perceived stress and elevated cortisol, whom the study classifies as high risk
#### Constraints
- The article states PA did not eliminate the elevated academic risk, so PA support alone is not sufficient
- Evidence comes from a single military institution and preclinical cohorts, limiting generalisability

### Target Learners
- Preclinical undergraduate medical students in military-based institutions

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining cognitive function and academic performance (GPA) under high academic stress
