Institutions should define generative-AI prohibitions by function, distinguish generation from format conversion, and require validated evidential methods before sanctions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The paper's normative policy recommendations are that institutions should consider 'defining 'use of generative AI' by reference to function rather than platform identity; explicitly distinguishing content generation from format conversion; adopting the operational criteria proposed in Section 4; and requiring validated evidential methods before imposing sanctions.' On the function-based interpretation, sanctioning students for transcription-only use is best characterised as policy misapplication rather than a proper misconduct finding. Policies should also specify which learning outcomes the prohibition serves.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Policies must specify which learning outcomes are served by the prohibition so the function-based interpretation can be applied with precision

Constraints¶

  • The paper does not claim to have empirically tested these proposals; it sets out an agenda for such testing
  • Claims about UK institutional policy prevalence rely on secondary sources

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students
  • policy-makers
  • faculty

Target Learning Goals¶

  • assessment validity
  • procedural fairness
  • preventing wrongful accusations

Related Strategies¶

  • Design task-sensitive institutional AI policies rather than blanket permitted/prohibited distinctions
  • Replace detector-centred assessment with a four-stage process-oriented model: declared starting point, versioned drafting, process artefacts, and targeted oral verification
  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
  • Apply four operational criteria—fidelity, non-augmentation, traceability, attestation—to identify transcription-only AI use
  • Design assignments that explicitly specify which tasks may be delegated to AI and which may not
  • Assess GenAI misconduct evidence against three probative credentials — relevance, credibility and inferential force — before imposing sanctions
