# On resubmission, never take rejection personally, use reviewer feedback to improve, make the proposal clearer and shorter, strengthen the science case, and spread referee risk across applications

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because most proposals and applications fail but can be re-used and improved, the article gives a resubmission checklist: "Critically read the feedback from the reviewers and use it to improve your application, preferentially after discussing it with colleagues", make the proposal clearer, shorter and more compelling, remove vague statements, strengthen the science case with archive data or simulations, publish ongoing projects, and increase visibility through talks and networking. For references, use changing sets of referees across applications to spread risk.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reviewer feedback, requested if not provided by default, and discussion with colleagues to interpret it
#### Constraints
- A vital part of a job application, the reference letters, is not under the applicant's control and usually not even seen by them

### Target Learners
- scientists resubmitting rejected proposals or job applications, especially junior researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- improving resubmitted proposals and managing the emotional and practical side of rejection
