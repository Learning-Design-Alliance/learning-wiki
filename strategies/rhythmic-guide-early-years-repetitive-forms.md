# Use rhythmic guides in early years to organize unit strokes and facile movement, with imposed rhythm reserved for simple repetitive forms rather than word writing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that the rhythmic guide in penmanship instruction be carefully supervised and scientifically directed. Its value in the early years is described as unquestioned, as an aid in organizing writing into unit strokes and in facile movement. For temporal rhythmitization, imposed rhythm is not thoroughly adaptable to constructing word forms but may be used to better advantage with simple repetitive forms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Use must be carefully supervised and scientifically directed.
#### Constraints
- After the subject has developed habits of natural rhythm, the rhythmic guide may retard speed and detrimentally affect form; imposed rhythm is not thoroughly adaptable to constructing word forms.

### Target Learners
- Young children in early penmanship instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- Organizing handwriting into unit strokes
- Developing facile, smoothly regulated movement

- [Better writers and older children anticipate the rhythmic beat while poorer writers and young children lag behind it; the arm is less accurate than the fingers except for younger children](../claims/beat-following-anticipation-versus-lag-arm-versus-finger.md) [~W]
