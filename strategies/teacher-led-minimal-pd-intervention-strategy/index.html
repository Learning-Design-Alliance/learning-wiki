Implement strategy interventions through regular teachers with minimal professional development and structured materials¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends delivering innovative interventions through students' usual teachers rather than researchers, supported by a one-day training, clear and systematic workshop structures, and ongoing teacher-researcher communication. It reports that "the initial training of the teachers, the many discussions between the teachers and the experimenters that followed, as well as the clear and systematic structure of the workshops, may have facilitated the teachers' appropriation of the intervention". This addresses criticism that researcher-led interventions have low replicability and limited practical impact.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A one-day teacher training session before implementation, materials sent ahead of time, and ongoing communication between teachers and researchers

Constraints¶

  • The study could not formally check implementation quality beyond informal communication; some teachers found the workshops too dense for 45 minutes

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students taught by their usual classroom teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • purposeful reading strategy acquisition through teacher-led classroom instruction

Claims¶

  • Direct instruction training improved 5th graders' task-model construction (question analysis) relative to an active control [+W]
  • The intervention did not improve 5th graders' extract-and-integrate performance [-W]
  • The intervention did not significantly improve performance on the integrated, unguided purposeful reading task [-W]
  • Training improved 5th graders' answer construction and evaluation within the experimental group, but not significantly relative to controls at post-test [+W]

Related Strategies¶
