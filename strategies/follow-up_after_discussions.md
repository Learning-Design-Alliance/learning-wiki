# Follow-up After Discussions

## Description
Teachers encourage students to take actions to address inequities revealed during the discussion, such as writing letters to the editor or meeting with the principal. They offer support to students feeling ashamed or guilty, emphasizing that these feelings can be a catalyst for action.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time for follow-up activities, resources for taking action (e.g., writing materials, meeting space), and a supportive teacher who can guide and mentor students.
#### Constraints
- Students may feel overwhelmed or discouraged if their efforts do not produce immediate results. Requires careful planning and support to ensure that actions are meaningful and impactful.
