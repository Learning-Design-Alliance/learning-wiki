# Apply Dong et al.'s five flipped-classroom recommendations step-by-step when integrating flipped simulation into PAL simulation courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article operationalizes five recommendations for flipped simulation: learning objectives should dictate simulation use; preparation work should be engaging; students should be accountable for preparation; flipped simulation should be integrated into larger curricula; and implementation requires "buy-in from faculty, administration, and students". Table 3 maps each step to concrete methods such as clinical observation, collaborative lesson plan development, peer facilitation, and reflective essays.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All activities, including preparatory work and simulations, must align with overall learning objectives
#### Constraints
- The authors state simulation-based education may not adequately stimulate motivation to learn, particularly among students who do not intend to pursue a career in critical care

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate medical trainees in simulation-based curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- Engagement, preparation, and application of theoretical knowledge in practical scenarios
