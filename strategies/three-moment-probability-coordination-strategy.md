# Teach probability in three coordinated moments across Chemistry, Mathematics and Physics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size

## Description
Because the standard probabilistic interpretation of QM requires probability calculus that does not temporally coincide across subjects, the article proposes teaching probability redundantly in three curricular moments. The author states that "Probability could therefore be taught in three different moments: as an anticipation when atomic and molecular orbitals are introduced in Chemistry, as an exhaustive study in Mathematics and as a reference when dealing with QM in Physics." This interdisciplinary redundancy is said to benefit all subjects involved.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curricular coordination between the Physics, Mathematics and Chemistry courses so the three moments can be sequenced
#### Constraints
- The ontological measure paradox can only be approached as quick nods during the high school period

### Target Learners
- scientific high school students in the pre-university year

### Target Learning Goals
- probabilistic interpretation of quantum phenomena
- probability calculus
