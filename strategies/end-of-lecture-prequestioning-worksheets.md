# Deploy prequestioning as a low-preparation end-of-lecture worksheet routine with immediate answer feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a concrete implementation: at the end of each lecture session students complete a worksheet with eight postquestions and four prequestions in about 15 minutes, after which "the course lecturer briefly presented the correct answers to each question before dismissing the class". Prequestions target concepts taught in the next session, so tested postquestions appear at least two days later. The authors judge the preparation burden moderate, since a standard worksheet design was reused with swapped questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The lecturer prepares prequestions, postquestions, and answers before each session
- A few minutes of class time at the end of each session
- Correct answers must be presented shortly after students answer
#### Constraints
- Students may be uncomfortable with guessing and errors (40% reported moderate or very high discomfort); benefits were not uniform across topics

### Target Learners
- undergraduate chemistry students
- students in similar content-heavy lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- retention and understanding of lecture content
