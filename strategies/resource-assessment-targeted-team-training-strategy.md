# Assess available resources and develop targeted team training to improve trauma care efficiency in resource-limited hospitals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that hospitals with limited medical resources first analyze their local trauma care workflow, then build a team from existing emergency department staff rather than activating a full multidisciplinary trauma team for every patient, and train that team through structured simulation. The authors conclude that "assessment of available resources and development of targeted team training could improve care in resource-limited hospitals", and suggest Chinese hospitals could employ the methods used in this study to improve care efficiency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Workflow analysis of the local trauma care process to identify delays before designing the team and training schedule
#### Constraints
- Activation of a full trauma team for every trauma patient imposes a substantial burden on institutions with limited medical resources

### Target Learners
- Emergency department physicians and nurses in Chinese hospitals and other resource-limited settings without formal ATLS training

### Target Learning Goals
- Efficient initial trauma care, including prompt initiation of life-saving tests and interventions
