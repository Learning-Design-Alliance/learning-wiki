# Randomized question-category assignment with oversize question pool and limited student choice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
To minimize sharing of question information between students, the instructor prepares more categories than needed — for a midterm, "I prepare six or eight categories and only have students complete two of them" — and categories are chosen randomly per student using a die. Students choose one category themselves to restore some agency amid exam anxiety, then roll a die for the other category, making identical exams rare.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A question pool larger than what a single exam requires
- A list of which categories each student has already completed, to prevent unfair advantage by repeating material on the final exam
#### Constraints
- Students expressed frustration and anxiety about the randomness of question selection and not having access to categories ahead of time

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics and astronomy majors

### Target Learning Goals
- broad studying across course material
- reduced cheating and question sharing
