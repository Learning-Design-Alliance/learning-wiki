# Adapt social and epistemic networks from research tools into pedagogical instruments for evidence-informed debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes repurposing social and epistemic networks, traditionally used by researchers for dataset analysis, as visual evidence that helps learners recall and reflect on team communication during debriefing. The identified comprehensibility and perceived usefulness "suggest the potential feasibility of adapting social and epistemic networks, traditionally utilised as analytical tools for researchers, into pedagogical instruments that support evidence-informed reflection in dynamic physical learning environments". Educators select time spans and display networks to stimulate reflection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educator guidance in interpreting the networks during debriefing
- Automated generation of evidence to avoid imposing additional workload on educators
#### Constraints
- Using video footage for reflection has been criticised for privacy concerns; the automated approach addresses the labour-intensive nature of reviewing footage

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing learners in simulation-based team training

### Target Learning Goals
- Reflection on team communication, engagement, roles and closed-loop communication
