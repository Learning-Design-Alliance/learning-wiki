Prioritize randomized controlled trials, established validity frameworks, and protocol publication in future VRS simulation research¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The review's authors recommend how future simulator-training research in VRS should be designed: they hypothesize it would be beneficial to focus on randomized controlled trials, which are expensive but needed to rule out bias; researchers must acknowledge that validity is imperative to education research, considering frameworks such as Messick's; and studies should adequately report methods to avoid bias, including publishing protocols. Streamlined designs and validation terms would make studies comparable and allow stronger conclusions.

Related Strategies¶

  • Future research should include long-term RCTs with adequate sample sizes, standardized outcome measures, and cost-effectiveness analyses
  • Future active-learning research should use randomised controlled designs with larger samples and standardised outcome measures
  • Future research should use standardized validated outcome measures, longitudinal follow-up designs, and methods linking learner gains to patient-relevant outcomes
  • Strengthen vascular simulation research with modern validity frameworks, longer skill-retention follow-up, cost-effectiveness analysis, and more nontechnical-skills and cadaver models
  • Prioritize larger multi-center RCTs with standardized AR systems, uniform metrics, and long-term follow-up when evaluating AR surgical training
