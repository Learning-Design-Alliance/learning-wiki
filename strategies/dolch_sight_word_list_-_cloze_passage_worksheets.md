# Dolch Sight Word List - Cloze Passage Worksheets

## Description
Cloze passage worksheets present short, grade-level texts in which selected Dolch sight words (e.g., *the*, *said*, *because*, *would*) have been deleted. Students read the passage and supply the missing words from context, from a word bank, or from memory. Each grade level typically includes three worksheets plus a nouns set, so the same format can be revisited across a school year with increasing difficulty.

## Design Implications

Cloze practice targets the automaticity of high-frequency word recognition, which is a prerequisite for fluent reading: when word identification is effortful, comprehension suffers because working memory is consumed by decoding [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]. Unlike flashcard drill, the cloze format forces learners to process each sight word within sentence context, supporting both recognition and meaning-making [~M]. The format also functions as a formative assessment: which blanks a student misses reveals which sight words are not yet automatic [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Printable passages at the student's instructional reading level, with blanks placed only on words the student has been taught
- A word bank or picture support for early-stage learners; pencils or a digital fill-in equivalent
- A follow-up check: teacher review of responses with immediate correction ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md))

#### Constraints
- Cloze items with multiple plausible answers (e.g., *can* vs. *will*) create ambiguity that penalizes comprehension rather than testing sight-word knowledge [-M] — blanks must be constructed so only the target word fits
- For students still decoding letter-by-letter, the passage load can overwhelm; the surrounding text must be decodable or the task shifts from sight-word practice to frustration [~M]
- Pure worksheet completion without oral reading or feedback risks shallow engagement; silent seatwork alone produces weaker gains than teacher-guided completion [-W]
- The Dolch list (compiled 1936) overlaps heavily with but is not identical to modern high-frequency lists (e.g., Fry); some words are archaic in contemporary early-grade texts [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Word bank vs. no bank**: banked versions emphasize recognition; unbanked versions emphasize recall and spelling
- **Oral cloze**: teacher reads aloud, students supply the word orally — appropriate for pre-readers and [ESL learners](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md)
- **Sentence-level cloze**: single sentences instead of passages, reducing load for beginners ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md))
- **Student-authored cloze**: students create passages with blanks for peers, shifting from recognition to production

### Target Learners
- Early elementary students (pre-K through grade 3) building a sight vocabulary
- Struggling readers who need high-frequency words to become automatic before fluency work [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- ESL/EFL learners, for whom the most frequent English function words carry little semantic salience and need contextual exposure [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Automatic recognition of high-frequency words in context
- Use of sentence context to predict and confirm word identity
- Reading comprehension at the sentence and passage level

### Instructions
1. Pre-teach the target sight words with flashcards or [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) so the cloze task is recognition practice, not first exposure.
2. Distribute the passage; read it once aloud with blanks unfilled so students hear the sentence structure ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) function: orienting before independent work).
3. Students fill blanks independently, using the word bank if provided ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
4. Review as a group or individually, having students reread the completed passage aloud; correct errors immediately and note missed words for reteaching ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)).
5. Reassess with a fresh passage containing the same missed words after 2–3 days ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)).

## Related Strategies
- Flashcard drill on the same Dolch list — pairs well: isolated recognition first, contextual application second
- Repeated oral reading — builds the fluency that automatic sight-word recognition enables
- [Act It Out](act_it_out.md) — kinesthetic pre-teaching of target words before the written cloze task

## Examples
- **Sight-word cloze units in core programs**: published curricula such as *Reading A–Z* high-frequency word books and Scholastic sight-word readers include cloze-style fill-in pages keyed to the Dolch 220 list.
- **Teacher-made grade-level sets**: a grade 1 set of three passages per term, each embedding 8–10 target words with a word bank, reviewed whole-class before independent completion.
- **ESL adult education**: cloze sentences using Dolch nouns (*apple*, *house*, *mother*) to anchor function words to concrete referents.
