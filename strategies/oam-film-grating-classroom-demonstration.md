# Classroom demonstration of OAM beams with a Class 2 laser pointer and camera projection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends mounting the fork diffraction grating and laser on a breadboard or desk to show OAM beams as a research-based learning demonstration. It specifies that "a Class 2 laser pointer at 650 nm with a maximum power of 1 mW is required" for classroom safety, and that a camera, including a phone camera, may be needed to see the spot in a well-lit room or to project the output for large lectures or asynchronous videos. Key concepts such as diffraction and OAM should be covered before the demonstration, and students can be asked to predict the outcome.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A Class 2 laser pointer at 650 nm with maximum power of 1 mW, and coverage of key concepts such as diffraction and OAM before presenting the demonstration
#### Constraints
- With low laser power in a well lit classroom it may be difficult to see the laser spot on a screen, so a camera may be required

### Target Learners
- school students
- undergraduate students in large lectures

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement with quantum mechanics concepts
- understanding diffraction and OAM
