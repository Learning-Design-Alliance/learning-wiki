# Authentic Learning Tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Authentic learning tasks ask learners to engage with problems that resemble those encountered in real professional, civic, or everyday contexts — ill-structured, situated in a meaningful scenario, and directed toward a genuine purpose or audience. Rather than practicing decontextualized exercises, learners perform the discipline itself: investigating, designing, evaluating, or arguing as a practitioner would. The task, not just the content, carries the authenticity.

## Design Implications

Authentic tasks support transfer because knowledge encoded in the context of use is more accessible when similar conditions recur [Anchored instruction improves problem-solving transfer when problems are situated in realistic scenarios.](../patterns/anchored-instruction.md) [+M]. They also strengthen motivation by giving work a visible purpose and audience [Autonomy and meaningful purpose support intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]. Authenticity is a design dimension, not a binary: tasks can be authentic in surface features (realistic artifacts) or in underlying thinking (genuine disciplinary reasoning), and the latter matters more for learning [Shaffer & Resnick's "thick authenticity" argument.](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep3404_4) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A realistic problem scenario with genuine stakes, constraints, and an identifiable audience or purpose ([Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md))
- Access to the tools, resources, and information sources practitioners would actually use
- Sustained time and space for investigation, iteration, and [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) during the task
- Assessment criteria that evaluate the quality of disciplinary performance, not just the correctness of an answer ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))

#### Constraints
- High task complexity can overwhelm novices; without [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) or scaffolding, learners flounder in unguided search [-S]
- Authentic tasks consume substantial instructional time relative to the number of distinct objectives covered [-M]
- Surface realism without disciplinary depth ("authentic-flavored" tasks) adds production cost without improving learning [~M]
- Ill-structured tasks can leave learners uncertain about what counts as success; without clear criteria and exemplars, performance and motivation both suffer [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Case-based tasks**: learners analyze and decide within a documented real case ([Case-Based Learning](../elements/case-based-learning.md))
- **Simulated performance**: realistic role, tools, and constraints without real-world stakes (e.g., clinical simulations)
- **Real-world projects**: genuine clients, data, or community partners ([Community-Based Learning](../principles/community-based-learning.md))
- **Anchored problem solving**: realistic video or narrative scenarios designed to surface disciplinary questions, as in the Jasper Woodbury series [CTGV anchored instruction.](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep3102_2) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Learners who already have foundational knowledge and are ready to apply it in variable, messy conditions [Case-based learning improves exam performance when learners have sufficient prior structure.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]
- Learners whose motivation depends on seeing the relevance of what they are learning [Self-efficacy and perceived relevance predict persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Novices need more structure within the authentic task (worked models, part-task practice) before full complexity is productive [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Application and transfer: using knowledge under realistic, variable conditions ([Application of Knowledge](../elements/application-of-knowledge.md))
- Problem-solving and decision-making in ill-structured domains
- Professional identity and practices: reasoning, communicating, and collaborating as a practitioner
- Integration of knowledge across topics, which fragmented instruction rarely achieves [+M]

### Instructions
1. Identify the real-world performance the learning should enable, and the context, audience, and constraints that surround it ([Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md)).
2. Design a task that requires that performance, with an ill-structured problem, realistic resources, and a deliverable for a real or simulated audience.
3. Provide an entry model — a demonstration, exemplar, or [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) — so learners see what quality looks like before attempting the task.
4. Structure the work in phases with [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md), checkpoints, and opportunities for [Articulation](../elements/articulation.md) of reasoning.
5. Assess with criteria drawn from real-world standards, ideally involving [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) by audiences beyond the instructor.
6. Debrief to help learners abstract general principles from the situated experience, so transfer is not confined to the original context [~M].

## Related Strategies

## Patterns That Use This Strategy
- [Anchored Instruction](../patterns/anchored-instruction.md) — realistic narrative scenarios anchor problem solving
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — authentic tasks are the context for modeling, coaching, and fading
- [4C/ID](../patterns/4cid-four-component-instructional-design.md) — whole authentic learning tasks form the backbone of the task class sequence

## Examples
- **Jasper Woodbury Problem Solving series** (Vanderbilt Cognition and Technology Group) — video-anchored adventures in which students solve complex mathematical planning problems [CTGV anchored instruction.](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep3102_2)
- **Problem-Based Learning in medical education** (e.g., McMaster University's original MD curriculum) — patient cases as authentic tasks requiring diagnosis, reasoning, and self-directed inquiry.
- **Project-based science curricula** such as [WISE (Web-Based Inquiry Science Environment)](https://wise.berkeley.edu) — learners investigate authentic scientific questions with real data and tools.
