# Graded-difficulty probe tests interspersed in a fixed work period to locate the difficulty level a fatigued performer can still manage

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article adopts Thorndike's proposal to measure degree of difficulty by interspersing, within and at the close of a rigidly fixed long work-period, tests of decreasing digit size — four-place by three-place, four-place by four-place, five-place by five-place, and smaller — to find the level at which performance fails. The present experiment used this cue to ask whether efficiency ends abruptly or tails off by degrees. It requires pre-built problems and recorded times and errors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems made up beforehand so work proceeds with no more interruption than recording the answer and elapsed time
- A practice period to stabilize performance before the fatigue test
#### Constraints
