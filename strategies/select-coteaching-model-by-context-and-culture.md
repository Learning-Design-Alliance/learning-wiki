# Select co-teaching models cautiously against student characteristics, teacher preferences, curricular demands, and cultural context

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because the study found no grammatical-proficiency advantage for team teaching in an Iranian EFL context, the authors recommend that co-teaching classes be used cautiously and that implementation require "the meticulous consideration of cultural background, which might vary across different contexts". This echoes Cook and Friend's (1995) advice quoted in the article that approaches to co-teaching should be selected on the basis of student characteristics and needs, teacher preferences, curricular demands, and pragmatics such as available teaching space.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examine co-teachers' and students' needs judiciously before adopting a model; consider cultural background and institutional norms such as single-teacher domination
#### Constraints
- The finding applies to team teaching for grammar with male junior-high students in one Iranian setting; the authors flag single-gender focus and particular setting as limitations

### Target Learners
- EFL learners in single-teacher-dominated education systems
- junior-high students

### Target Learning Goals
- grammar instruction
- appropriate instructional-model selection
