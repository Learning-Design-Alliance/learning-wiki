# Graduate courses should stress conceptual understanding, connect math to concepts, adjust pacing, and meet students where they are

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on students' accounts, the article supports instructional changes in graduate physics courses: stressing the concepts students will keep and use, making the conceptual reasons for mathematical steps explicit, slowing rapid pacing, adding historical context for how field conclusions were reached, and instructors "being more willing to meet the students where they are." Participants also suggested reorganizing course content and using assessments that foster deep thinking rather than rewarding technical procedures.

## Design Implications

### Target Learners
- physics graduate students in core courses

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding, integration of physics and mathematics, epistemological sophistication
