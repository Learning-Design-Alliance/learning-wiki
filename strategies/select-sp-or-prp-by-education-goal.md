# Include simulation-based education with standardized patients in curricula focused on infection control performance, using peer role-play as a lower-cost alternative where knowledge is the goal

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that "simulation-based education be included in curricula focused on performance" and that "nursing educators select the appropriate education method according to their practice environment and educational purposes". They also suggest peer role-play "can be a good alternative strategy for schools lacking financial support or a place for simulations", and call for developing "more complicated scenarios for senior-level students".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation requires standardized patients trained in scripts and scenario algorithms, simulation rooms, and debriefing time
- A safe simulation environment that ensures patient safety and allows friendly peer relationships
#### Constraints
- The study did not tackle cost-effectiveness of the education methods
- Findings come from one university with convenience sampling, so generalization requires care

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students
- third-year students beginning clinical practice

### Target Learning Goals
- infection control clinical performance
- knowledge and awareness of standard precautions

## Claims

- [Awareness of standard precautions increased significantly in both education groups, with no statistically significant difference between the simulation and peer role-play methods.](../claims/awareness-standard-precautions-increased-both-groups.md) [+W]
- [Both standardized-patient simulation and peer role-play education significantly increased knowledge of standard precautions within groups, with no statistically significant difference between the two methods.](../claims/both-methods-increase-standard-precaution-knowledge.md) [+W]
- [The standardized-patient simulation group outperformed the peer role-play group in the hand hygiene, personal protective equipment, respiratory etiquette, and worker safety performance subdomains, but not in safe injection practices.](../claims/sp-group-better-performance-subdomains.md) [+W]
- [Among third-year nursing students, infection control performance after education was significantly higher in the simulation using standardized patient group (20.50 ± 3.03) than in the peer role-play group (14.81 ± 3.04).](../claims/sp-simulation-higher-infection-control-performance-than-prp.md) [+W]
