# Add introductory coverage of Einstein field equations, metrics, geodesics and the Einstein summation convention to sophomore-level courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend that introductory GR coverage go beyond qualitative discussion to include the field equations, metrics, geodesics and the Einstein summation convention, noting that "Metrics, geodesics and the Einstein summation convention show up in areas of physics ranging from classical mechanics to quantum field theory". They observe that none of the six reviewed textbooks presents the field equations or explains how spacetime curvature is quantified. Such coverage adds a quantitative element and prepares students for advanced courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must be comfortable presenting tensor equations and the metric-geodesic relationships the article supplies
#### Constraints
- The article notes the field equations unpack into nonlinear, coupled partial differential equations, and that the math for uniform acceleration in S′ might be difficult for early undergraduates

### Target Learners
- sophomore-level modern physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- quantifying spacetime curvature
- calculating gravitational time dilation with the Schwarzschild metric
- building tools used across physics
