# Integrate simulation-based education into Malawian nursing and midwifery education via simulation centres and corners to alleviate clinical-teaching challenges

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study advocates integrating SBE into Malawian nursing and midwifery education to mitigate overcrowding at clinical sites and provide diverse learning experiences. The authors state that "simulation centres and corners should be established in all training institutions and health facilities to provide lecturers and students with ample time to bolster their clinical skills." SBE is positioned as a gap-filler, a supervision and assessment platform, and a means of mastering clinical skills without direct patient contact.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adequate infrastructure, resources, and skilled lecturers, which the abstract states successful implementation requires
- Formal SBE training for lecturers and clinical instructors, which the discussion says should be emphasised
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on qualitative perceptions from lecturers, clinical instructors, and students in Malawi, not on measured outcomes of implementation
- The authors note some collected data may have been influenced by researchers' prior interaction with participants

### Target Learners
- Final-year nursing and midwifery students in Malawian training institutions
- Nursing and midwifery lecturers and clinical instructors in Malawi

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical skill acquisition and competency development
- Interprofessional teamwork and communication
- Supervision and assessment of clinical performance
