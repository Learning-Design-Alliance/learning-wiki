# Use the vampire-themed Brewster-angle demonstration to visualize polarization properties of reflected light

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A demonstration sequence in which a 'secret' LCD screen shows vampire characters whose colours are complementary, and transparent plates at the Brewster angle act as the missing polarizer. Students observe reflected and transmitted images, see contrast peak near the polarizing angle, and check that reflected plus transmitted light recovers the incident white light. The authors state the experiences "must be preceded by a qualitative explanation of the underlying physics of polarizing filters" and that it is desirable students know how an LCD screen works.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A qualitative explanation of the underlying physics of polarizing filters before the demonstration
- Students should ideally have an idea of how an LCD screen works
#### Constraints
- The effects are not obvious and their explanation to students can be arduous, per the authors

### Target Learners
- high school students
- university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- visualizing properties of the polarizing angle
- understanding changes in polarization on reflection and transmission
