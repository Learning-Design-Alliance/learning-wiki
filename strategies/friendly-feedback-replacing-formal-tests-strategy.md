# Replace frequent pass/fail tests with friendly, autonomy-supportive feedback sessions in high-stakes preclinical courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy replaces weekly formal pass/fail oral tests with a friendly feedback conversation in which students' preparation is checked and feedback is given on the preparation, not on the person. Its teacher behaviors follow self-determination theory: "focusing on knowledge and not on knowledge gaps, considering students' perspective, giving feedback on preparation and not on personality". Students remain responsible for how they act on the feedback. In the TRUST study this condition lowered stress, anxiety and negative affect and raised self-efficacy, positive affect and intrinsic motivation without impairing perceived exam preparation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers receive instruction on the autonomy-supportive feedback principles in a preparative session before the course starts, as done in the study.
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted at one university in the anatomy dissection course, and performance was measured only via self-perception, so objective academic performance effects remain untested.

### Target Learners
- first- and second-year medical students in the anatomy dissection course

### Target Learning Goals
- anatomy knowledge acquisition while reducing test-related stress and supporting intrinsic motivation and self-efficacy
