# Introduce new science topics in students' own language through fictional dialogues that address common misconceptions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The curriculum opens each section with fictional dialogues between archetypal students that use "student-derived language" gathered from the project's research and "address commonly held misconceptions when appropriate." Students think about each dialogue, choose which fictional speaker they most agree with, and explain why, surfacing their preexisting ideas for later review. The design rests on research the authors cite that students learn better when new concepts are based upon their existing ideas about the world.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to research-documented student misconceptions and student-derived phrases for the target audience
#### Constraints
- Dialogues address misconceptions only when appropriate

### Target Learners
- general-education (GE) undergraduate introductory astronomy students

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting new cosmology concepts to students' existing ideas and confronting common misconceptions
