# Use isomorphic problem sets to prompt students to generate both mathematical and conceptual problems from the same content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
To reduce the gap between time spent on algebraic procedures and qualitative descriptions, the authors recommend drawing on characteristics of isomorphic sets of physics problems: having students generate quantitative and conceptual problems around the same content. They suggest that "generating both mathematical and conceptual problems from the same content may increase reﬂection of the content beyond the utilization of mathematical representations", and that if students begin with algebraic procedures, qualitative descriptions can then be explored.
