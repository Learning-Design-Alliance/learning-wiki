# Blend identical pre-class theory teaching with group-based virtual simulation case practice in the internship class

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's VR-group course design: both groups received the same pre-class PPT teaching of five typical structural heart disease cases and after-class video materials, with total learning time of no less than 20 hours. In the internship class, VR students were divided into small groups, each randomly selecting one case from the virtual simulation assessment mode and working together in the system on "consultations, physical examinations, and cardiac ultrasound operations," guided by a cardiac specialist explaining section operation and anatomical structure identification. This preserves a common theoretical foundation while adding integrated simulation practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the virtual simulation platform and teacher monitoring through the online course discussion area
- Randomization and comparable baseline demographics between groups for evaluative use
#### Constraints
- The study was single-center, with a relatively small sample and short observation time, mainly as a supplement to undergraduate clinical medicine courses

### Target Learners
- undergraduate clinical medicine students in their fifth semester

### Target Learning Goals
- cardiac ultrasound section operation and measurement
- case-based diagnostic and differential diagnostic reasoning
