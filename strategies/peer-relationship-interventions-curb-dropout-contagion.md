# Adapt interventions that leverage peer relationships to curb the social contagion of dropout in high schools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Beyond improved monitoring, the authors suggest prevention programs "could achieve better results by explicitly taking social networks into account". Interventions that directly leverage peer relationships have improved peer norms and curbed contagion of problematic behaviors like substance use or suicide attempts; peer cooperation strategies fostering positive interactions between at-risk and well-adjusted youth in middle school reduced negative peer influences and alcohol use. The authors propose adapting such interventions for the high school context to curtail dropout contagion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adaptation of existing peer-leverage intervention models to the high school context
#### Constraints
- The cited peer-leverage evidence concerns outcomes other than dropout (substance use, suicide attempts, alcohol use) and middle school settings

### Target Learners
- High school adolescents, including at-risk youth in disadvantaged communities

### Target Learning Goals
- High school perseverance and dropout prevention
