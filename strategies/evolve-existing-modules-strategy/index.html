Evolve existing disciplinary modules rather than replacing them with new courses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article proposes a methodological framework destined to force disciplinary course modules to evolve rather than to replace them wholesale. This strategy treats existing modules as the starting point and applies structured transformations to integrate professional competencies and high-level cognitive skills. The approach preserves disciplinary content while shifting the pedagogical methods used to teach it.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Existing disciplinary course modules must be available as the object of evolution
  • A methodological framework must guide the transformation

Constraints¶

  • Demonstrated in higher education contexts through three case studies

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • disciplinary knowledge
  • professional competencies
  • high-level cognitive skills

Claims¶

  • Evolved disciplinary modules produced more motivating and more efficient learning outcomes closer to employer expectations [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Integrate computational thinking into existing disciplinary teaching across grade bands rather than adding it on
  • Embed subject-specific web credibility modules into existing courses rather than creating standalone courses
  • Integrate AI literacy across grades and subject-area learning rather than confining it to electives
  • Embed AI literacy across disciplines by identifying intersections with existing content and using case studies, interdisciplinary modules, and collaborative projects
