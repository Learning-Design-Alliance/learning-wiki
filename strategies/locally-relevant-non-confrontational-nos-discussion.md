# Instructors should provide opportunities for students to discuss the nature of science and attempts to redefine it, using locally relevant, non-confrontational activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author's closing recommendation: instructors, especially of courses for non-science majors and current or future educators, should both clearly discuss the nature of science and "provide opportunities for students to think about and discuss together the nature of science and attempts to redefine science through ID or other movements." He argues the locally relevant and intentionally non-confrontational nature of the activity lets students engage without being put on the defensive.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Background instruction on the nature of science, including careful definition of terms
- A locally relevant controversy, such as state science standards the students will teach under
#### Constraints
- The author notes a more stark confrontation with student inconsistencies might force a change in viewpoint but was not the point of the exercise and may not succeed given deep-seated convictions

### Target Learners
- non-science majors
- current or future educators

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the nature of science
- evaluating attempts to redefine science

## Claims

- [In a 2007 class of 27 elementary education majors, no group or individual stated that ID should be defined as science, and most groups concluded ID is not science and should not be taught in science class](../claims/id-discussion-most-groups-id-not-science.md) [+W]
- [Students' objections to the word \"natural\" tended to correlate with their later \"fairness\" or \"both sides\" arguments for teaching ID in science class](../claims/id-discussion-natural-objections-correlate-fairness.md) [~W]
- [About six students objected that the word \"natural\" in the standards' definition of science was too limiting, with some suggesting it be removed](../claims/id-discussion-natural-word-objections.md) [~W]
- [About 25% of the class stated ID should be taught in science classrooms even while agreeing it is not science, an inconsistency the author flags as discouraging](../claims/id-discussion-quarter-taught-in-science-class-inconsistency.md) [-W]
