# Provide students with a faculty-level list of EBM-related terms and explicitly teach the use of PubMed/Medline and the Cochrane Library

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Two concrete recommendations follow from the survey's knowledge and retrieval findings. First, the authors suggest "a list of EBM-related terms which is constructed at faculty level, handed out and recommended for medical and health sciences faculty students during their studies might improve the transmission of EBM-related knowledge," targeting poorly understood terms such as intention-to-treat analysis. Second, the marked under-use of the Cochrane Library and PubMed by untrained students leads them to emphasise "teaching how to use these data retrieval systems for medical decision making."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty-level coordination to construct and distribute the shared term list; access to PubMed/Medline and the Cochrane Library during training
#### Constraints
- Knowledge and retrieval habits were self-reported; the article notes these may not reflect the real use students will make of EBM later as professionals

### Target Learners
- medical students
- health sciences students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of EBM-related terminology
- searching and evaluating scientific literature using professional databases
