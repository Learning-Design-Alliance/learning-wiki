# Add approximation-identification parts to standard problems so students decide what to ignore and when that is reasonable

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the block-pellet-spring problem, part 3 asks: "you must make a number of simplifying assumptions, some of which are stated in the problem and some of which are not. Discuss the additional approximations which you had to make to solve the problem." The author lists the stated assumptions (frictionless table, rigid support, horizontal table) plus four tacit ones (ideal Hooke's law spring, ignoring gravity on the pellet, ignoring air resistance, instantaneous pellet-block collision), and argues students rarely share the expert's blend that makes these choices automatic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems must pair the standard calculation with a part asking students to identify and justify the unstated approximations
#### Constraints
- The assumptions are reasonable only for "normal everyday" parameters (block of a few hundred grams, pellet of a few grams at a few meters per second, a typical hand-stretchable spring)

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- deciding what matters and what can be ignored
- building physical intuition
