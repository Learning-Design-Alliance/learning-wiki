Extend Jigsaw research to other grammar topics, language skills, and cooperative techniques¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In the discussion the author proposes a future research agenda: "various studies can be made in connection with both various topics of grammar and speaking, writing, reading and listening skills", and that studying not only Jigsaw I but also other cooperative learning techniques "will lead to more reliable results." This frames the present null result as limited to one topic area and one technique in one school.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Further studies across varied grammar topics, the four language skills, and multiple cooperative techniques

Constraints¶

  • The present study covered only spelling and punctuation rules with Jigsaw I in one socio-economically poor middle school

Target Learners¶

  • middle school students learning Turkish

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Turkish grammar, speaking, writing, reading and listening skills

Claims¶

  • Jigsaw I produced no significant difference in attitudes toward the Turkish course compared with conventional teaching [+W]
  • Jigsaw I produced no significant difference in grammar success scores compared with conventional teacher-centered teaching in grade-8 Turkish grammar [+W]
  • Prior Turkish research overwhelmingly reports positive achievement effects of cooperative learning techniques, in contrast to this study's null result [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Train students in cooperative learning before applying cooperative techniques
