Integrate metacognitive reading strategy tasks into regular EFL reading courses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the significant experimental-group gains and the null control-group result, the article recommends that teachers "assist EFL/ESL students to improve reading comprehension by integrating meta-cognitive reading strategy tasks in their regular English reading courses". It further suggests curriculum designers and instructors conceptualize, plan and implement reading strategies in reading classrooms, and notes the approach needs only teacher modeling, feedback and practice opportunities rather than sophisticated materials.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher modeling and feedback
  • opportunities for students to practice each strategy until they can read independently

Constraints¶

  • Effects were demonstrated under a specific term of training; the article states long-term follow-up treatment is needed to determine if effects persist

Target Learners¶

  • EFL/ESL university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English reading comprehension
  • strategic reading

Affordances¶

  • Metacognitive Sociocultural Reading Theory

Claims¶

  • Explicit reading strategy instruction produced a statistically significant pre-to-post gain in reading comprehension for Ethiopian EFL university students [+W]
  • Comprehension gains appeared on main-idea and inference items but not on word-meaning-guessing items [+W]
  • Skill-based teaching alone produced no statistically significant pre-to-post gain in reading comprehension in the control group [+W]
  • Explicit instruction of four reading comprehension strategies with short stories yields higher reading comprehension post-test scores than regular syllabus instruction in EFL preparatory students [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Incorporate explicit direct instruction of metacognitive reading strategies into the reading curriculum
  • Incorporate technology-based reading activities into classroom reading tasks and let students experiment with reading online while learning strategies
