# Authenticity trigger-event reflective journaling feeding a Best Authentic Self narrative with goal setting

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A two-part learner-centred activity sequence. Students first journal and diary authentic leadership experiences using Kolb-based reflective learning, identifying and reflecting on 20 incidents that challenged or developed their authenticity, guided by Cooper et al.'s (2005) trigger-event types (major life events, sensational or routine events, artificial triggers, culminations of small events). They then review their journal entries to write a Best Authentic Self narrative, adapted from Peterson's (2006) Best Self exercise, describing experiences of being authentic, why authenticity grew or was thwarted, and goals for development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reflective learning framework incorporating times when authenticity was developed and challenged
- Explanation and examples of trigger events given to students as part of the reflective exercise handout
#### Constraints
- The article notes the approach may be culturally bound, as students in Asian countries may prefer teacher-centred assessment and outcomes

### Target Learners
- year two degree students in an elective leadership course

### Target Learning Goals
- learning authentic leadership
- self-awareness of authentic experiences
- setting personal developmental goals
