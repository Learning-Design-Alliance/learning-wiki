# Establish ways of understanding research traditions behind English-language articles, including disciplinary thesauri and collaborative systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that the field establish ways of understanding the traditions behind research articles written in English by non-English language authors and build collaborative systems to illustrate nuances. As a concrete direction, it suggests that "future work could investigate the use of disciplinary thesauri and relate to the different traditions, also including examples of research articles", citing the Slovakian translation vocabulary of EdTech neologisms as a precedent. It also calls for publication language and local research practice to be considered more prominently for research to yield impact.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaborative systems and thesauri must relate terms to the different national research traditions rather than assume literal translations carry the same meanings
#### Constraints
- The article notes literal translations may not reflect the same meanings, e.g., the connotation of media in education cannot be sufficiently gathered from any literal translation to German

### Target Learners
- EdTech researchers publishing in English as a second language
- International EdTech journal readers and reviewers

### Target Learning Goals
- Interpreting English-language EdTech research in light of the traditions behind it
- Cross-country collaboration on shared challenges such as the digital divide and data privacy
