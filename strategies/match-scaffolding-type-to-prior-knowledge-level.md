# Match scaffolding type to learners' prior professional knowledge level

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends organizing learning environments for diagnostic competences by matching scaffolding to the learner's knowledge base: learners with low prior knowledge benefit from scaffolding with more guidance and lower self-regulation demands (examples, roles), while learners with sufficient conceptual knowledge and application experience benefit from scaffolding allowing higher degrees of freedom, such as built-in reflection phases. The meta-analysis was designed to generate evidence on whether the professional knowledge base moderates scaffolding effects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An assessment of learners' prior professional knowledge base (low vs. high) before selecting scaffolding type
#### Constraints

### Target Learning Goals
- advancement of diagnostic competences through scaffolded problem-solving
