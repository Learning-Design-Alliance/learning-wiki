# Use eye-catching physical demonstrations to begin mathematics outreach at busy science fairs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend opening outreach conversations with a striking physical demonstration, since first impressions determine whether secondary school students engage at all: "A physical demonstration can be an eye-catching way to begin an activity or spark oﬀ a conversation about mathematics." They motivate this by the noisy competition for attention at fairs with hundreds of exhibitors, "possibly involving loud music and/or dancing robots!", and the risk that "you'll lose them in seconds" if the opening sounds boring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A device that is simple to build and operate, illustrating a mathematical model of a real phenomenon
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is framed for crowded event settings such as science fairs, where many competing exhibitors vie for attention

### Target Learning Goals
