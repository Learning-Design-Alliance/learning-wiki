# Run the Methods course as a summer workshop taught by a master inservice teacher, with LPA follow-up through action research teams

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a concrete recipe for university-supported professional development: a Methods of Physics Teaching course taught by a master inservice teacher under the "peer teaching principle," scheduled as a summer workshop. From their experience the authors report that "a 'workshop format' which immerses the teachers in collaborative efforts for the better part of each day for a minimum of four weeks is most effective, because it stimulates the formation of strong working relationships among the teachers." The course should incorporate physics education research results, best curriculum materials, technology use, and collaborative learning experience, and the Local Physics Alliance should follow up with action research teams that solve practical classroom problems and share results with peers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A local alliance of physics teachers to assure sufficient enrollment in advance
- A master inservice teacher willing and prepared to teach the course, ideally with adjunct professor status
- University scheduling of the course and award of academic credit
#### Constraints
- A two semester course is needed to cover the main topics of introductory physics; summer scheduling is much preferred over evening or Saturday schedules; rural teachers need expense support to attend

### Target Learners
- inservice high school physics teachers
- preservice physics teachers mixed into the course

### Target Learning Goals
- pedagogical content knowledge integrating science content, curriculum, learning, teaching and students
- capacity to conduct action research on classroom practice
