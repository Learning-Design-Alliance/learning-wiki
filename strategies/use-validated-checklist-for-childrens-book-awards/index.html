Use a validity-tested criterion-based checklist when awarding or selecting children's books¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A strategy for centres, juries, parents, teachers, and publishers: assess children's books with a checklist whose validity has been tested, covering design, content, and instructional qualities together. The article recommends that "this checklist may be used by centres for children' s literature in diverse communities and cultures" and argues that assessment based only on content, omitting design and instructional qualities, should be abolished. It contrasts this with award bodies whose criteria's validity and reliability have not been tested.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Raters must learn how to use the checklist before assessing books

Constraints¶

  • The article calls for feedback on the checklist's validity across communities and cultures and for briefer versions, noting it is time-consuming

Claims¶

  • Trained raters given quality children's books rate their content qualities significantly higher than raters given non-quality books [+W]
  • Trained raters given quality children's books rate their design qualities significantly higher than raters given non-quality books [+W]
  • Trained raters given quality children's books rate their instructional qualities significantly higher than raters given non-quality books [+W]
  • Criterion-referenced tests require empirically-based construct validation studies [+W]
