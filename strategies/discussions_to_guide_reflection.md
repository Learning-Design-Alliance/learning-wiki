# Discussions to Guide Reflection

## Description
Invite students to think about their thinking. Conduct discussions about students' problem-solving processes. Invite students to share their metacognition, reveal their intentions, detail their strategies for solving a problem, describe their mental maps for monitoring their problem-solving process, and reflect on the strategy to determine its adequacy. During these kinds of rich discussions, students learn how to listen to and explore the implications of each other's metacognitive strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A classroom culture that recognizes individual paths of improvement, electronic tools for easy sharing, and a commitment to collective support
#### Constraints
- Discussions must be respectful in order for learners to be open and honest in their discussion.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Foster metacognition, enhance problem-solving skills, and build habits of empathy, flexibility, and persistence.

### Affordances
- Builds a sense of community, provides diverse perspectives, and encourages mutual support and learning. It also reduces individual competition and promotes collaborative problem-solving.

### Personalization
- The level of personalization is limited in that the discussion takes place with an entire group; students should have opportunity to discuss problems that are related to their individual learning.
