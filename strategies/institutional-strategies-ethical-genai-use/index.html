Strengthen institutional policies, ethics education, verification, disclosure, and data protection to support ethical GenAI use¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Based on the model's findings, the authors recommend a set of institutional actions: "strengthen clear institutional policies, integrate ethics education across the curriculum, promote critical verification of AI-generated content, encourage transparent disclosure of AI use, and reinforce data protection practices." These recommendations target the dimensions the model found to contribute to ethical GenAI use, and the authors state the evidence should be translated into educational programs, AI disclosure protocols, and evaluation criteria for algorithm-assisted academic work.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional capacity to issue clear policies and embed ethics education across the curriculum

Constraints¶

  • The supporting evidence is cross-sectional and predictive, so the recommendations rest on associations rather than demonstrated causal effects of interventions

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students
  • university administrators and faculty designing AI governance

Target Learning Goals¶

  • responsible and ethical academic use of generative AI

Affordances¶

  • Six Dimension Model Ethical Genai Use

Related Strategies¶

  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
  • Build ethical AI integration on four coordinated levels: faculty professional development, ethics curricula, clear institutional guidelines, and interdisciplinary updating
  • Foster communities of practice for AI governance instead of rigid top-down policies
  • Institutions should respond to GenAI with clear governance frameworks and structured professional training programs for staff.
  • Establish comprehensive institutional AI governance frameworks including task forces, roadmaps, and transparent policies
  • Fund AI literacy initiatives integrating AI ethics into curricula and adapting assessments
  • Institutions should adopt equity-informed GenAI policies, require AI-use disclosure, and educate reviewers on World Englishes variation
  • Strategic steps to move beyond informal AI innovation: recognise redesign in workload models, embed innovation in promotion criteria, set institutional AI principles, redesign assessment, and fund scaling pilots
  • Accrediting bodies and professional organizations should provide AI policy frameworks integrating academic integrity, data ethics, and equitable access, and programs should invest in faculty development
  • Institutions should move toward deliberate, transparent policies governing AI use in admissions and reconsider what application essays should assess
  • Design educators should strengthen primary research, foster critical verification habits, and move students from consumers to makers of GenAI tools
