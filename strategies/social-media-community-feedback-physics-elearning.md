# Integrate social media channels to build community and provide timely feedback in virtual physics courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends using social media messaging and group tools — WhatsApp, Slack, Discord, GroupMe, Google Hangouts, Twitter threads, and Facebook groups — to give timely feedback outside class hours and to strengthen students' sense of community, since "Microblogging (e.g. Twitter) has been shown to combat this flaw, strengthening a sense of community in virtual classes within higher education." It argues such integration can be key to battling low retention rates in virtual education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors should discuss and include a syllabus statement about proper conduct and expectations for online privacy and consult university social media/privacy guidelines
#### Constraints
- Grades, records, or personal information must never be shared via platforms not maintained by the university due to privacy laws
- Not all students have smartphone access, and instructors often rate social media poorly on perceived usefulness

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in virtual or hybrid courses

### Target Learning Goals
- sense of community in online courses
- timely instructor feedback outside class hours
