# Balance gamified interactive activities with metacognitive reflection and blend traditional explanations with interactive tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implication for practice is balanced instructional design: while interactive tools can greatly enhance motivation and engagement, educators must structure them carefully to avoid overstimulation and ensure cognitive depth. It specifically recommends "combining gamified quizzes with metacognitive reflection activities" to sustain both enjoyment and conceptual rigour, and proposes that "Blended learning models — mixing traditional explanations with interactive tasks — may offer the optimal strategy" to leverage both methods' strengths.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interactive tools must be pedagogically scaffolded so stimulation does not overwhelm cognitive processing
#### Constraints
- Derived from one study in a single computing course with 100 students; the authors call for cross-disciplinary comparison to determine when interactive learning is most effective

### Target Learners
- University undergraduates, particularly in computing and STEM contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustaining conceptual depth and reflective thinking alongside engagement and enjoyment
