# Use exaggerated versions of dissipative scenarios in which thermal energy is perceptible to support learners in accepting imperceptible thermal energy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy has learners relate an original dissipative scenario to an exaggerated version with much more total energy, in which the dissipated thermal energy would be associated with perceptible warmth, and then infer thermal energy in the original scenario. The authors report that "teachers resolve these challenges by relating the original scenario to an exaggerated version in which the dissipated thermal energy is associated with perceptible warmth." Exaggeration can be produced by imagining the scenario repeats many times or by relating it to an extreme version.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An exaggerated comparison scenario in which the thermal effects become perceptible, such as repetition building up warmth or an extreme high-energy version
