# Use community hospital clinical clerkships to increase student exposure to common diseases and community-oriented care

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend leveraging regional hospital rotations because university hospitals often manage complex or rare cases, while the NMLE increasingly emphasizes common (level A) diseases. The article states that "clinical clerkships at community hospitals could be strategically utilized if the goal is to enhance exposure to common diseases", and that topics rising in the topic-modeling analysis, such as care, daily living support, and community health care, are well demonstrated through community hospital clerkships where students encounter patients needing long-term care and interprofessional collaboration. A balanced integration of university-affiliated and regional rotations is advocated.

## Design Implications

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- exposure to common diseases, long-term care, and community-oriented, patient-centered care competencies
