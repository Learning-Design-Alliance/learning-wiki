# Design simulation-based IPE scenarios that give every profession an active problem-solving role

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that scenario development allow active participation across professions, since one included study's scenarios were "primarily focused on medication errors caused by nurses or doctors, with limited opportunities for pharmacists to actively engage in problem-solving". It concludes that educational methods providing each profession opportunities to practice in given situations can enhance training effectiveness, and that scenarios incorporating multiple viewpoints can maximize training effectiveness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios must provide each profession with opportunities to practice in given situations
- Practitioners need a deep understanding of the purpose and essence of IPE education
#### Constraints
- Merely presenting the concept of IPE is insufficient; the review found pharmacy students' self-efficacy did not increase when scenarios limited their active role

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students
- undergraduate medical students
- pharmacy students

### Target Learning Goals
- interprofessional collaboration
- problem-solving in collaborative care teams
