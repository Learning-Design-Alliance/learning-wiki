# Scientific Community Labs: open-ended empirical questions with student-designed experiments and group reports

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Reformed labs replace detailed protocols with a half-page instruction sheet containing a one-sentence empirically answerable question (e.g., 'What affects the acceleration of a rolling object?'). Students in groups of four design an experiment with available equipment, make measurements, analyze results, and present them to the class. Labs are held for two-hour periods with 20–24 students and one TA, with two weeks per laboratory giving four hours to plan, implement, analyze, and discuss each experiment. Group reports reduce TA grading load by a factor of eight, allowing thorough written feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions must be answerable empirically with available equipment
- Students must work in groups of four and write group reports
#### Constraints
- Labs are deliberately scheduled before the related lecture topic, keeping the purpose centered on physics as an empirical science rather than following up on theoretical treatment

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the construction of knowledge through measurement and analysis
- experimental design
