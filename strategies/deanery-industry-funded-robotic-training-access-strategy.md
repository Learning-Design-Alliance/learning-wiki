# Multi-source funding model to remove trainee financial barriers to robotic training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a resource-efficient funding arrangement in which the cost per trainee (approximately £1,500 covering Versius Trainer access, course materials and simulation resources) was fully reimbursed by the local postgraduate deanery, online modules and the first assist course were subsidised by the robotic manufacturer through an educational partnership, and consultant trainers contributed under existing NHS contracts. The authors state that "Financial and educational support through the Deanery, individual hospitals and industry is integral to the success of robotic training", and that this eliminated financial burden on trainees.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Commitment of training stakeholders: consultant surgeons, deanery representatives and industry
- Institutional support integrating training duties into existing clinical and educational roles
#### Constraints
- In this pilot, access was limited to six STs due to resource constraints

### Target Learners
- General surgical specialist trainees without independent funding for robotic courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Equitable access to robotic surgical training regardless of trainee seniority or personal finances
