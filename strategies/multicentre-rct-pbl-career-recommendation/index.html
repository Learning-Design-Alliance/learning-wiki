Strategies

Conduct multi-centre, multi-country randomised controlled trials with adequate confounding adjustment to clarify PBL-career associations¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 3 against) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that the ideal way to clarify the conflicted results on PBL and career choice would be through a multi-centre, multi-country randomised-control trial with a large study population. It further calls for new longitudinal studies with good power that adequately adjust for confounding variables, noting that evidence has only been conducted in a small selection of the countries that use PBL in their medical education.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Large study population with good statistical power
  • Adequate adjustment for confounding variables
  • Multi-centre and multi-country design

Constraints¶

  • Current evidence is limited to a small selection of countries using PBL, with multiple studies from the same medical schools (three at University of New Mexico, two at McMaster, two at Harvard)

Target Learners¶

  • medical students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • career choice formation in medical education

Claims¶

  • Kaufman et al. found significantly more PBL students retained or switched to an interest in primary care during medical school (P = 0.05) [+W]
  • Matsui et al. found more non-PBL graduates working in primary care (9% difference) with no significance reported [-W]
  • Three studies reported a significantly increased number of PBL graduates working in primary care compared to non-PBL graduates [+W]
  • PBL-based medical curricula do not predispose students to a career in a specific specialty, with 7 of 11 studies showing no significant difference in career choice between PBL and non-PBL graduates [-W]
  • One study found significantly fewer PBL graduates working in primary care, with the authors suggesting medical school admission as a confounding factor [-W]
  • Woodward et al. found no significant difference in specialty choice but PBL graduates held a greater number of certificates in family medicine [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Rigorously compare PBL with traditional methods before broad PGME implementation, using larger samples, controls, and follow-up
