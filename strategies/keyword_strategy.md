# Keyword Strategy

## Description
The keyword strategy is a mnemonic technique that enhances memory by linking new information to keywords already encoded in memory. This involves identifying a keyword that sounds similar to the new word and is easily represented by a picture or drawing. The teacher then creates a picture that connects the word to be learned with its definition. This strategy is particularly effective when the information is new to students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of relevant keywords, creation of visual aids (pictures or drawings), teaching students the association process, and regular review.
#### Constraints
- May not be suitable for abstract concepts or words that lack easily identifiable keywords. Effectiveness relies on the quality and relevance of the selected keywords and associated imagery.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- K-12 and higher education learners, especially beneficial for those with learning disabilities or difficulty memorizing new vocabulary.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve vocabulary retention, link new information to existing memory, enhance visual association with new concepts, facilitate recall of definitions

### Affordances
- Enhances encoding of new vocabulary by associating it with familiar keywords and visual imagery. Improves memory recall by providing a concrete link between the word and its definition. Makes learning new words more engaging and memorable.

### Personalization
- Teachers can tailor keywords and pictures to the interests and backgrounds of their students, making the learning experience more relevant and engaging. Students can also participate in generating their own keywords and images to enhance personalization.

## Assessment Evidence
- Improved vocabulary test scores, increased ability to recall definitions, enhanced application of new vocabulary in context, and positive student feedback on the strategy.

## Impact
- Studies have shown that the keyword strategy significantly improves vocabulary acquisition and retention among students with and without learning disabilities. Research supports its effectiveness in helping students link new information to existing knowledge for better recall.

## Examples
- To teach 'carline' (witch), use the keyword 'car' and show a picture of a witch in a car. To remember the definition, think of the keyword (car), the picture (a car), and what was happening in the picture (a witch was in the car), leading to the definition (witch).
