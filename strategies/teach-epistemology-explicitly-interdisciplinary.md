# Teach epistemology explicitly and use biologically authentic examples in physics for life-science students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because biology students value resources like life-is-complex and function-implies-structure that can conflict with physics' toy models and fundamental laws, the article recommends instructors "teach not just the content but the epistemology explicitly" and create situations where students see the value of blending physics-style and biology-style thinking through biologically authentic examples. Lessons should explicitly demand resolution between the disciplines' epistemological resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Extensive negotiations among all relevant disciplines to build the course
- Students must bring in biology and chemistry knowledge in group-work recitations
#### Constraints
- Cannot take for granted that students will value toy models or the power of principles; their use must be justified

### Target Learners
- biology and life-science (pre-health-care) undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- valuing mathematical modeling in biology contexts
- blending physics and biology epistemological resources
