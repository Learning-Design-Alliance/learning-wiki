# Integrate collaborative learning into nursing education for newly acquired skills, with instructor numbers matching group numbers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's authors recommend, on the basis of their trial results, that instructors "integrate the collaborative learning method into the education system to learn the right knowledge and skills" and that "collaborative learning be implemented across different courses with a sufficient number of instructors in teaching basic nursing skills." The strategy is to use the Jigsaw-style group structure for newly acquired psychomotor skills, with group spokespersons sharing knowledge across groups, and to staff one instructor per group so each group receives adequate guidance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A sufficient number of instructors; the article states the number of teaching staff should be equal to the number of groups
- Small groups working interactively on distinct topic headings with cross-group knowledge sharing
#### Constraints
- The article's evidence comes from first-year nursing students at one university learning enteral nutrition; the authors call for multi-center, larger sample, or longitudinal studies to increase generalizability

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduate nursing students learning basic nursing skills

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquiring accurate knowledge and psychomotor skills for newly learned procedures, and strengthening self-directed learning skills
