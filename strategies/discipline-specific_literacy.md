# Discipline-Specific Literacy

## Description
Discipline-specific literacy involves teaching reading, writing, speaking, and thinking skills unique to each subject area. It recognizes that literacy practices vary across disciplines and that students benefit from explicit instruction in the specific ways texts are constructed, interpreted, and used in different fields. This approach reduces student anxiety by fostering deep engagement with content through interactive activities, enabling them to work through the material collaboratively. This strategy can be implemented using a read-write-think-discuss model to deeply engage students with course content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to discipline-specific mentor texts, graphic organizers, checklists, and vocabulary. Implementation involves interactive activities and opportunities for students to write for authentic audiences and purposes.
#### Constraints
- Instructors may need training to understand and teach discipline-specific literacy practices effectively. Availability of appropriate mentor texts and resources may vary across disciplines.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance comprehension of subject-specific content, improve discipline-specific communication skills, reduce anxiety around reading and writing in specific subjects, and foster deeper engagement with course content.

### Affordances
- Improves literacy skills by grounding them in content area knowledge, increases learner motivation and confidence, and supports effective communication within specific disciplines. It clarifies expectations for discipline-specific writing and promotes self-monitoring during the composition process.

### Personalization
- The types of mentor texts, graphic organizers, checklists, and vocabulary can be tailored to the specific discipline and level of the learners.
