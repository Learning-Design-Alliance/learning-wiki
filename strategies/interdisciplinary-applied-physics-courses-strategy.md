# Design interdisciplinary physics courses (e.g., physics for biology and chemistry majors) around applications of physics to the partner discipline without compromising standards

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 5 studies (2 qualitative, 2 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends specially designed courses for non-physics majors as a way to develop interdisciplinary degrees and promote interdisciplinary research. A merged physics course for biology and chemistry majors should be "designed as the application of Physics to Chemistry and Biology," including biophysics topics such as mechanics of body movement, electromagnetism of the nervous system, fluid mechanics of living systems, and chemical physics of bonding in proteins and lipids, with separately designed labs including physiology and anatomy experiments. Class demonstrations and simple theoretical and experimental projects make the course more interesting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Associated labs designed separately, including experiments giving an idea of physiology and anatomy
- Courses designed very carefully so they do not compromise on standards
#### Constraints
- If enrollment is not very large, the biology and chemistry versions can be merged into one course

### Target Learners
- biology and chemistry majors
- undergraduate non-physics science majors

### Target Learning Goals
- applying physics to biological and chemical systems
- developing interest in interdisciplinary research
