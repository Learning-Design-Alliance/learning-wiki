# Listening and reading for specific information

## Description
This strategy directs learners to extract particular details — names, dates, figures, key phrases, or specific ideas — from spoken or written input, rather than attempting exhaustive comprehension. Learners receive a purpose or task before engaging with the material, listen or read selectively, monitor their comprehension, and infer missing meaning from linguistic and contextual clues. It is a core comprehension strategy in second-language pedagogy and in academic study skills, where learners must process lectures, reports, and examinations efficiently.

## Design Implications

Selective attention to a stated purpose reduces the processing burden of full comprehension and directs working memory to task-relevant input [Relevancy of emphasis directs attention.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [+M]. Effectiveness depends on giving learners the purpose *before* exposure: tasks assigned after input begins produce unfocused processing and weaker extraction. Pre-teaching key vocabulary and activating relevant background knowledge further reduces the lexical and conceptual load of real-time comprehension [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Authentic or level-appropriate texts and audio with identifiable, extractable target information
- A pre-listening/pre-reading task that states the specific purpose (e.g., a table to complete, questions to answer)
- Explicit instruction and [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) modeling of how skilled listeners and readers locate and verify target information
- Guided practice with graduated task difficulty, followed by feedback on accuracy of extraction

#### Constraints
- Learners with limited vocabulary or decoding fluency cannot allocate attention to selective search; automatic word recognition is a prerequisite for higher-level comprehension [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Over-reliance on scanning for details can displace global comprehension — learners may extract facts without building a coherent mental model of the whole text [~M]
- Fast, unsegmented authentic audio can exceed processing capacity for lower-proficiency learners, causing them to lose the thread entirely [-M]
- Tasks that name the target too precisely (e.g., "listen for the word *tariff*") encourage word-matching rather than meaning-based comprehension [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Pre-task vs. while-task framing:** purpose given before exposure (stronger) vs. questions answered during or after (weaker but useful for assessment)
- **Modality:** listening tasks benefit from replay and segmentation; reading tasks allow self-paced re-reading and [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md)
- **Scaffolding intensity:** from word lists and partial transcripts down to unaided extraction as proficiency grows
- **Task format:** gap-fills, tables, schedules, mind maps, cause–effect charts, note-taking frames

### Target Learners
- Adult second-language learners transitioning to academic study or workplace training, where lecture and document comprehension are high-stakes [+M]
- Intermediate learners who have basic decoding fluency but lack strategic processing habits; beginners need vocabulary and fluency support first [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Exam candidates (e.g., IELTS, TOEFL) who must locate answers under time pressure

### Target Learning Goals
- Comprehension of specific factual information in extended input
- Strategic processing: setting purposes, monitoring comprehension, inferring from context
- Note-taking and information-transfer skills that support later study tasks

### Instructions
1. Activate relevant background knowledge and pre-teach essential vocabulary ([Activation](../elements/activation.md); [Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md))
2. Present the purpose and the extraction task (a table, questions, or graphic organizer) before exposure ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
3. Model the strategy on a short excerpt, verbalizing how to locate and verify target information ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md))
4. Have learners engage with the full material, extracting the targeted information ([Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md) or [Lectures](../elements/lectures.md))
5. Debrief: compare answers, discuss where information occurred and how it was identified, and have learners explain their reasoning ([Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md)) [+M]
6. Repeat with progressively longer, faster, or less scaffolded material

## Examples
- Learners complete a train schedule while listening to station announcements, extracting times and platform numbers.
- Students fill in a cause–effect chart while listening to a recorded university lecture on climate policy.
- ESL learners read a short news article to answer "who, what, when, where" questions, then justify their answers by pointing to the relevant sentences.
- IELTS preparation courses train candidates to read questions first, predict the type of answer (number, name, date), and listen for paraphrase rather than exact wording.
