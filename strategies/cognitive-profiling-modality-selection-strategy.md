# Integrate cognitive profiling and task-specific modality selection into surgical simulation training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy pairs assessment of individual cognitive profiles (e.g., OSIVQ classification into object visualizers, spatial visualizers, and verbalizers) with selection of a training modality matched to task complexity and learner characteristics. The article reports that VR training was most effective overall and "appears to be more inclusive, supporting a broader range of cognitive styles," and concludes that "these findings support the integration of cognitive profiling and task-specific modality selection into surgical training." It is intended to optimize both effectiveness and equity in surgical education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment instruments for cognitive style (the study used the OSIVQ) and baseline visuospatial ability (Titmus and mental rotation tests) must be administered before assigning learners to modalities.
#### Constraints
- The study was not planned to investigate thinking styles primarily, and reliance on self-report instruments such as the OSIVQ may limit the precision and objectivity of cognitive profiling; the authors state future research should explore the effectiveness of such individualized training approaches.

### Target Learners
- Medical students and surgical trainees without prior laparoscopic experience

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic and complex laparoscopic psychomotor skills, including depth perception, instrument handling, and bimanual coordination
