# Use scenario-based learning in teacher-education professional knowledge courses, with curricular activities fostering positive attitudes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that "Scenario-based learning method is applicable in the professional knowledge courses at the institutions bringing up teachers", and that curricula be arranged to include activities helping preservice teachers develop positive attitudes toward teaching. They further propose future scenarios delivered via video, other methods beside scenario-based learning, longitudinal assessment of attitudes from the first grade, and research on why preservice teachers choose teaching and on socio-psychological variables other than attitudes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios tied to real professional problems encountered during school practice, with opportunities for online and face-to-face discussion of solutions
#### Constraints
- Recommendations extend beyond what was tested: video scenarios, longitudinal monitoring and other methods were not evaluated in this study

### Target Learners
- preservice teachers in teacher-education institutions

### Target Learning Goals
- positive attitudes toward the teaching profession

## Claims

- [A scenario-based blended learning environment combined with teaching practice is associated with a statistically significant increase in preservice teachers' attitudes toward the teaching profession](../claims/scenario-blended-learning-positive-teaching-attitudes.md) [+W]
