# Progression of four simulations — narrow single slit, double slits, triple slits, wide single slit — as the heart of an interference and diffraction laboratory

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The laboratory centers on a sequence of simulations: first, a single narrow slit familiarized students with the simulation and showed more was needed for interference; then a double slit simulation revealed the conditions for constructive and destructive interference with visible waves and easy measurements; next, a triple slit experiment made students reconsider those conditions; finally, a wide single slit simulation showed diffraction effects arising from waves. The laboratory also included pen and paper work and short laser/slit experiments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need familiarity with the simulation controls before quantitative tasks, addressed via the single-slit baseline measurement
#### Constraints
- The laboratory was tailored for a particular class aimed at future physics majors, though tweaking for other courses should be straightforward

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in an instructional laboratory

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding conditions for constructive and destructive interference
- single-slit diffraction
- reasoning about extrema conditions in multi-slit systems
