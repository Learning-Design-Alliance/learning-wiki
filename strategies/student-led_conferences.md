# Student-Led Conferences

## Description
Student-led conferences (SLCs) are meetings where students take the lead in discussions with parents about their progress and learning, supported by the teacher. Unlike traditional parent-teacher conferences, SLCs involve students in preparing, reflecting on, and discussing evidence of their learning and growth, often through student portfolios. This approach empowers students to take responsibility for their learning, encourages goal-setting and assessment, and engages families in richer conversations about student progress.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires dedicated preparation time for students to select work samples, reflect on their progress, and practice presentations. Teachers need to provide guidance and structure to support student-led discussions. A student-led conference form can be used to guide questioning.
#### Constraints
- May require significant preparation time for students and teachers, especially when implementing for the first time. Some students may feel anxious or unprepared to lead discussions. Parents may initially be hesitant to shift from traditional conferences.

### Target Learning Goals

- Encourage student responsibility, engage families, promote reflection, set goals, demonstrate progress, and empower student voice.

### Affordances
- Promotes student ownership and accountability, enhances family engagement, fosters self-reflection, and improves communication skills. Reduces teacher workload by focusing on student-led discussions and insights.

### Personalization
- Adaptable for different grade levels by adjusting the complexity of reflection prompts and portfolio requirements. Flexible formats allow for student-selected work samples and individualized goals.

## Assessment Evidence
- Increased student engagement and ownership, improved communication skills, richer family-teacher interactions, and more student-driven goal setting.

## Impact
- Schools implementing SLCs report increased student responsibility, enhanced family engagement, and deeper understanding of student progress. Higher parent turnout has been observed due to increased student investment in the process.
