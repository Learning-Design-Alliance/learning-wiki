Strategies

Combine video clips with Small Group Activity techniques, using video-support techniques such as slowing playback, pre- and post-viewing comprehension sessions, and repeating or pausing scenes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that speaking teachers use video clips as supportive learning media together with Small Group Activity teaching-learning, because that combination produced the better speaking results in their study. It also lists concrete video-handling techniques: "slowing down the speed of the videos, having comprehension sessions pre- and post-viewing and repeating important scenes and/or pausing screenings" to help students understand the language in each video.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need access to suitable video clips and class time for small group discussion alongside viewing.

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from one quasi-experiment with two seventh-grade classes in Banda Aceh; the recommendation is the authors' own suggestion from that setting.

Target Learners¶

  • young EFL learners at junior high school level

Target Learning Goals¶

  • EFL speaking performance (fluency, vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, comprehensibility)

Claims¶

  • Both groups improved their speaking scores from pre-test to post-test, but the Small Group Activity group's mean gain (25.35) was larger than the Individual Activity group's (11.64) [+W]
  • The Small Group Activity group outperformed the Individual Activity group on all five measured speaking aspects (vocabulary, fluency, grammar, pronunciation, comprehension) [+W]
  • Video clips combined with Small Group Activities produced significantly higher EFL speaking post-test scores than video clips combined with Individual Activities for young learners [+W]
