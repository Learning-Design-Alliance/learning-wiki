# Administer the cognitive load self-report tool after simulation debriefing to identify excessive strain and adapt simulation teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using the adapted tool during simulation debriefing to inform teaching: 'By assessing students’ perceptions of cognitive load during simulation applications, the tool can help identify areas where students may experience excessive cognitive strain, enabling instructors to adapt their teaching strategies accordingly.' It also suggests the tool can evaluate the effectiveness of different simulation-based teaching methods and optimize scenario complexity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administer the tool after the debriefing session; in the study 'the questionnaire was completed by each student in a controlled and distraction-free environment'
#### Constraints
- Use with students without previous simulation experience 'might be premature given the anxiety and stress specific to a first simulation practice'

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students in simulation-based courses

### Target Learning Goals
- optimizing cognitive load in simulation design
- evaluating simulation-based teaching methods
