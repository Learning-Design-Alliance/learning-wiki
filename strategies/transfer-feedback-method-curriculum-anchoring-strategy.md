# Transfer the feedback method to new settings by anchoring MeSH terms in the local curriculum and using diverse question and participant samples

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' recommendation for adapting the method beyond the Charité PTM: pair the widely available LLM-plus-MeSH component with "a structure that connects the MeSH terms to the academic curriculum of the organizing institution", such as organ systems or medical disciplines, and ensure "samples of questions and test participants that are sufficiently diverse and cover all stages of the academic programme". Heterogeneous samples are needed so clustering yields separable response patterns and precursor relations are meaningful across knowledge levels.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A curricular grouping structure for MeSH terms adapted to the implementing faculty.
- Question sets spanning a wide range of knowledge levels and test-taker samples covering all study stages.
#### Constraints
- The methodology is not expected to be copied verbatim; adaptations are needed for curriculum, organization and student-body differences.
- Precursor analysis benefits from sizeable test data and may differ for smaller-scale tests.

### Target Learners
- students in other progress-testing faculties or medical programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- personalized, curriculum-aligned feedback on knowledge gaps
