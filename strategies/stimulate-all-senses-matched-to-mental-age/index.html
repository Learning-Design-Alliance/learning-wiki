Special-education teachers should use teaching strategies that stimulate all the senses, matched to learners' mental age¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In its Recommendations section the article advises that "The teachers specialized in special education need to use modern strategies in teaching wherein the learners are stimulated to use all the senses in various activities", and that mental-age differences among learners be taken into account before implementing any particular teaching strategy. Resource materials should conform to learners' mental age and the chosen strategy, and specialized committees should ensure families understand program structures so their involvement yields better results.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Resource materials must conform to the learners' mental age as well as to the teaching strategy applied

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation arises from a study limited to a Saudi Arabian sample of primary-school learners with learning disabilities surveyed in 2017

Target Learners¶

  • learners with learning disabilities in special education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • visual perception development
  • overcoming learning difficulties across academic and developmental domains

Claims¶

  • No significant difference in the experimental group's visual perception between measurements taken after the program and one month later [~W]
  • During the multi-sensory program all thirty participants identified the presented visuals and showed positive engagement [+W]
  • The experimental group's visual perception average rose from pre-test to post-test after the multi-sensory program [+W]
  • Primary-school learners with learning disabilities taught with a multi-sensory strategy scored higher on visual perception at post-test than learners taught with the traditional method [+W]
