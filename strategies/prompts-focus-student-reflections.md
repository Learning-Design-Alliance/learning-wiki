# Use reflection prompts to focus student reflections on desired topics, in both formal and informal environments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using explicit prompts to steer student reflection. In its conclusions it states that "In both formal and informal environments, prompts are effective at focusing student reﬂections to desired topics," opening the possibility for online scaffolding exercises leading to greater metacognitive awareness and associated curricular gains. Prompts succeed in helping students focus, stay on topic, and engage in metacognitive reflection, though they also constrain and depersonalize reflections relative to unguided journals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prompts adapted to the desired reflection topics, as the GRF questions and 2015 journal prompts were adapted from prior work
#### Constraints
- Explicit prompts combined with classroom framing resulted in reflections that were more constrained and less personal than unguided journals

### Target Learners
- first-year and sophomore STEM majors
- incoming first-generation and deaf/hard-of-hearing students in a summer program

### Target Learning Goals
- metacognitive awareness
- focused reflective practice
