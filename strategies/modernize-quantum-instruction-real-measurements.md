# Modernize quantum instruction by including how to measure the momentum of a single quanta and by teaching measurement beyond the von Neumann paradigm

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends updating quantum courses to teach how momentum of single quanta is actually measured, going beyond the von Neumann theory to describe real experiments built on single-quanta sensitivity, amplification, and geometry. The author states: "To modernize quantum instruction, we should definitely include a discussion of how to measure the momentum of a single quanta." He argues this prepares students for quantum-focused workforce needs of the second quantum revolution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must go beyond the von Neumann theory of measurement to describe how real measurements work
- Instruction should discuss the subtleties of the uncertainty principle, including its inapplicability to single shots
- Instruction should present ontic versus statistical interpretations of the wavefunction
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is aimed at quantum instruction for students preparing for quantum-focused workforce fields, particularly quantum sensing

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate quantum mechanics students
- students preparing for quantum-focused workforce careers

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding real quantum measurement practice
- understanding the scope and subtleties of the Heisenberg uncertainty principle
- preparation for the second quantum revolution workforce
