# Staggered multi-level implementation of curricular and co-curricular innovations, one element per year with three-year evaluation baselines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The program implemented innovations along two compartments (curricular and co-curricular) with a fixed timetable: only one element per year, each tied to the annual programmatic assessment plan, and each carried out a minimum of three years before evaluation. Elements included a first-year colloquium, CURE-based upper-level laboratories, public science outreach, Junior Review, conference attendance, expanded excellence awards, and experiential learning. The authors suggest a "multi-level approach is most efficacious" for programmatic innovation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Shared faculty responsibility for implementation and data collection; consultation of physics education research literature adapted judiciously to local context
#### Constraints
- The article states it is currently impossible to say which elements had the greatest impact, since implementation and evaluation periods overlapped and elements ran simultaneously

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics majors at small, non-PhD-granting liberal arts programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Increased major retention and graduation
- depth of experience for individual graduates
- women's retention in physics
