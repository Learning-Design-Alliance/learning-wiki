# Three student investigations on the mechanical Paul trap, done in isolation or together

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A sequence of three investigations on the 3D-printed mechanical Paul trap — balancing a ball on the stationary saddle, comparing trapping at 2 Hz and 3.5 Hz, and searching for the threshold frequency while varying ball diameter — that "can either be done in isolation or together to have the student(s) experience the physical phenomena in the mechanical Paul trap and visualize how a real Paul trap works." Abstract field strength is explained through multiple representations combining the analog and the simulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Investigation 2 prescribes repeating the experiment at least ten times for each frequency and noting the trapping time for each trial
#### Constraints
- Investigation 3 lets students choose their approach; some groups may rely heavily on computer simulation and others do not use it

### Target Learners
- students in lower parts of the educational system
- Swedish upper secondary mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- discerning rotation frequency as a critical aspect of trapping
- understanding saddle-point instability
- finding how ball size affects the threshold frequency

### Affordances
- Variation Theory Three Stages Marton
