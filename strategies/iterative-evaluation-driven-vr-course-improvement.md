# Iteratively improve a VR course through continuous evaluation and informal feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course was run twice a year with pre/post surveys each semester, and feedback drove concrete changes: an optical help-request button, a central Veyon monitoring system, staffing reduced from five to three (with tasks delegable to trained student tutors), hardware connection and ventilation fixes, and software realism updates such as reflex simulation. "Based on the evaluations and the informal feedback that some students had minor but relevant difficulties, particularly with the hardware, two further changes were introduced for subsequent semesters."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recurring course runs with per-semester evaluation surveys and a mechanism for collecting informal student feedback
#### Constraints
- A stated limitation is that conditions changed slightly over time due to the adjustments, complicating long-term comparison

### Target Learners
- medical students in compulsory simulation courses

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained course quality and usability of VR-based training
