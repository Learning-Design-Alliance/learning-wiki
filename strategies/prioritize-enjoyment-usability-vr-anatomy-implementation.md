# Prioritize enjoyment and usability when selecting and implementing VR anatomy software, including pre-session practice and technical support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educators emphasize enjoyment and ease of use when integrating VR anatomy software: design engaging, game-like or group-based learning activities around the software, choose devices and software based on usability, and build familiarity before assessed learning tasks. They state that "Promoting familiarity with the VR system before the learning tasks would enhance usability and improve students' confidence (self‐efficacy) in handling the system", via on-campus practice sessions and in-classroom technical support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to VR headsets and software; staff or technical support available during sessions; a practice session before the learning session
#### Constraints
- Students reported technical difficulties such as glitches and eye strain, underscoring the need for robust technical support; the recommendation rests on a small, single-institution study

### Target Learners
- higher education anatomy students (MSc and undergraduate)

### Target Learning Goals
- learning spatially complex anatomical structures such as the eye and ear

### Affordances
- Getamel Technology Acceptance Model Elearning
