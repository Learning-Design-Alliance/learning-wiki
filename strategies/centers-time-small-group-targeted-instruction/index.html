Deliver targeted small-group instruction to low-scoring children during existing centers time with counterbalanced teacher assignment¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's implementable delivery model: in each classroom, the eight lowest-scoring children were randomly assigned two each to patterning, reading, mathematics, and social studies instruction for 15 minutes three times per week during centers time from November through April. Because "each classroom teacher had the same number of children in each condition, teacher and classroom effects were controlled," and all children received useful instruction, which the authors say controlled Hawthorne effects. The design requires no special staffing beyond the classroom teacher.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An existing centers time devoted to individualized or small group activities
  • Random assignment of selected children to instructional conditions within each classroom

Constraints¶

  • Tested with first-graders who scored poorly on a patterning screening test in one urban district; the authors recommend future research vary ability levels, patterns, teaching methods, and measures

Target Learners¶

  • First-grade children with relatively poor understanding of patterns

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved reading and mathematics achievement on standardized tests

Claims¶

  • Patterning and mathematics groups outperformed the other groups on Woodcock-Johnson Mathematics Concepts, and patterning exceeded the other groups on all Key Math scales except Geometry and Multiplication [+W]
  • Patterning and reading groups both scored higher than mathematics and social studies groups on TOWRE Word and TERA reading measures, and patterning exceeded mathematics and social studies on the GORT [+W]
  • Patterning instruction produced significantly better performance than reading, mathematics, or social studies instruction on the patterning posttest and far generalization test [+W]
