# Introduce short open-ended and non-routine questions as a soft transition to performance-based evaluation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend that education systems transitioning to performance-based evaluation begin by developing measurement tools that include "a few short open-ended questions and non routine questions", so that student behaviors during problem solving can be analyzed to inform later stages. They also suggest short-term and long-term term papers designed to measure knowledge, method use, and reasoning, with curriculums rearranged accordingly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students' skill of producing opinions should be considered important and evaluated before the transition
#### Constraints
- The authors state it is very difficult or impossible to measure knowledge, method use and reasoning with multiple-choice, completion, or recall-based operation tests

### Target Learners
- 9th-grade mathematics students
- national education systems planning assessment reform

### Target Learning Goals
- reasoning
- modeling
- applying knowledge to real life
