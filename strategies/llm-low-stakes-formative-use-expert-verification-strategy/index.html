Frame LLM use in biomedical education around low‐stakes formative tasks with expert‐guided verification and rationale requests¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that LLM use in foundational biomedical education focus on low‐stakes formative applications such as "self‐testing, feedback, and faculty‐reviewed practice question generation instead of higher stakes assessment contexts". Educators should frame LLM use as a tool supporting lifelong critical thinking rather than an authoritative answer source, model good prompting, and have learners request answer rationales, evaluate reasoning, and verify outputs against authoritative sources and content experts. Educators should also consult discipline‐specific benchmarking data when choosing applications, given performance variability between disciplines.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Human content experts and authoritative resources must remain central so students can verify LLM outputs

Constraints¶

  • The article states these uses are most applicable to low‐stakes formative contexts rather than higher stakes assessment; caution is urged in complex or image‐focused fields such as pathology

Target Learners¶

  • pre‐clerkship medical students
  • students in foundational biomedical science courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self‐testing and formative feedback in biomedical disciplines
  • development of lifelong critical thinking skills and verification habits

Related Strategies¶

  • Treat AI as a guided support tool and explicitly build verification and skepticism into learning goals
  • Open a MOOC with low-stakes diverse diagnostic worksheets whose results are fed back as strong and weak points
  • Design learning-oriented LLM prompts around guided questions, step-by-step support, reflection, and a thinking partner rather than direct answers
  • Benchmark AI assistants on specialized educational tasks before classroom deployment
  • Apply error mitigation such as self-consistency before deploying LLM-generated help, and frame unmitigated LLM feedback as an imperfect source
  • Make output verification an individually accountable practice within team assignments via rubric requirements
  • Use LLMs cautiously as supplementary learning tools in dental education, with output verification and professional supervision
