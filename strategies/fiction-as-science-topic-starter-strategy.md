# Use fiction with balanced scientist portrayals as topic starters and discussion vehicles in science education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends selecting literary works whose portrayal of science suits the educational goal: Peev's The Photon Starship contains scientific speculations usable "as educational topic raisers", Stars and Waves "can be a good vehicle to introduce the audience to academic life, provided such points are addressed and discussed", and Copenhagen is "of more help for discussions on scientific ethics" than for physics content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Works must be chosen to match the discussion goal, and problematic portrayals must be addressed and discussed with the audience
#### Constraints
- Some candidate works are too long to be useful topic starters, and some portrayals (e.g. Inspector Morse) can hardly help as a science introduction

### Target Learners
- students
- general outreach audiences

### Target Learning Goals
- introducing scientific concepts, academic life, and scientific ethics
