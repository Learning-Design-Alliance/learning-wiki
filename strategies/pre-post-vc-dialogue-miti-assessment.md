# Assess MI skill change by MITI-coding users' own typed dialogue from precourse versus postcourse virtual client conversations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy is to evaluate course effectiveness by analysing "the users' inputted text from the V-CCT (precourse vs postcourse) using the Motivational Interviewing Treatment Integrity (MITI) code". Because the client's language is predetermined, the MITI assesses the health professional's own language. Coding covers four global scales (Cultivating Change Talk, Softening Sustain Talk, Partnership, Empathy) summarised into Technical and Relational Globals, plus ten behavioural counts such as percentage complex reflections and reflection-to-question ratio, with proficiency and competency thresholds provided.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Users complete a V-CCT conversation before and after the online MI course
- Two members of the research team code the inputted text using the MITI and the scores are averaged
#### Constraints
- No feedback is provided during the conversation flow in the pilot version, and users are not shown which responses are MI-consistent, to preserve objective assessment of skill development

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate health professional students completing the online MI course

### Target Learning Goals
- Demonstrate change in MI conversational skills between precourse and postcourse interactions
