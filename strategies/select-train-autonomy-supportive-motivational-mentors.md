# Select and train motivational mentors who regulate their own motivation and use autonomy-supportive behaviors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends selecting experts who "demonstrate consistently strong motivation and the ability to effectively regulate their own motivational states", with domain expertise because motivation is context-dependent. Practical mentors include classroom teachers, school counselors, academic advisors, and high-achieving peer mentors, possibly via a collaborative team approach. Mentors should avoid controlling strategies and instead demonstrate autonomy-supportive behaviors such as offering choices within structure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Specialized training in modeling motivation, feedback, scaffolding, and fading support
- Capacity to articulate motivational processes and break strategies into learnable components
#### Constraints
- Many educators currently rely on controlling motivational strategies such as external rewards, punishment, and grade-based compliance, which the article says undermine intrinsic motivation

### Target Learners
- Elementary through postsecondary students

### Target Learning Goals
- Motivational self-regulation
- Autonomy-supportive mentoring relationships
