# Professional associations and health institutions should jointly enhance CAM knowledge and incorporate CAM education into psychology education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation is a coordinated strategy in which professional associations and health institutions work together to raise clinical psychologists' CAM knowledge and embed CAM education in psychology training. Participants framed this as answering personal and institutional barriers, with associations organizing credible, inexpensive training and institutions providing support. As the conclusion states, "professional associations and health institutions should work together in enhancing knowledge of CAM and incorporating CAM education into psychology education."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Professional associations and health institutions must collaborate, since participants expected associations to organize credible training and institutions to fund and permit attendance.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a qualitative study of 43 Indonesian clinical psychologists and may not generalize to different cultural settings, as the author notes.

### Target Learners
- Clinical psychologists in public health centers
- Psychology students in professional training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge of complementary and alternative medicine treatments and their scientific evidence
- Cultural sensitivity in client psychoeducation about CAM

## Claims

- [Participants recommended teaching only CAM treatments that fit brief psychotherapy sessions, favoring NLP, hypnotherapy, brain gym, and EMDR](../claims/brief-session-cam-treatments-endorsed.md) [+W]
