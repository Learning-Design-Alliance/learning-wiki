# The co-constructed directed-arc representation (Fig. 4b) may be a useful tool for explicitly teaching the meaning and use of non-Cartesian unit vectors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors suggest that the representation Dave and Jules co-constructed—dashed directed arcs for angles and short arrows for unit vectors at a point—could be taught explicitly. They write that "the co-constructed representation of Fig. 4b might be a useful tool to explicitly teach students to help them better understand the meaning, use, and application of non-Cartesian unit vectors, especially 𝜃 and 𝜑." This is a forward-looking recommendation grounded in one observed episode.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students would need to construct or be shown the directed-arc and unit-vector-arrow representation for spherical coordinates
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is speculative, drawn from a single group's unique co-construction rather than a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate E&M students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the meaning, use, and application of spherical unit vectors theta-hat and phi-hat
