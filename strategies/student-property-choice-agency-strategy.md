# Balancing a defined protocol with student agency through a property-choice exploratory phase

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because input and agency are key CURE aspects, the article recommends giving students choice within a defined protocol. In a final-project CURE, students are asked "in an exploratory phase of the project, to identify another property to calculate and analyze"; they read about the property and discuss extraction with the instructor, with calculations of response properties like elastic moduli by finite differences as an example. In a single-session CURE, students "dig into further outputs and information provided by the code" and report something interesting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- "it is important that the tool should be rich enough to provide more information than the minimum of what is required in the project"
#### Constraints
- Additional property calculations typically would not "constitute reliable research data" given attainable student knowledge in the course

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in materials-related courses

### Target Learning Goals
- independent inquiry and creative ownership of a research question
