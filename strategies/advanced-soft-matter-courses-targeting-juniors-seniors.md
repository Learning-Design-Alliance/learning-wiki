# Offer advanced-level soft matter courses to third- and fourth-year undergraduates through physics, biomedical engineering, and chemistry and biochemistry departments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on survey findings that third- and fourth-year undergraduates showed the greatest improvement and that most respondents supported new courses, the article recommends offering soft matter courses at advanced level within physics, biomedical engineering, and chemistry and biochemistry departments. Respondents agreed that "the government should increase the funding in soft matter research" and that WPI should open a soft matter course. The article argues juniors and seniors already have adequate background knowledge, making interest easiest to boost, whereas the material may be too hard for first- and second-year students and too easy for graduate students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Respondent support for course offering and government funding as measured in the survey
- Instructors address the top-impressed concepts (self-assembly, academic values, career opportunities) in course design
#### Constraints
- The article states the concepts may be too hard for first- and second-year students but too easy for graduate students

### Target Learners
- third- and fourth-year undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- soft matter physics concepts
- programmable self-assembly
- active matter phenomena
- awareness of soft matter careers
