# Increase active prescribing practice during undergraduate training, including writing prescriptions during clinical clerkships

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article identifies lack of prescribing practice as a key driver of poor competencies and recommends increasing active, experience‐based activities. It notes that "Gaining experience in writing drug prescriptions may be an important area for curriculum improvement, because “experienced” students made significantly fewer inappropriate drug prescriptions." It further observes that problem‐based learning curricula "may put more emphasis on writing prescriptions during clinical clerkships," and that students currently spend most study hours on passive activities rather than active ones such as clinical prescribing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Opportunities for students to write prescriptions during clinical clerkships
- Curricular emphasis on active rather than passive learning activities
#### Constraints
- The association between experience and fewer errors is observational and does not establish causation

### Target Learners
- final‐year medical students
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- writing complete and accurate prescriptions
- reducing inappropriate drug prescribing
