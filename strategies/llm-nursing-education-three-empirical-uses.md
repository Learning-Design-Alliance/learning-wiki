# Three primary empirical uses of LLMs in nursing education: student learning support, faculty item development, and licensure exam evaluation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The concept analysis catalogs how LLMs are actually used in nursing education: "(1) to support students in assignments and learning activities, (2) to assist faculty in developing and evaluating exam questions, and (3) to evaluate the performance of LLMs on nursing licensing exams." Uses include tutoring, generating explanations, academic writing support, simulation scenario generation, PhD data-analysis support, NCLEX-RN item generation combined with human expertise, automated writing evaluation, and case creation for case-based learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty item-generation uses are reported to work best in combination with human expertise
#### Constraints
- Some students struggled to connect nursing problems to individualized care plans due to overreliance on LLM-generated content

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students
- graduate nursing students
- nursing faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- academic writing
- clinical decision-making
- licensure exam preparation
- case-based learning
