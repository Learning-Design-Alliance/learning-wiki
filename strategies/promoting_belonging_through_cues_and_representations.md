# Promoting Belonging Through Cues and Representations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy treats the ambient signals of a learning environment as a design surface. What is on the walls, which examples appear in problems, whose photographs are on the department page, what objects fill the room, and which names recur in the reading list all communicate who this subject is for — and learners read those signals long before anyone tells them explicitly whether they belong. The strategy is to audit those cues and change the ones that narrow the field, replacing stereotype-linked signals with ones that admit a wider range of people without singling anyone out.

## Design Implications

The evidence that ambient cues matter is unusually clean. Cheryan and colleagues changed nothing about a computer science classroom except the objects in it — swapping *Star Trek* posters, video games, and soda cans for art, plants, and general-interest magazines — and women's stated interest in the subject rose to match men's, with no effect on men. The cues had not told anyone they were unwelcome; they had signalled a prototype, and learners assessed their fit against it. That is what makes this strategy distinctive: the intervention is on the environment, not on the learner, and it requires no one to be told they might not belong.

Belonging matters because doubt about it is self-confirming. Learners uncertain whether they belong interpret ordinary difficulty — a bad grade, an awkward seminar, having no one to sit with — as evidence about their fit rather than as normal experience, and withdraw accordingly. Brief interventions that reframe those difficulties as common and temporary produce durable achievement gains for students facing this doubt [Belonging Interventions Improve Outcomes](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. Environmental cues work on the same mechanism earlier: they reduce the ambiguity that the doubt feeds on. Both the structural and symbolic aspects of a room contribute [Classroom design affects learning progress.](../claims/classroom-design-affects-learning-progress.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Control over the visible environment: displays, materials, examples, images, room contents
- An honest audit of what the current cues signal, which usually needs someone who does not already fit the prototype to look
- Representation that is accurate to the discipline rather than assembled for display — learners detect tokenism reliably
- Consistency between cue and experience; a welcoming wall in a room where participation is dominated by a few makes the gap more visible, not less
- Examples and problem contexts reviewed as carefully as images, since they carry the same signal more subtly

#### Constraints
- Cue changes shift interest and comfort; they do not on their own change instruction, assessment, or who gets called on [Learning Space Redesign Alone Insufficient](../claims/learning-space-redesign-alone-insufficient.md) [-M]
- Representation that reads as decorative or added-on can undercut the message, and singling learners out as representatives of a group is actively harmful [-M]
- Belonging interventions are targeted, not universal: the framing that helps a student experiencing belonging doubt is inert for one who is not, and effects vary considerably by context [~M]
- Making identity salient at the wrong moment can prime stereotype threat rather than reduce it; the strategy works through ambient signal, not through announcement [-M]
- Wall display competes for attention, so belonging cues have to be added within a limited visual budget rather than on top of an already-crowded room [Cognitive Overload Degrades Learning](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-M]
- Cues cannot substitute for the absent thing they gesture at; images of diverse practitioners in a department with none is a message learners can read

#### Implementation Variability
- **Object and décor audit** — removing stereotype-linked objects and replacing them with neutral or broad ones, the manipulation Cheryan tested directly
- **Representation in examples** — names, contexts, and scenarios in problems and cases drawn from a wide range without comment
- **Practitioner visibility** — images and short profiles of people working in the field, chosen for range and for actually being in the field ([Cultivating Belonging Through Environmental Cues](cultivating_belonging_through_environmental_cues.md))
- **Texts as mirrors and windows** — reading selections in which learners can see both themselves and others ([Windows, Mirrors, and Sliding Glass Doors](windows-mirrors-and-sliding-glass-doors.md))
- **Student work as the display** — the room's contents produced by its current occupants, which represents them without curation
- **Narrative belonging interventions** — brief structured exercises reframing early difficulty as common and temporary ([Belonging Interventions](belonging-interventions.md))

### Target Learners
- Learners in fields with a narrow public prototype — computing, engineering, physics, philosophy — where the mismatch signal is strongest
- Learners underrepresented in the setting, who face the belonging question that better-represented peers do not
- Students at transition points — first year, new school, new subject — where the environment is being actively read for cues
- First-generation students, for whom institutional signals are least familiar and most consequential
- Universal in application, targeted in effect: changing cues costs nothing for learners who were not questioning their fit

### Target Learning Goals
- Belonging and its downstream effects on persistence and achievement [Belonging Interventions Improve Outcomes](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Interest and continued participation in the subject, particularly the decision to take the next course
- Reduced stereotype threat in performance settings
- Willingness to participate visibly — asking questions, seeking help, contributing in seminar
- Engagement, via the relational climate the cues support [Teacher Student Relationships Improve Engagement](../claims/teacher-student-relationships-improve-engagement.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Audit with fresh eyes.** Photograph the space and list what a newcomer would infer about who belongs here. Ask someone outside the prototype to do this; the cues are invisible to those they fit.
2. **Remove before adding.** Take out the stereotype-linked objects and images first. Removal was the larger half of the effect in the research, and it costs no display space.
3. **Review the examples, not only the walls.** Check names, contexts, and scenarios across problem sets and cases; these carry the signal into every lesson.
4. **Show practitioners with range.** Include people currently doing the work, presented for their work rather than as demographic illustration.
5. **Let the room fill with the learners' own work.** Student-produced display represents the actual occupants and needs no curation ([Involving Learners in Classroom Design](involving_learners_in_classroom_design.md)).
6. **Never appoint a representative.** Do not ask a learner to speak for a group, and do not use a learner as an example of who belongs.
7. **Normalize early difficulty explicitly.** Tell all students that finding the start hard is common and temporary, as a general statement rather than one directed at anyone ([Belonging Interventions](belonging-interventions.md)).
8. **Check the experience matches.** Track who speaks, who is called on, and who comes to office hours; cues that promise inclusion the interaction does not deliver make the gap more legible.

## Examples

**The computer science classroom swap:** Cheryan and colleagues replaced science-fiction posters, video games, and technical clutter with art, plants, and general-interest magazines; women's interest in computer science rose to parity with men's, with no change to the curriculum.

**Neutral-context problem sets:** A mathematics department reviews every worked example for names and scenarios, replacing a narrow set with a wide one — no commentary, just a changed default.

**Practitioner walls in a laboratory:** Photographs and one-line descriptions of current researchers in the field, selected for range across background and route into the discipline, presented as people doing work rather than as representation.

**Student-work display in place of commercial posters:** The room's walls carry what its current occupants have made, which represents the actual class and avoids the curation problem entirely.
