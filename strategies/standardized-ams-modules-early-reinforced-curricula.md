# Implement standardized AMS modules early in dental and medical curricula, reinforced throughout training, with targeted support for underperforming groups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing "standardized AMS modules across medical curricula nationwide", introduced early and reinforced throughout training, alongside targeted interventions for underperforming groups (female students, majority-ethnic students, older students) using gender-sensitive, culturally inclusive and age-appropriate approaches. It further recommends qualitative follow-up research and partnerships with health ministries to develop nationwide AMS policies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional and national commitment to curriculum standardization and periodic assessment of prescribing competencies
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a cross-sectional survey of three Nigerian universities, limiting generalizability to other regions or countries

### Target Learners
- undergraduate clinical dental students
- medical students in early clinical training

### Target Learning Goals
- antimicrobial stewardship knowledge and attitudes
- rational antibiotic prescribing competencies
