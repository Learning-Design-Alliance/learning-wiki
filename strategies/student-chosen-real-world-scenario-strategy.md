# Use student-chosen, real-world-anchored scenario topics with peer-designed cases in interprofessional CPR simulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy has students select their simulation scenario topic from real materials such as "a newspaper, a video or a title," then set their own learning objectives and design the case between sessions. The article reports this "improved interest and increased motivation as students saw the connection between a real fact and their simulated scenarios," and made students feel more responsible for engaging with scientific evidence. It was implemented in interprofessional teams mixing nursing and medicine students, with scenarios aligned to ERC guidelines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A prebriefing phase where teams voluntarily choose the topic and set baseline learning objectives
- A homework period between sessions for scenario design and evidence search
- Facilitator guidance throughout
#### Constraints
- The work at home between the two sessions can be a limit, especially in busy periods
- Responsibility for peer learning scared some students

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing and medicine students in interprofessional teams

### Target Learning Goals
- CPR knowledge and technical skills
- Motivation and responsibility for evidence-based learning
