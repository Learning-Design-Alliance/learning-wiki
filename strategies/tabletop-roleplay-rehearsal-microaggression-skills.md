# Use tabletop simulation and role-play in small groups so faculty can rehearse exact words, receive feedback, and retry

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The curriculum teaches microaggression behavioral skills through experiential simulation: tabletop simulation walks participants through case-based scenarios deciding their next words or actions, and role-play has participants enact a role with the option to "start, stop, or pause the scenario at any moment." Small groups let participants "rehearse exact words they would use in a space where they could make mistakes, obtain facilitator feedback, and have opportunities to try again," which the authors link to building confidence and comfort.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators present in small groups to stimulate active communication, reflective pauses, and brainstorming, and to deliver feedback
#### Constraints
- Modalities were chosen because they are easy ways to practice communication in a virtual space

### Target Learners
- Faculty leaders who interact with trainees and medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Behavioral skills for acknowledging, apologizing for, setting expectations about, and debriefing microaggressions
