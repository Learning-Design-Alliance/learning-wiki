# Use and train pre-service teachers in learning journals, with low-stakes grading and instructor feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using learning journals in teaching and learning, especially for pre-service teachers, and training them on using learning journals. Implementation in the study used prompts about understanding, learning difficulties, attitudes and feelings, journals filled at the start or end of lessons, and instructor checking that supplied feedback the instructor did not get in the classroom. Reflection papers were initially not numerically graded, following a low-stakes writing model where grading may interfere with learning, but were evaluated pass/fail with extensive feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training for pre-service teachers on using learning journals
- Instructor review of entries and feedback to students
#### Constraints
- Some students were concerned by the pass/fail approach and preferred specific performance guidelines and scoring

### Target Learners
- pre-service teachers
- university students in education courses

### Target Learning Goals
- reflective thinking
- self-regulated learning
