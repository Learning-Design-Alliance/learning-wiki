Integrate innovative instructional approaches (gamification, VR/AR, robotic coding, STEM) and out-of-school settings into PBL¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 2 review), q2–q3 · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that integrated PBL variants showed descriptively higher effect sizes than pure PBL, the author recommends that "Current instructional approaches and methods such as gamification, virtual and augmented reality, robotic coding, and STEM can be further integrated into PBL." The author also recommends integrating out-of-school learning environments such as museums and science centers so students learn problems more authentically, and publishing more primary-school-level experimental studies.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • PBL as the base instructional model into which approaches are integrated

Constraints¶

  • The meta-analysis found no significant difference between instructional variations, so the integration recommendation rests on descriptive, untested differences

Target Learners¶

  • science students at all educational levels

Target Learning Goals¶

  • science academic achievement

Claims¶

  • PBL effect sizes on science achievement do not differ significantly across instructional variations of PBL [-W]
  • PBL has a high positive effect on science academic achievement compared to traditional instruction (random-effects g = 1.009) [+M]
  • Integrating virtual reality and gamification increased students' learning outcomes, motivation, engagement, and self-efficacy [+W]
  • Review syntheses: VR/AR most often improved academic achievement and motivation, but sometimes performed no better than conventional technologies [+M]
  • AI applications contributed to STEM students' higher-order thinking, including problem-solving, computational thinking, and self-regulated learning [+W]

Related Strategies¶
