# Provide targeted support strengthening identity and perceived recognition for women earning lower grades, and foster a climate where students see themselves as physics people regardless of final grade

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's pedagogical implications recommend fostering a learning environment where students, "regardless of their final grades", can equally see themselves as 'physics people'. Because women with lower grades report lower physics identity and perceived recognition than men, the authors suggest it "could be useful to provide targeted support that can help strengthen their sense of identity and increase their perception of being recognized in the physics community". Instructors and TAs sending recognition messages might counteract negative grade effects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors and TAs must send messages recognizing students as belonging in physics, which the article suggests might counteract the negative effects of lower grades.
#### Constraints
- The study's correlational design means the recommended supports address effects whose causal status the authors say requires further research, e.g., via counterbalanced grading curves or message-framing manipulations.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate women in calculus-based Physics 1 earning grades below an A

### Target Learning Goals
- physics disciplinary identity
- perceived recognition as a physics person
- persistence in physics pathways
