# Two-session, team-based implementation with instructors facilitating rather than leading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementable classroom recipe across two 75-minute sessions: 'On the first day, teams of students assembled several microfluidics devices. On the second day, the teams then tested the devices they had created.' Teams of two or three work from step-by-step visual handouts at their own pace while instructors troubleshoot without leading, and the handout encourages testing devices in a different order than assembled to enable comparison between mixers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two 75-minute class sessions with device kits per team
- Step-by-step handouts with assembly questions and less explicit testing instructions listing desired outcomes
- Backup kits: one per device for every 2-3 teams
- One to two fully assembled devices of each type for visualization and experimentation
#### Constraints
- Students verbally communicated that double the allotted time for additional exploration would have been welcome
- Common assembly challenges were orienting and aligning layers and achieving a water-tight seal

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate engineering students working in teams of two or three

### Target Learning Goals
- independent device construction and troubleshooting
- learning from errors such as misaligned layers and misconnected fluidic ports
