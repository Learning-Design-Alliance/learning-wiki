# Provide explicit online learning-strategy instruction alongside open-access self-study systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because only advanced students benefited from unaided self-study, the authors argue educators "must take time to instruct students not only on what they must learn but how to learn online, especially self-study," and that online systems should build in pedagogical knowledge and study strategies for challenging content. This positions strategy instruction as a condition for equitable benefit from open-access systems like CHLS.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classroom instruction must consciously include strategies for online as well as face-to-face learning
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as an implication from one quasi-experiment where lower and intermediate students did not benefit without such support

### Target Learners
- Secondary EFL students of all ability levels using online self-study systems

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-directed online English language learning and study strategies
