# Simulation-based assessment gate requiring three passed assessments before clinical intubation practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The program used a simulation-based gatekeeping strategy: "STRs must pass three separate assessments, each requiring a success rate of 90% or higher, completion within 5 min, and the occurrence of no more than one complication per assessment before engaging in clinical operations." Assessment methods included standardized scenarios, timed tasks, and complication tracking, evaluating both technical skill and clinical judgment before independent practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- High-fidelity simulators and standardized scenario scripts
- Faculty evaluation capacity for timed assessments
- Complication tracking during simulation
#### Constraints
- The authors note well-designed simulation training might not fully replicate complex emergency scenarios

### Target Learners
- standardised training residents preparing for clinical procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- technical intubation skill proficiency
- clinical judgment under time pressure
- complication avoidance
