# Recruit educated peers or non-physicians as instructors for the technical teaching of basic practical skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the majority of reviewed controlled studies showing basic practical skills can be taught equally well by educated peers or non-physicians, the authors recommend recruiting personnel other than physicians to teach these skills. They state this recommendation "refers merely to the technical part of the teaching which was in the focus of this review." The recommendation is qualified by exceptions where experts or full-time faculty produced better performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructed peers or trained personnel as instructors
- Focus on technical skill teaching
#### Constraints
- One study showed better bladder catheterization performance after expert teaching
- Students taught by full-time faculty performed better than those taught by part-time faculty
- Competence-based integration with communication and clinical reasoning might require medical experts as role models

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Technical performance of basic practical skills
