# Blinded video-based peer review with structured scoring and written feedback for surgeon skill assessment and improvement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Surgeons submit deidentified videos of their own procedures, which are segmented into key operative steps and scored by multiple blinded peer reviewers using a validated instrument; reviewers also provide free-text written feedback, and each surgeon receives an individualized score card with scores, comments, and video links. In this study "each video clip was deidentified and distributed to at least 3 of the 24 blinded peer surgeons," and reviewers "rated technical skill and provided written feedback." Participants reported high satisfaction, and the collaborative later housed the videos and feedback on a YouTube site for wider learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Surgeons submit at least 1 deidentified video of a recent, representative robot-assisted partial nephrectomy case
- Each video clip is evaluated by at least 3 blinded peer reviewers using the SPaN score sheet with scoring criteria
- Each participating surgeon receives an individualized score card containing specific scores, free-text reviewer comments, and a link to the video clips
#### Constraints
- Submission of videos for review was voluntary; self-selection by the surgeon may have right skewed the distribution of scores
- Reviewers were not provided patient and tumor characteristics, so they could not integrate information that impacts surgical decision-making into their scoring

### Target Learners
- practicing urological surgeons performing robot-assisted partial nephrectomy

### Target Learning Goals
- improving technical skill in robot-assisted partial nephrectomy
- identifying areas for improvement in one's own technical skill through structured peer feedback
