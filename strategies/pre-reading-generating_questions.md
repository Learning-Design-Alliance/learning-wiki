# Pre-Reading: Generating Questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Before reading a text, learners generate questions they expect or hope the text will answer. The instructor may scaffold this with a topic, title, headings, or images, and learners record their questions to revisit during and after reading. The strategy combines two mechanisms: activating relevant prior knowledge and creating an information gap that the text can close.

## Design Implications

Generating questions before reading converts reading from passive reception into goal-directed search, which improves attention allocation and comprehension monitoring [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]. Questions also function as prequestions: attempting to anticipate answers before study enhances later learning of that material even when the anticipated answers are wrong [High-confidence errors improve retention.](../claims/high-confidence-errors-improve-retention.md) [+S]. Teaching students *how* to generate generative, text-connectable questions — rather than yes/no or purely factual ones — is what drives the effect; trained question generation reliably improves comprehension across grade levels [Rosenshine et al. (1996)](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543066002181) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A visible "hook" into the text — title, headings, images, or a short excerpt — so questions are grounded rather than arbitrary
- Explicit modeling of good question forms ([Think-Aloud](../elements/advance-organizers.md) or worked question examples: "Why…?", "How does X compare to Y…?")
- A structure for recording questions (KWL chart, question log, discussion board) and returning to them after reading
- Time during reading to seek answers, and a follow-up ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or written response) that closes the loop

#### Constraints
- Prequestions direct attention toward the questioned content and away from un-questioned material, impairing recall of the latter [Prequestions in video lectures improved learning of prequestioned content but reduced learning of non-prequestioned content.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2016.07.008) [~M] — if only some topics are prequestioned, coverage of the rest suffers
- For learners with strong prior knowledge of the topic, pre-reading questions add little and can consume time better spent reading [Guidance becomes less effective as expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Untrained question generation produces shallow, factual questions with minimal comprehension benefit [Rosenshine et al. (1996)](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543066002181) [-M] — the strategy requires instruction, not just assignment
- Questions that cannot plausibly be answered by the text create frustration and off-target reading

#### Implementation Variability
- **KWL charts** (Ogle, 1986): learners list what they Know, what they Want to know, and later what they Learned — the "W" column is the question-generation step
- **Guided peer questioning**: students use trained question stems to quiz a partner before or during reading [King (1992)](https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312029002303) [+M]
- **Instructor-posed prequestions**: the instructor supplies the questions instead of learners generating them; cheaper but forfeits the strategy-training benefit
- **Reciprocal teaching**: question generation is one of four comprehension strategies students rotate through in small groups (Palincsar & Brown, 1984)
- **Pretesting variant**: learners attempt to answer questions before reading rather than generate them; both versions produce a pretesting benefit [Richland et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016496) [+S]

### Target Learners
- Novice readers and students with limited topic knowledge, who otherwise read without a retrieval structure [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]
- Students learning self-regulated reading strategies; question generation is trainable even in upper-elementary grades [Rosenshine et al. (1996)](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543066002181) [+S]
- Less valuable for expert readers with strong domain knowledge, who already generate questions spontaneously [Guidance becomes less effective as expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension of expository text: locating and integrating key ideas
- Strategy acquisition: internalizing self-questioning as a transferable reading habit
- Curiosity and engagement: closing information gaps sustains attention through longer texts
- Prior knowledge activation: surfacing and testing existing beliefs against the text

### Instructions
1. Present the text's title, headings, and visuals; ask learners to predict what the text will explain ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md)).
2. Model generating one or two generative questions aloud, showing how to convert headings into "why" and "how" questions.
3. Have learners write 2–3 of their own questions and share them ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or pair exchange).
4. Learners read with their questions visible, marking where the text answers each one.
5. After reading, revisit the questions: answer them, note what the text did *not* answer, and revise understanding — connecting this step to [Application](../elements/application.md) or a short written response.

## Related Strategies
- [Activating Prior Knowledge](activating-prior-knowledge.md) — question generation is one of the most reliable activation moves; the questions surface what learners already believe
- [Reciprocal Teaching](../elements/reciprocal-teaching.md) — embeds question generation among three other comprehension strategies during reading

## Examples
- **KWL (Ogle, 1986)** — A widely used elementary expository-reading routine: the "Want to know" column is the generated question set, revisited in the "Learned" column after reading.
- **[Reading Apprenticeship](https://readingapprenticeship.org/)** — A disciplinary-literacy framework in which instructors think aloud their own questioning of texts and students build personal question logs before and during reading.
- **Prequestions in video lectures** — Embedding questions before video segments improved learning of the questioned content but reduced attention to non-questioned content (Carpenter & Toftness, 2017), so designers should prequestion across all key topics rather than a sample.
