# Use a clinically and pathologically complex case as the core instructional context to foster morphology-based clinicopathological reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend selecting a clinically and pathologically complex learning scenario with substantial educational value for case-based and discussion-oriented teaching. In this study, the case of hepatitis B-related liver cirrhosis with upper gastrointestinal bleeding and hepatic encephalopathy "required students to integrate hepatic pathological changes" such as hepatocellular degeneration and necrosis, collapse of the hepatic framework, and pseudolobule formation with disease progression and end-stage complications. The authors state such case complexity "is well suited to case-based and discussion-oriented educational models" and may support learners in integrating information across domains and engaging in analytical reasoning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful case selection to ensure the case provides opportunities for students to reflect on professional responsibility and social accountability
- Sufficient instructional guidance to connect humanistic discussions to the intended learning objectives
#### Constraints
- Inappropriate case selection or insufficient instructional guidance may result in superficial discussions of humanistic issues

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in pathology laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- morphology-based clinicopathological reasoning
- integration of theoretical knowledge with pathological morphologic features and clinical reasoning
