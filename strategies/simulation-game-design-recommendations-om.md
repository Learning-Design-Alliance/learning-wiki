# Design web-based simulation games with website attractiveness, access monitoring, individual performance assessment, and prerequisite quantitative knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's conclusion lists design and implementation issues for web-based simulations in operations management education: "game website attractiveness, access monitoring system, individual performance assessment", supplementing team marks with member performance evaluation for fairness, officially integrating the simulation in the syllabus, and requiring prior quantitative-analysis knowledge so students can apply quantitative decision techniques.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of team performance needs to be supplemented with member performance evaluation to ensure fairness in marking.
- Students registering the course should have prior knowledge on quantitative analysis.
#### Constraints
- The recommendations arise from a single quasi-experiment with a relatively small number of participants (53) at one Vietnamese university.

### Target Learners
- business undergraduate students in operations management courses

### Target Learning Goals
- demand forecasting, capacity planning, inventory lot sizing, reorder point determination, and sequencing decision-making
