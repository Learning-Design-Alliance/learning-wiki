# Learning Goal Setting

## Description
Learning goal setting is the practice of defining clear, specific objectives *before* engaging in a learning experience — such as shadowing, observation, a project, or a unit of study. Learners articulate what they want to find out, write down their current assumptions, and identify what evidence would challenge or confirm those assumptions. This converts a diffuse experience into a directed inquiry: attention is pre-committed to targets, and post-experience reflection has explicit criteria to evaluate against.

## Design Implications

Goal setting works because specific, difficult goals regulate effort, persistence, and attention more effectively than vague intentions to "do your best" [Locke & Latham's goal-setting theory](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705) [+S]. For novices, goals framed around learning and process (what to understand, what strategy to try) outperform goals framed around outcomes or performance (looking competent, hitting a score) [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]. Goals also serve a self-regulatory function: they provide the reference standard against which learners monitor progress, and self-monitoring against explicit goals improves self-regulation [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated time before the experience to articulate and document goals in writing
- Goals that are specific and challenging but attainable [Specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md) [+S]
- A structure for recording prior assumptions so they can be tested against observation rather than silently filtering it
- A follow-up [reflection](../elements/act.md) or [check-in](../principles/check-ins.md) in which progress against the stated goals is reviewed

#### Constraints
- Vague or overly broad goals ("learn about teaching") provide no regulatory benefit; specificity is the active ingredient [Specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md) [-S] when violated
- Outcome-oriented goals can *harm* novice performance and persistence after failure; learning-oriented goals are required for inexperienced learners [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [-M]
- Pre-set goals can bias observation — learners may notice only what confirms their goals; explicitly documenting assumptions and counter-evidence questions mitigates this
- Requires honest self-assessment; learners with low [self-efficacy](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) may set defensively easy goals that produce little growth [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Individual written goals** — learners draft personal questions and assumptions before shadowing or field observation
- **Contract-style goals** — goals negotiated with a mentor or instructor, then revisited at checkpoints
- **Micro-goals** — breaking a large learning goal into achievable sub-goals, which raises self-efficacy through proximal success experiences [Achievable micro-goals](achievable_micro-goals.md); proximal sub-goals increase self-efficacy and interest (Bandura & Schunk, 1981) [+S]
- **Assumption-auditing variant** — goals paired with explicit statements of what the learner expects to see, so disconfirming evidence becomes salient

### Target Learners
- Novices entering an unfamiliar environment (e.g., pre-service teachers observing classrooms), where unguided observation yields little [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]
- Learners with low task engagement, for whom goal setting increases task value and commitment [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Advanced learners benefit less from externally set goals but can use self-set goals as part of [self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Metacognitive: planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's own learning [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Attitudinal and motivational: building commitment, task value, and self-efficacy
- Observational/inquiry skills: learning to gather evidence systematically rather than impressionistically

### Instructions
1. **Elicit prior assumptions.** Learners write what they currently believe and expect to observe, activating prior knowledge [Activation](../elements/activation.md).
2. **Set specific learning goals.** Each learner drafts 1–3 specific, challenging goals framed as learning/process goals ("I want to identify three ways the teacher checks for understanding"), not outcome goals [State objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md).
3. **Define evidence criteria.** For each goal, specify what observations would count as evidence, including evidence that would contradict current assumptions.
4. **Engage in the experience** with goals visible — e.g., carrying an observation protocol or focus question into a shadowing session.
5. **Reflect against goals.** Debrief by reviewing each goal, the evidence gathered, and assumption changes; connect to next-cycle goals [Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md).

## Examples
- **Pre-service teacher shadowing** — Before observing a classroom, a candidate writes: "My assumption is that quiet students are disengaged. Goal: document three behaviors of quiet students that indicate engagement or disengagement." The post-observation debrief compares evidence to the assumption.
- **[Mastery learning programs](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md)** — Learners set target competencies before each cycle and self-assess against explicit criteria.
- **Medical clerkships** — Students on rotation set case-specific learning objectives (e.g., "present one patient history and receive feedback on differential diagnosis") rather than passively following the team.
