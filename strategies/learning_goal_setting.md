# Learning Goal Setting

## Description
This strategy involves defining clear objectives prior to engaging in a learning experience, such as shadowing. By articulating specific questions and challenges to explore, learners can maximize the impact of their experience. This includes writing down assumptions, selecting a student with specific qualities, and identifying personal motivations for the learning goal. It focuses learners to observe without bias, challenging existing beliefs and facilitating more meaningful insights.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated time for reflection, a willingness to challenge existing assumptions, and a structured approach to defining and documenting learning goals.
#### Constraints
- Requires self-awareness and honesty. If learning goals are too broad or vague, the experience may lack focus. Resistance to challenging personal biases may limit the insights gained.

## Assessment Evidence
- Success is measured by the extent to which the learning goals are achieved, the insights gained, and the changes made as a result of the experience. This can be documented through reflections, action plans, and feedback from participants.

## Impact
- Studies show that setting learning goals before an experience can increase engagement and improve outcomes. By focusing observations and challenging biases, this strategy fosters more meaningful and impactful learning.
