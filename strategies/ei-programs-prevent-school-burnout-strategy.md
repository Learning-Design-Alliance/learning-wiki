# Develop emotional intelligence programs in secondary education to prevent school burnout after adverse academic events

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes developing emotional intelligence programs in the educational context so that adolescents respond better to negative academic events such as failing or repeating a year. The authors state: "We therefore propose the development of emotional intelligence programs for improving the response to situations that generate negative feelings, such as low performance." They add that emotional content should be included in programs working on general academic competencies, using educational applications, to build personal resources for coping with academic challenges.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must improve students' response to situations that generate negative feelings, such as low performance, targeting stress management and mood components of emotional intelligence.
#### Constraints
- The study did not consider factors such as the number of subjects failed or academic expectations that could affect stress and distress experienced by participants.

### Target Learners
- High school students aged 14 to 18

### Target Learning Goals
- Managing stress and negative emotions in adverse academic situations
- Preventing school burnout
- Supporting academic wellbeing and engagement
