# Integrate same-class peer mentorship into medical curricula as a cost-effective support for academically underperforming students in resource-limited settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends pairing academically underperforming students with high-performing peers from the same class level, with trained mentors holding regular weekly meetings focused on academic challenges, goal-setting, and emotional support. It states that "same-class peer mentorship could be integrated into medical curricula as a cost-effective intervention for supporting academically underperforming students", particularly where resource constraints limit traditional academic support services.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentors must be trained by researchers or educationists in mentoring techniques, communication skills, and confidentiality (6-hour training in the study) before the programme begins
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that mentors, being high-performing students, may have varying levels of teaching or mentoring skills, introducing variability in mentee outcomes

### Target Learners
- Academically underperforming preclinical medical students in low- and middle-income settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance on continuous assessment tests
- Classroom engagement and participation
- Proactive learning behaviours
