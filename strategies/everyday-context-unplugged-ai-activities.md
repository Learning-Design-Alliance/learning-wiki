# Teach AI concepts through everyday-context, unplugged and hands-on activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article introduces AI concepts to young children who are still learning to read and write by grounding them in familiar, embodied activities: a quesadilla-cooking activity to introduce what an algorithm is, a tangram activity for how AI learns to recognize animals or humans, a bingo game with key terms from previous lessons, a garden activity where students program their teammates, and protoboard and simple electric circuit activities to learn how a robot works. These activities let children experience that "everyone can have a different answer given the same instruction", making abstract computing ideas concrete without requiring reading or programming fluency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors to prepare and run the activities
- Everyday materials and contexts familiar to children in the local setting
#### Constraints
- Questions and examples must be contextualised locally, since children may know little to none about AI, Robotics and ethics (bias)

### Target Learners
- Children aged 6 to 9 who are still learning writing and reading skills

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding what an algorithm is
- How AI learns to recognize animals or humans
- How sensors and robots work
