# Re-examine the role of numeric score thresholds in residency screening to reflect clinical readiness rather than unequal access to supports

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends critically examining Step 2 CK's role in residency evaluation as reliance on numeric score thresholds increases. It concludes that "these findings argue for a re-examination of Step 2 CK's role in residency evaluation to ensure that high-stakes testing reflects clinical readiness rather than unequal access to supports." It also recommends qualitative and mixed-methods research on how program directors interpret scores in the context of disability disclosure and holistic selection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Residency selection processes that consider whether score differentials reflect true differences in clinical readiness or construct-irrelevant variance
#### Constraints
- Score thresholds used by residency programs vary widely by specialty and institution, so effects differ depending on specialty of interest

### Target Learners
- medical students with disabilities
- medical students from URiM backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable evaluation of clinical readiness in residency selection
