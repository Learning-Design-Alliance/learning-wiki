# Position micro-credentials to deliver more than certification: emphasize competency-based, personalized skill development for early adopters and address extrinsic motivations for mainstream adopters

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report derives positioning guidance from its adoption-segment findings. Its key point is that "micro-credentials should deliver more than certification and validation" and must also reflect attributes teachers most value, such as being competency-based and personalized. For mainstream adopters, the report says appealing to them "will have to address the extrinsic motivations of these educators," possibly requiring new strategic initiatives and different messaging.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Micro-credentials must address a variety of intrinsic motivations, and reflect attributes teachers value such as being competency-based and personalized, to succeed with early adopters.
#### Constraints
- The report warns some teachers are wary that administrators could use micro-credentials as a new assessment tool or make them mandatory, which could turn them into a hoop to jump through.

### Target Learners
- K-12 public and private school teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Ongoing professional learning and recognition of specific teaching competencies
