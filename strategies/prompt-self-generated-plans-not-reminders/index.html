Prompt self-generated planning rather than simple reminders to protect student agency and avoid overreliance¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 review), q2–q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that mobile interventions "prompt students to apply self-regulation strategies on their own, rather than simply reminding them to act in a certain way," and proposes adaptive assignment of agency levels, offering less support as students become more advanced. Prompting students to create their own study plans helped maintain a regular study routine, whereas simple reminders to study more often created overreliance on external prompts.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interventions must monitor student progress to adapt the level of support offered as students become more advanced

Constraints¶

  • Mobile prompts risk reducing motivation and undermining development of self-regulated learning skills, especially when they intrude on moments that could serve as opportunities to practice independent action

Target Learners¶

  • school students, including 9–13-year-olds in the authors' studies

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-regulated learning
  • self-generative learning activities
  • maintaining a regular study routine

Claims¶

  • On days without reminders, reminded students were significantly less likely to study vocabulary than control students, suggesting overreliance on reminders [+W]
  • In two studies of children aged 10–12 and 9–13, students were more likely to study vocabulary on days they received a mobile-app study reminder than on days they did not [~W]
  • Within-person reminders of implementation intentions improve students' short-term study behaviour [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Combine technological and human-level factors to promote engagement with mobile interventions
