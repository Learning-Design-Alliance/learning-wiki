# Three-part reform strategy: TA training with equity and anti-bias preparation, supplemental nature-of-science and lab-skill activities, and full Real-Time Physics adoption with E-CLASS monitoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 5 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article lays out three directions for future growth after the partial reform failed to shift E-CLASS scores. First, the authors "have begun to develop a robust TA training module to ensure that student inquiry is being supported effectively and fairly," motivated by observed microaggressions in lab social interactions. Second, they are "creating small supplemental learning activities so students can explicitly learn about the nature of science and develop lab skills." Third, they will offer "a full sequence of Real-Time Physics labs," while using the E-CLASS survey to monitor self-efficacy of female and underrepresented minority students and nature-of-science understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- TA preparation that includes equity and anti-bias training in setting up and managing the lab as a sociocultural environment
#### Constraints
- The prior piecewise adoption without specialized TA training, student and TA buy-in, or targeted lab-skill development did not produce gains over national norms

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics and chemistry majors in introductory labs

### Target Learning Goals
- expert-like attitudes and lab skills
- understanding of the nature of science
- self-efficacy of female and underrepresented minority students
