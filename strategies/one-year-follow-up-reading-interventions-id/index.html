Future reading intervention studies for students with disorders of ID should include at least a one-year follow-up to establish sustainable effects¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 6 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 1 causal, 1 review, 1 theoretical), q3–q4 · 2 of 6 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The included studies reported no long-term effects on reading, and the only study with long-term follow-up used a waiting-list control design that precluded using the control results. The authors recommend: "Future reading intervention studies for students with disorders of ID should therefore include at least a one-year follow-up in light of existing evidence of a fade-out effect within the first year or two following interventions". While long-term measurement is costly, they argue it is important to establish whether interventions have sustainable effects.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Studies must include long-term postintervention effect measures, ideally with control groups retained at follow-up

Constraints¶

  • Long-term measurement is costly and time-consuming, as the article states

Target Learners¶

  • Students with disorders of intellectual development

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Maintenance of reading skills after intervention completion

Claims¶

  • Follow-up effects of targeted K-6 interventions remain positive up to 2 years after intervention ends, with evidence of fadeout [+W]
  • Longer-run effects of targeted secondary interventions are small and not statistically significant, with only seven studies providing follow-up evidence [+W]
  • Social-emotional learning benefits persist at follow-up [+M]
  • Evidence on long-term persistence of self-regulation intervention effects is mixed: no fadeout for metacognitive strategy interventions versus substantial fadeout in higher-quality RCT syntheses [~W]
