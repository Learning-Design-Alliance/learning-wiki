# Use non-fine-tuned pre-trained sentiment analysis models for applied research on sentiment–variable relations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that applied researchers wanting to relate sentiment to other variables use non-fine-tuned pre-trained sentiment analysis models, since creating or fine-tuning a model consumes data and time and yields non-replicable results. In this study the Microsoft pre-trained model analysed 6072 words in about 10 minutes at no cost, returning sentiment labels and 0–1 polarity scores. The trade-off is lower reliability than trained models, which the authors suggest can be mitigated by careful open-ended question wording that avoids simple, sarcastic or metaphorical responses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful formulation of the open-ended question to prevent overly simple, sarcastic or metaphorical answers
- Attention to potential bias in students' feedback when relating sentiment to other variables
#### Constraints
- Lower reliability values than trained or fine-tuned models; the model was not specifically trained for the educational context

### Target Learners
- secondary school students providing open-ended feedback

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing students' sentiments and opinions about teachers' verbal behaviour
