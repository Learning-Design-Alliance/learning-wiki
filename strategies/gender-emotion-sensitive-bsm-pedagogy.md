# Gender- and emotion-sensitive classroom adjustments based on students' embodied emotional response patterns

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends tailoring classroom practice to students' embodied emotional responses: for male students, who show more deactivation during neutral emotional states, teachers could incorporate "more interactive and dynamic activities, such as group discussions or competitive tasks"; for female students, whose bodily sensations are more activated under anxiety, teachers might integrate "relaxation exercises or providing emotional support". These adjustments are proposed to create a more tailored and emotionally supportive learning environment that enhances engagement and focus.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need awareness of students' embodied and emotional responses during learning, such as stronger anxiety activation or neutral-state deactivation.
#### Constraints
- The recommendations rest on cross-sectional, subjective bodily maps; the article notes the cross-sectional design precludes conclusions regarding dynamic changes of bodily responses during learning.

### Target Learners
- high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- emotional engagement and participation in subject learning
- managing anxiety to enhance focus
