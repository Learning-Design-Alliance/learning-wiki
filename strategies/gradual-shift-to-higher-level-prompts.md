# Gradually shift the adult role from reading to listening and higher-level prompting as the child becomes familiar with a book

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Within dialogic reading, the adult progressively reduces their own reading and increases the child's storytelling. As the article describes, "the adult reads less, listens more, and gradually uses higher level prompts" to move the child beyond naming objects toward thinking about what is happening in the pictures and relating it to the child's own experiences. This progression is implemented through repeated readings of the same book with the PEER sequence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Repeated readings of the same book so the child becomes increasingly familiar with it
- Adult use of the PEER prompt-evaluate-expand-repeat sequence
