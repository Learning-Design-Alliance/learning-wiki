# Explicitly teach problem-solving heuristics (visualizing, checking conservation principles, examining limiting cases) to help students build and employ physical intuition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing instruction that makes expert heuristics explicit for students. It concludes that "It may be useful to design instructional s trategies that explicitly teach problem solving heuristics and help students build and employ intuition in physical problems". The heuristics named are visualizing and analyzing the problem qualitatively, examining the applicability of conservation principles, and examining limiting cases, which professors used and students rarely did.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must make the heuristics explicit rather than assuming students will acquire them
#### Constraints
- The article notes the zero-friction limiting case in this problem class yields no useful clues and can lead to incorrect inferences
