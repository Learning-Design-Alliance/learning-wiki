# Educator and leadership actions to address inequitable AP STEM offerings: advocacy, recruitment, dual enrollment, and funded leadership review

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article closes with a set of implementable actions for educators confronting the structural inequities its data reveal. Teachers and staff with privileged backgrounds should advocate for systemic changes, since faculty of color bear "cultural taxation" through unpaid equity labor. Schools without AP Physics should establish programs for equal access, existing programs should reflect the school population, collaboration with prior-course teachers can identify prospective students, and dual enrollment with local two-year colleges should be pursued where obstacles cannot be overcome.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Support from district and school level leadership with funding for both lab equipment and professional development for faculty
#### Constraints
- Where obstacles for implementation cannot be overcome, such as not having enough students to offer second-year AP physics classes, dual enrollment with local two-year colleges is the recommended route

### Target Learners
- high school students at underserved (majority Hispanic, SED) public schools

### Target Learning Goals
- equal access to AP STEM coursework including AP Physics
