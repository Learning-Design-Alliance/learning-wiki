# Differentiate AICA support by CT level: open-ended exploration for high CT students, structured SRL scripts for low CT students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes two differentiated instructional strategies grounded in its findings. For high CT students, "unrestricted access to AICA should be granted" with encouragement to explore multiple code-implementation methods and engage in metacognitive reflection. For low CT students, structured SRL scripts should guide them through "goal setting – strategy selection – monitoring – evaluation" to internalize external prompts into an autonomous regulation cycle, transforming the AICA from an answer source into a cognitive partner.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires knowing students' CT levels, e.g. via a validated CT assessment, to assign the appropriate support type
#### Constraints
- The article states reflection journals are inherently subjective and may not fully capture actual cognitive states; novelty effects on behavior and performance could not be completely eliminated

### Target Learners
- eighth-grade students (aged 13–15) in K–12 coding education

### Target Learning Goals
- Python coding knowledge and skills
- self-regulated learning skills
- computational thinking
