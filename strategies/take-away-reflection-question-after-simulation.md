# Use an open-ended take-away question after simulation to prompt reflection and gather qualitative evidence on valued learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course embedded a reflective open-ended question, "What is the one thing you are going to take away with you at the end of this course?", which served a dual purpose. The authors state it "serves to help a participant cement that learning in their memory by facilitating reflection and allowing participants time to frame learning outcomes from the session", while also gathering evidence on which course elements contributed most to learning. Responses fed the thematic analysis of communication, leadership and teamwork.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time within or after the course for participants to complete the open-ended reflection
#### Constraints
- Logistical challenges in the live educational setting limited the amount of qualitative data that could be collected

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate clinical trainees in simulation courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Reflection on learning outcomes
- Consolidation of non-technical skills learning

## Claims

- [Thematic analysis of open-ended responses identifies communication, leadership and teamwork as the most valued learning outcomes, especially in IP courses](../claims/simulation-debrief-themes-communication-leadership-teamwork.md) [+W]
