# Design recommendations for a contextually appropriate sim-IPE module in resource-constrained settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 4 mixed) · 5 studies (3 review, 2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Participants and faculty proposed concrete design features: dedicated simulation-trained personnel per department, joint lectures modeling interprofessional collaboration, faculty cross-teaching, early introduction of interprofessional values from first year, small group sizes, preparatory and debriefing sessions, attendance follow-up, curriculum harmonization before simulation activities, and leveraging capacity grants for funding. Faculty cautioned that "Implementation does not start from the simulation center. It starts from what I call the 'software', the curriculum."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each department should have someone specifically trained in delivering simulation-based learning
- Curriculum harmonization and timetabling should precede simulation activities
#### Constraints
- Current simulation lab space is too small to accommodate all students together
- No institutional policy currently permits cross-departmental teaching loads

### Target Learners
- undergraduate pharmacy students
- health professions faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- communication skills
- role clarity
- teamwork
- pharmaceutical care competencies
