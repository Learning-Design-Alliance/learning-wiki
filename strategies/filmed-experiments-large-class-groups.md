# Use filmed experiments in geography lessons to explain course material and save time, especially for large class groups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
All teachers were positive about the use of a filmed experiment in the learning path and would use the film in their usual lessons because it explains the course material, gives extra insight, saves time, and students were attentive. Teachers think that students in small class groups could conduct the experiment themselves, especially in small research projects, while for large class groups the experiment on film is seen as a solution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A filmed version of a classroom experiment that illustrates the learning content
#### Constraints
- Some constraints on the use of the real experiment in the classroom relate to school or classroom infrastructure and to teachers' fear that the experiment could fail

### Target Learners
- secondary school geography students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding cloud and wind formation processes
