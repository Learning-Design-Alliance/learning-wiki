# Use standardized-patient simulations and digital learning games to train health professionals to avoid medical jargon and communicate in plain language

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend experiential training to counter professionals' insensitivity to jargon: "Simulations of clinical encounters with standardized patients, for example, may be helpful," increasing awareness of jargon use and offering practice in plain language. They further suggest that as gamification becomes useful in medical education, educators may develop digital-based learning games that let students become aware of medical jargon and learn the corresponding plain language replacements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators with capacity to develop simulation encounters or digital learning games embedding jargon-plain-language pairs.
#### Constraints
- Recommended in the Taiwan context where medical terminology learned from English texts often lacks common plain-language substitutes.

### Target Learners
- Health professional students and practitioners

### Target Learning Goals
- Awareness of medical jargon use and ability to explain medical conditions in plain language
