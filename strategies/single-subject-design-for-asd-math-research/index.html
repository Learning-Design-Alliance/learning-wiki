Strategies

Use single-subject designs in future studies of word problem solving interventions for students with ASD¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The researcher recommends that future studies focused on students with ASD use a single subject design, as it would be more sensitive to this population and allow repeated measurement via CBM two or three questions at a time each day. This methodology may better assess change over time and whether a causal relationship exists between the intervention and performance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Future studies should examine only one or two types of word problems, or problems identical in nature with different stories and numbers

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • students with autism spectrum disorders

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mathematical word problem solving

Claims¶

  • Two of three students with ASD declined in word problem solving performance from pretest to posttest after the intervention [+W]
  • SWD in both treatment and control groups showed a statistically significant pretest-to-posttest improvement on question 8 [+W]
  • No statistically significant difference was found between posttest scores of SWD in the treatment group and SWD in the control group [+W]
  • The K-N-W-S graphic organizer intervention package did not produce statistically significant pretest-to-posttest gains in word problem solving for students with disabilities in the treatment group [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Prefer repeated measurement of cognitive load over a single delayed rating to capture changes during learning or performance
