# Implement simulations as one-hour in-class activities with pre-test, guided group exploration, and immediate post-test

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
To maintain class structure, each simulation replaces a lecture as a one-hour activity. The implemented sequence was: administer the pre-test in 10 minutes, give students the activity guide with steps and open-ended questions, have students work in groups of two, three or four completing the simulation in about 45 minutes, then complete the post-test in about 10 more minutes. Pre- and post-exams were made much alike, except for format, "to facilitate the correlation between exams on a question-to-question basis."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment exams and the simulation are taken by the students after covering the material in class
- Pre- and post-examinations designed to measure the impact of the simulation taken
#### Constraints
- The article notes the possibility of using a control group was discarded due to the relatively small number of students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate modern physics students in courses without a laboratory

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of the physical ingredients of key modern physics experiments
