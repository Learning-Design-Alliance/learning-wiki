# 'What's My Emotion?' Game Check-In

## Description
Students use the EQ Feeling Chart to identify their feelings. One student acts out their feeling through body language and facial expressions, while the rest of the class guesses the emotion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- EQ Feeling Chart, space for students to act out emotions, and a willingness to participate in a game.
#### Constraints
- Some students may feel uncomfortable acting out their emotions. The effectiveness of the activity depends on creating a safe and supportive classroom environment.

### Target Learners

- All ages, adaptable for pre-K through college students

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Identify and express feelings, develop non-verbal communication skills, increase emotional awareness, foster empathy and understanding

### Affordances
- Provides a fun and engaging way to explore emotions. Encourages students to develop their non-verbal communication skills. Promotes empathy and understanding through guessing and observation.

### Personalization
- Students can choose from a range of emotions or focus on specific emotional categories. The level of participation can be adjusted based on individual comfort levels.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe student participation in acting out and guessing emotions. Assess the accuracy of students' guesses and the expressiveness of their actions.

## Impact
- Acknowledging emotions calms the amygdala and reduces the intensity of felt emotions, helping individuals feel more in control.  Promotes emotional literacy and awareness.
