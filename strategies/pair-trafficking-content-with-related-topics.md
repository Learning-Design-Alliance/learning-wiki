# Pair human trafficking content with related existing topics when adapting the curriculum to other institutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
For institutions adapting similar modules, the authors recommend leveraging topic adjacency to fit constrained curricula: "consider pairing human trafficking content with related topics like trauma-informed care, difficult communication, refugee care, overuse injuries, sexually transmitted infections, or intimate partner violence, among others." They also note a wide array of simulation methods, from mixed mannequin-and-SP use to exclusively SPs playing patient and trafficker, and advise centers to employ whichever resources are available while focusing on recognizing subtle signs and mastering communication skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each institution must examine its curriculum and determine the most appropriate home for the content
#### Constraints
- Additional instructional time is rarely available, so content must complement or replace existing curriculum

### Target Learners
- medical students
- healthcare professional trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing subtle signs of trafficking
- mastering trauma-informed communication skills
