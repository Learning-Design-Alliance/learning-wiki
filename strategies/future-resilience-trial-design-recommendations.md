# Design future resilience-intervention research as large pragmatic RCTs with sufficient follow-up and explicit resilience definitions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review closes with design recommendations for future studies of resilience interventions in practicing physicians. It recommends that "future research include large pragmatic RCTs with sufficient follow-up time", and that trials define what they mean by resilience and whether the outcome measure actually measures resilience or another proxy outcome. It also calls for a consensus on the definition of resilience and how it is measured, and for longer follow-up to ensure effects are sustained.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trials should define what they mean by resilience and whether the outcome measure actually measures resilience or another proxy outcome
#### Constraints
- Current evidence base is heterogeneous, small, and of very low to low quality, so no specific intervention can yet be recommended

### Target Learners
- physicians who have completed training and work in any setting

### Target Learning Goals
- improve resilience and reduce negative outcomes including burnout, anxiety and depression
