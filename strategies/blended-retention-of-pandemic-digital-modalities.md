# Reshape the anatomy curriculum toward a blended form by retaining digital modalities introduced during the pandemic

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 3 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
After the lockdowns ended, the department attempted to "reshape the curriculum toward the blended form by retaining the digital modalities introduced during the pandemic." Instructional videos and image-based quizzes were reused as supplements to in-person laboratory activities, and Moodle was used to conduct class-entrance tests, allowing prompt review and feedback. The article argues innovations from COVID-19 must continue to be adapted into current anatomical pedagogy rather than merely reverting to pre-pandemic routines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Moodle learning-management system for class-entrance tests with prompt review and feedback
- Instructional videos and image-based quizzes retained as supplements to in-person laboratory activities
#### Constraints
- The shift to online learning exposed underlying curriculum shortcomings that persisted even after the return to in-person classes, motivating comprehensive curricular revisions

### Target Learners
- First-year medical students in a gross anatomy course

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained improvement in anatomy teaching and learning effectiveness
- Maintaining formative feedback alongside in-person instruction
