# Use PEA as a design vocabulary to specify policy goals, enforcement mechanisms, and authority arrangements when building LLM-based programming support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that instead of describing a system only as a tutor, chatbot, hint generator, or feedback tool, designers specify the Policy goal it encodes, the Enforcement mechanism enacting it, and the Authority arrangement determining who can adjust it during use. A design walkthrough shows the same pedagogical goal can produce different learning conditions depending on enforcement and authority. Future directions include collaborative help routing, grounded AI literacy, and governed content generation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designers must specify policy, enforcement, and authority explicitly rather than relying on generic system labels
#### Constraints
- Different configurations require different evidence, including learning outcomes, help-seeking quality, peer interaction, AI literacy, instructional alignment, and teacher workload

### Target Learners
- Introductory programming learners
- CS educators and tool designers

### Target Learning Goals
- Pedagogically bounded, configurable, and accountable LLM assistance for programming learning
