# Design CPC/CPD frameworks as a 'mixed' model emphasising practical, simulation-based and team-based activities with evidence of patient contact

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that regulators implementing continuing professional competence schemes adopt a 'mixed' model combining mandatory and voluntary elements, weighted toward practically orientated activities. It states the model "should include evidence of patient contact" and lists preferred activities: "practical training scenarios; annual cardiac recertification; e-learning followed by related practice; training on simulation manikins." Passive individual activities such as e-learning alone, project work and journal reviews attracted least interest.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Practitioner consultation or needs assessment prior to introducing the programme, which the article identifies as rarely reported but associated with practice change
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from Irish pre-hospital practitioners surveyed before CPC implementation; the authors note those with valid email addresses and interest in the topic responded, limiting generalisability

### Target Learners
- Registered pre-hospital practitioners (EMTs, Paramedics, Advanced Paramedics)

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintenance of professional competence in pre-hospital emergency care
