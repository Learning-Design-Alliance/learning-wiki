# Change assessment approaches under emergency online conditions: low-stakes assessments, group projects, honor-code exams, and equitable grading accommodations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advises instructors to treat the transition as extraordinary circumstances and freely change assessment strategies, grading rubrics and the weighting of pre- and post-transition material. It suggests reducing or eliminating the traditional final exam in favor of "many low-stakes assessments, final projects and online presentations", group projects with individual accountability, honor codes for at-home exams, incompletes or modified requirements for constrained students, and not giving a grade lower than what students had earned before going online.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Willingness to revise grading rubrics and weightings, and for exams, an honor code
#### Constraints
- Students may lack home resources; isolation can increase anxiety, which group work can reduce

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students, especially those disadvantaged by the abrupt online transition

### Target Learning Goals
- demonstrating learning through projects and presentations rather than high-stakes exams
- collaboration and reduced isolation
