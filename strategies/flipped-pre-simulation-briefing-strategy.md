# Deliver flipped-learning pre-simulation briefing materials (short online videos plus a theory-based briefing form) before simulation practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy uploads preparatory materials — protocols, a lecture video on the neonatal resuscitation algorithm, and a briefing form structured by Tanner's clinical judgement model — to an online platform before class, so students study in advance and discuss and role-play in class. The article reports that "flipped learning was developed and applied to the pre‐simulation briefing curriculum prior to the neonatal nursing simulation exercise", raising critical thinking, self-confidence and clinical judgement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online platform access before class; expert-validated briefing form and video content; group discussion time in class
#### Constraints
- The article recommends online videos not exceed roughly 10–20 minutes for concentration; its own 90-minute video was linked to failure to raise knowledge or lower anxiety

### Target Learners
- senior undergraduate nursing students
- nursing students preparing for neonatal intensive care practice

### Target Learning Goals
- neonatal emergency and resuscitation care
- clinical judgement
- critical thinking
- confidence in nursing performance
