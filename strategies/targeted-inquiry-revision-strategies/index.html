Targeted low-resource revisions that raise the inquiry level of existing laboratory activities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Rather than replacing a whole laboratory course with an authentic research-based one, instructors can make targeted changes that shift individual activities up one inquiry level. The article recommends: "Replace multiple ﬁll-in-the-blank activities with fewer short-answer questions" (confirmation to structured); ask students to categorize samples themselves before instruction (structured to guided); and "Exchange directions about how to do something with challenges to achieve a goal" (structured to open). These changes need few additional resources and no additional time.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • First assess the inquiry levels of each laboratory class using the Buck et al. rubric
  • Target the classes with the highest percentage of lower-inquiry activities for revision

Constraints¶

  • All introductory geology laboratory classes contain some lower-level inquiry activities, which can still be effective for introducing basic information

Target Learners¶

  • introductory undergraduate geoscience students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • science process skills
  • content understanding of rocks, minerals, fossils, faults, and streams

Affordances¶

  • Buck Five Level Inquiry Rubric

Claims¶

  • Revised-section students scored significantly higher on the rock and mineral portion of the midterm exam despite identifying half as many samples [+W]
  • Students in revised higher-inquiry rock and mineral laboratory sections scored significantly higher than traditional-section students on postlab assessments [+W]
  • Students in the revised geologic time laboratory scored significantly higher on the final exam Earth history question [+W]
  • Rubric-guided revisions measurably shifted the inquiry-level composition of the three targeted laboratory classes toward higher levels [+W]
