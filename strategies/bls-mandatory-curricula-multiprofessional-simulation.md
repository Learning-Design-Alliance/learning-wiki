# Integrate basic life support as a mandatory curricular activity and use multi-professional simulation in teaching-service settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends two implementable actions arising from its findings: "The insertion of BLS as a mandatory activity in the curricula of the health courses can be an important action to increase the quality of care for CRA by students", and conducting simulation training with multi-professional teams within teaching-service integration, since each participant acting in his or her role reinforces the importance of each role in CRA care. The study itself trained physicians, nurses, nursing technicians, residents and interns together in a university hospital.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained facilitators, adequate physical and human resources, and a clinically adapted simulation environment
#### Constraints
- Multi-professional team building activities are not usual in the Brazilian context
- High cost of immersion courses and lack of regional offerings limit professional adherence

### Target Learners
- Medical students in internship phase, nurses, nursing technicians, resident physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic and advanced life support knowledge and team performance in cardiorespiratory arrest care
