# Design crowdsourcing HITs with example videos, explicit handwriting guidelines, and correctness rules to elicit usable tutorial videos

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper's HIT design asked workers to watch example videos of what a good explanation looks like, provided explicit guidelines showing good and bad handwriting examples, and stated rules that the video must be original, must be mathematically correct, and may not contain images of children, nudity, or profanity. The authors conclude crowdsourcing is feasible "provided that appropriate guidelines (e.g., about using clear handwriting) on how to craft the explanations are provided."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Workers must give informed consent and sign a video recording release so videos can be used in subsequent learning experiments
#### Constraints
- Effort per video is considerable – often an hour or more of total time according to participant self-reports

### Target Learners
- adult crowdsourcing workers (18 years or older) on Amazon Mechanical Turk

### Target Learning Goals
- creating novel video explanations of how to solve specific mathematics problems (logarithms)
