Use explicit CT-framed worksheets and templates (storyboards, decomposition, abstraction) to integrate computational thinking into biology and engineering activities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In Y2 the team created worksheets and templates that allowed students to decompose animal structures and functions, abstract and represent a sequence of animal movements, and compare animal and robot structure–function. Students completed a storyboard of 3 labelled sketches (abstraction) showing digging at several points of time, and in Y3 added vector arrows for motion paths and internal structures, plus a worksheet supporting abstraction from animal analysis to a mimicking robot mechanism.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Worksheets and templates that frame animal analysis as decomposition and abstraction
  • Teacher prompts for classwide comparative-anatomy conversations (Y3)

Constraints¶

  • In Y1, CT goals were stated in front matter but not explicitly supported in student and teacher materials, so student work was not analyzed in CT terms

Target Learners¶

  • middle school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • decomposition
  • abstraction
  • algorithmic thinking
  • structure–function analysis

Claims¶

  • The Y2 curriculum supported students in enacting the CT practices of decomposition and abstraction in biology and engineering work [+W]
  • Narrowing the design task in Y2 to a digging robot with targeted biology resources supported students in completing structure–function analyses and biomimetic designs [+W]
  • With the revised Y3 curriculum, students conducted more in-depth structure–function analyses that guided their robot designs, mimicking both structure and function [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Narrow the design task and provide targeted structure–function resources to enable discipline-driven technology design
