# Scaffold planning and metacognitive monitoring rather than only suppressing gaming behavior

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because gaming appears to involve maladaptive self-regulation — students rely on system feedback, avoid planning, and engage in reactive processes — the authors argue interventions should move past suppression or prevention. As they write, "learning systems should scaffold students’ planning and metacognitive monitoring skills that are less effective during gaming episodes". This includes SRL supports such as goal setting, metacognitive prompts, and progress monitoring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Systems must be able to identify the underlying regulatory mechanisms leading to gaming, integrating behavioral and cognitive indicators.
#### Constraints
- The authors note designing effective interventions requires understanding why students game, including motivation, time constraints, and platform design; some interventions like gamification have shown limited success.

### Target Learners
- students using intelligent tutoring systems or digital learning games

### Target Learning Goals
- adaptive self-regulated learning
- reduced gaming the system behavior
- deeper engagement with learning content
