# Address scalability, instructor engagement, gender participation, localization, and evaluation design when adopting arts-based STEM integration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article identifies open issues to guide future adoption of arts-based integration in technical curricula. It notes that "guidelines and best practices are generally not available to naturally include the teaching of professional skills in technical subjects as a single unit", and that generalisation requires intentional generalisation practices with institutional support. It also recommends attending to teacher engagement, promoting STEM careers at preuniversity levels via liberal arts, extending the approach to other engineering fields, reinforcing female participation in STEM, adapting the methodology to local conditions, and designing balanced qualitative-quantitative evaluations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must be convinced of the importance of professional skills as a valuable teaching and learning objective
- Curricula need 'space' and preparation for such teaching objects
#### Constraints
- Generalisation only comes after intentional generalisation practices, where institutional support plays a relevant role
- Adapting the general methodology to local conditions is a nontrivial task

### Target Learners
- computer science and engineering students at technical universities
- preuniversity students during technical university visits

### Target Learning Goals
- professional skills integrated with technical competencies
- female engagement in STEM careers
