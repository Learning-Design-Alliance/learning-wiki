# Divide practice among bonds so each bond helps the others and shift practice to new types of situations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends improving on past hit-and-miss customs by the "proper division of practice amongst bonds, the arrangement of learning so that each bond helps the others, the adroit shifting of practice of a bond to each new type of situation requiring it to operate under changed conditions, and the elimination of excess practice where nothing substantial is gained." Notable improvements are expected from these means.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must keep close account of the learning of pupils unless the material for practice is adequate, well-balanced and sufficiently motivated
#### Constraints
- Without adequate, well-balanced, sufficiently motivated material, disastrous under-learning of many bonds is almost sure to occur

### Target Learners
- Elementary school pupils of median or varying ability in arithmetic, grades I to VI

### Target Learning Goals
- Formation of strong, transferable arithmetical bonds without wasteful over-learning
