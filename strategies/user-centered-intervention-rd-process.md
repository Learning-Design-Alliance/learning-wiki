# User-centered R&D with A/B tests and rapid prototyping to tailor interventions to a target population

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a multi-phase R&D process used to prepare the National Study of Learning Mindsets intervention for entering 9th graders, involving several years and thousands of pilot participants. The authors "adapted procedures from user-centered design: A/B tests (in which small variants on a message are tested against each other in multifactor randomized studies) and rapid prototyping (in which early-stage materials are shared with user groups who point out areas that could be improved)". Phase 2 immersed researchers in the psychological world of 9th graders; Phase 3 showed the revised intervention better increased challenge seeking at posttest than the base version, and the final version showed positive effects at a national scale.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Several years of development and thousands of pilot participants; interventions are highly dependent on subtle features of materials and procedures matched to the target population.
#### Constraints
- It is not clear the revised treatment could be easily transported to other contexts such as colleges or the workplace; successful replication there may require new rounds of R&D.

### Target Learners
- entering 9th-grade high school students in a nationally representative sample

### Target Learning Goals
- growth mindset adoption
- challenge seeking
- academic achievement (GPA, on-track status)
