Use computer simulation-based instruction to teach complex biology concepts such as DNA replication and transcription¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends Computer Simulation Instruction strategy as a tool to make difficult biology concepts easier and more concrete for learners. It states it is "pertinent to provide tools that can make teaching and learning difficult biology conce pts such as genetics easier and concrete", and implemented this by teaching undergraduate education students DNA replication and transcription concepts with computer simulations instead of blackboard lecture. The strategy was evaluated against a lecture-method control with pre- and post-tests and a semi-structured interview.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors need access to simulation-based teaching materials for the target biology concepts
  • A success test such as the DRBST is used to measure achievement before and after instruction

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from a single quasi-experiment with 70 education students at one college in the 2015-2016 academic year

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate bachelor of education (B.Ed and D.Ed) students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding DNA replication and transcription concepts in genetics

Claims¶

  • Female biology education students' academic achievement in DNA replication increased significantly more than male students' [+M]
  • Simulation-based teaching produced significantly higher post-test scores across fill-in-the-blanks, matching, and short-answer test sections [+M]
  • Education students taught DNA replication with simulation-based instruction achieved significantly higher post-test scores than students taught by traditional lecture methods [+M]
  • Simulation-based teaching produced significantly higher retention scores in DNA replication than conventional lecture teaching [+M]
  • Simulation-based biology teaching produced significantly higher post-test success scores than traditional teaching methods for education students. [+M]
  • Students recommend integrating computer-based simulation cases into each course to make learning more concrete [+M]
