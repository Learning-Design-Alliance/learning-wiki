Guide and track peer-support assistants in a more organized manner to increase the supported group's effect size¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In its suggestions the article recommends strengthening the organizational side of peer-support programs: "to increase the effect siz e of the supported group, assistant should be guided and tracked by their instructors in more organized manner". In the study this took the form of LTA booklet logs checked weekly by class-teachers; the authors suggest intensifying such instructor oversight. They also call for qualitative research on the affective impact of peer support, since the quantitative design could not capture it.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructor time for regular guidance and tracking of assistants
  • A logging instrument (LTA booklet) that makes peer-support activity visible to teachers

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is offered without a tested comparison of guidance intensities in this study

Target Learners¶

  • university-level peer supporters (assistants) and the lower-level peers they support

Target Learning Goals¶

  • increasing achievement gains for supported English learners

Claims¶

  • Peer-supported low-achieving university students significantly outperformed unsupported controls on English posttest scores after a 15-week LTA program [+W]
  • Unsupported low-achieving students showed only a slight achievement increase (d = 0.19) under traditional instruction [+W]
  • Acting as a Language Teacher Assistant did not significantly change successful students' English achievement in either direction [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Schedule about 90 minutes a week of structured peer-assisted learning in which pairs of students at different ability or proficiency levels work together on academic tasks
