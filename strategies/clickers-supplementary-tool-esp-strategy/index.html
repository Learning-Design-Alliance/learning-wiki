Use clickers as a supplementary tool to enhance ESP learners' performance, including embedding clicker activities in ESP course book iTools sets¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that ESP teachers use clickers in their classes and that course designers allocate more clicker use in the iTools sets of ESP books instead of providing just videos of related topics. It argues clickers "may be used as a supplementary tool to enhance performance of ESP learners in the field of tourism and hospitality", turning students' smartphones from distractors into pedagogical tools.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors need a computer, a projector, and clickers, which can be replaced with mobile phones running classroom applications.
  • Questions should be prepared in advance and supported with pictures or videos where relevant.

Constraints¶

  • The article states results cannot be generalized to the entire population of tourism and hospitality students due to the diverse nature of these students.

Target Learners¶

  • ESP students at 2-year vocational schools, particularly in tourism and hospitality

Target Learning Goals¶

  • ESP language performance in tourism-specific topics

Claims¶

  • During clicker implementation, students' average answering time decreased for both correct and incorrect answers while accuracy stayed nearly stable [+W]
  • The experimental group's ESP scores improved significantly from pre-test to post-test under clicker use, while the control group's did not [+W]
  • Ten weeks of Kahoot! clicker use produced significantly higher ESP post-test scores than the same instruction without clickers [+W]
  • The control and experimental groups were statistically equivalent on the ESP pre-test before the clicker implementation [+W]
  • Male and female clicker users did not differ significantly in ESP post-test scores, but only males improved significantly from pre-test to post-test [~W]
  • Students attributed improved understanding to the discussion process of the implementation model rather than the clicker technology itself [~W]
  • Students perceived clickers' greatest course impact as lesson variation and fun, with learning influence a distant second [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Combine anonymous clicker questions with small-group discussion of conceptual questions
