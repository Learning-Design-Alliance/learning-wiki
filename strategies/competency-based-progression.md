# Competency Based Progression

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Competency based progression (CBP) restructures pacing so that advancement depends on demonstrated mastery of explicitly defined competencies — measurable statements of what a learner can do — rather than time in seat, age, or unit completion. Learners move on when they can reliably perform the competency, and receive additional time, support, or alternative pathways when they cannot.

## Design Implications

CBP operationalizes [Mastery Learning](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md): Bloom's argument that most learners can master most objectives given the time and instruction they individually need [+S]. Meta-analytic evidence shows mastery learning formats produce positive achievement effects, largest when mastery thresholds are high and corrective feedback is built in [Mastery learning improves achievement, especially with high mastery standards and corrective feedback.](../claims/adaptive-learning-improves-outcomes.md) [+M]. Because advancement is criterion-referenced, assessment must be frequent, transparent, and aligned to the competency definitions — CBP collapses the distinction between learning and [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) into continuous [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit, observable competency definitions with performance criteria (rubrics or proficiency scales)
- Multiple, varied assessment opportunities per competency — a single high-stakes test contradicts the model
- Systems for tracking individual progress and routing learners to [Practice](../elements/practice.md) or reteaching when mastery is not yet demonstrated
- Flexible time structures: schedules, grading, and credit rules that permit variable pacing

#### Constraints
- Group-based instruction and fixed schedules undermine CBP; without genuinely flexible pacing, "competency-based" becomes relabeling of traditional grading [-M]
- Low or loosely enforced mastery thresholds produce shallow advancement — learners pass with fragile knowledge and accumulate gaps [Mastery learning effects shrink when mastery standards are weak.](../claims/adaptive-learning-improves-outcomes.md) [~M]
- Advanced learners can stall in structured environments if "mastery" is defined as completing the same work rather than deeper performance; CBP requires extension pathways, not just remediation [~M]
- Assessment burden is high: designing, scoring, and tracking competency evidence at scale strains teachers and platforms [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Fully personalized platforms** (e.g., Khan Academy mastery tracks, Summit Public Schools' Basecamp/PLP) automate pacing and item routing
- **Hybrid classroom models** retain whole-class anchor activities while individualizing advancement on core competencies
- **Higher education programs** (e.g., Western Governors University, Purdue's degree-competency pilots) replace credit hours with direct competency assessment
- **Standards-based grading** is a partial implementation: reporting by competency without changing pacing

### Target Learners
- Struggling learners who need more time than uniform pacing allows — the group for whom mastery learning shows the largest gains [Mastery learning improves achievement, especially with high mastery standards and corrective feedback.](../claims/adaptive-learning-improves-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Adult and returning learners with variable prior knowledge, who can accelerate through familiar material
- Learners motivated by visible, attainable progress markers; autonomy over pacing supports intrinsic motivation [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Less suited, without strong self-regulation support, to learners who disengage when given pacing control [Self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) must be explicitly taught [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural and foundational knowledge where later learning depends on prior mastery (mathematics, language, technical skills)
- Cumulative skill sequences with clear prerequisite structure
- Less appropriate for goals that are inherently developmental or discursive (e.g., aesthetic judgment, discussion-based reasoning), where "mastery" criteria are hard to specify [~W]

### Instructions
1. **Define competencies** — decompose the domain into observable, assessable competencies with proficiency criteria; sequence them by prerequisite structure ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) can make the map visible to learners)
2. **Set a mastery threshold** — decide what counts as "demonstrated" (e.g., consistent performance across two tasks, not one)
3. **Diagnose on entry** — place learners by current competency, not by grade or cohort ([Activation](../elements/activation.md) of prior knowledge doubles as placement)
4. **Provide instruction and practice** — pair initial instruction with [Practice](../elements/practice.md) and immediate, corrective [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md)
5. **Assess on demand** — let learners attempt competency demonstrations when ready, with retakes after further [Fading](../elements/fading.md) of support
6. **Advance or remediate deliberately** — route non-mastery to targeted reteaching, not repetition of the same instruction; use [Adaptive Difficulty](../elements/adaptive-difficulty.md) where platform support exists

## Examples
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — course mastery tracks gate progression on demonstrated proficiency per skill, with spaced retakes
- **[Western Governors University](https://www.wgu.edu)** — fully competency-based degrees; students progress by passing objective and performance assessments, not credit hours
- **[Summit Public Schools](https://summitps.org)** — K-12 network using competency-aligned projects and a personalized learning platform for pacing and progress tracking
- **Teach to One 360** — middle-school math model that assembles daily personalized learning sequences from a competency map
