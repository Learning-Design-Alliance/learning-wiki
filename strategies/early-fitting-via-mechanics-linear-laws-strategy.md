# Introduce visual fitting earlier in the physics course through mechanics with linear, proportional relationships

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend not waiting for the electricity topic to introduce fitting: many experiments in mechanics and thermodynamics, which students meet earlier, can be better understood if fitting is already established. Since students are more accustomed to manipulating straight lines and interpreting proportional laws, the next step they propose is to design a learning path on a mechanics issue and test it in class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A learning path on a mechanics topic in which the visual fitting method is implemented and tested with students
#### Constraints
- The article states the fit of an exponential curve is not the best way for introducing the visualization; the mechanics learning path had not yet been tested at the time of writing

### Target Learners
- secondary-school physics students in early topics (mechanics, thermodynamics)

### Target Learning Goals
- establishing the fitting process before advanced statistical tools are taught
- interpreting proportional laws through model-data comparison
