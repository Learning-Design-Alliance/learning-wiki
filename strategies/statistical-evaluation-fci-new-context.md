# Always run statistical tests of reliability and discrimination when administering the FCI in a new context

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Before using the FCI with a new student population, evaluate its reliability and discrimination power statistically, focusing on both item analysis and the whole test, using measures such as the item difficulty index, item discrimination index, point biserial coefficient, Kuder-Richardson reliability, and Ferguson's delta. The article concludes: "statistical tests should always be do ne when FCI is administered in a new context." It also recommends that questions 6, 16, 19 and 29 "might be replaced with other questions in a high-achie ving group" and that one might consider constructing a special high-achieving FCI suitable for Physics majors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to item-level and total-score response data for the target population
#### Constraints
- On a high-achieving group there is a substantial risk of encountering ceiling effects, with a decrease in discriminatory power; the test remains useful for weaker parts of the student population

### Target Learners
- university physics instructors administering the FCI to new student groups

### Target Learning Goals
- valid assessment of students' force-concept understanding
