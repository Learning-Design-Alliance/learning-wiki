# Implement large introductory courses in inquiry-based, laboratory-based environments using existing staffing plus undergraduate teaching assistants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors argue that institutional barriers, not staffing ratios, are the main obstacle to inquiry-based algebra-based physics courses. They state it is possible "to teach large introductory classes in an inquiry-based, laboratory-based environment with approximately the same number of faculty and graduate TA's presently assigned to the course" by using time effectively and undergraduate teaching assistants, citing Studio Physics and SCALE-UP as examples.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment; effective use of faculty and TA time; undergraduate teaching assistants
#### Constraints
- The limitation often arises more with equipment and laboratory classroom space than with instructor-to-student ratio

### Target Learners
- students in large introductory algebra-based physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding and scientific skills through inquiry-based laboratory work
