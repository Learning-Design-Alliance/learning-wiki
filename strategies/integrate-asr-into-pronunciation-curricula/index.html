Strategies

Integrate ASR into school pronunciation curricula and train teachers to use it¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (1 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 design), q2–q3 · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on their findings, the authors advise schools to "use asr in the curricula plans of the English language subject", extend speech recognition to other English classes at different scholastic levels, and have "English language teachers may be trained to be able to use asr in teaching pronunciation". They further recommend attention to integrating ASR into learning environments, using it as an active tool in the educational process, and call for more research with other classes, bigger samples, and different techniques and methods.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers trained to use ASR; speech recognition set up and used as active tools in the educational process

Constraints¶

  • The authors note generalization is limited to similar third-grade samples, one program, and one school context, and the instructor being a researcher is not ideal

Target Learners¶

  • Primary and other scholastic-level EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English pronunciation development

Claims¶

  • Third-grade EFL students taught pronunciation with ASR software significantly outperformed peers taught with regular instruction [+W]
  • Within ASR-based pronunciation practice, individual work produced higher post-test scores than pair work and group work [~W]
  • Whether individual or peer practice works better with ASR-based pronunciation training is unsettled: one study found individual work best, a meta-analysis found peer practice gave larger effects [~M]
  • Guided ASR practice did not produce statistically significant improvement in any specific pronunciation error type, with some errors unimproved or regressed [-W]
  • Amount of ASR practice (days per week, session length) showed no observable difference in pronunciation improvement [~M]
