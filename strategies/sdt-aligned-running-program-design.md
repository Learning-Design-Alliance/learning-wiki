# Design running programs to support autonomy, competence and relatedness needs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete recipe the article used and recommends: structure physical activity programs so students "choose their own running pace and schedule within a given timeframe to support their autonomy," track their own weekly progress to build competence, and run in small peer groups to develop connection. The article further recommends institutions provide safe running tracks, offer beginner-friendly sports workshops with instructor praise for small wins, and let students lead and manage sports programs they are passionate about.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A safe running space or track, a flexible timeframe for participation, and attendance tracking to support the design
#### Constraints
- The article notes the strategy was implemented without a control group, so effects of other physical activity forms were not accounted for

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- intrinsic motivation to learn
- mental wellbeing
- physical activity engagement
