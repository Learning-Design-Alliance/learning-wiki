## Description
The review's headline substantive finding, stated in its abstract, is that "the opportunity for dialogue, the exposure of students to authentic or situated problems and examples, and mentoring had positive effects on CT skills." Each feature maps onto the taxonomy's categories: dialogue (learning through discussion, adversarial or cooperative), authentic/anchored instruction (real-world problems, simulations, cases, role-play), and mentoring (one-on-one expert–novice interaction). The strategy recommends designing CT lessons so that students discuss problems together, work on genuine problems, and receive one-on-one coaching.
