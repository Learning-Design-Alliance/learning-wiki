# Scale regional in-service PBL training by field-testing the curriculum with future tutors, then certifying them as regional tutors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The project first trained 32 health professionals in three editions of a 3-day introductory course on PBL principles, including simulated tutorial groups using the 7 jumps. A first pilot course field-tested the curriculum, timetable, manuals and materials with these future tutors; the field test was considered positive with only minor modifications needed. Of 24 pilot participants, 21 accepted to become tutors, joining the 11 curriculum developers as 32 regional tutors who ran courses from mid-2018, reaching 562 trainees by 2020 — more than the 400 initially planned.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Selection of tutors based on previous in-service training experience and willingness to learn and practice the PBL approach
- Formal involvement of the regional health authority to facilitate participation
#### Constraints
- Staff turnover in maternity hospitals and local health authorities means services are quickly staffed by professionals not updated on the topic

### Target Learners
- regional tutors and maternal and child health professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- breastfeeding knowledge, attitudes and practices; scalable multi-professional in-service training
