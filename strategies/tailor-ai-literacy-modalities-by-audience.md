# Tailor AI literacy instructional modalities to each audience: interactive K-12 activities, discipline-specific higher-ed applications, and workflow-focused workforce workshops

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends matching learning modalities, goals, and instructional methods to each learner group. For K-12, "Activities are designed to be interactive and engaging, such as using block-based coding tools like Scratch to integrate AI modules"; for higher education, CS majors do advanced programming while non-CS majors apply AI tools in fields like healthcare or economics; for the adult workforce, workshop-oriented online activities emphasize practical AI use to optimize workflows; for the public, community workshops and public lectures foster critical media literacy by debunking AI myths.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must combine readings, activities, labs, and reflections across all levels
#### Constraints
- Depth and teaching modalities should vary to suit the unique needs of each group; the article notes AI literacy for non-CS majors is still nascent

### Target Learners
- K-12 students
- CS and non-CS undergraduates
- adult workforce
- general public

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational AI concepts
- ethical reflection
- practical AI tool use
- critical media literacy about AI
