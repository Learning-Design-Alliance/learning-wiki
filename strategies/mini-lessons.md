# Mini Lessons

## Description
A mini lesson is a deliberately brief, tightly focused teaching segment that introduces exactly one concept, strategy, or skill. It typically follows a predictable arc — connect to prior learning, briefly teach or model the target, then hand off to learner activity — and is bounded in time so that the majority of a session is spent with learners working, not listening.

## Design Implications

Mini lessons manage attention and cognitive load by limiting the amount of new information presented before learners apply it [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Their brevity is only effective when the scope is genuinely singular: a mini lesson that smuggles in three ideas becomes a lecture. The structure pairs a short [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) or explanation with immediate learner application, so the teaching moment stays adjacent to use [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A single, clearly named learning target for the segment
- A brief activation of relevant prior knowledge ([Activating Prior Knowledge](activating-prior-knowledge.md)) so new content has something to attach to
- A model or worked example of the target skill, often via [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)
- A planned transition into [Practice](../elements/practice.md) where learners apply the target immediately
- A time budget (5–15 minutes) enforced by design, not by discipline

#### Constraints
- Too much content in one segment overloads working memory and degrades learning [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-S] — the "mini" constraint is load-bearing, not cosmetic
- Ineffective when learners lack the prerequisite knowledge the lesson assumes; the brief format leaves no room to build missing foundations
- Whole-class mini lessons can waste time for learners who already know the content or who need substantially more support; [Adaptive Learning](../principles/adaptive-learning.md) or small-group variants address this
- Repeatedly interrupting productive learner work with new mini lessons fragments practice and reduces its benefits

#### Implementation Variability
- **Whole-class launch**: one mini lesson opens a workshop-style block, then all learners practice
- **Small-group / targeted**: delivered to a subset of learners identified through [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) or [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md)
- **Mid-workshop teaching point**: a second, even shorter segment delivered when observation reveals a shared stumbling block
- **Asynchronous video**: 3–7 minute recorded segments learners access on demand, common in flipped and online courses

### Target Learners
- Novices who need a bounded, low-load introduction before attempting a task [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Learners who benefit from predictable routine; the repeated arc (connect–teach–practice) reduces extraneous processing of *what is happening* so attention goes to content
- Less well served by whole-class mini lessons: advanced learners (redundant) and learners with large gaps (insufficient) — both need differentiated formats

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural skills and strategies that can be named, modeled, and practiced in one sitting
- Conceptual introductions that will be deepened through application, discussion, or [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md)
- Not well suited to complex, multi-faceted understandings requiring extended exploration or [Inquiry](../principles/active-learning.md)

### Instructions
1. **Connect** (1–2 min): name the learning target and link to prior work ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) or a quick activation prompt)
2. **Teach** (3–7 min): explain and model the single target, ideally with a [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) and [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md); keep examples worked and annotated
3. **Check** (1–2 min): a fast comprehension check or [3-2-1 Reflection](3-2-1_reflection.md) to surface confusion before release
4. **Release to practice**: transition learners into [Practice](../elements/practice.md), [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md), or independent work applying the target
5. **Regroup as needed**: use observation during practice to plan the next mini lesson — targeted, small-group, or whole-class

## Examples
- **Reading and writing workshops** (Teachers College Reading & Writing Project, [https://readingandwritingproject.org](https://readingandwritingproject.org)) — a 10-minute mini lesson opens each workshop block: connection, teaching point with demonstration, active engagement, link to independent work.
- **Flipped classroom videos** — instructors record 5–8 minute single-concept segments (e.g., one statistical test per video) that learners watch before applying problems in class.
- **Code.org curricula** ([https://code.org/curriculum](https://code.org/curriculum)) — short whole-class introductions of one programming concept, followed immediately by hands-on puzzle practice.
