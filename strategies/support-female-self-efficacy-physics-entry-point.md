# Support female students' self-efficacy at their entry point into physics education in high school or college

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends intervening at students' entry point into physics education: "A starting point for this change is students’ entry point into physics education at either the high school or collegiate level." Supporting students' self-efficacy at this early point is argued to support development of the cognitive and affective traits that increase the likelihood of pursuing and succeeding in physics careers. This strategy targets changing the status quo in which physics instruction harms female students' self-efficacy.

## Design Implications

## Claims

- [Female high school physics students were much less likely to experience very high self-efficacy states (4.5% vs 38%) and twice as likely to experience very low states as male students.](../claims/female-physics-self-efficacy-state-distributions.md) [+W]
- [Female high school students experience much lower self-efficacy states in physics than male students, with a very large effect size of d = 0.88, and the difference does not occur in other school courses.](../claims/gender-gap-self-efficacy-states-high-school-physics.md) [+W]
- [The large gender difference in self-efficacy states occurs in physics and only in physics, across both high school and university settings, indicating physics instruction systemically harms female students' physics self-efficacy.](../claims/physics-instruction-systemically-harms-female-self-efficacy.md) [+W]
