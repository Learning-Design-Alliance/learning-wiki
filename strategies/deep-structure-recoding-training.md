# Teach learners to recode semantically incongruent representations by comparing strategies and studying the problem's deep structure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy addresses the crucial step learners struggle with: recoding a semantically incongruent initial representation. The article defines recoding as the process by which, when the initially encoded interpreted structure cannot be translated into an appropriate, tractable algorithm, "a new representation can be abstracted by recoding the interpreted structure". In the training studied, children were instructed to compare the two strategies, identify their initial semantic representation, and were shown a visual representation of the deep structure to help them recode their initial encoding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must be able to identify their initial semantic representation
- Training must focus on the mathematical relations between the described entities and the deep structure of the problems
#### Constraints
- The recoding process is described as costly and one that does not systematically happen

### Target Learners
- 4th and 5th grade students solving arithmetic word problems

### Target Learning Goals
- adopting more efficient solving algorithms on problems whose world semantics misalign with the required mathematical semantics

### Affordances
- Seco Semantic Congruence Model
