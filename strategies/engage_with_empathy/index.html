Engage with Empathy¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (4 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q3–q4 · 0 of 5 report an effect size

Description¶

Engage with Empathy is a strategy for effective online communication in which participants take the time to read and understand the original post, exchange ideas with one or more users, tag and mention community members, carefully craft their messages, present evidence and personal experiences, uphold the community's agreed-upon norms, and consume as much as they contribute. It treats empathic communication as a learnable practice — supported by sentence frames, norms, and modeling — rather than a fixed personality trait Empathy is partly trainable through structured practice and perspective-taking. [+M].

Design Implications¶

Empathic engagement builds the social presence that online discussion depends on: learners participate more and interpret ambiguous text-based messages more charitably when they perceive real people behind the posts Social presence in computer conferencing improves perceived learning and interaction quality. [+M]. Because text strips tone and nonverbal cues, empathy must be deliberately designed into prompts, norms, and feedback structures rather than assumed Text-based communication increases misinterpretation of intent relative to richer media. [~M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Basic understanding of online communication, access to the platform, and a commitment to respectful interaction
  • Explicitly agreed community norms (netiquette charter) that participants helped author
  • Sentence frames or response protocols that make perspective-taking concrete
  • Instructor or moderator modeling of empathic responses, especially when disagreement arises

Constraints¶

  • Requires conscious effort and is difficult for participants unfamiliar with digital norms [-W]
  • Depends on community norms and active moderation; in unmoderated spaces, empathic norms erode quickly [-M]
  • Brief empathy training produces short-lived effects unless reinforced by ongoing practice and community structure Brief empathic interventions fade without ongoing reinforcement. [~M]
  • Forced or formulaic empathy ("say something nice") can feel inauthentic and suppress genuine disagreement [-W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Sentence frames for agreement and disagreement ("I appreciate the experience shared by , but in my experience ")
  • Structured roles in discussion (summarizer, questioner, connector) that rotate perspective-taking duties
  • Asynchronous vs. synchronous formats: asynchronous gives time for careful crafting; synchronous demands faster, less polished empathy
  • Peer feedback protocols that require restating the author's point before critiquing it

Target Learners¶

  • Students new to online or blended learning who lack digital communication norms
  • Participants in diverse or intergroup online communities, where empathic engagement improves attitudes across difference Structured empathy-building activities improve intergroup attitudes. [+M]
  • Educators and moderators whose responses set the tone for the whole community

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Digital citizenship: ethical, respectful participation in online communities
  • Perspective-taking and social awareness as discussion skills
  • Sustained, high-quality peer interaction that supports Community of Inquiry social presence

Instructions¶

  1. Establish norms: co-create a netiquette charter with the community before discussion begins (Class Discussion).
  2. Read first: require participants to summarize or restate the original post before responding (Active Listening).
  3. Model: the instructor posts exemplar empathic responses, including a respectful disagreement, and annotates why (Coaching).
  4. Respond with frames: provide sentence frames for agreeing, disagreeing, and building on ideas (Peer Discussion).
  5. Connect members: use tagging and mentions to draw quieter members into exchanges (Check-In).
  6. Reflect: periodically review anonymized exchanges as a group and evaluate them against the norms (Whole-Class Sharing).

Related Strategies¶

  • Active Listening — the face-to-face counterpart; restating before responding is its online form
  • Building Empathy — the broader principle this strategy operationalizes in digital contexts
  • Teachers should learn about students' lives and commitments outside school and build relationships, which students say increases respect, effort, and engagement

Examples¶

  • When presenting a new idea, use the sentence frame: "According to _, we should think about __ in this way: _." When disagreeing, say: "I appreciate the experience shared by _, but in my experience ____."
  • In a discussion forum, a student begins a critique by quoting and paraphrasing the original poster's claim before offering counterevidence, then tags the poster so they can respond.
  • Instructors in online courses post weekly "presence" messages that acknowledge students' circumstances and model warm, specific responses to student posts.
