# Expand funding, structured mentorship and early career guidance rather than removing merit-based selection criteria

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that medical schools and professional bodies address extracurricular disparities by increasing opportunity rather than lowering standards. It argues that "Increases in funding for subsistence should therefore be coupled with bursaries for conference attendance, funded research placements and structured mentorship schemes which would allow all students to compete on merit rather than financial privilege." It further recommends formal mentorship programmes, structured undergraduate research programmes, and mandatory early career guidance sessions with rotations, shadowing and workshops from the first year.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Responsibility extends beyond medical schools: royal colleges and professional associations are described as well placed to coordinate and resource such initiatives.
#### Constraints
- The authors caution against efforts reduced to mere 'tick-box' exercises and tokenism, and note removing academic selection from recruitment risks disincentivising engagement in research and leadership.

### Target Learners
- UK medical students, particularly those from lower socioeconomic backgrounds or without familial links to medicine

### Target Learning Goals
- Equitable access to extracurricular achievement opportunities (research, leadership, presentations, prizes) that influence specialty training selection
