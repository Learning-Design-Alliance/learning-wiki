# Survey learners' preferences on anatomy practical exam structure before designing assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that anatomy curriculum directors gather their own learners' perspectives on assessment formats before designing practical exams, since erroneous assumptions about preferred formats may result in wasted effort and dissatisfied students. The authors used a voluntary REDCap survey with multiple choice and drop-down questions plus optional free responses, distributed after students had experienced multiple exam formats. They encourage other institutions to follow a similar process.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must have experienced the exam format variations being asked about, so they can give informed preferences.
#### Constraints
- Findings from one cohort cannot be generalized to all medical students; voluntary response bias is possible since 49.1% of eligible students did not respond.

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- anatomy knowledge assessment
