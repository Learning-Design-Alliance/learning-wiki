# Apply combined PBL-CBL to anaphylactic shock teaching in fundamental nursing while budgeting extra preparation time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's lessons for practice state that "Combined PBL-CBL can be applied to anaphylactic shock teaching in fundamental nursing to improve students' test scores and critical thinking" and that "teaching plans should account for the extra learning time students need for preparation". The strategy pairs the interactive group-based method with explicit planning for its higher time cost, and the discussion suggests providing more structured scaffolding for pre-class preparation to optimize this aspect.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teaching plans that account for the extra learning time students need for preparation
- Teacher-prepared pre-class materials and guided group discussion structure
#### Constraints
- Evidence is from a single-center study with relatively short-term follow-up, which the authors state may affect applicability

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students in fundamental nursing courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Anaphylactic shock prevention and care
- Test performance and critical thinking

### Affordances
