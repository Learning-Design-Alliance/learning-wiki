# Brief randomized audiovisual sampling to estimate epistemic game frequency in a live classroom

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents "a means of 'sampling' audiovisual data in a live classroom of several simultaneous groups" using hand-held cameras in brief 1-2 minute recordings of each lab table. Game counts are normalized by dividing by total recording time in minutes to avoid skewing by recording length. This provides a preliminary quantitative measure linking group dynamic with driving learning orientation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Voluntary written consent from recorded students; normalization of game counts by total recording time per group
#### Constraints
- Footage time per lab group was not always approximately equal; students who asked for help got prolonged exposure to recording

### Target Learners
- introductory algebra-based physics laboratory students

### Target Learning Goals
- estimating frequency of problem solving strategies in group work
