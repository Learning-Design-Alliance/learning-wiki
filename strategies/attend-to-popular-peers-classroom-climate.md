# Teachers should attend to popular peers' impact on classroom climate, friendship processes, and achievement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's Educational Impact and Implications Statement recommends that "Teachers need to appreciate and attend to popular peers and their impact on classroom climate, friendship processes and academic achievement in early adolescence." Because popular students' endorsed goals create norms governing which friendships form, persist, and socialize achievement, monitoring and shaping the goals popular peers display is a lever for influencing peer dynamics around academics. This shifts attention from teacher-managed goal framing to the peer-driven normative environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be able to identify popular students and the achievement goals those students visibly endorse
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is early-adolescent math and science classrooms in small urban Midwestern US communities; generalization to other domains or ages is not tested

### Target Learners
- Early adolescents in elementary and middle school classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement and classroom peer climate around achievement
