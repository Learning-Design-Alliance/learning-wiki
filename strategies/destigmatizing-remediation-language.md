# Replace deficit-framed remediation language with conditioning and qualification-standard terminology

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper recommends moving institutional culture away from terminology implying pathology and deficit. "We recommend reducing the use of terms such as remediation, diagnosis, deficits, etc." and suggests phrasing such as 'not meeting a qualification standard', academic conditioning, performance coaching, and learning strengths and weaknesses. The underlying athletic-conditioning model treats remediation as analysis of recent performances and targeted exercises, not discovery of a pre-existing flaw.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Language change must be paired with the underlying cultural shift recognizing complex factors impacting performance
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge no solution will fit all situations or schools

### Target Learners
- medical students
- medical school faculty and leadership

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing stigma around help-seeking
- reframing academic difficulty as performance conditioning
