# Design at the Margins

## Description
This principle involves designing with the students at the edges of a community in mind, particularly those disempowered due to identity or poverty. It emphasizes centering race in the work and operates under the belief that what works for those at the margins will be beneficial for all learners. It involves identifying marginalized students (e.g., students of color, those receiving interventions, experiencing family trauma, living far from school, or English Language Learners) and using methods like empathy interviews and student shadowing to understand their experiences. This approach favors process over urgency, ensuring that solutions are deliberate and equity-centered.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a commitment to equity, a willingness to listen to and prioritize the voices of marginalized students, and resources for conducting empathy interviews and student shadowing.
#### Constraints
- Can be time-consuming and require significant effort to implement effectively. May face resistance from stakeholders who are not committed to equity. Relies heavily on the accuracy and depth of information gathered through empathy interviews and student shadowing.
