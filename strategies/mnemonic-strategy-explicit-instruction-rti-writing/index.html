Implement low-cost mnemonic story-writing strategies with explicit instruction and planning time within RTI¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The author draws two practice implications from the trial: both ART and T3 can be implemented in classrooms with no real added monetary cost, using art media, pencils/pens, and paper, and teachers could discuss the strategies at grade- or division-level meetings. The article states that "the results of the study confirm that mnemonic strategy instruction produces gains for struggling writers" and that offering students planning time such as art and verbal dialogue can help improve story writing within RTI's explicit-instruction paradigm.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Art media, pencils/pens, and paper readily available in classrooms
  • Teacher time to read about and discuss the strategies at grade- or division-level meetings

Constraints¶

  • Results come from a small sample of almost only White children and should be generalized with caution

Target Learners¶

  • struggling writers in second grade and older grades

Target Learning Goals¶

  • story writing
  • self-regulation of the writing process

Affordances¶

  • Art Ask Reflect Text Strategy

Claims¶

  • ART students achieved larger effect sizes for story content (1.46) and number of words written (.95), while T3 students achieved the largest effect size for story quality (1.13) [+W]
  • In a randomized control trial with 12 fourth-grade struggling writers, the ART and T3 groups differed significantly on story content, and T3 differed significantly on story quality [+W]
  • T3's comparatively small story-content effect is attributed to its lack of specific story-content cue questions, while verbalizing aloud during pre-writing helped story quality [+W]
