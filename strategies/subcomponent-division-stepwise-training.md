# Divide a complex procedure into measurable subcomponents to support stepwise training and objective skill assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends decomposing a novel surgical technique into measurable subcomponents, each with defined start and end milestones, so that learning of individual skills (e.g., purse-string suturing) can be tracked separately from overall procedural fluency. As the authors state, "dividing the procedure into measurable subcomponents may support stepwise training and objective assessment". The video-annotated timepoint framework and RA-CUSUM analysis operationalize this approach for the RiSSA technique.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Consistently annotated intraoperative milestones with reliable video identification of each step
- A risk-adjustment model to account for case complexity when computing expected performance
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from a single surgeon at a tertiary referral center and require validation in larger, multi-surgeon cohorts before broader adoptability conclusions

### Target Learners
- surgeons in early adoption phases of a technically demanding procedure

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of intracorporeal suturing and anastomotic procedural fluency
