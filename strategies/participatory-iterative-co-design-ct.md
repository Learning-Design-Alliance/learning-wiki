# Engage teachers, families, and designers in participatory, iterative co-design of CT learning activities rather than adopting packaged solutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report's fourth commitment grounds CT work in research-practice partnerships with co-design teams of learning scientists, district leaders, teachers, families, children, curriculum designers, and media developers. Stakeholders share perspectives, brainstorm which CT skills could be consequential in their context, and collaboratively develop and test CT-integrated activities and digital apps. The approach aims to capitalize on children's and families' funds of knowledge and identify activities that bridge home and school learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time allocated to promote flexibility and build rapport, cultivating openness and mutual respect so all stakeholders feel comfortable and confident in the design process
#### Constraints
- As with most collaborative processes, co-design requires deliberate allocation of time that a packaged solution would not need

### Target Learners
- Preschool children (ages 3-5) and their families; K-12 students and teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- CT-integrated learning activities and digital games that resonate with families and children across home and school contexts
