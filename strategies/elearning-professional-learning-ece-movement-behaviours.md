# Use e-Learning professional learning to build ECEs' competence, confidence, and motivation for physical activity promotion, pending larger-scale effectiveness testing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends e-Learning as a delivery mode for ECE professional learning in physical activity and sedentary behaviour, concluding that "Utilizing e-Learning to train both pre- and in-service ECEs in physical activity and sedentary behaviour may be an effective strategy to ensure they are competent, confident, and motivated". It notes the virtual nature of the course increases potential for population-level reach, with only simple modifications needed to tailor it for other settings. The recommendation is explicitly conditional on further testing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Broader dissemination should wait until the course's effectiveness is tested on a larger scale with a comparison group, as the article states
#### Constraints
- The pilot had no control group, a small pre-service sample, ~40% in-service retention, and a non-validated knowledge questionnaire, so findings should be interpreted with caution

### Target Learners
- pre-service early childhood educators
- in-service early childhood educators

### Target Learning Goals
- promoting physical activity and minimizing sedentary behaviour in childcare settings

## Claims

- [In one uncontrolled pre-post study, ECEs' correct screen-time guideline selection for the Canadian 24-Hour Movement Guidelines for the Early Years rose from 11.6% before to 61.9% after the e-Learning course](../claims/teach-elearning-improves-guideline-knowledge.md) [+M]
- [In-service ECEs' self-rated behavioural intention and perceived behavioural control scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test across all seven measured childcare movement behaviours after the e-Learning course, in one uncontrolled pre-post study](../claims/teach-elearning-in-service-intention-control-all-behaviours.md) [+M]
- [Both pre-service and in-service early childhood educators significantly increased their total physical activity and sedentary behaviour knowledge scores after completing the TEACH e-Learning course](../claims/teach-elearning-increases-ece-knowledge.md) [+M]
- [Pre-service ECEs' behavioural intention and perceived behavioural control increased significantly only for select behaviours, with no significant differences for the remaining behaviours](../claims/teach-elearning-pre-service-selective-intention-gains.md) [~M]
- [In one pre-post study with no comparison group, in-service ECEs' self-rated task and barrier self-efficacy rose significantly from pre- to post-course; pre-service ECEs' self-rated barrier self-efficacy changed significantly, while task self-efficacy did not significantly change](../claims/teach-elearning-self-efficacy-group-differences.md) [~M]
