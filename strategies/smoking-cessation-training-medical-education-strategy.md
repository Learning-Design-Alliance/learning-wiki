# Train physicians in smoking cessation during medical school and provide continuing medical education to practicing primary care practitioners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends eliminating the training barrier by introducing smoking cessation techniques early in physicians' medical education and by offering continued medical education to primary care practitioners in private practice. It states that training "has been positively associated with better practice and engagement in smoking cessation" in studies the article cites from Egypt and Switzerland. The strategy targets the knowledge and practice gaps the survey documented for waterpipe and cigarette cessation counseling.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Physicians in medical school or continuing education settings must receive smoking cessation training covering waterpipe as well as cigarette cessation techniques
#### Constraints
- The article notes the small sample and conference-recruited participants may limit generalizability to primary care physicians in Lebanon

### Target Learners
- primary care practitioners
- family medicine physicians
- medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- smoking cessation counseling knowledge and skills
- knowledge of waterpipe tobacco cessation techniques
