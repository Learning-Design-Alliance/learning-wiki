# Deliver short, low-cost, in-service theoretical-practical CPR training with small groups of at most six participants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's intervention consisted of two short theoretical-practical CPR sessions of about one hour and thirty minutes, taught in-service by an AHA BLS-certified instructor, combining lectures and multimedia with immediate practical demonstration and hands-on practice of all maneuvers. The authors state that "a short-term training program, with low-technology materials, carried out in-service and with more spaced intervals between training, such as the one applied in this study, can also facilitate its replication in other hospital institutions", and that low-fidelity simulation is especially important for institutions with more limited resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An AHA BLS course-certified instructor and a low-fidelity manikin with bag-valve-mask device and training AED, as used in the study's practical sessions
#### Constraints
- The training time was shorter than the standard AHA BLS course as only content related to adult CPA was taught

### Target Learners
- Nurses and nursing technicians in hospital clinical care and intensive care units

### Target Learning Goals
- Recognition of cardiac arrest and performance of high-quality basic life support CPR maneuvers
