# Use CUSUM plots to identify steps needing targeted tuition and to provide visualised evidence of progression in surgical curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating CUSUM into the trainee's curriculum, with plotted graphs providing readily visualised, accurate, comparable evidence of progression and competence in place of the implicit logbook approach. Because CUSUM "was able to highlight areas for improvement, guiding further training in addition to defining competence", it can direct targeted tuition to the specific procedure steps a trainee finds hardest. The authors also propose developing software to facilitate the data entry and analysis, since the calculations are detailed and time intensive.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prospective recording of each procedure's binary success/failure outcome for each defined step
- Supervision during procedures and pre-defined step-specific acceptable/unacceptable failure rates
#### Constraints
- Demonstrated in a single trainee; validation in a larger cohort is required before routine use
- Patient experience and complication rates were not incorporated and would need parallel audit

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees in urology learning flexible cystoscopy

### Target Learning Goals
- achieving and maintaining procedural competence; identifying individual steps needing targeted practice
