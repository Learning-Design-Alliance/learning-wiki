Work out SMS delivery schedules with students and plan ahead for delivery conflicts and missed messages¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's pedagogical implications advise that "delivery schedules should be worked out together with students" because unexpected messaging can be disturbing and discouraging rather than motivating. Teachers should plan ahead to avoid conflicts between their schedules and delivery times, consider technologies supporting timed delivery, inform students that messages may go missing, and prepare countermeasures in advance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A proper delivery schedule agreed with students and closely followed

Constraints¶

  • No message delivery technology is perfect or always reliable; intended messages may end up missing

Target Learners¶

  • University foreign-language learners using mobile phones for vocabulary study

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Sustained engagement with daily vocabulary learning tasks

Claims¶

  • Learners' written reports identify fragmented-time use, convenience, and reminder effects as advantages of SMS vocabulary learning [+W]
  • Learners' reports identify technical and pedagogical disadvantages of SMS vocabulary learning, including storage limits, segmented messages, and superficial processing [+W]
  • Learners showed positive attitudes toward receiving SMS vocabulary messages, and instant messaging raised intercultural awareness [+W]
