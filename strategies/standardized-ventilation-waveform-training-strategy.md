# Deliver standardized mechanical ventilation training covering modes, synchrony and waveform analysis to improve asynchrony recognition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structured training in mechanical ventilation as the actionable route to better asynchrony recognition, since prior large-scale training (>100 hours) is "not standardized and applicable in every hospital". In this study, trained professionals were defined as those completing at least three theoretical class units covering modes and settings of mechanical ventilators, patient-ventilator synchrony, and waveform analysis, with at least six cumulative hours, and training was associated with higher recognition accuracy and roughly 4-fold increased odds of identifying at least half of the asynchronies. The authors also point to automated assistance and simulation tools for teaching waveform recognition skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- At least three theoretical class units (cumulative time of at least six hours) covering ventilator modes and settings, patient-ventilator synchrony, and waveform analysis
#### Constraints
- The study is observational; before-and-after intervention comparisons of training were not carried out and are listed by the authors as needed future work

### Target Learners
- ICU physicians
- respiratory therapists
- residents in intensive care

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying ineffective triggers, double triggers, auto-triggers and reverse triggers from ventilator waveforms
