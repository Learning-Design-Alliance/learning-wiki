# Structured case-based or simulation-based exercises that vary clinical modifiers and require explicit justification of choices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A curricular recommendation the authors derive from the rising share of Type IIIb items, which demand context-sensitive management decisions after a diagnosis is reached. The article recommends that "curricula should incorporate structured case-based or simulation-based exercises that vary clinical modifiers (eg, renal function, allergies, and social context), requiring explicit justification of choices." It also suggests visual or video-based materials may reinforce the reasoning processes Type IIIb captures, and that students be guided away from one disease-one answer memorization toward comparative reasoning among multiple plausible options.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cases must embed patient-specific contextual modifiers and require learners to justify management choices among plausible options
#### Constraints
- Grounded in trends observed in the Japanese NMLE; the article does not report empirical testing of this strategy itself

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students preparing for licensing examinations and clinical practice

### Target Learning Goals
- context-sensitive clinical decision making and management reasoning beyond diagnosis
