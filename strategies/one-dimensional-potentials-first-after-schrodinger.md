# After introducing the Schrödinger equation, begin applications with the one-dimensional potential barrier and well before the hydrogen atom to support student motivation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The author recommends that once the Schrödinger equation is introduced, applications follow starting with simple one-dimensional potentials. He writes that his favorite cases are the simple potential barrier and potential well, which have applications "from scann ing tunneling microscope to the nuclear reactions in stars," and that such applications have strong impact on student motivation to attend the course. The hydrogen atom, as in Schrödinger's original paper, is named the best example but is deferred because "simpler is better."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The Schrödinger equation must already have been introduced
#### Constraints
- The ordering reflects the author's teaching preference; no comparative effectiveness data are reported

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in introductory quantum mechanics

### Target Learning Goals
- apply the Schrödinger equation to physical potentials and connect quantum mechanics to real applications
