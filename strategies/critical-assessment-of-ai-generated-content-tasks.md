# Design literature-class tasks in which students critically assess AI-generated content and propose improvements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, as AI enters literature education, assessment should shift toward creativity, critical thinking and evaluative judgement, and that literature classes use tasks "where students critically assess AI-generated content, identify potential biases, and propose improvements." The authors argue this strengthens critical and evaluative skills and encourages deeper engagement with literary analysis. This study's rubric-based group analysis of alternative endings is an instance of such evaluative work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instructional design that assigns students authentic AI-generated texts to evaluate against criteria such as fidelity, context and character integrity
#### Constraints
- The article flags that educators should ensure careful integration to avoid overreliance on AI outputs and preserve students' personal creativity and critical thinking

### Target Learners
- undergraduate English literature students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical and evaluative judgement of texts
- literary analysis skills
