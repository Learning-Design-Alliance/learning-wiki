# Use the hinotori™ system for structured skill acquisition in pediatric surgical training for novice robotic users

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating the hinotori™ platform into pediatric surgical training because its intuitive operability and steep learning curve allow surgeons without formal robotic training to reach precise performance from the outset. It states the system "can be readily adopted by novice robotic users and is particularly suitable for structured skill acquisition in surgical training", and that its ergonomic design, intuitive controls, and reduced internal collisions and operator fatigue make it suitable for pediatric minimally invasive procedures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Continued training is likely to further improve both efficiency and accuracy, per the learning-curve findings
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a single-center pilot trial with limited statistical power; the article states approximately 10–20 participants may be required for adequately powered confirmatory trials

### Target Learners
- Pediatric surgeons with minimal robotic exposure
- Novice robotic users in surgical training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition of robotic suturing precision and basic laparoscopic-style skills in confined pediatric operative spaces
