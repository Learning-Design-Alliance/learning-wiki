# Run a parallel full-semester business course and reintroduce industry-submitted topics and coaches in a hybrid model

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on challenges in prior iterations, the authors plan structural changes: "future iterations of the course will include a parallel business-focused course on the quantum industry" running the full semester so business students gain quantum knowledge before merging with technical teams, addressing the unattractiveness of mid-semester enrollment. They also plan to "reintroduce industry engagement by allowing companies to submit both project topics and coaches" on top of a stable foundation of academic topics, plus expanded networking events, a hybrid mentorship framework from industry experts and alumni, and quantum startup simulation labs with challenge-based sprints.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A stable foundation of academic topics to ensure continuity while industry partners participate
- A selective application process through which business students merge with technical teams
#### Constraints
- The earlier fully industry-sourced topic model caused topic shortages and, in extreme cases, course cancellations due to inconsistent external participation

### Target Learners
- Master's-level business and technical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Quantum industry literacy for business students
- Entrepreneurial mindset development
