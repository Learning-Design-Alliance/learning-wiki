# Use POGIL and higher-order thinking questions to build preservice teachers' critical thinking and problem solving skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that lecturers improve students' higher-order thinking skills by using POGIL, and that students be propelled to deliver and protest opinion, identify and clarify the problem, and respond and present various solutions. It also suggests that "higher order thinking questions need to be trained to generate the scientific concept through inquiry-oriented course", and recommends integrating digital technology into inquiry courses to foster 21st century skills through e-learning in higher education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lecturers who understand how to apply constructive teaching methods in accordance with students' characteristics
- Inquiry-oriented course activities with higher-order thinking questions
#### Constraints
- The article cautions adopters to consider the characteristics of the POGIL method, since the type of problems, lecturer's role, learning environment, teaching materials, and students' active participation in teamwork affect learning outcomes

### Target Learners
- preservice elementary teachers
- higher education students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical thinking skills
- problem solving skills
- 21st century skills
