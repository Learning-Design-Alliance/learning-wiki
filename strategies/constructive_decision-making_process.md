# Constructive Decision-Making Process

## Description
Brainstorming and displaying a constructive decision-making process as a visual reminder in the classroom helps students make good choices. The process involves stages such as staying calm, analyzing the situation, considering choices and consequences, making a choice, and evaluating the outcome.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials for creating a visual display (poster, whiteboard, etc.), class time for brainstorming the process, and opportunities for students to refer to the visual reminder during decision-making.
#### Constraints
- Students may not always refer to the visual reminder when making decisions. The process may not be applicable to all types of decisions. Some learners may find the structure too rigid.

### Affordances
- Provides a structured process for decision-making, enhances problem-solving skills, promotes ethical and moral reasoning, encourages self-reflection, and improves responsible behavior.

### Personalization
- The visual reminder can be customized with different prompts, visuals, or wording. Steps can be adapted to fit various age groups or specific situations. Learners can be given options for how to display the process.

## Examples
- Display a poster with the steps: 1) Stay calm, observe, and identify the problem. 2) Stop, think, and analyze the situation. 3) Capture your thoughts as you consider the choices and their consequences. 4) Make a choice to solve the problem. 5) Evaluate and reflect on the outcome.
