# Provide targeted SMT training strategies for students whose physical characteristics make high-force targets harder, alongside training for realistic self-assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends tailoring chiropractic SMT training to individual student factors rather than uniform practice. It states that "providing female and short students with additional strategies to achieve higher forces may support the development of their competence as well as their confidence", and that training programs must address both competence and realistic self-assessment given observed overconfidence among some students. It also suggests integrating biomechanical and psychosocial components.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must account for individual student factors such as sex and height when preparing students for higher-force targets
#### Constraints
- The article notes it remains unknown how SMT force–time characteristics affect clinical practice, and that the cross-sectional design limits extrapolation beyond the testing scenario

### Target Learners
- Chiropractic students in years 1–3 of professional education

### Target Learning Goals
- Modulating spinal manipulation force–time characteristics (preload, peak thrust force, time to peak force)
