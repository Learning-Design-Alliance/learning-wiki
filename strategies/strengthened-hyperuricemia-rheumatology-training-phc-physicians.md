# Strengthen theoretical and clinical training in hyperuricemia and rheumatology for primary health care physicians to reduce misprescribing risk

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation is for strengthened formal training in hyperuricemia and rheumatology for PHC physicians. It reasons that the observed knowledge gaps and high ULT misuse "suggest important gaps in formal training" and that such training would "reduce the risk of malpractice, associated morbidity, and resulting costs". This is the authors' forward-looking recommendation derived from their survey findings, not a tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Targets the knowledge areas the survey found most deficient, including factors and mechanisms underlying hyperuricemia and guideline-based treatment prescribing patterns
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is based on a cross-sectional self-report survey with selection bias toward females, young doctors, and family physicians, as the article states

### Target Learners
- primary health care physicians (family medicine specialists, general practitioners, and other specialists in primary care)

### Target Learning Goals
- guideline-based knowledge of asymptomatic hyperuricemia management, including pathophysiology, diagnosis, and appropriate prescribing
