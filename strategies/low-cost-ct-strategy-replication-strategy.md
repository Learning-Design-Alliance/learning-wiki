# Replicate the CT-based astrophysics strategy in diverse contexts using accessible, low-cost tools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extending the strategy to other natural-science topics and STEM areas, to earlier educational levels, and to contexts with limited laboratory access. They argue that "El uso de herramientas accesibles, como simulaciones computacionales, dispositivos móviles y materiales de bajo costo, refuerza la viabilidad de este enfoque en diversos escenarios educativos", promoting more inclusive and equitable science teaching. They also call for replicating the strategy in distinct educational contexts to validate its applicability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Use of accessible low-cost materials, such as computational simulations, mobile devices and homemade apparatus like the CD spectrophotometer and homemade radio detector.
#### Constraints
- The authors state that application to one specific student group limits generalization until the strategy is replicated in other educational contexts, and that external factors such as prior interest and familiarity with computational tools were not fully controlled.

### Target Learners
- Secondary students across diverse educational contexts, including rural institutions with limited laboratory access

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding of abstract physics and astrophysics concepts through computational tools
- Development of analytical and computational thinking skills
