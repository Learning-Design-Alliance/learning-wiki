# Define the target inference population first, then recruit across institution types using advisory boards, advertisements, snowball sampling, and information sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
To avoid the generalizability threats of convenience sampling, the article recommends researchers "first decide the population they aim to make inferences about" and design recruitment around it. Concrete tactics include grant advisory board members with relevant networks, postings on national societies and listservs, snowball sampling, virtual information sessions, personalized outreach to department heads at underrepresented institutions, and tracking contacts and declines in a spreadsheet.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Begin recruitment at least six months before data collection begins; community colleges may need up to two years to build rapport and navigate IRB collaboration
#### Constraints
- Instructor participants are likely to be DBER researchers themselves, which may bias findings

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students and instructors across institution types, including minority-serving institutions and community colleges

### Target Learning Goals
- Generalizable multi-institutional research findings
