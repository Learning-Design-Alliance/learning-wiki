# Plan service capacity, biopsy demand, patient navigation, and outreach to older women when implementing early cancer detection training programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their trial experience, the authors recommend that implementers of community or provider education campaigns "should prepare to meet an increased demand both initially and longer term", that health systems "must plan for increased biopsy demand" and determine imaging, biopsy, and pathology workflows, and that dedicated staff such as patient navigators support patients through the medical system. They also recommend algorithms for managing benign breast conditions and efforts to target older women.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Plan for increased patient demand at health centers and hospitals both initially and longer term, including staffing adjustments
- Determine the role of diagnostic imaging, biopsy algorithms, where biopsies occur, pathology review, and results sharing
- Dedicated staff such as patient navigators to monitor and address loss to follow-up
- Algorithms to guide management of women with suspected or confirmed benign breast conditions
#### Constraints
- Diagnosing earlier-stage breast cancer will not reduce mortality unless access to timely and effective treatment is ensured
- A screening-focused program would be expected to produce an even higher incidence of benign disease

### Target Learners
- health system planners and program implementers in low-resource settings

### Target Learning Goals
- anticipate and manage service demand generated by early detection training programs
