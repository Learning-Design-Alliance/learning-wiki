# Difficulty-stratified role play: scripted RP for junior students, unscripted RP for advanced stages

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends matching the source of role-play scripts to learners' stage: "the primary stage should focus on history taking and case writing skills training, when a scripted RP model can be used", while advanced stages should develop clinical thinking with "an unscripted RP model". This follows from their finding that script writing by juniors lacking disease knowledge raised difficulty and lowered engagement. The strategy operationalizes difficulty stratification in clinical-skills teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students at the primary stage lack the specialized theoretical knowledge needed to write good case scripts, so pre-scripted clinical narratives must be provided
#### Constraints
- The article ties unscripted RP to advanced stages with more clinical thinking; its evidence covers junior students only

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate medical students in diagnostic laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- history-taking skills
- case-writing skills
- clinical thinking in complex scenarios
