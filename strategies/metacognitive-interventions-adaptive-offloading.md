# Use metacognitive interventions to promote adaptive use of cognitive tools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's main practical conclusion is that because 'metacognitive processes play a key role in triggering' offloading, 'metacognitive interventions could play an important role in promoting the adaptive use of cognitive tools'. More accurate understanding of one's memory ability allows more effective targeting of external resources. The authors speculate such interventions could have greater impact on everyday functioning than brain training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must shift metacognitive confidence or beliefs about memory ability, as in the practice-difficulty and feedback manipulations of Gilbert et al. (2020, Experiment 3)
#### Constraints
- The review notes the effects of metacognitive interventions on cognitive offloading have been inconsistent across studies, and the cross-domain organisation of metacognition and offloading is still poorly understood

- adults
- older adults

### Target Learning Goals
- remembering and fulfilling delayed intentions through adaptive use of external reminders
