# Design and study MOOCs with attention to learner subpopulation diversity: analyze retention and performance by demographic group, and use exam participation as a participation proxy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's methodological approach treats the diverse MOOC population as a research laboratory for Physics Education Research: it segments enrollees by self-reported demographics, defines "Active" participants via click-stream thresholds (greater than 500 events before the first midterm), counts exam participants receiving credit higher than zero, and tracks retention by education level and gender through the exams. It also recommends designing courses for populations with education beyond a bachelor degree, as some MOOCs have done.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Self-reported demographics collected at enrollment and instructor dashboard performance metrics; "Active" participants defined as more than 500 events in the weeks before the first midterm
#### Constraints
- Exam participation as a proxy for participation "does leave out small populations using MOOCs in non-traditional ways"; retention rates relative to Active students are "subject to bias in our selection of 'Active' students"; Junior High and PhD populations "are perhaps too small for statistical inference"

### Target Learners
- MOOC course teams and researchers studying large online learner populations

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding participation, retention, and performance across diverse MOOC learner subpopulations
