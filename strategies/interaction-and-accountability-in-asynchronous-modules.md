# Build interaction and accountability into asynchronous modules for novice learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that asynchronous learning for novices should include mandatory interaction and knowledge checks: "frequent knowledge checks and student accountability would create a positive learning condition", and built-in interaction with peers and faculty may enhance utility. They cite literature emphasizing interaction among peers and instructors as key to asynchronous course success.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curriculum developers must identify why students may not have positive attitudes towards this modality and create solutions to address these barriers
#### Constraints
- The article presents these as hypotheses and recommendations, not as tested manipulations in this study

### Target Learners
- novice medical learners in emergency medicine and acute care

### Target Learning Goals
