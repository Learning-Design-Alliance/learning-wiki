# Adopt the combined CBL/FC approach in nephrology education as an effective alternative to lecture-based teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that teachers "consider using the CBL/FC approach in nephrology education since it is very effective in cultivating students' abilities without extra pressure", concluding it "could be a better option than the traditional LBT approach in nephrology education". The strategy delivers the same topic through pre-class case materials, small-group case presentation, and interactive in-class discussion, and the article reports it improved case-scenario performance and satisfaction without increasing learning burden.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers prepare the same topic materials for both formats and are trained before teaching
- Students are divided into small groups of 4 or 5 and prepare case presentations in advance
#### Constraints
- The study did not include CBL-only or FC-only groups due to limited participants, which limits the conclusion about which approach yields the best teaching efficacy
- New assessment systems are still needed to evaluate the effectiveness of the CBL/FC approach in detail and accurately

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in clinical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- applying theoretical knowledge to clinical practice
- clinical thinking and problem-solving
- patient care skills
