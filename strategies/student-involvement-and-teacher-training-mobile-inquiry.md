# Involve students in the design of inquiry-based mobile learning activities and provide teachers with in-service training and constant support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's key recommendations for implementing inquiry-based mobile learning environments. Students "could be more involved already in the design process when these types of inquiry learning activities are planned", so assignments focus more on students' own interests and needs and negative experiences are addressed during the process, also supporting self-regulation development. Teachers "should be offered the possibility to attend in-service training programs that help them to update their pedagogies, and also constant support should be available", since attempting to use ordinary pedagogy in a mobile environment is likely to hinder engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pedagogy used during lessons should be integrated with the educational technology so that they complement each other
#### Constraints
- The article notes un-assisted discovery learning is not an optimal solution; scaffolds for self-regulation and students' own reflection are needed

### Target Learners
- Lower secondary school students and their teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained interest development in STEM; self-regulated learning; effective technology-integrated pedagogy
