Evaluate motivational theories rigorously on predictive, practical, explanatory, and developmental value before integrating¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (2 theoretical, 1 associational), q1–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The essay recommends that researchers rigorously evaluate the theories they use rather than inheriting them from supervisors or social contagion. It proposes four evaluation criteria: predictive value (how much theories predict real-world outcomes), practical value (how much they inform end-users and what factors teachers, school leaders, or students could change), explanatory value (conceptual breadth), and developmental trajectory, noting most current theories lack foundations for developmental hypotheses.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Researchers must be willing to apply the 'logic of subtraction' to their own research programs, removing constructs with small effect sizes or redundant constructs

Constraints¶

  • The essay notes most current theories do not provide a foundation for developmental hypotheses, so models must be updated or new ones created

Target Learners¶

  • motivation researchers
  • doctoral students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • selecting and refining theories with demonstrated predictive and practical value

Claims¶

  • Predictive modeling across four theories found critical teacher behaviors for engagement that did not fit within one theoretical model [+W]
  • Fragmentation is caused by researchers themselves via misaligned incentives that favor staying in theoretical camps [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Shift journal editorial and review practices to reward theoretically integrative work
  • Balance explanation-focused and prediction-focused methodological strategies in motivation research
  • Evaluate quantisation choices empirically on the target task and hardware rather than inheriting them from other studies
