Redesign assessment so AI-generated text cannot substitute for student work: reflective, contextualised and project-based assignments¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends accommodating generative AI rather than trying to detect it. Teachers could "set reflective and contextualised written assignments that could not be generated by AI", for example an independent research project followed by a report, feedback, and a critical reflection on that feedback. Assignments where AI is not allowed could be assessed for style, expression, voice and personal reflection.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers willing to move away from standard essay coursework
  • feedback cycles tied to each student's specific project

Constraints¶

  • Invigilated exams are an alternative but the article calls them formal and time consuming

Target Learners¶

  • school, college and university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic writing
  • critical reflection
  • argumentation

Claims¶

  • Neither humans nor machines reliably detect machine-generated text; humans detect it at chance level [+W]
  • GPT-3 fabricates plausible-looking but fake references and apparently invents the research studies they cite [+W]
  • GPT-3 can generate a complete short academic paper, including abstract, introduction, discussion and APA references, in under five minutes [+W]
  • GPT-3 can generate a plausible student essay from a short prompt, including a heading and citation, on its first attempt [+W]
  • Plagiarism detection software does not detect AI-generated essays because the text is original, not copied [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Embed AI as a learning partner and redesign assessments for AI-rich contexts
  • Address generative AI in higher education through assessment redesign and proportional reflective practices rather than surveillance or punitive controls
  • Redesign assessment toward progressive, authentic, and reflective methods rather than intensive paperwork
  • Redesign assessments to require critical thinking, originality, interactive activities, and real-time or proctored formats
  • Redesign assessment reflectively for possible AI use, using supervised tasks for lower-order skills and intentional AI use with reflection for higher-order work
  • Institutional recommendations: shift from detection to process-oriented assessment, transparent AI-use protocols, and revised integrity policies
  • Adapt non-invigilated written assessments and their marking criteria in response to Gen-AI susceptibility
