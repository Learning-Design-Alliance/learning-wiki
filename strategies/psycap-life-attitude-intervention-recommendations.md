# School-, teacher-, family-, and policy-level practices to cultivate psychological capital and positive life attitude in Chinese middle schools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's actionable recommendations for the high-pressure Chinese middle school context: schools could "embed psychological capital training programs—targeting self-efficacy, resilience, hope, and optimism—into moral education classes, class meetings, or school-based mental health courses"; homeroom and subject teachers can use positive feedback, role modeling, and differentiated instruction; families can join regular parent workshops to recognize internalizing distress; and policy makers could incorporate psychological capital and life attitude into curricula, well-being monitoring, and evaluation frameworks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher training in the early identification and intervention of internalizing symptoms is recommended to support the teacher-level practices
#### Constraints
- Recommendations are tailored to the Chinese context of high academic pressure and competitive tracking, with particular attention to Grade 9 students preparing for the high-stakes secondary school entrance examination

### Target Learners
- middle school students, particularly Grade 9 students preparing for entrance examinations

### Target Learning Goals
- academic self-confidence
- positive life attitude
- reduction of internalizing symptoms
- academic performance
