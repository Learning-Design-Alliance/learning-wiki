# Pair trainees for real-time competency assessment and feedback during skills practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Trainees work in pairs, taking turns operating and observing; the observing partner records the time of each attempt and rates performance against the checklists. The protocol states that pairing "allows the trainees to give each other real-time competency assessment and feedback, which is motivational", while also saving trainer time and training-center space. The authors report their own unpublished protocol data found no outcome distinction between training alone and in pairs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A partner available to observe and time each attempt, and trained peer tutors available on demand for expert assessment
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge partner communication dynamics and partner experience may be an influencing factor, explored via subgroup analysis

### Target Learners
- medical students in surgical skills training

### Target Learning Goals
- laparoscopic suturing proficiency with self- and peer-assessment
