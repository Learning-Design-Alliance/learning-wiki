Six state policy recommendations to stabilize, support, and expand the early childhood workforce and build diverse TK pathways¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends six steps for state policymakers: (1) clearly map out and communicate career pathways into TK and other ECE programs; (2) develop high-quality pathways tailored to experienced early educators, where "the state should therefore consider ways to evaluate and give credit for knowledge and skills candidates already have as they work toward a credential"; (3) provide grants to institutions of higher education for new preschool-to-3rd-grade credentialing programs; (4) set appropriate requirements for assistant TK teachers, such as 12 units of ECE coursework or the equivalent; (5) make new investments in the broader early educator workforce beyond TK, coupled with competitive wages; and (6) collect new data to monitor ECE workforce needs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • State agencies must clarify requirements and financial supports; counties must coordinate their own workforce development pathways
  • Institutions of higher education need up-front investments in staff, curriculum, and candidate recruitment

Constraints¶

  • The 10:1 ratio requirement is contingent on funding availability
  • Policymaking should consider the whole ECE workforce, since early educators leaving for TK may affect staffing and quality in the programs they leave

Target Learners¶

  • experienced early childhood educators
  • assistant TK teachers
  • teacher candidates in high-need schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • TK and ECE teacher preparation
  • credential completion
  • workforce retention and diversity

Related Strategies¶

  • Six state policy steps to support early educator preparation, paired with adequate compensation and retention policies
  • Federal strategy to develop and support a well-qualified ECE workforce through compensation, career-ladder scholarships, preparation programs, and coaching
  • Build high-retention, supportive pathways into teaching for candidates of color
  • Use local resources to serve children birth through age 3 to stabilize community providers as 4-year-olds move into TK
