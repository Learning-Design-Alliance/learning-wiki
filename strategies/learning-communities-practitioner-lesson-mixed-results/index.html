For community college practitioners: treat one-semester learning communities as capable of long-term gains but expect only modest short-term credit effects for developmental education students¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Drawing on the six trials, the article offers lessons for community college practitioners considering implementing learning communities. The first lesson is that "One-semester learning communities can have a long-term impact and even boost graduation, as shown in the study of the Kingsborough program." The second tempers expectations: "the combined results all six trials suggest that on average learning communities for developmental education students produce only a modest impact on credits earned in the targeted subject of English or mathematics." Practitioners should therefore weigh short-term credit effects against possible long-term completion benefits.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Programs include the four components: linked courses and cohorts, faculty collaboration, integrated instructional practices, and student supports

Constraints¶

  • The studies cannot pinpoint which Kingsborough features mattered most for its larger impacts

Target Learners¶

  • community college students, particularly those in developmental education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • credits earned in English or mathematics
  • persistence
  • degree completion

Claims¶

  • A one-semester learning community program at Kingsborough Community College increased six-year degree completion by 4.6 percentage points [+W]
  • Across six colleges, learning communities for developmental education students produced on average only a half-credit short-term impact on targeted-subject credits and no impact on persistence [+W]
  • Short-term impacts on targeted-subject credits varied by college, with Kingsborough's estimated impacts the largest [+W]
  • Linked learning communities produced no statistically significant or substantively important effects on academic achievement [~W]
  • Linked learning communities produced no statistically significant or substantively important effect on degree attainment [~W]
