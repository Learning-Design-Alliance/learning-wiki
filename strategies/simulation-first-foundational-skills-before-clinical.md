# Simulation-first framework: acquire foundational procedural skills on simulation models before clinical practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose a "simulation-first" educational framework "wherein trainees acquire foundational operational skills using pork belly models prior to advancing to the clinical TAP block". This progressive approach builds self-efficacy through repetitive successful practice in a low-risk environment before trainees face the psychological pressure of performing on patients. The authors state this "may approach enhances both educational outcomes and patient safety."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to low-cost, high-fidelity simulation models such as the pork belly model
- Structured didactic training preceding simulation practice
- Feedback during simulation training from experienced staff
#### Constraints
- The study does not isolate the effect of the model itself from increased practice volume
- Findings require confirmation in larger, multi-center studies
- Short-term follow-up only; long-term skill retention not assessed

### Target Learners
- anesthesiology residents
- novice procedural trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational operational skills for ultrasound-guided procedures
- self-efficacy in procedural performance
- patient safety during skill acquisition
