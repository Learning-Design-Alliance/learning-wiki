# Measure online learners' attentional states by integrating multimodal sensing with retrospective recall and thought probes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 10 claims (10 for) · 6 studies (2 design, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 6 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

## Description
To know when online learners have attentional problems, the article integrates eye tracker, webcam, egocentric glasses, screen recording, and mouse and keyboard events, flags off-screen segments, and asks learners to label their quadrant in a retrospective recall interview while randomly presented mind-wandering prompts capture covert state. This operationalizes the 2x2 attentional-cognition matrix for CAI and links attention time to learning outcomes such as SCORE/TIME efficiency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Synchronized recording software across all data sources
- Annotation software flagging off-screen instances and generating video clips of nine still frames
- A retrospective recall interview (about 15-20 minutes) after the post-test
#### Constraints
- Validated only with high-prior-knowledge graduate learners showing a ceiling effect, in a non-naturalistic research-lab setting

### Target Learners
- online STEM learners in computer-assisted instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- monitoring and understanding learners' moment-to-moment attentional states during online learning

### Affordances
- Twobytwo Attentional Cognition Matrix Cai
