Extended professional development pairing CTE teachers with science teacher partners in a community of practice¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 8 claims (5 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The experimental treatment mirrored the Math-in-CTE model of extended professional development: experimental group CTE teachers received six days of extended PD, were paired with science teacher partners, and engaged with them in a community of practice to map curricula, develop science-enhanced lessons, and critique and refine lessons. Teachers received ongoing support from their science teacher partners at their schools and participated in two days of additional PD at a midpoint in the treatment period. The article recommends future studies apply the model to other CTE areas and increase the amount of inquiry in the pedagogical model.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Science teacher partners at the same schools as the CTE teachers
  • Extended PD sessions during which teacher teams create, present, and refine science-enhanced lessons with full-group feedback

Constraints¶

  • Teachers who participated volunteered and were randomly assigned, so an element of self-selection existed; teachers also received monetary compensation, raising questions about whether results would differ for instructors representing a wider array of teaching abilities, motivation, and CTE contexts

Target Learners¶

  • secondary agricultural education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • science knowledge and skills embedded in CTE curricula

Claims¶

  • 12 of the 13 experimental agricultural education teachers still teaching explicit science (92%) used Science-in-CTE materials one year after the pilot study [+W]
  • Of the control agricultural education teachers still teaching explicit science a year later, only 2 of 7 (29%) used the Science-in-CTE method and lessons [+W]
  • 80% of partnered science teachers voluntarily incorporated Science-in-CTE methods or examples into their science classes [+W]
  • Sustained usage rates in Science-in-CTE were consistent with those reported in the Math-in-CTE follow-up study [+W]
  • Science-enhanced CTE instruction produced a statistically significant positive impact on posttest science achievement for students in the second, third, and fourth quartiles on pretest science achievement [+W]
  • The magnitude of the Science-in-CTE intervention effect increased with each quartile increase in pretest science achievement, from d = .15 in the second quartile to d = .45 in the fourth [~W]
  • Treatment group membership had a statistically significant effect on the pretest quartile slope, indicating pretest achievement moderated the treatment effect [~W]
  • Relative to the control group, the Science-in-CTE intervention appeared to have no impact on students in the first (lowest) quartile on pretest science achievement [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Shift from one-day fragmented workshops to collaborative, extended, content-focused professional development
  • Pair Tier 2 intervention delivery with professional development workshops and in-class coaching visits
  • Brief initial training with extensive follow-up coaching and on-site facilitation for whole-school program implementation
  • Pair new instructional practices with sustained in-class coaching after initial professional development
  • Pair program adoption with two days of initial training and weekly coaching meetings
