Embed TPACK-based instructional applications and real classroom technology practice in teacher-education courses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that universities give prospective teachers opportunities to make technological applications in real classroom environments and to include contemporary TPACK-based instructional applications within relevant lessons. It states it is "recommended to suplly opportunities to the prospective teachers at the universities in which they can make the technological applications in a real classroom environment". The strategy was implemented by introducing TPACK-based applications, teaching courses with them, and requiring students to use them in the materials they produced over 14 weeks.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Courses must go beyond basic computer skills to cover pedagogical concepts, content and program knowledge together with teaching with technology

Constraints¶

  • The article notes obstacles to technology integration reported in prior work, such as limited internet access, excessive class size and teachers' insufficient knowledge of using technology

Target Learners¶

  • prospective science teachers at universities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • effective technology integration in teaching
  • self-efficacy in science education

Affordances¶

  • Tpack Framework Intersection

Claims¶

  • TPACK-based education applications raise prospective science teachers' self-efficacy belief levels toward science education more than the current curriculum after a 14-week course [+W]
  • The self-efficacy advantage of TPACK-based applications persists three months after the course ends [+W]
  • Within the experimental group, self-efficacy rose significantly from pretest to posttest and follow-up, with no significant drop from posttest to follow-up [+W]
  • The current-curriculum instruction in the control group produced no significant change in prospective teachers' self-efficacy across pretest, posttest and follow-up [+W]
  • Before the intervention, the experimental and control groups did not differ significantly in self-efficacy toward science education [+W]
