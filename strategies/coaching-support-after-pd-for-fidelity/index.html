Pair new instructional practices with sustained in-class coaching after initial professional development¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's implementation model pairs a one-day workshop with ongoing coach support once instruction begins. Coaches worked with teachers at least once per week, more frequently as needed, and coaching included modeling, co-teaching, monitoring student work, observation and feedback, and lesson-planning support. The authors caution that the robustness of the instruction across class levels may be possible only when at least the observed fidelity levels and this amount of support are received, making coaching a condition for reproducing the findings.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Coaches assigned to each teacher worked with teachers at least once per week during implementation of each of the three units.

Constraints¶

  • The article limits this to its own setting: results may be limited to the Southeast and Southwest regions, and robustness may depend on reaching the observed fidelity levels and support amounts.

Target Learners¶

  • middle school social studies teachers implementing new content-area instructional practices

Target Learning Goals¶

  • faithful implementation of multi-component instructional practices
  • maintaining a firewall between treatment and comparison conditions
