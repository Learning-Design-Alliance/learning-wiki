# Communicate that VR assessment complements rather than replaces existing examination formats, and retain manual recording during early implementation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
To counteract student reservations about technology replacing human assessors and standardized patients, the authors propose "clear communication that VR simulation is intended merely as an additional option to complement, not merely replace, existing examination formats." They also report retaining manual recording of performance data during early implementation, despite the software offering automated checklist-based evaluation, because of reservation and reliance on technology in the early stages.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness of student concerns about replacing human assessors and SPs with technology
#### Constraints
- Automated performance evaluation was available in the VR software but was deliberately not used in this implementation

### Target Learners
- medical students participating in OSCE assessments

### Target Learning Goals
- acceptance of VR-based clinical competence assessment
