Tiered ECE implementation: virtual and case-based learning first, then simulated patient interaction, then supervised real patient contact¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), q3–q4 · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend a sequenced implementation model: "It is recommended to actively utilise digital and AI technologies early on, building foundational knowledge and decision-making skills through virtual simulations and case-based learning; then enhancing procedural proficiency through structured simulated patient interactions; and finally progressing to supervised, incremental patient contact in real clinical settings." They state this tiered approach manages patient safety, alleviates student anxiety and addresses resource constraints, and suggest combining modalities to reduce costs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • ECE content should align with students' accumulation of theoretical knowledge
  • real clinical settings demand carefully structured supervision balancing educational objectives, student stress and patient safety

Constraints¶

  • Simulation-based approaches frequently require costly equipment or well-trained standardised volunteers; high costs, logistical complexity and uncertain short-term outcomes limit widespread adoption

Target Learners¶

  • preclinical dental students across academic years

Target Learning Goals¶

  • clinical skills, clinical reasoning, professional identity and self-efficacy development

Affordances¶

  • Ece Three Type Taxonomy

Claims¶

  • Early clinical exposure significantly improves dental students' overall competency compared with traditional teaching (SMD = 1.25), though evidence certainty is low [+M]
  • ECE improves theoretical knowledge, clinical practice performance and self-efficacy, with the largest effect on self-efficacy [+M]
  • ECE benefits both junior and senior preclinical dental students, with a larger pooled effect in junior students [+M]
  • The positive effect of ECE holds in both randomised and nonrandomised study designs [+M]
  • Live simulations with real patients showed the greatest effects among simulation types (g = 2.27, medical education only) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Position immersive technologies and virtual patients as supplementary components within blended learning rather than stand-alone replacements for established clinical communication training
  • Sequence interview training stepwise from AI-VPs to standardized patients to real patients
  • Implement AI simulations as complements within a stepped simulation continuum, with layered interaction modes and blended feedback
  • Sequence virtual simulation before authentic video across skill-development stages in teacher observation training
  • Use virtual patients to replace passive instruction and train clinical reasoning before bedside learning, without diminishing bedside teaching hours
  • Use fidelity-aware simulated patients as a low-risk complement to supervision for deliberate psychotherapy practice
  • Frame genAI virtual patient experiences: prompt experimentation with AI capabilities and orient students to its limits
  • Use structured training pathways with supervised early practice and experience-informed patient counselling in hip arthroscopy
  • Use chatbot standardised patients combined with CBL to address limitations of traditional SP approaches.
  • Use virtual simulations to enhance cognitive learning while prioritizing in-person laboratory sessions for psychomotor and technical skills
