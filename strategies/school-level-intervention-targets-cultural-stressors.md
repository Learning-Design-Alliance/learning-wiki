# Shift responsibility for coping with cultural stressors from youth and families to school-level intervention targets identified by participants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that interventions combatting cultural stressors target contexts (schools) rather than only equipping youth and parents with coping skills. “A focus on how contexts can be changed to reduce cultural stressors shifts the responsibility away from youth and families by pointing to contextual-level intervention targets.” Participants’ reimagined realities supply these targets: greater Latinx representation, caring and trained teachers, bilingual resources, anti-discrimination rules and allies, and supportive infrastructure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Incorporate youths’ and parents’ voices and hopes (aspirational capital) when designing contextual-level interventions.
#### Constraints
- Targets are drawn from participants in six high schools in one Central Texas district with high Latinx and economically disadvantaged enrollment.

### Target Learners
- Latinx youth and their families in secondary schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing cultural stressor experiences at school
- Promoting student and parent engagement
- Fostering student well-being and academic thriving
