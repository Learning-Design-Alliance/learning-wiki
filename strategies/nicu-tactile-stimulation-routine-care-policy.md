# NICUs adopt tactile stimulation as routine care policy and train pediatric nurses in its application

## Description
The article's own recommendations call for institutional and workforce changes: "Health care institutions such as Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU), should include in their policies neonatal stimulation especially tactile st imulation as a routine care interventions for stabl e premature infants." It further recommends educational programs to build nurses' skills, reflecting the stated nursing role of implementing tactile stimulation techniques within the comprehensive nursing care plan and providing customized stimulation based on each infant's needs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educational programs for health care professionals, especially pediatric nurses in NICU, to increase their skills in applying tactile stimulation to medically stable premature infants
#### Constraints
- Recommended only for medically stable premature infants; the article recommends a larger sample and longer follow-up before broader conclusions

### Target Learners
- Pediatric nurses and health care professionals in neonatal intensive care units

### Target Learning Goals
- Applying appropriate tactile stimulation techniques to premature infants as part of comprehensive nursing care
