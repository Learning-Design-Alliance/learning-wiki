# Teach graphical literacy skills explicitly, emphasising selection, construction and reasoning with common representation forms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article endorses explicit teaching of graphical literacy, citing McKendree et al. (2002), who found many curriculum problems that might be addressed by teachers providing an "emphasis on the skills of selection of, construction with, and reasoning about commonly occurring forms of representation." The idea of teaching graphical literacy explicitly was also proposed by Fry (1981) and Cox (1999) and is supported by Schwonke et al. (2009). A situated alternative, as in switchER2, encourages thought about representations in the context of current problems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must allocate instructional time to representation skills across the curriculum, as McKendree et al.'s curriculum study implies.
#### Constraints
- The situated switchER2-style approach suggests students would have to confront significant cognitive loads as well as breaks in the flow of their problem solving.

### Target Learners
- School students across the curriculum

### Target Learning Goals
- Graphical literacy: selecting, constructing with, and reasoning about commonly occurring forms of representation
