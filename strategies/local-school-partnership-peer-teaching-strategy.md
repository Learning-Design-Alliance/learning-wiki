# Partner with local schools to embed peer-teaching service-learning in undergraduate science courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors organize service-learning partnerships in which undergraduates teach age-appropriate, course-aligned curricula to secondary students. The authors conclude that "partnering with local schools to provide teaching opportunities for undergraduates will have cognitive benefits and allow non–science major students to master subjects beyond their primary interests." Implementation involved faculty-led training sessions, random assignment via lab-section enrollment, and assessments at multiple distances from the curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty training of undergraduates in the teaching method and content
- A curriculum aligned both with secondary science standards and the university course goals
- Coordination of lab-section times with secondary school class schedules
#### Constraints
- The authors note the effort involved in organizing and adding a service-learning component to an undergraduate science course

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in non–majors science courses

### Target Learning Goals
- deepened understanding and retention of course-specific science content
