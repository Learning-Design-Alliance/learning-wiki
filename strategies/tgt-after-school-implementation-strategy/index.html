Strategies

Implement TGT game-based cooperative learning in after-school programs with adequately trained teachers and extended treatment duration¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends practical adjustments for integrating web-based game playing: extending treatment to 4-6 weeks across topics, recruiting teachers with sufficient ICT and pedagogic skills, and running TGT outside regular class time because sessions overran the 40-minute period. It states "TGT technique can be employed in an after school program so that other school subjects would not be affected."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers with sufficient ICT skill as well as pedagogic skills for conducting the classes
  • Goals and expectations clearly informed every time participants work in groups

Constraints¶

  • Each TGT session took almost 60 minutes rather than the estimated 40-minute class session
  • The study lasted only 3 weeks on three topics, so generalizations should be considered with caution

Target Learners¶

  • Grade VIII secondary school students in rural Bangladesh

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Mathematics achievement (measurement, algebraic equations, algebraic fractions)
  • Positive attitudes toward mathematics

Claims¶

  • The study school faced contextual barriers to game-based learning: teacher shortage, low teacher pedagogic profile, minimal ICT infrastructure, remoteness, and overcrowding [+W]
  • The TGT experimental group significantly outperformed the lecture-based control group on the mathematics posttest [+W]
  • Grade VIII students taught mathematics with the TGT web-based game technique showed a significant pretest-to-posttest achievement gain over three weeks [+W]
  • Cooperative TGT significantly improves Grade 11 students' mathematics understanding compared with conventional teaching over a five-week intervention [+M]
  • Cooperative TGT significantly improves Grade 11 students' mathematics communication compared with conventional teaching [+M]
  • Mathematics understanding and communication gains from Cooperative TGT were not sustained after the intervention was withdrawn [-M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Adopt cooperative TGT as an alternative mathematics teaching approach, supported by teacher training and ministry guidelines
