Implement classroom videogame interventions with teacher training, curriculum alignment, extended schedules, and administrative support¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's teacher, director, and authors converge on recommendations for implementing videogames in classrooms: teacher training in videogames for learning, better integration with the curriculum matching game content with lesson plans, longer intervention duration than the four weeks used, and administrative roles providing access to resources and liaison with the Puerto Rico Department of Education. The authors "suggest alternating game time and game periods, as well as evaluating available educational games looking for compatibility with the Puerto Rico Department of Education curriculum."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher training in videogames for the learning process
  • Better integration with the curriculum, matching game content with lesson plans
  • Administrative provision of access to resources and a liaison with the Puerto Rico Department of Education

Constraints¶

  • The four-week intervention was understood by the teacher and director as too brief
  • The game software is not based on state or national sixth grade English school curriculum

Target Learners¶

  • sixth-grade public school students learning English

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English vocabulary acquisition
  • bilingual fluency

Claims¶

  • Students advanced significantly in game levels each successive week of the intervention [+W]
  • Students mastered significantly more game words each successive week of the four-week intervention [+W]
  • Sixth-grade students in a Puerto Rican public school who played My English Coach in class showed significant pre-post growth in English receptive vocabulary, but so did a same-school control class that did not play it, while an external control group did not [~W]
  • Qualitative findings showed increased student motivation toward English class and learning vocabulary through videogames [+W]
  • Videogame play in class fostered peer competition, peer support, and student attention and immersion [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Brief initial training with extensive follow-up coaching and on-site facilitation for whole-school program implementation
  • School and district leaders' recommendations for implementing effective classroom coaching programs
  • Support program implementation with curriculum-embedded professional learning: workshops, planning and classroom coaching, leadership walkthroughs, and student-work data analysis
  • Secure administrator commitment and multi-year investment when implementing a blended learning program
  • Plan game use deliberately and combine it with traditional vocabulary techniques
