# Enrich low-knowledge/low-motivation worksheets with explicit scaffolding, worked examples, and guided solution structures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size

## Description
Based on think-aloud feedback from in-service teachers reviewing FACET worksheets, materials for learners with limited knowledge and low motivation should add clearer instructional scaffolding and structured support. The article reports that "teachers suggested several refinements: clearer instructional scaffolding, starting worksheets with explicit mathematical operators, integrating worked examples, and adding guided solution strategies such as prefilled rows or structured hints." This is an implementable recipe for worksheet design drawn directly from the expert evaluation step.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of the target learner's knowledge and motivation profile
#### Constraints
- Derived from exploratory feedback on grade 8 mathematics worksheets outside authentic classroom contexts

### Target Learners
- students with limited mathematical knowledge and low motivation

### Target Learning Goals
- task clarity, scaffolding toward independent problem solving
