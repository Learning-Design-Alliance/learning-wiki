# Use listening-based journal tasks in which students comprehend a listening input and then respond in their own words

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends journal writing that is "in part, listening-based and task-based": students receive a topic-related listening assignment and write their input on what they heard. Because listeners tend to remember message content rather than exact words, students were not asked to reproduce the listening verbatim but to "reflect, analyze and create language content using their own words." The authors argue this forces comprehension of the listening before writing and connects writing practice directly to the listening skill the tests measure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A topic-related listening source (e.g., news articles about the target-language country)
- Task instructions that require summarizing and personal response rather than verbatim reproduction
- Teacher feedback with correction codes and follow-up self-correction
#### Constraints
- Frequency and task demands were adjusted by semester (once weekly in semesters one and two, twice weekly with critical-thinking tasks in semester three)

### Target Learners
- adult foreign-language learners in intensive immersion-style courses

### Target Learning Goals
- listening comprehension
- reading comprehension
- written expression in the target language
