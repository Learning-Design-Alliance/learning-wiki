# Non-routine practitioners of advanced procedural skills should receive periodic reinforcement or retraining after initial simulator training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that clinicians who do not routinely perform lung isolation receive ongoing reinforcement of the skill, because performance decays measurably after two months of non-use. It suggests "non-thoracic anesthesiologists may require on-going re-enforcement of their lung isolation skills by performing certain numbers of successful procedures each year" and alternatively that such anesthesiologists "may require reinforcement/retraining of advanced skills in some intervals".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Initial training to a defined proficiency benchmark before the reinforcement schedule begins
#### Constraints
- The decay interval tested was a fixed 2-month period of no practice; decay in clinical patients was not directly tested

### Target Learners
- Anesthesiology residents and clinicians who do not routinely perform thoracic anesthesia

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of rarely-used advanced procedural skills such as lung isolation
