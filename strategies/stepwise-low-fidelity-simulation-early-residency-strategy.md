# Incorporate stepwise, low-fidelity simulation training and assessment early in ophthalmology residency, with safe transition to live surgical teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a structured residency curriculum that begins with low-fidelity simulation and assessment early in training before transitioning trainees to live surgery. It states that "educators in low-and-middle income settings are encouraged to use cheaper, low-fidelity models for training, of which many examples are available", and that high-fidelity models are not essential for good quality simulation teaching. For simulation to work, the article says instruction, supervision, feedback, a curriculum and outcome measurement are required.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- instruction, supervision, feedback, a curriculum and outcome (surgical competency) measurement, as the article states are required for SBSE to work
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that a purely simulation-based surgical curriculum may confer a false sense of security if trainees cannot transfer skills to live surgery

### Target Learners
- early-stage ophthalmology residents in sub-Saharan Africa

### Target Learning Goals
- intraocular surgical skills
- safe transition to live surgery
