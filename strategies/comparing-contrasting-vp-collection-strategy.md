# Populate VP collections with multiple cases per key symptom to enable comparing and contrasting, and treat the blueprint as a dynamic document

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (3 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building collections in which similar clinical presentations appear in multiple cases with varying contexts, so students weigh differential diagnoses by relative probability and typicality of findings. To this end, "we excluded key symptoms that were only present in one VP from the final blueprint", reducing the number of key symptoms. The authors also advise treating the blueprint as dynamic: "we consider the blueprint a dynamic document we will regularly consult and update during and after creating the VPs to preserve the high quality of our VP collection."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A larger collection of VPs; the article cites Kassirer's point that learning clinical reasoning is fostered by a larger collection
#### Constraints
- The article states there is no gold standard for the optimal composition of a VP collection, and that authenticity is not permanent because patient populations fluctuate, requiring revisions and updates

### Target Learners
- medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning by comparing and contrasting similar cases in varying contexts
