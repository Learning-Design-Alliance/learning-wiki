# Design admissions processes that call upon desirable personality characteristics rather than administering personality tests directly

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that medical schools shape selection processes so that the tasks themselves reward constructive personality characteristics and sanction disruptive ones, instead of using direct personality tests, which applicants can fake and whose ideal values are unknown. As the authors conclude, "different selection tools might call upon different personality traits" and schools "may use this effect to select applicants with suitable personalities." The multifaceted process studied required a 40–60 hour portfolio and a day of writing, patient-lecture, scientific-reasoning and MMI-like assignments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Selection tasks must be able to reward suitable behaviour, so that extremely high or low trait scores likely lead to lower scores in the process
#### Constraints
- Differences between admission groups were small; no ideal personality values are available; results come from a single medical school and generalization is unclear

### Target Learners
- medical school applicants

### Target Learning Goals
- selecting applicants with personality characteristics suitable for the medical profession
