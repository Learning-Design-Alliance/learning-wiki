Use an unweighted GPA of 3.0 as a college readiness benchmark signaling a high probability of four-year college graduation¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that high schools and districts treat an unweighted GPA of 3.0 as a validated readiness standard: "a cutoff that gave students a 50 percent or greater likelihood of graduating from a four-year college within six years" in the authors' Chicago analysis. GPA is emphasized because it captures non-cognitive skills like work effort, predicts graduation, and avoids the graduation-versus-college-access trade-off created by raising exit-exam standards.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Districts must track unweighted GPA and be able to report graduates' college outcomes to validate the benchmark locally

Constraints¶

  • In the Chicago data, average GPAs of Latino and African American graduates remained below the 3.0 benchmark
  • The article warns the FAFSA-style selection concerns do not apply here, but notes grade inflation is one theory driving rising GPAs

Target Learners¶

  • Urban high school students, particularly low-income and minority students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • College readiness and four-year college completion

Related Strategies¶

  • Align high school graduation standards, assessments, and course requirements with college readiness so a diploma signals preparation for credit-bearing college work
