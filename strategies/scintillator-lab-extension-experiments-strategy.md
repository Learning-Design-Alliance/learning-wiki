# Extension laboratories: PSD figure-of-merit optimization, neutron time-of-flight, cross-correlation, and coincidence anisotropy measurements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's appendix recommends additional instructional experiments that reuse parts of the setup. "Pulse-shape discrimination (PSD) optimization is a challenging parameter optimization problem that could be explored with only one detector and neutron source," using a figure of merit from Gaussian fits to optimize the short gate. A time-of-flight laboratory needs only two detectors, one source, and an optional shadow bar; cross-correlation of fission events needs two detectors and one fission source; and the well counter is described as well-equipped to measure coincidence anisotropy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- For the TOF extension: two detectors, one neutron source, and optionally a shadow bar to account for room return
- For PSD optimization: Gaussian fits to neutron and photon ratio distributions to evaluate the figure of merit
#### Constraints
- The nonlinear neutron light-output response makes it difficult to calculate incident neutron energy directly, motivating TOF

### Target Learners
- advanced nuclear engineering and physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- delayed fluorescence physics
- curve fitting and figure-of-merit evaluation
- neutron energy spectroscopy
- advanced coincidence analysis
