# Integrate simulation as a curriculum-embedded component with trained instructors and technological infrastructure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that simulation be treated "not as a standalone intervention but as a longitudinal, embedded component of clinical education". Curriculum designers should integrate simulation as a complementary tool rather than a substitute for clinical training, educators should be trained in debriefing and feedback techniques, and policymakers should invest in the necessary technological infrastructure and resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors trained in debriefing techniques and effective feedback delivery
- Technological infrastructure and resources to support simulation-based learning
#### Constraints
- Simulation should complement, not substitute for, clinical training

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- Transfer of theoretical knowledge to clinical practice
- Clinical competence and readiness
