Sustain intervention gains through subsequent high-quality interventions and attention to underlying skills¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's concluding recommendations follow from the underlying skills account: intervene on non-mathematical skills with stable effects on mathematics achievement, and provide continued high-quality intervention for low-achieving children after the initial program ends. As the authors state, "the most effective way to maintain relatively high levels of mathematics achievement produced by high quality interventions, such as Building Blocks, may be subsequent high quality interventions."
