# Integrate computational fluency practice into science lessons by having science teachers present numerical, computation-based examples

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that science and technology teachers, and subject teachers generally, help students develop computational fluency by integrating mathematics into their lessons. Because "computational fluency is frequently used in science, science teachers can easily integrate it into their own lessons". This shares the load of computational practice between subjects, eases the mathematics curriculum, and gives students more time for conceptual understanding of science topics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Science teachers need sufficient mathematical content knowledge and pedagogical content knowledge; the article notes a need for further research on science teachers' mathematics CK and PCK
- Collaboration between science and mathematics teachers, which the article documents as currently lacking in Turkey
#### Constraints
- The article notes that most math teachers were not equipped with necessary science knowledge and that science and mathematics teachers in Turkey do not work in collaboration
- Torigoe's cited research found most science teachers taught their subject symbolically, which did not help students master the content

### Target Learners
- middle and high school students in science classes

### Target Learning Goals
- computational fluency applied in science contexts
- understanding of science topics through numerical examples
