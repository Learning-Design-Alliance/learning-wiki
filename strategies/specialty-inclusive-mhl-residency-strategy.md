# Integrate longitudinal, specialty-inclusive mental health literacy education into residency training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends systematically embedding mental health literacy into undergraduate and postgraduate medical education, with practice-oriented modules tailored to surgical and non-psychiatric residents, promoted psychiatry rotations, and flexible online or asynchronous formats for residents with caregiving responsibilities. It states that "Comprehensive, practice-oriented educational modules tailored to the needs of resident physicians—particularly those in surgical or non-psychiatric specialties—are warranted."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured, longitudinal programming rather than single-session efforts; institutional support mechanisms addressing both MHL and physician well-being.
#### Constraints
- Evidence for specific components (mini-rotations, online modules, case-based anti-stigma content) is drawn from prior studies cited in the discussion, not tested in this survey.

### Target Learners
- Resident physicians, especially in surgical and non-psychiatric specialties

### Target Learning Goals
- Mental health literacy: recognition of mental health problems, help-seeking, knowledge of resources, stigma reduction
