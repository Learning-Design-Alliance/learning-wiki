Administer a consistent, research-validated developmental screener to all participants prior to random assignment in preschool field trials¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

As a feasible alternative to relying on administrative records that reflect unequal access to diagnosis, the article proposes that "researchers might consider administering a consistent, research-validated screener for hearing, vision, and developmental delay to all program participants prior to random assignment." This would generate an exogenous baseline special needs measure for every enrolled child. The article also suggests collecting referral and evaluation data from sources such as Child Find.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The screener must be administered to all program participants before random assignment to yield a baseline measure exogenous to treatment.

Constraints¶

  • The article presents this as an approach not yet used in the literature but likely feasible in field-based trials.

Target Learners¶

  • preschool-aged children applying to oversubscribed public preschool programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • unbiased estimation of program effects for children with special needs
  • early identification of hearing, vision, and developmental delays

Claims¶

  • Measuring special needs earlier improves internal validity but narrows the subgroup to children diagnosed before preschool, creating an external validity trade-off [+W]
  • The HSIS baseline special needs imbalance was concentrated in informal care settings and among higher-incidence disabilities, supporting an access-to-diagnosis mechanism [+W]
  • Estimated intent-to-treat effects of Head Start on cognitive and socioemotional outcomes for children with special needs are sensitive to how the subgroup is defined, with consistently larger effects under the IEP definition [+W]
