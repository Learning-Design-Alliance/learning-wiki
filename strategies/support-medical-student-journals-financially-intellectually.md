# Medical schools and research funders should support the development and maintenance of medical student journals financially and intellectually

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes medical student journals as a strategy for cultivating future clinical academics, since MSJs provide "a student-friendly environment where students can submit their work, develop research-related skills, and familiarise themselves with the peer-review process". It explicitly recommends that medical schools and funders support MSJs financially and intellectually, and suggests targeted interventions to reduce the gender gap should be introduced at the medical-school level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A functioning medical student journal with a peer-review process open to student submissions
#### Constraints
- The article notes concerns about the presumed low quality of MSJ articles, with a cited analysis finding most MSJs to have opaque peer-review policies

### Target Learners
- medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- academic writing and manuscript preparation
- research and publishing skills
- familiarity with the peer-review process
