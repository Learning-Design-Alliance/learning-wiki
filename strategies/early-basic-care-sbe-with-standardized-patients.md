# Include simulation-based experiences with standardized patients focused on basic care from the early stages of nursing education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating simulation-based experiences centered on basic care—such as hygiene, mobilization, vital signs, and feeding—into early nursing curricula, before students enter clinical rotations. They argue these experiences develop technical, emotional, and communicative skills in a safe and realistic environment while building confidence and professional identity. The recommendation rests on the study's finding that novice students experienced these encounters as emotionally intense but formative, with errors carrying formative rather than punitive value.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardized patients trained to portray specific clinical conditions
- A psychologically safe prebriefing and reflective debriefing structure
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single Spanish university with session exposure varying from 1 to 4 simulations, which the authors state may have influenced differences in perceptions

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate nursing students without prior hospital contact

### Target Learning Goals
- basic care competencies
- therapeutic communication
- professional identity
- preparedness for clinical practice
