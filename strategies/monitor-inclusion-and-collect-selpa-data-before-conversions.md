# Monitor preK and K–12 inclusion rates and collect systematic SELPA service and funding data before permitting more single-district SELPA conversions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report recommends that if large districts are allowed to leave multidistrict SELPAs, the state should monitor inclusion rates for students receiving special education services in preK and grades K–12, review IEP placement-decision documentation during monitoring, and provide training and guidance on placement best practices. It also recommends collecting data on SELPA services, fund use, and administration costs: "the state should consider collecting additional data to better understand differences in per pupil funding within and between SELPAs."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- State capacity to monitor inclusion rates and review IEP placement documentation across district sizes and SELPA configurations
#### Constraints
- Findings are exploratory and cross-sectional; the study does not support causal inferences about the effects of large districts withdrawing from multidistrict SELPAs, and implications beyond California are limited because the SELPA membership requirement is unique to California

### Target Learners
- Students receiving special education services (students with IEPs) in California school districts, including preK and K–12 students in small, midsized, and large districts

### Target Learning Goals
- Access to the general education curriculum and least restrictive environment placement; equitable special education service provision and funding across district configurations
