# Focus mindset interventions on supporting specific growth-oriented habits and behaviors rather than on influencing overarching beliefs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that because introductory astronomy students already strongly endorse growth-mindset beliefs but do not consistently act on them, the "most useful interventions will be those that focus explicitly on encouraging and supporting specific growth-oriented habits and behaviors that have been demonstrated to improve learning rather than on influencing overarching beliefs." It advises training students to interpret academic challenges constructively and to respond mindfully to classroom experiences, and supporting adoption of learning behaviors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have sufficient time and resources to devote to their education; the article notes some students face financial or personal challenges that make follow-through difficult
#### Constraints
- Interventions fostering growth-mindset beliefs alone may hit a ceiling effect when baseline agreement is already high, as observed in this study

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory general education courses

### Target Learning Goals
- adopting growth-oriented learning behaviors and interpretations of academic challenge
