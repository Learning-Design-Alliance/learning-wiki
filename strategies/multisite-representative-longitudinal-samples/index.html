Scale to multisite, large, representative longitudinal samples while complementing them with exploratory small-sample studies¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 associational), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends moving from widely used convenience samples, which often consist of a biased selection and relatively few participants, to multisite studies that "scale studies to large, representative samples" needed to integrate multiple levels, functions, and time points. It stresses representativeness in socioeconomic status and cultural background with worldwide coverage, and argues large-scale and exploratory studies are complementary: exploratory work generates hypotheses and theories that large-scale studies later test.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Must be based on a common framework
  • Use measures comparable across studies
  • Allow reproducible analyses such as via pre-registered studies

Constraints¶

  • Large-scale studies typically involve hypothesis-driven research; exploratory studies with relatively lower samples are also needed in this relatively new field

Target Learners¶

  • Children and adolescents, including multicultural and varied socioeconomic populations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Neurodevelopmental and learning-related outcomes across reading, math, and executive function

Claims¶

  • The study's invariance findings may be biased by excluding students who missed a test and by sample heterogeneity [+W]
  • Empirical research on the Pepper robot in formal education is recent, concentrated in primary education, and dominated by qualitative designs with small samples [+W]
  • School-based intensive longitudinal studies show wide heterogeneity in sample size, study duration, and assessment frequency, with a median sample of 157 participants and average duration of about 13 days [+W]
