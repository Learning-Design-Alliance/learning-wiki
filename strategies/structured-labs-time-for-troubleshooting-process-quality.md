# Improve structured labs via time for testing and troubleshooting and emphasis on process quality over product

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
From their data, the authors identify two small manipulations that might allow structured labs to better prepare students for the cognitive tasks involved in UREs: "providing time for testing and troubleshooting equipment (for example, spreading experiment across multiple weeks) and placing emphasis on the quality of students' process rather than the product they obtain". They observed that when the goal was to obtain a known result, this corrupted the process, and that producing new knowledge did not emerge as important from their work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lab activities must allow enough time for testing and troubleshooting, e.g. by spreading an experiment across multiple weeks
#### Constraints
- Derived from focus group data with students in relatively conventional courses; the authors call for targeted interviews or surveys with different samples to establish generalizability

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in introductory lab courses

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental physics cognitive tasks
- troubleshooting and iteration skills
- preparation for research experiences
