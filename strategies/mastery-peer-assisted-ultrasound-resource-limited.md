# Adopt mastery learning and peer-assisted learning models to maximize ultrasound training effectiveness in resource-limited settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adopting "mastery learning and peer-assisted learning models" to address scarce faculty and equipment in ultrasound education. By "clearly defining achievement goals for each module and utilizing peer tutors to provide close feedback", the curriculum can compensate for limited faculty feedback while freeing faculty to resolve complex difficulties. This recommendation responds to the study's finding that one faculty member taught 105 students with limited devices, and that students noted insufficient direct feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clearly defined achievement goals for each training module
- Peer tutors available to provide close feedback
#### Constraints
- The article notes the study's resource-constrained model has limited scalability for typical 15- or 16-week academic semesters in South Korea

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate Korean Medicine students
- Novice ultrasound learners in medical education

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic ultrasound image acquisition skills
- Procedural competency in musculoskeletal ultrasound
