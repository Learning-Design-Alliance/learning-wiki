Use eDelphi studies with expert panels to establish consensus on definitions of emergent learning design constructs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article demonstrates using a Delphi methodology to generate consensus among subject-matter experts via repeated, iterated surveys, which is suited to emergent concepts where shared agreement is difficult to determine. Consensus items were operationalized with three criteria: "(a) interquartile range was less than or equal to 1, (b) standard deviation was less than or equal to 1, and (c) there was 80% agreement on a rating of 1–3 (disagree) or 4–6 (agree)". Initial survey items should be generated in open-ended format from an extensive literature review to support instrument validity.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Initial instrument items derived from an extensive literature review or systematic content analysis
  • Multiple survey rounds with items amended based on participant responses
  • A defined set of consensus thresholds combining central tendency and dispersion measures

Constraints¶

  • The Delphi methodology and sampling strategy may introduce bias; the authors note the sample was skewed toward higher education and suggest replication with different thresholds or broader samples

Target Learners¶

  • Learning design researchers and practitioners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Establishing shared conceptual definitions and competencies in learning design fields
