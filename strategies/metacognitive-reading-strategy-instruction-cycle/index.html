Teach metacognitive reading strategies as a before-during-after cycle of planning, self-monitoring through thinking aloud, and self-evaluation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article's instructional recipe walks learners through planning before reading (thinking about the passage's topic, what they know, and how the text might be organized), monitoring during reading, and evaluating after reading. The instructor encouraged learners "to monitor their comprehension by themselves (self-monitoring) through thinking aloud", asking questions such as whether they understood what they just read and which strategies worked well. This cycle was delivered across sixteen sessions following Oxford's (1990) training model with TOEFL reading passages.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sixteen sessions of strategy training with reading passages matched to learners' proficiency level
  • Explicit teacher explanation of the concept and kinds of learning strategies before practice

Constraints¶

  • The article reports some cognitive strategies were not transferable to learners' minds and learners rejected applying them

  • upper-intermediate EFL learners aged 16-26
