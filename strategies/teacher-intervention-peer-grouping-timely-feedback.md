# Teacher intervention in peer grouping and timely feedback to keep peer-assisted learning objective

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because familiar residents preferentially form peer support groups, making feedback less objective, the article recommends that "in the teaching group discussion stage, it is also necessary for teachers to intervene, rationally allocate group members, and guide the discussion of problems" so every resident provides objective feedback. It also recommends timely and effective feedback, since delayed video-assessment feedback causes details to be forgotten.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers available to allocate group members and guide discussions; a system delivering homework feedback promptly after peer and teacher evaluation
#### Constraints
- The strategy responds to the article's own findings that peer ratings ran higher than teacher ratings and that video-assessment feedback was delayed

### Target Learners
- medical residents in standardized residency training

### Target Learning Goals
- objective peer feedback and clinical skill reflection
