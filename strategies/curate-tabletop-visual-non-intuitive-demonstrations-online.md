# Curate tabletop, visually driven demonstrations with non-intuitive outcomes for online physics lessons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A strategy for selecting live physics demonstrations that work over a video-conferencing camera. The authors advise choosing "tabletop demonstrations" that "fit easily within the field of view of the [laptop] camera", preferring visual over auditory effects, and picking demonstrations "with non-intuitive outcomes" so the element of surprise compensates for the lost drama of a live in-person demonstration. Examples used were a silicone lid holding up a tablet, tuning-fork resonance, and a low-temperature-differential Stirling engine.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A camera and microphone setup on the instructor's side and viewing/listening capability on the students' side
#### Constraints
- Demonstrations must fit within the camera's field of view without the instructor moving about significantly; auditory demonstrations are limited by microphone and speaker capability

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting physics theory with real-world experiences
