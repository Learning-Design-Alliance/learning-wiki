# Double-dose algebra: tracking with supports as an alternative to detracking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The double-dose algebra strategy keeps homogeneous classes but gives low-skill students two periods of algebra—a support course plus regular algebra—along with curricular resources, stand-alone lesson plans, and professional development workshops three times a year for teachers. The article presents it as an alternative to detracking, since "merely eliminating tracking is not clearly preferable," and shows the supports mitigated tracking's typical harms for low-skill students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires doubled instructional time for below-norm students
- Requires curricular resources and lesson plans for double-dose teachers
- Requires ongoing professional development for teachers of support courses
#### Constraints
- A full set of mediating factors cannot be separated for low-skill students because instructional time, resources, and professional development changed simultaneously
- Benefits depended on schools sorting students into separate algebra classes per policy guidelines

### Target Learners
- Ninth-grade students with 8th-grade math scores below the national median
- Ninth-grade algebra students generally

### Target Learning Goals
- Algebra achievement (test scores)
- Algebra course passing/failure
