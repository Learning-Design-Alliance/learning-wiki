# Target instructional support in inquiry-based science at students with weak understanding of the control-of-variables strategy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers identify and specifically support students whose understanding of the control-of-variables strategy is weak when running inquiry-based science instruction. It states that "teachers should particularly support students lacking proper understanding of this experimentation skill in inquiry-based instruction", because such support might help them discard misconceptions and restructure their concept knowledge toward a more proficient profile. The recommendation rests on the latent transition analysis showing CVS understanding predicts transitions to more proficient knowledge profiles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers able to assess or identify students' CVS understanding before or during inquiry instruction
- Inquiry-based instruction in which students design and interpret experiments
#### Constraints
- Derived from a teacher-guided floating-and-sinking curriculum unit in Swiss elementary classrooms; the article notes implementation was not formally evaluated in this sample

### Target Learners
- Elementary school students, first to sixth grade, especially those with low CVS understanding

### Target Learning Goals
- Restructuring misconceptions toward more proficient conceptual knowledge in science inquiry topics

### Affordances
- Lta Floating Sinking Knowledge Profiles Taxonomy
