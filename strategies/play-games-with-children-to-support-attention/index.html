Support children's attention development by sharing time playing various games and puzzles¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that "Attention may be improved by sharing time with children and playing various games with them", and that families support this by sharing time and playing games chosen based on the child's age. It suggests activities such as combining pieces into shapes, children developing their own games, and using jigsaw puzzles, lego blocks, domino games and die games.

Claims¶

  • A 10-week brain teaser curriculum significantly improved six-year-old children's attention span scores from pre-test to post-test [+W]
  • The between-group comparison favored the brain teasers curriculum over the National Education Ministry Pre-School curriculum for attention spans [+W]
  • Modeling training improved imaginative play in disadvantaged kindergartners, with gains in imaginativeness, verbal communication, spontaneity, and attention span persisting two months [+W]
