Strategies

Situate learners in problematic situations with not-quite-defined questions across 15 technological, cognitive and ethical activities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The course design placed learners "before problematic situations in order to enable him to identify the problem, formulate d hypotheses for the resolution and evaluate the most appropriate solution", presenting "not quite defined questions" to stimulate reflection and resolution. Specifically, "we have been d esigned 15 activities, five for each activity: technological, cognitive and ethical", delivered across 30 two-hour meetings in the e-learning platform. This technique enabled self-reflection and self-evaluation by the learner, enabling true understanding of media messages.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An e-learning platform with synchronous and asynchronous tools (forum, blog, wiki, chat, email) for collaborative activities.

Constraints¶

  • The strategy was implemented with future teachers in a university e-learning course; the article does not report testing it in other settings.

Target Learners¶

  • university students in primary education sciences (aged 19-27)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • digital skills across technological, cognitive and ethical dimensions
  • problem identification and hypothesis formulation

Affordances¶

  • Three Dimensional Digital Literacy Framework

Claims¶

  • Post-test cognitive-dimension performance is strong for reading, selecting and interpreting text (50%) and evaluating information relevance and reliability (52%), but poor for organising data and tables and formal-logic comparison [+W]
  • Post-test ethical-dimension results are non-uniform: 47% on respecting users in the network, 40% on privacy protection and 30% on knowledge of differences [+W]
  • Post-test Instant DCA profiles show sufficient technological expertise on interface identification (59%) and technical problem solving (52%) but very low technological understanding (26%) [+W]
  • A 60-hour e-learning activity programme significantly increased pre-service teachers' Instant DCA scores on the technological, cognitive and ethical dimensions [+W]
  • R-I-Z-A-based assessment found satisfactory qualitative outcomes: 86% improvement in resources, 81% excellent interpretation performance, and 76% able to produce a medial message [+W]
  • Students developed self-regulation, gaining the ability to monitor problem-solving processes and correct irrelevant strategies, with self-evaluation enabling reflection on their actions [+W]
