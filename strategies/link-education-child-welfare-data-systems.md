# Link education and child welfare data systems to make the foster care achievement gap visible and trackable

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report calls for accessible, linked, comprehensive, and current education and child welfare data to inform greater cross-agency collaboration. Because the systems previously had "neither a shared definition of the foster-care population nor a common unique student identifier," students' education needs went unrecognized and unmet. Linking the data systems creates a baseline for tracking academic progress and provides information for strengthening policy and practice aimed at narrowing the achievement gap.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cross-agency data-sharing agreements between the education and child welfare agencies, as the Arizona Department of Child Safety and Arizona Department of Education arranged
#### Constraints
- The report notes district-level counts based on students' first school of enrollment are likely an undercount because of students' changing foster care status and high school mobility

### Target Learners
- K–12 students in foster care

### Target Learning Goals
- Raising awareness of the academic vulnerability of students in foster care and creating a baseline for tracking their academic progress
