# Tailor professional trainings to target perceived barriers alongside knowledge and inquiry behavior

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that trainings for health professionals should go beyond general information about trauma and directly target the perceived barriers that hinder trauma inquiry, such as fear of harming or offending clients. The authors state that "Tailored trainings that target both perceived barriers to trauma inquiry and trauma inquiry behavior seem necessary to change relevant attitudes and behaviors." They argue that reducing perceived barriers in addition to changing behavior seems critical for long-term implementation, since professionals may fall back into old behavior if barriers such as the belief that asking is harmful are not reduced.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainings should include content that directly addresses specific barriers, e.g., discussing the fear of retraumatization and explaining the difference between screening questions and trauma-focused psychotherapy
#### Constraints
- The article notes that information-only trainings about trauma and its consequences might be ineffective in changing perceived barriers and inquiry behavior

### Target Learners
- health care professionals in substance use disorder treatment settings

### Target Learning Goals
- systematic inquiry of traumatic events
- willingness to ask clients about trauma
