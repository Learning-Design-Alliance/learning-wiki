# Digital Literacy Instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Digital literacy instruction teaches learners how to effectively use digital technology, communication tools, and networks to locate, evaluate, use, and create information. It spans operational skills (using devices, search engines, and platforms), information literacy (judging credibility and relevance of sources), and productive skills (creating and communicating digital content). Effective programs treat evaluation and creation as core, not add-ons: learners who only receive tool training do not transfer skills to new platforms or unfamiliar information contexts.

## Design Implications

Digital literacy is best taught through authentic tasks — real searches, real websites, real audiences — rather than isolated tool tutorials, because skills must transfer to technologies that change faster than curricula can. Lateral reading — leaving a source to check what other sites say about it — is the evaluation strategy that distinguishes expert fact-checkers from novices, and it can be taught directly [+S]. Instruction should model expert evaluation moves explicitly, then fade to independent practice, consistent with [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) logic.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to devices, connectivity, and instructors who can model evaluation and creation, not just operation
- Authentic tasks with real information needs (research questions, genuine audiences, publishable artifacts)
- Explicit [Practice](../elements/practice.md) evaluating unfamiliar sources, since evaluation skill does not emerge from tool familiarity alone
- Recurring opportunities to revisit and update skills, since platform-specific knowledge decays

#### Constraints
- Assumes a "digital native" baseline that does not exist: self-reported fluency with technology is a poor predictor of evaluation skill, and young learners frequently cannot distinguish sponsored content, biased sources, or fabricated claims [-S]
- Tool-specific training transfers poorly; skills tied to one interface or platform fail when the interface changes [-M]
- Requires sustained instructional time; one-off workshops produce little durable change in evaluation behavior [-M]
- Access gaps (devices, bandwidth, home support) can widen rather than narrow equity gaps if instruction assumes out-of-school digital experience

#### Implementation Variability
- Embedded vs. standalone: integrating evaluation tasks into subject-area research projects produces more transfer than separate "computer class" periods
- Whole-task vs. part-task: teaching search, evaluation, synthesis, and creation as an integrated project vs. drilling sub-skills separately; whole-task approaches align with evidence that [whole-task performance improves transfer](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [+M]
- Media type: evaluation instruction applies to search results, social media, video, and AI-generated content; lateral reading routines generalize across all of these

### Target Learners
- Novices of any age with limited prior exposure to digital tools; explicit modeling reduces the search and trial-and-error burden [chunking reduces working memory load](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- K–12 and adult learners alike — but instruction must be calibrated to actual skill, not age-based assumptions about "digital natives" [-S]
- Learners with strong operational fluency still benefit from evaluation-focused instruction, which targets a different skill set than tool use

### Target Learning Goals
- Information evaluation: judging credibility, bias, and evidence quality of online sources
- Procedural skill: operating search, communication, and creation tools efficiently
- Content creation: producing digital artifacts for [authentic audiences and purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md)
- Metacognitive habits: monitoring one's own searching and verification behavior

### Instructions
1. Diagnose actual skills with an authentic task (find and evaluate a source on an unfamiliar topic), not a self-report survey
2. [Model](../elements/coaching.md) expert search and evaluation moves aloud — lateral reading, checking authorship, comparing sources — making decisions visible
3. Provide guided [Practice](../elements/practice.md) on curated sets of contrasting sources (credible, biased, sponsored, fabricated); [multiple contrasting cases support abstraction](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M]
4. Prompt learners to [articulate](../elements/articulation.md) why they judged a source credible or not; [self-explanation improves conceptual understanding](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]
5. Assign an authentic creation task — a researched product for a real audience — with [assessment](../elements/assessment.md) criteria covering evaluation and creation, and [feedback at the task and process levels](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
6. Revisit and re-practice across the term; [spaced repetition improves retention](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+M]

## Related Strategies
- Media literacy and civic online reasoning programs — the evaluation-focused branch of digital literacy with the strongest outcome evidence
- Inquiry-based research projects — the authentic-task context in which digital literacy skills are most durably applied

## Examples
- **Stanford History Education Group — Civic Online Reasoning curriculum** ([https://cor.stanford.edu](https://cor.stanford.edu)): free lessons teaching lateral reading, source checking, and evidence evaluation; field-tested in classrooms with measured pre/post gains.
- **Common Sense Education Digital Citizenship Curriculum** ([https://www.commonsense.org/education](https://www.commonsense.org/education)): K–12 lessons on media balance, privacy, and evaluating online information.
- **Northstar Digital Literacy** ([https://www.digitalliteracyassessment.org](https://www.digitalliteracyassessment.org)): standards-based assessment and instruction for adult learners, widely used in adult basic education programs.
