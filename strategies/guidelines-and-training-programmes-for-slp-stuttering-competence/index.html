Strategies

Develop guidelines and training programmes to improve SLPs' competence in stuttering management¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review states that its insights "can inform the development of guidelines and training programmes to improve SLPs' competence and ultimately benefit people who stutter". This strategy directs universities, professional bodies and employers to use the synthesis of influencing factors when designing education and support. It responds to the review's call for integrated information to determine SLPs' education and support needs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Programme design should target the factors the review identified, including continuing professional education, caseload experience and support to implement treatment

Constraints¶

  • The review notes the small number of studies globally meeting the inclusion criteria limits the generalizability of its findings

Target Learners¶

  • speech–language pathologists managing stuttering

Target Learning Goals¶

  • clinical competence in stuttering assessment and treatment

Claims¶

  • Continuing professional education in stuttering is associated with higher perceived competence [+W]
  • Multiple factors influence SLPs' perceived competence in managing stuttering, including support, knowledge, formal education, experience, familiarity, prior outcomes and perceived complexity [+W]
  • Higher perceived competence is associated with greater frequency of seeing people who stutter on SLPs' caseloads [+W]
  • SLPs perceive themselves as less competent in treating stuttering than in assessing it [+W]
  • Perceived competence in stuttering management varies across different settings and regions [+W]
