Choose widely accessible software such as Excel when designing a course fully instructed with software¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that when a whole course is to be taught with a software, wide availability should be a selection criterion, framing accessible ICT use as contributing to the social aspect of sustainable development through equality of access. It argues that "Teaching a subject using a software which could be bought and possessed by everyone meets the social aspect of sustainable development" and that "being widely available is an important criteria" when designing a fully software-instructed course. In the study, all pre-service teachers already had Excel 2010 installed and needed minimum guidance for basic skills.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The software must be one students already possess or can easily obtain
  • Students need only minimum guidance for basic skills such as entering data, opening and saving files and creating graphs

Constraints¶

  • Duller (2008), cited by the author, recommends teaching statistics with Excel with a critical point of view, finding some Excel functions tricky, unmeaningful or inaccurate

Target Learners¶

  • pre-service teachers
  • university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • statistics concepts
  • ICT integration in education for sustainable development

Claims¶

  • Ms Excel can be used to instruct most of the Basic Statistics Course content conceptually with pre-service science teachers [+W]
  • Excel-based statistics instruction improved pre-service teachers' attitudes towards statistics more than theoretical instruction on all six SATS-36 factors [+W]
  • The common effect of instruction type and group was significantly positive in all six dimensions of attitudes towards statistics [+W]
