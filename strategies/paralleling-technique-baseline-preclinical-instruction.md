# Adopt the paralleling technique as the baseline for preclinical periapical radiography instruction and develop unified instructor training guidelines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends standardizing preclinical oral radiology instruction around the paralleling technique, given that Institution A students using it with receptor holders were significantly more positive than Institution B students using the bisecting angle technique with finger support. The authors state the observed variation "underscores the need for greater standardization in teaching oral radiology by adopting the paralleling technique as the baseline for preclinical instruction and developing unified instructor training guidelines."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recognition that the bisecting angle technique may still be required due to anatomical restrictions, insufficient occlusal support, limited viewing conditions, limited mouth opening, or young children
#### Constraints
- There is no global consensus on how these techniques should be taught in undergraduate dental programs

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students
- dental educators

### Target Learning Goals
- periapical radiographic technique
