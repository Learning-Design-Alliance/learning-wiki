# Collaboration-level inclusive-climate practices: anti-harassment onboarding, moderated telecon turn-taking, junior-first question order, student-only debriefs, and a Climate and Equity committee

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
NANOGrav pairs programmatic outreach with collaboration-level climate policies. All new members must read and abide by an anti-harassment and diversity policy as part of membership approval, reinforced by regular meeting presentations on harassment, unconscious bias, microaggressions, and imposter syndrome. In telecons, the authors report that "requiring members to speak only after raising their hand and being called on by the moderator" makes a large difference toward inclusivity so that all viewpoints, not only the loudest, are heard. Meetings follow best practices including junior members asking questions before senior members and student-only debriefing sessions, and a Climate and Equity committee formulates policies and practices supporting all members.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An anti-harassment and diversity policy that all new members read and abide by during membership approval
- A moderator empowered to call on raised hands during telecons
- A committee tasked with formulating policies and practices, not investigating interpersonal disputes
#### Constraints
- Enforcement of anti-harassment policies must often happen at the individual institution level
- Demographics are somewhat limited by those of member institutions, which may have disparate admissions and hiring processes

### Target Learners
- graduate students
- postdocs
- senior researchers in distributed research collaborations

### Target Learning Goals
- inclusive professional work environment
- respecting and valuing diversity
- retention of early-career researchers
