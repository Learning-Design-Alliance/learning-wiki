# Use the identified urban competency themes to inform primary care education and practice for urban areas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that its findings guide training for urban primary care: with urban population concentration progressing, the authors write that "our study will give certain suggestions for primary care education and practice necessary for urban areas." Educators can use the ten extracted urban competency themes, including integration of fragmented care and collaboration with various medical care and welfare personnel, as objectives when designing urban general practitioner training programs, complementing the existing AAFP urban/inner-city training emphasis on underserved care.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training programs should attend to the urban context, including segmented healthcare services and diverse patient socioeconomic backgrounds
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a preliminary qualitative study of 10 early-career, board-certified Japanese general practitioners, which the authors state limits generalizability

### Target Learners
- General practitioner/family physician trainees preparing for urban practice

### Target Learning Goals
- Providing contextual care suitable for urban areas
- Integrating fragmented care and coordinating with medical and welfare professionals

### Affordances
- Contextual Care Primary Care Framework
