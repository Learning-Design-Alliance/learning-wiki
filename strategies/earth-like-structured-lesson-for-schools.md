# Embed Earth-Like in a structured lesson for school classes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' recommendation from the questionnaire results is that the website works best for school students when embedded in teacher-led structure. They write that "the responses from the high school student demographic suggest that the site would be most effective as part of a structured lesson where students where challenged to consider the ways a planet might differ from our own." Supporting additions include a five-minute chalkboard video introducing exoplanets and the habitable zone, which "could also act as an introduction to a class, setting the scene for the framework of the website", and a Frequently Asked Questions page clarifying why modelled planets are labelled Earth-like.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured lesson in which students are challenged to consider ways a planet might differ from Earth; the chalkboard video can serve as a class introduction
#### Constraints
- Without structure, high school students struggled to understand the main message and did poorest on the Earth 2.0 comprehension question

### Target Learners
- high school students
- in-service physics teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- recognising the limits of what is known about Earth-sized exoplanets
- considering the range of ways planets can differ from Earth
