# Use active-learning techniques throughout — dice-rolling, think–pair–share, and role-playing — to elicit and combat probability misconceptions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course uses active learning "as much as possible, including dice-rolling whenever possible", plus "liberal use of think–pair–share interspersed within the 75-min classes" for numerical Bayes questions, predicting real-world practices, and open-ended design questions. Role-playing exercises have students "act out the roles of a desperate patient, a drug company rep, and an insurance company" while debating acceptable type I:type II error trade-offs. Dice exercises with regular and irregular dice target outcome orientation and equiprobability bias.
