# Integrate resilience-building strategies into teacher training programs, prioritizing psychosocial facets of academic success and pairing them with study-skill instruction when grade improvement is the goal

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teacher education programs incorporate resilience-building supports, since resilience in this study was associated with coping with study demands and social adaptation rather than grades. The authors state that "Teacher training programs may consider incorporating resilience-building strategies to support students in managing academic stress and fostering professional preparedness." They suggest tailoring supports by profile, e.g. workshops promoting autonomy and emotional self-regulation for introverted candidates and structured support for perseverance and self-efficacy for candidates with depressive or anxious symptoms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Resilience treated as a trainable professional competency rather than a fixed trait
#### Constraints
- The article notes resilience showed only a minor, non-significant trend with grade-related performance, so resilience training should be paired with study-skill or instructional supports when grade improvement is the goal

### Target Learners
- University students in teacher training programs (pre-service teachers)

### Target Learning Goals
- Managing academic stress
- Coping with study demands
- Social adaptation and professional preparedness
