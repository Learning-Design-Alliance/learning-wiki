# Keep community gatekeeper training brief (about two hours) to enable lay public participation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends brevity as a design feature for community-facing gatekeeper training: "All told, it required just two hours, similar in length to standard QPR training." The authors contrast this with Mental Health First Aid and ASIST, which "require one to two full days of instruction", a length they "see as too long to expect significant participation levels from the general public." The strategy is to compress film, skills practice, and discussion into a single short event open to the public.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multi-modal compression of didactic, experiential, and discussion components into the short format
#### Constraints
- Effects were evaluated immediately post-event; durability is unknown

### Target Learners
- general lay public in at-risk communities

### Target Learning Goals
- suicide-prevention gatekeeper skills and self-efficacy
