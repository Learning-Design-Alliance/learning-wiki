# Distance from broad global solutions and design education around specific local needs, letting communities decide the purpose school serves

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (6 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's implications section recommends that to reimagine education for hunter-gatherer communities, educators and policymakers "must distance ourselves from broad global solutions and focus on specific local needs." It argues that until stigma is reduced and economic opportunities associated with schooling improve, attendance mostly does not fulfil its promise, and that individuals and communities should decide for themselves the purpose school serves and whether children should be compelled to attend.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recognition of local knowledge systems and community self-determination in education policy and school design.
#### Constraints
- The review states that simply providing access to education does not straightforwardly lead to economic, social, and gender equality, and that historically embedded structural and cultural barriers play a critical role in continued educational exclusion.

### Target Learners
- Hunter-gatherer children and communities in the Global South

### Target Learning Goals
- Relevant, appropriate, and accessible education aligned with community-defined purposes
