# Import a smartphone photo of an oscilloscope screen into GeoGebra to sample a curve quickly in many points

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The strategy replaces visual reading of measures directly on a small oscilloscope screen with photographing the screen from a distance of about 3 meters to reduce fish-eye distortion, importing the picture into GeoGebra, aligning its grid to the Cartesian axes, and clicking on the curve to create sampling points. The article states this procedure allows students to sample the curve in a high number of points in very little time while strengthening computer competences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A smartphone camera, an oscilloscope displaying the discharge curve, and GeoGebra with the picture aligned to the Cartesian grid and axis units defined
#### Constraints
- Measures can be difficult due to the little screen, the spread of the light trace, the scaling factors and the lack of a fine grid; residual picture distortion and misalignment affect reliability

### Target Learners
- secondary-school physics students
- pre-service physics teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the physical meaning of a fitting process
- sampling experimental data
- analytic geometry competences
