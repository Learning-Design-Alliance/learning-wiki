# Structure PBL sessions around case-specific 3D-printed models with teacher-facilitated group inquiry and self- and peer-assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable recipe: give trainees a standardised PBL training course beforehand; open with learning objectives; present cases where students identify problems and explore them with 3D models; hold group discussions integrating interdisciplinary knowledge from internet and library resources; have group representatives report; close with self-assessment, peer assessment, and teacher feedback that "clarified complex issues, and provided further learning resources and guidance".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All students complete a basic standardized training course on PBL before the intervention
- Teacher role shifts to facilitator and supporter guiding group inquiry
#### Constraints
- The trial tested this strategy only as a combined 3DP-plus-PBL package, with no 3D-printing-only or PBL-only arms, so the observed benefits reflect the combined strategy

### Target Learners
- Assistant general practitioner trainees in spinal surgery / orthopaedics rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning, problem-solving, teamwork, self-directed learning, and knowledge of thoracolumbar fractures
