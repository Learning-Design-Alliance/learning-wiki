Use a three-group design (two experimental, one control) to isolate the added explanation principle's effect¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that future studies compare the refined strategy against the original one directly. They suggest "an experimental design that should include two experimental and one control group", with explanation assisted REACT in the first experimental group and REACT alone in the second, so the added explanation principle's effect can be seen more clearly.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • One experimental group taught with explanation assisted REACT strategy
  • A second experimental group taught with the original REACT strategy
  • A control group for baseline comparison

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • prospective science teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • learning impulse, momentum and collisions concepts

Claims¶

  • Explanation assisted REACT strategy produces significantly higher post-test conceptual understanding of impulse, momentum and collisions than traditional teaching [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Standardise outcome measurements and isolate individual instructional design features in future research
  • Compare discovery-based activities to a lecture on identical content to isolate the benefit of direct manipulation
