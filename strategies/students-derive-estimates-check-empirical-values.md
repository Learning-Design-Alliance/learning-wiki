# Have students derive material-property estimates themselves and check them against empirical values

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends extending this material through problem sets in which students reproduce the paper's estimates and compare them with measured values. As printed: "Students may be asked to derive some of the estimates in this paper for themselves, and look up the empirical values to see how their estimates hold up." An appendix supplies sample questions, including estimating graphene's mass per surface area, cellular-solid density as a function of metal fraction, and muonic-atom materials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need access to handbooks or references for empirical values to compare against their estimates
#### Constraints
- Some appendix questions are noted as unnecessarily challenging for introductory courses, e.g. three-dimensional materials with non-simple cubic crystal structures

### Target Learners
- freshmen in introductory physics courses
- advanced high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- practicing back-of-the-envelope estimation
- connecting molecular physics to measurable material properties
