# Intervene in Abusive Language

## Description
Teachers must step in when abusive language occurs. This involves addressing the student using offensive language, discussing the impact of their words, and fostering empathy for those who are hurt. Techniques such as restorative justice and involving parents can be helpful.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher who is aware of the language being used in the classroom, who is willing to intervene when necessary, and who has the skills and resources to address the situation effectively.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging to address in the moment, may require additional support from administrators or parents, and may not always be effective.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All students, particularly those who use or are affected by abusive language.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Create a safe and respectful classroom environment, prevent and address abusive language, promote empathy and understanding, and teach students about the impact of their words.

### Affordances
- Helps students understand the impact of their words, promotes empathy and respect, and creates a safer learning environment.

### Personalization
- The response to abusive language should depend on the situation and the students involved. Teachers should be willing to include students and their solutions in responses to these situations.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the frequency and severity of abusive language in the classroom, assess student understanding of the impact of their words, and gather feedback from students about the safety and respectfulness of the learning environment.

## Impact
- Research suggests that addressing abusive language in schools can create a safer and more respectful learning environment, promote positive social-emotional development, and improve academic outcomes.

## Examples
- A teacher hears a student use a homophobic slur and immediately says, 'I don't allow that term in my classroom. I need to talk with you after class about that.' The teacher then meets with the student to discuss the impact of their words and to foster empathy for those who are hurt.
