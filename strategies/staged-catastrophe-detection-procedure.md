# Apply catastrophe detection in stages: bimodality first, then individual time-series jumps, then remaining flags

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 theoretical, 1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
A staged procedure for detecting developmental transitions: begin with bimodality screening in a cross-sectional study, then locate jumps in individual time series, then choose among the remaining flags depending on the domain and measurement possibilities. The article advises that 'Research in developmental transitions should start with the detection of bimodality' because its detection is relatively simple and it is a necessary criterion, and that catastrophe detection 'should precede catastrophe modelling'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Bimodality detection requires only a cross-sectional study; detection of the sudden jump 'requires longitudinal experiments'.
#### Constraints
- 'In conservation research highly frequent measurements are problematic', and it is not clear what can be regarded as a perturbation in that domain, limiting the perturbation-based flags.

### Target Learners
- children acquiring Piagetian conservation
- developmental researchers designing transition studies

### Target Learning Goals
- detecting developmental transitions before fitting catastrophe models
