Build an after-school/out-of-school-time pipeline into teaching to diversify the teacher workforce¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The brief proposes recruiting and preparing staff with experience in after-school or out-of-school time (OST) programs to enter teaching, calling this an "underutilized strategy" and a "potentially high-leverage pipeline of teachers of color." It details three moves: allocate resources for recruitment relationships between nontraditional preparation programs and OST providers; increase scholarships, loan forgiveness, and tuition reimbursements for candidates with OST experience, focused on candidates of color and GYO participants; and adopt statewide guidelines and supports for nontraditional pathways including licensure test preparation and at least one year of mentor-teacher support and coaching before participants become teachers of record.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • States must both increase targeted recruitment of after-school/OST staff and structure preparation programming to draw on this group's experiences while enrolled

Constraints¶

  • North Carolina currently lacks several supporting conditions: no targeted scholarships or loan forgiveness, and no program approval standards compelling programs to recruit and graduate candidates of color

Target Learners¶

  • after-school and OST program staff
  • teacher assistants and paraprofessionals
  • candidates of color entering teaching

Target Learning Goals¶

  • increasing the racial diversity of the teacher workforce
  • supporting nontraditional entry into teaching

Affordances¶

  • Six Strategy Educator Diversity Policy Framework

Related Strategies¶

  • Grow-your-own and recruitment initiatives: alternative programs, Minority Serving Institutions, high-school pipeline programs, and district human-capital efforts to diversify the teacher workforce.
