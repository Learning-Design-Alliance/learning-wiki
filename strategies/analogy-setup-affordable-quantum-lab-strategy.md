# Use an analogy setup with simulated quantum measurements to make a quantum student lab affordable, quick, robust and portable

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends an analogy experiment design for student laboratory courses: single-photon sources are replaced by diode lasers producing short light pulses, and quantum measurement effects are simulated by built-in electronics that randomly assign detector clicks. The authors state "the absence of single-photon sources makes the setup considerably more affordable, the assembly and alignment of the setup much quicker and easier, furthermore, the hardware is much more robust and portable". The simpler setup also lets students concentrate on the experiment's objectives without lengthy light-source adjustments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An electronic module simulating quantum measurement randomness in the detection apparatus
#### Constraints
- Quantum measurement effects are simulated rather than genuinely quantum; the setup demonstrates the protocol rather than single-photon physics

### Target Learners
- university students in physics lab courses

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding quantum key distribution and its underlying quantum features
