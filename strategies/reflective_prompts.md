# Reflective Prompts

## Description
Reflection prompts encourage learners to think deeply about their learning experiences. These prompts can be used at the end of a class period or when a large piece of work is submitted. Prompts include sharing one thing learned, posing a question for future investigation, responding with a single word, analyzing what worked and what didn’t, identifying a point of pride in their work, and suggesting how they would approach the task differently next time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time allocated at the end of a lesson or upon submission of a major assignment. A list of reflection prompts.
#### Constraints
- May feel repetitive if used too frequently without variation. Requires a classroom culture that values reflection and self-assessment.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote deeper engagement with complex ideas, allow more students to find broader meaning in their work, help teachers to more deeply understand and adapt to the intellectual processes of our students

### Affordances
- Provides structured opportunities for reflection, promotes metacognition, helps students connect learning to personal experiences, and gives teachers insights into student thinking.

### Personalization
- Prompts can be chosen based on individual student needs or learning goals. Adaptations can be made for verbal, written, or digital formats. Sentence stems may be provided to scaffold responses.
