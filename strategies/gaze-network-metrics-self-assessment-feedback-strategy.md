# Use gaze-based network metrics for real-time self-assessment feedback and personalized learning interventions in visual tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend adapting the correlated gaze-based network metrics to actively intervene in students' learning: without external assessments, "these metrics could provide self-assessment feedback to students during the learning process", and at different learning stages they could track progress and enable personalized interventions. Instructors can also use the metrics as complementary evidence invisible to traditional tests. The approach is presented as more transparent and computationally lighter than deep-learning alternatives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- High-quality gaze data collected through non-intrusive and distraction-free methods, which the authors note is currently lacking
#### Constraints
- Integration of gaze-driven adaptive interventions into real-time systems remains in its early stages

### Target Learners
- students in domain-specific visual learning tasks, such as dentistry students reading radiographs

### Target Learning Goals
- visual expertise development and anomaly detection performance
