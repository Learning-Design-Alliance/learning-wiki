# Provide PEC training, ophthalmic equipment and government-supported integration of eye care into primary health care

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends strengthening primary eye care in eastern Nepal through professional training of government primary health care workers, provision of basic ophthalmic equipment, and greater government support for integrating eye care with the government health system. It states that "to improve PEC there needs to be greater government support for ophthalmic training and resource provision, and advocacy for the integration of PEC with existing primary health care". The recommendation rests on the study's findings of inadequate knowledge, missing equipment and stakeholder demand for training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- PHCWs willing and available to take on additional eye care training, as all surveyed PHCWs stated
#### Constraints
- The study was not statistically powered to compare the three regions, and the mountain region was under-represented, which may introduce selection bias

### Target Learners
- government-employed primary health care workers (nurses, health assistants, auxiliary health workers, community medical assistants, auxiliary nurse midwives, doctors) in low-income settings

### Target Learning Goals
- recognise, manage and urgently refer common eye conditions including corneal ulcer, conjunctivitis, cataract and ophthalmia neonatorum
- measure visual acuity
