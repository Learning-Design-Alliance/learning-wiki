# Use per-student misconception scores from research-designed multiple-choice instruments for class-level formative assessment and intervention design

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents a method for computing "misconception scores" for each student from the discovered distractor loadings, then tracking prevalence of each misconception before and after instruction by ability band. The authors state their work should "serve as a guide for developing, evaluating, and improving specific interventions for each of these while providing Physics instructors with a new and useful tool for class-level formative assessment." This lets instructors see which misconceptions their class holds and how instruction shifts them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre- and post-instruction administrations of a research-designed multiple-choice instrument such as the FCI
- The fitted MNCM-Bayes model and robust distractor vectors with their loadings
#### Constraints
- The article notes many misconceptions are poorly remediated for students of average or lower ability, so score changes should be interpreted per misconception rather than as overall progress

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students at high school and university level

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnosing and remediating force-and-motion misconceptions; evaluating instruction per misconception
