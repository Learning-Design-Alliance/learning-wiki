# Separate numerical scores from narrative feedback and enhance feedback specificity to mitigate outcome bias

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that supervisors assessing residents' clinical reasoning separate the numerical score from the narrative feedback, since outcome knowledge biases scores but also enriches specific process feedback. They further recommend enhancing specificity in all situations, especially positive feedback for favorable outcomes, and using linguistic politeness strategies to build a trustworthy environment. As printed: "separating the numerical score from the narrative feedback could enhance the effectiveness of the assessment process."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supervisors must be aware that narrative feedback may offer more learning opportunities than numerical scores
- A trustworthy feedback environment, particularly when recipients are in a prevention-focused mindset
#### Constraints
- Cautioning reviewers against outcome influence has proven ineffective; complete withholding of outcome is neither feasible nor desirable in daily practice

### Target Learners
- general practice residents

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- diagnostic decision-making
- responsiveness to feedback
