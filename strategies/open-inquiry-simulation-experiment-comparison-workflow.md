# Open-inquiry lab workflow in which students design experiments, build simulations, compare them, and iterate

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article prescribes a recurring student workflow across its modules: "Develop their own experiments to address a specified phenomenon", "Develop (or adjust) computer simulations", "Compare the results of experiments with the ones produced by computer simulations", redesign as needed, and report results. Students choose equipment, may build their own devices, and must decide how to evaluate errors and how to compare simulation to experiment, making each lab a small research project.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must be willing to learn and practice Wolfram Mathematica to guide, assist, and evaluate student work
- Students need prior experience handling open-ended tasks, designing experiments, and analyzing errors
#### Constraints
- The instructor "should not do the work for students" and must give them freedom and challenge to explore

### Target Learners
- advanced high school physics students
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental design
- computational modeling
- error analysis
- comparing models with empirical data
