# Self-directed exploration of common concrete artefacts in ESL multimedia lessons for preschoolers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's learning procedure made exploration the core activity: "The whole learning process was designed to be self-directed." Pairs of subjects freely explored randomly-placed artefacts, paced their own lessons, and triggered content by grasping, pointing or gesturing, with topics set in advance but no explicit direction on which artefact to explore when. Artefacts were common, concrete items (office, food, fire, vehicles, gardening) chosen because they are observable near school and representable in digital multimedia, with no abstract concepts introduced.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Common physical artefacts within a 15-minute walk from school; individual artefacts rather than collections; simple one-dimensional tasks suited to the pre-operational stage
#### Constraints
- Formative feedback was withheld during the study for fear of interfering with results

### Target Learners
- preschoolers aged five to six, non- or limited-English-speaking

### Target Learning Goals
- ESL vocabulary and real-life knowledge building
