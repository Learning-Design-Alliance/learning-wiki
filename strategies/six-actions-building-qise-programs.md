# Six program-building actions: gathering subject-matter experts, institutional commitment, industry relationships, laboratory resources, assessment mechanisms, and faculty cluster hires

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article distills workshop consensus into concrete steps for launching interdisciplinary QISE programs. As printed, "a successful program would greatly benefit from having taken many of the following actions and possessing a clear path to address those that are missing." Specific recipes include including pedagogy experts from the beginning, assigning multiple faculty per course, leveraging industry access such as "cloud quantum computing time not available to the public," incorporating internships and industry committee members, and cluster hires spanning several departments. The article cautions the steps are not a prescription and none is necessary or sufficient alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Strong support and buy-in from all involved departments
- Commitment to administering assessments developed by discipline-based education researchers
- Mechanisms ensuring coordination and consultation between departments during cluster hiring
#### Constraints
- The article states not all actions are necessary nor sufficient to guarantee success, and every university will develop its program differently

### Target Learners
- post-secondary students in new interdisciplinary QISE degree programs

### Target Learning Goals
- relevant, industry-aligned, well-assessed quantum education programs
