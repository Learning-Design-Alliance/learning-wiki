# Model-Eliciting Activities (MEAs)

## Description
Model-eliciting activities (MEAs) are activities that encourage learners to invent and test models. They are posed as open-ended problems that are designed to challenge learners to build models in order to solve complex, real-world problems. The learner products provide windows into the thinking processes they used to develop the solutions. MEAs look like authentic, real-world statistical problems and require learners to work in teams to generate solutions via written descriptions, explanations, and constructions. In this way, MEAs are thought-revealing as the learners' products provide windows into the thinking processes they used to develop the solutions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires careful planning and preparation, access to resources and materials, and a supportive learning environment. Instructors need to facilitate and guide learners through the activity, providing support and feedback as needed.
#### Constraints
- Can be time-consuming and resource-intensive. May require significant upfront planning and preparation. Learners may struggle with self-direction and time management.

### Affordances
- Provides learners with a way to build models to solve complex, real-world problems. Learners see the relevance of the learning, as MEAs help them develop skills for living in a knowledge-based, highly technological society.

### Personalization
- MEAs can be designed to align with learners' individual interests, needs, and learning styles. Flexible project topics and assessment methods allow for personalized learning experiences.

## Assessment Evidence
- Assess the quality of their final product, and evaluate their ability to apply knowledge and skills to real-world problems. Measure gains in content knowledge, self-confidence, collaboration, and engagement through pre- and post-assessments.

## Impact
- Leads to increased learner engagement, improved understanding of math concepts, enhanced confidence in math skills, and promotion of transfer of learning to real-world situations. Increased learner outcomes and increased motivation. Increased ability to apply knowledge and skills to real-world problems.
