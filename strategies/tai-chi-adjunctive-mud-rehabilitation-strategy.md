# Use 12-week moderate-intensity 24-form Yang-style Tai Chi as a non-pharmacological adjunctive intervention in MUD rehabilitation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests Tai Chi warrants further investigation as a potential non-pharmacological adjunctive intervention that could complement standard rehabilitation programs. The implemented protocol was 60-minute sessions of 24-form Yang-style Tai Chi three times a week for 12 weeks at moderate intensity (65% to 75% of HRmax), led by a professional martial arts coach, with step-by-step instruction progressing from basic postures to the complete sequence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sessions led and overseen by a professional coach; heart-rate monitoring to maintain moderate intensity; participants medically cleared for moderate aerobic exercise
#### Constraints
- Findings come from male-only samples in a compulsory isolation rehabilitation centre and may not translate to female patients; the active-control design cannot isolate Tai Chi-specific effects from general exercise benefits

### Target Learners
- men with methamphetamine use disorder in residential rehabilitation

### Target Learning Goals
- reduced attentional bias to drug cues
- attentional regulation
- cognitive recovery

### Affordances
- Hypofrontality Functional Recovery Tai Chi
