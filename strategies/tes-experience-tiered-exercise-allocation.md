# Differentiate the training program by surgeon experience level: synthetic exercises for young residents, biological exercises for senior groups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that biological-material exercises were too difficult for young residents, the authors recommend tailoring the program to three categories: "for category A, we recommend synthetic material exercises, for category B we believe that introduction of the biological material exercises is useful, while for category C the focus should be set on the biological material exercises." This keeps early training within reach while preserving the higher-fidelity biological tasks for more experienced trainees.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainers able to classify participants into experience categories (young residents, senior residents, specialists) and to supervise and score biological exercises against quality criteria
#### Constraints
- Derived from a pilot study with a relatively small number of participants, without randomization

### Target Learners
- young surgical residents (years 1-3)
- senior residents (years 4-6)
- specialist surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- progressive acquisition of transanal endoscopic surgery skills matched to prior experience
