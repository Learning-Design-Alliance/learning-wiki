# Brain Breaks

## Description
Brain breaks are short periods of time when learners change up the routine of incoming information. These breaks refresh thinking and help learners discover another solution to a problem or see a situation through a different lens. During these minutes, the brain moves away from learning, memorizing, and problem solving. The brain break helps to incubate and process new information.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires brief periods of time (e.g., 1-5 minutes). May use movement, games, or creative activities.
#### Constraints
- Some learners may find it difficult to transition back to learning. Requires consistent implementation to see benefits.

## Examples
- The Junk Bag: Pick any object out of the junk bag and ask students to come up with two ways this object could be reinvented for other uses. Squiggle Story: Draw one squiggly line. Give students one minute to stand and draw with their nondominant hand, turning the line into a picture or design of their choice. Opposite Sides: Have students stand and blink with the right eye while snapping the fingers of their left hand. Symbolic Alphabet: Sing the alphabet with names of objects rather than the letters. Other Languages: Teach sign language or make up a spoken language. Mental Math: Give a set of three instructions, counting the sequence to a partner for 30 seconds. Invisible Pictures: Have a student draw a picture in the air while their partner guesses what it is. Story Starters: Begin a story for one minute, either individually or with a partner. The students then complete or continue it with a silly ending. Rock Scissors Paper Math: With that call, students lay out one, two, three, or four fingers in the palm of their hand. The first player to say the correct sum of both players’ fingers wins.
