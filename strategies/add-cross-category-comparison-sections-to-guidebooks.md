# Add sections to instructional books showing exemplars from different categories simultaneously so readers can compare across categories

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that instructional books whose pictorial content is grouped by category (butterfly guides, bird guides, dermatology and radiology textbooks) add "an additional section devoted to simultaneous depictions of exemplars from different categories on the same page (or on consecutive pages) so that readers can conveniently compare across categories". This targets perceptual category learning while preserving topical organization. It follows from the finding that simultaneous different-category display matched interleaving.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- One pedagogical aim of the material must be perceptual category learning
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge topical organization may be unavoidable for a guidebook's appeal; the recommendation is an addition, not a replacement

### Target Learners
- readers of field guides and medical textbooks learning visual diagnostic or identification categories

### Target Learning Goals
- discrimination and identification of real-world perceptual categories (e.g. species, skin disorders)

### Affordances
- Discriminative Contrast Account Of Spacing In Induction
