# Refine program materials based on teacher process evaluation to reduce preparation burden and content density

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article shows that revisions informed by a prior process evaluation improved acceptability: preparation effort ratings improved (median 2 to 3) and supporting materials were rated more useful. Changes included calculating minimal vegetable quantities per classroom, reducing the variety of foods in meal-preparation lessons, and reducing content density while retaining behavioural focus and curriculum alignment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A prior acceptability evaluation identifying specific barriers (e.g., preparation effort, content density) to target in the revision
#### Constraints
- Preparation effort remained rated only neutrally after revision; the authors note that "short of providing pre-cut vegetables, it is unlikely that further improvements can be made" to the tasting element

### Target Learners
- Primary school teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Feasible implementation of classroom vegetable tasting and sensory education activities
