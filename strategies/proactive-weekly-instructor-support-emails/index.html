Provide proactive weekly instructor-initiated support emails to all online students without requiring them to ask for help¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q4 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Amplified assistance in U-Pace is "tailored assistance provided weekly by email to all students during the process of learning without them having to ask for help". The article motivates this with evidence that students most in need of help are unlikely to seek assistance, and that instructor support is important for motivation in online courses. Instructors base messages on engagement and performance data from the learning management system, combining instructional support, behavior-based praise, and belief-modifying elements.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructor time equivalent across conditions was logged in this study; instructors must invest time in preparing and sending messages
  • Access to learning management system engagement and performance data

Constraints¶

  • The article states future studies could examine whether amplified assistance integrated with concept mastery in a face-to-face classroom would produce comparable outcomes

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduates in online courses, including at-risk students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Student motivation and engagement
  • Content mastery
  • Academic success in gateway courses

Affordances¶

  • U Pace Instruction Model

Claims¶

  • Neither concept mastery alone nor amplified assistance alone produced greater learning than face-to-face instruction [-M]
  • Final grades of A or B did not differ between U-Pace and either single-component condition (concept mastery or amplified assistance) [~M]
  • U-Pace instruction produced greater learning on a proctored cumulative exam than concept mastery, amplified assistance, and face-to-face instruction in an RCT [+M]
  • At-risk students earned fewer A or B grades and lower cumulative exam scores than students not at risk, but at-risk U-Pace students outperformed at-risk students in all other conditions [+W]
