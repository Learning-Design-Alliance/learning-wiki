# Validate DTI findings with comparable survey conditions, an additional self-assessment instrument, and objective clinical reasoning tests

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors' concluding recommendations for future DTI research: surveys must be "conducted under comparable circumstances" with a higher rate of return; an additional inventory should investigate possible self-over- or under-assessments; and the DTI itself should be further validated against objective tests such as key feature, script concordance or situational judgement tests. These steps are proposed because the pilot study showed that drawing curriculum-effect inferences from measured DTI differences requires further steps beyond the present design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Surveys must be conducted under comparable circumstances across sites, with a higher rate of return
#### Constraints
- The pilot study's data are not necessarily representative of the respective cohorts; the traditional-course sample was small and showed great variance

### Target Learners
- medical students in clinical semesters

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- diagnostic thinking
