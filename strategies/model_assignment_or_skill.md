# Model Assignment or Skill

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Modeling a skill or assignment means the instructor (or a recorded expert) works through the task while verbalizing their thought processes — what they are doing, why, and how they monitor their own understanding. This makes normally invisible expert strategies observable and imitable, so learners become aware of the strategies available for mastering the skill and of how to self-monitor during execution.

## Design Implications

Modeling works because it converts tacit expert knowledge into explicit, observable steps, reducing the unguided search novices would otherwise undertake [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]. The verbalization is the active ingredient: silent demonstration of the end product teaches far less than narration of the decisions along the way. Modeling must be paired with independent practice, since observation alone produces fluency illusions rather than transferable skill [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A skilled practitioner who can genuinely articulate their reasoning, not just perform the skill ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md))
- A task representative of what learners will actually do, at an appropriate grain size (whole assignment, one section, or one move)
- A structured follow-on activity in which learners apply the modeled strategies ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)), ideally with [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) or feedback
- For recorded models: pause points or prompts that invite learners to predict the next step before it is revealed

#### Constraints
- Ineffective if learners lack the background knowledge to parse the modeled task — the model becomes noise rather than guidance [~M]
- Over-reliance on modeling without fading into independent practice hinders problem-solving development [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [-S]
- Learners systematically overestimate how much they learned from watching, so models should not substitute for doing
- For learners with strong prior knowledge, explicit modeling can be redundant and even depress performance [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Live vs. recorded:** live modeling allows responsive adjustment; recorded models allow rewatching and pausing
- **Full vs. partial models:** complete models suit novices; partial models with deliberate gaps invite learner contribution ([Fading](../elements/fading.md))
- **Positive vs. contrasting models:** modeling a flawed attempt alongside a correct one sharpens discrimination ([Non-Examples](../elements/non-examples.md))
- **Peer models:** a near-peer working through the task can be more relatable and signal that struggle is normal

### Target Learners
- Novices encountering a task type for the first time [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]
- Learners who need support with metacognition and strategy application — the verbalized self-monitoring gives them a script for their own thinking
- Less beneficial for experts or advanced learners, for whom the model adds redundant load [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural skill acquisition: learning how to execute a task, not just what the finished product looks like
- Metacognitive strategy learning: internalizing expert self-monitoring, checking, and revision moves
- Schema formation: building a mental model of the task's structure and decision points

### Instructions
1. Choose a representative task and identify the invisible decisions an expert makes while doing it.
2. Model the task, verbalizing reasoning and self-monitoring throughout ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)); include at least one moment of noticing and correcting an error, so learners see recovery, not just fluency.
3. Check understanding by asking learners to predict or justify the next step before it is revealed.
4. Move learners immediately into guided practice on an analogous task ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)), with [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) that references the modeled strategies.
5. Fade the support across successive tasks ([Fading](../elements/fading.md)) — from full model, to partial model, to independent work.

## Examples
- A writing instructor drafts a literature review paragraph live, verbalizing how she evaluates source credibility, decides what to paraphrase, and revises her topic sentence — then students draft their own paragraphs in class.
- [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org) — narrated step-by-step problem-solving videos model mathematical reasoning, followed by practice exercises with on-demand hints.
- A physics instructor models a problem, deliberately takes a wrong turn, notices the inconsistency, and repairs it — teaching error-monitoring alongside procedure.
- [Codecademy](https://www.codecademy.com) — annotated working code examples shown inline before learners write their own version.
