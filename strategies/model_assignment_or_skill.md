# Model Assignment or Skill

## Description
Modeling a skill or assignment by verbalizing thought processes while working through it. This allows learners to become aware of the strategies they can use to master a skill and monitor their understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a skilled practitioner or instructor who can articulate their thought processes clearly. Access to resources such as videos, examples, or demonstrations.
#### Constraints
- May not be effective if the learner does not have the necessary background knowledge to understand the modeled task. Over-reliance on modeling without independent practice may hinder the development of problem-solving skills.

### Target Learners

- All learner levels, particularly beneficial for learners needing support with metacognition and strategy application

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Increase learner awareness of problem-solving strategies, improve self-monitoring skills, support conceptual development

### Affordances
- Provides a clear, step-by-step example of how to approach a task. Makes internal thought processes visible, promoting self-awareness and metacognition. Can be revisited and re-analyzed for deeper understanding.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted to different skill levels by varying the complexity of the modeled task. Models can be provided in various formats, such as video, live demonstration, or written examples.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learner's application of modeled strategies in independent practice. Assess the learner's ability to articulate their own thought processes when solving similar problems. Track the learner's progress in mastering the modeled skill.

## Impact
- Modeling supports conceptual development and strategy acquisition. Research suggests that explicit modeling combined with think-alouds enhances learner's problem-solving abilities and metacognitive skills.
