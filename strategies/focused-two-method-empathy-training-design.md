# Design empathy training as focused two-method combinations (didactic plus rehearsal or reflection) rather than stacking all five methods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that program developers focus on the combinations the NMA identified as most effective rather than relying on common practice: "our study shows that NMA can be an insightful tool for identifying effective interventions, thus providing some direction of what key ingredients program developers and educators should focus on, rather than solely relying on what is common practice". Since feedback appeared in four of the five most frequent interventions but was absent from three of the five significant combinations, dropping feedback may also be more cost-effective. The article concludes interventions "need not be expensive nor prolonged to be effective".
