# Use group discussions and student presentations to quality-control students' physical intuitions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because intuitions act below consciousness, the chapter argues teachers cannot directly observe whether students are forming useful or faulty concepts. It recommends that students justify their intuitions by explaining them to others and giving convincing reasons, for example through group discussions or individual student presentations, mirroring the challenges that scientific progress requires. The author notes we assess mathematical facility with written tests, but intuition needs this social form of defence and challenge instead.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must attend to the soundness of the intuitions students develop, since faulty concepts risk going undetected
#### Constraints
- The chapter warns that without this quality control teachers risk leaving students with faulty concepts

### Target Learners
- school students
- university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- developing sound scientific intuitions
- learning to justify and defend scientific reasoning
