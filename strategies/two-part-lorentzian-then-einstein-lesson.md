# Two-part lesson: Lorentzian derivation of the transformations first, then Einstein's kinematic reinterpretation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article prescribes a concrete teaching sequence: "The lesson should consist of two main parts." In the first part the preferred frame is defined only as the frame where Maxwell equations are valid, ether as a material medium is omitted, and the Lorentz transformations are derived from Lorentz-Fitzgerald contraction and Larmor dilation using two light clocks separated after synchronization. In the second part, arguments for Einstein's reinterpretation of the transformations and its advantages are discussed, including the inverse transformation and the conventionality of distant simultaneity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A derivation showing that clocks separated after synchronization in the moving frame display a reading difference that compensates the light travel-time difference, using the light-clock pedagogical trick of Appendix 2.
#### Constraints
- The authors state neither time dilation nor length contraction can be presented as symmetric phenomena at the beginning, since that symmetry is as counterintuitive as Einstein's light postulate; symmetry is restored only when relativity of simultaneity is introduced.

### Target Learners
- university physics students in introductory relativity courses

### Target Learning Goals
- deriving and conceptually understanding the Lorentz transformations and relativity of simultaneity
