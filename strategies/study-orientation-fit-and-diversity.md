# Consider both interest fit and interest diversity during study orientation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that program interest diversity explained far more variance in study results than individual student-program fit, the authors draw a policy implication: "Educational policy makers should therefore be aware of the importance of both interest fit and interest diversity during the process of study orientation." The strategy is to use RIASEC-based orientation tools that inform students not only about their personal fit with a program but also about the interest composition of the program's student population, since different programs appear to reward different interest patterns.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Orientation instruments must measure student RIASEC profiles and program environment profiles on a commensurate scale, and program-level interest diversity must be computable from student deviance scores.
#### Constraints
- The evidence is correlational and program-level in one open-access institution; the authors note the strong-environment prediction was made specifically for open access settings with low interest diversity.

### Target Learners
- prospective higher education students choosing among open-access study programs

### Target Learning Goals
- informed study program choice and study orientation

### Affordances
- Homogeneity Theory Interest Diversity
