# Schedule periodic retrieval practice of previously acquired knowledge at intervals of about two months or longer

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 7 studies (4 causal, 3 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q4` · 2 of 7 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that curricula build in periodic retrieval practice of previously learned content, because widely spaced retrievals extend the accessibility of knowledge at low cost relative to acquisition. It reports that "intervals of 2 months, or possibly longer, enhance long-term retention even for content that is not consistently retrieved at the end of training". The strategy applies to academic knowledge whose long-term maintenance matters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must previously acquire the knowledge base being maintained; retrieval sessions must be scheduled at long intervals rather than massed.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the benefits' extension beyond the 5-year observation period and to more complex, integrative content than word pairs remains to be established.

### Target Learners
- students maintaining academic knowledge such as foreign language vocabulary

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term maintenance and accessibility of previously acquired knowledge
