# Focus a resident's first root-cause analysis on a near-miss event rather than a reportable incident

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because reportable medical incidents are rare in the clinic, the program's RCA exercise usually targets a potentially serious near-miss event drawn from the departmental incident learning system. The authors state: "Our experience with this approach has been positive, and we recommend that at least the first experience of a resident with RCA be focused on a near‐miss event." This lets residents analyze a case without the time pressures of a reportable incident. The RCA expectation is decoupled from the one-month rotation since worthy events may not arise during that period.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a departmental near-miss incident learning system provides the cases; the resident attends weekly safety committee meetings where near-misses are discussed.
#### Constraints
- Programs without a departmental incident learning system may review published case studies or publicly available RO-ILS cases instead, which the authors expect to be less rich.

### Target Learners
- medical physics residents
- physician residents

### Target Learning Goals
- root-cause analysis of safety events
- incident learning
