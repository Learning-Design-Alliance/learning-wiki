Match the writing technique to the classroom's dominant personality traits¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's suggestions advise teachers to consider learner personality when choosing a writing technique: "Teacher should realized that the students' characteristics such as their personal traits before choosing the teaching techniques." It specifically recommends Roundtable "for a classroom dominated by students with introvert personal traits," and Clustering where extroverts dominate, because the Tukey results showed each trait group achieved more under its matched technique.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • knowledge of students' personal traits, gathered in the study via a personal-traits questionnaire administered before and after treatments

Constraints¶

  • the matching pattern was established in one 2x2 factorial study of two intact grade-nine classes in descriptive writing

Target Learners¶

  • junior high school students with introvert or extrovert personal traits

Target Learning Goals¶

  • descriptive writing achievement

Claims¶

  • Students with introvert personal traits achieved higher descriptive-writing scores than students with extrovert personal traits [+W]
  • Teaching technique and personal traits interacted significantly in their effect on descriptive-writing achievement [+W]
  • Introvert students achieved more under Roundtable while extrovert students achieved more under Clustering [+W]
