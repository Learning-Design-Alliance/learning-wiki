Implement STAD cooperative learning as blended learning combining web-based and text-based forms with the lecturer present¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends implementing STAD-type cooperative instruction with web-based and text-based forms used simultaneously and complementarily, because each form excelled on a different outcome aspect: web-based on cognitive results and text-based on affective results. The authors state that "Optimal learning result can be achieved by using internet as learning instrument, collaborating with conventional learning together" in blended or hybrid learning. They also caution that lecturers remain necessary to confirm learning results.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Lecturers must remain present to give confirmation of learning results, since web-based instruments cannot fully replace them

Constraints¶

  • Findings come from one quasi-experimental study of 67 undergraduates in a single Organizational Behavior course at STIKOM Surabaya

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate Information Systems students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • cognitive mastery of Organizational Behavior topics
  • affective attitudes and cooperative behaviors

Claims¶

  • The web-based and text-based STAD groups did not differ significantly on the pretest [+W]
  • Text-based STAD cooperative learning produces better affective learning outcomes than web-based STAD [+W]
  • Web-based STAD cooperative learning produces significantly higher cognitive study outcomes than text-based STAD in an Organizational Behavior course [+W]
  • Learning strategy and achiever motivation interact significantly on cognitive study outcomes [~W]
