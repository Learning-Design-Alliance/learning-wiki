# Use simulated ward rounds with standardized patients to teach ward round competences to students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structured instruction of ward rounds in undergraduate education, including simulation with standardized patients, to prepare students for clinical routine. Cited work shows simulation "provides students the opportunity to reflect their performance with the help of peers, tutors or simulated patients". Students can also train to cope with faults and disruptions and understand other participants' perspectives by playing the role of the patient or nurse.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured instructions of conducting ward rounds in undergraduate education give medical students the chance to get ready for the clinical daily routine
#### Constraints
- The wards depicted in the interviews represent only the status quo of actual ward round practice in Germany, and have to be proven for general validity

### Target Learners
- medical students
- junior clinicians

### Target Learning Goals
- ward round competences
- coping with faults and disruptions
- perspective taking of other ward round participants
