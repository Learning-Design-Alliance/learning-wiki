# Use unsupervised clustering of student written responses to surface class-level themes and track how ideas change over time

## Description
The article recommends that educators and researchers apply this unsupervised clustering method to large datasets of student written responses to "understand the general ideas expressed by students as a class" and to track how those ideas change from week to week or semester to semester. It is positioned as a rapid, exploratory sense-making tool rather than a grading method, revealing patterns humans may miss.
