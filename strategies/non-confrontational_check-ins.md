# Non-Confrontational Check-ins

## Description
Non-confrontational check-ins involve engaging students in informal, positive interactions before they enter the classroom to socialize them into productive behaviors. This can be done by using "nonconfrontational statements" and "informal chitchat" at the door to address potential issues proactively. This approach helps to set a positive tone for the lesson and prevent disruptive behaviors before they start. By connecting with students individually and addressing any concerns or issues before they enter the classroom, teachers can create a more positive and focused learning environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires teachers to be proactive and engaged at the classroom door. Teachers need to be able to build rapport with students and address issues in a non-confrontational manner.
#### Constraints
- May be time-consuming, especially with larger classes. Requires teachers to be skilled in building relationships and addressing issues proactively. Some students may be resistant to this approach.

## Examples
- As students enter the classroom, a middle school teacher greets each one by name and asks a simple question, such as 'How was your lunch?' or 'Are you ready for today's lesson?' If a student seems upset, the teacher might say, 'Is everything okay? You seem a little down today.' This approach helps to build a connection with students and address any potential issues before they disrupt the class.
