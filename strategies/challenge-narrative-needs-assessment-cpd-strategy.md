# Use open-ended survey questions about challenges encountered in practice, analyzed by qualitative content analysis, as a CPD needs-assessment approach for intrinsic competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy asks physicians open-ended questions such as "Describe three challenging situations encountered in the past 12 months" and codes the narratives to competency frameworks via qualitative content analysis, rather than relying on quantitative self-assessment of needs. The authors used it with 411 specialists and found challenge narratives surfaced roles like Communicator and Health Advocate that were rare in perceived needs. They position it as "an innovative way to develop educational content that meets the learning needs of physicians".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An online or written survey channel to collect open-ended narrative responses from practicing clinicians
- Coders able to conduct thematic content analysis with inter-rater agreement checks
#### Constraints
- The study found generalizability limited because data were predominantly from one region and an academic medical center
- More research is needed to warrant devoting entire CPD activities to single challenge themes; the authors suggest modifying existing activities instead

### Target Learners
- Practicing medical and surgical specialists in continuing professional development

### Target Learning Goals
- Identifying learning needs for intrinsic competencies such as communication, collaboration, health advocacy, leadership, scholarship and professionalism
