Design CSCL environments for older learners that support memory reactivation as well as constructive and interactive engagement¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that older adults engaged interactively but needed to reactivate memory before elaborating, the article states that "older adults may require the reactivation of memory to engage in elaboration" and that the results "offer insights into designing effective CSCL environments tailored to the learning needs of older adults." The strategy is to pair concept-map-based collaboration with support for recall of studied material, since older adults' interactive episodes repeatedly involved re-reading the text.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Aquisition of the studied text during collaboration, low-technology-familiarity surfaces, and tasks whose structure permits elaboration rather than only retrieval

Constraints¶

  • The article notes older adults' unfamiliarity with technology may limit benefits, and cognitive flexibility declines with age, posing challenges for collaboration

Target Learners¶

  • older adult learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • elaboration and integration of new conceptual knowledge during collaboration

Claims¶

  • Age groups differ in ICAP engagement distribution: younger adults engage more in active mode, older adults more in constructive and interactive modes, during collaborative concept-map learning [+M]
  • ENA reveals different utterance co-occurrence structures by age: older adults show a stronger active–interactive connection while younger adults' constructive–interactive connection is not statistically confirmed [+M]
