# Use a computer-based format for mini-CEX feedback provision to speed feedback completion in busy clinical settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy shifts the mini-CEX and its written feedback from paper to a computer-based format, on the finding that "a computer-based format facilitates the provision of feedback in a mini-CEX evaluation across EPs". Because typing is faster than handwriting for most people, the authors argue the format favors faster feedback provision and has "obvious benefits in a busy ED setting", possibly extending to mini-CEX assessments in other disciplines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to computers running the ED teaching support software at the point of assessment, as in the study where the assessor and trainee sat in front of a computer immediately after evaluating the patient
- Content identical to the paper format, as it was in the study
#### Constraints
- The facilitating effect was uneven across junior and senior assessors; the authors propose some barriers to usage among senior assessors may exist
- The study compared paper versus computer within emergency physicians only; trauma surgeons remained on paper due to lack of funding

### Target Learners
- PGY1 residents in emergency department rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- Provision of complete written feedback (positive feedback, suggestions for development, agreed action plan) during workplace assessment
