# Interview students rather than experts to ground survey language in student interpretations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that surveys are traditionally designed around expert ideas, but students are not yet experts, so survey developers should interview students about what practices mean to them. The authors "conducted semi-structured interviews with nine students from an introductory algebra-based physics lab for non-majors and six students in upper-division physics labs," asking participants to define each practice, describe it in their course, and say whether they found it valuable. This mapped the breadth of interpretations before writing closed-response questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interview questions must ask students to define each practice and describe what it looks like in their course, then whether they find it valuable
#### Constraints
- Interpretations of a practice such as analyzing data can differ significantly between students, so language must be specific

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics laboratory students across course levels

### Target Learning Goals
- ensuring survey questions about physics practices are interpreted correctly by students
