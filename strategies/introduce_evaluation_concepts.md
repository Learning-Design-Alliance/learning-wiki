# Introduce Evaluation Concepts

## Description
Introduce concepts like “relevance, accuracy, bias, and reliability” and using these terms regularly as a part of classroom discourse can help students get used to questioning sources and the motivations authors may have.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit teaching of the terms, integration into classroom discussions, and examples of sources to analyze.
#### Constraints
- Learners may initially struggle to apply these concepts effectively. Requires ongoing reinforcement and practice.

### Target Learners

- Middle and high school students

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Familiarize learners with key concepts for evaluating sources, encourage critical questioning of sources, and understand author motivations.

### Affordances
- Provides a foundational understanding of source evaluation, encourages critical thinking, and helps learners recognize potential biases in information.

### Personalization
- The concepts can be adapted to different age groups and subject areas. Real-world examples relevant to learners' interests can be used.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learners' ability to define and apply the concepts in discussions and source analyses. Assess their ability to identify relevance, accuracy, bias, and reliability in various sources.

## Impact
- Improved critical thinking: By understanding these concepts, learners become more discerning consumers of information. Enhanced media literacy: Learners are better equipped to evaluate the credibility and reliability of sources.
