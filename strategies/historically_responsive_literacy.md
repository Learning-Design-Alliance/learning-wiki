# Historically Responsive Literacy

## Description
Historically Responsive Literacy (HRL) is a framework that seeks to cultivate literacy, intellect, and self-efficacy by connecting learning deeply to who we are and what agency we have in the world. It draws inspiration from 1830s-era literary societies and centers around the goals of identity development, skill development, intellectual development, criticality, and joy. HRL helps teachers reframe curriculum, objectives, goals, and standards around these elements, drawing upon Black history as a model for a more complete and comprehensive education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A commitment to culturally responsive teaching, knowledge of Black history and literature, and a willingness to challenge traditional educational practices.
#### Constraints
- May require significant curriculum redesign and professional development. Some educators may resist changes to traditional practices.
