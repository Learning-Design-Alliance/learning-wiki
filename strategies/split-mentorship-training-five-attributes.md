# Separate faculty and student mentorship training workshops emphasizing five core attributes of effective mentorship

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Spectrum developed and implemented a formal mentorship training in Fall 2023, funded by an AAS EPD mini-grant, split into two parts so faculty and students train separately "to encourage a safe space for growth without an intrinsic power dynamic." The workshops "emphasize the five core attributes of effective mentorship developed by Pfund et al. (2016): research, interpersonal, psycho-social/career, cultural responsibility, and sponsorship," presented alongside the barriers and how each attribute mitigates them. Training includes role-play case studies with debriefs, bystander intervention discussion, and AJEDI education resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regularly occurring meetings or sessions over time are more effective than one-time short training for building durable mentorship skills, per the article
#### Constraints
- The article notes implementing regular sessions requires a greatly reconfigured mentorship training, and that smaller steps are more likely to engage busy faculty and students where no prior training existed

### Target Learners
- faculty and senior career researcher mentors
- early career researcher and student mentors

### Target Learning Goals
- culturally responsible advocacy mentorship
- recognizing academic red flags
- effective communication in mentoring relationships
