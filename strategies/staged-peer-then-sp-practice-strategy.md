# Use a staged approach to simulated patient practice: start with peer role-play for comfort and feedback, then progress to standardised patients

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends sequencing clinical skills practice so students first work with peers, obtaining "informal feedback, share ideas and information", and later move to semi-formal standardised patient situations. This preserves the affordability of peer role-play while capturing the skills benefits of SPs, and addresses the financial barrier the authors identify as the main obstacle to SP use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A pool of trained standardised patients must be available for the later stage, with SPs attending preliminary training sessions on the examination procedures
#### Constraints
- SPs may not be used regularly "given the expense, time and resources"; the article notes the main barrier to SP use "remains the financial aspect"

### Target Learners
- health professional students (e.g., exercise physiology, medicine) with limited curricular time for musculoskeletal practice

### Target Learning Goals
- musculoskeletal assessment and rehabilitation clinical skills
- professional preparation for real patient encounters
