# Use simple classroom demonstrations (fan, light ball, spring scale) of a jet impinging on a curved surface to teach flow curvature, lift, turbulence and entrainment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends a low-cost demonstration setup in which a jet of air attaches to and deviates around a curved surface, with the reaction force measured on the solid. It states the Coandă effect "is an excellent paradigm, particularly in a classroom setup, to demonstrate by simple means (a fan, a light ball and a spring scale)" how flow curvature relates to lift forces perpendicular to the flow direction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A fan, a light ball and a spring scale, or alternatively a party balloon suspended by a thread near an air jet
#### Constraints
- The demonstration should not be presented as explaining airplane lift, since aircraft fly in extended flows, not narrow jets

### Target Learners
- physics students in classroom settings

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding lift forces
- distinguishing submerged jets from extended flows
- understanding entrainment and turbulence

### Affordances
- Lift Flow Deviation Jet Vs Extended Flow
