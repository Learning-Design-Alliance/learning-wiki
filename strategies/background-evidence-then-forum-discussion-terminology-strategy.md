# Use evidence-based background readings plus open discussion prompts to surface terminology debates in professional and public autism education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the MOOCs, Week 1 taught issues around language and terminology "by drawing upon findings from the Kenny et al. (2016) paper", then invited all learners — including professionals who may have been naive to the debate — to discuss which terms they use and how important the issue is. This let learners discover how each term may be perceived differently by the autistic community; the article reports learners commented on "becoming more aware of how identity-first language are preferred and perceived to be more respectful by the autistic community".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Provision of shared background evidence so all learners reach a common baseline knowledge before discussion
#### Constraints
- Analysis treated the dataset as a whole rather than splitting by learner group, so group-specific conclusions rest on post hoc endorsement counts

### Target Learners
- education and healthcare professionals
- family and friends of autistic individuals
- general public MOOC learners

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding stakeholder perspectives on autism language
- applying respectful, individualised terminology choices in practice
