# Tailor CME programs to physician subgroups and add interactive, competency-based formats and online self-assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the observed subgroup preferences, the authors recommend adapting CME/CPD programs to physician populations: "given female physicians' solid preferences for communication and interpersonal skills, CME programs could incorporate more case-based discussions, role-playing, and interactive workshops." They further recommend integrating core competencies such as patient-centred care, teamwork and evidence-based practice, competency-based training with real-life scenarios, patient simulations and feedback loops, strengthening CME for primary healthcare physicians, and "developing online self-assessment tools for CME/CPD competencies in everyday practice for physicians and program organisers."

### Context
#### Requirements
- Needs-assessment data on the target physician population to direct program development
#### Constraints
- The article notes self-assessed needs may not correlate with physicians' actual skill levels

### Target Learners
- physicians across institution types, sectors and career stages, with emphasis on primary healthcare physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- communication and interpersonal skills, patient-centred care, teamwork, evidence-based practice, and everyday-practice competency self-assessment
