# Use cluster analysis of student profiles to tailor teaching and enable early identification and support of at-risk students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose applying person-centered clustering as an ongoing educational practice: "Clustering medical students can help educators tailor teaching methods and materials to different learning profiles and identify clusters of students who may benefit from or struggle with specific curriculum modifications." They add that early assessment of performance predictors "could help identify patterns associated with students who may be at risk, allowing for early intervention and support."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collection of multiple learner variables (sociodemographics, entrance exam scores, baseline skills, terminology) at course start; sufficient sample size for clustering.
#### Constraints
- Profile stability over time is untested; the authors call for longitudinal follow-ups to determine whether profiles change.
- Findings are from one single-center course; replication at other institutions is needed.

### Target Learners
- Pre-clinical medical students, including those at risk of poor academic performance

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance in pre-clinical medical coursework
