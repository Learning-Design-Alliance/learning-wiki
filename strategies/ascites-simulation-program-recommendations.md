# Recommendations for ascites puncture simulation programs: standardized purpose-built models, longer evaluation horizons, and added patient-communication assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on its limitations, the study recommends implementing standardized simulation models specifically designed for ascites puncture, extending the evaluation period to assess long-term performance, and incorporating assessments of patient communication and information skills to ensure comprehensive evaluation of learners' preparedness for real-world scenarios. The authors note their own centre lacked a dedicated paracentesis dummy and used a multitasking mannequin with a stoma orifice, which learners found lacking in realism.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to or development of a standardized, procedure-specific paracentesis simulator; an evaluation schedule extending beyond 3 months; communication-skills assessment instruments
#### Constraints
- In resource-constrained settings a dedicated model may be unavailable, as in this study, where an adapted multitasking mannequin reduced fidelity

### Target Learners
- medical students in procedural clerkships, especially in resource-constrained settings

### Target Learning Goals
- safe and complete performance of ascites puncture including patient communication
