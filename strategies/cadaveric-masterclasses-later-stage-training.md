# Reserve cadaveric simulation for later-stage masterclasses rather than novice training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that cadaveric simulation, though highly rated by participants, showed no OR performance benefit for novices and was not cost-effective for a single procedure. It recommends that "live animal or cadaveric simulation can take place at the later stages of training in the form of “masterclasses” alongside other procedures to make full use of them, and benefit from anatomical variation."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cadaveric material with cost, licencing and availability addressed
- Later-stage trainees rather than novices
#### Constraints
- Cadavers may not be suitable for training at the novice phase and are not cost-effective in isolated use for a single procedure

### Target Learners
- Advanced surgical trainees beyond the novice phase

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural skills with exposure to anatomical variation
