# Include simulation training with practice-and-feedback cycles in surgical fellowship curricula for uncommon procedures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend including inanimate simulation training in fellowship curricula for techniques fellows rarely perform in the operating room, because cycles of practice and feedback aid skill acquisition. They state that inclusion of simulation training in the curriculum of fellows "can contribute to shortening of the time required for negotiating the learning curve for the PJ technique in the actual operating room."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Objective assessment tool (OSATS) and supervisor feedback to each trainee
- Repeated drill sessions over time
#### Constraints
- Whether improved quasi-surgical skills on an inanimate model link directly to real intraoperative performance remains unclear per the authors

### Target Learners
- surgical fellows training in low-volume complex procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- acquiring technical proficiency in complex anastomotic techniques
