# Turn physics-simulation violations in games into teachable moments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because game physics engines have flaws, students can hunt for violations such as characters standing on thin air, bodies sinking into floors, or momentum non-conservation when characters are shot. The activity asks that "Students spot violations of physics and discuss why such eﬀects do not occur in the real world." The authors state this could enable students to better appreciate the profundity of physical laws, especially when contrasted with real-world laboratory experiments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to game scenes containing detectable simulation errors, and discussion of the algorithms (e.g., collision detection) that produce them
#### Constraints
- Violations arise from computational shortcuts such as motion-capture blends with ragdoll physics, so explanations must address the game's algorithms rather than physics alone

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- normal forces, momentum conservation, distinction between physical law and computational approximation
