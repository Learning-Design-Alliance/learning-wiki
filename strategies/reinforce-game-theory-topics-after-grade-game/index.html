Follow the Grade Game with reinforcement of interdependence, dominant strategy, and Nash Equilibrium¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that instructors using the Grade Game as an active learning exercise follow it with a discussion reinforcing the game theory concepts it embodies. The author states that "An instructor who uses the Grade Game in class may wish to follow up with a reinforcement of the above topics (interdependence, dominant strategy, Nash Equilibrium) and how they relate to the topic of oligopoly" to support student understanding and retention. In the article's own implementation, a discussion of dominant strategies and the Nash Equilibrium is held after the Game is played and results are announced.

Requirements¶

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate economics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding and retention of dominant strategy, Nash Equilibrium, and their relation to oligopoly

Claims¶

  • Regression predicts students choosing to Cooperate score 9.3 points lower overall in the course, significant at the 5% level [+W]
  • Regression predicts Cooperators score 6.7 points lower on the in-class exam, a negative but unemphasized relationship [~W]
  • The exam-score difference between Cooperators and Non-cooperators was significant only at the 10% level, not the 5% level [~W]
  • Non-cooperators were far less likely to score below 70% overall in the course (9.1% vs 38.8% of Cooperators) [+W]
