# Embed the tool in the subject's workflow and design feed-forward prompts, symbolic-answer handling, and progress tracking before full release

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors distil six transferable recommendations for educators adopting the approach: "Keep the human in the loop at solution verification, not real-time grading"; design feedback prompts explicitly around feed-forward, locating the error and guiding next steps rather than revealing the answer; invest early in symbolic-answer entry, preview, and algebraic-equivalence handling and pilot with a small group; provide clear progress tracking and topic or question-type selection; embed the tool in the subject's workflow, for example by linking it to specific topics or low-stakes checkpoints, rather than releasing it as a standalone optional resource; and be transparent that the AI can hallucinate while solutions are human-verified.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feed-forward prompt design is described as the hardest behaviour to deliver consistently
- Symbolic and notation-heavy answers need careful input handling
#### Constraints
- Voluntary, unweighted activities attract uneven engagement; generating feedback for every submission carries time and compute costs students notice

### Target Learners
- engineering educators teaching large mathematical subjects

### Target Learning Goals
- trustworthy scalable formative practice
- student-directed practice and calibrated trust in AI feedback
