# Apply Youth Participatory Action Research principles to school-based citizen science design to support student agency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The commentary argues instructors and designers of CCS in schools could look to the practices and principles of YPAR to enhance meaningful learning. It cites Anyon et al.'s (2018) systematic review's three core principles: YPAR "is grounded in youths' lived experiences and concerns", "is participatory such that youth are collaborators in the methodological and pedagogical processes", and "is transformative, where youth design interventions to improve lives and communities." Applying these principles might help teachers support students' agency over their own learning and identities with science.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students investigate questions that matter to them that could result in transformational change, and truly partner in research decision-making
#### Constraints
- The commentary states adhering to all these YPAR principles in school contexts is incredibly time-consuming and difficult

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in school-based CCS projects

### Target Learning Goals
- student agency over learning
- identity development with science
- mutualism amongst scientists, students, and local communities
