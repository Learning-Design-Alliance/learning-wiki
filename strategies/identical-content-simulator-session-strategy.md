# Replace or supplement a lecture with a short high-fidelity simulator session of identical content and duration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's design pairs a 30-min traditional course with a 30-min simulator-based course containing "the same pedagogical content", adding individual hands-on manipulation. This keeps teaching time and content constant between formats, isolating the effect of the simulator modality, and produced higher scores and satisfaction in the randomized comparison.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identical theoretical content and session duration across formats
- Small sessions (8-10 students) so each learner can manipulate the simulator
- An external, blinded evaluator for the assessment
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only immediately after teaching; medium- and long-term retention untested
- High-fidelity simulators are expensive; low-fidelity alternatives were not tested

### Target Learners
- medical students in preclinical or early clinical years

### Target Learning Goals
- anatomical knowledge
- spatial understanding of angiographic views
- interpretation of clinical angiographic cases
