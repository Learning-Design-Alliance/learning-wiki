Institutions should respond to generative AI in education with guided critical engagement rather than prohibition¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The authors argue that because public sentiment remains positive while structural concerns grow, students and the public "are not rejecting AI but are looking for frameworks to engage with it responsibly." They recommend integrating AI into teaching practices and updating misconduct policies, developing critical digital literacy frameworks, and shaping engagement to foster critical thinking rather than restricting access.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutions must move beyond reactive governance and develop proactive frameworks equipping users to engage critically with new tools

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on observational sentiment analysis of one platform's discourse, not on tested institutional interventions

Target Learners¶

  • students in educational institutions using generative AI
  • educators and institutional policymakers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • responsible and ethical use of generative AI in learning
  • critical digital literacy regarding AI's ethical and societal dimensions

Related Strategies¶

  • Encourage students to use AI-generated responses while observing ethical guidelines
  • Design task-sensitive institutional AI policies rather than blanket permitted/prohibited distinctions
  • Embed GenAI literacy and ethical use directly in nursing curricula rather than prohibiting it
  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
  • Shift from punitive detection to proactive educative approaches, co-created detailed guidelines, and assessment designs GenAI cannot do well
  • Embed AI in guided accept/reject/justify workflows, teach digital critical thinking explicitly, and integrate institutional authorship standards
  • Integrate AI literacy instruction into study programs alongside responsible tool design
  • Transition from restrictive AI governance to frameworks that formally integrate operational and epistemic workflow competencies with subject-sensitive guidance
  • Embed structured AI literacy in business curricula: teach verification, comparison, and transparent AI-use declarations rather than restricting or unrestrictedly encouraging GenAI
  • Move beyond permit-or-restrict GenAI policies toward policies that clarify appropriate use, support equitable access, and extend rather than replace independent reasoning
  • Provide explicit pedagogical guidance on effective and ethical GenAI use within specific assessment contexts, beyond prohibitions
  • Design authentic, scaffolded activities that convert students' intuitive GenAI concerns into explicit ethical competencies
  • Build educator AI literacy and pedagogical competence alongside student critical engagement with AI-generated content
  • Develop student AI literacy and institutional GenAI policies, guidelines and safeguards for responsible integration
