# Track the specific questions that drive students into explanation-building and reuse them as discussion prompts, structuring the environment so students elicit and follow up on such questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers aiming to support sensemaking leverage recurring questions in the classroom. As printed: "one could keep track of which specific questions drive students towards this kind of explanation-building and try to use them as discussion prompts, and/or set the learning environment up so that students are encouraged to elicit and follow up on these types of questions". The intent is to elicit more rich sensemaking discussions by centering the vexing, mechanism-seeking questions students themselves articulate.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions must rest on a perceived gap or inconsistency in students' own understanding to function as sensemaking drivers
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that nudges are likely ineffective without an underlying vexing inconsistency in knowledge; in their case a specific interviewer nudge also helped sustain the discussion

### Target Learners
- physics instructors
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining sensemaking discussions
- explanation-building
