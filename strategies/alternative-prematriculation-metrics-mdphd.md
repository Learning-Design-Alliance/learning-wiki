# Seek alternative prematriculation metrics beyond MCAT and uGPA for selecting and ranking MD–PhD applicants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend moving beyond MCAT scores and uGPAs when evaluating MD–PhD applicants and programs, since these parameters were not predictive of key training outcomes in their data. They suggest prematriculation variables that may have a synergistic effect, including "scientific publications prior to MD–PhD program entry, length and depth of prematriculation research experiences, grants and fellowships received prior to matriculation, the quality of letters of recommendation, and undergraduate science awards". They also propose assessing leadership, teamwork, and perseverance, implemented at Vanderbilt via a challenge essay.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A follow-up study evaluating prematriculation and postmatriculation variables, which the authors report they have initiated, to establish predictive validity before adoption.
#### Constraints
- The evidence base is a single-institution retrospective study; the authors state the analysis may not be applicable to other MD–PhD programs and cannot be generalized beyond the MCAT score range of matriculants in their sample.

### Target Learners
- MD–PhD program applicants and matriculants

### Target Learning Goals
- Selecting applicants likely to succeed as physician-scientists
- Predicting training performance and research career outcomes
