# Weigh instructor workload trade-offs before widely implementing smartphone-based video feedback in nursing curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers and school administrators consider workload implications before broad adoption: reviewing videos and sending feedback may increase instructor workload, while reducing the need for face-to-face consultations may reduce workload in other aspects of teaching. These issues, the authors state, need to be further explored and considered before the method is widely implemented in nursing teaching curricula.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor time to review each student video (5 to 15 minutes per video in this study) and to send feedback
#### Constraints
- The article states these workload effects are unresolved and need further exploration; it does not quantify either direction of the workload change

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
