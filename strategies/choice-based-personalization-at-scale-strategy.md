# Implement choice-based personalization at scale by letting learners focus assignments on their own locale or background

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy personalizes MOOC assignments by having learners choose topics tied to their own characteristics — in this study, their geographic locale — instead of pre-selecting materials or running adaptive algorithms. The authors show it is "a low-cost manipulation that is based on the vast and diverse population of learners in many MOOCs", implemented at the instructional-design level rather than through continuous monitoring of learner performance. They argue the same approach extends to cultural background in arts and language courses, diverse societies in social science, and diverse habitats in life and earth sciences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must have diverse personal knowledge or attributes relevant to the course topic that they can draw upon
- Assignments must be structured so learners can search for and choose their own resources, with peer feedback and a rubric
#### Constraints
- The manipulation was tested in a climate MOOC where localization is fairly simple; other subject areas may have different considerations
- Analyses are based on fewer than 5% of registered learners and rely on self-reports

### Target Learners
- MOOC learners, a diverse remote and asynchronous population

### Target Learning Goals
- Increased engagement and activity in the course
- Perceived understanding of locally relevant applications of course content
