# Stakeholder action to strengthen reformed science teaching beliefs, with lessons built on questioning and peer discussion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that education stakeholders act on teachers' beliefs: "We recommend Turkish stakeholders in science education taking actions to improve Turkish perspective of science teachers about reformed science teaching and learning" while US stakeholders continue enhancing reformed philosophies. They further advise that "Teachers should design lessons in a way that encourages children to learn novel content through questioning instead of through a reading, watching a demonstration, or listening to a lecture" and give students opportunities to examine, discuss, and challenge ideas with peers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-service and pre-service teachers' ability to integrate reform beliefs into practice, which the article calls vital to realizing the reform movement's philosophy
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a small, non-randomly sampled comparative survey rather than an intervention test

### Target Learners
- science teachers in Turkey and the US
- K-12 science students

### Target Learning Goals
- reform-oriented (constructivist) science teaching and learning practices
