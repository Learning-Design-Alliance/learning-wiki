# Multi-stakeholder strategies for managing children's screen time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article compiles strategies from the literature and its own findings for managing children's screen time across educational systems, parents and public health initiatives. These include "Scheduled tech-free times", "Establishing clear screen time rules" with daily or weekly limits especially before bedtime, encouraging alternatives such as reading, sports and the arts, parental role modeling, awareness campaigns, community programs for outdoor play, screen time audits with feedback, and legislation regulating addictive content. The authors state these will benefit stakeholders managing the problem.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires collaboration among educational systems, parents, and public health initiatives, as the article states addressing excessive screen time necessitates a collaborative approach.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the strategies rest on cross-sectional associational findings; further research is needed to rule out factors such as sleep, parental practices, family connectedness and parental supervision.

### Target Learners
- school children aged approximately 8–14 years in grades 3 to 9

### Target Learning Goals
- supporting academic performance and mental health by balancing screen time with outdoor activity and academic engagement
