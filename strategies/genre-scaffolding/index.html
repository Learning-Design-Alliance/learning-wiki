Genre Scaffolding¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 9 studies (4 causal, 3 quant-synthesis, 2 review), q2–q4 · 3 of 9 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Genre scaffolding teaches learners the socially recognized forms of writing — recounts, arguments, lab reports, narratives — by making their stages, structural moves, and characteristic language explicit. Learners analyze mentor texts, co-construct a piece with the instructor, then write independently as support is progressively withdrawn. The approach treats text structure as learnable knowledge rather than something acquired only through wide reading.

Design Implications¶

Genre scaffolding reduces the cognitive burden of composing by giving learners a structural frame, so working memory is spent on ideas rather than on inventing organization Chunking reduces working memory load. [+M]. It is most effective when structure is taught through analysis of authentic models followed by guided joint construction, not by handing learners a blank graphic organizer Explicit writing strategy instruction improves composition quality. [+S]. Comparing multiple texts of the same genre helps learners infer the underlying pattern rather than memorizing one exemplar Comparing contrasting cases improves learning. [+M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Authentic mentor texts in the target genre, ideally several for comparison
  • Explicit naming of the genre's stages and language features (e.g., thesis, evidence, rebuttal in an argument)
  • A joint-construction phase where teacher and students compose together before independent writing
  • A plan for fading support: model → co-construct → independent writing with checklists

Constraints¶

  • Over-reliance on fixed templates produces formulaic writing and weak transfer to new topics or prompts [-M]
  • Teaching structure without purpose or audience reduces writing to fill-in-the-blank exercises and undermines motivation [-M]
  • If mentor texts are far above learners' reading level, structural analysis consumes resources needed for composing Cognitive overload degrades learning. [~M]
  • Rigid genre instruction can disadvantage learners whose home discourse differs from school genres if differences are framed as deficits rather than resources [~W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Reading-to-writing cycle: analyze published models → deconstruct stages → joint construction → independent writing (Sydney School / teaching-learning cycle)
  • Strategy-integrated variant: pair the genre frame with planning and revising strategies such as Self-Regulated Strategy Development, so learners also manage the writing process
  • Contrastive variant: juxtapose two genres on the same topic (e.g., news report vs. editorial) to highlight how purpose shapes structure
  • Digital variant: use annotated exemplars with margin commentary instead of whole-class deconstruction

Target Learners¶

  • Novice writers and English language learners, who benefit most from explicit structural maps Explicit writing strategy instruction improves composition quality. [+S]
  • Adolescent writers in content-area courses who must produce discipline-specific texts (lab reports, source-based arguments)
  • Less beneficial for experienced writers, for whom heavy scaffolding can constrain development [~M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Text structure knowledge: recognizing and producing genre-specific organizational stages
  • Disciplinary literacy: writing like a scientist, historian, or critic
  • Transferable planning: using genre knowledge to plan unfamiliar writing tasks

Instructions¶

  1. Build background: activate what learners already know about the topic and the text type (Activation).
  2. Deconstruct models: guide analysis of 2–3 mentor texts, naming the stages and language features; use Advance Organizers to frame the genre's structure before reading.
  3. Contrast cases: compare texts that do and do not meet genre expectations to sharpen discrimination (Comparing contrasting cases improves learning. [+M]).
  4. Joint construction: compose a text together, with the instructor scribing and learners negotiating choices.
  5. Independent writing: learners draft using a fading checklist rather than a full template; support with Annotating exemplars of their own drafts against the genre stages.
  6. Review and revise: evaluate drafts against the genre's purpose and audience, not just its parts.

Related Strategies¶

  • Self-Regulated Strategy Development — complements genre knowledge with process self-regulation
  • Reciprocal Teaching — same model→guided→independent fading logic applied to reading comprehension
  • Mentor Text Analysis — the text-study phase of the genre cycle in isolation

Examples¶

  • The teaching–learning cycle (Reading to Learn, Australia): a whole-school genre pedagogy in which teachers deconstruct curriculum genres with students before joint and independent construction (https://www.readingtolearn.com.au).
  • The Writing Revolution (Hochman & Wexler): sentence-level to paragraph-level scaffolds built around the structures of expository genres, embedded in content instruction (https://www.thewritingrevolution.org).
  • AP/IB source-based essay instruction: teachers scaffold argumentative writing by deconstructing scored exemplar essays, co-constructing a response to a new prompt, then fading to independent timed writing.
