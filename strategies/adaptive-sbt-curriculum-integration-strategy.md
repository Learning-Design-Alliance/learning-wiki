# Multifaceted integration of simulator-based training into medical curricula with adaptive, learner-group-specific teaching methods and qualified supervision

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a four-part approach to integrating simulator-based training: expanding it in mandatory curricula (especially for physicians), developing content frameworks tailored to different learner groups, ensuring consistent supervision by qualified instructors, and establishing institutional guidelines for standardized implementation and evaluation. It stresses "adaptive teaching methods tailored to the distinct needs of different learner groups", since students seek foundational training (e.g., emergency medicine, airway management) while physicians favor specialized scenarios aligned with their clinical expertise.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Qualified instructors providing oversight and supervision of simulator training; needs-oriented learning objectives; institutional guidelines supporting standardized implementation and evaluation; tools to measure learning success such as the DOPS approach
#### Constraints
- High acquisition costs, limited budgets, technical constraints, and limited realism are cited barriers; no single simulator can universally meet all needs, so equipment should be chosen against the training program's specific objectives and learner demographic

### Target Learners
- third-year medical students
- practical-year medical students
- physicians in postgraduate and continuing education

### Target Learning Goals
- practical clinical skills
- ultrasound competence
- theory-to-practice transfer
- patient safety
