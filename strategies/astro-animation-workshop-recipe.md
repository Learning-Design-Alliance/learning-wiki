# Run art/science workshops as short scientific presentations followed by hands-on animation activities, refined through participant feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable workshop recipe: "Each session begins with a brief, accessible lecture by an astronomer" introducing a phenomenon such as Pluto or solar eclipses, followed by a short lecture on animation principles, then a hands-on animation phase. Surveys at the beginning and end measure changes in interest, understanding, and attitudes, and brief interviews capture participant reflections. Post-workshop takeaways led to concrete improvements: a brief introductory segment on animation fundamentals, scanning drawings instead of shooting them with a camera, and adding participant interviews about their drawings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An astronomer to deliver the accessible lecture and materials for hands-on animation activities
#### Constraints
- Some participants initially felt uncertain about basic animation principles, motivating the added introductory segment

### Target Learners
- teenagers from underrepresented communities
- conference attendees and astronomy enthusiasts
- families

### Target Learning Goals
- basic astronomical concepts
- creative expression of scientific ideas through animation
