# Diagnose computation fluency with brief calculator-free quizzes, then provide practice or a math-for-physical-sciences course before the topic is needed

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors give “a brief quiz” to a sample of students to determine whether fluency deficits are significant, consider a placement test measuring math automaticity, and offer “a 1-3 credit course” in math for the physical sciences prior to or concurrent with introductory physical science courses for students with deficits, plus review and practice in high school before a topic is needed in physics or chemistry.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A brief calculator-free math automaticity quiz to identify deficits before a topic is needed in introductory physics or chemistry
#### Constraints
- Whether a one-semester course would help to a significant number of students will vary depending on student backgrounds; without experiments the size of the benefit is unknown

### Target Learners
- first-year college undergraduates with computation deficits entering introductory physical science courses
- high school students already in the K-12 pipeline

### Target Learning Goals
- arithmetic and algebraic automaticity for solving scientific calculations
