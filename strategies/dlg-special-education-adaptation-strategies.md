# Adapt DLG materials and routines to include students with special educational needs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the first DLG implementation in a public special school, teachers developed concrete adaptations to make dialogic reading possible: they "chose adapted versions of universal literature books that incorporated a wealth of vocabulary and best met their students' needs", adapted books using pictograms, laminated pages, tied books to tables, pre-prepared each student's intervention, used direct questions to motivate participation, placed role-model peers near students with behavioural problems, and explicitly taught the principles of dialogic learning as rules with accessible visual material. These strategies made DLGs feasible for students aged 6–21 with moderate or severe intellectual disabilities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- adapted versions of universal literature matched to students' needs
- teacher preparation of each student's intervention before the session
- explicit teaching of dialogic learning principles as rules with visual supports such as pictograms
#### Constraints
- developed in a segregated special education setting; strategies were identified through interviews with the principal and teachers in one school

### Target Learners
- students with special educational needs in primary, secondary, and transition-to-adult-life courses

### Target Learning Goals
- participation in dialogic reading of universal literature
- fluency in presenting interventions

### Affordances
- Dialogic Learning Seven Principles Dlg
