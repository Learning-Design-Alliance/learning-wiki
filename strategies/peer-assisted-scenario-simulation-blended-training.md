# Blend peer-assisted learning with scenario simulation: role-assigned team simulation with peer feedback and instructor-led debriefing for trauma first aid training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy integrates peer-assisted learning with scenario-based simulation for emergency procedure training. Learners preview theory, then work in teams of nine on simulated clinical scenarios with assigned roles (physician, nurse, family member), rotating roles across simulations; "Instructors provided real-time feedback to correct procedural inaccuracies". The article states "The scenario-based component provides realistic, high-stakes contexts for practicing trauma first aid, while the peer-assisted structure encourages active reflection, communication, and collaborative problem-solving."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor team trained in the methodology with collective lesson preparation and pre-designed scenario cases with 1–2 condition variations
- Standardized case templates covering patient background, clinical progression, and key decision points
- Learners must pass a pre-course theoretical assessment before enrollment
#### Constraints
- Scenario-based simulation alone "can be overly teacher-centered" so learners may act correctly in simulation but fail in real emergencies
- Peer-assisted learning effectiveness "might be related to the differences in peers' teaching experience and clinical proficiency"

### Target Learners
- final-year undergraduate medical interns in emergency department rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- trauma first aid procedural skills
- team cooperation and communication
- clinical decision-making
