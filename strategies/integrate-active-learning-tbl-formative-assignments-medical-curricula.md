# Integrate student-active teaching methods such as TBL and formative assignments into preclinical curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educators in comparable integrated medical curricula emphasize active learning resources and provide guidance on effective study practices. They argue that "educators in similar contexts consider placing greater emphasis on active learning resources, such as TBL and formative assignments, to foster deeper learning", based on the observed associations between valuing these methods and higher exam scores.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curricula must offer active learning resources such as TBL sessions, interactive lessons, and formative assignments with feedback for the recommendation to apply.
#### Constraints
- The underlying evidence is correlational, from a single institution with a 38% response rate and self-selection bias; causation is not established.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in preclinical integrated curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- complex biomedical subjects such as renal physiology
- deep learning and academic performance on summative exams
