# Extend training time, improve simulator transparency, and evaluate the simulator from a teaching perspective in future studies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's closing recommendations call for additional studies that "extend training time, explore the use of different transparent materials and evaluate the efficacy of the simulator from a teaching perspective". The authors reason that the relatively low intervention-group scores may have reflected insufficient training time and trainee unfamiliarity with the examination simulator, and that improved transparency (for example with hydrogels) might further improve assessed procedural skills. They also note the study did not address tutor satisfaction with the transparent simulator as a teaching tool.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Additional studies with longer training periods, alternative transparent materials such as hydrogels, and evaluation of tutor satisfaction
#### Constraints
- Whether increased training time and trainee familiarity with the simulator would increase or decrease the differences between trainee groups requires further investigation, per the authors

### Target Learners
- novice trainees in endourological simulation programs

### Target Learning Goals
- ureteroscopic and cystoscopic procedural proficiency
