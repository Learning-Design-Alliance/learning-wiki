# Design VR learning tools with learner-centered UI/UX choices that limit cybersickness and extraneous cognitive load

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing VR educational experiences around human- and learner-centered design principles: intuitive interfaces, gesture-based interaction mimicking natural movements, touch-activated annotations as signifiers for wayfinding, asymmetric hand functions, and body-region-organized specimen libraries. The authors state that "VR user interface (UI) and user experience (UX) design features have crucial effects on learners' overall experience and learning in the VR space," and that diminishing extraneous cognitive load frees resources for learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- interdisciplinary team including software developers, UX/UI designers, instructional designers, artists, and subject matter experts
- uncomplicated graphics to reduce cybersickness likelihood
#### Constraints
- exposure limited to 30 minutes with breaks
- navigational control restricted to the learning purpose
- correlational evidence only; the team does not equate correlation with causation

### Target Learners
- high-school-aged learners in informal science education
- diverse learners with varied visual aptitude

### Target Learning Goals
- engaging anatomy learning
- increased attention on content rather than navigating technology
