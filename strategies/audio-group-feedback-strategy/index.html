Provide audio group feedback on assignments to raise students' perception that the instructor cares about their learning while saving instructor time¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends delivering whole-class feedback as an audio file (recorded with free screencasting tools such as Jing or embedded LMS audio tools like Canvas) in addition to individual written feedback. The authors report that in their study "it took approximately five minutes using a Jing software screencast to upload to record and upload the audio file," and cite evidence that audio feedback took 3.81 minutes per student versus 13.43 minutes for written feedback. They conclude that perception of instructor caring about students' learning "can be increased when audio group feedback is provided versus written group feedback."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Individual written feedback should still be provided to each student alongside the group audio feedback, as the investigators did for ethical reasons

Constraints¶

  • Three of the four satisfaction measures (engagement, useful feedback, timely feedback) showed no significant difference, so the benefit is specific to perceived instructor caring; the authors call for a study tracking instructor time with the new tools

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate online students

Claims¶

  • Online students given audio group feedback rated the instructor as more genuinely concerned with whether students learned than students given written group feedback [+M]
  • Audio versus written group feedback showed no statistically significant difference on perceived instructor engagement in class [~M]
  • Audio versus written group feedback showed no statistically significant difference on perceived timeliness of feedback on student progress [~M]
  • Audio versus written group feedback showed no statistically significant difference on perceived usefulness of feedback on student work [~M]
  • Students are more likely to open audio feedback files than written feedback files, per the literature the review cites [+W]
