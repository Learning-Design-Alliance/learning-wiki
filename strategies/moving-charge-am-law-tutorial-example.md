# Use a charge moving in a straight line as an application of the AM law and displacement current, suitable as a tutorial introduction to the law

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes analyzing the magnetic field of a uniformly moving charge via the AM law applied to a disk surface bounded by a circle, showing the resulting expression leads to the Biot-Savart law for nonrelativistic speeds. The authors state "the characteristics of the problem make it ideal for developing a tutorial to introduce the AM law," and that the situation helps clarify cause-effect relationships in electromagnetic fields outside the standard capacitor example.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should already have encountered the Biot-Savart law for a moving charge
#### Constraints
- The derivation linking the AM-law expression to Biot-Savart's law holds only when the charge's velocity is much smaller than the speed of light

### Target Learners
- students in introductory electromagnetics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- apply the Ampère-Maxwell law beyond the standard capacitor example
- recognize limits of validity of Ampère's law with time-dependent electric fields
