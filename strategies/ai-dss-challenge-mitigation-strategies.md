# Mitigation strategies pairing each AI-DSS challenge with a targeted countermeasure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review maps each identified challenge—model interpretability, algorithmic bias, scalability, data security, ethical implications, cost and infrastructure, and resistance to adoption—to a specific mitigation strategy, such as explainable AI models, diverse representative datasets, cloud-based architectures, encryption and GDPR/FERPA compliance, ethical design frameworks with stakeholder input, open-source tools, and training that emphasizes evidence-based pedagogical benefits. As the article puts it, institutions should "Offer training and emphasize evidence-based pedagogical benefits" to address educator resistance, and adopt explainable AI models such as decision trees for interpretability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stakeholder engagement and comprehensive training programs to demonstrate AI-DSS value
- Regular fairness audits, e.g., maintaining disparate impact ratios below 0.8
#### Constraints
- Resource-constrained institutions often lack the budget to deploy advanced systems, limiting equitable access

### Target Learners
- online learners in MOOCs, institutional LMSs, and adaptive learning environments

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable and trustworthy AI-supported learning and assessment
