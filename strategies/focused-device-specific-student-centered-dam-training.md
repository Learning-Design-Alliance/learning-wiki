# Use focused, device-specific student-centered training sessions rather than spreading practice across many devices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In the discussion the authors recommend moving from broad multi-device workshops toward more concentrated training: "More focused and specific device training for individual student centered-learning will probably helps to reach competency-based learning for DAM." They suggest that dividing the total training time across three workshops covering a large number of devices might have influenced results, and that further studies should define the optimal strategy of procedural simulations within a global educational process for the best cost-effectiveness ratio.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors who are local experts prepared in each device, as in the study's workshops
- Simulation equipment for the devices taught (mannequin heads, fiberscopes, cricothyrotomy trainers)
#### Constraints
- The article states one cannot yet presume that a unique short procedural simulation alone is the best strategy, since it did not compare simulation to standardized theoretical teaching
- Optimal timing, repetition and cost-effectiveness remain to be defined by further studies

### Target Learners
- second-year anesthesiology and intensive care residents

### Target Learning Goals
- competency in difficult airway management devices and decision making
