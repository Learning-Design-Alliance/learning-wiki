  • Kagan Cooperative Learning Strategies - A family of structured, role-defined peer interaction routines (e.g., Round Robin, Gallery Walk, Two Stray One Stay) that guarantee equal participation in small groups.
  • Keep an extra unassigned spectrum (a red giant) in reserve to challenge students who finish early or reach an incorrect answer - Besides the ten spectra given to students, facilitators hold back an eleventh spectrum of a red giant.
  • Keep Instructions Concise - Deliver task instructions in short, sequenced units aligned to task phases, minimizing extraneous language so working memory is spent on the task, not on parsing directions.
  • Keep students' lived experiences at the center of data use and ensure data reflect students' lives - This strategy warns that data users can become absorbed in trends and patterns tied to policies and programs and lose sight of the students they aim to serve.
  • Keep Your Love of Teaching - Sustain teacher enthusiasm, humor, and authentic enjoyment of students and content as a deliberate instructional practice.
  • Key Word Flash Cards - Create flash cards pairing each mathematical operation with the key words that signal it in word problems.
  • Keyboarding Instruction - Systematic instruction in touch-typing to build fast, accurate, automatic keyboarding so that transcription does not compete with composing.
  • Keyword Method - A two-stage mnemonic strategy in which learners link a foreign word to a familiar keyword that sounds similar, then form an interactive image connecting the keyword's meaning to the word's actual mean
  • Keyword Mnemonic - A two-step mnemonic that links a foreign word or unfamiliar term to a familiar keyword that sounds similar, then forms an interactive mental image connecting the two.
  • Keyword Strategy - The keyword strategy is a mnemonic technique that enhances memory by linking new information to keywords already encoded in memory.
  • Kids React - Students react emotionally to a curated set of online images (e.g., "school lunch" search results), chart their responses, and surface the assumptions and biases driving those reactions.
  • Know Your Own Perspectives - Reflect deeply on your own attitudes and beliefs and how they may impact your opinions and actions.
  • Knowledge-Tracing-Driven Exercise Recommendation - The survey's first application area uses traced knowledge states to choose learning resources automatically.
  • KWL - A three-phase metacognitive routine — Know, Want to know, Learned — that activates prior knowledge before reading and consolidates it afterward.
  • KWL Chart - A graphic organizer that structures a lesson around what students Know (K), Want to know (W), and have Learned (L) about a topic.
