# Build adaptive reinforcement content in free interactive platforms (Genially.com, Canva) to bypass ITS cost and skill barriers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors create educational reinforcement content adaptable to student needs using free design software such as Canva or Genially.com, which offer customizable templates and automated tools such as conditional screen jumps, video insertion, and error-specific explanations. This "removes the barrier to access to adaptive tools, as there is no need to pay for subscriptions to the ITS services currently on the market." The platforms also allow unlimited free modifications of content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must provide the content to be worked on the platform
#### Constraints
- The instructor is responsible for designing each interaction and providing feedback for each foreseen error, since AI pedagogical functionalities cannot be integrated

### Target Learners
- university instructors without programming or advanced digital skills

### Target Learning Goals
- adaptive quantitative reinforcement content creation
