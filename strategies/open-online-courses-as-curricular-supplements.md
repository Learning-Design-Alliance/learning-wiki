# Open online courses as flexible supplements for skills under-represented in formal curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article argues that online anatomical and healthcare education courses can serve an important function when implemented effectively and aimed at specific audiences: delivering training for continuing professional development, lifelong learning, and transferable skills that serve as effective supplements to, rather than replacements for, conventional teaching in areas under-represented in formal curricula. Reported benefits include increased learner interest, motivation, skills development, knowledge enhancements, and long-term retention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Effective implementation aimed at specific audiences
#### Constraints
- Open online courses face limitations in participant attrition and engagement, and significant learning gains may not yet have been widely demonstrated

### Target Learners
- Medical and healthcare students
- Lifelong learners
- Continuing professional development participants

### Target Learning Goals
- Transferable skills training
- Supplementary anatomical education beyond formal curricula
