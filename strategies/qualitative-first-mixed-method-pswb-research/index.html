Prioritise qualitative-first and mixed-method research designs, with targeted scales and school collaboration, when investigating music and PSWB¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends future study be pre-empted by qualitative designs to inform scale choice and design, that any future study use multiple reporting methods to triangulate data and address misreporting, and that quantitative designs be treated as a "'best-approach-to-a-bad-situation'". It further recommends scales designed to measure change, culturally appropriate simplified wording, scales matched to intended outcomes, electronic or supervised one-on-one delivery, collaboration with schools to facilitate randomisation, recruitment of at-risk populations, and purposeful sampling of smaller samples.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Researchers must consult school communities when planning studies to identify existing commitments, needs, and structures that may impede or support a project

Constraints¶

  • The article states inherent tensions: larger samples clash with small-group recommendations, fewer scales conflict with varied benefits, and culturally sensitive scales increase burden or produce incomparable data

Target Learners¶

  • adolescent school students
  • culturally and linguistically diverse students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • valid measurement of psychosocial wellbeing benefits of musical participation

Claims¶

  • A five-week school-based music therapy program for bereaved students captured no statistically significant survey benefits but well-endorsed interview-reported benefits [~W]
  • Interviews after the Keys to Success program reported some PSWB benefits inconsistently, with students reporting they did not understand the program or the survey [~W]
  • A four-week classroom music program (Keys to Success) produced no statistically significant self-reported PSWB benefits in a quasi-experimental study with Year 7 students [~W]
