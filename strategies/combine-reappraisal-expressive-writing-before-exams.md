# Combine short arousal-reappraisal and expressive-writing exercises before each exam to boost performance in large STEM courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q4` · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's tested recipe pairs an online reappraisal assignment (reading passages framing physiological arousal as helpful) completed before each exam with a 3-minute in-class expressive-writing exercise on exam day, including an embodied-cognition step of crumpling and discarding the writing. The abstract concludes this combination "can be a relatively easy manner to boost exam performance in a large-enrollment science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) course and encourage emotion regulation."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both components are administered before each of the course's high-stakes exams, with an active control doing parallel professional-development exercises for the same participation points.
#### Constraints
- Benefits were small (a 1.06% boost in exam points), did not reduce trait anxiety, and did not differentially help women or highly anxious students; the study was one replicate at one institution.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large-enrollment STEM courses

### Target Learning Goals
- improving high-stakes exam performance via emotion regulation
