# Consider less musically driven sonification approaches, such as iconification, when crossing musical cultures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
As one approach to transcending musical cultures, the article suggests avoiding heavily musical sonification designs. The authors write that "associating data with the sounds of physical objects (sometimes called "iconification"; [1]), may be one popular alternative" to musically driven sonification. They present this as a possible direction for future work rather than a tested solution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sounds of physical objects that audiences can recognize as standing for the data
#### Constraints
- The article notes rigorous evaluations of effectiveness and audience preferences with culturally diverse participants are starkly lacking

### Target Learners
- Culturally diverse audiences for astronomy education and public communication

### Target Learning Goals
- Communicating data through sound in ways less dependent on musical-cultural conventions
