# Counseling programs and Cognitive Behavioral Therapy to reduce social networking dependency among at-risk students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that universities support students at risk of social networking addiction through mental-health counseling programs and Cognitive Behavioral Therapy. Based on the CBT method, thoughts are treated as the determinant of emotion, so controlling negative thoughts and managing behavior is proposed as a route to reducing dependence on social networks. The authors also recommend informing students through workshops about the negative consequences of addiction to social networks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- University authorities to take interventional steps; programs targeted at students with high anxiety, stress, depression, or low self-esteem, whom the article identifies as more at risk.
#### Constraints
- The article does not report testing these interventions; they are recommended, not evaluated, in this study.

### Target Learners

### Target Learning Goals
