# Adapt Peyton's 4-stage approach for virtual procedural skills teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors adapted Peyton's 4 stage teaching approach for acquisition of procedural skills to the virtual setting. Students were shown a skill in its entirety via a pre-recorded video, then in breakout rooms the instructor deconstructed the skill, students performed it under supervision, and were given real-time feedback; the process was repeated for each subsequent skill. Instructors 'pinned' student screens to enlarge the view of the individual undertaking the skill before observing the next student.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-recorded demonstration videos, breakout room facilities, and instructors able to give immediate feedback via pinned student screens.
#### Constraints
- Instructors commented on the need for clear articulation of instructions because they could not rely on tactile feedback or non-verbal communication available face-to-face.

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate ophthalmology students
- Medical students learning procedural skills online

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition of procedural and surgical skills through demonstration, deconstruction, supervised performance and feedback
