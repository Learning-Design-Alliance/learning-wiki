# UDL-aligned instructor practices to support students with disabilities in introductory STEM courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend instructors implement Universal Design for Learning practices to address identified course barriers. These include highlighting critical features and big ideas, providing graphic organizers or checklists or guides for notetaking, giving weekly quizzes structured similarly to tests, providing study guides or practice tests, inviting students to discuss accommodation needs early, and framing mental health support as natural rather than tied to individual deficit while reminding students of campus resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must account for the increased time some students need and actively welcome and encourage accommodation use
#### Constraints
- A limitation of the UDL framework is that it is not content specific, so disciplinarily-specific UDL-aligned practices need further investigation

### Target Learners
- postsecondary students in introductory STEM courses, including students with disabilities

### Target Learning Goals
- effective engagement with STEM course content
- reduced test anxiety
- monitoring learning progress

### Affordances
- Social Relational Perspective Disability
