# Collect social network survey data at midsemester to identify at-risk students while intervention is still possible

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a specific timing and logistics recipe for network-based retention monitoring. Network data collected at the end of week 8 gives meaningful predictive measures, and by then the out-of-class network is basically fully developed. The authors note that "modern technologies allow for fast electronic collection and analysis of network data", so surveys can be taken on phones or laptops at the end of a lecture and processed electronically, placing no additional burden on instructors. This enables a single collection per semester.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A social network survey asking students to report in-class and out-of-class interactions with peers
- Collection timed at about week 8 of the semester, when the out-of-class network has reached equilibrium
#### Constraints
- The 8-week development time was observed in a semester system; the authors note quarter and trimester calendars leave less time for the network to fully evolve and should be examined

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory STEM courses

### Target Learning Goals
- early identification of students at risk of not persisting
- increasing persistence in STEM majors
