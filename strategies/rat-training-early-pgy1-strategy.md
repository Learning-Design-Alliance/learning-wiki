# Offer residents-as-teachers training as early as PGY-1, using interactive formats and supervised teaching opportunities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends starting RaT training at the beginning of residency because most residents teach learners in their first two years, and proposes a structured programme covering needs assessment, curriculum development, faculty development, interactive training sessions, paired teaching opportunities with faculty mentor feedback, assessment, curriculum integration, continuous evaluation, rewards, and sustainability. As the authors state, "we recommend offering residents-as-teachers training as early as possible in PGY−1." Training sessions should be interactive, using "role-playing, case-based learning, and simulations."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of residents' current knowledge and teaching skills before goal-setting
- Faculty members who lead the programme receive specialised training
- Teaching opportunities paired with an experienced faculty mentor who observes and provides feedback
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is grounded in a survey with a 9.9% response rate and voluntary sampling, which the authors state limits generalisability
- Cross-sectional design precludes conclusions about causality

### Target Learners
- Clinical residents (PGY-1 onward) across specialties at a faculty of medicine

### Target Learning Goals
- Teaching skills, giving feedback, clinical teaching of medical students
