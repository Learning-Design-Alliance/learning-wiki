# Use camera-based joint visual attention measurement to evaluate collaboration quality without eye-trackers or one-time self-reports

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (6 for, 3 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that psychologists, learning scientists, and experimental designers use its computer-vision joint attention method to evaluate learning and collaborative processes. It states they "can use our method to objectively evaluate the quality of learning or collaborative processes without constraints of one-time, self-reported assessments", replacing costly eye-tracking with a simple color camera.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video recording of the collaborative activity with a color camera
- The trained gaze following model for gaze point prediction
#### Constraints
- The method was tested only on co-located dyadic interactions; validation on other collaborative learning scenarios and larger teams is left to future work

### Target Learners
- students in co-located team-based learning activities

### Target Learning Goals
- objective evaluation of collaboration quality and learning processes
