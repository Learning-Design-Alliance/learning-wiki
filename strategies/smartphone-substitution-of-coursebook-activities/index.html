Substitute paper-based course-book activities with smartphone versions while keeping tasks identical¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In high-stakes EFL contexts bound by a prescribed course book, teachers can copy easily substituted activities such as gap-fill, multiple-choice, and short-answer tasks verbatim into apps like Whatsapp and Socrative while strictly avoiding extra functionality, so the smartphone version is a pure substitution. The study found this substitution alone produced significantly faster initiation and less distraction. The authors conclude the increased engagement is a direct result of smartphone use because activities were identical.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Careful selection of easily substituted activity types and copying text verbatim into apps while avoiding other functionality
  • Standardised, verbatim teacher instructions and controlled variables so groups differ only in writing versus typing

Constraints¶

  • Generalizability is limited to participants sharing the technological preferences of the Saudi male 19-21-year-old intermediate EFL sample
  • Smartphone use may still have been a novelty; effects over longer periods are untested

Target Learners¶

  • EFL preparatory-year university students in high-stakes prescribed-curriculum programmes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Increasing task engagement during required course-book activities

Claims¶

  • EFL students using smartphones initiate classroom activities significantly faster than peers doing identical paper-based activities [+W]
  • Students completing smartphone activities show significantly less distraction than students doing identical paper-based activities [+W]
  • Teacher-observed negative emotions do not differ significantly between smartphone and paper-based activity groups [+W]
