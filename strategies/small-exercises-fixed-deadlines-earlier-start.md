# Design assignments as smaller pieces with fixed due dates to encourage earlier student work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that course organization can encourage students to start work earlier and follow their diurnal preferences. Two design factors are proposed: breaking projects into multiple smaller pieces, since "when projects are broken into multiple smaller pieces, students tend to start earlier", and a strict no-late-submissions policy. The European context, which combined tens of weekly sub-projects with fixed deadlines, showed students working at least 25 hours earlier than US students on average.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires an assessment system that enforces deadlines, since submitting work after the deadline was not possible in the European context
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge there are likely many other explanations for the observed differences and that these are hypotheses rather than tested manipulations

### Target Learners
- CS1 introductory programming students

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing procrastination and encouraging earlier engagement with programming assignments
