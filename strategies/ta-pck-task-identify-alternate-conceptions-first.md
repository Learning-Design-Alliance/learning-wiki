# Explicitly ask TAs to first identify alternate conceptions for each incorrect answer choice before deciding which is most common

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a structured approach to the CSEM-related PCK task in TA training: TAs should first identify what alternate conceptions may lead students to select each incorrect answer choice, and only afterwards decide which is most common. This addresses the observed tendency of TAs to fixate on one partially correct answer choice and "neglected to consider other answer choices carefully". The authors suggest this as a productive approach for TA professional development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- TA training should sequence the task: enumerate alternate conceptions per incorrect choice before ranking their commonality
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a suggested productive approach, not as an intervention it tested

### Target Learners
- first-year physics graduate teaching assistants

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of introductory students' common alternate conceptions in electricity and magnetism (pedagogical content knowledge)
