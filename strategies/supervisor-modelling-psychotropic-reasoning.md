# Supervisor modelling of pharmacological reasoning with patient-pertinent questions between meetings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a training approach in which gifted clinical supervisors take time with residents to discuss particular patients, examine pharmacological management in the context of comprehensive assessment, articulate diagnostic considerations linked with treatment options while thinking aloud about reasons for and against each, and acknowledge gaps in knowledge or evidence. Residents are given specific, patient-pertinent pharmacological questions to be discussed at a next meeting after some reading. The author reports this approach was invaluable to his own learning and had an enduring effect on supervised residents.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clinical training supervisors willing and able to invest time in case-based discussion with trainees
#### Constraints
- The paper notes it remains a major challenge even for outstanding training programs to establish and maintain such high-quality training consistently across sites and institutions

### Target Learners
- psychiatry residents

### Target Learning Goals
- competent clinical use of psychotropics
- diagnostic and treatment reasoning
