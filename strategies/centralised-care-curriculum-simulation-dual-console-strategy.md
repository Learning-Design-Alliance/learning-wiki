# Introduce new surgical techniques through centralised care, a validated learning curriculum, mandatory simulation training and dual-console supervised procedures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose a structured pathway for introducing innovative surgical techniques so that the learning process is "as effective and short as possible". It combines centralised, high-volume care with a validated learning curriculum, mandatory simulation training before live surgery, and dual-console robot procedures allowing direct supervision of trainees by a certified proctor. The strategy is grounded in the finding that the learning phase lasted at least 61 procedures with measurably worse survival during it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Centralised health care with sufficient procedure volume to accumulate experience
- A validated learning curriculum
- Simulation training followed by dual-console robot-assisted procedures with a certified proctor supervising trainees
#### Constraints
- The authors state further research in larger populations and other centres is needed to determine to what extent the 61-procedure learning-phase length is universally applicable

### Target Learners
- Gynaecological oncology surgeons and surgical trainees adopting robot-assisted laparoscopy

### Target Learning Goals
- Reaching oncological surgical proficiency in robot-assisted laparoscopy while minimising harm to patients during the learning phase
