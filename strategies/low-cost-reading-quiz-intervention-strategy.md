# Adopt low-cost online reading quizzes as a course-structure intervention in community college STEM courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends preclass online, open-book reading quizzes as an inexpensive way to add course structure and encourage self-regulated learning at community colleges. It notes reading quizzes are "a low-cost intervention in terms of the instructor time required to create and implement them" and that the data indicate they could be highly attractive for instructors with large teaching loads.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Some assigned preclass reading
- Online delivery with automatic right/wrong grading
- Consistent implementation across the term
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a course that already included clickers and weekly homework; effectiveness in courses without active learning is untested, and the study cannot rule out unmeasured population changes or instructor improvement over time

### Target Learners
- Community college students, including low-income and underrepresented STEM students

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved exam performance through structured preclass preparation
