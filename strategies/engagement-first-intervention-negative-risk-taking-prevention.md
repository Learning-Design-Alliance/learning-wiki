# Prioritize academic engagement in interventions to prevent negative risk-taking and address both domains to interrupt vicious cycles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends "prioritizing academic engagement, rather than academic achievement, in efforts to prevent and reduce adolescent negative risk-taking behaviors, given that academic engagement is a consistent, robust protective factor against negative risk-taking behaviors". It also recommends that interventions "address both domains simultaneously to interrupt the self-perpetuating downward spiral", through early identification and coordinated academic and behavioral support such as tutoring and study skill training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Engagement is more malleable through school-based interventions than prior academic achievement
#### Constraints
- The vicious cycle between negative risk-taking and achievement indicates single-domain interventions may be insufficient

### Target Learners
- adolescents with declining academic achievement or emerging negative risk-taking behaviors

### Target Learning Goals
- academic engagement
- reduction of negative risk-taking behaviors
