# Use a pre-test, post-test, and delayed retention test with the same achievement test to evaluate both achievement and permanence of technology-supported teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 6 studies (6 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 6 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study's design applies one validated achievement test three times: as a pre-test to check group equivalence, as a post-test after the application, and a third time "four weeks later" as a retention (permanence) test. This lets a researcher measure not only immediate achievement gains but also how much learned information students recall after a delay, comparing dependent- and independent-samples t-tests across the three administrations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated achievement test covering the unit's critical behaviors, with reliability established (here KR-20 = 0.797)
#### Constraints
- The authors note the four-week, four-hours-per-week implementation period was short and recommend longer-range studies with larger and more various groups

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students in intact classes

### Target Learning Goals
- Measuring academic achievement and retention of instructed subject-matter knowledge
