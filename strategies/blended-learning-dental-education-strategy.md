# Implement blended learning, combining online and on-site methods, in post-pandemic dental education rather than fully online delivery

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that dental institutions integrate blended learning — "combining online and on-site methods" — into curricula beyond the pandemic, because all study groups (students, staff, parents) preferred it and fully online delivery was seen as reducing engagement and being inefficient for psychomotor skills. It further recommends faculty development in digital pedagogy, robust technological infrastructure, targeted support for female and junior students, and regular assessment of learning outcomes to refine blended strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional technical support adequate to adopt TBL (reported adequate by 71.7% of staff)
- Faculty development programs focused on digital pedagogy and robust technological infrastructure
#### Constraints
- Hands-on clinical training remains essential in dental education, so TBL should be implemented selectively rather than across all teaching activities
- Findings come from a single educational institution, restricting generalizability

### Target Learners
- Dental undergraduate and postgraduate students
- Dental teaching staff

### Target Learning Goals
- Theoretical dental knowledge
- Practical and clinical skills
- Student engagement and satisfaction
