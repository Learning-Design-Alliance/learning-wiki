# Encourage Cooperation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Learning is enhanced when it is more like a team effort than a solo race. Good learning, like good work, is collaborative and social, not competitive and isolated. Learners articulate and share ideas, respond to others' reactions, and negotiate shared understanding — processes that expose gaps in reasoning and deepen comprehension. Cooperation differs from mere grouping: it requires structured positive interdependence, individual accountability, and explicit collaborative skills [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S].

## Design Implications

Cooperative structures work because articulating ideas to peers forces elaboration and reveals misconceptions that solo study conceals [Active learning improves exam performance relative to lecture alone.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. But the benefit is conditional on design: unstructured group work routinely produces unequal participation, social loafing, and consensus without scrutiny. Effective cooperation combines group goals with individual accountability so that every member's contribution is visible and necessary [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task genuinely requiring multiple perspectives or pooled effort — not a task one person could complete alone
- Structured roles or turn-taking that distribute participation ([Assigned Positions](../elements/assigned-positions.md))
- Individual accountability mechanisms: individual write-ups, random reporting, or individual assessment tied to group work
- Time for groups to develop norms; facilitation skills from the tutor or instructor ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))

#### Constraints
- Unstructured grouping degrades into division of labor where strong students do the work and weak students disengage [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [-S]
- Groups formed around friendship rather than task fit can suppress dissent and reduce cognitive conflict
- Cooperative tasks add coordination overhead; for simple factual recall or early skill acquisition, solo practice is often more efficient [~M]
- Novice groups without adequate prior knowledge may reinforce each other's errors; a knowledge baseline or [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) helps

#### Implementation Variability
- **Think-pair-share and small-group discussion** — low-structure cooperation suited to large classes ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
- **Formal cooperative learning** — multi-week teams with assigned roles and graded interdependence (Johnson & Johnson's model)
- **Peer tutoring and reciprocal teaching** — learners alternate between expert and novice roles
- **Collaborative case analysis** — groups analyze authentic cases with divergent stakeholder perspectives ([Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md))

### Target Learners
- Learners in higher education and adult programs who can articulate ideas verbally
- Heterogeneous groups benefit most: mixed-ability teams raise achievement for both stronger and weaker members when accountability is enforced [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]
- Learners at risk of isolation benefit socially as well as academically; cooperative structures support belonging [Belonging interventions improve academic outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Less effective for learners who lack the prerequisite knowledge to contribute meaningfully, or in highly competitive individual-assessment cultures that undercut group goals

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding through explanation, argument, and peer critique
- Communication and perspective-taking: discussing concepts with students of different backgrounds and viewpoints
- Collaborative writing and knowledge production [Collaborative writing improves text quality.](../claims/collaborative-writing-improves-text-quality.md) [+M]
- Metacognition: hearing others' reasoning exposes one's own assumptions

### Instructions
1. Form small heterogeneous groups (3–5 members) with deliberate composition, not self-selection alone.
2. Assign a task with genuine positive interdependence — a single product, shared case analysis, or jigsawed expertise ([Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md)).
3. Assign rotating roles to distribute airtime ([Assigned Positions](../elements/assigned-positions.md)).
4. Build in individual accountability: individual write-ups, random spokesperson selection, or individual quiz components.
5. Facilitate rather than answer — circulate, probe reasoning, and model collaborative moves ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md)).
6. Debrief both the content and the collaboration itself, so group process skills improve over time.

## Examples
- A learning center forms standing study groups; tutees work collaborative tutoring assignments and analyze cases together, then individually submit reflection notes to preserve accountability.
- **PALS (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies)** — a structured peer-tutoring program with role reversal and point systems, extensively validated in K–12 reading and math.
- **Team-Based Learning (Michaelsen)** — individual readiness assurance tests followed by team tests, making preparation individually accountable while rewarding group deliberation.
