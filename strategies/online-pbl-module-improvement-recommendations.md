# Improve online module delivery through earlier technology training, higher-quality lecture recordings and better annotation equipment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on participants' experiences, the authors propose concrete improvements for future online variants: schedule the technology demonstration earlier, deliver video lectures and handouts more promptly so students can annotate during lectures, and ensure recordings show both the speaker and the slides. They state that "investment should (and has been) directed towards the purchase of the best possible quality of 'drawing tool' used for capturing complex visual illustrations in digital format to illustrate the answers to student questions". Both staff members indicated they would prefer to provide synchronous feedback, although students were generally satisfied with the audiovisual recordings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordination between module and blended learning coordinators before the module starts
- Recording equipment that captures both lecturer and slides, plus a quality digital drawing tool
#### Constraints
- The authors note these are small improvements of a technical or organisational nature identified in one pilot case study

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate healthcare students undertaking part-time online modules

### Target Learning Goals
- Statistics and other declarative-plus-procedural content delivered online
