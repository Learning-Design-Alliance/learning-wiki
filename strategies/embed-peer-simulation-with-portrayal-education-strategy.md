# Embed peer simulation with targeted portrayal education as a lower-cost alternative to simulated-patient SBE

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding peer simulation, in which students portray patient roles, into health professional curricula as an alternative to paid simulated patients, preceded by targeted education in portrayal skills. It argues cost savings may exist "because students would not need to be recruited or additionally remunerated if portraying patient roles were part of a standard curriculum", and that peer simulation might also develop empathy, communication and professionalism. The authors state peer simulation "can be embedded into health professional programs".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Education in patient role portrayal before students portray roles, so portrayals are authentic and realistic
#### Constraints
- Whether peer simulation maintains the 'power of simulation' for learners was not tested; the study design limits conclusions about whether portrayals were sufficiently realistic for student engagement

### Target Learners
- health professional students in pre-registration programs

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical simulation-based learning at reduced cost; empathy, communication and professionalism development
