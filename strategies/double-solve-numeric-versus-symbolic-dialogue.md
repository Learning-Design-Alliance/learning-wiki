# Solve the same exercise twice — once with numeric substitution and once with symbolic algebra — then compare both modes in dialogue with students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 5 studies (2 qualitative, 1 associational, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
To foster symbolic algebraic development rather than the habit of immediate numeric substitution, the first illustration of the Algoritmo-Rayuela used one exercise solved twice. The article prints: "el primer ejercicio empleado para ilustrar la implementación de los diferentes pasos del Algoritmo-Rayuela se resolvió dos veces." The first pass replaced each variable by its numeric value, imitating the students' familiar mode; the second pass restarted the whole setup operating algebraically with symbolic expressions. Teaching staff and students then analyzed both modes comparatively and stated the advantages of symbolic handling.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An exercise suitable for both numeric and symbolic resolution
- A comparative dialogue session where the advantages of symbolic handling are enunciated
#### Constraints
- The strategy is aimed at countering the initial impulse the course team observed: replacing variables by numeric values as the starting move of a resolution

### Target Learners
- first-year university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- symbolic algebraic development of solutions
- verifying units
- studying limiting cases and the dependence of unknowns on data variables

## Claims

- [In one first-year physics teacher-training course, students positively valued the Algoritmos-Rayuela strategy for ordering information, orienting the solution steps and making their work comprehensible to a third party.](../claims/algoritmos-rayuela-positively-valued-testimonies.md) [+W]
- [Instructor observation of parcial resolutions found work that was generally ordered, included the corresponding graphs, and postponed numeric substitution to final stages of the solutions.](../claims/exam-resolutions-ordered-graphs-postponed-numerics.md) [+W]
- [Many introductory students find symbolic problems more difficult than numerical ones and prefer plugging in numbers early rather than solving symbolically first](../claims/intro-students-symbolic-difficulty-early-numbers.md) [+W]
- [Students who solved a symbolic problem incorrectly using conflicting meanings of the same symbol t solved the numeric version correctly, with plugging in numbers acting as a cue to specify symbol meaning](../claims/plugging-in-numbers-cues-symbol-meaning.md) [+W]
- [Correct numeric solutions far more often began by finding an unknown in terms of known quantities (86% vs 50%), while symbolic solutions more often began with equations containing multiple unknowns (32% vs 2%)](../claims/strategy-differences-numeric-symbolic-solutions.md) [+W]
- [Students unfamiliar with many-symbol equations may freeze and try to reduce symbols to numbers rather than parse them as physical quantities](../claims/students-freeze-with-many-symbol-equations.md) [+W]
