# Use a randomized controlled trial design where emergency obstetric simulation-based training is not yet widely established

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend moving beyond pre-versus-post designs when evaluating simulation training in new settings. They write that "In settings where emergency obstetric simulation-based training is not yet widely established, we propose to use a randomized controlled trial design as most previous studies were set-up as pre- vs. post-interventional design." This recommendation grounds the stepped wedge trial at Mulago Hospital.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A setting where the training intervention is not yet widely established, allowing randomized sequencing of the rollout
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge organizational constraints such as exam periods and cluster inconsistency may make this design hard to implement in a low-income country, so pre-post analyses will also be performed

### Target Learners
- Health worker trainees in low-income clinical settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Rigorous evaluation of simulation-based training effectiveness on clinical outcomes
