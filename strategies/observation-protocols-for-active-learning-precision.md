# Use classroom observation protocols to characterize instructional behaviors and increase precision of active-learning descriptions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends observation protocols as a way to increase the precision of descriptions of classroom instruction. It states: "Another way to increase the precision of descriptions is the use of observation protocols that can characterize classroom instruction behaviors." Named examples include the Teaching Dimensions Observation Protocol, the Classroom Observation Protocol for Undergraduate STEM, the Practical Observation Rubric to Assess Active Learning, and the Measurement Instrument for Scientific Teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Protocols document the frequency of multiple instructional practices and include categories of active-learning strategies
#### Constraints
- The article notes such information is most valuable when used in conjunction with data on student performance, attitudes, social psychological factors, and self-reflective practices

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate biology students

### Target Learning Goals
- Characterizing classroom instruction behaviors for research purposes and providing feedback to instructors on their practices
