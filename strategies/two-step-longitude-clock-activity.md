# Teach longitude derivation in two consecutive steps: first a Longitude Clock exercise with five time readings, then plotting seven positions from Cook's second voyage

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This strategy sequences the learning in two steps of increasing authenticity. Students first learn the concept with the Longitude Clock and five worksheet time readings, then take the role of Cook's navigator and determine latitude and longitude of seven destinations from Table 2 measurements, locating them on an online map. The abstract states that "the students learn in two steps how longitude can be derived from time measurements", first building the clock and then plotting Cook's 1772–1775 voyage positions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Worksheets, the Longitude Clock or app, calculator, pencil, and a computer, tablet or smartphone with internet connection for the online map service.
#### Constraints
- The article suggests a duration of 90 minutes and notes the second activity requires converting times with minutes and seconds into decimal hours.

### Target Learners
- Students aged 14–19 in middle or secondary school

### Target Learning Goals
- Determining longitude from time measurements
- Applying the method to real historical navigational data
- Interpreting latitude and longitude on a map
