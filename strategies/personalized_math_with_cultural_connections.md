# Personalized Math with Cultural Connections

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy anchors mathematical concepts in contexts drawn from students' interests, communities, and cultural practices — e.g., using hip-hop lyrics for ratio problems, local sports statistics for data analysis, or community budgeting for proportional reasoning. Personalization can be done by the teacher, co-designed with students, or generated adaptively by a system. The goal is to make abstract mathematics meaningful and accessible by connecting it to knowledge students already hold.

## Design Implications

Relevant, interest-based contexts increase engagement and can improve performance, particularly for learners whose interest in the mathematics itself is still developing [Personalization to student interests improves performance and learning in math.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]. However, the connection must do cognitive work: a context that is merely decorative adds extraneous load without supporting the mathematics [Decorative illustrations do not improve learning.](../claims/decorative-illustrations-do-not-improve-learning.md) [-S]. The context should map structurally onto the target concept, not just dress it up.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Genuine knowledge of students' interests, communities, and cultural practices (gathered through surveys, conversations, or community involvement — not stereotypes)
- Contexts that preserve the mathematical structure of the problem; the math must remain the target, not the context
- Willingness to co-design problems with students and revise materials over time

#### Constraints
- Superficial or stereotyped "cultural dressing" (e.g., tokenistic name swaps) can read as insincere and does not improve learning [-M]
- Overly rich or unfamiliar contexts can increase extraneous cognitive load, especially for novices [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [~S] — the context must be *more* familiar than the math, not less
- Personalization to interests shows weaker effects for students already highly interested or expert in the mathematics [~M]
- Time and resource intensive; teachers cannot sustain fully individualized contexts without shared problem banks or adaptive tools

#### Implementation Variability
- **Teacher-crafted contexts**: whole-class problems built around shared student interests
- **Co-designed problems**: students contribute contexts, deepening ownership and [Authentic audiences and purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md)
- **Adaptive personalization**: platforms like [ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org) or TeachEngineering-style problem banks vary contexts by learner interest
- **Community-based projects**: math drawn from local data (e.g., [Meaningful Math](https://meaningfulmath.algebra.org) curricula), connecting to [Community-Based Learning](../principles/community-based-learning.md)

### Target Learners
- Middle and high school students in diverse classrooms where interest in mathematics is uneven
- Students with developing individual interest in math, for whom situational interest can scaffold engagement [~M]
- Students whose cultural knowledge is typically absent from standard curricula; culturally responsive teaching supports belonging and participation [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Less beneficial for advanced students who already transfer fluently across contexts [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: mapping abstract structures onto familiar situations
- Engagement and persistence in mathematics
- Transfer: recognizing the same mathematical structure across multiple contexts (use multiple, varied contexts rather than one)
- Positive mathematics identity and reduced math anxiety

### Instructions
1. **Learn your students.** Survey interests, observe community practices, and involve families; avoid assumptions.
2. **[Activate prior knowledge](../elements/activation.md)** by opening with the familiar context before introducing formal notation, so the context primes relevant schemas [Activation improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
3. **Model with the context.** Use a [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) to show how the situation translates into mathematical representation, making the mapping explicit.
4. **Vary the contexts.** Present the same structure in two or three different cultural contexts so students abstract the mathematics rather than binding it to one scenario [~M]
5. **[Apply](../elements/application.md)** — have students solve problems in a context of their choosing, then write or discuss their own context-based problems.
6. **Discuss and generalize.** Use [Peer Discussion](../elements/peer-discussion.md) to name the shared mathematical structure across contexts, then move toward formal, context-free representation.

## Examples
- **Walkington's adaptive personalization studies** — algebra problems personalized to hobbies (sports, music, gaming) improved performance in [ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org)
- **Culturally responsive curricula** — e.g., [Meaningful Math](https://meaningfulmath.algebra.org) and algebra units built on community data collection
- **Hip-hop based education** — programs using lyric analysis for ratio, rhyme schemes for patterns, and beat timing for fractions
