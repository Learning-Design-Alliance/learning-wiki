# Five-step cross-cultural adaptation procedure: forward translation, back-translation, expert committee review, pre-test, and test-retest

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A replicable procedure for adapting a validated assessment instrument to a new language and healthcare context. The article states: "This study followed the five steps proposed by Ramada-Rodilla et al.: forward translation of the PEACE-Q into Portuguese, back translation into English, cultural adaptation, pre-test, and test-retest." Two independent translators produced a synthesized version, five context-specific items were replaced, two bilingual translators back-translated, and a nine-expert committee rated each item on a four-star equivalence scale before a 20-resident pre-test checked clarity and ambiguity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two forward translators and two bilingual back-translators
- An expert committee rating item equivalence (four-star scale from complete retranslation to no editing needed)
- A pre-test sample reporting item clarity and ambiguity
- A test-retest administration (seven days apart in this study) with a sample meeting the COSMIN minimum of 50 individuals
#### Constraints
- Items tied to the source country's healthcare context must be replaced and re-validated, as was done for the five Japan-specific items

### Target Learners
- Medical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Valid measurement of palliative care knowledge to identify gaps and inform educational program design
