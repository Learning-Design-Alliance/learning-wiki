# Deliver yoga for arthritis in a safe, supportive, non-competitive environment with empathetic providers and tailored modifications

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy captures providers' accounts of what makes yoga delivery work for people with arthritis: creating a "safe and supportive space" where attendees practise without feeling judged, building a therapeutic relationship, being empathetic and compassionate, encouraging acceptance of physical limitations, and offering tailored modifications such as props and chairs. The article presents this as providers' perceived good practice for arthritis treatment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Providers must gather attendees' health information (e.g., via health questionnaire) to identify contraindicated practices
#### Constraints
- Derived from provider perceptions in a qualitative interview study, not from tested outcomes

### Target Learners
- Adults with arthritis attending yoga sessions in the UK

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe, adapted yoga practice supporting physical and mental well-being in arthritis
