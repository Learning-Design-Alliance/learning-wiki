# Bedside hands-on training combining lectures, pelvic model practice, and supervised clinical application

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A multi-component hands-on training recipe for teaching a clinical skill: face-to-face teaching on pelvic silicon models after lectures illustrating the technique, followed by clinical supervision on the labor ward where an instructor midwife guides attendants in applying the technique, then independent application under complete supervision. In this study, "Almost 92% (84/92) of birth attendants in both intervention hospitals participated in the hands-on training", delivered over 3 days per hospital with one instructor midwife staying 12 days.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor midwives available for extended on-site supervision (12 days per hospital in this study)
- Pelvic silicon models and lecture materials
- Scheduling across multiple days so staff on different shifts can attend
#### Constraints
- No data were available on how many supervised deliveries each birth attendant performed; the authors note heavy workload, lack of time and motivation, and fear of complications as barriers when implementing a new technique

### Target Learners
- Birth attendants (doctors and midwives) in hospital labor and delivery wards

### Target Learning Goals
- Correctly applying the bimanual perineal support technique during spontaneous vaginal delivery
- Reducing routine episiotomy use
