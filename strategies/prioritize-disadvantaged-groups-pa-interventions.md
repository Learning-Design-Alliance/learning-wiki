# Prioritize inclusion of adolescents with parents with short education or born abroad in future physical activity interventions aiming at academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future physical activity interventions targeting academic performance deliberately include adolescents from socioeconomically disadvantaged groups, because these adolescents showed lower fitness, physical activity, cognitive and academic performance. They state: "future PA interventions should prioritize the inclusion of these groups" and that "future studies should tailor their interventions to ensure the inclusion of these groups", then test whether raising PA and fitness improves their academic performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must reach adolescents whose parents have short education (≤12 years) or who were born abroad, groups the study found to have lower fitness and grades.
#### Constraints
- The study is cross-sectional, so whether improving PA and fitness would improve academic performance in these groups remains untested; the authors call for longitudinal and randomized controlled designs.

### Target Learners
- Swedish grade-seven adolescents (mean age 13.4) from socioeconomically disadvantaged families

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance (math and language grades)
- Cardiorespiratory fitness and physical activity levels
