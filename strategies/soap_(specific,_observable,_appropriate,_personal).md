# SOAP (Specific, Observable, Appropriate, Personal)

## Description
SOAP is an acronym that serves as a guide for teachers when selecting behaviors for students to self-monitor. Each letter represents a criterion: the behavior should be **Specific** (precisely defined, not vague), **Observable** (detectable by the student without inference), **Appropriate** (matched to the student's developmental level and the instructional context), and **Personal** (meaningful and relevant to the individual student). The acronym operationalizes the behavioral-assessment principle that self-monitoring interventions succeed or fail at the point of target-behavior selection.

## Design Implications

Self-monitoring is one of the most consistently supported self-regulation interventions, but its effects depend on the student being able to detect and record the target behavior reliably [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]. SOAP criteria exist precisely to make that detection possible: a vague target ("behave better") cannot be counted, so the student's self-observation produces noise rather than feedback. Well-specified targets also make progress visible to the student, which supports self-efficacy and persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A functional understanding of the student's current behavior, including baseline frequency and context of the problem behavior
- Operational definitions that a student can apply independently — "raised hand and waited to be called on" rather than "was respectful"
- A recording method the student can manage (tally card, checklist, digital counter) and a regular time to review the record
- Teacher modeling of the counting/recording procedure before independent use ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) or [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md))

#### Constraints
- Complex, internal, or multi-step behaviors (e.g., "comprehend while reading") resist operational definition; forcing them into a SOAP format produces unreliable self-ratings [-M]
- Behaviors that are observable only by others (e.g., "interrupts peers") require the student to make social judgments that inflate or deflate accuracy, especially for students with attention or social-cognition difficulties [-M]
- If the selected behavior is not personally meaningful to the student, compliance with recording tends to erode and the intervention becomes teacher-managed rather than self-regulated [-W]
- Overly easy targets produce ceiling effects with no learning; overly difficult ones produce repeated failure records that can depress self-efficacy [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Self-monitoring of performance** (counting correct answers or products) versus **self-monitoring of attention/behavior** (counting on-task moments) — performance monitoring often produces stronger academic gains [+M]
- Reactive (student records after the fact) versus proactive (student checks before acting) monitoring
- Self-evaluation add-ons: students compare their record against a teacher rating, which improves accuracy of self-assessment over time
- Fade the recording device as the behavior becomes habitual, transferring control from external tool to internal regulation

### Target Learners
- Students with ADHD or self-regulation difficulties, for whom self-monitoring interventions show some of the largest effects [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Students in [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) programs who need concrete entry points into monitoring their own behavior
- Young learners or novices who cannot yet select their own targets — SOAP lets the teacher scaffold target selection before students set their own goals
- Less applicable to students who already regulate the target domain well; monitoring an already-mastered behavior adds load without benefit [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Behavioral self-regulation: increasing on-task behavior, work completion, or prosocial behavior
- Metacognitive monitoring: building the habit of observing one's own performance
- Self-efficacy: making incremental progress visible and attributable to the student's own actions

### Instructions
1. Define the target behavior using the SOAP criteria; write an operational definition the student can restate in their own words ([State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md))
2. Model the detection and recording procedure, thinking aloud as you count instances ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md))
3. Have the student practice recording while you observe, then correct discrepancies between student and teacher counts ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))
4. Establish a regular review routine in which the student compares records against a goal and graphs progress ([Check-Ins](../principles/check-ins.md))
5. Fade the recording scaffold as accuracy and the target behavior stabilize, shifting target selection to the student

## Related Strategies
- Self-Monitoring — SOAP is the target-selection procedure within this broader intervention family
- Goal-Setting — SOAP-defined behaviors become the concrete goals students monitor progress toward
- Self-Evaluation — comparing student records against teacher or criterion ratings improves monitoring accuracy

## Examples
- A third-grade teacher defines "looked at my worksheet and wrote an answer" (Specific, Observable) as the target for a student who daydreams during independent work; the student tallies each minute the behavior occurred and graphs daily totals.
- A middle-school resource room uses a self-monitoring card for work completion: students check off each completed problem set at the end of class, with the teacher spot-verifying a sample.
- Self-monitoring of performance in math fact fluency: students time themselves on a worksheet and record problems correct per minute, a variant that typically outperforms attention-only monitoring for academic outcomes.
