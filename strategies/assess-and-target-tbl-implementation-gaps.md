# Assess local TBL implementation fidelity and target interventions at missing standard steps and components to improve the student TBL experience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators first survey faculty on how often they implement the standard TBL steps and components, then develop targeted curricular interventions enforcing the missing elements. In its own words, "Assessment of the implementation of the standard TBL steps and components and development of corresponding targeted interventions may improve the TBL experience of medical students." The authors add that their process "could be generalized to help educators interested in improving TBL in their home institutes." Specific interventions used included identical IRAT/TRAT items, the 4 S's with periodic stopping points, immediate feedback, peer evaluation, and moving TBL sessions from Fridays to Mondays to increase preparation time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A faculty survey to determine the frequency of implementation of TBL standards as described in the literature
- Faculty facilitation quality, including pacing with specific time goals and frequent feedback during application activities
#### Constraints
- The article notes its evidence is limited to student perception in one course iteration without a control group, and that peer evaluation processes may need to be tailored to each institution

### Target Learners
- Medical students in undergraduate medical education

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved student perception of TBL learning experience and engagement in active learning
