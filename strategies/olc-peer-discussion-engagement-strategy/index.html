Use online-learning components with peer discussion groups and student choice to increase engagement and attitude toward art¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that art teachers engage students in online research and peer discussion groups as a strategy to raise engagement, attitude, and digital literacy practice. It reports that "students who participated in online-learning components experienced increased levels of engagement, felt the online-learning components provided more choice, and helped them create better quality art products." Students became intrinsically motivated when given choice and located artists they found personally meaningful.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students need access to technology devices, such as through a BYOD program, and an acceptable use policy with Internet access

Constraints¶

  • Device access at home was uneven: several OLC students had difficulty accessing technology at home and were unprepared with devices in two of three projects

Target Learners¶

  • eighth-grade visual arts students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • art achievement
  • digital literacy practice
  • student engagement
  • attitude toward art

Claims¶

  • OLC students made greater gains in art achievement across units than face-to-face students [+W]
  • OLC students demonstrated digital literacy skills more frequently than face-to-face students, especially locating art resources online [+W]
  • OLC students reported more favorable attitudes toward art and technology, particularly feeling more choice in what they learned [+W]
  • An online-learning component with peer discussion groups did not significantly raise eighth-grade art project scores compared with face-to-face instruction, though a small practical effect was found [~W]
  • Online research was associated with higher creativity in student artwork, with OLC students producing more novel work with a more distinct artistic voice [+W]
  • OLC students showed significantly higher engagement in art than face-to-face students [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Empower student autonomy by making supports student-facing and increasing default access, which can raise engagement and reduce barriers
