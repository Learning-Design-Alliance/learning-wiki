# Award participation marks for attempting at least 10 quiz questions per lecture within 14 days and scoring at least 60%

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The instructors incentivized timely quiz participation by awarding course credit for regular engagement: "For each lecture, attempt at least 10 questions within 14 days of the lecture, scoring at least 60%." Students could earn 8% (2017) or 6% (2018) of the final grade this way, with a pro rata formula for partial participation. The authors implemented the deadline because engagement soon after presentation supports assimilation and procrastination harms performance, and they attribute the strategy's success to spaced retrieval practice and transfer-appropriate processing of the testing effect.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A question bank populated by student-authored questions for each lecture, and Quizzical tracking of time stamps, attempts, and correct answers to compute participation marks.
#### Constraints
- The article reports F students were less consistent in meeting the participation obligations throughout the term, focusing instead on intense use just before an exam.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- regular spaced retrieval practice
- exam preparation that mimics the summative MCQ format
