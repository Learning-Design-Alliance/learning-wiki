# Combine a virtual simulation system with a jaw simulation model in preclinical implant training, teaching the virtual system first

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors "modify new teaching methods by including virtual simulation systems and jaw simulation models into their teaching courses", splitting an eight-hour operation session into two four-hour blocks on the two modalities. The virtual system lets students grasp the whole clinical implantation process and receive targeted feedback, while the jaw model compensates for the virtual system's limited fidelity of vision, hearing, and touch. The authors also suggest the combination can decrease faculty time in instruction and supervision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to both a virtual simulation system (e.g., Zhonghui UniDental-MS01) and a jaw simulation model with a dental implant toolbox
- Unified theoretical lectures before operation training
#### Constraints
- The guiding role of teachers cannot be completely replaced by technology
- The fidelity of virtual simulation of vision, hearing, and touch cannot be completely compared with actual reality
- Results cannot explain the application value of all virtual simulation systems because equipment models are not uniform across geographies

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate stomatology/dental students in preclinical implant training

### Target Learning Goals
- Theoretical implantology knowledge
- Preclinical implant surgical skills and implant placement accuracy
