# Video submission sites should let uploaders declare ownership and specify Creative Commons licence conditions, streamlining educators' identification of reusable content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes modifying the video submission process on sites such as YouTube so users can declare ownership and "specify some requested level of protection under creative commons licence", with optional openness to permission requests, posted in the submission details. Creative Commons licences combine four conditions (Attribution, Non-commercial, No derivative works, Share-Alike) to let creators protect content for educational use while preventing unfair commercial exploitation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formal endorsement by video platforms, e.g. tickboxes in the file submission process and creative commons specific searches
#### Constraints
- The article notes this already occurs to some extent informally, but lacks platform-level facilitation

### Target Learners
- university lecturers and other educators sourcing video content

### Target Learning Goals
- locating copyright-cleared video suitable for lectures
