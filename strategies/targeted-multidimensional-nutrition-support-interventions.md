# Universities should deliver targeted multidimensional interventions — food security programs, nutrition education, and psychosocial support — for socially and nutritionally vulnerable student profiles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that institutions move beyond uniform interventions and prioritize students in vulnerable profiles identified through multivariate profiling. It states that "Universities should prioritize targeted actions for students in socially and nutritionally vulnerable profiles, including food security programs, nutrition education, and psychosocial support services". The recommendation rests on the observed co-occurrence of disadvantage, poor dietary patterns, and lower academic satisfaction in the at-risk cluster.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of high-risk student profiles, which the authors say cluster analysis enables
- Institutional capacity to combine food security, educational, and psychosocial components
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness of such interventions has not been evaluated; the article calls for future research to evaluate policy interventions
- Cross-sectional findings cannot confirm that the interventions will change academic outcomes
