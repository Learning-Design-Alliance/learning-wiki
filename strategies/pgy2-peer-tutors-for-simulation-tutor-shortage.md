# Use peer-assisted learning by PGY-II residents to staff simulation-based learning where expert tutors are scarce

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends addressing the shortage of simulation tutors by having near-peer PGY-II residents conduct simulation-based learning for PGY-I residents, since "It is difficult for all hospitals to prepare such teaching staff" of specially trained experts. Their three-hour peer-led program achieved knowledge gains equivalent to lectures plus additional positive self-evaluation outcomes. The authors caution that PGY-II residents must not be forced into tutoring without demonstrated advantages to them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Volunteer PGY-II tutors who receive debriefing education before teaching
- Scenarios matched to what junior residents should and could do
#### Constraints
- General concern exists that PGY-II residents may have insufficient knowledge and incorrectly teach topics; the authors note this negative aspect seemed more pronounced in simulation than in lectures
- A comparison against expert-led simulation has not been performed and is recommended

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate-year-I medical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Initial case management skills before starting clinical practice
