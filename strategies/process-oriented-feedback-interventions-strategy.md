# Supplement simulation-based procedural training with three process-oriented interventions: BPMN-based teaching, self-labelling of one's own recorded execution, and an individual process mining feedback report

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy supplements a deliberate-practice simulation program with three cognitive interventions for trainees: teaching the procedure via a reference process model in business process model notation, having each resident label the activities in a video of their own execution, and delivering an individual process mining feedback report. The report "included procedural execution diagrams, visual comparisons with ideal performance, and nonexecuted and repetitive activities," explained and discussed with each resident before deliberate practice sessions. It is intended for procedural training contexts where feedback is otherwise subjective or unavailable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A reference process model of the procedure defined by expert consensus (here via Delphi methodology)
- Video recordings of trainee executions and a labelling platform to convert them into event logs
#### Constraints
- The three interventions were cognitive tasks that did not include hands-on training
- Effects were demonstrated in a simulated environment without evidence of transfer to a clinical setting

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate year-one anaesthesia and emergency medicine residents without previous experience in central venous catheter placement

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural proficiency in ultrasound-guided internal jugular central venous catheter placement, specifically correct step sequence and reduced repetition of steps
