# Provide a post-visit hair bar with culturally familiar styling products and close the visit by answering families' lingering questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because children’s hair is likely to be disheveled after cap removal, the review recommends that “we recommend creating a “hair bar ” for participants, including disposable combs, hair dryers, and hair products that are familiar to the populations from which participants are drawn.” Teams should consult focus groups or pilot participants about helpful products, and ask participants at the end of the visit whether they have lingering questions about the data collected and next steps, so families leave with a clear understanding of why data were collected.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Products vary regionally and by cultural identity, so labs should ask pilot participants which products and community purchase locations are most helpful.
#### Constraints
- The review frames these steps as needed both to increase future research participation and to improve relations between scientists and Black and Latinx participants.

### Target Learners
- Black and/or Latinx children and families completing pediatric EEG visits

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining family comfort and trust to support future research participation and community–scientist relations
