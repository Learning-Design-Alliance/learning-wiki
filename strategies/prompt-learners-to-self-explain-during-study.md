# Induce self-explanation by prompting learners during study and problem solving

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The meta-analysis supports designing learning activities in which learners receive prompts to self-explain while studying or solving problems. The reviewed research investigated "learning outcomes for participants who received self-explanation prompts while studying or solving problems", and the pooled result (g = .55) supports building such prompts into instructional materials across learning tasks such as studying text and studying worked problems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prompts must be delivered while learners study or solve problems, matching the conditions under which effects were measured.
#### Constraints
- Relying on instructor-scripted prompts has limitations, per the authors' recommendation to explore computer-generation of prompts.

### Target Learners
- K-12 and higher education students across subject areas

### Target Learning Goals
- improved learning outcomes from study and problem-solving activities

### Affordances
