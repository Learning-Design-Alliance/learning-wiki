# Use non-anonymised post-level interviews as an additional training assessment tool alongside, not instead of, anonymised feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend supplementing anonymous scheme-level surveys with non-anonymised face-to-face interviews of trainee-trainer pairs, because post-level feedback enabled targeted improvements at the level of each individual placement, the main unit of intervention. They caution that trainees in difficulty may avoid giving feedback or may not speak honestly when responses are linked to them, stating "non-anonymised feedback may best serve as an additional training assessment tool, rather than an alternative to anonymised feedback". High response rates (88% trainees, 97% trainers) suggest non-anonymising is not a deterrent to participation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All trainees must feel they can both participate and speak honestly about their training posts
- Senior sponsorship (emails from the Medical Director and Vice Dean for Education and Training) to encourage participation
#### Constraints
- Participants may feel pressured by senior-figure emails; struggling trainees may withhold true feedback
- Whether high response rates would be maintained over time is unknown, as survey fatigue might set in

### Target Learners
- postgraduate medical trainees
- clinical trainers

### Target Learning Goals
- quality improvement of individual training placements
- post-level identification of strengths and weaknesses
