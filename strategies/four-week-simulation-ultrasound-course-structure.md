# Structure a four-week ultrasound course with weekly theory plus supervised simulation-based hands-on practice covering FAST, E-FAST, RUSH and CLUE protocols

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's training program delivered 20 hours over four weeks: a 1-hour weekly theoretical class and 4-hour weekly simulation-based practical application using the Sonosim device with a mannequin. Content covered "basic ultrasound training (basic physics and knobology)", the FAST and E-FAST exams, the RUSH exam, and the CLUE protocol. Practical applications were repeated until each student mastered probe handling, device knowledge, anatomy, and pathology recognition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two emergency medicine specialists qualified in ultrasound training as instructors
- Weekly scheduled theoretical and hands-on sessions over four weeks
#### Constraints
- The authors believe long-term successful ultrasound training should not be for less than 4 weeks

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students without prior ultrasound training

### Target Learning Goals
- perform focused ultrasound examinations for trauma, shock, and cardiac evaluation
