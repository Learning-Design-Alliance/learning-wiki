Strategies

Schedule retrieval practice before sleep rather than before extended wakefulness¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that sleep-dependent consolidation selectively benefited no-feedback retrieval-practice memories, the authors advise that "for learners, using retrieval practice before sleep yields superior memory outcomes compared to retrieval practice followed by a longer period of wakefulness." The strategy exploits the offline replay of weakly encoded traces during sleep, so learners should place retrieval practice sessions close to sleep rather than in the middle of a long wakeful interval.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The learner must actually sleep after the retrieval practice session for the offline consolidation benefit to accrue
  • The recommendation is grounded in nap evidence with weakly associated word pairs and no-feedback retrieval

Constraints¶

  • Benefits were observed only for retrieval practice without feedback items; restudy and feedback retrieval showed no significant nap advantage in this study
  • Evidence comes from a controlled lab paradigm with 90-minute naps rather than habitual nighttime sleep

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate learners memorizing paired associative material

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Long-term retention of studied material

Affordances¶

  • Dual Phase Consolidation Retrieval Practice

Claims¶

  • Nap benefits for no-feedback retrieval-practice items appear at both 90-minute immediate and 24-hour delayed tests [+W]
  • Napping reduces forgetting only for items learned by retrieval practice without feedback, not for restudied or feedback-retrieved items [+W]
  • Wake groups show greater recall-change differences between learning conditions than nap groups, driven by no-feedback items [+W]
