Sequence future adult-learning evaluation from usability observation to assistance-condition comparison with supervised independent assessment¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends that a future adult-learning study "should first examine whether the workflow is understandable and usable in the intended course", distinguishing interface difficulties from workplace-problem difficulties. A subsequent evaluation could compare course-defined assistance conditions while holding learning objectives, task access and instructional time as constant as practicable, with independent performance assessed through clearly defined, preferably supervised tasks and a prespecified rubric, trained raters, and reported agreement before reconciliation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Facilitator preparation, activity duration and acceptable forms of AI help should be specified rather than inferred from application events; raters should be trained and agreement reported before reconciliation

Constraints¶

  • No sample size, power result, effectiveness claim or commercially ready status follows from the current rehearsal

Target Learners¶

  • adults in workplace learning programmes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • retained workplace reasoning and independent judgement

Related Strategies¶

  • Conduct field studies of GenAI-assisted coding under realistic development conditions, explicitly accounting for team structure, project complexity, and organizational processes.
  • Document prior research on a solution, starting from community-needs assessment efforts, before planning future evaluations
