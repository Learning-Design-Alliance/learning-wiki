# Replace or augment rubber-band demonstrations with alternative materials and historical-music material to teach frequency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Teachers in the 2022 discussion proposed replacing rubber bands and steel rulers with other demonstrations. Zhen Cui proposed tuning and amplifying guitar-string vibration complemented by explaining the guitar "fret" concept; Yi Zhang proposed using a flashlight and curtain to amplify tuning-fork vibration for observation, plus a teaching approach combining history of science and music, using ancient scholars' work such as the "Pythagorean Intonation" as material to guide students to understand frequency's role and promote concept construction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials must make vibration phenomena observable, since the naked eye struggles to distinguish vibration speed of rubber bands.
#### Constraints
- The author argues these alternatives were proposed without understanding the cause of the confusion, so their effectiveness is unverified: visual inspection of teaching improvement alone cannot show they solve the problem.

### Target Learners
- primary school science students

### Target Learning Goals
- establishing the concept of frequency in the Sound unit

## Claims
