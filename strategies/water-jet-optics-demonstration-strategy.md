# Use a water jet optics demonstration to teach total internal reflection as the principle underlying fiber optics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A light beam from a He-Ne or diode laser enters a water container and exits through a spout; when the cap is removed, water flows out forming a waveguide and the light follows the water flow through multiple total internal reflections. The article calls this "similar to John Tynda ll's experiment in 1870" and uses it to argue that "Water jet optics and fiber optics are based on the same principle: total internal reflection."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A water container with a spout and cap, a He-Ne laser or diode laser, and a screen; a dark room makes the effect impressive
#### Constraints
- Works because the refractive index of water is larger than that of air, satisfying the condition for total internal reflection

### Target Learners
- college students in introductory optics or fiber optics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding total internal reflection
- understanding how optical fibers guide light
