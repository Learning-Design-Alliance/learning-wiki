# Create opportunities and environments promoting physical activity in students with low academic achievement to help reduce school dropout

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools deliberately create physical activity opportunities aimed at students with low academic achievement, framing fitness improvement as a lever on both achievement and dropout risk. The authors write that "Creating opportunities and environments to promote physical activity in students with low academic achievement could be a recommended strategy for schools to reduce school dropout", which they connect to a Spanish and European Union 2020 policy priority. In practice this means protecting and expanding physical education and school sports offerings rather than reallocating that time to academic subjects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools must retain or provide physical education sessions and spaces for a wide variety of sports and physical activities
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional, so the strategy rests on associations rather than demonstrated causality
- The authors state there is "no consensus of the physical activity characteristics for improving cognition or academic achievement"

### Target Learners
- Compulsory secondary education adolescents, especially low academic achievers

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement across school subjects
- School retention / dropout prevention
