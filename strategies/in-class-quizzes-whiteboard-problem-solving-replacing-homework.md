# Supplement or replace homework assessment with in-class low-stakes quizzes and whiteboard group problem-solving when online solutions undermine the homework model

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy responds to the decoupling of homework grades from test achievement: the authors' third proposed response, which they have "begun to adopt in practice", is to "supplement or replace the homework portion of our courses with in-class assessments like low-stakes quizzes or group problem-solving exercises at the whiteboard." Moving assessment into class removes the opportunity to submit copied solutions, since the instructor can observe the work being done.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor must devote class time to the quizzes or whiteboard exercises in place of graded homework.
#### Constraints
- The authors state the problems worked this way are "necessarily shorter and often more conceptual", and that any additional in-class exercise reduces the material the instructor can cover in the course.

### Target Learners
- calculus-based introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- problem-solving skill in mechanics and electricity and magnetism
- genuine individual mastery of homework-level material
