Deliver psychosocial-support nudges to at-risk college students via scalable text messaging during transition periods¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends text messaging as "a highly efficient, scalable, and direct means of reaching college students," given mobile phone ownership above 95% among 18- to 29-year-olds. Nudges should be interactive (eliciting dialogues and offering advice), automatically enrolled with opt-out available, and timed to transition periods such as summer, when students are disengaged and decisions about reenrollment are made. The authors conclude community colleges should explore psychosocial strategies and text messaging alongside broader reforms such as guided pathways.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A platform able to deliver interactive two-way text messages and escalate to human staff only for questions outside its scope
  • Coordination with college registrar data to identify unenrolled students and tailor nudges

Constraints¶

  • The intervention did not directly address the structural inequities that produce identity threats
  • Effects were concentrated among STEM-pathway and summer-enrolled students; non-STEM students showed no significant benefit

Target Learners¶

  • first-year community college students
  • STEM-pathway students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • fall reenrollment
  • persistence toward a credential

Affordances¶

  • Wise Interventions Psychosocial Barriers Framework

Claims¶

  • Treatment-effect heterogeneity by engagement: first-day unsubscribers reenrolled 26 percentage points below control, while full-treatment students benefited whether or not they responded [+W]
  • The nudge intervention raised reenrollment by 10 percentage points among STEM-pathway students but had no significant effect on non-STEM students [+W]
  • The nudge intervention raised reenrollment by 10 percentage points among summer-enrolled students but not significantly among students not enrolled in summer courses [+W]
  • Summer-enrolled treatment students engaged more with the nudges than non-summer-enrolled students, while STEM and non-STEM students engaged similarly [+W]
  • A 7-week summer text-message nudge campaign increased fall reenrollment among first-year community college students by 7 percentage points relative to control [+W]
