# Combined organizational-support, mentorship, simulation-based confidence-building, and workload-management interventions for new emergency and critical care nurses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that nursing managers adopt targeted interventions to reduce burnout and raise emergency response competence: "Tailored Training Programs", "Peer Support Systems" establishing mentorship, "Confidence-Building Initiatives" using simulation-based training and regular feedback, and "Workload Management" through equitable task distribution and shift rotation. It also recommends enhancing "systematic, simulation-based emergency training" combining case-based teaching with practical drills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Nursing managers and hospital administrators able to organize training programs, mentorship, psychological support, and shift scheduling
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional and correlational; the article states causal sequencing requires longitudinal validation

### Target Learners
- Newly qualified nurses (≤ 3 years' experience) in emergency and critical care departments

### Target Learning Goals
- Emergency response competence
- Coping confidence and self-efficacy in high-pressure clinical situations
