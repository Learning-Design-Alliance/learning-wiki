# Introduce mathematical and physical formalism only after intuitive understanding based on applications has been aroused.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A design strategy drawn from the authors' experience running the quantum MOOCs: present topics using examples and applications close to everyday life, and defer formal descriptions until a basic understanding exists. The article recommends: "Mathematical and physical descriptions and concepts should only be introduced and used once a basic understanding and curiosity have been aroused." The authors report this approach lowers the entry barrier and leads to less frustration among learners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course content must be selectable so that only minimal mathematical and physical knowledge is required at the start.
#### Constraints
- The authors state this from their experience with an audience without prior knowledge; formal definitions were deliberately omitted in Intro 2.

### Target Learners
- Adult learners without prior knowledge of mathematics or physics

### Target Learning Goals
- Intuitive understanding of quantum computing concepts before formal treatment
