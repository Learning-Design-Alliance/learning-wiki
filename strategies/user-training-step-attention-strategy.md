# Give the User Training step further attention in entry-to-practice programs, using 2–4 hours of experiential bootcamp-style wheelchair skills learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article concludes that the User Training step "merits further attention in entry-to-practice programs", noting that programs with fewer total hours tended to spend one hour or less on it. It points to wheelchair skills training's "growing body of evidential literature" which "typically entails 2–4 hours of experiential learning, using a bootcamp approach, to adequately prepare students for clinical application".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must include the User Training step and allocate experiential learning time consistent with the 2–4 hour bootcamp approach the article cites.
#### Constraints
- Most programs that did not address the User Training step at all had 16 hours or more of content, suggesting the decision was not entirely related to time.

### Target Learners
- Occupational therapy entry-to-practice students in Canadian university programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Competence in wheelchair user and caregiver training as part of the WHO wheelchair service provision process
