# Adopt AR-based activities as a supplementary tool within a blended learning approach when transitioning schools toward a SMART campus

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that school administrators, policy makers, and STEM Department Heads implement AR-based activities "as a supplementary learning tool as part of their blended learning approach" when transitioning schools into SMART campuses. This follows the review's finding that AR apps have been a successful aid across the STEM subject areas and that technology coupled with adequate pedagogical foundations enhances teaching and learning quality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Practitioners must address intrinsic STEM issues such as expensive or insufficient laboratory systems, insufficient computers, few CAD-knowledgeable instructors, and teachers who prefer traditional methods
#### Constraints
- Wang's study cautions that educators should take careful consideration of the educational goal architecture, the data size shown on the cellphone monitor, and the teaching machinery and school facilities setting when incorporating AR apps

### Target Learners
- K-12 and higher education students; school administrators and policymakers in the Philippines and the region

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving STEM teaching and learning across disciplines as schools adopt AR-enhanced blended learning
