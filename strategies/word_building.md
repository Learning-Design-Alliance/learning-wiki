# Word Building

## Description
Word building is a literacy strategy used to reinforce vocabulary and spelling skills by having learners manipulate letter tiles or squares to construct words. This hands-on activity promotes phonemic awareness, letter-sound correspondence, and word recognition. Learners rearrange letters to form target words, often drawn from a specific context like a song or reading material. This method enhances memory and retention through active engagement and multisensory learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Letter tiles or squares, word lists, chart paper (optional), writing materials. Preparation involves cutting apart letter squares and selecting target words.
#### Constraints
- May be time-consuming to prepare letter tiles. Requires sufficient space for learners to manipulate letters. Effectiveness depends on clear instructions and active facilitation by the instructor.

- Early learners (K-2), learners with dyslexia, English language learners, and anyone needing to reinforce basic spelling and phonics skills

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Improve spelling skills, strengthen word recognition, reinforce letter-sound correspondence, develop phonemic awareness, enhance vocabulary, build foundational literacy skills

### Affordances
- Engages learners actively in constructing words, improving retention and recall. Promotes phonemic awareness and understanding of letter-sound relationships. Provides immediate feedback as learners manipulate letters. Supports multisensory learning, catering to different learning styles.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted for different skill levels by selecting appropriate words. Provides scaffolded support for struggling learners by giving hints or partially completed words. Offers a challenge for advanced learners by having them build longer or more complex words.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learners' ability to rearrange letters accurately and efficiently. Collect and review the words they build to assess spelling skills. Track progress over time to measure improvement in word recognition and construction.

## Impact
- Hands-on activities like word building enhance phonemic awareness and letter-sound correspondence, crucial for early literacy. Direct instruction in phonics and vocabulary supports reading and spelling skills. Active engagement improves retention and comprehension.
