# Establishing Clear Expectations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Establishing clear expectations means making learning goals, performance criteria, procedural requirements, and behavioral norms explicit and visible to learners before and during instruction. It is carried out through tools such as rubrics, exemplars, advance organizers, stated objectives, and modeled norms — so that learners can direct their effort toward the intended outcomes rather than guessing at them.

## Design Implications

Clear expectations reduce uncertainty about what counts as success, freeing working memory for the task itself and enabling self-regulation: learners cannot monitor progress against a target they cannot see [Advance organizers improve learning by clarifying the structure of upcoming content.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]. Expectations also function as a motivational lever — learners who understand criteria and see them as attainable engage more persistently, and transparent criteria support the feedback loop that drives improvement [Assessment for learning improves achievement by making criteria and progress visible to learners.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Expectations must be paired with exemplars and feedback to be actionable; criteria stated in the abstract rarely change learner behavior.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit, learner-facing statements of goals and success criteria, ideally with [Rubrics](../elements/rubrics.md) or checklists
- Worked or annotated exemplars showing what meeting the criteria looks like ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md), [Non-Examples](../elements/non-examples.md))
- Consistent enforcement and reference: expectations revisited at [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md), not stated once and abandoned
- Alignment between stated expectations and what is actually assessed ([Constructive Alignment](../patterns/constructive-alignment.md))

#### Constraints
- Overly detailed or numerous expectations increase extraneous load and can fragment attention [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-M] — prioritize the few criteria that matter most
- Rigid, teacher-imposed expectations can undermine ownership and intrinsic motivation, particularly for experienced or autonomous learners [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~M] — co-constructing criteria or offering choice mitigates this
- Expectations that are stated but not enacted (assessed criteria differ from announced ones) erode trust and produce worse compliance than saying little [Checklist-style evaluation is ineffective in online settings when not tied to meaningful criteria.](../claims/checklist-evaluation-ineffective-online.md) [-W]
- Low or ambiguous teacher expectations become self-fulfilling: learners tend to perform at the level implicitly signaled [Teacher expectancy effects influence student achievement.](../claims/teacher-expectancy-effects-influence-student-achievement.md) [~S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Rubric-based**: analytic rubrics shared before an assignment, used by learners for self- and peer-assessment
- **Exemplar-based**: showing annotated strong and weak samples rather than abstract criteria
- **Norm-based**: co-created classroom norms for discussion and collaboration, revisited regularly
- **Contract-based**: individualized expectations in [Competency-Based Assessment](../principles/competency-based-assessment.md) or [Adaptive Learning](../principles/adaptive-learning.md) contexts

### Target Learners
- Novices, who lack the tacit knowledge to infer unstated criteria from context [Advance organizers improve learning by clarifying the structure of upcoming content.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]
- Learners from groups historically subject to low teacher expectations; explicitly high, communicated expectations combined with support improve outcomes and belonging [Belonging interventions improve student outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Self-regulated learners benefit most, since clear criteria make self-monitoring possible; learners without self-regulation skills need the expectations paired with modeling and feedback

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural and product quality: knowing what a finished, acceptable product looks like
- Self-regulated learning: enabling learners to plan, monitor, and evaluate against criteria
- Classroom discourse and collaboration: norms that make discussion and peer work productive

### Instructions
1. Define the target performance and its criteria before instruction begins, aligned with assessment ([Constructive Alignment](../patterns/constructive-alignment.md)).
2. Communicate goals and criteria in learner-facing language, using an [Advance Organizer](../elements/advance-organizers.md) or rubric.
3. Show what success looks like with annotated exemplars and [Non-Examples](../elements/non-examples.md) ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)).
4. Have learners apply the criteria themselves — self- or peer-assessment against the rubric ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)).
5. Reference expectations during work via [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) and [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) tied to the same criteria.
6. Fade explicit scaffolding of criteria as learners internalize standards ([Fading](../elements/fading.md)).

## Examples
- **Understanding by Design (Wiggins & McTighe)** — units begin with published performance criteria; teachers share rubrics with students before work begins. [https://www.ascd.org/books/understanding-by-design-expanded-2nd-edition](https://www.ascd.org/books/understanding-by-design-expanded-2nd-edition)
- **Writing rubrics in AP courses (College Board)** — publicly released scoring rubrics let students self-assess drafts against the exact criteria used by graders. [https://apcentral.collegeboard.org](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org)
- **Project-based learning entry events (PBLWorks/Buck Institute)** — projects open with a project rubric and exemplar products so students know the standard from day one. [https://www.pblworks.org](https://www.pblworks.org)
