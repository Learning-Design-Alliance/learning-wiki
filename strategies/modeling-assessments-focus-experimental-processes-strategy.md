# Assessments of modeling ability should include test items focused on experimental processes, with coupled multiple-response formats

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because iteration is common in lab activities, the authors argue that measuring students' competence with iterative aspects of modeling should be an objective of future modeling assessments. They state that "test items must focus on experimental processes as well as outcomes," and suggest that "coupled multiple- response items, like those used in the Colorado Upper- division Electrostatics Diagnostic (CUE)," may be more appropriate than multiple-choice items for assessing reasoning when prioritizing one type of iteration over another.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment objectives must include iterative aspects of modeling, which this study found to be common in lab activities
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is made for optics contexts and parallels a prior recommendation about assessing troubleshooting of electric circuits

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics lab students

### Target Learning Goals
- modeling ability in experimental physics
