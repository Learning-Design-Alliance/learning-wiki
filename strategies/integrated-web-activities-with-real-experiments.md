# Use web-assisted activities and simulations in an integrated way with real experiments rather than as a replacement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's discussion recommends that web-assisted activities and simulations be combined with traditional, hands-on teaching rather than substituting for it. Citing Hennessy et al. (2007), it notes that "by using simulations, teachers moved away from using 'real' experiments", and concludes integrated teaching techniques are preferable. It suggests such activities are most suitable when students have conceptual difficulties.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must retain real experiments alongside the digital activities
#### Constraints
- Suggested for use especially when there are conceptual disagreements or students have difficulty understanding the subject

### Target Learners
- 6th grade science students

### Target Learning Goals
- Meaningful and permanent learning of science concepts
