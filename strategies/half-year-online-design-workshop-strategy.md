# Run a half-year online design workshop combining 3D construction input, detector lectures, virtual visits and masterclasses, culminating in an in-person collaborative build

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a nation-wide call to young people aged 15 and older for a half-year workshop to construct an ALICE LEGO model. The design stage used "almost weekly meetings of 4 hours with seven tutors" and combined online design sessions with lectures on detector technology, virtual ALICE visits, masterclasses and information on studying physics. The format gave participants first-hand experience of working on sub-projects in a research collaboration and ended with a weekend in-person assembly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online video-conferencing infrastructure and participant proficiency with remote learning
- Tutors to run the weekly four-hour sessions
- A brick-ordering and sorting process for the final build
#### Constraints
- Initially 30 participants signed up but only 16 completed the final construction

### Target Learners
- young people aged 15 and older, high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- detector technology and the physics questions of ALICE
- 3D construction and design
- the dynamics of working in a research collaboration
