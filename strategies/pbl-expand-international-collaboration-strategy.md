# Expand international networks and geographical diversification to strengthen PBL research and practice in medical education

## Description
The article recommends that the PBL research community pursue greater global collaboration and geographical diversification, since international co-authorship showed the highest field-weighted citation impact while output remains institutionally and geographically concentrated. It guides medical schools to form research groups that collaborate with leading institutions or authors to develop contextualized research as the empirical basis for improving curricular design, teacher training, and pedagogical innovation. The authors state that "Collaborative work is essential to demonstrate the relevance of these emerging fields".
