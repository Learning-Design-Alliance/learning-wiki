# Use quantitative work analysis (O*NET questionnaires) to generate local competency frameworks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article demonstrates generating a national competency framework by administering standardized work-analysis questionnaires (O*NET-GWA and O*NET-WS) to a large stratified sample of practitioners, then using EFA and CFA to derive the latent competency dimensions. It argues this quantitative approach complements the qualitative methods (Delphi, behavioral event interviews) used elsewhere, and that "it is preferable to generate local competencies rather adapting or adopting those that already exist in other countries."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A stratified sample large enough for factor analysis (the article used a 1:10 to 1:15 questions-to-participants ratio)
- Randomly splitting the sample for EFA and CFA
#### Constraints
- The authors note further qualitative work (behavioral event interviews, focus groups, Delphi) might amend the preliminary model

### Target Learners
- doctors, and by extension other health professionals whose competencies are being defined

### Target Learning Goals
- establishing competency frameworks to support outcomes-based educational reform
