# Co-facilitate school prevention programs with young adult community mentors to increase student buy-in and cultural relevance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Each program session is delivered by a study-team group facilitator partnered with a "mentor," a young person from the community recruited from local colleges or organizations such as the Youth Opportunity center. Pilot work suggested that "a co-facilitator closer in age to the students and/or familiar with the students' cultural context promoted student buy-in and trust." Co-facilitators provide real-world examples of skills use; to preserve comparability, community members co-facilitate both RAP Club and Healthy Topics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A local source of young adult community members, such as employment training programs, and facilitator training restricted to one program per person
#### Constraints
- The approach is described for urban school settings; the article notes most urban settings have employment training programs or similar organizations that can serve as a source of co-facilitators

### Target Learners
- Urban middle school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Engagement with and trust in program skill content
