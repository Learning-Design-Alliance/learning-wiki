# Incorporate teleconsultation practice with automated nonverval behavior feedback at any point in a communication skills curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends giving medical students additional practice opportunities with standardized patients via teleconsultation, coupled with review of human and computer-generated feedback including nonverval behavior reports. Because the between-group difference vanished at the second assessment, the authors conclude that "EQClinic could be incorporated at any period during the teaching curriculum." The strategy targets reflection on nonverval behaviors that manual annotation is too time-consuming to provide at scale.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to standardized patients willing to conduct teleconsultations, and the EQClinic platform's scheduling, interaction, detection, and feedback components
#### Constraints
- Only 30% of students completed all study components, with dropout increasing as the semester progressed; the appropriate dose for sustained improvement remains unexamined

### Target Learners
- medical students in university clinical communication skills courses

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical communication skills including nonverval communication and reflective practice
