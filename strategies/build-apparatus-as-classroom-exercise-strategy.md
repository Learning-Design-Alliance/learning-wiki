# Use apparatus construction itself as a classroom exercise for discussing practical experimental considerations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Beyond using the finished pendulum in experiments, the authors recommend having students build the mouse-pendulum themselves as a classroom activity, so that practical measurement issues come up naturally. They state: "The ease of construction allows building the pendulum to be a good classroom exercise, during which practical experimental considerations (such as how to minimize friction) may be discussed." Because no glue is used, rods of varying lengths can be substituted during experiments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mouse casings must be cut away to access the encoder roller; a drill bit turned by hand makes the rod hole; the pendulum may be mounted directly to the roller or coupled to eliminate measurement friction
#### Constraints
- Moderate amplitude motion requires cutting back plastic near the roller; full 360-degree motion needs additional mounting hardware

### Target Learners
- high school students
- undergraduate laboratory students

### Target Learning Goals
- practical experimental skills such as minimizing measurement friction
- understanding of apparatus design and calibration
