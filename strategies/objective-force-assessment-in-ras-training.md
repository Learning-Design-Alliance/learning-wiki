# Integrate objective force-based assessment of tissue handling into robotic surgery training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 4 studies (3 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that tissue handling be measured objectively during robotic surgery training rather than assessed only by task scores, because force interaction data are scarce and tissue handling has never been the focus of basic skills curricula. They state that "objective assessment of tissue handling should be integrated into RAS training," building on their use of a force-measuring device to track learning curves of mean non-zero force and peak force across training tests.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A force-measuring device capable of capturing instrument-tissue force interaction during trial tasks
#### Constraints
- The transferability of trained tissue handling skills to the operating room remains unknown per the authors

### Target Learners
- medical students
- first-year surgical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- robotic surgery tissue handling skills
- force-controlled instrument manipulation
