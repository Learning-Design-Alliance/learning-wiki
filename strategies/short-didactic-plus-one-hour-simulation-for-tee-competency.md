# Combine a short standardized didactic with one hour of hands-on simulation to initiate foundational pediatric TEE competency during residency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating simulation training as a component of pediatric cardiac anesthesia education, based on its trial curriculum: two hours of didactic preparation plus one hour of hands-on simulation. The authors state this model "provides a streamlined alternative to prolonged specialized fellowship training for achieving initial clinical proficiency," and that foundational TEE competency can be initiated during residency. They further suggest combining simulation with clinical practice to reinforce both basic and advanced skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standardized foundational course (2-hour synchronized online didactic plus guided self-learning) preceding hands-on training
- Structured one-on-one hands-on sessions with instructor observation followed by supervised trainee practice
#### Constraints
- The trial assessed short-term skill acquisition only, not long-term retention or competency; the sample was limited to anesthesiology residents at a single center

### Target Learners
- anesthesia residents (novice TEE operators)

### Target Learning Goals
- independent acquisition of the 11 standardized basic perioperative TEE views in pediatric patients
