School personnel practices to promote belonging for students with chronic medical conditions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends concrete school-level actions: "acknowledging the extra struggle that a student with a chronic medical condition may experience and communicating empathy can promote perceived belonging"; creating a safe environment for students who feel different because of absence or visible signs of condition; and expressing awareness of less obvious issues such as fatigue and processing speed delays. It also suggests offering high-risk students specific opportunities for positive teacher and peer relationships.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • School staff (teachers, administrators, coaches) must understand the importance of perceived belonging for all students, especially those with chronic conditions

Constraints¶

  • The article states these are ways findings can be addressed in the school setting; it did not experimentally test these specific practices

Target Learners¶

  • students with chronic medical conditions
  • adolescents at risk of not completing high school on time

Claims¶

  • Perceived school belonging predicts on-time high school completion more strongly for students with chronic medical conditions than for healthy peers [+W]
  • Students with chronic medical conditions are less likely to complete high school on time than their healthy peers [+W]
  • Adolescents with chronic medical conditions report lower perceived school belonging than their healthy peers [+W]
  • Students with chronic medical conditions show demographic differences from healthy peers in the Add Health subsamples [+W]
  • Health status predicts perceived school belonging beyond demographic controls [+W]
