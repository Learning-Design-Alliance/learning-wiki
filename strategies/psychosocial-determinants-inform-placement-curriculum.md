# Use knowledge of salient psychosocial determinants to design curricula that identify struggling students early and foster adaptability and self-efficacy before and during placements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends translating its determinants findings into curriculum design and support structures. It states that "Knowledge of salient psychosocial determinants associated with clinical performance can inform curriculum design", including "practical strategies for early identification of students who may struggle in clinical placement settings, and ways to foster salient student skills and abilities before and during placements to optimise clinical placement performance outcomes". The strategy targets the factors the study found credible: adaptability, clinical self-efficacy, perfectionistic strivings, and stressor and adversity exposure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Measurement of student psychosocial factors such as adaptability and clinical self-efficacy before and during placements
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is observational; the article has not tested whether interventions targeting these factors improve performance
