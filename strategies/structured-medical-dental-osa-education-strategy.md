# Targeted education and system-level change: structured craniofacial and oral-appliance modules in medical curricula, competency-based CDE, national referral guidelines, and integrated medical-dental referral pathways

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a four-part education and system-level response to the measured knowledge gaps: adding structured modules to MBBS programs on craniofacial assessment and oral appliance therapy, competency-based continuing education covering PSG interpretation and referral timing, adoption of national referral guidelines such as the AADSM's, and integration of dentists into sleep medicine practices with digital referral pathways. It argues these measures would "create a better foundation of competencies for residents" and enable GPs to identify and refer suitable patients to dental sleep medicine specialists.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires institutional commitment to curriculum updates in MBBS programs and CPD/CME structures that include dental-sleep content
#### Constraints
- The article frames this in the Indian context, where medical and dental systems are mainly parallel with limited interdisciplinary training and referral systems

### Target Learners
- General medical practitioners (allopathic and AYUSH)
- Medical undergraduates and residents
- Dentists seeking dental sleep medicine certification

### Target Learning Goals
- Recognising craniofacial risk factors for OSA
- Interpreting PSG results
- Knowing when and how to refer for oral appliance therapy or surgical intervention
