# Muon lifetime measurement project teaching coincidence, veto, and exponential decay

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Students stack three scintillator boxes and measure the mean lifetime of muons from the characteristic two-pulse signature of a stopping muon and its decay electron, using the downmost channel as a veto against coincident muons. The recorded time intervals are fitted with an exponential function in muonic. The article notes this experiment "illustrates also the measurement of statistically fluctuating quantities as well as the law of exponential decay" and introduces special relativity discussions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A measurement time of about one week is required to gather enough statistics to be confident in the fit
#### Constraints
- For some scintillators the results are affected by electronic noise; very short time intervals can be excluded from the fit via a GUI element to suppress this noise contribution

### Target Learners
- students learning particle-physics measurement techniques

### Target Learning Goals
- coincidence and veto techniques, statistical fluctuations, exponential decay, special relativity
