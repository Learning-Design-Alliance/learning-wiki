# Course-based near-peer mentorship as a scalable alternative to faculty mentorship in large-enrollment STEM programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends course-embedded near-peer mentorship—pairing first-year students with third- or fourth-year students with similar goals and backgrounds—as a scalable response to student:faculty ratios that make universal faculty mentorship infeasible at large institutions. The authors conclude that "course-based near-peer mentorship may be an effective and scalable approach that can promote student academic success", embedding mentorship inside a credit-bearing seminar so support reaches large, diverse cohorts rather than only students who seek out research-based mentoring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Upper-division mentors with similar goals and backgrounds, trained in mentorship techniques
- A credit-bearing course structure with weekly structured assignments
#### Constraints
- The article notes rigorous empirical evidence on near-peer mentorship effectiveness and mechanisms remains thin, and that its own evidence is observational with self-selected enrollment

### Target Learners
- first-year STEM undergraduates at large-enrollment public universities, including PEERs and first-generation students

### Target Learning Goals
- transition to university academic expectations, effective study habits, and short-term retention in the major
