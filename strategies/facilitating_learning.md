# Facilitating Learning

## Description
Facilitating learning repositions the teacher from primary source of content to designer and guide of student activity. The facilitator structures tasks, poses questions, mediates [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) and [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md), and distributes authority over topics, materials, and pace to students. In culturally diverse classrooms, this shift also functions as an equity move: students gain power over what and how they learn, which raises intrinsic motivation and connectedness to the material [Autonomy support improves intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S].

## Design Implications

Facilitation works only when it is structured, not laissez-faire: guided forms of student-centered learning outperform unguided discovery, and pure minimally-guided instruction is less effective than explicit teaching for novices [Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than explicit instruction.](../claims/minimal-guidance-less-effective-for-novices.md) [-S]. Effective facilitators therefore scaffold inquiry, monitor progress, and intervene contingently rather than stepping back entirely [Contingent scaffolding improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]. Giving students genuine choices about topics and materials supports autonomy and motivation [Autonomy support improves intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S], and participatory, interactive formats improve achievement relative to lecture alone [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks with enough structure that students can make progress without continuous direction ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md), clear goals, worked entry points)
- Formative monitoring so the facilitator knows when to intervene ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
- Willingness to share authority: topic questionnaires, student-selected readings, and student-led presentations
- Norms and routines for peer interaction ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))

#### Constraints
- Minimally guided facilitation harms novices, who lack the schemas to benefit from open inquiry [Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than explicit instruction.](../claims/minimal-guidance-less-effective-for-novices.md) [-S] — the facilitator role must be paired with scaffolds for learners with low prior knowledge
- Students accustomed to teacher-directed classrooms may initially resist or flounder with increased autonomy; autonomy support works when it is gradually transferred, not abruptly granted [~M]
- Facilitation requires more planning and in-the-moment diagnosis than lecturing; under-resourced or overloaded teachers often default to transmission
- Cultural mismatch: in some classroom cultures, a reduced teacher role reads as disengagement rather than empowerment [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Spectrum from light-touch facilitation of [Discussion-Based Learning](../patterns/discussion-based-learning.md) to full [Collaborative Inquiry](../patterns/collaborative-inquiry.md) projects
- Student-selected reading and presentation of culturally relevant material as a low-cost entry point
- Rotating facilitation among students to distribute authority further

### Target Learners
- Students with moderate to strong prior knowledge, who can productively direct their own inquiry [Minimal guidance is less effective for novices than explicit instruction.](../claims/minimal-guidance-less-effective-for-novices.md) [~S]
- Students in culturally diverse classrooms, where shared authority improves belonging and engagement [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Less suitable, without heavy scaffolding, for complete novices in a domain

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding through discussion and inquiry rather than transmission
- Self-regulated learning: students practice directing their own goals and monitoring
- Motivation and engagement: ownership of topics and materials [Autonomy support improves intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. Survey student interests and cultural perspectives (questionnaires, interest inventories) and let them shape topic selection.
2. Design a structured task or question set with entry-point support ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md), [Case-Based Learning](../elements/case-based-learning.md)).
3. Launch with brief framing or modeling, then hand activity to students ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md)).
4. Circulate, question, and scaffold contingently — intervene when groups stall, not preemptively [Contingent scaffolding improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M].
5. Have students share and present their own materials and findings ([Application](../elements/application.md), [Articulation](../elements/articulation.md)).
6. Debrief both content and process, and adjust the degree of autonomy for the next cycle.

## Related Strategies

## Examples
- **[Collaborative Learning](../patterns/collaborative-learning.md)** — the facilitator designs group tasks and monitors, while groups carry the cognitive work; structured cooperative formats show reliable achievement gains [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]
- **[Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md)** — the facilitator models early, then fades into coaching as students take over the work
- **Student-selected readings seminar (K–12 and higher ed)** — students bring in materials reflecting their cultural and social perspectives and present them to the class, with the teacher facilitating discussion rather than evaluating content
- **[Flipped Classroom](../patterns/flipped-classroom.md)** — content delivery moves outside class time so the facilitator can devote face-to-face time to guided student activity
