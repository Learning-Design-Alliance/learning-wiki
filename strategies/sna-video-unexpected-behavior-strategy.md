# Use SNA on classroom video to identify groups and individuals interacting in unexpected ways and assess program community goals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends applying SNA to video-coded interaction networks as an assessment approach: graphing on-topic ties over time to check whether group behavior shows "a balance of engagement with science and community formation" or unexpected patterns, then using directional network maps to trace individual contributions behind group-level trends. The authors used this to flag one table that may not be developing as a community and one individual driving off-topic interaction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video data of group activities, the clip-based directional on-topic/off-topic coding scheme, and normalization by possible ties when group sizes differ
#### Constraints
- Demonstrated on a single activity from one two-week program; the authors state the preliminary analysis "only begins to scratch the surface" and plan to extend to other activities, other time points, and ties beyond within-group interactions

### Target Learners
- students in small-group science activities
- first-generation and DHH students

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing supportive community development and engagement in authentic science practice
