# Add external critical-care rotations and a more standardised hybrid training structure to increase procedural exposure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that relying solely on ED rotations may not yield sufficient exposure, citing that a trainee would perform only about 13 ETIs during ED-specific training time. It "reinforces the need for external rotations in critical care specialities" and suggests a hybrid approach combining standardisation, akin to the UK Acute Care Common Stem, with the current flexible programme.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainees must complete external rotations in critical care specialities such as ICU or anaesthesia
#### Constraints
- The Australasian programme currently leaves placement location to trainees, who source their own employment

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine trainees in a 5-year programme

### Target Learning Goals
- Achieving procedural competency through sufficient procedure volume
