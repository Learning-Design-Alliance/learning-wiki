Trainers of chemical-handling workers should adopt concept attainment as part of their training programs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In the conclusions the article recommends that workplace trainers become acquainted with CATM and implement it, noting the method is little known in the cleaning sector and has mostly been limited to schools and universities. The article states it "would be beneficial for trainers of workers, whether from external training institutions or those in-charges of companies' internal training programs to familiarize themselves with the method, and consider implementing it as part of their training programs", and calls for increased awareness of the method among trainers.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Awareness-building among trainers about the method and its demonstrated effectivity

Constraints¶

  • The evidence base for this recommendation in this article is a single quasi-experimental study with cleaning workers in Norway

Target Learners¶

  • adult workers handling chemicals
  • workers with low-level formal education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • chemicals health hazard knowledge
  • workplace safety competence

Affordances¶

  • Concept Attainment Teaching Methodology

Claims¶

  • Knowledge retained one month after CATM training, with post-test2 scores exceeding post-test1 in both periods [+W]
  • CATM training produced statistically significant knowledge gains among cleaning workers in both training periods [+W]
  • CATM-trained cleaning workers outperformed certified cleaners and supervisors on chemicals-hazard knowledge [+W]
  • Switching from written short-answer to multiple-choice test format did not substantially change score levels [+W]
