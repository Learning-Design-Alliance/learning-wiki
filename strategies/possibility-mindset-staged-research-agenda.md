# Adopt a staged research agenda: measure the three belief dimensions, test incremental validity, and run component-level and person-centered tests

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article outlines a staged research agenda for measurement and incremental validity testing of possibility mindset. It specifies that the representation supports both component-level tests, in which each dimension independently predicts outcomes, and person-centered tests, in which recurring profiles emerge across classrooms and campuses. It also proposes testing whether the core possibility dimensions add predictive power beyond established constructs such as belonging expectations, opportunity beliefs, and momentary affective windows.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Measurement instruments must capture environment-change and pathway-flexibility beliefs alongside self-change beliefs, treating mixed profiles as instructionally diagnostic rather than nuisance variance
#### Constraints
- Whether the three components cohere as a higher-order latent factor is an empirical question, but the article states it is not the theoretical payoff on which the paper rests

### Target Learners
- Students in educational settings with varying degrees of structural constraint

### Target Learning Goals
- Predicting persistence, withdrawal timing, help-seeking patterns, and strategy switching

### Affordances
- Possibility Mindset Three Belief Dimensions
