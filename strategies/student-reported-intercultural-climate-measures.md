# Use student-reported measures of intercultural education and teacher attitudes when monitoring school social climate

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because student-reported intercultural education practices and teacher attitudes showed consistent positive associations with belonging, wellbeing, and (for attitudes) lower victimization, while principal- and teacher-reported predictors "showed negligible effects", schools monitoring social cohesion should capture students' own perceptions of intercultural practices and teacher attitudes rather than relying solely on institutional reports.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to student-level survey data on perceptions of intercultural education practices and teacher intercultural attitudes
#### Constraints
- Findings are cross-sectional associations from PISA 2018 and do not establish causal effects

### Target Learners
- Students in diverse secondary school classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- School belonging, wellbeing, and reduced peer victimization
