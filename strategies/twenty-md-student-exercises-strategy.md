# Twenty open-ended student exercises using an interactive molecular dynamics simulation in undergraduate thermal physics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Section VI presents a collection of twenty numbered exercises, from basic phase behavior and comparison to an ideal gas through free expansion, heat capacities, critical point, phase diagram, velocity and speed distributions, atmosphere in a gravitational field, thermal expansion, Brownian motion, fluctuations, reversibility and chaos, thermal conductivity, and diffusion. The article states: "The following exercises are designed for use in undergraduate courses at the beginning and intermediate levels." Many require a spreadsheet or plotting software, and instructors should supplement them with software-specific instructions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Software that provides a means of collecting the required data
- a spreadsheet program or other plotting software for many exercises
- software-specific instructions for configuring the system and acquiring the needed data
#### Constraints
- The exercises use natural units, though they could be augmented by asking students to convert results to conventional units

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students at the beginning and intermediate levels

### Target Learning Goals
- thermal physics concepts illustrated with numerical data for a nontrivial system
- recognizing when idealizations of undergraduate courses break down
