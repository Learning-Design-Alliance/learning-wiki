# Three-arrangement ring-magnet demonstration for quantitatively comparing action and reaction forces

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An implementable classroom activity in which students measure pressures p0 through p4 for three arrangements of two ring magnets on a vertical PVC pipe: each magnet separately, the pair in attractive mode, and the pair in repulsive mode where the top magnet floats. From these readings students compute the 'action' force via Eq. (5) and the 'reaction' force via Eq. (7) and compare them, seeing "how 'action' and 'reaction' are quantitatively equal and opposite". The comparison of nearly equal computed forces provides clear quantitative evidence of the third law.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The calibrated smartphone-in-Ziploc apparatus with effective area known
- Two almost identical ring magnets and a PVC pipe passing through their holes
#### Constraints
- The method measures force via pressure change, so it presumes the calibrated effective area and the bag-based setup described in the article

### Target Learners
- secondary-level physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- quantitative understanding that reaction to an action is opposite in direction and equal in magnitude
