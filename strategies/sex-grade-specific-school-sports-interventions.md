# School-based sports team participation interventions, tailored by sex and grade, to promote academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends applying school-based interventions that incorporate sports team participation to promote better academic performance in adolescents, with tailoring by demographic characteristics. It states that "school-based interventions incorporating sports team participation can be applied to promote better academic performance in adolescents" and that "sex- or age-specific interventions are recommended when designing strategies to promote academic performance." The tailoring follows from the study's findings that the dose-dependent association varied across sexes and grades, with females and higher graders named as examples of groups needing differentiated approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adolescents must have opportunities to participate in team sports, whether community-based or school-based, since the study's operationalized definition could cover either
#### Constraints
- The article notes maintaining sport participation by adolescents is a challenging matter, which could be a barrier to gaining benefits
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional, so a causal association between sports participation and academic achievement could not be drawn

### Target Learners
- High school students in grades 9–12 (adolescents aged 14–17)

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved self-reported academic performance (mostly A's or B's grades)
