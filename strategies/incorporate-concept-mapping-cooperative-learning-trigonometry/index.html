Mathematics teachers should incorporate concept mapping and cooperative learning strategies in senior secondary trigonometry instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that both strategies significantly outperformed conventional teaching, the authors recommend that mathematics "teachers especially those teaching the subject in the senior secondary schools in Nigeria should learn to incorporate the use of concept mapping and cooperative learning strategies". They further recommend that curriculum planners embed both strategies in curriculum guidelines and that textbook authors include them among different pedagogical approaches.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers learn the strategies through training; the article recommends seminars, workshops and symposia organized by government and associations like the Mathematical Association of Nigeria

Constraints¶

  • Recommendation grounded in a single quasi-experimental study in Kano metropolis, Nigeria, on trigonometry achievement only

Target Learners¶

  • SSII senior secondary school students in Nigeria

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved achievement and interest in mathematics, especially trigonometry

Claims¶

  • SSII students taught trigonometry with concept mapping or cooperative learning achieved significantly higher post-test scores than students taught with conventional methods [+W]
  • Scheffe post-hoc comparisons ranked concept mapping highest, followed by cooperative learning, then conventional teaching, with all pairwise differences significant [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Adopt cooperative TGT as an alternative mathematics teaching approach, supported by teacher training and ministry guidelines
