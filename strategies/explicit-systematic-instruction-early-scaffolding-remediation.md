# Provide explicit, systematic reading instruction with heavy scaffolding in the first phases for students needing remediation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), `q3`–`q4` · 2 of 2 report an effect size

## Description
Based on the null results, the authors recommend that curricula include "explicit, systematic instruction approaches" for students needing remediation in reading skills that bring them to grade level, and that intervention programs provide "a great deal of support and scaffolding during the first phases of instruction." The intent of the scaffolding is to lead learners to an independent skill level with the least amount of confusion and error. The article also stresses that the instructional environment must allow teachers to assist, accommodate, and rearrange skills to meet struggling readers' needs within the high school day.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instructional environment in which teachers are willing to make the necessary requirements to assist, accommodate, and rearrange skills within the high school day
#### Constraints
- Recommended for students who did not meet proficient levels and did not respond to initial teacher instruction, not for all readers

### Target Learners
- high school students who did not respond to initial reading instruction and need remediation

### Target Learning Goals
- bringing struggling readers to grade-level reading proficiency
