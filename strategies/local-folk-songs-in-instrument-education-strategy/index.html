Use local folk songs adjusted to the instrument in music and instrument education¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that folk songs belonging to a region be analysed, adjusted to the teaching instrument, and included in individual instrument curricula, because the study found such materials contributed to performance and motivation. They write that "It must be a duty for all music educators to own this value and make it available in music education", and extend the recommendation to other provinces and to orchestra/room music arrangements.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Folk songs must first be analysed for modal structure and playing techniques and adjusted to the target instrument

Constraints¶

  • The supporting experiment involved only six viola students at one faculty, so the recommendation's evidence base is small

Target Learners¶

  • music teacher candidates in fine arts and music education departments
  • individual instrument students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • instrument performance
  • cultural knowledge and evaluation of one's own culture
  • transfer of culture to the next generation

Claims¶

  • Viola students taught with Erzurum folk songs adjusted to viola scored significantly higher on viola playing skills at post-test than control students taught transposed works [+W]
  • Experimental and control viola students did not differ significantly in playing skills at pre-test [+W]
  • The three selected Erzurum folk songs are modal works analysable into maqam structures and viola playing techniques [+W]
  • Experimental group's within-group pre-to-post change in viola playing skills was not statistically significant despite a large descriptive mean increase [~W]
  • Control group viola playing skills did not change significantly over the experimental period [+W]
