# Storytelling in Explainer Animations and Videos

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy embeds instructional content within a narrative structure — a character, problem, and resolution — delivered through animation or video. The video typically opens with a realistic situation the audience recognizes, introduces the concept as a means of resolving the situation, and closes with the outcome. Narrative context gives discrete facts a causal structure, which supports encoding and retrieval far better than isolated assertions.

## Design Implications

Storytelling works because narratives induce *transportation* — absorption into a story world — which reduces counterarguing and increases elaboration of story-consistent content [Narrative transportation increases story-consistent beliefs and reduces counterarguing.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701) [+M]. However, the multimedia design matters as much as the story: narration and animation must be coordinated, and seductive but irrelevant detail (including emotionally engaging story elements unrelated to the learning goal) actively harms retention [Decorative illustrations and interesting-but-irrelevant details do not improve and can impair learning.](../claims/decorative-illustrations-do-not-improve-learning.md) [-S]. The story must carry the concept, not merely decorate it.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A story whose problem structure maps onto the concept being taught — the concept should be the mechanism by which the character's problem is resolved
- Coordinated narration and visuals following multimedia design principles (segmenting, signaling, conversational narration) [Multimedia design principles improve learning from narrated animation.](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511811678) [+S]
- Tools for producing animation or video (e.g., Vyond, Powtoon, After Effects, or simple screencast tools)
- Chunked segments — keep explainer videos short and pause-able to manage processing load [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Production cost is high; poorly designed stories confuse more than they teach
- Emotionally vivid but conceptually irrelevant story elements create seductive details that depress retention and transfer [Cognitive overload degrades learning when extraneous processing exceeds working memory capacity.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-S]
- Learners with high prior knowledge may find a slow narrative framing redundant and prefer direct exposition [Expertise reversal: guidance effective for novices becomes less effective or harmful as expertise grows.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2009.11.001) [~M]
- A single story provides one context; transfer to dissimilar situations is limited unless multiple varied cases are presented [Cognitive flexibility theory: multiple representations and cases needed for transfer to ill-structured domains.](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep3102_3) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Character-driven explainer** — a recurring persona encounters problems across a course, building continuity
- **Problem-first cold open** — present the dilemma before naming the concept, letting learners generate hypotheses first [Activation of prior knowledge improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- **Story + worked example hybrid** — the narrative sets up the problem, then shifts to a [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) of the solution procedure
- **Learner-authored stories** — learners create their own explainer animations, converting consumption into generation

### Target Learners
- Novices, who benefit from concrete narrative context anchoring abstract concepts [Anchored instruction improves learning when instruction is anchored in realistic problem contexts.](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1802_1) [+M]
- Learners with low motivation for the topic, for whom narrative interest provides initial engagement [Narrative transportation increases story-consistent beliefs and reduces counterarguing.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701) [+M]
- Less beneficial for advanced learners, who extract concepts faster from concise expository formats [Expertise reversal: guidance effective for novices becomes less effective or harmful as expertise grows.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2009.11.001) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: explaining *why* a phenomenon occurs (the story supplies the causal chain)
- Attitude and behavior change: narratives are especially effective for persuasion and health/safety messaging [Narrative transportation increases story-consistent beliefs and reduces counterarguing.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701) [+M]
- Retention of factual content embedded in a memorable episode structure

### Instructions
1. Identify the single concept the video must teach and the realistic problem it solves; discard story elements that do not serve that concept.
2. Draft a story arc: familiar situation → complication → concept introduced as the resolution mechanism → outcome.
3. Storyboard visuals alongside narration, applying [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) to keep each segment under a few minutes.
4. Produce the animation following multimedia principles: narration over redundant on-screen text, signaling of key elements, conversational tone [Multimedia design principles improve learning from narrated animation.](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511811678) [+S].
5. Pair viewing with an activity — prediction prompts before, application or discussion after — rather than passive watching [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S].
6. Assess transfer with a novel scenario, not recall of the story itself.

## Patterns That Use This Strategy
- [Anchored Instruction](../patterns/anchored-instruction.md) — the story is the anchor: a realistic problem context in which knowledge is developed and used
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — narrative can model expert decision-making in context

## Examples
- **[TED-Ed](https://ed.ted.com)** — animated lessons that open with a concrete question or scenario (e.g., "Why do we dream?") before delivering the concept, with follow-up quiz and discussion items on the lesson page.
- **[Kurzgesagt – In a Nutshell](https://www.youtube.com/@kurzgesagt)** — science explainers that personify abstract systems (cells, immune responses) as characters in a conflict, making causal mechanisms visible.
- **Workplace compliance eLearning** — a character navigating a stressful deadline is used to teach stress-management techniques; the technique is shown resolving the character's problem, then learners apply it to their own described situation.
