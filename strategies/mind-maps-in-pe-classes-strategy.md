# Use mind maps as an in-class learning-teaching instrument in physical education to develop cognitive and psychomotor skills together

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends applying mind maps as an in-class learning-teaching instrument in physical education so that lessons no longer prioritize only the psychomotor domain. In the study, the experimental group received 40-minute mind map lessons weekly for 9 weeks alongside practice taught with a spectrum of teaching styles (command, practice, reciprocal, self-check), while the control group used conventional teacher-centered instruction. The authors concluded the maps "were usable for the volleyball unit" and turned PE into a course that also pays importance to cognitive development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- One week of introduction to the mind map technique before the intervention
- Regular feedback on the mind maps created by students
- Planning topics so both groups learn the same topics at the same time
#### Constraints
- The technique has been found to lead to time loss during classes, and students experience problems expressing every desired concept visually; it might not be usable in every field, course, or topic, and teachers cannot check whether students work on their maps after school

### Target Learners
- 6th-grade secondary school students in physical education

### Target Learning Goals
- Volleyball unit cognitive knowledge and psychomotor skill development

### Affordances
- Mind Map Technique Visual Associative
