# Conduct virtual patients as a collaborative interactive activity within a lecture rather than as an independent session after the lecture

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends running branching-tree virtual patients as a facilitated, discussion-based group activity embedded in the lecture, where a tutor asks why decisions were taken, presents consequences of alternatives, gives feedback, and operates the VP based on students' consensus decisions. The article reports that "using VPs in a collaborative learning activity enhances students’ situational interest with minimal investment in cognitive load than its use as an independent learning activity after the lecture."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A tutor-facilitated session with projector-displayed VPs, structured student discussion to reach consensus, and continuous instructor feedback during the VP decisions
#### Constraints
- The article's evidence is limited to one maxillofacial trauma topic, 4th-year dental students, and a small number of VPs

### Target Learners
- 4th-year dental (undergraduate health-professions) students

### Target Learning Goals
- Diagnosis and treatment planning of mandibular fractures; clinical decision-making in maxillofacial trauma
