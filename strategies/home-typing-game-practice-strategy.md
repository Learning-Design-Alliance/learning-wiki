# Assign short, gamified home typing practice as a low-cost school or parent-delivered fine motor intervention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing a freely available, easy-to-administer typing-game intervention to help children improve manual dexterity, with parents supporting delivery at home. Children were asked to "play this game 5 times a week for around 10 min each time, based on recommendations from a meta-analysis of intervention studies", with consistency monitored via email and a diary. The authors conclude this gives "an exciting premise for future studies to explore this intervention in children with clinically diagnosed fine motor difficulty and in children with dysgraphia."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Parental oversight and weekly progress checks; a diary recording game chosen, dates played, and levels progressed
#### Constraints
- The study tested typically developing children; application to children with dysgraphia or developmental coordination disorder is proposed as future research, not yet tested

### Target Learners
- children aged 8–10 years in mainstream schools

### Target Learning Goals
- manual dexterity and fine motor skill improvement
