Design learning activities that develop empathy and emotional intelligence alongside environmental content, and allow time for attitude change¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's suggestions section recommends that learning materials address issues important in daily life and engage students' interest, and that "activities should be designed fo r students' emotional intelligence" while developing environmental awareness. It also cautions that "the development of attitudes in teaching process will not occur in a short time, so learning activi ties should be designed considering this case." This strategy pairs EI-enriched activities with realistic timelines for attitudinal outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learning materials related to issues of great importance in daily life that engage students' interest

Constraints¶

  • Attitude development will not occur in a short time, so activities must be designed with that in mind

Target Learners¶

  • primary and secondary school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • environmental attitudes
  • empathy skills
  • emotional intelligence

Claims¶

  • Web-assisted instruction with emotional intelligence content produced higher post-test academic achievement, environmental attitudes and emotional intelligence than traditional lecture in 8th-grade energy-saving instruction [+W]
  • When pre-test scores were controlled, the software had a significant effect on academic achievement, with the model explaining 44% of variance [+W]
  • Within the experimental group, AAT, EIS and EAS scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test [+W]
