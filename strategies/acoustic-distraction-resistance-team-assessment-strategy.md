# Assess surgical team skill by measuring the team's resistance to acoustic distraction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose a new assessment approach in which a surgical team's skill is gauged by how little its performance degrades when the acoustic environment shifts from pleasant music to noise. They write that "the negative linear relationship between team experience and impact of acoustic condition creates an opportunity for us to assess the skills of a surgical team by examining its resistance to environmental noise." This would supplement existing surgical team performance instruments and serve the long-term goal of improving team performance and patient safety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teams must perform the same task under at least two acoustic conditions so a music-noisy difference time can be computed.
- A validated measure of team experience or skill is needed to interpret the difference time.
#### Constraints
- The authors state "further validation is needed before we can comfortably introduce this approach for assessing team performance."
- Results may not generalize to other surgical specialties because collaboration patterns differ.

### Target Learners
- surgical residents
- surgical teams in simulation training

### Target Learning Goals
- team collaboration and communication skills
- laparoscopic psychomotor and coordination skills
