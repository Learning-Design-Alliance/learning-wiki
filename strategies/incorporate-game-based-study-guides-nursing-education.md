# Incorporate game-based mobile study guides into nursing education for clinical assessment skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that "it is recommended that nursing education incorporate mobile applications designed as game-based study guides," arguing that such guides let learners gain experience and solve clinical problems in safe, simulated environments without involving actual patients. The recommendation rests on the study's own finding of higher post-intervention knowledge and practical skills in the intervention group, plus converging prior literature on serious games and mobile apps in nursing education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An Android-compatible device for the evaluated version; content must be clinically accurate and reviewed by experts.
#### Constraints
- The authors note the evaluation was in a simulation environment and lacked follow-up, advising clinical-setting assessment before broader judgments.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical assessment skills such as head trauma evaluation
