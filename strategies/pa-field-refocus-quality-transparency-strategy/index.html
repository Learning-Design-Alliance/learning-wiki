Refocus physical activity research on study quality, transparent reporting, fidelity monitoring and differentiation of intervention characteristics¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's authors recommend that the field improve primary study and review quality, transparency of reporting and dissemination, and differentiate between intervention characteristics such as dose and type so findings can meaningfully inform policy. They state the field "needs to refocus its efforts towards improving study quality, transparency of reporting and dissemination". Specific recommendations include long-term follow-up, brain imaging outcomes, monitoring PA dose, reporting effect sizes, examining qualitative PA aspects, and preregistering analysis plans.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Reviews should include a-priori designs, overviews of excluded studies, risk-of-bias assessment and consideration of study quality in synthesis, per the critical AMSTAR-2 domains the article lists

Constraints¶

  • The article states current conclusions are inconsistent, so recommendations aim at future research rather than established effects

Claims¶

  • Evidence for physical activity benefits on academic achievement in youth is inconsistent: six of twelve reviews concluded benefits, six mixed or inconclusive [+W]
  • Eight of thirteen reviews concluded physical activity benefits cognitive performance, five reported mixed or inconclusive evidence, and none suggested worse cognition [+W]
  • Meta-analyses of physical activity effects on youth cognition and academic performance show small effect sizes (0.1–0.3) with substantial heterogeneity [+W]

Related Strategies¶
