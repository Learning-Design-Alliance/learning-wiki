# Address self-efficacy, social belonging, and growth mindset, help students interpret performance accurately, and provide recognition for effort

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors support persistence of all students, especially women, by addressing motivational factors: promoting a growth mindset with activities that scaffold experiences with adversity, helping students interpret grades accurately (e.g., a B is above average), providing micro-affirmations during group work, training TAs to avoid discouraging responses, and giving explicit recognition for students' efforts or improvement. It notes psychological-intervention research shows these "can have a long-lasting beneficial impact that reduces achievement gaps."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions are used at the beginning of the term, and the article states "the culture of the class must be consonant with the message of the interventions."
#### Constraints
- The recommendations draw on prior research cited by the authors; the present study measured GPA trends, not the interventions' effects.

### Target Learners

- women in STEM majors
- first-year introductory STEM students

### Target Learning Goals
- STEM major persistence
- self-efficacy
