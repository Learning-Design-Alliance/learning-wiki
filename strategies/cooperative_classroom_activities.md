# Cooperative Classroom Activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Cooperative classroom activities structure learning tasks so that students work together toward shared goals, with success depending on the contributions of every group member. Unlike merely seating students together, genuine cooperation requires [positive interdependence](../elements/positive-interdependence.md) — a "sink or swim together" task structure — combined with individual accountability, so that each student's learning is both supported by and responsible to the group [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability reliably improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S].

## Design Implications

Cooperative activities outperform purely competitive or individualistic structures on achievement, peer relationships, and motivation when they are deliberately engineered rather than assumed [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability reliably improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]. Social interdependence theory explains why: when outcomes are linked positively, students engage in *promotive interaction* — explaining, elaborating, and checking each other's understanding — which produces the elaboration and rehearsal that individual work often lacks [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability reliably improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task genuinely requiring multiple perspectives or divided labor — not a worksheet that one student can complete alone
- [Positive interdependence](../elements/positive-interdependence.md): shared goals, rewards, resources, or roles that link member outcomes
- [Individual accountability](../elements/individual-accountability.md): each member's learning is separately assessed or visible, preventing social loafing [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability reliably improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]
- Explicit teaching of collaborative skills (turn-taking, disagreement norms) and time for [group processing](../elements/group-processing.md) — reflection on how the team worked

#### Constraints
- Groups without individual accountability invite social loafing and free-riding, eroding achievement gains [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability reliably improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [-S]
- Group rewards tied only to a single group product (no individual assessment) show weak or null effects on individual learning [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability reliably improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [~S]
- Unstructured "group work" where one student does the work while others watch produces neither cooperation nor learning [-M]
- Highly competitive classroom climates can undermine the peer norms cooperative structures depend on [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- [Jigsaw classroom](jigsaw-classroom.md): each member masters a unique piece and teaches it to teammates — interdependence built into the task itself
- [Think-pair-share](../patterns/think-pair-share.md): low-overhead pairs structure for discussion before whole-class sharing
- [Peer tutoring](peer-tutoring.md): asymmetric cooperation where explaining benefits both tutor and tutee
- [Group roles](../elements/group-roles.md): assigned functions (recorder, skeptic, facilitator) distribute participation and prevent dominance

### Target Learners
- Students across achievement levels benefit, but low achievers gain the most from peer explanation and modeling [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability reliably improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]
- High achievers benefit from the elaboration required to teach others, though gains are smaller than for low achievers [+M]
- Students with weak peer relationships or low academic self-confidence, since cooperative success experiences build belonging and efficacy [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding that benefits from verbal elaboration and [self-explanation](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]
- Collaborative and communication skills as explicit outcomes
- Complex, ill-structured tasks where multiple perspectives improve solution quality [~M]

### Instructions
1. Define a shared goal that cannot be met by one person alone; build in [positive interdependence](../elements/positive-interdependence.md) through divided resources, complementary roles, or a joint product.
2. Build in [individual accountability](../elements/individual-accountability.md): individual quizzes, random member reporting, or individually graded components.
3. Assign [group roles](../elements/group-roles.md) and teach the interpersonal skills the task requires before starting.
4. Monitor groups during work, intervening with [coaching](../elements/coaching.md) on both content and collaboration.
5. Close with [group processing](../elements/group-processing.md): a brief structured reflection on what the team did well and what to change next time.

## Examples
- **[Jigsaw classroom](jigsaw-classroom.md)** — Aronson's original desegregation design: students become "experts" on one segment of material in expert groups, then teach it to their home teams.
- **[Think-pair-share](../patterns/think-pair-share.md)** — Instructor poses a question; students think silently, discuss with a partner, then share with the class.
- **Team Accelerated Instruction (TAI)** — Slavin's math program combining team-based practice with individual mastery assessments, one of the best-validated cooperative curricula.
- **[Kagan Structures](https://www.kaganonline.com)** — A library of named cooperative structures (e.g., RoundRobin, Numbered Heads Together) that operationalize the four principles of PIES (Positive interdependence, Individual accountability, Equal participation, Simultaneous interaction).
