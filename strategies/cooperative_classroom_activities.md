# Cooperative Classroom Activities

## Description
Design classroom activities that involve cooperative work rather than competitive or individualistic work. Research suggests that learners are more motivated and successful when working in groups. Learners feel a sense of responsibility to the group to try their best, and this leads to a positive feedback loop of effort and success, encouraging the development of a growth mindset.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires careful planning to design cooperative activities that are engaging and meaningful. Instructors need to provide clear guidelines and support to help learners work effectively in groups.
#### Constraints
- Some learners may prefer to work individually or may struggle to work effectively in groups. Instructors need to address these challenges and provide appropriate support.

### Affordances
- Encourages learners to work together and support each other, creating a positive learning environment. Helps learners develop a sense of responsibility to the group and experience the positive feedback loop of effort and success.

### Personalization
- Groups can be formed based on learner interests, skill levels, or learning styles. Tasks can be assigned based on individual strengths and areas for improvement.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learner engagement in cooperative activities, monitor their success rates, and assess their sense of responsibility and teamwork. Compare the effectiveness of cooperative activities versus competitive or individualistic activities.

## Impact
- Research suggests that learners are more motivated and successful when working in groups. Students feel a sense of responsibility to the group to try their best, and thus will experience the positive feedback loop of effort and success, encouraging the development of a growth mindset.
