# Support shape exploration in sonification tools with a tactile frame or a tablet implementation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
To improve shape recognition through sonification, the article recommends adding "a support or tactile frame to guide the hand of the participants", such as a transparent plexiglass surface that lets the webcam track the hand while giving the user a haptic reference for hand placement. It also suggests implementing a tablet version, since tablet-based sonification shows high recognition rates of stylised shapes even with minimal training. These address hand-tracking problems observed during testing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A transparent surface (e.g. plexiglass) that allows the webcam to recognise the hand while providing the user a visual and haptic reference about hand placement in the two-dimensional space
#### Constraints
- The article proposes these as possible improvements; they were not tested in the study
- Some students tended to move their hand towards the webcam instead of in front of it, causing hand-recognition problems

### Target Learners
- middle-school students exploring images through sound
- blind and visually impaired students

### Target Learning Goals
- recognising shapes of images through sound
- improving hand-tracking reliability during image exploration
