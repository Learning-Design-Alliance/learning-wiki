# Offer trauma-specific training to psychotherapists to reduce fears/doubts about trauma therapy and decrease undertreatment of PTSD

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, from a practitioner's perspective, "offering rauma-specific training could on the one hand reduce fears/doubts among LPTs about trauma therapy and thus decrease undertreatment of PTSD" and on the other help disseminate evidence-based PTSD interventions. It rests on the correlational findings that trained LPTs reported higher readiness and fewer fears/doubts. The authors frame this as a correlative relationship, not a demonstrated causal effect of training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must be trauma-specific in the sense the article defines: training in any trauma-specific approach therapists self-classified as trauma therapy.
#### Constraints
- The authors state that as causality is lacking, the result did not allow the conclusion that provision of training would increase readiness in all LPTs.

### Target Learners
- Licensed psychotherapists in Germany

### Target Learning Goals
- Increasing treatment readiness for patients with PTSD symptoms
- Reducing self-perceived fears and doubts about trauma therapy
