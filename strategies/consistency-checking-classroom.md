# Consistency-checking classroom: have students make their thinking explicit and compare reasoning elements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Drawing on the just-in-time proposal, the author suggests that students may not be aware they are using incompatible reasoning, and that 'The role of an instructor might be to create a classroom environment in which the consistency of reasoning would be emphasised.' Such a classroom focuses on students making their thinking explicit and comparing the different elements of their reasoning, requiring skills not taught in traditional or modified tutorial classrooms. The author notes this effect could be experimentally measured.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must help students develop skills of making thinking explicit and comparing reasoning elements, which may not be taught in traditional classrooms.
#### Constraints
- The proposal is speculative: the author states the suggestions are made without sufficient evidence to come to a conclusion, and the predicted benefit is a testable hypothesis, not a measured result.
