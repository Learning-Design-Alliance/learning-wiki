Strategies

Design guidelines for future comics-based reading interventions: four-way analysis, class-specific materials, single trained teacher, longer programme¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the null and subgroup findings, the authors recommend that future studies analyse comics effects with four-way methods accounting for age and gender, and "provide the comics used in the literary-didactic method programme specifically for each class separately, not pairwise". They further recommend a larger and more varied sample, implementation by the same qualified teacher without concerns about comics, a longer intervention with additional sets for self-applying the method, and examination of long-term effects and teachers' beliefs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Agreement with teachers at least several months prior to the start of the school year
  • A teacher qualified to use comics as a literary-didactic method and without concerns about doing so

Constraints¶

  • The original programme's short, intensive seven-week format and pairwise text difficulty across grade pairs limited the interpretation of its effects

Target Learners¶

  • primary pupils in grades 2 to 5 from various backgrounds

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading literacy
  • reading motivation
  • reduction of gender differences in reading

Claims¶

  • Using comics as a literary-didactic method did not significantly improve primary pupils' reading comprehension or ability to perceive text incoherence compared to control instruction [+W]
  • The comics programme did not reduce gender differences in reading literacy or reading motivation [+W]
  • Comics use showed significant interaction effects with class, and with gender and class, on attitude towards reading, with better results for older girls [+W]
  • Comics use showed significant interaction effects with class and gender on interest in reading, with gains among fourth-grade boys and fifth-grade pupils of both genders [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Recommendations for future PBL and concept cartoon research and materials
  • Permit teachers to adapt and integrate programme materials in their own way in future trials, and consider remote delivery of training and support when scaling
