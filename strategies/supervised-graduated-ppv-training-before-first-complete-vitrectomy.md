# Supervised, low-intensity graduated surgical training before a trainee's first complete vitrectomy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes a training regimen in which novice vitreoretinal surgeons with no prior intraocular surgery experience assisted and practised individual steps of pars plana vitrectomy under close supervision for two years before performing their first complete vitrectomy. Trainees were "trained by the same surgeon (MV)" and spent "one day a week in the operating theatre assisting the experienced surgeon in approximately five PPV". The authors report that both trainees subsequently matched experienced-surgeon outcomes from the start of independent practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A training surgeon who supervises practice of surgical steps before the trainee's first complete procedure
#### Constraints
- The article notes this was relatively low-intensity training with only one day a week in the operating theatre, and states that with higher-intensity training the two-year period can be significantly reduced

### Target Learners
- beginner vitreoretinal surgeons with no previous intraocular surgery experience

### Target Learning Goals
- performing 25-gauge pars plana vitrectomy with gas tamponade for uncomplicated rhegmatogenous retinal detachment
