# Source lab data from an experiment students perform themselves rather than third-party datasets

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy grounds a computational lab in data the students generate physically, rather than in externally supplied datasets. The article contrasts this with traditional numerical labs: "instead of sourcing the data from some third-party observations, the source of the data here is an experiment performed by the students themselves, hence making it a suﬃciently ‘hands-on’ lab catering to both computation and experiment." The marble-surface experiment supplies raw trajectory video that students then transform computationally.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the physical apparatus (printed surface, marbles, camera) so students can record their own trajectory videos before computational analysis.
#### Constraints
- Manual launch variability means instructors should plan for many trials to obtain viable datasets.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- computational data analysis
- orbital mechanics
- connecting experiment with computation
