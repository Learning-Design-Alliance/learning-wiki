# School-based screening and adapted school-context interventions for early detection and treatment of social anxiety disorder

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends prioritising early detection and treatment of SAD, noting only about half of affected individuals ever seek treatment, often 10–15 years after symptom onset. It points to educating "school staff and peers in the recognition of SAD symptoms", two-step school screening, and school-adapted interventions such as the SASS programme, shown superior to non-specific counselling in 138 high school students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained school staff and peers able to recognise SAD symptoms and facilitate referrals to relevant mental health services
- Access to evidence-based treatments, which the article notes are efficacious but seldom available for young people and adults with SAD
#### Constraints
- The study is based on treatment-seeking individuals seen in specialist care, and the results may not generalise to milder forms of the disorder
- Whether improvement in SAD symptoms or remission from the disorder has a positive effect on educational outcomes is unknown, although it seems plausible

### Target Learners
- adolescents and young people with social anxiety disorder
- high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- early detection of social anxiety disorder in school settings
- reducing social anxiety and increasing school functioning
