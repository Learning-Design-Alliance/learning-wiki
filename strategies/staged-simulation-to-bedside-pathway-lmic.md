# Adopt a staged, LMIC-sensitive pathway linking simulation-based preparation to authentic supervised bedside practice, with competency-based progression and EPAs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose a pragmatic reform model in which simulation is an "on-ramp" to bedside care rather than an endpoint, operationalised through "competency-based progression with clear milestones or entrustable professional activities (EPAs)." Simulation should be required for core procedures, scaled to local context with low/mid-fidelity equipment and shared higher-fidelity hubs, and integrated with assessment and debriefing. Hospital–university agreements should schedule small-group bedside encounters, set reasonable learner–patient ratios and safeguard teaching time, while blended learning maintains instructional continuity when patient exposure fluctuates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional collaboration between hospitals and universities to safeguard real-patient learning and teaching time

#### Constraints
