Allow learners to place their own characters into visual story tools to increase purpose and utility¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends extending the software concept by allowing learners to place their own characters into the tool, arguing that "being able to pose and warp one's own creation would increase the meaning which one attatches to the activity". Combining this with producing products for a meaningful audience may increase natural use of pose and warp features.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Software must support learner-created posable characters

Constraints¶

  • The article notes all such speculation needs to be tested

Target Learners¶

  • Primary school children making visual stories

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Visual storytelling
  • Narrative writing

Claims¶

  • Half of participants significantly posed or warped characters in 71% or more of their pictures while the other half did so 30% of the time or less [+W]
  • Children are capable of more complex graphical interaction than most current software permits [+W]
  • Software cartoons depicted significantly more character poses and far more physical character-to-character interaction [+W]
  • In the Ramayana sub-study, software-group stories scored higher for strong descriptions of characters or actions at both story stages [+W]
  • Children using a visual story software process produced more on-topic story content than a control group using brainstorming and independent writing [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide explicit process prompting (including picture-to-text transcription guidance) when visual storytelling precedes writing
  • Have students draw their own cartoons to express concerns and consolidate understanding of an issue
