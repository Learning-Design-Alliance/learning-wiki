# School psychologists, special educators, and clinicians are encouraged to use the daily report card to address both on-task and disruptive behaviors with students who have ADHD

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the conclusion the authors recommend: "School psychologists, special educators, and clinicians are encouraged to use the daily report card to address both on-task and disruptive behaviors with students who have ADHD". This recommendation follows directly from the meta-analytic evidence that the DRC significantly improved desirable behavior and produced moderate-to-highly effective non-overlap effect sizes in single-case studies. Practitioners should note the moderator findings when implementing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Operationalized target behaviors with specific goal criteria
- Teacher feedback and contingent rewards as part of DRC implementation
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is single-case design studies with students with ADHD; the authors note future research is needed on home-school communication elements

### Target Learners
- Students with ADHD in elementary through secondary school

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving on-task and disruptive classroom behavior
