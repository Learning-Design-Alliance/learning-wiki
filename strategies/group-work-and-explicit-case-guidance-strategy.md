# Promote group work throughout a condensed course and provide explicit guidance when assigning clinical case problems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends to "Promote group work wherever possible", noting group work was promoted in weekend quizzes, dissection, and clinical case problems, and that engagement increases when students discuss what they have learned with the group. It also advises that "explicit guidance is provided when assigning clinical case problems": after specific guidelines and criteria were created for case presentations, almost all students felt the clinical relevance of the course was explicit and faculty perceived stronger presentations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured group formats such as quiz groups, dissection teams of eight with assigned pairs, and groups of four for case work
- Specific guidelines and objectives for case presentations, including differential diagnoses, relevant anatomy, final diagnosis, treatment guidelines, prognosis, and evidence from current literature
#### Constraints
- In the first iteration, adequate guidance for solving cases was lacking and the clinical relevance was not explicit to students

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- collaborative skills necessary for clinical care of patients
- clinical application and presentation of anatomical knowledge
