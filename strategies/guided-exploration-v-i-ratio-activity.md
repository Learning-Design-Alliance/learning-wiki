# Guided exploration activities in which students measure V/I ratios across varying voltages to confront the constancy of resistance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends providing students "with guided exploration activities within a coherent curriculum that challenge these incorrect notions". Students connect a resistor to batteries with different voltages, measure current, calculate the ratio of V/I for different cases, and interpret why the ratio does not change when voltage changes. Analogous explorations with a capacitor vary the charge and check whether Q/V stays constant, and students comment on statements provided to them, only one of which is correct, discussing reasoning with peers and instructor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities must be embedded within a coherent curriculum and build on students' current knowledge
#### Constraints
- The article notes it is not sufficient to tell students that R=V/I does not imply resistance depends on voltage or current, as students can quickly revert to incorrect interpretations

### Target Learners
- calculus-based introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- reasoning appropriately about equations
- understanding intrinsic properties of circuit elements
