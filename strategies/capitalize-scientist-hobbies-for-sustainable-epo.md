# Sustain scientist participation in outreach by designing projects that capitalize on scientists' hobbies and creativity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementable strategy for volunteer-based outreach programs: choose project formats that match what participating scientists already enjoy doing. The article reports that "The most sustainable projects are those which capitalize on the collective creativity of scientists" and that "the most successful programs in this regard are those which capitalized on the hobbies of the participating scientists." DED gave eager scientists creative writing opportunities; DarkBites drew on a graduate student excited to write text and a postdoctoral artist who was delighted to illustrate.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identify scientists with existing enjoyment or talent (e.g., writing, art) before assigning project roles
- Provide training opportunities so members may discover unrecognized talent for science writing
#### Constraints
- It is difficult to incentivize scientists to participate in EPO programs unless they are already inclined to do so; dedicated-team writing competed with research, teaching, and administrative duties

### Target Learners
- volunteer scientists in large research collaborations
- general public and social media audiences

### Target Learning Goals
- science communication skill development for scientists
- public engagement with science
