# Teach Bayes' theorem with natural frequency trees and dot diagrams rather than the equation, introduced late in the semester

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course teaches Bayes' theorem "using dot diagrams and natural frequency trees (Sedlmeier and Gigerenzer, 2001; Figure 1) rather than via the equation", in medical-screening contexts such as mammography and HIV testing. Bayesian probability is introduced "much later in the semester, out of fear that content on subjective probability would accidentally reinforce the outcome orientation". Conditional probability is first explored with real breast cancer data before formal instruction distinguishing prob(A|B) from prob(B|A).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prior grounding in type I versus type II errors and forward probability before Bayesian content
#### Constraints
- Bayesian content deliberately delayed until late in the semester to avoid reinforcing the outcome orientation misconception

### Target Learners
- undergraduates with only college algebra as prerequisite

### Target Learning Goals
- applying Bayes' theorem to clinical screening
- distinguishing conditional probabilities in either direction
