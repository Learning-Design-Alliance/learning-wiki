# Use Current Events And Fictional Characters To Teach Social Awareness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy teaches social awareness — perspective-taking, empathy, and recognition of social norms and inequities — by anchoring discussion and analysis in two kinds of concrete material: current events drawn from news and community life, and fictional characters from literature, film, or media. Learners examine what characters or real people experienced, felt, and chose, then connect those cases to their own lives. It is typically carried out through structured discussion, role-play, and reflective writing rather than direct instruction about social skills.

## Design Implications

Narrative material is effective for social-emotional learning because characters provide psychologically safe, low-stakes targets for perspective-taking: learners can discuss a fictional character's dilemma or a distant news event without the interpersonal risk of disclosing personal experience [Reading literary fiction improves empathy and perspective-taking.](../claims/fiction-reading-improves-empathy.md) [+W]. Current events add authenticity and perceived relevance, which supports engagement and transfer to real social contexts [Authentic purposes increase motivation and learning.](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) [+M]. The strategy enacts [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md) by giving learners repeated, structured practice adopting others' viewpoints.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Age-appropriate, well-vetted news stories or narratives with genuine social or ethical content
- Structured discussion protocols or prompts that direct attention to characters' emotions, motives, and constraints — not just plot
- A facilitator skilled in managing discussion of sensitive or contested topics
- A bridge activity connecting the case to learners' own experience ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), reflective writing, or [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md))

#### Constraints
- Effects of fiction on empathy are small and depend on transport and quality of reflection; passive consumption without guided discussion produces little measurable gain [Reading literary fiction improves empathy and perspective-taking.](../claims/fiction-reading-improves-empathy.md) [~W]
- Poorly framed current events can heighten anxiety, stereotype threat, or polarization, particularly when events involve learners' own communities [-M]
- Fictional exemplars that are one-dimensional or stereotyped can reinforce rather than reduce biased social schemas [-M]
- Discussion dominated by a few voices limits perspective-taking benefits for the rest of the group [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Fiction-first:** use novels, picture books, or film characters (e.g., the [Reading With Relevance](https://readingwithrelevance.org) curriculum) as the anchor, then connect to real issues
- **Events-first:** start with a curated news story, then use role-play or writing from the perspectives of people involved
- **Structured programs:** embed both in a sequenced SEL curriculum such as [Second Step](https://www.secondstep.org) or [Facing History and Ourselves](https://www.facinghistory.org), which pairs historical/current events with narrative testimony
- **Media analysis:** have learners critique how news coverage portrays different groups, adding a media-literacy dimension

### Target Learners
- K–12 learners, with picture books and read-alouds effective from early elementary [Dialogic reading improves expressive language.](../claims/dialogic-reading-improves-expressive-language.md) [+S]
- Learners who resist direct social-skills instruction but engage through story and character
- Mixed-perspective groups, where differing interpretations of the same event or character become the discussion material

### Target Learning Goals
- Perspective-taking: inferring others' emotions, motives, and constraints
- Ethical reasoning: evaluating choices and consequences in social dilemmas
- Social awareness: recognizing group norms, inequities, and community issues
- Empathy and prosocial attitude formation [Reading literary fiction improves empathy and perspective-taking.](../claims/fiction-reading-improves-empathy.md) [+W]

### Instructions
1. Select a current event or fictional narrative with a clear social dilemma and multiple stakeholder perspectives.
2. Establish discussion norms and activate relevant background knowledge ([Activation](../elements/activation.md)) so learners enter the case with needed context.
3. Present the material, pausing at decision points to ask "What is this person feeling? What are their options?" ([Anchored Instruction](../elements/anchored-instruction.md)).
4. Facilitate structured [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or role-play ([Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md)) in which learners argue from assigned perspectives ([Assigned Positions](../elements/assigned-positions.md)).
5. Bridge to learners' lives: reflective writing or a [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) connecting the case to their own social experiences.
6. Close with an application or action step — a class project, letter, or community connection — to support transfer.

## Related Strategies

## Examples
- **[Facing History and Ourselves](https://www.facinghistory.org)** — pairs historical documents, survivor testimony, and current events with structured reflection to teach civic and social awareness in secondary classrooms.
- **[Second Step](https://www.secondstep.org)** — elementary SEL curriculum using scripted scenarios and character vignettes for perspective-taking practice.
- **[The Choices Program](https://www.choices.edu) (Brown University)** — curriculum units in which students role-play stakeholders in current policy debates.
- **Read-aloud plus character interview** — after a picture book, students "interview" a character about their feelings and choices, then write a journal entry from that character's perspective.
