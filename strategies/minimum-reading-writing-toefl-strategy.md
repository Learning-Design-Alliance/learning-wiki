# Require minimum reading and writing TOEFL scores for entrance to global courses in Japan

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that overall TOEFL scores did not correlate with FCI performance while reading ability did, the authors recommend that Japanese institutions admitting international students to English-medium courses "certainly warrant[] the implementation of a minimum reading and writing score on TOEFL, and similar tests, for entrance to global courses in Japan." They note most Japanese institutions currently ask only for an overall score, and argue lower bounds on reading and writing ability are important for success on university courses in general.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions must have access to TOEFL or similar test subscores for reading and writing, not only the total score
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single small high-end TOEFL sample (range 80-120) at one Japanese university; the authors state the English-ability question is far from settled

### Target Learners
- international students entering English-medium university science courses in Japan

### Target Learning Goals
- academic English reading proficiency sufficient for conceptual science assessment
