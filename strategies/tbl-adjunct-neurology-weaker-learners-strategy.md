# Use TBL as an adjunct teaching method for undergraduate neurology, particularly for academically weaker learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 11 claims (10 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 5 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend TBL as a useful adjunct for undergraduate neurology education: "our results suggest TBL may be a useful adjunct teaching method for undergraduate neurology education, particularly for academically weaker learners". The strategy deploys the modified IRAT-GRAT format on clinical scenarios in a single venue with team consensus answering, targeting neurophobic students who lack neurology knowledge. It is grounded in the study's adjusted score gains and the larger effect observed in weak students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Preparatory reading material distributed one week in advance
- Teams randomly assigned with simultaneous display of consensus answers
- Facilitators available to clarify misconceptions
#### Constraints
- The larger effect in weak students is based on a subgroup analysis that the authors state needs corroboration by further studies
- Evidence comes from a single 49-student study using a modified (not full) TBL programme

### Target Learners
- third-year medical undergraduates
- academically weaker students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical neurology knowledge
- addressing neurophobia
