# Formative Assessment With Feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Formative assessment is the process of eliciting evidence of student understanding *during* instruction — before grades are final and while teaching can still respond — and interpreting that evidence against explicit learning goals. The feedback component closes the loop: it tells learners where they are relative to the goal, what the gap is, and what to do next. The strategy is carried out through low-stakes checks (quizzes, drafts, exit tickets, discussion probes), timely feedback on that evidence, and instructional adjustment by both teacher and learner.

## Design Implications

Formative assessment works because it converts assessment from a measurement event into an instructional act: the evidence gathered reshapes what happens next for both teacher and learner [Formative assessment practices improve achievement when evidence is actually used to adjust instruction.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Feedback is the mechanism by which the gap between current and desired performance becomes visible and actionable, but its effect depends entirely on what the learner does with it — feedback that is not acted on is decoration, not instruction.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit learning intentions and success criteria shared with learners, so feedback has a reference point ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md), [Constructive Alignment](../patterns/constructive-alignment.md))
- Assessment tasks that actually elicit evidence of the target learning, not just completion ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
- Time and structure for learners to *use* the feedback — revision, retry, or re-derivation ([Application](../elements/application.md))
- A low-stakes or no-stakes grading context, so the assessment reveals rather than conceals misunderstanding

#### Constraints
- Feedback that directs attention to the self rather than the task ("you're so smart") can depress performance; over a third of feedback interventions in Kluger & DeNisi's meta-analysis actually lowered achievement, typically those invoking ego involvement [Feedback directing attention to the self rather than the task can reduce performance.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [-S]
- Grades or scores delivered *with* comments cause learners to ignore the comments; the grade satisfies the question "how did I do?" and stops the search [Feedback accompanied by a grade is acted on less than feedback alone.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [-M]
- Feedback is ineffective when learners have no opportunity to act on it — comments on final, unresubmittable work are effort spent on nothing [-M]
- Overly frequent or voluminous feedback can overwhelm novices and degrade learning under high [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) demands [~M]
- When feedback reveals performance is far from the goal and learners see no path to closing the gap, it can reduce motivation and effort [~S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Timing:** immediate feedback suits procedural skills and prevents error consolidation; delayed feedback can benefit complex conceptual tasks by allowing initial self-correction [~M]
- **Source:** teacher feedback, automated feedback ([Adaptive Mastery Learning](../elements/adaptive-mastery-learning.md)), and structured peer feedback each work when criteria are clear; peer feedback additionally benefits the giver through evaluation practice
- **Format:** hints and cueing questions outperform full correct answers for deeper learning, because they leave cognitive work with the learner [~M]
- **Scale:** whole-class feedback (addressing common errors from a batch of work) trades individualization for sustainability

### Target Learners
- Struggling and novice learners benefit most from specific, task-level feedback about *how* to improve [Formative feedback with specific guidance improves learning, especially for lower-achieving students.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]
- High-achieving learners benefit more from feedback about processes and self-regulation than from redundant task-level confirmation [~M]
- Learners with fragile self-efficacy need feedback framed against criteria and progress, not peer comparison

### Target Learning Goals
- Skill and procedure refinement: identifying and correcting errors before they consolidate
- Conceptual understanding: surfacing misconceptions while they are still revisable
- Self-regulation: building learners' capacity to judge their own work against criteria ([Self-Regulated
