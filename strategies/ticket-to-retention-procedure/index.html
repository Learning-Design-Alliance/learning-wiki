Implement the ticket to retention: distribute one to five target questions, have students write answers, discuss with two classmates, listen, revise, and submit¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The TtR is a classroom assessment technique in which "a sheet with one to five target questions is distributed to students." Students write answers using their class notes, discuss responses with a classmate, listen to that classmate share answers, repeat the sharing-and-revising cycle with another classmate, and submit written responses to the professor. It combines the ticket to leave, one minute paper, half-sheet response, post-write, and think-pair-share, shifting the emphasis from assessment to student retention.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Questions specifically focus on the key concepts the professor wants students to understand rather than vague questions
  • Students are encouraged to use their class notes to answer and revise answers
  • Enough class time is invested so students do not feel rushed or stressed

Constraints¶

  • The authors found that on occasion the TtR was rushed at the end of class and not given the attention it deserved
  • Professors should not announce assignment instructions during the TtR because students miss important information

Target Learners¶

  • college undergraduates

Target Learning Goals¶

  • long-term retention of key course concepts

Claims¶

  • Students recalled a high percentage (87-88% on average) of concepts reinforced with the TtR strategy on unannounced midterm and final examinations [+W]
  • All three students who failed the midterm passed the final, improving their scores by 22 to 27 points [+W]
  • Professors identified implementation challenges (rushing, unmonitored use) but found no evidence that the TtR reinforced incorrect information [+W]
  • Retention rates were similar regardless of whether courses met four times a week over four weeks or once a week over 15 weeks [+W]
  • Students reported recalling more information with the TtR and enjoyed discussing answers with classmates, but asked for the activity to start earlier in class [+W]
