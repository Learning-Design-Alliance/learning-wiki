# Model-then-present cycle: teacher models the presentation strategy, students orally present passages with peer discussion, then complete exercises

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's presentation strategy is a recurring classroom routine in which the teacher first models how to present key points of a passage orally, then students present the passage to peers in the next session with discussion before, during and after, followed by exercises done individually, in pairs or small groups. The article reports that "The researcher first modeled the presentation strategy to the experimental group and assured that each student recognized how to employ it appropriately." The cycle was worked for two weeks at a time, three 45-minute sessions per week, over ten weeks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must first model the strategy until all students know how to employ it appropriately
- Reading passages are taught in a first lesson before students present them in the next session
#### Constraints
- Studied only with Iranian intermediate EFL learners aged 18-27 at one university language center over ten weeks

### Target Learners
- intermediate EFL learners in a university English language center

### Target Learning Goals
- reading comprehension
- oral presentation of text content
- self-regulated and self-confident learning
