# Use school-level block randomization with a principal-components index to balance a diverse district sample

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study assigned schools an index score using principal components analysis with Title 1 status, percent of students who are black, percent eligible for free or reduced-price lunch, and the school-wide 2010 state assessment math score as inputs. Schools were ranked by index score and randomized into treatment or control by quartile. This block-randomization approach was chosen because of the diversity of the district, producing a well-balanced sample on prior test scores, racial composition, and lunch eligibility.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School-level covariates sufficient to construct a balancing index, and enough schools per block to randomize within quartiles.
#### Constraints
- Randomization at the school level is appropriate when the intervention is designed to alter the school data-use culture by spreading knowledge, strategies, and perspectives on data use throughout the school.

### Target Learners
- Elementary students in grades 4 and 5 in a large urban district

### Target Learning Goals
- Estimating causal effects of a school-level professional development intervention on teacher data use and student mathematics achievement
