Use the K-W-L strategy (What I know, What I want to learn, What I learned) to activate prior knowledge before reading¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

K-W-L asks students three questions: two before reading (What I know; What I want to learn) and one after (What I learned). The article describes it as useful "in activating student's prior knowledge and helping them determine their purpose for studying", with the teacher leading a discussion of the topic before reading and students writing answers after. It is recommended for language and Mathematics teachers and for second-language learners.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher leads a discussion of the topic to be read before students read
  • Students write their answers to the pre-reading questions after the reading session

Constraints¶

  • Described as useful for second language learners or students experiencing difficulty in constructing meaning

Target Learners¶

  • junior secondary school students
  • second language learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • vocabulary building
  • construction of meaning
  • comprehension of expository and narrative text
  • Mathematics concepts
