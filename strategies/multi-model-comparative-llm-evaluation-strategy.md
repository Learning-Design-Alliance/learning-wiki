# Use comparative evaluations involving multiple LLMs rather than single-model assessments in dental education settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that comparative evaluations involving multiple models may provide a more comprehensive understanding of the strengths, limitations, and potential risks associated with the use of LLMs in dental education and assessment settings. This follows from the observation that conclusions based on a single model may not necessarily reflect the behavior of other contemporary LLMs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to multiple publicly available LLM interfaces and a shared standardized prompt and question set
#### Constraints
- Rapid model updates may affect reproducibility over time

### Target Learners
- dental educators
- assessment designers

### Target Learning Goals
- selecting and evaluating LLMs for educational and assessment use
