Three-step tutor training model: initial training, daily debriefing, and individual consultation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article describes a tutor preparation approach implemented in "three closely integrated steps": an initial training phase during the first four weeks of the semester (approximately 1.5 hours twice a week), a 30-minute debriefing phase immediately following each tutoring session, and an individual consultation phase throughout the semester. Training focused on evidence-based literacy practices, using assessment data to inform instruction, and organizing instruction using a common framework. The authors attribute the intervention's impact partly to this experiential, context-focused tutor preparation model.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tutors are pre-service teacher candidates enrolled in a semester-long reading assessment and instruction course with a supervised field experience

Constraints¶

  • The model was implemented with pre-service teachers pursuing elementary education degrees at a local university

Target Learners¶

  • Pre-service teacher candidates serving as literacy tutors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Preparing tutors to identify reading difficulties and design effective instruction for at-risk early grade readers

Related Strategies¶

  • Foster a school-university collaborative relationship to support identification, assessment access, and communication
  • Intensive trainer-led observation and feedback model for tutoring professional development
  • Support curriculum implementation with initial multi-day training plus ongoing onsite consultation during the school year
  • Provide structured multi-phase professional development with ongoing support for intervention teachers
  • Coach-led initial implementation followed by teacher phase transition
