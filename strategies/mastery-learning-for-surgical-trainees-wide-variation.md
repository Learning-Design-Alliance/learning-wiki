# Use personalized mastery-learning programs given wide individual variation in performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q4` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors state that their "findings of wide variation in individual final-products scores emphasize the need for personalized approaches such as mastery-learning programs," in which trainees "advance based on demonstrated proficiency rather than on training duration or number of training attempts." This is a forward-looking recommendation grounded in the observed performance spread, not a tested intervention in this trial.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Proficiency standards must be defined so trainees can advance on demonstrated proficiency rather than time or number of attempts
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is speculative in this article; no mastery-learning intervention was tested in the trial

### Target Learners
- novice surgical trainees with heterogeneous skill levels

### Target Learning Goals
- achieving demonstrated proficiency in mastoidectomy
