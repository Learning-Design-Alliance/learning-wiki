# Differentiate task difficulty and materials within folk game activities according to children's differing fine motor levels

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because surveys showed girls were more proficient than boys with scissors and some children feared cutting curved graphs, the team adjusted materials and difficulty within the same activity. The article reports that "children with weaker ability cut straight lines and those more capable cut curves", with paper materials provided so weaker children overcame fear of difficulty while capable children fully practiced their skills, letting each child get further development and support toward a higher level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must know children's current levels and existing differences (e.g., through surveys and observation) and prepare varied material supply and contents for different levels
#### Constraints
- The article ties this differentiation to the observed gender difference in scissor use and to children's fear of difficulty with curved cutting

### Target Learners
- kindergarten middle-class children of mixed fine motor ability

### Target Learning Goals
- cutting, scissor control and other fine motor skills advanced from each child's current level
