Add regular plyometric training to sport practice to support balance in hearing-impaired athletes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors conclude that "the application of plyometric training in addition to the volleyball training is beneficial in terms of sporting performance" for hearing-impaired players, and extend the recommendation beyond volleyball: "the plyometric training can be applied to the athletes of other branches as well as to the volleyball players." The recommendation rests on the significant between-group balance differences found over eight weeks.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A strength training background is described as allowing plyometric training efficiency to develop more effectively; exercises should progress from light intensity toward challenging jump levels

Constraints¶

  • Three of six static balance parameters showed no significant between-group difference; one prior study cited (Sağıroğlu, 2008) found no significant balance effect at p>0.05

Target Learners¶

  • hearing-impaired athletes
  • volleyball players

Target Learning Goals¶

  • static balance
  • sporting performance

Claims¶

  • In the control group, only medial lateral R changed significantly from pretest to post-test; the other five static balance parameters showed no significant change [+W]
  • Within the experimental group, pretest-post-test gains were significant for overall stability R, overall stability L and anterior posterior L, but not for anterior posterior R or medial lateral R [+W]
  • No significant between-group differences emerged for anterior posterior R, anterior posterior L or medial lateral L static balance parameters [~W]
  • Eight weeks of plyometric training added to volleyball training produced significant pretest-posttest differences favoring the experimental group in overall stability R, medial lateral R and overall stability L among hearing-impaired volleyball players [+W]
