# Embed self-explanation with worked examples in high-school computer textbooks and train teachers in their use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author's forward-looking recommendation: "it is recommended to include this strategy in the textb ooks of the computer and information technology curriculum, esp ecially in topics oriented towards the teaching of programing." A second recommendation is to train in-service computer teachers and student teachers on the strategy, and a third is to incorporate a large number of worked examples in textbooks and in teaching applications such as database design or spreadsheets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher training on the strategy and on motivating students to write self-explanations
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single quasi-experiment with male freshmen in one Riyadh school

### Target Learners
- high school students in computer literacy courses

### Target Learning Goals
- acquiring programming knowledge and skills

## Claims

- [Prior studies reported that self-explanation supported by worked examples was more effective than the strategy alone and improved programming code writing and debugging](../claims/prior-self-explanation-worked-examples-evidence.md) [+W]
- [Freshmen high school students taught programming with self-explanation supported by worked examples achieved significantly better programming knowledge than students taught by the traditional method](../claims/self-explanation-worked-examples-improve-programming-knowledge.md) [+W]
- [Students taught programming with self-explanation supported by worked examples significantly outperformed traditionally taught students on the technical skills posttest of writing program code](../claims/self-explanation-worked-examples-improve-programming-skills.md) [+W]
