# Shared Reading

## Description
Shared reading is an interactive reading experience where an adult reads aloud while children follow along. The adult models fluent reading, uses engaging intonation, and encourages prediction and picture clue usage. Taking turns reading aloud is beneficial for older readers. It helps children connect print to story, builds tracking skills, and fosters an excitement for reading. Key components include making predictions, using picture walks, modeling fluency, asking comprehension questions, and connecting reading and writing through journaling and illustration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to age-appropriate books, a trained adult reader, and time for interactive reading sessions.
#### Constraints
- May be less effective if the adult reader is not engaging or lacks strong reading fluency. Some children may require additional, individualized support to fully benefit from the activity.

- Beginning and seasoned readers in preschool through early elementary school, including children who benefit from modeling and explicit instruction.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Improve reading comprehension, build print awareness, model fluent reading, enhance prediction skills, and foster a love of reading.

### Affordances
- Offers a structured, engaging way to develop literacy skills. It is especially beneficial for building confidence, expanding vocabulary, and improving comprehension. This approach allows for immediate feedback and correction, making it easier for children to learn and retain new information.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted by selecting books that match the child's interests and reading level. Scaffolding can be provided through targeted questioning and support with picture walks and predictions.

## Assessment Evidence
- Assess through observation of child's engagement and participation. Monitor comprehension through questioning and analysis of predictions. Evaluate improvement in fluency through oral reading assessments.

## Impact
- Research suggests that shared reading enhances literacy skills and comprehension. Studies show the importance of parent involvement and specific literacy-building strategies for pre-literacy development. (See Roberts, Jurgens, & Burchinal, M., 2005, and Phillips et al., 2008).
