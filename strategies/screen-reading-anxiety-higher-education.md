# Screen poor readers entering higher education for reading-specific anxieties rather than general well-being alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that universities screen students with reading difficulties specifically for social and non-social reading anxiety when they enter higher education, because general well-being screening would miss this distinct subtype. The authors argue that "screening for general well‐being in this population would be insufficient, exemplifying the need to screen for these specific anxieties when poor readers enter higher education", enabling targeted support for mental well-being. This follows their finding that reading anxiety is distinct from trait anxiety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated adult reading anxiety measure (the RAT-A) and a screening point at entry to higher education
#### Constraints
- The authors note reading anxiety did not affect academic achievement in their sample, so the rationale is well-being rather than grades

### Target Learners
- university students with reading difficulties

### Target Learning Goals
- mental well-being
- identification of reading-specific anxiety
