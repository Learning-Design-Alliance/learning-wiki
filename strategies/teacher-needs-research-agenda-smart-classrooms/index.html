Use teachers' a priori needs as a research agenda guiding smart classroom design and policy¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's forward-looking contribution is a design and policy recommendation set: "Based on these ﬁndings, we provide a research agenda with policy recommendations to improve the design and implementation of smart classrooms". It advises smart classroom designers, planners, education managers, and stakeholders to ground development in teachers' subjective and a priori needs — automating bureaucratic tasks, freeing time for study and planning, improving resources and conditions, and supporting attention to individual students' needs. The guidance is offered as complementary to emerging investigations of teaching needs under the new classroom paradigm.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Involvement of teachers in technology development so solutions align with the expectations and challenges teachers face.

Constraints¶

  • The article warns that developing technology without considering teachers' needs risks irrelevant solutions and resistance to adoption.

Target Learners¶

  • teachers at all educational levels, especially Brazilian educators in diverse levels and modalities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • more effective teaching and learning practices supported by teacher-centered smart classrooms

Affordances¶

  • Smart Classroom Four Aspect Taxonomy

Claims¶

  • Teachers aspire to enhanced cognitive abilities such as superintelligence and super memory to support teaching [+W]
  • Teachers report a need for automated grading and assessment to free time and prevent exhaustion [+W]
  • Teachers report a need for faster lesson planning and material preparation to gain time for higher-value activities [+W]
  • Teachers report a burden of daily bureaucratic tasks and a desire to automate them [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Investigate the cognitive needs of students in CoSIL and integrate content, CoSIL, and guidance tools to advance personalization
  • Prioritize teacher-centered AI implementations that enhance rather than replace human expertise
