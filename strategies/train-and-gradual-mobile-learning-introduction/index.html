Train students and teachers on academic mobile-phone use and introduce it gradually in small doses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that before mobile phones are used for teaching and learning, "students need much training an d orientation on how to use the mobile for teaching and learning purposes", and that institutions should change student attitudes toward academic phone use. In the discussion it adds that "The introduction of this new strategy should be gradual and in small doses so that students can get familiar with it". It also recommends that instructors be trained to apply mobile technology methodically with clearly designed lesson plans, and that absence be treated more seriously.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training and orientation for both students and teachers on academic mobile-phone use
  • Instructor training in methodical, purposeful application with clearly designed lesson plans

Constraints¶

  • The authors state the strategy needs to be practiced on a daily basis over a reasonably enough period of time so that it affects achievement

Target Learners¶

  • Foundation-year university EFL students in Saudi Arabia

Target Learning Goals¶

  • EFL achievement and positive attitudes toward mobile-assisted language learning

Claims¶

  • Foundation-year EFL students reported positive attitudes toward using mobile phones for learning, with 60% responding positively on willingness items [+W]
  • Students most strongly endorsed the perceived advantages of mobile learning, with 70.3% responding positively to the advantages items [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Adopt mobile learning and train faculty to produce mobile-fitted teaching materials
