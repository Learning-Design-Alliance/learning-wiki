# Use automated assessment across MCQs, short free-text answers, and essay marking to increase feedback during learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper identifies three main approaches to automated marking — multiple choice questions, short answer marking in free text, and essay marking — as fundamental to the Education Accelerator. Short answer marking allows answers of up to twenty words, supports hints or remedial material on incorrect responses, and can reward students with credit at second or subsequent attempts. Used creatively, MCQs can serve large numbers of students even in discursive subjects, though producing acceptable distractors requires major effort and MCQs can be boring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Major effort to produce acceptable distractors for MCQs; databases of reference answers for short-answer comparison
#### Constraints
- The paper states that for the foreseeable future some areas will be better adapted to automated marking than others, and that traditional methods are non-scalable and very expensive

### Target Learners
- large numbers of students in automated learning systems

### Target Learning Goals
- increased feedback during the learning process; diagnostic remediation directed to exactly what the student needs
