# Incorporate student-requested debugging curriculum elements: hypothesis generation training, guided debugging tasks, and a systematic debugging process

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Open-ended feedback on the exam yielded three requested curriculum elements. First, students "want to learn a guided way of developing hypotheses to investigate during debugging". Second, students requested "guided debugging tasks throughout the semester to begin developing an intuition for debugging", with one student desiring a whole course on debugging basics. Third, students were most interested in learning a systematic debugging process, reflecting on the unguided nature of their current approaches. These suggestions are planned to be combined with curriculum and assessment improvements in future iterations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor time to design guided debugging tasks and hypothesis-generation prompts within existing lab coursework
#### Constraints
- Derived from a single-semester pilot with twenty respondents in one microelectronics course

### Target Learners
- undergraduate electrical and computer engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- debugging hypothesis generation
- debugging intuition
- systematic debugging process
