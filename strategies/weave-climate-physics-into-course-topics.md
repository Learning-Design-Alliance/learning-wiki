# Introduce climate physics by weaving it into existing physics course topics in staged intersections

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Rather than a separate course, climate change is introduced through intersections with standard physics content. The article states: "The basic physics of climate change intersects with fundamental physics course topics in five areas – fluids, the electromagnetic spectrum, the idea of thermodynamic equilibrium and energy balance, the concept of resonance in oscillations, and the notion that electromagnetic waves are generated by oscillating electric dipoles." Climate physics is introduced in stages — with oscillations/resonance, after the EM spectrum, then explicitly — to avoid piecemeal learning, with depth depending on course level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A planning step mapping intersections between climate change and course subject matter, plus a schedule integrating the four pedagogy dimensions
#### Constraints
- The approach leaves out Earth-science details such as atmospheric structure, winds, ocean currents, and detailed absorption spectra, best explored in higher-level specialized courses

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate general physics students from non-science majors to calculus-based courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding the greenhouse effect, carbon cycle, and climate fundamentals within disciplinary course content
