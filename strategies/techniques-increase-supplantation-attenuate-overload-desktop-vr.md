# Search for techniques such as sufficient learning time, navigational guidance, interface design, and progressive or controlled disclosure of details to increase supplantation and attenuate overload in desktop VR

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that studies should search for techniques that might increase the supplantational characteristics of VR and for techniques that might attenuate the effects of channel and cognitive overloads. Specifically, the authors suggest "sufficient learning time, navigational guidance, and interface design" to increase supplantational characteristics, and "progressive or controlled disclosure of details" to attenuate overload effects. This strategy follows from the finding that channel noise and cognitive field overload can override supplantation-concreteness benefits of VR.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Desktop VR presentations of complex scenes should incorporate techniques such as sufficient learning time, navigational guidance, and interface design.
#### Constraints
- The study was highly exploratory and limited by its very small and non-random sample.

### Target Learners
- Technical training students with varying visual perceptual types

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning complex scenic environments such as operating rooms
