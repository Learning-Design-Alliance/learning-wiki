# Universities should develop programs or courses targeting healthy practices, sleep hygiene and self-efficacy to support academic achievement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that universities create programs or courses that target healthy practices, mitigate sleep problems, and build self-efficacy so that nursing students acquire "strategies to improve academic achievement". It rests on the study's findings that sleep quality and diet adherence relate to grades and self-efficacy. No such program was designed or evaluated in the study itself.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Nursing students must acquire knowledge about sleep hygiene and healthy habits and understand the impact of their own habits on clinical practice, as the article states
#### Constraints
- Findings come from students at a single university and may not generalize to the entire population of nursing students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- improving academic achievement through healthy lifestyle habits and self-efficacy
