# Supplement practice-based training with synchronous and blended e-learning while retaining in-person clinical experiences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that "practice based training can be supplemented by synchronous and blended e-learning which are interactive, flexible learning opportunities that promote the reinforcement of theoretical knowledge and promote mastery experiences, but in-person clinical experiences are also necessary to realize complete development of hand on skills and contextual judgment." Blended learning models incorporating e-learning with simulation and supervised clinical experiences are supported by the nursing education literature for promoting psychomotor skills acquisition and clinical reasoning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- E-learning must supplement rather than substitute hands-on clinical education, especially for skills and patient-facing competencies
#### Constraints
- Core nursing competencies such as psychomotor skills, clinical reasoning, and patient communication may not be entirely developed by online modalities only

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate and graduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- Psychomotor skills acquisition
- Clinical reasoning
- Theoretical knowledge reinforcement
