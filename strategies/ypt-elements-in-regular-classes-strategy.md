# Implement YPT-inspired investigation, presentation and debate elements in regular classes to develop curriculum-based hard and soft skills at scale

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends bringing at least some important YPT elements — scientific investigation, presentation and debate — into regular classes, since not every student can participate fully in the high-end competition. The YPT Toolkit collects classroom practices using simpler physics experiments ending with a report and a peer opposition gauging strengths and weaknesses of the investigation. Implementations sacrifice physical complexity, shifting development from high-level hard skills toward low-level hard skills and soft skills, which the article argues is what a large class might most benefit from.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simpler physics problems to create an environment of investigation
- A report and peer opposition stage to gauge strengths and weaknesses of the investigation
#### Constraints
- Implementations sacrifice physical complexity and therefore the development of high-level hard skills for more development of soft skills

### Target Learners
- high-school students in regular physics classes
- first-year university students

### Target Learning Goals
- scientific investigation process
- presentation skills
- debating skills
- curriculum-based hard skills
