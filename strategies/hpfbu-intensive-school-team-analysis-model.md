# One-week intensive school structure: lectures, short exercises, then team reproduction of published analyses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The school used a one-intensive-week format: three days of lectures and exercises covering particle physics fundamentals, ROOT, CutLang, statistics, reconstruction, uncertainties, and presentation skills; then students were "grouped into teams of 3 or 4" and each team was assigned an ATLAS or CMS analysis publication to reproduce with CutLang after 1.5 days of independent study, culminating in presentations where every member presented some aspect. Dormitory housing was allocated so peer-to-peer discussion could continue into late hours, and lectures were recorded and broadcast.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a computing room with prepared machines or a pre-installed virtual computer image
- Monte Carlo event samples produced by instructors or taken from the CERN LHC Open Data portal
- Instructors with prior ADL experience
#### Constraints
- Students expressed a need for improvement regarding the limited duration and the logistics of the computing classroom

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics or engineering majors
- a few embedded graduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- performing rudimentary analyses of Monte Carlo pseudo-data and real LHC data
- defending results in front of peers
