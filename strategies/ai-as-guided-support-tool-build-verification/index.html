Treat AI as a guided support tool and explicitly build verification and skepticism into learning goals¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that educators position AI as an augmentation rather than a replacement for mentorship. It states: "For educators, this means treating AI as a guided support tool rather than a replacement for mentorship, and explicitly building verification and skepticism into the learning goals." Simple routines such as regular check-ins and shared documentation are recommended to keep AI-assisted work transparent and allow errors to be caught early. The strategy rests on the observed failure modes and the students' developmental arc toward independent error detection.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Mentor or expert guidance remains available for higher-level strategic choices and specialized intuition.
  • Regular check-ins and shared documentation to keep AI-assisted work transparent.

Constraints¶

  • Derived from a single-cohort case study; the authors call for broader validation of the educational benefits of AI support.

Target Learners¶

  • novice students in project-based research settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical thinking and discernment about AI outputs
  • research and programming skills

Affordances¶

  • Human Ai Co Mentorship Complementary Roles

Related Strategies¶

  • Embed SRL-oriented AIGC pedagogy: prompt-and-reflect routines, verification rubrics, and feedback literacy
  • Embed the tool in the subject's workflow and design feed-forward prompts, symbolic-answer handling, and progress tracking before full release
  • Complement AI tutoring with group recitations or instructor guidance rather than treating it as a replacement for human instruction
