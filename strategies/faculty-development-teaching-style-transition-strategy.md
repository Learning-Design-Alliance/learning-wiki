# Use faculty development programs to provide structured training in teaching skills and support the transition from teacher to facilitator

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends structured faculty development programs in response to its finding that physician educators often attain teaching skills without formal training and that adjunct faculty attached significant value to lecturing. Such programs, the authors state, "help the medical educators in their transition from teacher to a facilitator, curriculum planner, evaluator, researchers and scholar, multitasking professionals." The strategy targets medical educators needing to shift toward student-centered, facilitative teaching in PBL curricula.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured training programs that develop teaching skills and pedagogical competencies of medical educators
#### Constraints
- The article notes faculty from TU and STMU were less inclined towards empowering students to set the pace of their learning process

### Target Learners
- medical faculty, including adjunct and junior faculty without formal teaching training

### Target Learning Goals
- transitioning from didactic information provision to facilitative, student-centered teaching roles
