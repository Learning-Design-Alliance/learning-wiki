# Exam structure rewarding reasoning over answers, with makeup exams and regrades to focus students on their own thinking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Every hour exam contains five questions — a multiple-choice multiple-representation question, two long-answer problems, an estimation problem, and an essay question — with explanations and reasoning worth far more than final answers. Makeup exams (averaged with the original grade) and written regrade requests push students to ask not only "What is the right answer?" but "Why didn't I get the right answer?" Weekly quizzes from the second week apply tutorial processes to new examples, demonstrating that memorizing answers is not effective.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Exams must include items calling on the epistemological skills the course develops
- Grading must emphasize reasoning and method over numerical answers
#### Constraints
- Typically 25% of the class takes the makeup exam, with 80-90% of those improving their grade significantly; about 30% of students write regrade requests

### Target Learners
- junior and senior life-science majors in introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- self-assessment of reasoning
- formative use of exam feedback
