# Integrate simulation-based health education into prevention and health promotion strategies for disadvantaged populations such as refugees and migrants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A policy-level recommendation that 'policymakers and health professionals prioritize the integration of simulation-based health education into prevention and health promotion strategies targeting disadvantaged populations, such as refugees and migrants'. The article argues such models can be structured for easy implementation by nurses, the primary implementers of health education, within refugee health services, NGO community-based programs, and migrant health centers to improve early diagnosis and advance health equity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Partnerships between health systems and community- and faith-based organizations already serving refugee communities; a structure that nurses as primary implementers of health education can easily deliver.
#### Constraints
- No long-term follow-up was conducted, so the article states the sustainability of awareness and skill improvements remains untested.

### Target Learners
- Refugee and migrant women
- Disadvantaged populations with low health literacy

### Target Learning Goals
- Breast cancer awareness and early-diagnosis behaviors
- Practical self-examination skills
