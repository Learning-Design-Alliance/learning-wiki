# Multimodal assessment of ultrasound competence mapped to Miller's pyramid levels

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that competence assessment be multimodal, combining formats so each facet of competence is assessed and each level of Miller's pyramid is met while compensating for individual formats' limitations. Concretely, it suggests multiple-choice or extended-matching questions for the basic 'knows' level, objective structured clinical examinations for 'knows how' and 'shows how' with repeat assessment as levels progress, high-fidelity simulators (including a recently developed validated TUS simulator test), and portfolios or supervised logbooks for 'does'. Assessment should also be standardised to ensure consistency across time and location.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formal structured multimodal assessment tools; standardisation across time and geographical location; adequate prior assessment of basic competence levels before logbook-based supervision
#### Constraints
- Real patients with genuine findings may be the most valid medium but are unlikely to be practical at the required scale; minimum procedure numbers remain undefined and may not be a valuable metric

### Target Learners
- respiratory and acute medicine trainees
- ultrasound trainees across specialty levels

### Target Learning Goals
- valid and reliable assessment of graded thoracic ultrasound competence
