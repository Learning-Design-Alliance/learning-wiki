# Goal Setting Conferences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A goal setting conference is a brief, structured one-on-one conversation in which a teacher and learner jointly identify a specific, attainable learning goal, agree on how progress will be evidenced, and schedule a follow-up check. The learner does most of the talking — articulating where they are, where they want to be, and what strategy they will try — while the teacher probes, refines, and records the commitment.

## Design Implications

Conferences convert abstract goals into proximal, self-set commitments, which raises persistence and self-efficacy [Proximal subgoals increase motivation and self-efficacy more than distant goals.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.41.3.586) [+S]. Specific, challenging goals outperform vague "do your best" exhortations, but only when learners are committed and have the strategies to pursue them [Specific difficult goals improve performance.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705) [+S]. For novices, conferences should emphasize *process* goals (what to do, which strategy to try) rather than *outcome* goals (grades, rankings) [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]. The conference also functions as formative assessment: the teacher gains diagnostic information about the learner's self-regulation that whole-class instruction cannot surface [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A predictable conference routine (e.g., 3–5 minutes per learner on a rotating schedule) so conferences are frequent enough to sustain momentum
- Visible evidence of current work (portfolio, draft, data dashboard) to ground the conversation in specifics
- A simple recording system — the goal, the strategy, and the follow-up date — that both parties can revisit
- Teacher skill in [coaching](../elements/coaching.md) moves: open probes, restating, and withholding premature advice

#### Constraints
- Conferences consume substantial instructional time; at scale they must be brief and rotational or they crowd out [practice](../elements/practice.md)
- Teacher-imposed goals delivered as directives undermine the ownership that makes conferences work; autonomy support is essential [Autonomy support fosters intrinsic motivation and engagement.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]
- Goals set without a monitoring mechanism are quickly abandoned; without a scheduled follow-up, self-monitoring decays [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Learners with very low prior knowledge may set goals misaligned with what would actually help; the teacher must supply enough structure to prevent unproductive goal choice [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Writing conferences** (e.g., Lucy Calkins' workshop model): conferring over a draft with a teaching point and a goal for the next revision
- **Data conferences**: reviewing progress data (reading levels, mastery dashboards) before setting the next goal
- **Peer conferences**: structured peer-to-peer goal talk using a protocol, with the teacher circulating
- **Micro-conferences**: 60–90 second check-ins during work time, trading depth for frequency

### Target Learners
- Developing self-regulated learners who can articulate preferences but need help forming strategy-linked goals [Self-regulated learning can be taught and improves achievement.](https://doi.org/10.1080/00405840209591340) [+S]
- Struggling learners who benefit from proximal, achievable commitments that rebuild efficacy [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Less useful for learners who already self-regulate effectively and may find routine conferences redundant [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-regulation: planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's own learning
- Metacognition: articulating what one knows and what to do next
- Motivation and persistence: building commitment through ownership of proximal goals

### Instructions
1. **Prepare with evidence.** Review the learner's current work or progress data before the conference so the conversation starts from specifics, not impressions.
2. **Open with the learner's voice.** Ask the learner to describe where they are and what is hard; use [check-in](../elements/check-in.md) routines to normalize the conversation.
3. **Co-construct one goal.** Narrow to a single, specific, proximal goal — prefer process framing for novices [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M] — and break it into [achievable micro-goals](achievable_micro-goals.md) where needed.
4. **Name the strategy.** Agree on one concrete strategy or [practice](../elements/practice.md) move the learner will use; model it briefly if it is new.
5. **Record and schedule follow-up.** Write the goal and strategy down (learner's notebook, conference log) and set the next check-in date; follow up reliably, since monitoring sustains the goal [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].
6. **Close with affirmation of effort and agency.** End by restating what the learner committed to and why it is within reach, supporting autonomy and efficacy.

## Examples
- **Reading/writing workshop conferring** (Teachers College Reading & Writing Project, [https://www.readingandwritingproject.org](https://www.readingandwritingproject.org)) — teachers hold brief 1:1 conferences during independent work, naming one teaching point and one goal per conference.
- **Data-driven goal conferences in competency-based schools** (e.g., Summit Public Schools' mentoring model, [https://summitps.org](https://summitps.org)) — weekly mentor–student meetings review progress dashboards and set the next week's goals.
- **Physical education goal talks** — students set personal fitness targets with the teacher before a unit and revisit them at mid-unit check-ins.
