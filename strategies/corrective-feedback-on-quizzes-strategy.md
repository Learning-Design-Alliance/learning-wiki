# Provide corrective feedback on low-stakes quizzes when using test-enhanced learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's first lesson learned is that "When administering quizzes for test-enhanced learning, providing feedback substantially improves the learning experience." In the Human Structure course, feedback took the form of a list of missed items on weekend quiz submission, with correct answers withheld to stimulate group study. This change coincided with a dramatic rise in quiz-helpfulness ratings and improved examination item performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mechanism for showing students which quiz items they missed after submission
- Withholding correct answers so students must correct errors through group study
#### Constraints
- Feedback was provided as a list of missed questions only; correct responses were not supplied

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- content retention through test-enhanced learning
- preparation for summative examinations
