# Increase coverage of dietary nitrate in undergraduate-level nutrition education, using nitrate as an exemplar for teaching nutrition research methodology

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend expanding education about inorganic nitrate and its health effects, with emphasis on recent developments in the scientific consensus, particularly at undergraduate level but also among graduates, to empower nutrition professionals to make more informed recommendations. They argue that "Greater coverage of nitrate in undergraduate-level nutrition courses could be of particular value" and that, given the breadth of nitrate research spanning cellular mechanisms, clinical trials, epidemiology and public health, "nitrate could serve as an excellent exemplar for teaching about nutrition research methodology". The strategy targets the education-level knowledge gaps the survey documented.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curriculum development on nutrition-related courses including Continued Professional Development, informed by the reported education-level differences in nitrate knowledge.
#### Constraints
- The authors note evidence about nitrate is rapidly evolving in a nascent research area, so education must emphasise recent developments in the scientific consensus.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate and postgraduate nutrition students
- Practising nutrition professionals such as dietitians and nutritionists

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding physiological effects, dietary sources, intake values and metabolism of dietary inorganic nitrate to inform dietary recommendations
