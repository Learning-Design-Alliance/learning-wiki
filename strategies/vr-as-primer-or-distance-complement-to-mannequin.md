# Use VR simulation as a primer or distance-learning complement to mannequin simulation in a blended approach

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that VR simulation "could complement legacy mannequin simulation methods by serving as a primer or a distance‐learning tool", given that most respondents preferred a mixture of modalities. They note retail VR HMD platforms are cheaper than dedicated high-fidelity simulators, more conducive to repetitive tasks and remote learning, and may maximize limited mannequin and tutor exposure. They also propose ViVaDeX as a remote self-learning component before mannequin-based simulation, and suggest potential for training skilled birth attendants in LMICs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to retail VR HMD hardware and the ViVaDeX application; future iterations should improve haptic feedback, facilitate peer observational learning, and improve overall usability
#### Constraints
- High initial hardware investment and lack of haptic feedback are stated disadvantages of retail VR HMD platforms

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- Skilled birth attendant trainees in low- or middle-income countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Normal vaginal delivery knowledge and procedural preparation
