Use embodied role-play and picture-based story writing with humour devices to teach difficult grammar distinctions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes two concrete activities used with the experimental group. First, students were renamed as inherent or non-inherent adjectives, pinned name labels on their shirts, and physically acted out how inherent adjectives follow nouns while non-inherent ones do not, with the teacher as "the director of a play". Second, students wrote short imaginative paragraphs and stories about coloured portraits and nature photos, deliberately using "metaphor, pun and ambiguous words and sentences to evoke laughter" in a student-centred classroom.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher control and orchestration; the article warns the roles "should be performed carefully and orchestrally by the teachers. Otherwise they will be aimless and purposeless and may create chaos in the classroom"

Constraints¶

  • Inappropriate humour such as "teasing, racist and sexist humour" must be avoided

Target Learners¶

  • Second-year undergraduate EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Distinguishing inherent from non-inherent adjectives
  • Producing written sentences using the target adjectives

Affordances¶

  • Linguistic Theory Of Humour In Grammar Teaching

Claims¶

  • Teaching English grammar with humorous activities produced more correct quiz answers than traditional instruction in a small classroom experiment [+W]
  • The authors report that humorous instruction changed students' mood, encouraged oral participation and reduced hesitation in speaking English [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Teach equivocal words (false friends) by embedding each target term in a short humorous story that makes the false-friend reading nonsensical
  • Use a staged picture-story-book procedure: whole-picture stimulus, picture-text matching in groups, ordering, then individual retelling
