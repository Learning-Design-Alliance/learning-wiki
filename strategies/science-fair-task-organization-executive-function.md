# Organizing, defining and prioritizing science-fair project tasks as a way to stimulate ADHD students' executive function

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy embeds deliberate planning demands inside a hands-on science project: because ADHD is characterized as an executive function disorder, the teachers made "organizing, deﬁning and prioritizing science fair tasks" a central feature of the six-week preparation. Students chose the experiment, researched online, took notes, listed materials, and made time-management decisions when experiments failed. The article presents this as a way to compensate for difficulty in planning what lies ahead in time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured project setting with deadlines and multiple tasks, such as a science fair preparation period, in which planning and prioritizing can be practiced
#### Constraints
- The article states it is not possible to correct directly an executive function deficit, so the approach teaches management of externalized forms of information and time control rather than remediating the deficit

### Target Learners
- Elementary school students with ADHD or learning disorders

### Target Learning Goals
- Executive function: managing time and planning future tasks
