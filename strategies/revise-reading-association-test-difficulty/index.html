Revise admission tests by grading reading difficulty more finely and making the association test of uniform difficulty¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on experience with the 1916 testing, the article recommends two changes: "The reading test should be presented with more degrees of difficulty so as to distribute the individuals more widely," and the association test "should be made of more uniform difficulty throughout," substituting for the first three parts, which were so easy that writing answers consumed more time than thinking the correct response. These changes were made for the September 1917 testing.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Scoring personnel familiar with the work to score the tests rapidly.

Constraints¶

  • The value of these changes is yet to be determined.

Target Learners¶

  • applicants to a university medical school

Target Learning Goals¶

  • accurate prognostic ranking of applicants' mental capacity

Claims¶

  • Combined weighted-time and reading-quality scores correlate substantially with first-semester medical school marks [+W]
  • Reading-test time scores correlate equivocally with academic marks, unlike raw association time [+W]
  • Test rankings show high retention of median, tertile and quartile positions in medical school marks [+W]
  • The mental tests were a better indication of medical school ability than the applicants' prior academic records [+W]
