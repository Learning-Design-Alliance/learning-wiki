Collect and analyze students' pre-inquiries before class and use the selected meaningful pre-inquiries to structure science lessons¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This strategy has the teacher gather students' own questions about an upcoming unit, select with experts those closely related to the curriculum, and run question-resolution lessons built around them. The article recommends that "teachers should not only analyze teaching materials such as curriculum and textbooks before class, but also understand and analyze student's thoughts by using student's pre -inquiries", reconstructing class to suit students' level and needs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A questionnaire or other means of collecting students' pre-inquiries before the unit
  • Consultation with science curriculum specialists or fellow teachers to select meaningful pre-inquiries

Constraints¶

  • The supporting study was conducted with elementary school students in South Korea on the 'Acid and Base' unit

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school 6th grade students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • science process skills
  • science-related affective domain

Claims¶

  • Pre-inquiry lessons raised students' awareness of science, science education and science-related jobs, but not awareness of the science-technology-society interrelationship [+W]
  • Pre-inquiry lessons raised interest in science and its subelements, with no significant difference in science and anxiety [+W]
  • The pre-inquiry class produced higher overall science-related affective domain scores than the control class [+W]
  • Science lessons built on students' meaningful pre-inquiries produce higher total science process skills than traditional textbook lessons in 6th-grade classes [+W]
  • The authors report no significant difference between groups in science and anxiety after pre-inquiry lessons [~W]
  • The pre-inquiry class improved specific science process subskills — measuring, inferencing, controlling variables and generalization — but not observing, classifying or predicting [+W]
  • Sixth graders' pre-inquiries about 'Acid and Base' were mostly information-type questions, and 24 of 52 were selected as meaningful pre-inquiries [+W]
