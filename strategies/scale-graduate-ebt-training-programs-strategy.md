# Scale up graduate teaching programs that include training in EBT methods to accelerate spread of evidence-based teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that although some institutions already have graduate teaching programs, "increasing these programs at scale, and including training in EBT methods could create a new avenue for accelerating the spread of evidence-based teaching and improved teaching across higher education." Because many students must seek teaching development beyond their departments and 84% of surveyed students in cited work saw value in EBT, dedicated institutional programs would fulfill student desire and potentially increase EBT implementation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must go beyond mandatory procedural/policy trainings toward pedagogy and EBT methods, and be of substantial duration
#### Constraints
- Current professional development opportunities that result in persistent benefits are not widespread, even at the institutional level

### Target Learners
- STEM graduate students, particularly life science PhD students bound for faculty careers

### Target Learning Goals
- Adoption of student-centered, evidence-based teaching practices in future faculty teaching
