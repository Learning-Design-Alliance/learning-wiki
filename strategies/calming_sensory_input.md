# Calming Sensory Input

## Description
Providing access to calming sensory input through various means, such as tactile bins filled with sand, rice, or beans for tactile stimulation; weighted blankets or stuffed animals for deep pressure; and chewy pendants or snacks for oral sensory input. Other calming auditory input includes playing white noise or calming music.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A collection of sensory tools and activities (e.g., tactile bins, weighted blankets, chewy pendants, noise-reducing headphones). A designated space for sensory activities. Clear guidelines for appropriate use of sensory tools.
#### Constraints
- Some sensory tools may be expensive or require special maintenance. Some learners may be sensitive or allergic to certain sensory materials. Requires careful monitoring to ensure safety and prevent misuse of sensory tools.

### Target Learners

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Reduce anxiety, promote self-regulation, provide sensory stimulation

### Affordances
- Provides a variety of sensory experiences that can help learners regulate their emotions and attention. Allows learners to choose the type of sensory input that works best for them. Can be integrated into various learning activities and settings.

### Personalization
- Offer a variety of sensory tools and activities to meet the diverse needs and preferences of learners. Allow learners to choose the type and intensity of sensory input that works best for them. Provide options for individual or group sensory activities.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learners' engagement and participation in sensory activities. Collect feedback from learners on the effectiveness of different sensory tools and activities. Track changes in learners' behavior, attention, and emotional regulation.

## Impact
- Research indicates that sensory integration activities can improve attention, reduce anxiety, and promote self-regulation. Studies have shown that weighted blankets can reduce anxiety and improve sleep quality. Chewing can provide calming proprioceptive input and improve focus.
