Use multimedia graphic novel courseware with audio narration and TLH-based inquiry questions to foster critical thinking in history learning.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This strategy recommends designing multimedia history courseware that combines graphic novel visuals with audio narration and embeds thought-provoking questions based on the TLH model. The study found that the GN mode with narration significantly outperformed text-based and redundant text-plus-narration modes on posttest scores, and that the TLH framework's inquiry questions promoted critical thinking rather than rote learning. Designers should develop graphic novel-based multimedia materials incorporating these elements to support critical thinking in history and similar content-area learning.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Graphic novel visuals combined with audio narration
  • TLH-based inquiry questions embedded in the learning material
  • Constructivist multimedia learning environment

Constraints¶

  • Tested with Secondary Two students learning the topic 'Japanese Occupation of Malaya 1942-1945' in Malaysian secondary schools

Target Learners¶

  • Secondary school students learning history

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Critical thinking skills in history learning
  • Historical thinking and inquiry skills

Affordances¶

  • Tlh Model Historical Thinking Framework

Claims¶

  • The GN mode provides equivalent benefits to both surface-approach and deep-approach students, with no significant difference in their posttest scores. [+W]
  • Students using the Graphic novel and Narration (GN) mode performed significantly better on critical thinking posttest scores than those in the Graphic novel and Text (GT) mode and the Graphic novel, Text and Narration (GTN) mode. [+W]
  • Students exposed to the GT mode obtained significantly higher mean posttest scores than students exposed to the GTN mode. [+W]
  • The interaction effect between students' learning approaches and the three treatment modes on posttest scores was not significant. [+W]
  • Pupils using the audio-and-image (AI) mode significantly outperformed pupils using the audio-image-text (AIT) mode on the music theory post-test [+W]
