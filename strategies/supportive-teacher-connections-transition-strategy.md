# Support lower secondary teachers' ability to connect with students in emotionally supportive ways during the transition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that conflict with new teachers after the transition acted as a risk factor for achievement and well-being, the article recommends that "school policies and structures aimed at enhancing a lower secondary school teacher's ability to connect with students in emotionally supportive ways may prevent the accumulation of non-supportive classroom experiences and other potential threats to learning outcomes". It also underscores leveraging adolescents' interpersonal relationships as an intervention tool to help them cope with academic challenges.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School policies and structures that enable teachers to build emotionally supportive connections with students early in lower secondary school
#### Constraints
- The article notes close relationships with new subject teachers may not yet have formed at the time of post-transition measurement, and the evidence is correlational

### Target Learners
- Adolescents transitioning from primary to lower secondary school

### Target Learning Goals
- Protecting academic achievement and school well-being during educational transitions
