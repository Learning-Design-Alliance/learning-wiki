Strategies

Introduce each geoscience topic through a real-world applied context before presenting supporting science¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes a teaching approach in which "The real-world example was always presented first, and then the supporting science was introduced, rather than building up the geological background and subsequently introducing an application for the concept." This reversed sequencing helps engineering students connect foundational science to applications from the outset. Faculty-led case studies ranged from brief vignettes to extended multi-session studies drawn from industry experience and applied research.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Faculty with industry experience or access to real-world case study materials to develop authentic applied examples

Constraints¶

  • Developed for a mining engineering geoscience course; the article notes giving real-world context is not a new teaching technique

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate engineering students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • connecting foundational science concepts to engineering applications

Claims¶

  • Mining engineering students' confidence that they could provide concrete examples of applied geoscience rose significantly over a course combining case studies and active learning [+W]
  • Students gained significant improvement in exposure to geoscience concepts through the case study–based curriculum [+W]
  • Students became significantly more willing to convince others that geoscience is important in mining after the intervention [+W]
