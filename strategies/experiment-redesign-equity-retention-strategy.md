# Redesign classroom experiments and assessment to reduce demographic disadvantages and test higher-order retention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose five changes to their experiment design and research methodology after their null result. These include handing out experiment instructions "at least a class in advance" to minimize disadvantages for "non-native English speakers and stu- dents with learning disabilities", testing concept retention at least two weeks later instead of immediately, changing class seating by a pre-determined formula to reduce peer-group advantages in post-experiment discussion, rewriting questions to test comprehension and critical thinking per Bloom's taxonomy, and recruiting more instructors to increase sample size and test broad applicability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor flexibility to distribute instructions in advance, delay assessment by at least two weeks, and rewrite post-experiment questions beyond factual knowledge
#### Constraints
- Proposed changes arise from a single case study at one private mid-sized Midwestern university with small samples and heterogeneous sub-groups

### Target Learners
- undergraduate Principles of Economics students, including non-native English speakers and students with learning disabilities

### Target Learning Goals
- concept retention and higher-order comprehension and critical thinking about public goods and market failure
