# Produce screencasts with clear audio, a planned roadmap with objectives and summary, low production polish, and text-light slides

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article shares practice-based strategies for producing engaging screencasts. Audio clarity comes first: "The first thing students will notice about your screencast video is not the video itself but how clear the audio is". Planning an outline creates a roadmap; videos should open by specifying learning objectives and end with a recap of key points. High production values are unnecessary, since rougher screencasts "can make them feel like a one-on-one tutorial, increasing connection with the student", and text on slides should be limited for mobile-device viewers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Invest in a good quality microphone so audio is not muffled or unclear
- Provide slides or notes replicating the information, keeping on-screen text limited
#### Constraints
- Students do not expect Hollywood levels of production, so high production is not required but the advice is drawn from personal teaching experience rather than controlled evidence

### Target Learners
- higher education students engaging with asynchronous screencasts

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining engagement with recorded core content
