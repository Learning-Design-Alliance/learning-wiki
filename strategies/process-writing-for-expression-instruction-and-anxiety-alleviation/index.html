Use the process writing approach for written expression instruction and to alleviate writing anxiety¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on its findings, the article recommends process writing for written expression studies: "Since the process writing approach decreases this anxiety dramatically, this approach is recommended for alleviating writing anxiety." It further states that obstacles to successful learning of writing skills should be accurately identified and varied techniques used to eliminate them, with process writing appearing beneficial in this regard. The recommendation rests on the study's significant effects on both success and anxiety.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Evaluation of written text conducted as part of the writing process
  • Teacher acting as guide during student writing practice

Constraints¶

  • The article cautions the approach may not eliminate anxiety resulting from students' personality traits and that whether anxiety can be changed by the approach should be retested through studies of longer duration

Target Learners¶

  • university students in teacher education
  • students learning written expression

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving written expression
  • alleviating writing anxiety

Affordances¶

  • Flower Hayes Cognitive Process Writing Model

Claims¶

  • The process writing approach significantly decreased writing anxiety relative to traditional instruction [+W]
  • The process writing approach significantly improved academic writing success among first-year preschool teaching students relative to traditional instruction [+W]
