Integrate e-learning as a supplement to rather than replacement of traditional teaching (blended learning)¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's design recommendation is that educators should not seek to replace traditional methods but supplement them, an approach it describes as a 'blended-learning' style. Ten included studies concluded an integrated approach works best, and thirteen studies used terms such as "adjunct", "complement", "supplement", "hybrid" and "blended" to describe integrating e-learning with traditional learning.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators should evaluate e-learning approaches and consider incorporation of suitable strategies into current curricula to ensure equitable access and student success

Constraints¶

  • For teaching new skills, use of only e-learning materials may not be sufficient, as direct observation and guidance of an expert is valuable; a blended-learning environment may be more appropriate

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate medical students in clinical medicine

Target Learning Goals¶

  • clinical knowledge, skills and reasoning across disciplines such as surgery, radiology and dermatology

Claims¶

  • Individual reviewed studies reported statistically significant knowledge and skill gains from e-learning interventions compared with controls [+W]
  • Across the review's randomised controlled trials, e-learning showed no significant differences in knowledge mastery versus controls, but greater short-term learner satisfaction [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Blended Learning
  • Integrate e-learning as an adjunct to face-to-face teaching in emergency medicine curricula
  • Integrate virtual and augmented reality as supplementary tools rather than full replacements for traditional learning methods
  • Integrate simulation technologies with traditional teaching through blended approaches tailored to specific clinical tasks
  • Position immersive technologies and virtual patients as supplementary components within blended learning rather than stand-alone replacements for established clinical communication training
  • Integrate GAI-based teaching into medical curricula with robust curricular integration, technological support, and systematic training for educators and students
  • Teachers and intervention designers should use mobile devices thoughtfully alongside other teaching approaches, ground designs in existing evidence, and evaluate rigorously
  • Include a variety of online and face-to-face components to accommodate different learning styles
  • Consider VR as an adjunct to dissection and evaluate cost-effectiveness and adverse reactions when adopting VR for anatomy teaching
