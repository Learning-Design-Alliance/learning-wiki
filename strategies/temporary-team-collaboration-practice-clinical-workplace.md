# Have medical students practice collaborating in temporary teams to mirror clinical workplace conditions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A forward-looking practice recommendation for medical education: because clinical teams change frequently due to rotations and individual duty schedules, classroom collaboration in temporary teams mirrors workplace learning conditions. The article concludes that "students are likely to benefit from practicing collaborating in temporary teams" when transitioning from classroom- to workplace-learning, and notes education in temporary teams might prepare effective decision-making irrespective of team composition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A collaborative learning format such as TBL in which team membership can be reassigned, which the article notes is impractical to manage per problem in a face-to-face session but feasible online.
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence comes from an online setting; the article states it remains a testable hypothesis whether the findings apply to the classroom setting.

### Target Learners
- medical students transitioning from classroom- to workplace-learning

### Target Learning Goals
- effective collaboration and clinical decision-making skills irrespective of team composition
