# Teach students to intentionally choose from alternative algebraic strategies when solving problems, after they have procedural fluency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy has students recognize and generate multiple solution strategies, articulate the reasoning and mathematical validity behind their strategy choice, and evaluate and compare different strategies for the same problem. The guide sequences it deliberately: "the practices proposed in Recom- mendation 3 are most effective if they are implemented once students have some fluency with algebra procedures and strate- gies." Classroom moves include reflective questions for selecting strategies, prompts to articulate reasoning, sample student dialogues comparing two strategies, and small-group comparison activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should already have some fluency with algebra procedures and strategies before the recommendation's practices are introduced
#### Constraints
- The guide rates the evidence Minimal, and its supporting evidence indicates the practices are most effective for procedural flexibility rather than for conceptual or procedural knowledge

### Target Learners
- Students learning algebra in grades 6-12 who have some fluency with algebra procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural flexibility in choosing among solution strategies
- Articulating mathematical reasoning behind strategy choices
