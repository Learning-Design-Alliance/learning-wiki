# Pair AI-permitted take-home assignments with supervised modifications, oral defense, prediction, and transfer tasks to verify understanding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A concrete response to LLM outsourcing: allow students to use AI for syntax, setup, or debugging while instructors directly assess whether they can explain and adapt the result. Practical options named by the article include a short oral defense tied to the student's own circuit and counts, a supervised modification of a qubit mapping, oracle, or input state, prediction of a new outcome before execution, and a transfer task that changes the algorithmic structure rather than only the seed. The article lists these as recommendations derived from its observed failure mode.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must be able to interact with students around their own submitted artifacts
#### Constraints
- These measures were not experimentally compared in this study

### Target Learners
- undergraduate quantum-computing and programming students

### Target Learning Goals
- explaining and adapting one's own code
- predicting circuit outcomes before execution
- transferring algorithmic understanding across structural changes
