# Structure senior digital-inclusion training modules around mentoring, collaborative workshops, and real-need tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that institutions and curriculum developers design training modules for older learners that combine structured mentoring systems, collaborative workshops, and educational tasks addressing learners' real everyday needs, such as searching a shopping app for dinner ingredients. This praxeological approach is presented as consistent with senior policies favouring solutions close to real-life situations and a friendly learning environment, and institutions such as Universities of the Third Age, NGOs, senior clubs, and local cultural centres are named as potential adopters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainers with emotional and pedagogical competences specific to gerontagogical work
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is trainer perceptions from one country; the authors call for comparative international and longitudinal research before firm generalisation

### Target Learners
- older adults in digital inclusion programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- digital competence, confidence, and autonomy in everyday technology tasks
