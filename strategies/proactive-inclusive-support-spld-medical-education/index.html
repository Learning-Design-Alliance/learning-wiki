Implement proactive inclusive support: early identification, flexible assessment design, structured mentoring, and increased staff awareness¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's implications for the field recommend that medical schools "implement comprehensive strategies addressing academic, emotional, and systemic needs of students with SpLD", with "early identification, mentoring, flexible curricula, assistive technologies and faculty training" identified as central to promoting equitable access and wellbeing. This shifts support from reactive individual adjustments (mainly extra exam time) toward proactive, inclusive, flexible educational design. Qualitatively reported student support needs centred on "flexible assessments, personalized learning strategies, early identification, and greater staff awareness".

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires institutional investment in staff awareness and expertise within medical schools, coordinated support systems, and provision beyond the single accommodation of additional examination time

Constraints¶

  • The evidence base provides limited information on how identified supports are implemented, evaluated, or experienced over time; most included studies were UK-based and dyslexia-focused

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate medical students with SpLDs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • equitable access to assessment and learning conditions
  • student wellbeing and retention
  • inclusion in medical education

Claims¶

  • Late identification of SpLDs is associated with poorer academic outcomes, increased stress, and higher burnout risk among medical students [+W]
  • With accommodations such as additional examination time, medical students with SpLDs show no statistically significant difference in MCQ performance (d = 0.045) [+W]
  • Parity in MCQ assessments does not extend to other formats: lower performance reported in EMQ, SAQ, OSCE, and delayed progression [+W]
  • Medical students with SpLDs report persistent academic, emotional, and social challenges including reading and writing difficulties, time pressure, anxiety, and isolation [+W]
