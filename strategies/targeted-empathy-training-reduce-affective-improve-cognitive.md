# Design targeted empathy training that reduces affective empathy and improves cognitive empathy within an appropriate range

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The review recommends that medical educators develop feasible training programs or courses, "such as patient narrative and creative arts," to help medical students reduce affective empathy and improve cognitive empathy within an appropriate range. The rationale is the review's finding that high affective empathy and low cognitive empathy are linked to poor mental health, while empathy supports communication skills and clinical competence. Training should be targeted to the dimensional profile that benefits students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training programs must be feasible for medical educators to implement
- Assessment of students' empathy levels to identify who needs which dimensional emphasis
#### Constraints
- The review notes scores on empathy scales may not accurately reflect medical students' actual empathy, suggesting evaluation by observing actual empathy behaviors

### Target Learners
- Medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining good mental health
- Enhancing communication skills and clinical competence
