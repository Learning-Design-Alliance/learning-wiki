# Map and audit existing teacher education courses across disciplines against the TDC framework to identify competency-building opportunities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because interdisciplinary approaches are not commonplace and subject-based university structures are difficult to change, the article recommends using the TDC framework to map or audit the content and pedagogy of existing or new teacher education courses across discipline areas. Staff from separate departments work with the framework to identify which components could be delivered or incorporated into discipline coursework—for example, health education faculty integrating wellbeing/safety competencies, or social science curricula analysing supply chain practices for the impacts-on-society pillar. The article states the framework 'could be used to map or audit the content and pedagogy of existing or new teacher education courses across discipline areas'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The framework must be well understood and accepted by all faculty, who must be willing to apply it when planning and developing their programs
#### Constraints
- This does not necessarily mean adopting an interdisciplinary approach to planning and teaching

### Target Learners
- pre-service teachers in initial teacher education programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- building holistic teacher digital competence across discipline coursework
