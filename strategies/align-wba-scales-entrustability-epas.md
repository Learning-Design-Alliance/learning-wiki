# Improve WBA rating scales by aligning them to entrustability and defining clerkship-specific entrustable professional activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
As the second recommended option, the article proposes improving the design and implementation of rating scales so scores become more informative. It states that "Reliability of scales and assessor discrimination can be enhanced if scales are 'aligned to the construct of developing clinical sophistication and independence'", i.e. entrustability, which implies defining learning outcomes and entrustable professional activities for each clerkship. It also endorses addressing organizational norms, transparency, support, accountability and psychological safety in assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning outcomes and relevant tasks (entrustable professional activities) must be defined for each clerkship to facilitate ratings against pre-defined expectations.
#### Constraints
- The article notes evidence on the value of rater training in WBA is conflicting, and time for mini-CEX training competes with time for patient management.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- Valid, informative workplace-based assessment of clinical skills
