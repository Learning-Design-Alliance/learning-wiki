# Encouraging Students to Ask for Help

## Description
This strategy focuses on creating a classroom environment where students feel comfortable asking for help when they are struggling. It involves normalizing trouble, teaching problem-solving strategies, and giving students the language to articulate their needs. The goal is to empower students to become self-directed learners who are not afraid to seek assistance when needed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit instruction on the importance of asking for help, a supportive classroom environment, opportunities for students to practice articulating their needs, and coaching from the teacher.
#### Constraints
- Requires a significant shift in classroom culture. Some students may still struggle with the concept of asking for help. Consistent reinforcement and modeling are essential.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learners, particularly students who are hesitant to ask for help or who struggle with self-advocacy

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote a growth mindset, reduce fear of asking for help, develop problem-solving skills, improve communication skills, and foster a supportive learning environment.

### Affordances
- Helps students overcome the stigma of asking for help, encourages them to embrace challenges, promotes a positive attitude towards learning, and fosters a culture of collaboration and support.

### Personalization
- Teachers can adjust their approach based on individual student needs. They can provide different levels of support and scaffolding, and they can tailor the language and strategies used to the specific struggles that students are facing.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe students' willingness to ask for help, track their usage of problem-solving strategies, analyze their ability to articulate their needs, and monitor their participation in collaborative activities.

## Impact
- Students who are comfortable asking for help are more likely to persist through challenges and achieve their learning goals. Creating a classroom environment where asking for help is normalized promotes a growth mindset and fosters a culture of collaboration and support.
