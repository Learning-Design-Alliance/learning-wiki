# Use a systematic review plus Delphi process to derive competency frameworks from empirical evidence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes a novel methodological approach combining a mixed-methods systematic review with a two-round Delphi expert panel to build a competency framework grounded in empirical research. The authors state: "Our use of systematic review together with the Delphi process allowed us to triangulate a large amount of data from qualitative and quantitative research literature, with the opinions of experts in both general practice and EBM." The systematic review identifies themes from the literature; the Delphi process rates and refines a preliminary 'straw model' framework for importance, achievability, and demonstrability; and the two theme sets are integrated into a final framework.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A panel of experts in the relevant field and practice domain
- A preliminary 'straw model' framework derived from mapping review themes to adult learning theory
#### Constraints
- The Delphi panel size was at the lower end of those reported as acceptable due to limited expert availability
- Only one reviewer performed study selection, quality assessment, and data extraction, which may impair reliability

### Target Learners
- Framework developers in medical education
- Curriculum designers

### Target Learning Goals
- Deriving evidence-based competency frameworks
- Triangulating literature evidence with expert consensus
