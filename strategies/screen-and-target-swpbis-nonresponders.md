# Screen for and monitor children at risk of nonresponse to universal SWPBIS and target nonresponders for more intensive tiered interventions

## Description
The article recommends that, given demonstrated variation in SWPBIS responsiveness, "school personnel should screen for and carefully monitor c hildren at risk for nonresponse and target nonresponders for more i ntensive in terventions." Consistent with the tiered public health model, children who do not respond adequately to universal SWPBIS require targeted and individually tailored preventive interventions based on systematic assessment. The strategy operationalizes the article's finding that baseline risk profiles predict responsiveness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Systematic assessment to identify nonresponders to the universal (Tier 1) supports
- Additional resources in training, professional development, coaching, and program materials for selective and indicated programs
#### Constraints
- The intervention schools in the trial were only systematically trained in Tier 1 supports; more intensive selective and indicated prevention programs require more resources

### Target Learners
- Elementary school children, particularly those in at-risk and high-risk baseline risk profiles

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing disruptive behavior problems and discipline referrals
- Reducing need for intensive school-based services such as counseling and special education referral
