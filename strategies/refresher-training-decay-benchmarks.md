# Use quantified decay benchmarks to schedule refresher training before skill proficiency declines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article offers its decay estimates as a planning tool: "Findings provide researchers with estimates of how much skill decay can be expected if skills are not frequently used and therefore, when refresher training should be considered." Organizations and trainers can use the half-loss benchmarks (approximately 6.5 months for accuracy, 13 for speed, 11 for mixed performance) to time refresher or retraining interventions for skills that are used infrequently, such as cardiopulmonary resuscitation or other hands-on procedures. The public significance statement frames this as "planning timely retraining or refresher interventions, thereby ensuring skill proficiency in applied contexts."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of the retention interval length and the performance measure type (accuracy, speed, or mixed) for the skill in question, so the appropriate decay benchmark can be applied
#### Constraints
- The benchmarks apply to procedural skills with a salient non-verbal motor component; task type, task complexity, intermittent performance opportunities, and task instructions were identified as possible moderators of decay rates

### Target Learners
- adults in professions where procedural skills are used infrequently, such as healthcare providers

### Target Learning Goals
- maintenance of procedural skill proficiency between uses
