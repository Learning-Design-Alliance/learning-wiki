Use early cheating-risk predictions only for low-stakes support with human review, never for disciplinary decisions¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends treating model output as a trigger for timely academic guidance rather than as an accusation. "A lower threshold may be suitable for low -stakes outreach, such as reminders, academic support, or guidance on acceptable AI use. It would not be suitable for disciplinary decisions." Instructors should review each flag in context, scores should stay separate from grading and misconduct records, and students need a means to question how their data were interpreted.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Human review of each flag in context, transparent communication, and a means for students to question how their data were interpreted.
  • The prediction score should remain separate from grading and misconduct records.

Constraints¶

  • The article states the model is an early demonstration, not a deployment-ready system, and both the risk-labeling rule and the decision threshold require local validation before use in another setting.

Target Learners¶

  • first-year undergraduates in hybrid higher-education courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic integrity
  • timely academic guidance and support before high-stakes exams

Related Strategies¶

  • Decide whether to use demographic predictors case-by-case based on context: risk of discriminatory decision-making, who holds final choice, and stakes
  • Combine in-school and out-of-school data sources to understand and support students flagged as at risk
  • Use an early warning system based on linked school and human services data to target resources and lessen risks before they become more serious
  • Deploy ML-based early-warning prediction to shift retention support from reactive to proactive intervention
