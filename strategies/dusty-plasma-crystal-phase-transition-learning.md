# Use dusty plasma crystal formation and phase transition experiments, analyzed via the radial pair correlation function, to teach crystalline structure of solids

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Students create a 2D or 3D Coulomb crystal of micron-sized charged dust particles in a DC or RF discharge device and observe the periodic arrangement of atoms in crystalline solids, visible even with naked eyes. The crystalline nature is confirmed through Voronoi diagrams and the radial pair correlation function g(r). The article states that "By changing the discharge parameters such as gas pressure and input power, the melting of crystal or phase transition can be understood by obtaining the radial pair correlation function", whose profile measures solid, liquid, and gaseous phases of the dust grain medium.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dusty plasma device in DC or RF discharge configuration at appropriate discharge conditions
- Freely available image analysis software or tools for computing Voronoi diagrams and g(r)
#### Constraints
- The article notes X-ray spectroscopy can obtain diffraction patterns but it is difficult to realize the different crystal basis of 3D crystals, motivating the dusty plasma model

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students
- postgraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding crystalline structure, single-crystalline, polycrystalline and amorphous solids, and solid-liquid-gas phase transitions
