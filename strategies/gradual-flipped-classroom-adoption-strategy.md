# Adopt the flipped classroom gradually: start small, front-load video preparation, gather student feedback, and scale up as comfort grows

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors' six-step recommendation set for teachers or administrators wishing to explore flipping. Teachers should "Start by flipping only a small part of your class," flip the modules most conducive to flipping, prepare instructional videos before the semester starts, adapt lessons to student responses, gather satisfaction data (noting comfort may take at least half the semester), and gradually increase flipped materials as comfort grows.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Front-end preparation of instructional videos and online materials before the semester begins
- A willingness to adapt lessons depending on student responses and reactions
- Teacher learning about new technologies, which the authors say should be implemented slowly and with a great deal of training
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that flipped modules and videos should replace traditional homework without being an extra burden, taking student contact hours into account

### Target Learners
- ESL/EFL teachers and administrators in any context globally

### Target Learning Goals
- Freeing class time for application of skills and improving achievement of student learning outcomes
