# Combine clinical simulation with complementary strategies and deliver it during hospitalization to prepare caregivers for discharge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that caregiver training for discharge use clinical simulation embedded in a package of complementary educational strategies. It reports that "using clinical simulation included using other strategies to complement training: expository dialogued class, conversation circle and audiovisual resources", and that educational actions should be carried out during hospitalization as a strategy to train family and caregivers for home care. Simulation formats included skills training with low-, medium- and high-fidelity simulators, conversation circles mediated by a static vinyl newborn mannequin, and educational videos with simulated patients.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation performed during hospitalization, before discharge
- Complementary strategies such as dialogued classes, conversation circles or audiovisual resources to reinforce training
- Involvement of nurses and family members in health education actions
#### Constraints
- The review states a gap regarding clinical simulation studies aimed at training caregivers for adult patients, and that most studies focused on caregivers of child patients

### Target Learners
- Informal family caregivers of patients with chronic conditions, especially technology-dependent children

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural home-care skills and coping with possible complications at home after discharge
