# Staged supervised introduction for surgeons learning LASPME: experienced-center observation, cadaver course, then supervised performance with periodic video review

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the discussion the authors recommend how surgeons learning this procedure should progress: prior esophageal surgery experience, initial exposure through cases or video observation at an experienced center, a cadaveric anatomy course, and finally supervised performance, with periodic review of surgical videos and outcomes. The article states surgeons should be "followed by a basic anatomy course in a cadaveric laboratory, and finally performing the procedure under the supervision of an experienced specialist." The authors also name building a standardized, modular system to shorten the learning curve as a future research direction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Surgeons learning the procedure should already have experience in esophageal surgery
#### Constraints
- The recommendation derives from a single-center retrospective experience; the authors note results may not generalize to other hospitals and that supervision with measurement and control of all outcomes is needed

### Target Learners
- surgeons adopting LASPME who already have esophageal surgery experience

### Target Learning Goals
- shortening and structuring the learning curve for a new minimally invasive esophagectomy technique
