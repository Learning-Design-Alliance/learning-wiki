# Assess executive functioning during college planning and teach self-regulation skills before higher education entry for students with ASD

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that transition planning for students with ASD include EF assessment and pre-entry self-regulation training, because EF predicted academic progress in their sample. They write that "caregivers or tutors and students who are planning for college should assess EF to identify strengths and areas of concern", sharing results with disability services, and recommend students "get the chance to improve their self-regulation while still in high school or while preparing for higher education".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- EF assessment results should be shared with relevant disability service offers, counselors or consultants in higher education, as the article suggests
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence comes from a sample of higher-education students with ASD with mean IQ in the high average range, which the authors state cannot be generalized to autism samples with lower level IQs

### Target Learners
- young adults with autism spectrum disorder in or preparing for higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- academic progress (study pace / credits earned)
- self-regulation and executive functioning skills
