# Implement PBL as five-to-six-member student groups researching real-life wave problems and presenting solutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's PBL implementation recipe: teachers "purposively divide students into five to six-member groups, and each group would be presented a problem to research and after make a presentation to the whole class under the guidance of their respective teachers". Members had individual responsibilities, made individual study before discussion, shared knowledge and experiences, and evaluated each other on participation, preparation, and interpersonal skills, with teachers posing open-ended questions for critical thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers trained in PBL question generation and lesson presentation
- Intervention ran during normal class hours (four hours per week), monitored by researchers
#### Constraints
- Applied specifically to the topic of mechanical waves in 13th-grade physics; guided by Bandura's social learning theory

### Target Learners
- secondary school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- expert-like attitudes towards physics; collaborative problem solving of real-life problems
