# De-emphasize Wien's displacement law in introductory classes and tailor Planck-curve presentation to the audience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that Wien's displacement law be de-emphasized generally and not presented at all in introductory classes, with no example attempting to explain the apparent color of the Sun. For science majors it recommends introducing both Bλ and Bν along with the Planckian FBSED, explaining why they differ; for non-science majors it recommends introducing the Planckian SED conceptually and showing only the Planckian FBSED, without asking students to comprehend the axis parameters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors and textbook authors omit Wien's displacement law and peak-color examples from introductory courses, and teach that temperatures are determined by fitting the Planck function or flux ratios, not the peak location
#### Constraints
- In higher-level classes the authors say it might be suitable to introduce Wien's displacement law once students understand the significance of the dispersion rule

### Target Learners
- introductory physics and astronomy students, science majors and non-science majors

### Target Learning Goals
- avoiding misconceptions about star colors and temperature determination from blackbody radiation
