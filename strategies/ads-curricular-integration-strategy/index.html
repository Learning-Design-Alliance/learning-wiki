Integrate AI-driven simulation into dental curricula as a complement to traditional training, attending to domain-specific effectiveness and the lack of long-term validated outcomes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (3 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on its synthesis, the review recommends that ADS may support complementary roles in dental education, particularly for "structured skill acquisition, deliberate practice, and feedback-driven learning". However, it advises that "the integration of ADS into curricula should consider variability in effectiveness across domains and the current lack of long-term and clinically validated outcomes". The strategy therefore positions ADS alongside — not replacing — traditional methods, with modality choice matched to the target domain (immersive VR/ITS for psychomotor skills, generative AI for engagement and perceived competence).

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Selection of ADS modality should match the targeted competency domain, given domain-specific effects across modalities.

Constraints¶

  • Current evidence lacks long-term and clinically validated outcomes; most studies used short-term post-intervention assessments.

  • Undergraduate dental students

  • Psychomotor clinical skills
  • Clinical reasoning

Claims¶

  • Affective outcomes (satisfaction, confidence, engagement) are consistently positive across ADS modalities but rest predominantly on subjective, short-term measures [+W]
  • Cognitive outcomes of AI-driven simulation are the most variable across modalities and designs, with generative AI gains often perceived rather than objectively validated [~W]
  • Evidence on AI-driven simulation in dental education shows moderate-to-high heterogeneity and limited certainty [~W]
  • AI-driven simulation is associated with a statistically significant improvement in overall learning outcomes compared to traditional training in dental education [+W]
  • Immersive VR-haptic and ITS modalities are more often associated with objective psychomotor and cognitive gains, while generative AI platforms are more often associated with affective outcomes [+W]
  • Several included studies found haptic VR training equivalent rather than superior to conventional dental training [-W]
