Train lexical flexibility for irregular words through pronunciation-flexibility instruction building on decoding¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In its discussion, the article points to emerging evidence that lexical flexibility, as measured by the SfV task, can be trained in children. It describes training protocols emphasizing flexibility in applying different pronunciations for letters or letter combinations, checking for matches and making approximations to known words, and a two-step instructional model in which direct instruction in simple decoding comes first and set for variability flexibility training follows as a second step.

Claims¶

  • Set for Variability performance is strongly correlated with word identification, word attack, phonological awareness, and vocabulary in grades 2–5 [+M]
  • Word attack and the word attack × max P→O surprisal interaction add variance beyond phonology, indicating phonology-to-orthography knowledge contributes to SfV performance [+M]
  • Word identification adds unique variance and moderates SfV performance, with a three-way word attack × word identification × max P→O surprisal interaction [+M]
