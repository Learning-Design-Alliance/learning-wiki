# Tailor programmatic IECMHC to teachers' child-specific teaching stress

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that IECMHC programs attend specifically to teaching stress — the proximal stress a teacher experiences in working with a specific child — when identifying consultative support needs. The article states that "Teaching stress (i.e., stress related to a specific child) may need to be a greater focus for programmatic IECMHC models to better support teachers and potentially impact systems that disproportionately utilize exclusionary methods of discipline and thereby reduce expulsion." Because high child-specific stress predicted the largest improvements, screening for teaching stress at intake can guide more tailored, equity-focused consultation approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of child-specific teaching stress (e.g., via the PERM Teacher Stress subscale) at consultation intake
#### Constraints
- The moderating findings are correlational, from an evaluation without a control group, so tailoring recommendations rest on observed pre-post patterns

### Target Learners
- Early childhood teachers experiencing high child-specific stress; IECMHC consultants and program administrators

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing expulsion risk and exclusionary discipline practices
- Supporting teacher well-being and perceptions of child behavior
