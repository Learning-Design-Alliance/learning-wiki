# Under time-limited simulation training, not every student needs to fulfil the leadership role if the course is well designed and debriefing is constructive

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' recommendation from their null leader-follower findings: with time limits that allow only one leader per group, rotating every student into the leader role is not necessary, because followers gained comparable knowledge and confidence. They write that "every student may not need to fulfil the leadership role, but a well-designed course and constructive debriefing are recommended". The strategy relies on small teams where all members can contribute and on structured post-scenario debriefing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-designed course with clear goals and objectives
- Constructive debriefing at the end of each scenario providing feedback from everyone
#### Constraints
- Stated for a 3-hour time-limited course with groups of 4-5 where only one student can be leader; the authors note the leader subgroup was smaller than the follower subgroup

### Target Learners
- fifth-year medical students in clinical clerkship

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnosis of types of shock, causes of shock, and initial management of circulatory shock resuscitation
