# Treat intensive retreat practice as a candidate active ingredient when designing and studying long-term mindfulness interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on the finding that RR slowing was specific to retreat practice, the article recommends that researchers give intensive retreat practice "increased consideration by researchers as an 'active ingredient' in mindfulness training". It notes that common interventions such as Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction emphasize daily practice with only brief retreat periods, and suggests retreat time matters for investigating long-term, public-health-scale behavior change rather than short-term intervention models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Study designs that separately quantify lifetime retreat hours and daily practice hours
#### Constraints
- Evidence is cross-sectional and observational; the optimal distribution of daily versus retreat practice remains an open question the article explicitly raises

### Target Learners
- adults participating in mindfulness-based interventions

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term physiological and psychological benefits of mindfulness training

### Affordances
- Retreat Quiescence Mechanism Account
