# Define basic design specifications for AAC apps and social media tools to reduce operational demands

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article argues that intervention attention has focused on teaching skills to the individual while neglecting design of AAC systems and mainstream tools, even though design substantially affects performance and is most easily amenable to change. It calls for basic design specifications to facilitate use across apps and social media tools for people with disabilities and to support rapid individualization, so users are not excluded when designs impose demands outside their capabilities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Research on the basic visual, cognitive, linguistic and motor processing demands of AAC systems to untangle the effects of specific system components and optimize designs
#### Constraints
- Without design principles in place, individuals are forever playing catch up learning new operational requirements as technologies emerge, or are excluded when designs exceed their capabilities

### Target Learners
- individuals with complex communication needs who require AAC, across a wide range of disabilities

### Target Learning Goals
- operational competence in navigating and operating aided AAC systems, apps, and social media tools accurately and efficiently

### Affordances
- Light Communicative Competence Aac Model
