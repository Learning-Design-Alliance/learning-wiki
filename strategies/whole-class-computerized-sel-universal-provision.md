# Embed whole-class computerized SEL activities as a universal, cost-effective supplement to PSHE provision

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends delivering socio-emotional learning as a universal whole-class computerized activity embedded in regular PSHE lesson time, led by the ordinary class teacher with no specialist training. Whole-class delivery is argued to be "more efficient, in reaching more children, and more effective, in allowing productive peer discussion that both elaborates and consolidates learning". The trial showed such delivery produced measurable socio-emotional gains over 10 weeks while requiring no mathematics or science content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classroom computer with the activity software installed, teacher training provided by a researcher at setup, and PSHE lesson time three times weekly
#### Constraints
- Assessment relied on bespoke pen-and-paper booklets collected only after the intervention, limiting comparison with existing literature; no record existed of which children had social-emotional difficulties

### Target Learners
- All pupils in Year 3 and Year 5 classes in state-funded primary schools, as a universal intervention

### Target Learning Goals
- Socio-emotional competence: emotion recognition and theory of mind within the PSHE curriculum
