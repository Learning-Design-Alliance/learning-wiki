# Present signed communication to families as a complement to, not a fallback from, oral communication, with information-giving repeated and extended

## Description
The study recommends that implant programs advise with caution about the range of likely outcomes, recognising that families are influenced by hopes as much as by information, and that information-giving be regularly repeated, extended, and evaluated through ongoing discussion and counselling. It urges making information about sign language and the Deaf community accessible before and after implantation, and making clear that communication mode "need not be an either/or option". Sign exposure is framed as providing fuller access to cognitive development and communication competence across situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Information-giving processes should be regularly repeated, extended, and evaluated through ongoing discussion and counselling.
#### Constraints
- Families are likely to be influenced by their hopes and aspirations for their children as much as, if not more than, by the information they have received.

### Target Learners
- parents deciding on cochlear implantation for deaf children
- children with cochlear implants

### Target Learning Goals
- informed implant decision-making
- fuller access to cognitive development and communication competence
- connection to Deaf community and identity
