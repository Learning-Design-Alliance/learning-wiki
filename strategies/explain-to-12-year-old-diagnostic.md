# Ask students to explain the instrument to a layperson (a 12-year-old) to diagnose device understanding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A specific assessment prompt used by the authors to probe whether students understand how a data-collection device works: students are asked how they would answer a visiting 12-year-old brother's question about how the phone measured acceleration, with diagrams welcome. The prompt is designed "to encourage the use of everyday parlance with sophisticated responses," and the authors used the resulting explanations to reveal the black-box problem and to compare understanding before and after the MyTech curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A prompt administered after students have used the device for data collection
#### Constraints
- Used as a diagnostic in small samples (n = 10 preliminary, n = 41 post-MyTech); responses were corrected only for grammar

### Target Learners
- introductory physics laboratory students

### Target Learning Goals
- explaining how a measurement instrument works in everyday language

## Claims
