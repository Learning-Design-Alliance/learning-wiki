# Use retention data to design frequent, shorter refresher courses for high-stakes, low-frequency clinical skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The protocol's discussion recommends that knowledge and skills retention data from the evaluation inform the development of OBLS refresher courses to maintain consistent healthcare worker performance over time. The authors note existing research suggests "more frequent, shorter refresher sessions yield better clinical performance and are preferred over longer, less frequent training," citing prior resuscitation training studies. The strategy targets sustaining skills in high-stakes, low-frequency clinical situations such as maternal cardiac arrest.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal retention data at 6 and 12 months to inform refresher course design
#### Constraints
- The article states that research on optimal retraining intervals for cardiac resuscitation curricula is limited

### Target Learners
- rural hospital-based and prehospital healthcare workers trained in OBLS

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining readiness and retained skills for managing maternal medical emergencies over time
