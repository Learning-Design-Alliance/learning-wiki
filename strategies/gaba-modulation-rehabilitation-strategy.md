# Target GABA modulation to enhance motor recovery and motor-skill interventions in rehabilitation settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that understanding GABA's role in motor learning can guide interventions for motor impairments: "Exploring the function of GABA in learning mechanisms could offer insights for innovative interventions tar- geting GABA modulation aimed at enhancing functional recovery." They suggest the micro-offline consolidation phenomenon, facilitated by decreased cortical inhibition, "could provide a model in the acute phase of recovery after stroke", informing neuropharmacology or non-invasive brain stimulation approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions require knowledge of individual GABAergic status in M1, which the article obtained via pre-learning pp-TMS assessment.
#### Constraints
- Neurophysiological measures were limited to a single session, so findings were restricted to the pre-skill acquisition phase.
- pp-TMS measures represent an indirect way to measure GABA and Glutamate levels, with mixed MRS–TMS correspondence findings reported.

### Target Learners
- patients with motor impairments in rehabilitation settings, such as post-stroke patients in the acute phase of recovery

### Target Learning Goals
- enhancing functional motor recovery and motor-skill learning through GABA-targeted interventions
