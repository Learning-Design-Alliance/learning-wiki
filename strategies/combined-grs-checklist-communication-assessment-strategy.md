# Combine case-specific checklist items capturing communication outcomes with a global rating scale for general communication competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Participants stressed "ensuring that items on the checklists and the global rating scale systematically capture the specific communication goals of each station", and assessing the outcome of the encounter rather than the technique used. The discussion reports research suggesting that combining the global rating scale and checklists increases the reliability and content validity of communication-competencies assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The global rating scale should be familiar to raters, achieved by having all faculty members use it for both summative and formative assessments throughout the undergraduate curriculum.
#### Constraints
- Alternative rating tools were lengthy, with 10 to 36 items, and unsuitable where the time between examinees is only 2 minutes; the four-item Analytic Global OSCE Ratings was considered the best fit for the exam context.

### Target Learners
- medical school graduates taking a high-stakes licensing OSCE

### Target Learning Goals
- general and case-specific physician-patient communication competencies
