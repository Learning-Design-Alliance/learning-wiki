# Adopt multiple imputation and improve missing-data reporting practices in PER quantitative studies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's conclusion lays out concrete steps for researchers, reviewers, and editors: minimize missing data during collection, use MI or another principled method, present evidence of MCAR if using complete-case analysis, and report participation rates and missing-data patterns. As printed: "Studies should state the participation rates in their data collection, describe the methods they used to address missing data, discuss patterns in the missing data, and discuss how the missing data may inﬂuence analytical results." The authors also recommend the community support publishing or publicly sharing datasets so the impact of missing-data methods can be checked.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Before implementing MI, researchers should examine the amount of missing data, patterns in the missing and complete data, and the mechanisms behind those patterns, and check that data meets the assumptions of the MI algorithm
#### Constraints
- Principled methods for handling missing data, such as MI, are not a panacea; they are only one component of the diligence necessary to address missing data

### Target Learners
- physics education researchers
- reviewers and editors of education research journals

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate, reliable, and replicable quantitative studies of student outcomes

### Affordances
