# Treat CPD as a strategic investment in quality improvement rather than a regulatory checkbox

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that, for policy, CPD should be treated as a strategic investment in quality improvement rather than a regulatory checkbox, and that sustained, multi-component CPD initiatives supported by institutional infrastructure are more likely to translate into improved patient outcomes. This strategy calls for coordinated planning, resource commitment, and cultural support for continuous learning. It positions CPD as a dynamic process rather than a one-time event to help close the gap between learning and patient care.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordinated planning and resource commitment
- Cultural support for continuous learning
#### Constraints
- The true goal of CPD is long-term enhancement of care quality, but few data were available on whether improvements were sustained beyond one year

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals and healthcare system policy makers

### Target Learning Goals
- Maximizing the impact of CPD on quality of care and patient outcomes
