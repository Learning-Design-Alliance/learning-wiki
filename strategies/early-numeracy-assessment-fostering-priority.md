# Prioritize early assessment and fostering of numeracy in the first school years, and treat autonomy-promoting motivation interventions with caution in early mathematics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends documenting the early school years as a crucial period for early numeracy, because achievement was established early and subsequently predicted motivation. It also cautions that practices promoting student autonomy "may not be the best approach in the early school years" for raising mathematics achievement, while explicitly refraining from a hasty conclusion since the study did not test interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should be tested with experiments, ideally randomized controlled trials, before concluding about motivational effects on achievement.
#### Constraints
- Findings are specific to mathematics and to the early years of primary school; the study did not test intervention impacts, and the relation may differ by ability level or achievement quality.

### Target Learners
- elementary school children in grades 1 to 4 (ages 7-10)

### Target Learning Goals
- early numeracy and mathematics achievement
- intrinsic motivation in mathematics
