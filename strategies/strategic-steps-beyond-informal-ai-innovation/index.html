Strategic steps to move beyond informal AI innovation: recognise redesign in workload models, embed innovation in promotion criteria, set institutional AI principles, redesign assessment, and fund scaling pilots¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article enumerates five implementable institutional actions: "Recognise AI-driven curriculum redesign in workload models so innovation is not dependent on unrecognised effort by individual educators"; embed AI-enabled teaching innovation in promotion and teaching award criteria; establish clear institutional principles for AI use in learning and assessment; redesign assessment toward reasoning and justification in AI-rich environments rather than merely tolerating AI; and create institutional pilots or funded initiatives that scale successful teaching innovations beyond individual courses.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional commitment of time and structural support so educators can thoughtfully redesign curricula and assessments

Constraints¶

  • The article notes universities frequently look to peer institutions before making major changes, creating cautious imitation that may slow transformation

Target Learners¶

  • university educators and institutional leaders

Target Learning Goals¶

  • institutional capacity for AI-enabled curriculum and assessment transformation

Claims¶

  • Technology implementation in higher education depends on individual initiative rather than institutional systems, with governance structures rarely including those responsible for implementation [+W]
  • Institutional strategic orientation most strongly influences the other enablers (PageRank 0.2430) [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Institutional recommendations: shift from detection to process-oriented assessment, transparent AI-use protocols, and revised integrity policies
  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
  • Redesign assessment toward supervised, oral, process-based and authentic formats to verify students' own reasoning under GenAI
  • Address generative AI in higher education through assessment redesign and proportional reflective practices rather than surveillance or punitive controls
  • Strengthen institutional policies, ethics education, verification, disclosure, and data protection to support ethical GenAI use
  • Fund AI literacy initiatives integrating AI ethics into curricula and adapting assessments
  • Role-specific pilot actions for shifting to an open knowledge system
