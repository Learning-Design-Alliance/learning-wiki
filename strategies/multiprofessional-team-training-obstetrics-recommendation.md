# Consider multiprofessional team training in obstetrics involving all staff within their units

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their null results and the literature, the authors recommend that "future research should consider multi-professional team training in obstetrics, involving all staff within their units." They argue that when the task involves teamwork, the training approach should shift toward a multi-professional model, citing Siassakos et al's observation that units showing improvements had trained nearly 100% of their staff within their own units. A mono-professional exception is justified only for a specific technical task performed by a single provider, such as repairing an episiotomy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training programs implemented within the units themselves, reaching nearly all staff
#### Constraints
- Mono-professional training remains appropriate only for specific technical tasks performed by a single health care provider

### Target Learners
- All maternity and neonatal care staff in obstetric units in low-resource settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Teamwork and coordination in obstetric emergencies
- Improved maternal and perinatal outcomes
