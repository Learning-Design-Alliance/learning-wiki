# Design and teaching recommendations for CAMI-based music appreciation instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the experiment's outcomes the authors recommend: keep "Concise Words on a Single Page Making Key Points Easy to Present" because too much text made pages hard to learn from; enhance software-student interaction with immediate feedback and pre-class/after-class access via CD or Internet; add melody explanation and instrumental ensembles; use animations to demonstrate the principle of sound production; adopt a diversified teaching pattern; and have teachers interact with students rather than act as a voiceover.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must interact with students in the classroom to avoid reducing concentration and causing learning fatigue
- Specialized classrooms with computers, a fixed single-beam projector, large screen and stereo hi-fi equipment
#### Constraints
- The original software emphasized single-instrument introduction and teacher use, so the recommendations target those gaps

### Target Learners
- vocational senior high school students in music appreciation courses

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding Chinese musical instruments, including sound production principles
