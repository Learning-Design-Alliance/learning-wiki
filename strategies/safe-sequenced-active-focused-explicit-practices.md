# Design skill-training programs following the SAFE practices: sequenced, active, focused and explicit

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 causal), `q2`–`q4` · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article reports that Durlak, Weissberg, and Pachan (2010) "recommended four practices associated to previously effective skill training, namely, SAFE: Sequenced, Active, Focused, and Explicit". As an implementable recipe, programs should sequence skill steps, use active forms of learning, focus session time on developing specific skills, and make skill targets explicit. The article cites this as a meta-analytic recommendation for after-school programs seeking to enhance personal and social skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Program sessions must be sequenced, active, focused and explicit in their skill targets
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is attributed to Durlak, Weissberg, and Pachan (2010); this article did not test it directly

### Target Learners
- Children and adolescents in school-based and after-school programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Personal and social skills such as communication, problem solving and emotion regulation
