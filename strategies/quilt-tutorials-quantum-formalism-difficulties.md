# Develop and assess Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs) that take documented quantum formalism difficulties into account

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author reports developing and assessing "Quantum Interactive Learning Tutori- als (QuILT) suitable for use in advanced undergraduate quantum mechanics courses". The recommendation is that instructional strategies targeting these concepts should take the documented difficulties into account, building tutorial materials around the surveyed questions on Hilbert space, the time-independent Schroedinger equation, and measurement outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional strategies focusing on improving student understanding of these concepts should take into account the documented student difficulties
#### Constraints
- The QuILTs described are suitable for use in advanced undergraduate quantum mechanics courses

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduate quantum mechanics students

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguishing physical space from Hilbert space
- interpreting the time-independent Schroedinger equation
- understanding quantum measurement outcomes and probabilities

## Claims

- [Advanced undergraduate and graduate students frequently confuse vectors and directions in 3D physical space with state vectors and operators in Hilbert space](../claims/confounding-physical-space-hilbert-space-qm.md) [+W]
- [Students believe successive measurements of continuous variables produce somewhat deterministic outcomes while successive measurements of discrete variables can produce very different outcomes](../claims/continuous-discrete-measurement-determinism-belief.md) [+W]
- [Many students believe the energy eigenfunctions are always the preferred basis for expanding the wavefunction, even for measuring observables other than energy](../claims/energy-eigenfunctions-preferred-basis-belief.md) [+W]
- [Only 29% of students correctly recognized that Hψ = Eψ holds only for stationary states, while 39% asserted it is unconditionally correct](../claims/hamiltonian-eigenvalue-statement-difficulty.md) [+W]
- [Eleven percent of students believed that a Hamiltonian operator acting on a state constitutes a measurement of energy, over-generalizing to Qψ = λψ for any observable](../claims/operator-action-measurement-conflation-qm.md) [+W]
- [Students confuse the probability of measuring position with the expectation value of position; only 38% correctly described possible position outcomes after an energy measurement](../claims/position-probability-expectation-value-confusion.md) [+W]
- [Working through the time-development QuILT improved average performance from 53% to 85% on pre-/post-tests in an advanced quantum mechanics course](../claims/quilt-time-development-pre-post-gain.md) [+W]
