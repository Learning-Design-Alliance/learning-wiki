# Attention Check

## Description
An attention check is a practice where a teacher announces a short pause, and students reflect on where their attention is. Learners are encouraged to notice what their mind is doing and to understand they have a choice about where to direct their attention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a quiet space and brief periods of time (e.g., 1-5 minutes). May use guided audio, videos, or instructor-led exercises.
#### Constraints
- Some learners may find it difficult to focus or may be skeptical of the practice. Requires consistent implementation to see benefits.

## Examples
- In the middle of a lesson, or during the middle of independent or group work time, ask students to pause and “check in with their attention.” Is their attention here, in class? Was their mind wandering? If so, where had their attention gone? Emphasize that mind-wandering is completely normal and to be expected — it’s what minds do! The important thing is to notice what the mind is doing, and then to understand you now have a choice about where to direct your attention.
