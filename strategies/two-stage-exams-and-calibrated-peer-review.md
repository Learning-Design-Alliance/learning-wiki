# Use two-stage (group) exams and Calibrated Peer Review to make talking, writing, and collaboration manageable in large courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Two implementable recipes the article offers for large classes. In "two-stage exams," students take an exam individually, return it, and retake it in groups; the article reports students' "performance on the group exam has been shown to improve" and the exam becomes a time to learn. In Calibrated Peer Review, students complete a writing assignment, rate three sample assignments to reach "calibration training," then evaluate three peers' and their own work, reducing grading burden while developing writing and evaluation skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Calibration training requires students to achieve a standard of agreement with the instructor's rating before evaluating peers' work; instructors should expect students to need support for challenging tasks.
#### Constraints
- Students may be particularly hesitant to talk in front of hundreds of peers.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large-enrollment courses

### Target Learning Goals
- collaborative learning during assessment and development of writing and peer-evaluation skills
