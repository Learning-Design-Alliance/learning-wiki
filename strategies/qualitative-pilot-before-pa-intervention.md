# Use pilot qualitative work with teachers and pupils to surface barriers and facilitators before large-scale physically active lesson interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates a pre-intervention qualitative design: demonstrating a sample 10-min Olympic-themed VFT to teachers (semi-structured interviews, n=12) and pupils (focus groups, k=3, n=18), then inductively theming transcripts to identify facilitators (flexibility, inclusion, peer enjoyment) and barriers (time, technology resistance, novelty fade). It argues such pilot work helps anticipate institutional and individual barriers and minimise attrition for newly developed interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recruitment of both teacher and pupil perspectives before trial design, using methods suited to children such as focus groups with a speaking token
#### Constraints
- Demonstration with the researcher may prompt different responses than independent classroom use; convenience sampling may bias responses

### Target Learners
- elementary school pupils and their teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- designing acceptable physically active curriculum lessons
