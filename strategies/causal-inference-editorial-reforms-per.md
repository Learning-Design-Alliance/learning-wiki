# Editorial and field-level reforms to raise the quality of causal reasoning in physics education research

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author recommends concrete structural changes so that policy recommendations rest on valid causal estimates: "Adding some experts in causal inference to the editorial board of PRPER (and other journals) might be helpful." He further suggests a referee checkbox indicating whether a review evaluated causal inference, and a causal reasoning primer written for PER by at least one author with domain-specific PER knowledge and one with solid grounding in causal inference. Any such conversation should include causal-inference experts from outside the PER community, and effects of an abrupt editorial change could be evaluated with a regression discontinuity analysis if a reliable outcome measure were found.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- participation of true causal inference experts from outside the PER community in any field-wide conversation
#### Constraints
- the effects of a primer or organized conversations would probably be gradual and harder to disentangle from confounders
- the author notes the primer's authorship would require expertise he himself lacks

### Target Learners
- physics education researchers
- PRPER editors and referees

### Target Learning Goals
- improving the validity of causal inference and policy recommendation in published PER
