# Break synthesis clicker questions into separate sub-questions before combining them into one question

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that multiple-choice synthesis problems, which combine mathematical skills with physics knowledge, be decomposed before peer discussion. They state it "may be advantageous to first break down such multiple-choice synthesis problems into separate multiple -choice sub-problems (to be posed as ICT and GCT) to make them more manageable for students to think about and discuss with peers before combining them into one clicker question." This follows their finding of no improvement on a synthesis question.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The sub-problems are posed as ICT and GCT before being combined into one clicker question
#### Constraints
- Recommended based on one question (Question III) in one case study where students did not improve after peer discussion

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- quantum mechanics concepts requiring synthesis of mathematical and physics knowledge
