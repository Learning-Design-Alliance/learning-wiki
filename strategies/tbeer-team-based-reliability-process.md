# Team-Based Efforts to Enhance Reliability (TBEER): constructive intercoder consensus during qualitative analysis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
TBEER is a team-based procedure for strengthening reliability in qualitative research by verifying trustworthiness during rather than after analysis. Over a two-month period, coauthors independently coded transcripts and reports, then compared, discussed, and debated codes to iteratively reach consensus through regular peer debriefing, with the lead author maintaining an audit trail. The authors contrast this with traditional post hoc inter-rater reliability, adopting a "constructive (during the process)" procedure instead of an "evaluative (post hoc)" test, and term the outcome intercoder consensus rather than reliability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple coders meeting regularly (at least weekly) over an extended period
- Independent initial coding by each coder before consensus discussion
- An audit trail documenting the team's deliberations
#### Constraints
- Developed for qualitative physics education research with group-level data; the authors note some scholars question whether traditional IRR tests fulfill their intended purpose

### Target Learners
- not applicable; a research-methods strategy for PER researchers analyzing student data

### Target Learning Goals
- enhancing rigor, transparency, and trustworthiness of qualitative findings in physics education research
