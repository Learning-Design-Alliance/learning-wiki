# Integrate computation in science classes with tasks beyond procedural programming use, and ensure students have sufficiently strong computational foundations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend designing computational activities that go beyond procedural use of programming as a tool, so that students' full sensemaking potential is realised and critical thinking is supported. They state: "If critical thinking is important to us, we should attempt to realise the full sensemaking potential in computational activities." They add it is "necessary to ensure that our students have suf- ﬁciently strong computational foundations to engage in these sensemaking tasks." Future research on thresholds for entering and resolving sensemaking with computation would inform how instructors integrate computation in science classes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have sufficiently strong computational foundations to engage in these sensemaking tasks
#### Constraints
- Further research is needed to determine under which circumstances the potential for sensemaking can be fulfilled; how this plays out in a classroom setting without an interviewer to sustain the process remains to be investigated

### Target Learners
- first-year university science students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical thinking
- sensemaking in computational science activities
