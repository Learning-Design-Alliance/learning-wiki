# Pictures & Visuals

## Description
This strategy involves the use of images, videos, and other visual aids to enhance learning and memory. Visuals summarize content, aid in understanding, and make learning more impactful and memorable. Visuals are more concrete and easily remembered than abstract words, and are processed more efficiently by the brain. Effective visuals can decrease learning time, improve comprehension, enhance retrieval, and increase retention. However, visuals can also negatively impact learning if used inappropriately, such as off-topic or poor-quality images.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to visual resources such as picture books, images, videos, and graphic organizers. Teachers need to integrate visuals purposefully into lesson plans and ensure they align with learning objectives.
#### Constraints
- Inappropriate or poorly selected visuals can distract learners and hinder understanding. Overuse of visuals without clear learning objectives can also be ineffective. Stock photographs and generic graphics may lack engagement.

### Affordances
- Visuals help learners make sense of content, direct attention, and facilitate emotional connections, leading to better engagement and retention. They simplify complex ideas and bridge learned materials with the unfamiliar.

### Personalization
- Visuals can be tailored to suit different learning styles and preferences. The level of complexity and detail can be adjusted based on the learner's existing knowledge and abilities. Visuals can be presented in various formats, such as photos, illustrations, videos, and graphic organizers.

## Assessment Evidence
- Measure improvement in comprehension and retention through quizzes and tests. Observe learner engagement and participation in discussions. Analyze learner-created visuals and graphic organizers for understanding and application of concepts.

## Impact
- Studies confirm that visual information is retained more effectively than written or spoken information. Visuals can improve learning by up to 400 percent. Visuals summarize content into smaller, and easier to process chunks, and when you select the right visuals, they offer more comprehensibility than text-based explanations or only audios.
