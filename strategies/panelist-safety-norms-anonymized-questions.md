# Protect panelist safety when students or vulnerable-population members speak, using moderator relationships, norms statements, and anonymized questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
When including student voices on diversity panels, the section establishes safety first: "if a person with authority who has a good relationship with the student is a member or moderator of the panel, this can be sufficient". Additional steps include reading a statement setting norms for audience questions and collecting questions via 3x5 cards or an electronic anonymized system filtered through the moderator, which also lets people ask questions they would be uncomfortable asking verbally. Options are discussed with the panelists themselves beforehand.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A moderator with authority and a good relationship with the student panelist; advance discussion of safety options with panelists from vulnerable populations
#### Constraints
- The authors frame these as practices they have found powerful, not as tested interventions; emotional and physical safety must be established before student participation

### Target Learners
- Students and members of vulnerable populations serving on panels; section members in the audience

### Target Learning Goals
- Hearing firsthand educational experiences of marginalized students so members can create safe, welcoming physics learning environments
