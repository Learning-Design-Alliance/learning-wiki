# Schedule technique demonstrations after the initial learning-curve phase to consolidate proper laparoscopic technique

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors observed temporary plateaus and dips in learning curves attributed to participants deviating from demonstrated methods, taking shortcuts, feeling rushed, or experimenting with techniques; terminal feedback restating the most efficient techniques rectified this. They recommend that "there may be benefit in scheduling demonstrations after the initial phase of the LC to consolidate proper technique and prevent this deviation."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Terminal participant-specific feedback by skill demonstrators to remind participants of appropriate technique and increase economy of movement
#### Constraints
- Recommended based on observed plateaus in a novice simulation trial; the authors note no definitive plateaus were reached within the ten skill sessions

### Target Learners
- novice surgical trainees and medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- basic laparoscopic procedural skills
