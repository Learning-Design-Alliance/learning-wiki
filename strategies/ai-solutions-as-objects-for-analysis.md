# Treat AI-generated solutions as worked-example-like objects for analysis that students reconstruct, question, and test

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Rather than treating an AI response only as an answer to accept or reject, the article recommends treating it as a "worked -example-like solution" for critical study: reconstructing the reasoning, noticing omissions and making assumptions explicit, asking clarifying follow-up questions, and checking whether the argument is physically and mathematically justified. For students who cannot follow the reasoning, the work may begin by locating where understanding breaks down.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need guidance on how to study AI-generated solutions critically; the article notes worked-example benefits depend on how examples are structured and how students are guided to use them.
#### Constraints
- Asking the tool to explain a part of the solution is not the same as understanding it, per the article.

### Target Learners
- pre-service physics teachers
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical evaluation of AI-generated solutions
- reconstructing and testing physical and mathematical reasoning
