# 90-minute resident-to-learner cesarean section simulation event with prework, role assignment, and debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A specific teaching recipe: learners receive an instrument-review PowerPoint and CS steps/time-out handout one week in advance; a 90-minute session includes a presurvey, a 10-minute instrument presentation, a full-team simulated CS (surgical scrub, time-out, skin incision to closure) with assigned essential and nonessential roles, projected video for the audience, and a facilitated debrief with a critical actions checklist. The article describes "a structured curriculum, pre-/postsimulation surveys, a surgical instrument review, a mannequin with the CS model containing a fetus in breech position, and live video streaming." Upper-level residents practiced teaching in a low-stress setting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Preceptor-to-surgical-team ratio of 1:1 or 1:2, with a faculty member or upper-level resident as preceptor; reserved simulation-center equipment; a classroom arranged to mimic an operating room.
#### Constraints
- The procedure took about 1 hour, varying with learner level; live video streaming is not a necessary component and had audio/lighting problems.

### Target Learners
- Third-year medical students
- OB/GYN residents at all levels

### Target Learning Goals
- Performing the steps of a cesarean section
- calling and using correct instruments per layer
- sterile technique
- surgical time-out
- teaching surgical skills
