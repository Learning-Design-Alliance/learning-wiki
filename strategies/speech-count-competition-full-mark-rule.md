# Use full-mark-attainable speech-count competitions to activate group discussion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends organizing intergroup competitions based on the number of speeches, with a scoring rule under which "each group could receive a full mark" (e.g., groups exceeding five speeches all earn 5 points regardless of ranking). The authors report that this rule "greatly promotes students' sense of responsibility and competitiveness" and helps identify students willing to take leading roles, after which scoring ceases to be the students' primary concern and discussion proceeds smoothly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Randomly drawn groups of roughly equal size with assigned roles (team leader, time keeper, recorder)
- A small share of the final grade allocated to classroom interaction
#### Constraints
- The authors state that simply dividing students into groups does not enable active participation; the competition structure is what activates discussion

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students in offline participatory classes

### Target Learning Goals
- active participation in group discussion
- engagement with evaluative clinical materials
