Pair reading interventions with a rewards system to motivate students with learning disabilities who dislike reading¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

After observing that one experimental participant scored below the maximum and stated he did not like reading, the author recommends that educators use a rewards system tied to focused reading and test performance. The article states educators "should utilize a rewards system and inform the students with learning disabilities that there is a reward for each one who focuses on what he or she reads and then performs well on the test". The author also wondered whether a reward would have motivated that participant to gain a full score.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators inform students in advance that a reward is available for focused reading and good test performance

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is speculative; the article did not test rewards, and the author proposes future study of video games for visual skills as a separate avenue

Target Learners¶

  • Students with learning disabilities who are unmotivated to read

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading comprehension and engagement with reading tasks

Claims¶

  • Experimental-group students with learning disabilities scored higher on a reading comprehension test after the group story-mapping intervention than control-group students [+W]
  • Experimental-group participants attributed their comprehension gains to the group story-mapping strategy in interviews [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use repeated reading probes several grade levels below the student's grade level, with visible goals, extrinsic rewards, and daily progress charting
  • Positive Reinforcement Systems
