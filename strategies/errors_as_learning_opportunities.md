# Errors as Learning Opportunities

## Description
Creating a classroom culture where errors become learning opportunities shifts the perception of mistakes as failures to opportunities for growth and understanding. When a student provides an incorrect answer, instructors should explore what aspects of the student's thinking were correct, providing a chance for the student to identify their error or misconception. For example, instead of immediately correcting a wrong answer or moving to a student with the right answer, the teacher can ask, "What is right about that student’s thinking?" This reinforces the idea that learning involves a process of discovery and refinement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classroom environment that values mistakes as learning opportunities. Time to explore student thinking and provide feedback. Willingness to shift from error correction to error exploration.
#### Constraints
- May require a significant shift in classroom culture. Some students may still feel uncomfortable with making mistakes in front of others. Requires careful facilitation to ensure the focus remains on learning rather than judgment.

- K-12 students, particularly those struggling with math or who have a fixed mindset about their abilities.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Promote a growth mindset, encourage self-correction, improve understanding of mathematical concepts, reduce anxiety around making mistakes

### Affordances
- Reduces anxiety about being wrong, encourages students to see mistakes as part of the learning process, provides insights into student thinking, allows for targeted instruction

### Personalization
- Adapt the types of errors addressed to the specific needs and levels of learners. Provide structured support for identifying errors, such as checklists or guided questions.
