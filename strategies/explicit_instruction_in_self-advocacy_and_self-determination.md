# Explicit Instruction in Self-Advocacy and Self-Determination

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy applies the structure of [Explicit Instruction](../principles/explicit-instruction.md) to the personal and interpersonal skills of self-determination: understanding one's disability, strengths, and needs; knowing one's legal rights; communicating needs to others; setting and pursuing goals; solving problems; and participating in decisions about one's own education. Rather than hoping students will absorb these skills implicitly, the instructor names the skill, models it, provides guided practice with feedback, and supports independent application in authentic settings.

## Design Implications

Self-advocacy skills respond to the same instructional conditions as academic skills: they are learned most reliably when taught directly, modeled, practiced with feedback, and applied in real contexts [Self-determination interventions produce positive effects when instruction is explicit and systematic.](../claims/self-determination-instruction-improves-outcomes.md) [+M]. Because these skills are socially situated, instruction must move beyond knowledge ("what are my rights?") to performance ("how do I request an accommodation in this meeting?"), which requires rehearsal with feedback rather than lecture alone [Self-determination instruction is more effective when paired with practice in authentic settings.](../claims/self-determination-instruction-improves-outcomes.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured curriculum or lesson sequence that breaks self-advocacy into teachable components (e.g., [Task Analysis](../methods/task-analysis.md))
- [Modeling](../elements/modeling.md) of target skills, including [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) to expose the reasoning behind self-advocacy decisions
- Guided [Practice](../elements/practice.md) with feedback, often through [Role-Play](role-play.md) or simulation
- Authentic application opportunities — real IEP meetings, real accommodation requests, real goal-setting — not only classroom simulations
- Student involvement in planning and decision-making ([Student-Led IEPs](../elements/student-led-ieps.md))

#### Constraints
- Instruction that stays at the knowledge level (defining rights, listing strategies) without behavioral rehearsal does not reliably produce self-advocacy behavior [Self-determination instruction is more effective when paired with practice in authentic settings.](../claims/self-determination-instruction-improves-outcomes.md) [-M]
- Skills taught in a single setting often fail to generalize to new people, environments, or demands without deliberate programming for transfer [Self-determination instruction is more effective when paired with practice in authentic settings.](../claims/self-determination-instruction-improves-outcomes.md) [-M]
- Instruction that overrides student voice — teaching students *what* to say without honoring their actual choices — undermines the autonomy the strategy is meant to build [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [-M]
- One-shot lessons or brief units produce weaker and less durable effects than sustained, multi-year instruction [Self-determination interventions produce positive effects when instruction is explicit and systematic.](../claims/self-determination-instruction-improves-outcomes.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Curriculum-embedded:** Programs such as the [Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction](https://www.ou.edu/education/zarrow/sdli) (SDLMI) embed goal-setting and problem-solving into ongoing instruction across settings
- **Lesson-based:** Standalone curricula such as [Self-Advocacy and Conflict Resolution](https://www.ou.edu/education/zarrow) or [Whose Future Is It Anyway?](https://www.ou.edu/education/zarrow/whose-future-is-it-anyway) teach discrete skills in sequenced lessons
- **Embedded in IEP process:** Students draft, present, and lead their own IEP meetings, applying skills in the highest-stakes authentic context available
- **Embedded in academic instruction:** Teachers prompt self-advocacy during content lessons (e.g., requesting clarification, selecting accommodations) rather than teaching it separately

### Target Learners
- Students with disabilities, particularly those transitioning to postsecondary education or employment, where self-advocacy demands increase sharply [Self-determination instruction improves post-school outcomes.](../claims/self-determination-instruction-improves-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Adolescents and young adults, who are developmentally primed for identity formation and autonomy [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Students who have historically been excluded from decisions about their own education and need structured support to develop participatory skills
- Not limited to special education: any learner who needs to navigate institutional systems, request support, or manage their own learning benefits from explicit instruction in these skills

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-knowledge: understanding one's strengths, needs, and rights
- Communication: requesting accommodations, disclosing appropriately, negotiating with instructors and employers
- Goal-setting and attainment: identifying meaningful goals, planning action, monitoring progress
- Problem-solving and decision-making: generating options, evaluating consequences, acting on choices
- Self-regulation: monitoring one's own behavior and adjusting strategies [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Assess current skills and needs.** Use a self-determination assessment (e.g., the [AIR Self-Determination Scale](https://www.ou.edu/education/zarrow)) to identify which components the student already uses and which need instruction.
2. **Name and explain the skill.** Define the target skill (e.g., requesting an accommodation) and why it matters, connecting it to the student's own goals.
3. **Model the skill.** Demonstrate the skill in context, using [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) to expose the reasoning: what to notice, what to say, how to handle a difficult response.
4. **Rehearse with feedback.** Provide guided [Practice](../elements/practice.md) through [Role-Play](role-play.md) or simulation, with immediate, specific feedback on both the behavior and the reasoning behind it.
5. **Apply in authentic settings.** Support the student to use the skill in a real context — an actual meeting, request, or decision — with support faded as competence grows ([Fading](../elements/fading.md)).
6. **Reflect and generalize.** Debrief the application: what worked, what was hard, where else the skill applies. Plan the next setting or variation for practice.

## Examples
- **[Whose Future Is It Anyway?](https://www.ou.edu/education/zarrow/whose-future-is-it-anyway)** (University of Oklahoma Zarrow Center) — a free curriculum in which students explore their disability, rights, and goals, culminating in preparing for and leading their own IEP meeting
- **[Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction](https://www.ou.edu/education/zarrow/sdli)** (SDLMI) — teachers coach students through a cycle of setting a goal, making an action plan, and self-evaluating progress, embedded in existing academic instruction
- **[I'm Determined](https://www.imdetermined.org)** (Virginia Department of Education) — a statewide initiative providing tools (e.g., the "one-pager," good-day plan) that students use to communicate their needs and preferences to teachers and IEP teams
- **[PEPNET 2](https://www.pepnet.org)** — resources for students who are deaf or hard of hearing to practice disclosing their needs and requesting accommodations in postsecondary settings
