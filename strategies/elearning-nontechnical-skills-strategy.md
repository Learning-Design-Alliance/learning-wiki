# Use serious games, recorded-session review, live webinars, and video-assisted debriefing to develop non-technical skills in remote simulation-based education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends learners enhance teamwork, leadership and clinical decision-making through serious games (games used with a pedagogical purpose), while mentors review recorded simulation sessions to assess performance and plan tailor-made interventions. It states virtual e-learning platforms should be implemented with live webinars, which "should be preferred to video-recorded lessons, to facilitate the debate among mentors and learners", and that debriefing sessions should be the core of e-learning education, including video-assisted debriefing using previously recorded sessions. This is the authors' recommendation for pandemic-era and future SBME.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recorded simulation sessions for review, e-learning platforms supporting live webinars, and capacity to run debriefing sessions with video support
#### Constraints
- Recommended for settings where face-to-face group simulation is not possible; no outcome data on effectiveness are reported in this article

### Target Learners
- pediatric trainees and mentors

### Target Learning Goals
- teamwork, leadership, clinical decision-making, and learning from mistakes through debriefing
