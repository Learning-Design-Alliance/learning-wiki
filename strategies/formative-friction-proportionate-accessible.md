# Formative friction: require goal statements, alternative comparison, and justification before high-stakes delegation, kept proportionate and accessible

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy distinguishes exclusionary friction from formative friction in AI-mediated workflows. "Formative friction asks users to state a goal before optimizing, compare alternatives before accepting a recommendation, confront a counterargument before submitting a claim, or explain a choice before delegating a high-stakes action." The article specifies that such friction should be proportionate and accessible: the burden should match the stakes, must not exclude disabled users, multilingual users, overburdened workers, or people with limited resources, and users should be able to challenge or adapt friction when it is misplaced. The aim is to keep necessary difficulty from becoming a polite name for exclusion while preserving occasions to exercise judgment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Friction must be proportionate to stakes and accessible, with users able to challenge or adapt it when misplaced
#### Constraints
- Empty friction blocks access without improving agency; exclusionary friction such as needless paperwork and opaque appeals burdens people with less time, money, or institutional power

### Target Learners
- workers
- students
- public-service users interacting with AI-mediated workflows

### Target Learning Goals
- exercising judgment before delegation, including goal formulation, comparison of alternatives, and justification of choices

- Five Post Instrumental Capacities
