# Combine recruitment with internal upskilling formats such as on-the-job learning, mentoring, weekly internal seminars and hands-on training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article reports that most companies pursue a mixed strategy of recruitment and training, since company needs are often too specific for external training. Internal formats include learning on the job, mentoring and onboarding laboratory stays, regular exchange meetings, and hands-on workshops. One start-up reports: "We have a weekly sort of seminar that's given internally", through which a non-quantum employee learned from colleagues. Upskilling builds hybrid quantum-classical expertise while employees continue classical careers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Novices must first reach an intermediate level of proficiency to benefit from expert mentoring, since the gap for novices is too big.
#### Constraints
- Start-ups lack the capacity for upskilling until they begin to scale up; certificates from upskilling programs may not be recognized in hiring if not considered credible.

### Target Learners
- engineers and technicians in quantum companies
- marketing and sales staff
- decision makers

### Target Learning Goals
- QT awareness and use-case recognition
- company-specific quantum skills
- hybrid quantum-classical expertise
