# Reflection on Learning

## Description
Reflection on learning involves linking a current experience to previous learnings (called scaffolding). It also means drawing forth cognitive and emotional information from several sources (visual, auditory, kinesthetic, tactile). It also means applying what is learned to contexts beyond the original situations in which something was learned. Reflection can be enhanced when we ponder our learning with others.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time for reflection during or after learning. Various strategies to guide students through a period of reflection (discussions, interviews, questioning, logs and journals).
#### Constraints
- Students may be caught in a dilemma: 'What am I supposed to do? How do I ‘reflect’? I've already completed this assignment! Why do I have to think about it anymore?'

## Impact
- Teachers who promote reflective classrooms ensure that students are fully engaged in the process of making meaning. They organize instruction so that students are the producers, not just the consumers, of knowledge.
