# Assess the linearity assumption before universal growth-mindset interventions, since shifting strong fixed-mindset individuals toward growth may backfire

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that mindset interventions not assume any movement toward stronger growth endorsement is beneficial. It warns that "promoting a sense of growth in these communities might inadvertently shift individuals into the trough and lead to worse performance", and that future work "should carefully assess this linearity assumption and consider how the same interventions might work for some but potentially backfire for others". Interventions should be tailored to individuals' starting position on the mindset continuum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of individuals' baseline mindset position relative to the vertex of the U-shaped performance function
#### Constraints
- Pushing strong fixed-mindset individuals toward moderate beliefs may move them into the performance trough, per the article's own U-shaped findings

### Target Learners
- university undergraduates
- students in communities with high structural constraints

### Target Learning Goals
- avoiding performance harm from poorly targeted mindset interventions
