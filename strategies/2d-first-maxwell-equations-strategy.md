# Introduce Maxwell's equations with two-dimensional examples before generalizing to 3D

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The paper's central instructional strategy is to 'introduce Maxwell's equations first in simple two-dimensional examples before generalizing them to their full three-dimensional form'. Examples can be presented as warm-ups from an imagined two-dimensional universe, or as slices of three-dimensional systems with translational symmetry such as an infinite line of charge or infinite solenoid. This gives electromagnetism the gentle 2D starting point that collisions, trusses, projectiles and orbits already enjoy in the physics curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires the bivector (or at least decorated-vector) description of magnetism, since cross products and curls in the traditional language require three dimensions
#### Constraints
- Only the pure bivector approach generalizes to higher dimensions such as relativistic spacetime; the same approach generalizes to higher dimensions as well

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students encountering Maxwell's equations for the first time

### Target Learning Goals
- applying Gauss's law and Ampere's law with symmetry arguments
- interpreting the line integral in Ampere's law as a sum

### Affordances
- Bivector Magnetic Field Tile Representation
