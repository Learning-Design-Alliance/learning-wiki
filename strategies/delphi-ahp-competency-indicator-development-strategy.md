# Use a combined literature review, semi-structured expert interview, and two-round modified Delphi pipeline with AHP weighting to build context-specific competency indicator systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's methodological recipe: generate a preliminary indicator pool from a literature review plus semi-structured interviews structured by a competency model, then run a two-round modified Delphi consultation with retention criteria (mean importance > 3.5, coefficient of variation < 0.20, full-score proportion > 20%), and set tier weights with analytic hierarchy process including consistency tests. The authors state this approach let them "systematically screen, integrate, and validate potential indicators" and achieve high consensus (authority coefficient 0.895).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Senior experts with relevant clinical and educational experience; anonymity between experts; two-week scoring windows with reminders; dual-entry data verification
#### Constraints
- Delphi results rest on subjective expert judgments that may reflect personal experience and professional preferences; regional imbalance in the expert panel (84% South China) required additional consistency analysis

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine residents (framework developers targeting residency training programs)

### Target Learning Goals
- Construction of standardized, quantifiable competency indicator systems for residency training
