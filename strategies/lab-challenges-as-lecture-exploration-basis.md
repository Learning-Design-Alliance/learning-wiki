# Use identified lab challenges, such as graph-interpretation difficulties, as the basis for deeper exploration in later lectures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
When students struggled during the dynamic friction activity, the authors recommend treating the difficulty as a resource: "Addressing such challenges in lectures throughout the course, for example by highlighting force and motion diagrams for different scenarios, could potentially alleviate these problems and help students construct their knowledge". The strategy connects laboratory difficulties back to instruction rather than treating them as failures, and the authors say it highlights "the value of using identified challenges as a basis for deeper exploration and understanding in future lessons".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must identify specific activities where students struggle
- Lecture time later in the course must be available to revisit the challenge
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a strategy that could potentially alleviate the problems; it was not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- High school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Interpreting acceleration and force-motion graphs
- Constructing knowledge of dynamics from experimental data
