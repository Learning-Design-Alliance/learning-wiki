# Size Angoff panels to test length and mix judge expertise; skip a second round unless test data will be provided

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article distils its simulation findings into three practice points for standard setting: "a panel of about fifteen judges provides a reliable and valid cut-score for a test consisting of 80 items or above", fewer than 15 items requires at least 20 judges with more judges as items decline, and panels should mix expertise levels unless experts are demonstrably not more stringent. A second round is redundant without additional test data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough judges must be available; the recommendation presumes judges' attributes resemble those simulated (normally distributed accuracy, stringency, leadership).
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from simulated data with standardised parameters; the authors state real examinations, examinees or judges may differ, and the recommendations may not apply to them.

### Target Learners
- medical education assessment programmes (MCQ and OSCE examinations)

### Target Learning Goals
- defensible standard setting / cut-score determination for competence decisions
