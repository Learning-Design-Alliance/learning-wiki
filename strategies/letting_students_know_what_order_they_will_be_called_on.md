# Letting students know what order they will be called on

## Description
Informing students of the order in which they will be called upon to respond can help reduce challenges related to inhibition. This proactive approach allows students time to mentally prepare their responses and manage their impulse to speak out of turn, thereby improving focus and participation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness of individual student needs and potential challenges, planning and preparation for calling on students, and consistent implementation.
#### Constraints
- May not be effective if the sequence is not followed consistently. Some students may become overly focused on the sequence rather than the content.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Students with challenges related to inhibition, impulsivity, or difficulty waiting their turn

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Reduce impulsivity, improve focus and attention, enhance student participation, support self-regulation

### Affordances
- Provides predictability and reduces anxiety by clarifying expectations. Allows students to plan and organize their thoughts, improving the quality of their responses. Fosters a more inclusive and equitable classroom environment where all students have an opportunity to participate.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted by using a variety of methods for calling on students, such as randomly selecting names, following a seating chart, or using a digital tool to generate a sequence.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe student behavior (e.g., reduced impulsivity, increased participation). Collect feedback from students on their comfort level and engagement.

## Impact
- By letting students know what order they will be called on, teachers can provide them with the opportunity to plan their response and wait until their turn, thereby improving focus and participation.
