# Have students examine plots of many gases' heat capacities to weigh evidence for the equipartition theorem's applicability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A teaching strategy for introductory thermal physics: after deriving U/N = 3kBT/2, show students Fig. 2 with the (erroneous) expectation that CV = 3N kB/2 independent of temperature, letting them discover the additional degrees of freedom of molecules before the theorem is even named. The article reports that "the examination of digital thermodynamic data — that has only recently become widely available — gives students an active role in judging the applicability of what they are learning", practicing how scientists "weigh the evidence".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the NIST Chemistry Webbook heat capacity data and the provided Python plotting code
#### Constraints
- The article notes the approach is introduced in an ahistorical manner in introductory courses, with deeper discussion deferred until statistical mechanics is studied

### Target Learners
- introductory thermal physics students
- statistical mechanics students

### Target Learning Goals
- judge the applicability of a physical concept against data
- practice weighing evidence as working scientists do
