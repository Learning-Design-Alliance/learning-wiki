# Dice-rolled measurement simulation as an active, slowed-down alternative to computer simulations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students convert a twenty-sided die roll into a quantum measurement outcome by comparing the roll percentage to the predicted probability Pm(+1). The author argues dice give students "active participation in generating quantum data" and that slowing the process gives time for reflection, since automation in computer simulations obscures the granular way data develop and the convergence of means toward expectation values.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A twenty-sided die or other random number generator, and a game table of state probabilities computed from Eq. (1)
#### Constraints
- Up to about twenty measurements can be done simultaneously without too much bother; computer simulations are more suitable when large sample sizes are needed

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in modern physics or quantum mechanics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- experiencing the randomness of individual quantum measurements
- seeing statistical means converge to expectation values
