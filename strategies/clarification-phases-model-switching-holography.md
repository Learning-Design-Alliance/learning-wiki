# Provide meticulous clarification phases in which students present conceptions without teacher scrutiny, work through alternative models, use model-switching simulations, and connect model switches to everyday experience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article formulates four concluding consequences for physics lessons drawn from the observed student conceptions. These include "Meticulous phases of clarification" in which students present their conceptions without teacher scrutiny, deliberate discussion of model conceptions via alternative approaches on different model levels, computer simulation programs that allow a model switch during the simulation process, and integration of model switching into students' everyday experiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Concealment and suppression of preconcepts and naive student conceptions is to be avoided, as the article states it leads to an ulterior consolidation of these concepts.
#### Constraints
- For holography specifically, the article states the separation between the ray model and the wave model should take place in a clear and structured manner, and that lesson plans combining both model levels carry the great danger of uncritical adoption by students.

### Target Learners
- secondary school level I and II physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of interference optics and holography
- meta-conceptual use and switching of models of light
