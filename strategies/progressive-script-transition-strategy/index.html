Schedule a progressive transition when switching a language between writing scripts¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that a change of alphabet be introduced progressively because transfer of handwriting skill between scripts is uneven. They state that "The transfer is slower for some features than for others, indicating the need for a progressive transition" and explicitly that they do not claim the transfer is rapid and systematic. The strategy applies to educational planners managing sequential digraphia, such as Kazakhstan's move from Cyrillic to Latin-based Kazakh, where pupils must develop handwriting proficiency in both scripts during the transition stage.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Pupils already possess some handwriting automation in the source script; transfer depends on the level of handwriting automation attained in the first alphabet.

Constraints¶

  • The transfer is not rapid and systematic; the authors explicitly do not claim it is.
  • Results come from a copying task only and do not predict global effects of a radical change in Kazakh culture or generalize to spontaneous writing or dictation.

Target Learners¶

  • Primary school children (grades 1–4) in a bilingual/sequential-digraphia education system

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Handwriting proficiency and fine motor control in a newly introduced script

Claims¶

  • Children's handwriting specificity transfers across scripts: 90% of Cyrillic–Latin feature rank correlations were significant and correlation levels increased with grade [+W]
  • For features such as Mean Velocity and Handwriting Density, Cyrillic improves faster than Latin, producing an increasing gap that indicates incomplete transfer [+W]
  • Transfer between scripts depends on the level of handwriting automation: Cyrillic–Latin differences in shakiness decreased with grade and were nearly non-existent by grade 4 [+W]
  • Some Cyrillic–Latin handwriting feature differences are constant across grades and reflect intrinsic differences between the alphabets, particularly pressure and tilt features [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Fine motor handwriting skills transfer positively from Cyrillic to Latin: Latin-script writing quality improved with grade despite zero Latin experience [+W]
