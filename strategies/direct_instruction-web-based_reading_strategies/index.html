Direct Instruction Web Based Reading Strategies¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 13 studies (5 causal, 4 review, 3 quant-synthesis, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 3 of 13 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

This strategy applies Direct Instruction to the specific demands of reading on the internet: navigating hyperlinks, evaluating source credibility, handling multiple open texts, and integrating information across pages. The instructor explicitly names, models, and sequences these strategies — often through screen-shared Think-Alouds — then guides practice before releasing learners to independent application. It treats web reading as a distinct skill set rather than an automatic extension of print reading.

Design Implications¶

Web reading imposes extraneous demands (navigation, pop-ups, fragmented attention) that consume working memory before comprehension even begins Cognitive overload degrades learning outcomes. [+S]. Explicit strategy instruction reduces unguided search and trial-and-error, which is especially valuable for novices Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices. [+M]. Because skilled web readers deploy strategies flexibly rather than mechanically, instruction must include guided practice with Fading of support, not just rule presentation Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility. [+M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Live or screencast modeling of authentic web tasks (searching, evaluating a source, cross-checking claims), with narration of decisions
  • A sequenced strategy set — e.g., question generation, search-term selection, credibility evaluation (author, evidence, corroboration), synthesis across sources
  • Guided practice with immediate, task-focused Feedback Feedback is most effective at task and process levels. [+S]
  • Authentic web texts and tasks; decontextualized worksheets do not transfer to live browsing

Constraints¶

  • Highly scripted delivery can produce rigid rule-following that fails when pages or tasks deviate from the practiced format [~M] — web texts vary too much for fixed procedures alone
  • Direct instruction alone underperforms discovery for learners who already have strong web reading strategies; the expertise reversal effect applies Guidance that helps novices can hinder more knowledgeable learners. [~S]
  • Cluttered pages, ads, and multitasking during practice sessions add extraneous load that undermines the instruction itself Cognitive overload degrades learning outcomes. [+S]
  • Strategy instruction without independent application produces dependence on the teacher rather than self-regulated reading Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility. [-M]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Gradual release ("I do, we do, you do") within a single lesson vs. a multi-week strategy unit
  • Whole-class screen demonstration vs. small-group coaching with live browsing
  • Curated link sets that reduce navigation demands as a scaffold, later removed
  • Integration with Annotating tools (e.g., Hypothesis) to make evaluation visible and assessable

Target Learners¶

  • Adolescents and adults with adequate print decoding but weak online reading comprehension — the most common profile of struggling web readers
  • Novices benefit most from explicit modeling; advanced readers gain little and may disengage Guidance that helps novices can hinder more knowledgeable learners. [~S]
  • English learners, when paired with Accessible Vocabulary and Syntax supports

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Online research and comprehension: locating, evaluating, and synthesizing web information
  • Critical evaluation of source credibility and misinformation resistance
  • Self-regulated reading: monitoring comprehension and adjusting strategy across texts

Instructions¶

  1. Name the strategy and its purpose (e.g., "triangulate a claim across three sources") using an Advance Organizer to frame the sequence.
  2. Model the strategy on a live page via Think-Aloud, making evaluation decisions explicit ("this site has no author or date — I need corroboration").
  3. Chunk the strategy into steps and post them as a visible reference, consistent with Chunking Chunking reduces working memory load. [+S].
  4. Guide whole-class practice on a new page, eliciting decisions rather than making them all yourself (Coaching).
  5. Release learners to paired or independent practice on authentic tasks, fading prompts as accuracy grows Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility. [+M].
  6. Debrief with Class Discussion on what strategies learners used and when they failed, building conditional knowledge.

Related Strategies¶

  • Reciprocal Teaching — a dialogic alternative that hands strategy leadership to students after initial modeling
  • Close Reading — complementary deep-reading routine applicable to individual web texts
  • Search Strategy Scaffolds — narrows in on the query formulation step
  • Use Mozilla's four basic web literacies to frame open learning for personal development
  • Teach AI-mediated judgement and make process, judgement and transfer visible in assessment

Examples¶

  • Coiro's Online Reading Comprehension practices — classroom-tested protocols for modeling internet inquiry, including think-alouds of search and evaluation.
  • News Literacy Project — direct instruction in evaluating source credibility, with modeled "lateral reading" demonstrations adapted from the Stanford History Education Group's research.
  • Stanford History Education Group — Civic Online Reasoning — published curriculum of explicit, sequenced lessons in evaluating online sources, with modeling and guided practice materials.
