# Evaluate AI tools with the same rigor as new drugs, diagnostic tests, or treatment protocols

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that health professionals critically assess AI technologies' performance, accuracy, and reliability, and that "AI-based tools must undergo extensive testing for accuracy, generalizability, efficacy, and fairness to ensure they meet clinical standards". It also stresses ongoing evaluation and monitoring to detect performance shifts or biases over time, given the continuous learning nature of AI systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Foundational understanding of AI principles to determine whether tools suit specific clinical settings
#### Constraints
- Regular reassessment is necessary because continuously learning AI systems may shift performance across patient populations and clinical scenarios

### Target Learners
- health care professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- critical appraisal of AI tools for clinical use
