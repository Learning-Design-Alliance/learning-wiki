# Use the medical health education item-writing evidence base to evaluate MOOC assessment quality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's approach: because the medical health education literature "has an extensive evidence-base with regard to writing quality MCQ items", MOOC assessments can be evaluated using a protocol derived from that literature. Researchers enrol in MOOCs, manually record questions, options and correct answers, then apply the adapted evaluation instrument to detect item-writing flaws.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Evaluators with topic expertise; manual enrolment in MOOCs to take quizzes, which the authors describe as a labour-intensive data gathering approach
#### Constraints
- Applied to English-language MOOCs in health sciences and computer science; question text cannot be redistributed due to platform copyright

### Target Learners
- MOOC developers, learning designers and education researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving the quality and fairness of multiple-choice assessment in online courses
