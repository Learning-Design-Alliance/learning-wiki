# Track when students join research groups as a departmental metric to surface hidden inequities in the group search

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that departments rarely record when students join research groups, leaving this critical step untracked. Because hidden inequities are most likely to go unnoticed when student outcomes and progress are poorly tracked, better understanding the factors influencing when students join may reveal systemic inequities in the search process. Departments could monitor joining timelines alongside program expectations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Departments must collect data on when students commit to research groups
#### Constraints
- The article notes tracking when students join and leave research groups is not a common metric for departments to record

### Target Learners
- physics PhD students, particularly first- and second-year students

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable access to research group integration and timely advisor-advisee matching
