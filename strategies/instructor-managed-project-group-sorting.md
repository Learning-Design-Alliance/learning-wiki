# Instructor-managed sorting of students into research groups using rankings plus group-dynamics considerations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
On project selection day, students ranked research projects after presentations, and instructors formed groups using those rankings while also weighing interpersonal dynamics. The authors describe this as an improvement over a lottery-style assignment: "The instructors sorted the students into groups largely based on the students’ rankings but also with consideration for the small-group dynamics, an easily-overlooked point that was very important."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Project options presented to students before selection; instructors with enough knowledge of students to judge group dynamics; sorting done without openly exposing the dynamics reasoning.
#### Constraints
- The earlier lottery-style process forced instructors either to abide by student choices or make obvious on-the-fly interventions, which the authors regarded as potentially regrettable.

### Target Learners
- high-school students working in small research groups

### Target Learning Goals
- effective collaboration on small-group research projects
