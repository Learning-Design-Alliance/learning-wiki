# Pair robot-system training with systematic team training including assistants and nurses when introducing robotic surgery

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that robotic surgery shifts the efficiency bottleneck from individual surgeon skill toward team coordination, so "the promotion of RA-TKA should not only be the technical training of the surgeon but also the systematic team training and integration including assistants and nurses". It cites evidence that assistant experience can extend average robot surgery time, and used a fixed team of 3 orthopedic physicians and 2 senior specialist nurses to control for assistant experience. Only when the whole team collectively surmounts the learning curve can precision and efficiency benefits be maximally released, per the authors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A fixed, trained surgical team (surgeon plus skilled assistants and nurses) supporting the robotic workflow
#### Constraints
- Whether prolonged RA-TKA incisions can be avoided through team collaboration remains unexplored, per the article

### Target Learners
- surgical teams adopting robotic surgery: surgeons, assistants, specialist nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- team coordination and proficiency in robot-assisted surgical workflows
