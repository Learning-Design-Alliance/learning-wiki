Incorporate media literacy measures and explicit nature-of-science instruction in climate change media studies¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that future studies of climate change media incorporate an overall measure of media literacy, per Cooper (2011), to explore its role in student knowledge and attitudes, including whether interventions differentially affect media literacy for students of different worldviews given the backfire effect. They also suggest that "explicit instruction on the nature of science, specifically the role of evidence in supporting ideas, may be a helpful approach for maximizing the effectiveness of evidence-based instruction."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Future studies should incorporate an overall measure of media literacy in order to explore the role it may play in student knowledge and attitudes

Constraints¶

  • The article did not itself measure media literacy or test nature-of-science instruction; these are recommendations for future work

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate mixed-majors introductory biology students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • climate change knowledge and attitudes
  • media literacy
  • nature of science conceptions

Claims¶

  • Comedic and authoritative scientific videos produced no significant difference in students' climate change knowledge or attitudes [~W]
  • Students perceived authoritative IPCC videos as more likely to influence voting than comedic videos [+W]
  • Self-reported understanding of climate change correlates only weakly with measured climate science knowledge [+W]
