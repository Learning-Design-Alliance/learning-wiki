# Coaching strategies to buffer precompetitive motivation loss in youth soccer

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article urges coaches, physical trainers and sport professionals to sustain young players' motivation through strategies and teaching methods that give players decision-making and prominence, create training situations similar to competition, use socializing teaching styles fostering peer help and team feeling, and attend to physical conditioning and diet. It states these measures aim "to reduce or alleviate the negative effects that the competition can have in soccer players of these ages".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coaches must design tasks with clear guidelines and goals, individualized teaching styles, and feedback where process prevails over result, as stated in the introduction.
#### Constraints
- The recommendations target U16 developmental-age players in categories the authors describe as more vulnerable to the negative effects of competition.

### Target Learners
- Under-16 developmental-age soccer players

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustain intrinsic motivation for sport participation
- Cope with precompetitive stress and reduce demotivation
