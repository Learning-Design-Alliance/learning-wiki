# Use structured reflective practice and faculty development programs to overcome the perception that emotional and socioemotional abilities are innate rather than teachable

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The study recommends "structured reflective practice or faculty development programs" to overcome the perception that socioemotional abilities "are innate rather than teachable." The authors state such approaches "would reinforce not only student competencies but also faculty capacity to model and guide socioemotional learning," citing Latin American frameworks from Chile advocating faculty development incorporating empathy and socioemotional teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty development frameworks that incorporate empathy and socioemotional teaching as core elements
#### Constraints
- Without consistent assessment and structured feedback, the impact of empathy initiatives may remain limited

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students and faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- Empathy, emotional awareness, and management of interpersonal relationships
