# Evaluate public engagement lectures with repeated pre/post/follow-up knowledge surveys of the same audience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors demonstrate an evaluation technique for one-off public engagement events: administer matched knowledge surveys to the same audience one week before, one week after and six weeks after a lecture, with varied distractor questions to prevent simple remembering of prior responses, and analyse binary pass/fail responses with non-parametric repeated-measures tests. They state "our study satisfies this recommendation by demonstrating an achievable evaluation technique that is statistically significant".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the audience at multiple time points via schools or comparable channels
- Surveys validated for age-appropriateness by science engagement professionals
- Distractor questions differing between surveys to mediate against responders remembering prior responses
#### Constraints
- Longitudinal evaluation suffers declining participation with time (only around 50% of children provided all three data sets), with major cost and resource implications

### Target Learners
- schoolchildren attending public science lectures

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of science content delivered in a public lecture
