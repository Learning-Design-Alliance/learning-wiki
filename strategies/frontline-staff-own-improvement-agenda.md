# Engage frontline staff in choosing their own improvement agenda within a coached lean programme

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
After brief expert training, frontline theatre staff were encouraged to develop their own agenda for change and select improvement projects, with iterative PDCA testing of new working systems under expert supervision. The authors report that "active engagement of the staff in selecting the programme of work was considered an important part of the intervention" and believe staff-led change yields more sustainable gains than work performed entirely by external consultants, while acknowledging the tension between guidance and direction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit support from management to overcome staff learned helplessness and enable implementation of staff ideas
- External coaching and support available to frontline project groups during the intervention period
#### Constraints
- Staff may not always choose projects that seem obviously beneficial to safety and reliability; balancing guidance against loss of engagement is difficult
- The level of external support used in the study could not be provided hospital-wide, so scaling up without losing effectiveness requires further work

### Target Learners
- frontline healthcare staff in operating theatre teams

### Target Learning Goals
- continuous improvement capability
- patient safety problem identification and problem solving

### Affordances
- 3D Model Safety Culture System Technology
