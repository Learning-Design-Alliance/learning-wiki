Revise multimedia learning theories by accounting for functionally specialized components, correcting multicomponent-model misinterpretations, and incorporating central executive research¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (3 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Rather than switching from a multicomponent to an embedded-processes view, the commentary recommends a revision/extension of multimedia theories that must "take into account functionally specialized components", "correct misinterpretations regarding the multicomponent model", and "incorporate findings from research on central executive functions". It argues central executive involvement is certain given the large amount of information in multimedia materials, and that findings on updating and task switching may prove fruitful for rethinking processing assumptions such as selection, organization, and integration in CTML, potentially leading to new instructional principles.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Multimedia theories must integrate both central executive (attentional) and functionally specialized (e.g., phonological and visuospatial) mechanisms, per the commentary's third argument

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is framed as needed revision of existing theories (CLT, CTML), not as a tested instructional manipulation

Target Learners¶

  • multimedia learning researchers and instructional designers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • designing multimedia materials consistent with working memory architecture

  • Embedded Processes Model Central Attentional Mechanism

Claims¶

  • Small between-task relative to within-task interference in dual-task studies supports functional separation of phonological and visuospatial working memory, and newer experiments led Cowan's group to add specialized verbal and visual resources to the embedded-processes account [+W]
  • Schweppe and Rummer's rejection of the multicomponent model as a basis for multimedia theories rests on a misinterpretation of the working memory–long-term memory relationship [+W]
  • Whether working memory has a single capacity limit or multiple capacity limits from functional specialization is highly relevant for the design of multimedia materials [+W]
  • Multimedia learning principles are not clearly attributable to the multicomponent model or to applied multimedia theories [~W]
  • Domain-specific interference, attributed by the embedded-processes model to similarity-based interference, underpins multimedia learning's modality benefits [+W]
  • The central executive coordinates WM subsystems and supports higher-level language-learning functions such as attention switching and strategy switching [+W]
