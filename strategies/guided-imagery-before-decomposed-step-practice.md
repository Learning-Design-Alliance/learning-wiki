# Use brief instructor-guided relaxation and visualization imagery sessions immediately before practicing decomposed surgical skill steps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementable teaching activity: before practicing each decomposed action and before continuous whole-task practice, trainees undergo 2 minutes of relaxation and imagery training in which they visualize correctly performing each step, guided by instructor cues. The article describes imagery training as mental training in which students "repeatedly simulate the skill operation process in their minds, thereby deepening their memory and understanding of the skills." In the trial this totaled 12 to 17.4 min per session across three sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instructor to guide the imagery cues; a quiet setting for relaxation and light eye closure; trainees willing to engage with imagery training
#### Constraints
- The article states imagery training was conducted at low frequency (three sessions) and that the link between participation and skill improvement is worth further verification in subsequent studies

### Target Learners
- surgical resident physicians learning laparoscopic psychomotor skills

### Target Learning Goals
- mental rehearsal of surgical procedures, concentration, and improved motor skill performance
