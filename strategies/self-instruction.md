# Self-Instruction

## Description
Self-instruction, also known as self-talk or self-statements, is a strategy where learners talk themselves through a task or activity to self-regulate their behavior. It involves using self-induced statements to direct or control actions, such as defining the problem, focusing attention, explaining strategies, self-evaluating, coping with difficulties, and self-reinforcing accomplishments. The goal is to progress from overt self-statements to covert, internalized speech.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The learner must be capable of performing the designated academic or behavioral skill. Self-statements should be developmentally appropriate and meaningful to the learner.
#### Constraints
- Self-instruction alone is useless if the learner cannot perform the designated academic or behavioral skill. Self-statements must be meaningful to the learner for successful implementation.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learner levels, particularly useful for learners with learning difficulties or those needing support with task initiation and completion

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance self-regulation, improve task focus, promote error detection and correction, develop coping strategies, reinforce positive behavior

### Affordances
- Provides an element of control over learning, efficient use of teacher's time, minimal maintenance once developed, promotes metacognitive awareness

### Personalization
- Self-statements can be tailored to individual needs and learning styles. Learners can choose from different types of self-instruction (problem definition, focusing attention, strategy, self-evaluation, coping, self-reinforcement) based on the task or situation.

- Observe the learner's ability to articulate and use self-statements during tasks. Monitor improvements in task completion, accuracy, and self-regulation.

## Impact
- Self-instruction interventions have been shown to improve self-regulation, task completion, and accuracy, especially among learners with learning difficulties.
