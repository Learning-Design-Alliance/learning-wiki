# Design CPD courses to target moral norm, beliefs about capabilities and beliefs about consequences to increase adoption of targeted behaviors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's Lessons for Practice recommend that CPD providers increase intention by targeting three modifiable psychosocial factors. For moral norm, courses "could raise participants' awareness of the needs of others (patients) using examples and role-play exercises"; for beliefs about capabilities, facilitators could check participants feel equipped, "promote feedback and provide reinforcing practical exercises"; for beliefs about consequences, courses "could provide information about the benefits of the behavior and personalized information about the consequences". The authors also suggest bringing the patient voice into courses and closing the intention-behavior gap with audit and feedback, if-then plans, or commitment-to-change statements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course design must define a main behavior change target and measure intention with a validated tool such as CPD-REACTION
#### Constraints
- The article notes there is no clear definition of when an intention score is high enough to result in behavior adoption; direct measures of practice change are more robust but difficult in pragmatic studies

### Target Learners
- physician specialists in CPD courses
- CPD providers and course designers

### Target Learning Goals
- increasing intention to adopt targeted clinical behaviors and closing the intention-behavior gap

### Affordances
- Godin Integrated Behavior Change Framework
