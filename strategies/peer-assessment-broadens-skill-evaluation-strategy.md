# Use peer assessment of individual procedures to broaden skill evaluation beyond randomly selected examinations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article suggests adapting the RPP model to the assessment phase, where peers evaluate each procedure individually rather than relying on examinations that assess one randomly selected skill. The authors note that random selection "hinders a comprehensive evaluation of students’ actual proficiency in other non-assessed skills and fails to identify their specific shortcomings across the full spectrum of nursing competencies." Peer evaluation of each procedure covers a broader range and identifies specific issues through detailed observation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standardized scoring checklist with criteria for each procedural step
- Trained peers able to observe and rate performance
#### Constraints
- Proposed as an extension of the studied model; the article did not directly test peer assessment as a replacement for formal examinations

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- comprehensive evaluation of operational skill proficiency across multiple procedures
