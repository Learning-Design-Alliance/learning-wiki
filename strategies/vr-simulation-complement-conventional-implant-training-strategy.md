# Use VR simulation as a complement to conventional theoretical training for dental implant surgical skill development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (7 for, 2 against) · 7 studies (3 review, 2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 7 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates that adding a VR dental implant training system to conventional theoretical knowledge training and clinical demonstration produces measurably better outcomes than conventional training alone. The VR group practiced simulated implant operations on virtual mandibular models across three arch regions, receiving immediate collision feedback and objective deviation measurement, while the control group received only lectures and observation of clinical procedures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to VR hardware including helmet, handle, and computing resources
- A validated 3D virtual surgical environment with collision detection
#### Constraints
- Some physicians reported dizziness after wearing the helmet for extended periods
- The cost of setup and maintenance has not been evaluated
- No evaluation of VR-based technology effectiveness across varied settings has been conducted

### Target Learners
- young physicians
- medical students
- graduate students in dental training

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical skill acquisition
- implant placement precision
- anatomical structure mastery
