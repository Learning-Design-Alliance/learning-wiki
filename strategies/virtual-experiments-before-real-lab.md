# Use computer-based simulated experiments before real-time laboratory experiments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the electrical engineering technology module, students build a circuit at a computer screen and run a simulation program that includes measuring instruments, reading values from voltmeter and ammeter displays. The authors state that "such virtual experiments enhance students learning: when they turn later to the real-time experiment, they feel more comfortable working with tangible equipment since they have already an idea how this circuit works." Students also learn MultiSim, a simulation tool typical of industrial environments. Similarly, in computer systems, simulation tools like RouterSim and VMware let students test possible solutions before the real laboratory, though they do not substitute for physical lab exercises.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation software such as MultiSim, RouterSim, or VMware
- A subsequent real-time laboratory experiment in which the simulated concepts are confirmed
#### Constraints
- The article states that simulation tools do not substitute the practical exercises in the physical laboratory

### Target Learners
- Electrical engineering technology and computer systems students in early major courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Confirming theoretical concepts such as Ohm's law through measurement and developing industry-relevant simulation skills
