# Advance clinical reasoning learning in VPs by providing large VP pools with selectable complexity, adaptive context and time pressure, and flexible feedback and activity recommendation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the framework's Context and Learner-centeredness categories, the authors recommend providing an "individually increasingly challenging context of the virtual clinical encounter", for example by adding irrelevant information or emotionally complex situations matched to skill level, while balancing authenticity and cognitive load. They propose using time on task to adapt VP complexity, such as limiting available time for advanced learners or challenging learners to solve multiple VPs simultaneously, and a flexible environment where learners choose level and type of feedback, and a VP system recommends next activities based on performance, self-direction and self-defined learning goals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adaptable VP content and computational models integrated in virtual patients; continuous, timely, and specific feedback provided by the VP system, an instructor, or summative peer or expert responses
#### Constraints
- The authors caution it is important "to balance authenticity and cognitive load to not overburden the learners"

### Target Learners
- Medical and healthcare students at varying skill levels

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning, including diagnostic and management decision-making
