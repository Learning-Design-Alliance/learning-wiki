# Foster students' metacognition as a long-term learning tool from the first years of schooling

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that early metacognitive efficiency predicted performance improvement across domains 10 months later, the authors conclude that metacognition "may play a fundamental role in guiding students' learning across domains" and highlight "the importance of creating educational programs fostering students' metacognition as a long-term learning tool". The recommendation applies from the first year of formal reading instruction, when metacognition appears partly dissociated from reading-specific linguistic ability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should target procedural metacognition (online confidence monitoring) rather than only self-report metacognitive awareness
#### Constraints
- The study measured metacognitive efficiency in experimental tasks, not the effect of any implemented metacognition-training program; the recommendation is inferred from predictive longitudinal correlations

### Target Learners
- early primary school students (Grade 1-2)

### Target Learning Goals
- domain-general learning improvement
- reading-related skill development
