Programming clubs should target upsolving discipline and peer network density through structured post-contest review and mentor-buddy systems¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that university programming clubs focus retention efforts on two behavioral dimensions: upsolving discipline and peer network density. Rather than simply running more contests or providing study material, the authors suggest "structured post-contest review sessions, mentor-buddy systems pairing experienced and newer participants, and monitoring of students who show irregular engagement following extended breaks." This follows directly from their finding that practice habits and social environment outweigh academic grades in predicting attrition.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Club infrastructure to run post-contest review sessions and pair experienced with newer participants

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on observational, non-causal associations from a small, geographically limited survey

Target Learners¶

  • University students participating in competitive programming clubs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Sustained engagement and independent problem-solving practice in competitive programming

Claims¶

  • Engineered practice-balance features outperform raw activity counts: the Intensity Ratio ranks third among Codeforces predictors, and upsolving habit dominates the survey model [+W]
  • The Skill-Application Paradox: stopped competitive programmers self-report higher theoretical confidence than active peers yet show significantly weaker practice habits and peer support [+W]
  • True attrition in competitive programming is preceded by an 83.71% reduction in monthly contest participation [+W]

  • Provide targeted intensive supports for educators at elevated risk, including check-ins, buddy systems, and fast-access mental health services
