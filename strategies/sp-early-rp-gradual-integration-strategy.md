# Use simulated patients in the early study period and integrate real patients gradually as communication skills develop

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends sequencing patient encounter types across the curriculum: simulated patients first, real patients later. It argues that "Using SPs in the early study period could create a safe space to attune the students to patient encounters and to lower their initial existing inhibitions", after which real patients should be integrated as skills advance. This follows from the finding that students facing real (or believed-real) patients received lower empathy ratings, likely due to inhibitions and lack of routine.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires conscious planning of SP and RP occupation within the curriculum blueprint for communication and empathy training
#### Constraints

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in the early study period

### Target Learning Goals
- empathetic behaviour in patient encounters
- communication skills such as history taking and informed consent discussions
