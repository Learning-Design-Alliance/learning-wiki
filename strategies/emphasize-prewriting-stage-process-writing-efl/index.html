Emphasize the pre-writing stage and teach writing as a process in EFL composition¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the study's findings, the researcher recommends that English teachers "Place more emphasis on teaching writing as a process not only as a product" and "Place more emphasis on the pre- writing stage due to its vital role in the writing process." This follows the reviewed literature (Khwaileh, 1991) noting that students given no opportunity to generate, record and refine ideas face great difficulty writing comprehensive, logical sentences. The story-mapping technique is offered as one such pre-writing support.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers allocate composition-class time to pre-writing activities such as story mapping before drafting

Constraints¶

  • Recommended in the Jordanian tenth-grade EFL context; the study tested story mapping, not process instruction generally

Target Learners¶

  • tenth grade EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • short story writing and creative writing skills

Claims¶

  • Story-mapping instruction produced higher short story writing post-test scores than traditional instruction in tenth-grade EFL students [+W]
  • Experimental-group students improved significantly from pre-test to post-test in short story writing after story-mapping instruction [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Process Writing
  • Train and model the collaborative feedback process before students notice gaps (pre-noticing stage)
