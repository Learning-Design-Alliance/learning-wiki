# Institutions serving Native American students should offer culturally responsive wellness initiatives promoting restorative sleep and meaningful movement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that institutions enrolling Native American students develop wellness initiatives that pair health behavior promotion with cultural identity and community connection. It suggests partnering "with tribal leaders or cultural organizations to offer activities such as traditional dance programs, land-based physical activities, or culturally rooted wellness workshops", alongside campus-wide sleep health campaigns, sleep hygiene workshops, flexible course scheduling, and designated wellness spaces.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional capacity to partner with tribal leaders or cultural organizations and to run campus-wide sleep and PA programming
#### Constraints
- The article frames these as recommendations based on cross-sectional, self-report associations; longitudinal and community-engaged research is needed to clarify pathways

### Target Learners
- Native American / Indigenous college students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Psychological well-being
- Academic performance and retention
