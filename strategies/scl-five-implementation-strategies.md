# Combine abbreviated high-intensity training with system-level implementation strategies (readiness assessments, serial knowledge assessment, bidirectional feedback, local instructor development)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (1 for, 4 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The SCL programme pairs its 2-day aHIT course with four supporting implementation strategies: bimonthly facility readiness assessments with immediate reporting to on-duty leadership; longitudinal provider knowledge assessment at baseline, post-course, and 1, 3 and 6 months; bidirectional active feedback between front-line providers and health system leadership reported biannually; and development and maintenance of a clinically relevant local instructor core through instructor training and monitored courses. The article states "The SCL programme employs five major implementation strategies" and describes each in turn.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An implementation team to conduct facility readiness assessments and report to district and ministry leadership
- Instructor candidates approved by the DHMT and monitored with structured feedback by senior SCL faculty
#### Constraints
- The article notes it is possible that similar knowledge results could have been obtained just from inventory support, independent of the training component

### Target Learners
- doctors and nurses providing initial care to seriously ill children in district health systems

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained provider knowledge of recognition and treatment of serious childhood illness
