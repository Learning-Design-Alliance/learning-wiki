# Evaluate AI-in-LMS deployments with cluster-randomized or stepped-wedge rollout linked to multi-year institutional outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's evaluation framework connects middle-school learning traces to medium- and long-term outcomes. Where feasible it uses cluster randomization at classroom or school level; otherwise 'the study uses a stepped-wedge design in which all participating schools eventually receive the AI-integrated LMS, but adoption is staggered across multiple waves'. Outcomes are organized in a time-horizon structure (short-term mastery and engagement, medium-term high-school trajectories, long-term postsecondary transitions), with fidelity measured from system logs and hierarchical/growth models for clustered longitudinal data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation fidelity measurement from system logs (usage, completion, dashboard interaction)
- Stable, privacy-preserving identifiers for multi-year linkage
- Attrition handling such as inverse probability weighting and multiple imputation
#### Constraints
- Long-term postsecondary outcomes depend on data-sharing agreements; contamination, novelty effects, implementation variability, and measurement drift are anticipated threats requiring mitigation

### Target Learners
- Middle school student cohorts followed through high school and beyond

### Target Learning Goals
- Durable academic gains persisting across educational transitions
