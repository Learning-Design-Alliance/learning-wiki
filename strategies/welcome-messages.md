# Welcome Messages

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A welcome message is the instructor's opening communication in a course — delivered as text, video, or audio — that greets learners, introduces the instructor and course purpose, previews how the course works, and invites initial engagement. In online and blended settings it is often the learner's first experience of the instructor's presence, and it functions simultaneously as orientation, social-emotional signaling, and norm-setting.

## Design Implications

Welcome messages are a low-cost intervention for establishing [social presence](../elements/belonging.md) and instructor immediacy, which correlate with satisfaction and perceived learning in online courses [~M]. They also serve an orientation function: previewing course structure and expectations reduces ambiguity that would otherwise consume working memory during the first weeks [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+M]. Messages that affirm that struggle is normal and that all students belong can buffer early attrition, particularly for at-risk groups [Brief belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A warm, personal tone — use of the instructor's name, photo, or face; second-person address to the learner
- Concrete orientation content: how to start, where to find things, what to do first, communication norms
- An explicit invitation to a first low-stakes action (post an introduction, complete a syllabus quiz, reply to the message)
- Timeliness: delivered before or on day one, when uncertainty is highest

#### Constraints
- Generic, template-like messages can actively undermine belonging by signaling impersonality [~W] — learners detect boilerplate
- Overly long messages bury the orientation content; the belonging signal and the logistics should be scannable and separate
- A warm welcome followed by an unresponsive instructor damages credibility more than no warmth at all [~M] — the message sets expectations the instructor must then meet
- Effectiveness fades quickly; a single message does not sustain presence without ongoing [check-ins](../elements/check-in.md)

#### Implementation Variability
- **Video welcome** — highest social-presence bandwidth; shows face, voice, and enthusiasm
- **Text announcement** — most scannable; best for logistics and "start here" checklists
- **Personalized individual messages** — highest belonging impact but costly at scale; feasible in small or cohort-based courses
- **Course-entry embedded message** — appears on the LMS landing page so it cannot be missed

### Target Learners
- First-time online learners, who lack the tacit knowledge of how an online course operates [~M]
- First-generation and underrepresented students, for whom belonging uncertainty is elevated at course start [Brief belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Large-enrollment course students who would otherwise experience the instructor as absent
- Less critical for small seminars where personal contact occurs naturally in the first session

### Target Learning Goals
- Affective: reducing anxiety, establishing belonging and motivation to persist
- Metacognitive/orientational: knowing how to navigate and what is expected
- Community formation: setting norms for discussion and interaction

### Instructions
1. Draft a personal greeting that names the instructor, states enthusiasm for the specific course content, and addresses learners in the second person.
2. Add a short "how this course works" section — first action, weekly rhythm, where to get help — consistent with [Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md) in the syllabus.
3. Include one belonging-affirming sentence acknowledging that the course is challenging and that difficulty is expected and surmountable.
4. End with a concrete, low-stakes first task and an invitation to reply or ask questions.
5. Follow up within the first week with individual or small-group [check-ins](../elements/check-in.md) to sustain the presence the message initiated.

## Related Strategies
- [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) — the recurring counterpart; welcome messages open the relationship, check-ins maintain it
- [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) — the orientation content of a welcome message functions as a course-level organizer
- [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md) — welcome messages are a natural place to invite students to share access needs early

## Examples
- **Penn State World Campus** — faculty guidance recommends a welcome letter plus a short video introduction posted before the course opens, with a "start here" module linked from the message.
- **First-day video message in an LMS announcement** — a 2–3 minute instructor video on the course landing page, paired with a text checklist of first-week tasks.
- **Belonging-affirming syllabus preambles** — courses that open with a message framing difficulty as normal and support as available, adapted from social-belonging intervention materials (e.g., the College Transition Collaborative toolkits).
