# Use repeated practice sessions with structured feedback to develop preclinical rest seat preparation skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study's design—three preparation sessions at fixed one-week intervals with feedback each session—produced significant score gains in both groups, leading the authors to conclude that "both digital and conventional training delivered over multiple sessions may support the development of preclinical skills in occlusal rest seat preparation." Each 45-minute session used standardized instruments (handpieces, burs, explorer, probe, KaVo typodont) on a phantom head, ensuring consistency across students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students complete a didactic course and instructor demonstration before practical sessions; standardized instruments and assessment rubrics are needed across sessions
#### Constraints
- The number of sessions required to achieve optimal and educationally meaningful outcomes remains unclear and warrants further investigation, per the authors

### Target Learners
- fourth-year dental students in preclinical prosthodontics

### Target Learning Goals
- psychomotor skill development in occlusal rest seat preparation
