# Using collective or shared learning to resolve unknown concepts during CLIL lessons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
When students encountered unknown words such as "contribution" and "subsequence", the teacher and all students jointly built definitions: one student offered a Dutch translation, the teacher checked an online dictionary, and everyone added and edited meanings until all understood in both languages. The author calls this "shared or collective learning" and "one of the most efficient ways to learn new concepts", also describing it as an example of scaffolding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher must allow time to stop at each unknown concept and involve the whole group in explaining it
