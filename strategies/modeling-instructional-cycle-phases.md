# Modeling instructional cycle: introduction and representation, coordination, application, abstraction and generalization, and incremental development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementable instructional sequence in which students first learn representational tools through phenomenological inquiry labs, coordinate multiple representations, apply the tools to specific situations, then abstract situation-specific models into a single general model through whiteboard discussions, followed by continued incremental development in new contexts. Table II lays out these phases for the constant acceleration model, from "Experimentation involving students moving with constant acceleration in front of motion detectors" to guided discussion that generalizes the model. The article notes generalization "groups representations to reduce the cognitive load on the student by allowing recall of a single general model rather than an array of distinct situation specific models."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Inquiry-based lab activities with representational tools; whiteboard board meetings; instructor-led discussion to abstract general characteristics; studio-format class time
#### Constraints
- The article notes unification into a single model "does not happen with one discussion, but instead incrementally over the course of a semester and requires maintenance on the part of the instructor"

### Target Learners
- university introductory physics students in studio-format courses

### Target Learning Goals
- model construction and generalization
- coordinating multiple representations
