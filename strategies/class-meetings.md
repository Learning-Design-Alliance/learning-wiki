# Class Meetings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A class meeting is a recurring, structured whole-group gathering — typically daily or weekly — in which students and teacher greet one another, share personal or academic news, discuss problems or upcoming work, and make collective decisions. Originating in the Responsive Classroom approach and related to Glasser's class meetings and Deweyan democratic practice, the format positions students as contributors to classroom governance rather than passive recipients of management.

## Design Implications

Class meetings operationalize social-emotional learning and community-building: they create a predictable forum where belonging, voice, and shared norms are rehearsed rather than merely stated. A sense of belonging to the school community is strongly associated with motivation, engagement, and prosocial behavior [Students' sense of school belonging predicts engagement and motivation.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M], and giving students authentic voice in classroom decisions supports autonomy and intrinsic motivation [Autonomy-supportive teaching increases intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]. The meeting's value depends on genuine student participation — a teacher-dominated meeting becomes a lecture with extra steps.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A predictable time, place, and ritual structure (greeting → sharing → activity → news/announcements or problem-solving)
- A circle or equivalent arrangement where all students can see one another
- Agreed norms for speaking and listening, rehearsed through [Active Listening](active-listening.md) routines
- A teacher stance of facilitator, not chairperson — students generate topics and solutions
- Follow-through: decisions made in meetings must visibly shape classroom practice, or student voice becomes tokenism

#### Constraints
- Meetings dominated by the teacher or by a few vocal students reduce the belonging and autonomy benefits for everyone else [~M]
- Without confidentiality norms and skilled facilitation, sharing segments can expose students to social risk or embarrassment
- Time cost is real: 15–25 minutes daily competes with instructional time; benefits depend on the meeting actually improving climate and reducing lost instructional time elsewhere [~W]
- In very large classes or fully online asynchronous settings, whole-group meeting formats lose the face-to-face reciprocity that drives their effects [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Responsive Classroom Morning Meeting** — four fixed components (greeting, sharing, group activity, news and announcements), daily in elementary grades
- **Glasser-style problem-solving meeting** — open agenda focused on resolving classroom conflicts and improving the social environment
- **Weekly academic meeting** — secondary adaptation: reviewing the week's work, setting goals, and surfacing misunderstandings, closer to a structured [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)
- **Restorative circles** — meeting format used to repair harm after conflict, with talking-piece protocols

### Target Learners
- Elementary and middle-grades students, where the evidence base is strongest [~M]
- Students at risk of disengagement or weak school belonging, for whom a visible role in the community matters most [Students' sense of school belonging predicts engagement and motivation.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Classrooms with persistent management problems, where collective norm-setting can reduce teacher-imposed discipline

### Target Learning Goals
- Social-emotional competencies: self-management, social awareness, relationship skills
- Classroom community and belonging
- Civic and democratic participation: practicing deliberation and collective decision-making
- Academic self-regulation: goal-setting and reflection when meetings include work review

### Instructions
1. **Open with a greeting ritual** — every student is greeted by name; this daily acknowledgment is the belonging mechanism in miniature.
2. **Run a sharing segment** — a few students share news or work; peers respond with questions or comments using [Active Listening](active-listening.md) norms.
3. **Include a brief group activity** — a game, song, or [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) exercise that builds cohesion and gives the meeting an energizing beat.
4. **Address news, problems, or decisions** — the teacher or students raise issues; the group deliberates and agrees on actions. Use [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) formats to surface concerns early.
5. **Close with a look ahead** — preview the day or week, connecting the meeting to academic work so the meeting is not experienced as separate from learning.

## Examples
- **[Responsive Classroom](https://www.responsiveclassroom.org)** Morning Meeting — the most widely documented implementation, used in thousands of US elementary schools and studied in randomized and quasi-experimental trials.
- **Restorative practice circles** in Denver Public Schools — class meetings adapted for community-building and conflict repair district-wide.
- **Weekly team meetings in EL Education (Expeditionary Learning) crews** — secondary adaptation where a small stable group meets for community, goal-setting, and academic reflection.
