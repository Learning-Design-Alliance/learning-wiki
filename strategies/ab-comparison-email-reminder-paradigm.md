# Deploy randomized A/B comparisons of email reminders through the LMS as a low-cost embedded experimentation paradigm

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates randomized A/B comparisons of email reminder messages as a form of embedded experimentation that instructors can run in everyday communication channels. Reminder variants were randomized weekly through the learning management system, and outcomes were measured from time-stamped homework data. The authors call this "a low-cost and effective strategy to test multiple messages alternatives and find the one that works better for students, without the use of external tools or resources."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a learning management system that can deliver messages to students with email notifications, and an online homework platform that logs time-stamped submissions for measuring start time and attempt rate outcomes.
#### Constraints
- The article states the team could not directly measure student engagement with the emails at the time of sending, such as if and when students opened the emails, which limits accurate inference between design decisions and student action.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in an introductory computer science (CS1) course

### Target Learning Goals
- Starting graded weekly homework earlier
- Increasing the rate at which students attempt homework
