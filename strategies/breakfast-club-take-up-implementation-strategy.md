# Promote breakfast club take-up through parent communication, universal encouragement, and a well-supported delivery team

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q4` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report identifies what made breakfast clubs work in practice: "Key factors for successful implementation of the breakfast clubs were: communication with parents to encourage take-up, an established school breakfast routine, and a well-functioning delivery team supported by the wider school." Activities thought to increase take-up included "promoting it to parents and encouraging all children to attend while sensitively targeting pupils most likely to benefit". Barriers included "earlier start times for pupils, breakfast charges, and a lack of ongoing promotion from the school".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools must compensate staff for additional hours of work and balance the supply of, and demand for, food, which the report names as the main challenges.
#### Constraints
- In some schools, barriers to take-up included earlier start times for pupils, breakfast charges, and a lack of ongoing promotion from the school.

### Target Learners
- Pupils in disadvantaged primary schools, and their parents

### Target Learning Goals
- increased breakfast take-up and reduced pupil hunger, supporting readiness to learn
