# Introduce new endoscopic techniques through structured national training programmes with competency-based assessment, accreditation and error/near-miss analysis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that introduction of new endoscopic techniques be managed through structured national training rather than unsupervised early practice, because "Gaining experience at the expense of patient safety is unethical". It specifies "Structured training programmes with competency-based assessment and accreditation process are required at a national level", and proposes error and near-miss event analysis as a more sensitive monitoring approach than mortality audit, citing the National Training Programme for Laparoscopic Colorectal Surgery as a model where trainee outcomes matched expert outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Competency-based assessment with formal trainer sign-off of trainee procedures
- National-level accreditation of procedural volume
- Monitoring of technical performance via error and near-miss analysis rather than audit alone
#### Constraints
- Mortality audit alone detects adverse events only after patient harm has occurred, per the article's critique

### Target Learners
- Trainee endoscopists learning new therapeutic endoscopic techniques

### Target Learning Goals
- Achieving procedural proficiency in endoscopic mucosal resection without compromising patient safety
