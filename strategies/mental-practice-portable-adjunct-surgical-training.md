# Offer mental practice as a portable, accessible, inexpensive and safe adjunct to simulation and cadaveric dissection in surgical training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend mental practice as a concurrent surgical education tool: "Mental practice is a means of surgical training that is portable, accessible, inexpensive and safe." It is designed to be easily incorporated into a surgeon's daily routine, unlike protocols requiring a psychologist-guided 30-min relaxation and imagery session. The article recommends determining to whom, when and how often this form of learning should ideally be applied, and offers support for further investigation and refinement of the technique.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated, expert-derived mental practice script with visual, cognitive and kinesthetic cues
- A brief relaxation period and introduction to mental practice before the script is read
#### Constraints
- This trial did not demonstrate a statistically significant benefit over textbook study, possibly due to sample size; the authors state it is unlikely to replace other adjunctive modalities for surgical education

### Target Learners
- Senior surgical residents (PGY-5 OHNS residents)

### Target Learning Goals
- Surgical skill acquisition and competency in mastoidectomy
