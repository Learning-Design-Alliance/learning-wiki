# Blended approach: video demonstration for large-group or presession instruction followed by standardized patient teaching and assessment for skill application

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a sequential blended model in which "high-quality VD can be used for large-group instruction or as presession preparatory material, following which students are taught and assessed on SPs for skill application and consolidation." This harnesses the scalability and consistency of video alongside the experiential depth and interactivity of SP simulation, saving time and resources compared with either method in isolation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained, motivated standardized patients (the article trained 4 ward attendants for 2 weeks with a scenario-based script and mock dry run)
- Peer-validated, good-quality video demonstrations of the skills
#### Constraints
- SP training and development is time-, finance-, and human resource-intensive

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in orthopedics/trauma postings

### Target Learning Goals
- Psychomotor skills for spinal injury management in primary/prehospital settings, including improvised cervical collar application and log rolling
