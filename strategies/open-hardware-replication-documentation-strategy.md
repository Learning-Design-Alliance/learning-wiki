# Provide online parts lists, build instructions, and design documentation so that other groups can replicate the exhibit.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors provide an accompanying website, www.sr.bham.ac.uk/exhibit, that "provides parts lists and information for others to build their own version of this exhibit." The site lists "example parts and variants at other price-points", from lab-grade optics down to inexpensive craft mirrors and laser pointers suitable for classes of students, alongside instructions for use by others. The authors are also "investigating making low-cost interferometer kits with novelty elements such as building blocks" as a more affordable means for schools to build their own Michelson interferometers. This open documentation gives the project what the authors call "a lasting role on an international scale".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A maintained website hosting component lists and instructions for use by others
- Design documentation covering hardware, software, and health-and-safety considerations
#### Constraints
- The authors' own construction cost was £3600, so full lab-grade replication may be costly; lower price-point variants are listed for budget-constrained builders

### Target Learners
- school groups
- university departments running public engagement schemes
- informal science educators and museum developers

### Target Learning Goals
- enabling others to build and operate a Michelson interferometer for gravitational-wave outreach
- extending public engagement capacity beyond the original institution
