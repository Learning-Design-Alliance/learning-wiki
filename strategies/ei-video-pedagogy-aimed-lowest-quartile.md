# Aim supplementary video pedagogy at the lowest quartile of students, with slower pacing and more detailed intermediate steps than classroom instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because evening EI is disproportionately used by students with weaker academic backgrounds, the article recommends designing supplementary videos for the lowest quartile rather than the middle. The authors state: "Recognizing that EI services are disproportionately utilized by students with weaker academic backgrounds, the pedagogy of the EI videos is aimed at the lowest quartile of students." An example taking 5 minutes in class is slowed to 10 minutes, with more intentional development of a plan and intermediate steps, since stronger students can fast forward but weaker students cannot ask a video questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course directors provide syllabus links directly to close analogues of assigned homework problems so cadets are sure the resource pertains to the task
#### Constraints
- Cadets who need more detail cannot slow the video down enough to see things that are not on it, and cannot raise their hands and ask questions if the instructor skips a step

### Target Learners
- cadets with weaker math backgrounds and below-average academic composites seeking extra instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- modeled problem-solving methods for assigned homework-type problems
