# Prioritize competency-based training models integrating VR-based surgical practice and AI-assisted procedural assessments in dental education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators translate bibliometric insights on virtual reality and artificial intelligence into practice by prioritizing "competency-based training models that integrate VR-based surgical practice and AI-assisted procedural assessments". It cites supporting studies such as Huang et al, where repeated VR training significantly enhanced students' implantology skills, and simulator work showing students practicing with an inferior alveolar nerve block simulator were more confident during first clinical injections.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educator access to VR haptic simulators and AI-assisted assessment tools
- Alignment of AI algorithms with the pedagogical needs of dental students through collaboration between dental educators, AI developers and clinical practitioners
#### Constraints
- The article notes its bibliometric methods do not directly assess pedagogical effectiveness, and further empirical research is needed on how these technologies impact student learning outcomes and practical skill acquisition

### Target Learners
- dental and stomatology students in preclinical and surgical training

### Target Learning Goals
- manual dexterity and surgical skill acquisition
- diagnostic skills
- clinical confidence
