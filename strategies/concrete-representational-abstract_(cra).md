# Concrete-Representational-Abstract (CRA)

## Description
CRA is an instructional approach that uses concrete materials, visual representations, and abstract symbols to teach math concepts. Learners first manipulate concrete objects to understand a concept, then draw pictures representing the objects, and finally use abstract numbers and symbols. It aims to ensure a thorough understanding of math concepts/skills. As instructors move through the sequence of instruction, the abstract numbers and/or symbols should be used in conjunction with the concrete materials and representational drawings to promote association of abstract symbols with concrete and representational understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires concrete materials (e.g., chips, unifix cubes, base ten blocks), drawing materials, and explicit teaching methods. Instructors need to describe, model, and provide ample practice opportunities at each level of understanding.
#### Constraints
- Learners may face barriers to abstract understanding if they do not understand the concept behind the skill, have difficulty with basic facts/memory problems, or repeat procedural mistakes.

### Affordances
- Provides a graduated framework for learners to make meaningful connections, linking learning experiences from concrete-to-representational-to-abstract levels of understanding. By linking learning experiences from concrete-to-representational-to-abstract levels of understanding, the teacher provides a graduated framework for learners to make meaningful connections. Blends conceptual and procedural understanding in a structured way.

### Personalization
- Can be tailored to different math concepts and skills. The type of concrete objects, drawing techniques, and strategies for moving to the abstract level can be adapted to suit individual needs.
