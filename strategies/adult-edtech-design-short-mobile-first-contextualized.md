# Design adult learning products with short lessons, visualizations, mobile-first simplicity, and contextualized content integrating basic and job skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on interviews with entrepreneurs, investors, and providers, the guide recommends three product-feature priorities for adult learners. First, products should "take into account the unique needs of low-skilled learners": "Keep lessons short, use visualizations, provide plentiful resources, increase learner-instructor connections, and help learners engage with their peers." Second, products should support anytime, anywhere learning through simple, mobile-first solutions. Third, developers should create contextualized, integrated content and apps, since every segment needs solutions integrating soft skills, job skills, and language skills with basic skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tools must be easy to use without adding time to students' or teachers' days; the guide quotes a practitioner saying technology needs to be impossibly easy to use or it will never be used
#### Constraints
- The guide notes there is widespread consensus that products for adult learners should be mobile-first, simple to use, and contain high-quality, modular, and relevant content, but providers are struggling to find such solutions

### Target Learners
- low-skilled adult learners
- adult English language learners
- incarcerated adults

### Target Learning Goals
- basic reading and math skills
- English language acquisition
- digital literacy
- job readiness
