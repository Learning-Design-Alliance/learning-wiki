Structural, school-level approaches to address teachers' explicit immigration attitudes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends structural, school-level approaches over individual bias-awareness training alone, noting that "interventions that target only individual bias awareness rarely yield lasting change." Suggested approaches include providing structured opportunities for teachers to collectively reflect on their beliefs, learn about cultural differences, and adopt inclusive strategies; identifying influential teachers to lead school climate discussions; and implementing structural policies that limit opportunities for bias, such as anonymized testing or cross-grading of exams.

Claims¶

  • The achievement gap between native-born and first-generation immigrant students is smaller in schools with more inclusionary teacher immigration attitudes across all three subjects [+M]
  • The interaction between school diversity climate (aggregated teachers' immigration attitudes) and immigrant status significantly predicts performance in science, mathematics, and reading [+M]
  • First-generation immigrant students perform better in science, mathematics, and reading in schools where teachers hold more inclusionary immigration attitudes [+M]
  • The climate × immigrant status interaction is robust across alternative specifications, sampling weights, and multiple imputation [+M]
  • School diversity climate shows no significant effect on performance for non-immigrant or second-generation students [+M]
