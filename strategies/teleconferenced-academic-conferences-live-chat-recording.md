# Run academic conferences by teleconference with live-chat question submission and recorded sessions stored for later review

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends replacing in-person weekday academic conferences with video teleconferences held at the usual time, using commercial platforms such as GoToMeeting, Zoom, WebEx, or Skype. The format preserves interactivity because "the lecturer can see who is currently attending, see responses of the trainees, and ask questions of specific audience members." Conferences are recorded and stored on a cloud account for resident review, except those with protected information such as morbidity and mortality conference.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A platform with a live video feed and a live chat function for question submission, which the authors say decreases participant talk-over and allows a more fluid presentation
- An institutional paid account or free user access to the conferencing software
- Staff investment of time and effort to overcome faculty unfamiliarity with the technology
#### Constraints
- Some faculty struggle with novel technology and some participants have poor bandwidth connections
- Reviewing imaging is difficult; the authors' workaround is recording short videos while scrolling through cross-sectional imaging, avoiding patient identifiers
- Conferences with protected information, such as morbidity and mortality conference, cannot be recorded

### Target Learners
- surgical residents (PGY 1-5)
- surgery faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- medical knowledge
- maintaining academic conference participation during restrictions on gatherings
