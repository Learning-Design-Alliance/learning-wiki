# Use didactic challenge questions that demand explanation rather than regurgitation of theory or equations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes confronting learners with questions whose required skill is explanation, not recall of formulas. It states that nothing better shows deficient physics understanding than having would-be physicists go through 'a series of didactic challenges where the skill is not to regurgitate a theory or an equation, but to explain.' Example challenges include why only two electrons occupy the first atomic layer, by which interaction the Sun shines, why an inclining spinning top does not fall over, and explaining spinning on oneself, with answers that may look paradoxical to common-sense logic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions must target conceptual explanation rather than equation recall or numerical problem-solving
#### Constraints
- Some answers may look paradoxical if approached with common-sense logic, which the author says a physicist should not rely on

### Target Learners
- would-be physicists
- physics students at university level

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of physics
- ability to explain physical phenomena
