# Provide structured sandwich feedback immediately after DOPS assessment as an alternative to traditional apprenticeship feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that clinical instructors implement sandwich feedback following DOPS assessment in apprenticeship training. Based on the trial results, the authors state that "providing sandwich feedback following the DOPS assessment can be potentially valuable as an alternative method to traditional approaches for apprenticeship training of Iranian OR technology students", and suggest instructors "consider the FBL in a similar format in other schools". The recipe: observe performance with the DOPS checklist, deliver private positive-corrective-positive feedback with an action plan, document it, and repeat a week later.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors briefed on the program structure before implementation
- Enough training time allocated within the apprenticeship schedule
- A blinded or consistent rater and standardized DOPS conditions when used for evaluation
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a short-term, single-center trial with a small sample; the authors call for multi-center studies with more samples to assess generalizability and long-term impacts

### Target Learners
- undergraduate operating room technology students
- clinical apprenticeship trainees in perioperative fields

### Target Learning Goals
- perioperative competence
- perioperative procedural performance
