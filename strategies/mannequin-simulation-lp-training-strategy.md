# Integrate mannequin-based simulation into supervised lumbar puncture training during hospital rotations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that supervisors revise how lumbar puncture is taught during clinical rotations by adding simulation on medical dummies, as practiced in most developed countries. Simulation is described as allowing repeated, realistic, risk-free practice of technical gestures, in line with the principle of "jamais la première fois sur le patient", so trainees acquire technique and confidence before operating on real patients. The article cites a thesis in which simulator-trained students achieved higher real-world success and felt more at ease performing LPs in hospital.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supervisor engagement to review current teaching modalities and integrate simulation sessions alongside traditional bedside teaching
- Availability of procedural simulation mannequins for training
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence cited in the article is a single thesis comparing theoretical training alone versus theory plus mannequin practice, not a large trial
- Trainee under-exposure during rotations and patient refusal are noted as ongoing barriers that simulation alone may not remove

### Target Learners
- Medical students in their clinical years (DCEM 4, TCEM)
- Interns and residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Performing lumbar puncture safely and competently
- Reducing trainee anxiety and fear of failure for technical procedures
