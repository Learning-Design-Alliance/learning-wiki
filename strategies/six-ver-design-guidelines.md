# Six practical design guidelines for building a Video Enhanced Rubric

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents six generalizable guidelines for designing and implementing a VER, structured using Cattaneo et al.'s (2019) three-level hypervideo model: (1) an educational narrative built on the personalization principle, (2) a non-verbal script layer with voice-over and gesture script, (3) keeping the distance between educational narrative and educational content to a minimum, (4) natural segmentation and fading at scene changes, (5) keeping rewind options accessible as a non-linear guideline, and (6) self-explanation prompts within natural segmentation using dynamic linking. Guidelines 1–4 address linear design, guideline 5 non-linear elements, and guideline 6 hypervideo exchange options. The authors present them "as tools to inspire educational researchers and practitioners alike in creating their own Video Enhanced rubric".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Guidelines should be applied in order of importance according to the roles, functions and methods identified with the RFMG framework
#### Constraints
- Guideline 4 assumes a working memory limitation of approximately four to seven items, so no more than seven constituent skills may be shown per segment

### Target Learners
- Learners in secondary pre-university education; the authors expect broader applicability to other complex skills

### Target Learning Goals
- Mental model development and formative assessment of complex skills such as presenting, collaboration, information literacy, and potentially critical/creative thinking, problem-solving and communicating

### Affordances
- Rfmg Framework Prioritize Design Guidelines
