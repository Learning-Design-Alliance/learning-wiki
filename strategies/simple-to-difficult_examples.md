# Simple-to-Difficult Examples

## Description
Start with simple examples when introducing a concept, then progress to more complex ones later. If a learner only sees simple instances of a concept, they will be less likely to classify the more difficult instances, causing under-generalization.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful sequencing of examples from simple to complex. Requires an understanding of the concept's varying levels of complexity.
#### Constraints
- May require significant planning to sequence examples effectively. Can be time-consuming.

### Target Learning Goals

### Affordances
- Builds confidence by starting with easier examples. Gradually increases cognitive load, leading to a more nuanced understanding.

### Personalization
- Adapt the difficulty level of examples based on learner progress and prior knowledge.
