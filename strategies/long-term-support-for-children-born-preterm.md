# Prioritize equity-focused public policy and long-term follow-up and support for families of children born preterm

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that public policy combine attention to social inequity with continued support for families of children born preterm, since "public policy aiming to increase equity in educational attainment could likely focus on socioeconomic status, sex-based disparities, and additional support to immigrants as well as to families of children born preterm." It specifically suggests "improving access to quality public daycare could be prioritized, alongside ongoing monitoring and support for children born preterm who experience educational difficulties."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ongoing monitoring of educational difficulties among children born preterm from early years to adolescence
#### Constraints
- Psychological counseling, physio- and occupational therapy, speech therapy and tutoring are not always covered by universal health care, per the article's conclusion

### Target Learners
- Children and adolescents born preterm, particularly extremely preterm

### Target Learning Goals
- High school graduation and postsecondary educational attainment
