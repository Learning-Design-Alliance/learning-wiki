Pair agent-based tools with instructor-provided cognitive scaffolding to bridge the gap between efficient retrieval and higher-order cognition¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that because students in agent-enriched contexts engage in self-regulated verification loops but "often struggle to spontaneously transition from these lower-order interactions to higher-order cognitive processing without external prompts", instructors should offer guiding strategies or cognitive scaffolding. It also recommends integrating agent-based tools into human-GenAI collaborative learning contexts and providing targeted scaffolding to bridge the prompt engineering gap for non-technical majors.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors available to provide guiding strategies or cognitive scaffolding alongside agent access

Constraints¶

  • Without external scaffolding or instructor-led prompts, students rely on GenAI for immediate problem resolution rather than critical inquiry

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate STEM students, especially non-technical majors using GenAI tools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • higher-order cognitive processing such as analysis, evaluation, and design

  • Four design principles for educational AI that empowers epistemic agency rather than passive offloading
