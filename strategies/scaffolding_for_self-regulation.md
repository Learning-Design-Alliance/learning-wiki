# Scaffolding for Self-Regulation

## Description
Scaffolding involves providing temporary support to learners to help them manage their emotional and behavioral responses in new or challenging situations. This support is gradually reduced as learners develop their self-regulation skills. For example, in emotionally safe contexts, educators can help students learn how to manage their reactions by providing structured activities, clear expectations, and immediate feedback. The goal is to provide enough support initially so that learners can succeed, then gradually reduce the level of assistance as they become more confident and capable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supportive and emotionally safe learning environment, clear expectations, structured activities, immediate feedback, and an understanding of the learner’s strengths and challenges related to self-regulation.
#### Constraints
- If scaffolding is not gradually reduced, learners may become overly reliant on external support. It requires careful observation and assessment of the learner’s progress to ensure that the level of support is appropriate.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Learners who struggle with self-regulation, particularly those who are highly reactive to new or challenging situations, including students in K-12 and adult learners

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Develop emotional self-regulation, improve behavioral control, build confidence in challenging situations, enhance emotional intelligence

### Affordances
- Creates a safe and supportive learning environment, helps learners manage emotional impulses, builds self-awareness, and promotes gradual independence in self-regulation. It also allows educators to tailor support to individual needs, ensuring that learners are not overwhelmed by the task.

### Personalization
- Scaffolding can be adapted to individual needs by adjusting the level of support based on the learner’s progress. For learners with significant challenges, more structured activities and frequent feedback may be necessary. For those who are more advanced, the level of support can be reduced to promote independence.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe changes in emotional reactivity and behavioral control. Monitor the learner’s ability to use self-regulation strategies independently. Collect self-reports from learners regarding their confidence and ability to manage challenging situations.

## Impact
- Research suggests that scaffolding can effectively support the development of self-regulation skills by providing a safe and structured environment for learning. Studies have shown that learners who receive appropriate scaffolding are more likely to succeed in challenging situations and develop confidence in their abilities.
