# Assess grit to identify students at risk of burnout and offer coping and strategy training to less gritty students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that assessing grit may help identify students at greater risk of burnout and motivational withdrawal, since gritty students were less exhausted and more engaged. Less gritty students may benefit from programs teaching learning and coping strategies, time management, and smart goals, and perseverance of effort may be fostered through parental academic socialization. The authors also urge therapists and school psychologists to address the negative side of high academic demands and provide accessible coping training, possibly integrated into the curriculum such as relaxation techniques or mindfulness in physical education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires valid grit assessment (e.g., the Short Grit Scale with its two facets) and delivery of coping, time-management, and goal-setting programs to identified students
#### Constraints
- The authors note grit may have a dark side when students persevere toward goals that exceed their resources, and that promoting perseverance for inappropriate goals can induce stress

### Target Learners
- Chinese high school students (grades 10-12)

### Target Learning Goals
- Burnout prevention, coping with academic demands, sustained engagement in schoolwork
