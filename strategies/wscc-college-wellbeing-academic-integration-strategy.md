# Integrate overall student well-being into college health programs and use the WSCC framework as a starting model for higher education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that college health programs move beyond traditionally high-risk topics and address chronic-disease-prevention behaviors in connection with academic success. They state "College health programs must integrate students' overall well-being – not only behaviors or experiences traditionally thought of as high risk (e.g., alcohol use or sexual assault) – into their efforts to support students," including campus-specific assessment and tailored programs and policies in collaboration with student success initiatives. They propose the Whole School, Whole Community, Whole Child (WSCC) framework, a CDC ecological framework emphasizing the interconnection between students' health and academic achievement, as a starting point in the absence of an existing collegiate framework.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Campus-specific assessments and tailored program and policy implementation in collaboration with student success initiatives
#### Constraints
- WSCC was developed for primary and secondary schools; higher education is a distinct setting, so it is offered only as a starting point

### Target Learners
- U.S. undergraduate college students

### Target Learning Goals
- Supporting student academic success (GPA, retention) alongside chronic disease prevention and well-being
