# Have students choose between instruments and justify their choice, combined with dedicated sessions on measurement uncertainties and data quality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that laboratory instruction include dedicated sessions on measurements and data quality and ask students to actively choose between instruments and justify their decisions. The authors state these findings "suggest that dedicating specific laboratory instruction sessions on measurements and data quality, and having students choose between different instrumentation and provide a justification for their decision, can positively impact students’ habits in the laboratory". In the studied course this was implemented through two active-learning sessions on measurement uncertainties plus a practical session using the questionnaire instruments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Open-ended justification prompts so instructors can see students’ reasoning; instruction on measurement uncertainties and systematic effects precedes the choice activity.
#### Constraints
- The article highlights the importance of discussing with students the relationship between the goal of a measurement and the instrument choice, to avoid students always aiming for the highest possible precision regardless of the situation.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory physics laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- basing experimental decisions on evidence and data quality rather than intuition or personal preference
