# Use audiovisual playback in meta-debriefing to reveal gaps between perceived and actual debriefer behaviors, with robust facilitator training to protect psychological safety

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article encourages audiovisual playback during meta-debriefing because it "enables learners to perceive mannerisms and behaviors they were previously unaware of, while delivering accurate data that can help minimize gaps between perceived and actual behaviors," providing a powerful impetus for self-reflection. Playback should be aligned with debriefers' learning needs, for example showcasing examples of learner engagement analyzed with an advocacy-inquiry approach. The authors recommend meta-debriefers undergo robust local audiovisual training given valid concerns of learner anxiety when watching videos of themselves and threats to psychological safety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Robust local audiovisual training for meta-debriefers; use aligned with debriefers' learning needs; delivery in a constructive and psychologically safe manner
#### Constraints
- The benefits of audiovisual playback are highly dependent on the skill of the facilitator rather than the content of the video itself; learner anxiety and apprehension when watching videos of themselves pose threats to psychological safety

### Target Learners
- Simulation faculty debriefers in meta-debriefing club or postcourse settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-reflection on debriefing behaviors
- Closing gaps between perceived and actual practice
