Structure group work to facilitate collaborative discussions and help-seeking behaviors among peers, particularly for low self-efficacy students¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend designing collaborative group work so that peer discussion and help-seeking occur, because their data show peer help was the experience most reported by students with lower initial self-efficacy. As they state, "structuring group work to facilitate collaborative discussions and help-seeking behaviors among peers may be particularly beneficial for building self-efficacy in low self-efficacy students." In their courses, groups of three to five students met weekly on quantitative assignments with instructors and teaching assistants circulating to answer questions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Persistent semester-long groups of three to five students
  • Regular collaborative in-class assignments on quantitative problems
  • Instructor and teaching assistant availability during group work

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is based on self-reported experiences in one introductory biology course at a single institution

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate introductory biology students, especially those with low initial self-efficacy for quantitative tasks

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Self-efficacy for solving quantitative biology problems

Claims¶

  • Students' self-efficacy for quantitative biology tasks significantly increased after completing collaborative group work assignments, with large effect sizes [+W]
  • Lower initial self-efficacy significantly increased the odds of students reporting that getting help from peers increased their self-efficacy [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Build relational school resources (teacher–student relationships, peer support, encouragement, extracurriculars) to scaffold self-regulation for low-efficacy marginalized students
