# Differentiate STEM instruction by cognitive sequence matched to students' grasping preference, using learning stations and teacher professional development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that students performed with higher change scores in units sequenced to match their preferred mode of grasping information, the authors recommend that teachers vary the sequencing of concepts. They suggest this "could be as simple as splitting the class based on learning preferences and setting up learning stations" so apprehension-preferring students begin with a concrete experience while comprehension-preferring students begin with abstract concepts. They further recommend professional development and preservice training on ELT-based lesson sequencing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-service teachers should be properly instructed in methods that allow them to teach using the full ELT cycle
- Careful attention during instructional design to ensure students receive exposure to the complete learning cycle
#### Constraints
- Teachers who delivered the experimental units reported that delivering instruction beginning with a concrete experience felt unnatural

### Target Learners
- secondary agricultural education students with both apprehension and comprehension preferences

### Target Learning Goals
- grasping abstract STEM concepts in agricultural education
