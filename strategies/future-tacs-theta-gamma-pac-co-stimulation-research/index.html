Future research should test theta-gamma phase-amplitude coupling co-stimulation protocols and extend tACS benefits to cognitively impaired populations¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on evidence that cross-frequency coupling supports cognitive processing, the authors propose that "tACS protocols with co-stimulation at theta and gamma frequency bands may be a viable option to increase cognitive improvements by inducing theta-gamma PAC." They also recommend testing tACS in older adults and patients with neurological diseases, and providing "individualized current intensity thresholds and electrode montage positions" to minimize side effects.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Simultaneous theta and gamma frequency delivery over multifocal areas

Constraints¶

  • The PAC co-stimulation benefit is a proposition supported by prior work the review cites, not tested within this meta-analysis

Target Learners¶

  • older adults
  • patients with neurological diseases

Target Learning Goals¶

  • working memory
  • cognitive functions impaired by decreased theta PAC

Affordances¶

  • Tacs Synchronization Ltp Mechanism Account

Claims¶

  • Online-tACS with theta and gamma frequency bands significantly improves cognitive performance, while delta, alpha and beta bands do not [+W]
  • tACS cognitive benefits concentrate in executive function for theta protocols and in executive and perceptual-motor function for online gamma protocols [+W]
  • tACS effects on cognitive performance depend on targeted brain region: theta on PFC and PPC and online gamma on PPC are effective [+W]
  • tACS protocols produce a small significant overall improvement in cognitive performance in healthy young adults [+W]
  • Offline-tACS with theta frequency band significantly improves cognitive performance, but offline alpha, beta and gamma bands do not [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use timing- and frequency-matched tACS protocols (online theta or gamma, offline theta) targeted to PFC or PPC to improve cognitive performance in healthy young adults
