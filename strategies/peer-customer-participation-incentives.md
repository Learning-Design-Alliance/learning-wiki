# Grade incentives and mandatory in-class activities to ensure peer-customer participation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because peer-customers must attend class for customer interviews, the article describes two mechanisms: a grade incentive where "A peer-customer receives a 50% weighting on their lowest technical assignment grade," and making all customer activities mandatory in-class activities (ICAs) worth 4% of the total grade. In the second iteration, all N=11 customers showed up to ICAs, whereas the first hybrid-instruction iteration had attendance issues with one unresponsive online customer. A contingency plan assigns a TA as stand-in customer if a peer-customer drops the class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Customer activities must be scheduled as mandatory in-class activities that are part of the grade
#### Constraints
- Hybrid instruction in the first iteration led to attendance issues; the incentive is planned to become contingent on ICA presence in future iterations

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students acting as peer-customers

### Target Learning Goals
- reliable customer availability for needs assessment and feedback
