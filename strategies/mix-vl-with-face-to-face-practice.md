# Incorporate virtual learning for communication training alongside face-to-face practice for added realism

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that virtual learning is an acceptable and beneficial adjunct for communication skills training, particularly for junior learners and safe practice, but should be combined with in-person role play. Students "expressed preference for a combination of VL, whichever modality, and face-to-face teaching, recognising benefits of both." The authors suggest VL may suit earlier learning stages while traditional role play may be preferential in final years as the last preparation before Foundation Year 1 roles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to VR headset or desktop application and technical support to address misaligned feedback issues
#### Constraints
- Role play's pressure and anxiety better replicate the clinical environment; participants felt face-to-face practice was needed to prepare for the reality of being with patients

### Target Learners
- Medical students, particularly junior learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Communication skills for handling angry patients and relatives
