# Define a specific scope for reflection in language classrooms rather than using open reflection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that language classrooms identify a particular focus for reflection rather than leaving it open, since classes building content knowledge may use open reflection but language classes need direction. Teachers should "present several examples of language use for students to consider", enabling students to draw insights about form and structure. Initial reflection on language knowledge should progress toward knowledge of how language is used communicatively.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers present examples of language use for students to examine
- Reflection scope is defined according to course objectives or students' major
#### Constraints
- Group reflection may mask issues pivotal for certain individuals

### Target Learners
- English language learners in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Language awareness and form-structure insights
- Communicative language use
