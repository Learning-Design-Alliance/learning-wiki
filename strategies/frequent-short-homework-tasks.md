# Break large homework assignments into smaller, frequent, short-duration daily tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educators restructure homework so that it is assigned often but kept short, rather than given as long problem sets. They "recommend that educators should consider breaking down large homework assignments into smaller, daily tasks that offer regular and spaced opportunities to engage with the material". This provides a middle ground in which students gain the achievement benefits of frequent homework while retaining evening time for other pursuits and avoiding stress and burnout.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers control both how often homework is assigned and how long each assignment takes; the frequency benefit was observed relative to a baseline of up to one or two times per week
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from eighth-grade mathematics and science students in Ireland; effects differ by subject, so daily frequency is supported for mathematics while three or four times per week was optimal for science

### Target Learners
- eighth-grade students

### Target Learning Goals
- mathematics achievement
- science achievement
