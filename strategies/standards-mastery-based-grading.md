# Standards-/Mastery-Based Grading

## Description
Standards-/Mastery-Based Grading (also called standards-based or mastery grading) assigns grades according to a learner's demonstrated level of proficiency on clearly defined learning objectives, typically on a rubric scale (e.g., beginning → developing → proficient → advanced) rather than a percentage of points earned. Criteria are made transparent to students in advance, and grades communicate what a learner knows and can do — not effort, behavior, or the ability to accumulate points. Reassessment is usually permitted, so a grade reflects eventual mastery rather than speed of mastery.

## Design Implications

Mastery grading aligns assessment with [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md): when criteria are explicit and feedback is criterion-referenced, grades become information learners can act on rather than verdicts [Feedback that answers "where am I going, how am I doing, where to next" improves learning.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+S]. The approach shifts learner orientation from outcome comparison toward mastery goals, which supports persistence and self-efficacy [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]. It is the grading counterpart of [Competency-Based Assessment](../principles/competency-based-assessment.md) and the [Competency-Based Learning](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md) pattern.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clearly articulated learning objectives, decomposed into assessable outcomes ([State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md))
- Transparent rubrics or proficiency scales shared with learners before instruction
- A tracking system that records mastery per objective, not just per assignment
- Multiple assessment opportunities and a [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) loop tied to each objective
- Institutional tolerance for gradebook structures that differ from point-averaging

#### Constraints
- Requires substantial restructuring of curriculum, assessments, and gradebooks; partial adoption (mastery rubrics but averaged into a single letter grade) undermines the coherence of the system [-M]
- Reassessment policies can be gamed in the opposite direction — learners may attempt assessments strategically with minimal preparation, relying on unlimited retakes [-W]
- In competitive-selection contexts (admissions, licensure), single holistic grades remain the currency; mastery transcripts may not translate [-M]
- Poorly written objectives produce rubrics that fragment skills into checklists, losing whole-task integration [Whole-task performance improves transfer.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Full standards-based report cards** (K–12 districts): each standard reported separately
- **Mastery grading in higher ed** (e.g., specifications grading, standards-based grading in STEM courses): binary mastery per objective with token-limited retakes
- **Hybrid models**: mastery grades for academic objectives, separate reporting for habits of work
- **Personalized mastery goals**: individual learners work toward personalized proficiency targets, adapting the model for heterogeneous readiness

### Target Learners
- All levels, but strongest evidence and uptake in higher education and professional training, where objectives are already outcome-defined
- Learners with low prior achievement, who benefit from criterion-referenced feedback and reassessment instead of early, permanent low grades [Feedback that answers "where am I going, how am I doing, where to next" improves learning.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+S]
- Anxious or grade-avoidant learners, for whom removing point-competition shifts focus to learning goals [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Mastery of well-defined knowledge and skill objectives (procedural and conceptual)
- Development of self-regulated learning: learners monitor their own proficiency against explicit criteria [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Less suited to purely emergent, discussion-built, or highly subjective learning outcomes where criteria cannot be specified in advance

### Instructions
1. Define and publish the learning objectives for the course or unit ([State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md)); decompose them into assessable outcomes.
2. Build a proficiency rubric per objective with concrete descriptors of each mastery level; share it before instruction begins ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)).
3. Design assessments that map each item or task to a specific objective ([Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md)).
4. Score against the rubric, recording mastery per objective in the gradebook rather than averaging points.
5. Deliver criterion-referenced feedback and offer reassessment on objectives not yet mastered ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)).
6. Convert mastery records to final grades using a transparent rule (e.g., "grade = mode of proficiency levels across objectives"), not a point average.

## Related Strategies
- Specifications grading — a higher-education variant using binary mastery judgments and token-based reassessment
- Ungrading — a more radical variant that removes grades entirely while retaining mastery feedback structures
- Retake/reassessment policies — the operational mechanism that makes grades reflect eventual rather than initial mastery

## Related Elements

## Patterns That Use This Strategy
- [Competency-Based Learning](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md) — mastery grading is the assessment layer of competency-based programs
- [Formative Assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md) — reassessment cycles enact the formative feedback loop
- [Mastery Learning](../patterns/mastery-learning.md) — Bloom's original model; grading by mastery is its reporting mechanism

## Examples
- **Mastery-based STEM courses** — Many university calculus and physics courses (popularized by Robert Talbert's *Grading for Growth*, 2023) use standards-based grading: a list of ~20 skills, binary or scale mastery per skill, unlimited or token-limited retakes.
- **K–12 standards-based report cards** — Districts using proficiency scales (e.g., 4 = exceeds, 3 = meets) per Common Core standard, with separate reporting of academic and behavioral standards.
- **Khan Academy** — Mastery tracking per skill with levels (attempted → familiar → proficient → mastered), a platform-scale implementation of mastery reporting ([khanacademy.org](https://www.khanacademy.org)).
