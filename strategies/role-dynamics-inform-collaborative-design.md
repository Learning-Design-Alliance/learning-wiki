# Use longitudinal role dynamics to inform group formation, role assignment, and targeted support in collaborative learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article closes by arguing that mapping how roles emerge and evolve over time gives teachers actionable information for collaborative course design. As the authors state, "understanding role dynamics and their evolving trajectories can help teachers better design future collaborative activities, assign roles, form groups, distribute tasks, and, more importantly, be able to support students." The multilayered analytics pipeline (LCA roles, ENA code networks, sequence mining, Markov process mining) is the concrete mechanism for surfacing free riders and role stability early enough to intervene.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to longitudinal online discussion data across consecutive courses, coding of that data against a theoretical framework, and analytic tooling for mixture modeling, sequence and process mining
#### Constraints
- Longitudinal data collection is, per the authors, "far from trivial" given effort, time, and student attrition; the study's evidence comes from one cohort of dental students in a PBL curriculum

### Target Learners
- first-year higher education students working in online PBL groups

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained active participation in collaborative learning
- prevention of free-riding and disengagement

## Claims

- [Three emergent roles — leader, social mediator, and peripheral explorer — were identified in online PBL discussions from CoI-coded discourse using latent class analysis.](../claims/coi-three-pbl-roles-lca.md) [+W]
- [Trajectories differed in transition structure: free-rider students migrated from social mediator and leader roles into the peripheral explorer role, and rarely returned to leadership directly.](../claims/free-rider-transition-structure.md) [+W]
- [Free riders earned significantly lower grades than students in the active constructive and social interactive trajectories, whose achievement levels were statistically indistinguishable.](../claims/free-riders-lower-grades-two-active-trajectories-equal.md) [+W]
- [Students' roles showed strong temporal stability: each trajectory returned to its initial roles across the 13 weeks, which the authors interpret as stable collaborative dispositions.](../claims/role-trajectories-return-to-initial-roles.md) [+W]
