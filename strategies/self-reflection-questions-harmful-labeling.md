# Self-reflection questions for researchers to avoid harmful labeling and stereotyping of student participants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The paper proposes a set of reflective questions researchers should ask before using labels or categorizations for students, such as "Would I use this label or categorization for my students if they were present in the audience?" and "Have I attributed a circumstance or situation beyond a student's control to their identity?" These questions aim to disrupt disparaging terms like "weaker," "lowest," "low-level," and "low-income" that attribute student circumstances and performance to identity rather than systemic inequalities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Researchers must be willing to critically examine their own terminology and categorization practices before presenting or publishing
#### Constraints
- The paper notes these questions pertain to all research populations, including students at all levels, postdocs, faculty and other professionals, and members of local communities

### Target Learners
- PER researchers
- physics education researchers presenting or publishing studies with human participants

### Target Learning Goals
- avoiding harmful stereotypes in research terminology
- recognizing systemic inequalities in educational contexts
