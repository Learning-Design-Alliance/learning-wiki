Deliver engineering graphics courses synchronously online to accommodate Converger and Assimilator learning styles¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on their findings, the authors suggest that Convergers and Assimilators may take well to synchronous online engineering graphics courses, and that understanding learning style impacts can allow instructors to accommodate non-dominant learning styles and improve student retention. The study's synchronous online delivery used broadcasting software (Wimba) allowing audio and desktop video capture, with typed student questions addressed immediately during lecture, while all students remained in the same physical computer lab.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Synchronous broadcasting software allowing audio and desktop video capture, with typed student-instructor interaction visible and addressed immediately during lecture

Constraints¶

  • The study targeted a mechanical engineering course and does not propose its results would be valid on other engineering majors

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate engineering students in synchronous online courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • engineering graphics and solid modeling proficiency

Claims¶

  • Assimilator students score significantly higher on the open-ended design project in synchronous online delivery than Assimilator face-to-face students [+W]
  • Converger students earn significantly higher final course grades in synchronous online engineering graphics instruction than Converger face-to-face students [+W]
  • Converger students score significantly higher on the open-ended design project in synchronous online delivery than Converger face-to-face students [+W]
  • Synchronous online students' learning performance scores were greater than face-to-face students' scores for all learner categories except Convergers was not statistically significant [~W]
  • The Converging learning style dominates among mechanical engineering graphics students in both synchronous online and face-to-face sections [+W]
