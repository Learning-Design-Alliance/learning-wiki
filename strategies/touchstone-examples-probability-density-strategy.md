# Use touchstone examples chaining abstract probability-density ideas to observable physical systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course builds abstract ideas by chaining them back to easily observable phenomena, giving students touchstone concepts for more complicated topics. Superposition is chained to waves on a spring, potential energy diagrams to carts on ramps, and probability density to a ball toss and oscillating glider before electrons in a two-slit experiment. The strategy lets math-phobic students reason graphically and qualitatively with as little algebra as possible.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must revisit the touchstone systems when the abstract concept appears, as lab-tutorial 7 does with the harmonic oscillator potential energy graph
#### Constraints
- The authors report that teaching only about the classical systems, including graphs of their potential energies, was not sufficient to help students learn the material; additional instruction in quantum systems was needed

### Target Learners
- non-science majors with unfavorable expectations about physics

### Target Learning Goals
- probability density in bound-state quantum systems
- connecting speed of an object to likelihood of finding it in a region
