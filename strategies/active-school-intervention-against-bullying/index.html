Schools should intervene actively against bullying rather than relying on adolescents' social support networks¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 7 claims (3 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that schools take a more active role in tackling bullying because peer and family support, while protective for academic outcomes, cannot mitigate the mental-health consequences of victimisation. The conclusion states that "support from friends and family alone cannot mitigate against the strong negative effect that bullying has on mental health" and that early, wide-ranging intervention may prevent serious consequences and avoid specialist psychiatric referral.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Schools must recognise that existing social support from friends and family is insufficient to protect bullied pupils' mental health

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on an observational prospective study in East London secondary schools, not on a tested intervention

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school adolescents (years 7 and 9, ages 11–14)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • protecting academic achievement and mental health of bullied pupils

Claims¶

  • Social support from friends and family does not buffer the effect of bullying on depressive symptoms [+W]
  • Bullying predicts depressive symptoms two years later for boys but not for girls [+W]
  • Bullied adolescents are about half as likely to reach the national academic achievement benchmark for their age group two years later [+W]
  • Adolescents with moderate or high friend support are about half as likely to report being bullied [~W]
  • High family social support is associated with lower odds of depressive symptoms [~W]
  • High levels of support from friends protect bullied adolescents from poor academic achievement, but moderate or low friend support does not [~W]
  • Only moderate (not high or low) family support protects bullied adolescents from poor academic achievement [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Prevent and intervene early against bullying in preadolescence to protect cognitive development
  • Two-step bullying intervention: first reduce victimisation and rebuild motivational factors, then raise achievement and engagement
  • Bystander Intervention Training
