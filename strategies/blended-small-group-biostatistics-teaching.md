# Blend didactic lectures with small-group active learning activities in biostatistics teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 2 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The block curriculum replaced purely didactic delivery with blended modalities including lecture, problem-based learning, massive open online course, and inquiry-based learning. "Students were divided into small groups (20 students/group) to undertake a literature review, participate in group discussion, undergo lab practice of the software, and undertake research design outside of the classroom." The authors report this increased small-group problem-solving in which students explore statistical concepts in detail, and 77.96% of block students approved of the reform.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Software lab access and group structures of about 20 students
- Real examples and reduced complicated formulae in teaching materials
#### Constraints
- A few students indicated content duplication between intermediate modules

### Target Learners
- First-year medical postgraduates in biostatistics

### Target Learning Goals
- Statistical thinking, real-life data analysis, and reduced anxiety about learning biostatistics
