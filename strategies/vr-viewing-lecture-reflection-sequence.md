# Sequence immersive VR viewing with an introductory lecture and teacher-facilitated reflective group dialogues to make VR experiences collaborative

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The learning design places an individually-oriented VR medium inside a three-part activity sequence so that the experience becomes a shared resource for dialogue. As the authors state, "our developed learning design involved the following three, temporally organized activities: (1) an introductory lecture by one of the teachers, (2) VR experiences, and (3) reflective group dialogues." Five headsets ran at a time, students familiarized themselves with VR applications beforehand to avoid the awe factor, and dialogues of 20–30 minutes in groups of 2–5 immediately followed viewing. The design targeted the interdisciplinary topic of Health and life skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must first familiarize themselves with VR applications before the learning activity, which the authors note is recommended to avoid the awe factor.
- Five headsets were available, so only five students could view the film simultaneously.
- Reflective dialogues require a teacher or researcher facilitator per small group.
#### Constraints
- The VR headset makes collaboration physically difficult during viewing; the design relies on the surrounding activities, not VR-internal collaboration.
- Learning designs were co-developed with teachers and appropriated to local curriculum needs, which the authors note is necessary for teachers to find immersive technologies relevant and manageable.

### Target Learners
- upper secondary students (17–18 years old) in K-12 education

### Target Learning Goals
- learning about interpersonal relationships, exclusion and discrimination
- managing thoughts, emotions and relations with others
- reflecting on personal stories of racialized exclusion and the US civil rights movement
