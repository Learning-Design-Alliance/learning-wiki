Provide reliable Internet/Wi-Fi and sufficient devices when implementing mobile learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (3 for, 4 against) · 8 studies (3 associational, 2 causal, 2 qualitative, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 0 of 8 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the technical problems students encountered and the small device count, the article recommends that "Researchers should provide Internet and Wi -Fi during mobile learning studi es" and that future research be implemented with more mobile devices and larger samples. It also suggests using mobile learning in courses students do not like or lack interest in, to develop positive attitude, and having students develop animations via mobile devices as a direction for future research.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Reliable Internet and Wi-Fi infrastructure during mobile learning studies
  • Sufficient mobile devices for all participants

Constraints¶

  • Slow Internet connection and notification restrictions of the mobile learning management system were problems observed in this study

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • supporting course learning with mobile devices

Claims¶

  • Interviewed students reported increased motivation and learning support from mobile learning, alongside technical problems [-W]
  • Teachers identified cost, bulky headsets, Wi-Fi connectivity, and technical issues as key challenges to integrating VR into ML2 teaching [-W]
  • During spring 2020 remote instruction, internet connectivity and hardware/software problems interfered with course participation for large shares of undergraduates [-M]
  • Technological prerequisites such as internet access and device availability were identified only by the South African panel, with equity of education as a distal outcome [+W]
  • Technical issues (login scanning, internet connectivity, projector setup, and glitches) frustrated students and created a significant barrier to playlist implementation [-W]
  • Reliable Internet access was a larger barrier to remote and hybrid learning than device access, especially in urban areas [+W]
  • Mobile devices served as the primary tool for learners and teachers during the emergent remote teaching process of the pandemic [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Address technology access disparities with offline or low-tech alternatives when implementing gamified learning
  • Consider Socio-Cultural Factors
  • Enrich blended courses with varied authentic online resources and mobile-device access, per learner recommendations
