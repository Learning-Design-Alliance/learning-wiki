# Train patient instructors in examination recognition and feedback skills before first deployment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a concrete training recipe: instruction on "basic anatomical and physiological features" of the cardiovascular system, lungs, abdomen and thyroid, followed by examination skills, correct recognition of examination performance (e.g. stethoscope placement, palpation depth), and training in giving specific feedback including communication skills. This two-session, four-hour-per-session program enabled PIs to reach gold-standard feedback agreement comparable to physicians.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must cover anatomy and physiology of the target organ systems, examination technique, error recognition, and feedback delivery
#### Constraints
- These were the PIs' first real assignments; the authors note PIs "have not yet been able to acquire sufficient routine" and quality may improve with experience

### Target Learners
- standardized patients serving as patient instructors

### Target Learning Goals
- performing and recognizing correct physical examination technique
- delivering structured feedback on examination performance
