Match exam format to question type: use open-book for long-answer deep-thinking questions and closed-book or AI-restricted invigilated formats for short-answer recall questions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends differentiating exam delivery by question style in the LLM era. For "longer‐answer questions and essays require more critical thinking", open-book formats with online information access remain suitable; for short-answer and recall questions, where "facts can be quickly found using LLMs", the review advises either a closed-book model or an open-book format with restrictions on AI model use requiring invigilation. OBEs would still build students' efficiency at finding information online.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutions must provide secure testing platforms and equitable access to necessary technologies for all students, as the review states.
  • Where AI use is restricted, invigilation is required.

Constraints¶

  • The review notes LLM assistance will become more sophisticated over time and may eventually undermine skills assessed by OBEs, extending beyond recall to clinical reasoning.
  • All included evidence predates widespread LLM introduction.

Target Learners¶

  • University STEM students
  • Medical students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Information retrieval and application
  • Critical thinking and deep synthesis
  • Knowledge recall

Claims¶

  • The authors argue that LLMs such as GPT-3 threaten OBE validity by enabling answers without deep understanding, particularly in unproctored settings [+W]
  • LLMs perform worse on complex, open-ended, or subjective assignments than on short, clearly formulated answers [+W]
  • Proctoring effects on exam performance are mixed, and technical difficulties and cheating were reported in open-book online exams [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use a combined assessment approach: allow AI and internet access for an initial information-gathering phase, then shift to closed-book for independent synthesis
