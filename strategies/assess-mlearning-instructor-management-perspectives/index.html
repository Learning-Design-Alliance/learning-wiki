Assess m-Learning adoption from instructor and university-management perspectives in addition to learner perceptions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that universities evaluating m-Learning should not rely only on student views. It observes that "usually the success of m-Learning is considered only based on perceived benefits from learner perspectives (students)," while instructors and university management are "the first in line to accept or reject any new learning para digm" and motivate students into trying the platform. It calls for surveys classifying CSFs from students', instructors', and management's perspectives as background for a new conceptual framework.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Universities must gather opinion data from instructors and management as well as students before or during m-Learning initiatives

Constraints¶

  • The present meta-analysis could not cover instructor and management perspectives because evaluative studies focused on learners, which the authors call a major drawback

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students
  • university instructors
  • university management and administration

Target Learning Goals¶

  • institutional adoption and successful implementation of mobile learning

Claims¶

  • A meta-analysis of 19 m-Learning studies identifies 21 candidate critical success factors, of which six are shortlisted for statistical meta-analysis [~W]
  • All six shortlisted CSFs have aggregated means above 2.5 on a 5-point Likert scale, indicating appreciable perceived influence [~W]
  • Content is the CSF with the highest aggregated mean and a strong positive correlation with learner perceptions (r = 0.80454) [+W]
  • Ownership and learner community development are positively correlated with learner perceptions (r = 0.6064 and r = 0.64153) [+W]
  • Technical competence of students is negatively correlated with learner perceptions (r = -0.5595) [+W]
  • User friendly design of content correlates highly positively with learner perceptions (r = 0.92961) [+W]
