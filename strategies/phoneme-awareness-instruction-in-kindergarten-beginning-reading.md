# Include phoneme awareness instruction in kindergarten beginning reading programs to improve early literacy outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (4 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 7 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 3 associational, 1 causal), `q2`–`q4` · 2 of 7 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's stated implication is that "Implications for improving beginning reading instruction" follow from the finding that phoneme awareness training improved kindergarten children's phoneme segmentation, letter knowledge, reading, and invented spelling. The implementable action is adding structured phonological/phoneme awareness instruction to the kindergarten curriculum, delivered by the classroom's own teachers and teaching assistants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained instruction (an 11-week program in this study) delivered by trained classroom staff
#### Constraints
- Demonstrated with low-income, inner-city kindergarten populations; one of three reading measures did not show the advantage

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten children beginning reading instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- Phoneme segmentation and early word reading and spelling
