# Think-Pair-Share three-phase cooperative discussion strategy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Think-Pair-Share is a cooperative discussion strategy developed by Frank Lyman and colleagues at the University of Maryland in 1981, working in three phases: (1) Think, where the teacher provokes thinking with a question and students take a few minutes to think; (2) Pair, where students pair up to compare answers and identify the best ones; and (3) Share, where pairs share their thinking with the class. In this study it was incorporated as an additional teaching-learning strategy in one course section while the other section received identical content and lecturing without it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students are given time to think through their own responses before discussion moves on
- Students jot down ideas before turning to a partner to increase individual accountability
#### Constraints
- In this study TPS was used only as an addition to didactic lecturing in one section of a single Health Assessment course

### Target Learners
- baccalaureate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical thinking skills
- problem solving
- peer interaction and participation
