Paragraph Shrinking¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size

Description¶

Paragraph Shrinking is a core activity of Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS), in which paired students alternate roles as "Reader" and "Coach." The Reader reads a paragraph aloud, then "shrinks" it by stating the main idea — who or what the paragraph is about, the most important thing about that who or what, and the main idea in ten words or fewer. The Coach monitors, prompts re-reading when the summary misses the point, and awards points for accurate, concise summaries before roles switch.

Design Implications¶

Paragraph Shrinking operationalizes summarization as a generative learning strategy: producing a ten-word main idea forces selection, integration, and restatement rather than passive re-reading [~S]. The strict word limit functions as a constraint that pushes learners past copying toward genuine comprehension monitoring, and the peer-coach structure provides immediate, low-stakes feedback on every paragraph. Because the procedure is highly scripted, it delivers the benefits of structured summarization without requiring teacher-led whole-class instruction.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructional-level reading material for each pair (texts matched to the weaker reader)
  • Teacher modeling of the shrinking routine before students work independently (Think-Aloud demonstration of identifying the who/what and main idea)
  • Trained student pairs who understand both roles and the point system
  • A timer and predictable session structure (typically 10–15 minutes within a PALS session)

Constraints¶

  • Effectiveness depends on faithful implementation; untrained or off-task pairs drift into simple turn-taking without genuine summarization [-M]
  • The ten-word limit can encourage shallow, topic-naming summaries ("it's about dogs") rather than idea-level summaries if the coach does not push for the "most important thing" [~M]
  • Less effective when texts are too difficult for the Reader, since decoding effort crowds out comprehension resources chunking-reduces-working-memory-load [~M]
  • Requires sustained use over weeks; one-off sessions show little measurable gain [-W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • In elementary PALS (Grades 2–6), shrinking follows partner reading; in secondary PALS, it is embedded in longer strategy sessions
  • Teachers can pre-teach the who/what/most-important-thing heuristic with short, easy paragraphs before grade-level text
  • Variants allow written shrinking (one sentence) for older students or as a bridge to writing summaries
  • Works in dyads or triads; triads suit odd class sizes with the third student as scorekeeper

Target Learners¶

  • Students in Grades 2–6 learning to distinguish main ideas from details
  • Struggling readers who need frequent, supported practice with comprehension monitoring
  • English learners, who benefit from the repeated oral restatement structure [~M]
  • High-achieving readers in the Coach role, who deepen their own summarization through evaluation and feedback

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Main idea identification and paragraph-level summarization
  • Comprehension monitoring: noticing when a summary doesn't capture the paragraph and re-reading
  • Oral academic language: restating ideas concisely in one's own words

Instructions¶

  1. Pair students and assign initial Reader and Coach roles; provide matched texts.
  2. Model the routine with a Think-Aloud: read a paragraph, name the who/what, state the most important thing, then shrink to ten words or fewer.
  3. Reader reads the paragraph aloud while the Coach follows along.
  4. Coach asks the shrinking questions in order: "Who or what was the paragraph about? What was the most important thing about the who or what? Say the main idea in ten words or fewer."
  5. Coach awards points for a correct who/what, a correct most-important-thing, and a main idea within ten words; prompts re-reading and a second attempt when a summary misses.
  6. Partners switch roles at the next paragraph and continue for the timed session.
  7. Teacher circulates, providing feedback on coach quality and reinforcing accurate summaries.

Related Strategies¶

  • Reciprocal Teaching — shares the goal of comprehension monitoring but uses teacher-modeled predicting, questioning, clarifying, and summarizing in small groups rather than scripted dyads
  • Peer Tutoring — the broader family of structured peer-role activities; Paragraph Shrinking is the summarization-specific instance
  • Summarization — the general strategy; Paragraph Shrinking is a constrained, peer-delivered implementation
  • Arrange PALS tutor training through AIR by specifying training type, grade level, group size, and location

Examples¶

  • PALS Reading (Vanderbilt Kennedy Center / IRIS Center) — Paragraph Shrinking is Activity 2 in the PALS sequence, following partner reading and preceding prediction relay; implementation materials are distributed through the IRIS Center and Kansas IRIS.
  • Grade 2 classroom — Pairs read a science trade book; after each paragraph the Reader shrinks it ("Frogs live near water. They lay eggs there."), earning up to three points per paragraph, with the teacher conferring briefly with pairs that consistently miss the most-important-thing step.
