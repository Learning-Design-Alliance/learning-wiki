# Integrate simulation, case-based learning, and communication-focused training into pre-clinical delirium curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students advocated incorporating high-fidelity simulations, role-playing, and case-based video materials into pre-clinical curricula to bridge the theory-practice gap. The authors recommend that "simulation, case-based learning, and the use of visual materials" may improve nursing students' ability to recognize and manage unpredictable clinical conditions such as delirium. Future simulation should include both hyperactive and hypoactive delirium scenarios.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation scenarios should include realistic patient behaviors such as pulling out cables and devices to allow intervention practice
#### Constraints
- The article notes no simulation scenarios specifically focused on delirium management had been implemented at the study institution at the time of research

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- Delirium recognition and management
- Therapeutic communication
- Clinical confidence and preparedness
