# Include midwives and obstetricians in anesthesia provider courses to support multidisciplinary practice change

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course team added up to 8 obstetricians/midwives per course on days 2 and 3 "at local request because multidisciplinary training is known to increase the effectiveness of quality improvement interventions and the ability to apply learning to a clinical setting." The discussion further supports this: neonatal resuscitation faded as a practice-change theme among anesthetists because midwives perform most neonatal resuscitation, and the authors conclude this "supports the idea of including midwives on the SAFE-OB course." Participants were selected by the country's chief of anesthesia in consultation with local hospital directors because local engagement with hospital management supports implementation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Local request and stakeholder buy-in (chief of anesthesia, hospital directors)
- Course content that includes areas specifically relevant to midwives, such as neonatal resuscitation
#### Constraints
- Midwives and obstetricians did not complete the anesthesia knowledge and skills assessments because the tests contained anesthesia-specific questions

### Target Learners
- Midwives and obstetricians working alongside anesthesia providers in low-income countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Multidisciplinary management of obstetric and neonatal emergencies
