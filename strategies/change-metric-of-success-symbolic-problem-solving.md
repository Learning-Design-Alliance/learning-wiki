# Change the metric of success in problem solving so the advantages of symbolic problem solving become visible

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends changing the metric of success in problem solving so that the advantages of symbolic over numeric problem solving are clear, because traditional textbook problems that use the correct numeric result advantage numeric solving, leaving students likely to "experience success when working with numbers, and failure and frustration when working with symbols." Suggested alternative metrics include examining the generalized system with multiple sets of possible measured values, examining limiting cases, interpreting the relationship between variables, and interpreting the association between the symbolic equation and the physical system.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities must make the advantages of symbolic problem solving visible through metrics such as examination of the generalized system using multiple sets of possible measured values, examination of limiting cases, interpretation of the relationship between variables, and interpretation of the association between the symbolic equation and the physical system.
#### Constraints
- The article notes that even if the instructor tells them to work symbolically before plugging in numbers, many students will find they are much more successful plugging in numbers as soon as they can, which it calls a major barrier to learning the benefits of symbolic problem solving.

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- appreciating the benefits of symbolic problem solving
- interpreting symbolic equations as models of generalized physical systems
