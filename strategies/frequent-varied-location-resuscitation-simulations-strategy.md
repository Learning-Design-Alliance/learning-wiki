# Offer brief resuscitation simulations multiple times per year and in varied hospital locations to combat skill loss and expose learners to room variation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend repeated, distributed delivery of this brief simulation: "each staff member who works on the acute care floor and who could be a first responder to a code should be able to participate in this simulation multiple times per year to avoid skills loss." This responds to participant feedback that training should occur more frequently and in different locations, and to evidence cited by the authors that resuscitation knowledge and skills diminish over time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A program structure reaching all eligible employees, which the authors state they have not yet achieved
#### Constraints
- Scheduling educational sessions with different types of learners with variable schedules is a stated limitation

### Target Learners
- All acute care floor staff who could be first responders to a code event

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of first-responder resuscitation skills and knowledge over time
