# Diagnose context-specific causes of underperformance via student interviews or focus groups before deploying one-size-fits-all psychological interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because achievement gaps and intervention efficacy are population- and context-dependent, the article recommends that faculty first gather local evidence before choosing an intervention. It states that "one-size-fits-all interventions may not be the most productive first step" and that "It may be more productive to begin with student interviews or focus groups to interpret data and diagnose why certain populations of students are underperforming." The diagnosis then guides either customized interventions or course-design changes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must collect local data (interviews, focus groups, surveys) to identify which sociocognitive constructs are salient for underperforming populations in their specific course.
#### Constraints
- The article notes psychological interventions' efficacy is highly population and context dependent, and the gender gap it targeted did not even appear in the control section of the studied course.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate STEM students in large-enrollment courses

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing gendered achievement gaps in exam performance
