# Target HBV education and training to rural regions, nurses and midwives, and elementary school staff, and enforce patient privacy policies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends developing effective educational programs to improve HBV knowledge among health professionals and the public, stating "there is a need for development of effective educational programs to improve HBV knowledge". It specifically urges targeting education to nurses and midwives, who may be the only health workers at newborn deliveries in rural regions, and to elementary school staff who can promote catch-up vaccination, alongside establishing and enforcing patient privacy policies to abolish discrimination.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educational programs must address the specific knowledge deficits found: asymptomatic nature of chronic HBV, disease outcomes, transmission modes, prevention, and monitoring tests
#### Constraints
- The authors note knowledge alone may not translate into practice, citing prior work suggesting information "may have been memorized, but not enough training was given on how to put that information into practice"

### Target Learners
- healthcare and public health professionals in China
- nurses and midwives
- elementary school teachers and administrators

### Target Learning Goals
- HBV transmission, prevention, and screening knowledge
- translating knowledge into sound preventive and reporting practices
