# Year-long weekly solar-elevation measurement programme as a hands-on introduction to experimental physics and astronomy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a measurement programme suitable for first-year university students: measure the Sun's elevation as a function of time during a day, repeated typically once a week over a full year, then fit the extracted hmax and tmax with simple analytic expressions to obtain the obliquity, latitude, and orbital eccentricity. The author states students thereby "learn important concepts in experimental physics, see concrete applications of kinematics and changes of reference frames, and benefit from a hands-on introduction to astronomy".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Commitment to repeated measurements across a full year
- Polynomial fitting of daily h(t) data to extract hmax and tmax
#### Constraints
- Weather interruptions (partly cloudy days) reduce the accuracy of the extracted hmax and tmax

### Target Learners
- first-year university students

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental physics skills
- kinematics and reference-frame changes
- astronomy
- data fitting
