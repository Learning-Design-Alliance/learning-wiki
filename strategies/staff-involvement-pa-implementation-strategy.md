# Involve school staff in developing PA interventions and support implementation with a workshop, a PA coordinator, and team exchange of ideas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementation strategy in which researchers involve teachers and principals before or during early intervention development, and schools build team support by discussing evidence on PA's cognitive and academic benefits, organizing a single background-and-practice workshop, assigning an enthusiastic teacher as "PA coordinator", and holding regular follow-up meetings where team members exchange ideas, tips and tricks. The article recommends this to produce interventions more likely to be implemented successfully in daily school practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Decisions made by both principal and teachers, as team support seems essential for successful and sustainable implementation
#### Constraints
- The article notes its participants were a group of fairly motivated teachers and principals, limiting generalisability to less motivated staff

### Target Learners
- Primary school teachers and principals

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained implementation of additional physical activity in school routines
