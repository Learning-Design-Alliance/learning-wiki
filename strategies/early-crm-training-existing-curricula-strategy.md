# Incorporate CRM training early into existing EM residency curricula using a low-resource simulation course to encourage continued deliberate practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating basic CRM training early into existing EM education, arguing this "encourages continued deliberate practice, discussion, and improvement of essential CRM skills." The course is designed to be feasible with one simulation room, a single debriefing facilitator, and minimal confederate staff, making it an option for any EM residency without access to a large simulation center.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- One simulation room, a single simulation debriefing facilitator, and minimal other staff or faculty to serve as confederates
#### Constraints
- The pilot did not assess retention of CRM skills beyond the two-week course; further studies are needed to determine if simulated CRM skills affect behaviors or success in the ED

### Target Learners
- Novice emergency medicine residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic crisis resource management skills required of successful emergency physicians

## Claims

- [A simulation-based CRM course improved teams' overall and category CRM skills from pretest to posttest, with large effect sizes (|r| = 0.581–0.601) that did not reach statistical significance after Bonferroni correction](../claims/crm-course-large-effect-sizes-nonsignificant.md) [+W]
