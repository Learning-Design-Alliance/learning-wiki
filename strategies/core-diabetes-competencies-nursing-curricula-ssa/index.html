Include core diabetes competencies in nursing curricula and expand continuing professional development across SSA¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends strengthening diabetes training for frontline nurses, embedding core diabetes competencies in nursing curricula across SSA, and using cost-effective continuing professional development. It states "we suggest the inclusion of core diabetes competencies in nursing curricula across SSA" and that facilities should "harness existing cost-effective educational programmes, such as continuing professional development (CPD)".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Diabetes pathophysiology and epidemiology content, clinical skills such as BG monitoring, and culturally appropriate education on diet and self-management must be included in curricula.

Constraints¶

  • Further research is needed on tailor-made strategies for adapting educational interventions to local, resource-constrained contexts.

Target Learners¶

  • Nurses and other frontline healthcare workers in sub-Saharan Africa

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Diabetes pathophysiology and epidemiology
  • Blood glucose monitoring and complication management
  • Culturally appropriate diet and self-management education

Claims¶

  • Nurses in sub-Saharan Africa show knowledge gaps in diabetes pathology, insulin therapy, treatment and complications [+W]
  • Specific knowledge deficits in Ghana and Libya studies: type 2 diabetes, DKA and complications [+W]
  • Barriers to nurses' diabetes knowledge acquisition include lack of guidelines, inadequate staffing, salary and training resources [~W]
  • Facilitators of nurses' diabetes knowledge acquisition include experience, refresher nutrition courses and NCD training [+W]
  • Many nurses in SSA have adequate knowledge of diabetes self-management support and care in some studies [~W]
  • Educational programmes are associated with significant improvements in nurses' diabetic foot care practice, measured in seven of the nine included studies [+M]
  • Nurses begin with low baseline knowledge of diabetic foot care, and educational qualifications may moderate post-training knowledge gains [~W]
