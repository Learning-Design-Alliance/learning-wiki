# Design AI tutors for complementarity across role paradigms and move beyond static prompting toward dynamic learner modeling

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's forward-looking design recommendations are threefold: use factorial designs crossing role paradigms with content domains to disentangle how an agent teaches from what it teaches; establish educational value with learning-outcome designs using pre- and postlearning assessment and individual-level linkage; and address persistent deficits in adaptive difficulty and feedback by moving "beyond static prompting toward dynamic learner modeling and memory-augmented tutoring." Rather than seeking a single best role, it points toward orchestrating different pedagogical strengths across an interaction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Factorial designs crossing role paradigms with content domains
- Learning-outcome designs with pre- and postlearning knowledge assessment and individual-level linkage
#### Constraints
- The study confounded role paradigm with content domain, so role-related observations are descriptive rather than causal

### Target Learners
- medical students in role-play-based curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- adaptive difficulty calibration
- formative feedback quality
- balanced affective and cognitive teaching functions
