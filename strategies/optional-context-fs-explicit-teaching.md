# Include or exclude sentence context in explicit formulaic sequence teaching based on teaching motivation, since context adds no measurable learning benefit for low-proficiency learners.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the study found "no significant differences in learning gains between teaching frequent formulaic sequences with or without sentence context," the article advises that teaching professionals may decide to either include or exclude context sentences depending on their teaching motivation. If contextualized teaching is preferred, the context sentences should be comprehensible to the learners, with proficiency taken into account when selecting them. In the study, more than 97% of the token coverage of context sentences came from the most frequent 2,000 BNC word families.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- If context sentences are used, they should be comprehensible to the learners, with learner proficiency considered in sentence selection
#### Constraints
- The null context effect was found with low-proficiency learners; the authors note advanced learners may benefit from context sentences (Griffin, 1992)

### Target Learners
- Low-proficiency EFL university students

### Target Learning Goals
- Receptive knowledge of form and meaning of formulaic sequences
