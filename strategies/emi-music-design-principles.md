# Five design principles for integrating music into EMI instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
From its synthesis, the review proposes five design principles for EMI practitioners: "Alignment: Choose music that connects thematically or linguistically with disciplinary content"; Brevity (keep tasks short, ≤3 minutes, to respect content delivery); Scaffolding (structure pre-, while-, and post-activity phases); Recurrence (brief, regular activities for cumulative gains); and Learner Agency (allow choice within curated options). The review states these principles serve EMI's dual objectives of disciplinary knowledge acquisition and language development and can be adapted flexibly across fields.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks must be short, purpose-driven, and linked to the lesson's conceptual agenda to avoid being perceived as peripheral add-ons
#### Constraints
- Direct EMI evidence remains sparse; principles are extrapolated largely from EFL findings

### Target Learners
- EMI university students
- EFL adolescents and adults

### Target Learning Goals
- listening engagement
- oral fluency
- disciplinary content comprehension

### Affordances
- Music Language Processing Mechanisms
