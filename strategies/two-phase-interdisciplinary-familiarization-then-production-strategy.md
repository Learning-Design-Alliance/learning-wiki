# Structure an interdisciplinary course with a discipline-familiarization first half and a permanent-group artistic-production second half

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend offering the interdisciplinary process as a 3-unit general education class and splitting the semester: "during the first half of the semester, students are introduced to each other's disciplines and familiarized with each other's disciplinary language and belief systems" using problem-based learning practices, with small interdisciplinary discussion groups of changing composition and instructor-selected teaching materials. In the second half, students are assigned to permanent groups to work on the artistic product, show or perform it to friends and the campus community, and combine the demonstration with an open discussion about their experiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A dedicated course (the authors suggest a 3-unit general education class) providing substantially more shared time than the 10-15 hours available in the original design
#### Constraints
- The authors state that given the large divide between the disciplines, students needed more time to develop a common framework, which the embedded design could not provide

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science and non-science majors

### Target Learning Goals
- developing a common interdisciplinary framework and vocabulary
- collaborative problem solving across disciplinary cultures
