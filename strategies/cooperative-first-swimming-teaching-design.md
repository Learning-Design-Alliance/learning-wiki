# Design swimming teaching around cooperative and understanding models rather than single skill-oriented traditional instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that swimming teaching shift from a single skill orientation to body-mind synergistic development by adopting cooperative learning and understanding teaching as the main design, using "heterogeneous grouping, task-driven, emotional sharing and other links" to enrich social interaction and emotional practice. Concrete elements include four-person heterogeneous groups with defined roles (recorders, demonstrators, supervisors, sharers), group tasks such as 50 m relay swims, mutual evaluation, and a 5-min end-of-lesson emotional sharing session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers trained in the specific model with standardized lesson preparation; group tasks and sharing time built into each lesson
#### Constraints
- Evidence limited to adolescents aged 12–16 without swimming foundation, breaststroke content, and a 16-week program; long-term durability not verified

### Target Learners
- Adolescents aged 12–16 in school swimming programs

### Target Learning Goals

- Social adaptability
- Swimming skills
