# Use differentiated immediate feedback, explanation of correct responses and open-ended questions in small-group social cognition sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article identifies the training techniques used across sessions: "Open-ended questions were asked to induce conversations, and feedback was provided as well as an explanation of the correct or expected response." Correct answers led to positive feedback while incorrect answers elicited explanations about the correct response, and the experimenter reminded children of the general principle to promote generalization of concepts. Sessions also used varied materials and a speaking slot in small groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An experimenter who provides differentiated immediate feedback and explanations of correct responses during small-group sessions
#### Constraints
- The article reports these techniques within its own 15-session program for preschoolers and does not isolate their individual effects

### Target Learners
- preschoolers in small groups of about 4 children

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of emotions and beliefs
- social problem solving
