# Visualizations and Mind Maps

## Description
Visualizations and mind maps present information graphically — as concept maps, diagrams, timelines, flowcharts, or infographics — so that relationships among ideas are visible rather than buried in prose. A mind map radiates from a central concept with branching associations; concept maps and diagrams additionally label the *type* of relationship between nodes. The strategy can be enacted by providing learners a ready-made graphic or by having learners construct one themselves.

## Design Implications

Visual representations exploit the capacity of the visual channel to carry information in parallel with verbal processing, improving recall when words and pictures are integrated [Dual coding of verbal and visual information improves recall.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-recall.md) [+S]. Graphic organizers are most valuable for novices, who lack the schemas to impose structure on new material themselves [Graphic organizers support comprehension for learners with low prior knowledge.](../claims/graphic-organizers-support-novice-comprehension.md) [+M]. However, visuals must carry the structure of the content; decorative images that do not map to the to-be-learned relations add load without benefit [Decorative illustrations do not improve learning.](../claims/decorative-illustrations-do-not-improve-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A visual whose spatial arrangement genuinely encodes the conceptual structure (hierarchy, sequence, causality, part–whole), not decoration
- Labels on nodes *and* relationships — unlabeled arrows leave learners guessing the connection
- Integration of visual and verbal information on the same display rather than split across pages [Chunking and integrating information reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- For learner-constructed maps: a modeling example, criteria for good links, and a revision cycle

#### Constraints
- Provided organizers can encourage shallow copying; learners who construct their own graphic organizers often outperform those given one [Learner-constructed graphic organizers outperform provided ones.](../claims/learner-constructed-graphic-organizers-outperform-provided.md) [~M]
- Benefits shrink or reverse for high-knowledge learners, for whom the organizer restates what they already know [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Overly dense or ornate visuals (3D effects, gratuitous icons) impose extraneous load and depress learning [Decorative illustrations do not improve learning.](../claims/decorative-illustrations-do-not-improve-learning.md) [-M]
- Mind maps' free-form radial structure suits brainstorming and association but poorly represents causal chains, procedures, or quantitative relations — use flowcharts or diagrams instead

#### Implementation Variability
- **Advance organizer**: present a skeletal visual before instruction to frame incoming content ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
- **Fill-in-the-map**: provide nodes, have learners supply or verify the links — an intermediate step between given and fully constructed maps
- **Progressive disclosure**: build the map incrementally during instruction rather than showing the finished artifact at once
- **Learner-generated**: students draw their own maps as a summarization or assessment activity ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) in visual form)

### Target Learners
- Novices and low-prior-knowledge learners, who benefit most from externally supplied structure [Graphic organizers support comprehension for learners with low prior knowledge.](../claims/graphic-organizers-support-novice-comprehension.md) [+M]
- Learners struggling with dense expository text; the visual offloads relational structure from working memory [Chunking and integrating information reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Less valuable for experts, who already hold the schema and may find the map redundant [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: seeing how ideas relate, not just what they are
- Knowledge organization: building well-structured schemas that support retrieval and transfer
- Summarization and study skills: condensing material into a retrievable visual form

### Instructions
1. Identify the core structure of the content (hierarchy, cycle, causal chain, comparison) and choose a visual form that matches it — do not default to a mind map for everything.
2. Decide whether to provide, co-construct, or have learners build the map; for novices, start provided and shift toward construction as knowledge grows [Learner-constructed graphic organizers outperform provided ones.](../claims/learner-constructed-graphic-organizers-outperform-provided.md) [~M].
3. Label every node and every link with the relationship type ("causes," "is a kind of," "precedes").
4. Integrate the visual with concise text on the same display; avoid split-attention between map and legend [Chunking and integrating information reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M].
5. Use the map actively: have learners explain it aloud, predict a missing branch, or revise it after new content ([Activation](../elements/activation.md), [Application](../elements/application.md)).
6. Revisit and revise the map across the unit so it functions as a growing external model rather than a one-off artifact.

## Related Strategies
- [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) — a visualization presented *before* instruction to scaffold incoming information
- [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) — a verbal counterpart that maps unfamiliar structure onto familiar; often paired with a visual
- [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) — learner-generated marks and notes that can take graphical form
- [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) — the working-memory principle that visual grouping enacts

## Examples
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — concept summaries and diagrams accompanying video lessons in science and economics.
- **MindMeister / Coggle** — collaborative mind-mapping tools used for brainstorming and shared concept maps in classrooms.
- **Biointeractive (HHMI)** — published diagrams and visual models (e.g., nutrient cycling, genetics) designed to encode scientific processes rather than decorate text.
- **History timelines in the Reading Like a Historian curriculum** ([Stanford History Education Group](https://sheg.stanford.edu)) — structured visuals that situate documents in causal and chronological relation.
