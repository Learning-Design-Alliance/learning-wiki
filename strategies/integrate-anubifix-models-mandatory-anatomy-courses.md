# Integrate laparoscopic Anubifix™-embalmed human models into mandatory surgical anatomy courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose incorporating their laparoscopic cadaver model into existing mandatory surgical anatomy teaching so that residents train "before the job" in a safe, blame-free environment outside the operating room. The strategy combines technical laparoscopic skills practice with "correct interpretation of the anatomical image", which the authors state is lacking in box trainers and virtual reality simulators. Embedding the model in a compulsory course addresses residents' reported lack of time for voluntary lab practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An Anubifix™-embalmed specimen prepared with colored anatomical landmarks, airtight abdominal closure, laparoscopic working stations, and supervision by both a surgeon and an anatomist
#### Constraints
- The article reports the strategy only as tested in a Dutch national anatomy course with eleven residents evaluating the model via questionnaire

### Target Learners
- first- and second-year surgical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- laparoscopic surgical anatomy of the colon and rectum
- laparoscopic technical skills
