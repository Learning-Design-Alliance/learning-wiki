# De-emphasize Answers, Emphasize Reasoning

## Description
Spend more time on reasoning than on finding the correct answer. Teachers can present worked examples with wrong answers and have learners find the error, or they can rethink the way they use class participation, focusing on the thought process rather than the answer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A shift in classroom culture from answer-focused to reasoning-focused, teachers who are skilled in facilitating discussions about problem-solving strategies.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging to implement in traditional, answer-focused classrooms. Requires careful planning and facilitation to ensure that discussions remain productive.

### Target Learners

- All learners, particularly those who struggle with math or lack confidence in their problem-solving abilities.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->

### Affordances
- Encourages learners to explore different problem-solving strategies, promotes deeper understanding, and reduces anxiety by de-emphasizing the importance of a single correct answer.

### Personalization
- Teachers can tailor activities to the individual learner's reasoning process, providing support and guidance where needed.
