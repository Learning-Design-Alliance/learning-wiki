# Consider optimal physical activity when planning the school curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that optimal physical activity and stress-hormone release may determine school performance and state that "These results should be taken into consideration while planning the school curriculum." They note school occupies at least eight hours of a child's day and propose activities during lunch breaks, classroom periods and physical education without affecting normal studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools must have curricular time and settings (lunch breaks, classroom periods, physical education) in which physical activity can be promoted without displacing normal studies
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is a cross-sectional observational study; the authors note self-reported data and socioeconomic and environmental factors as limitations

### Target Learners
- school adolescents aged 12–18 years

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement across school subjects
- executive functioning as measured by mathematics performance
