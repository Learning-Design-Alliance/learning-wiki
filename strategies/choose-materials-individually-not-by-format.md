# Choose individual texts and videos appropriate for specific students rather than deciding by representational format

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's general discussion recommends that material selection be tailored to the individual student rather than governed by whether the material is text or video. It states it is "crucial for (preservice) teachers to choose individual texts and videos that are appropriate for specific students and to avoid making general decisions based on the representational format", because perceiving videos as universally easy can overstrain students when videos are long and complex.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need information about individual students' achievement, self-regulation competencies, and the length and complexity of candidate materials.
#### Constraints
- Short videos with simple content are easy to process, but long and complex videos may be harder to understand than texts, so format-based generalizations fail.

### Target Learners
- Primary and secondary school students in differentiated classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- Selecting appropriately difficult material formats to support academic development without over- or under-challenging students
