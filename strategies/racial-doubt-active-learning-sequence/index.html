Sequence of active-learning exercises for raising racial doubt in race-related courses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes a concrete lesson sequence: students fill out the Census race question and discuss difficulty self-identifying; classify people from twenty-seven countries by nationality alone as white, not white, or not sure; discuss in small groups why they classified as they did; read key literature, watch interactive lectures and the documentary Race the Power of an Illusion, and visit the RACE exhibit; write and share personal narratives of their racial experiences; and attempt to classify photographed individuals by race. The sequence aims to make race-related issues personal and to let students "take the role of the other."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Class meetings with enough time for discussion and active-learning projects; the authors believe the projects would be equally effective for classes meeting twice a week for an hour and a half

Constraints¶

  • The article states breakout sessions and guided discussion did not occur in the non-treated control course because its structure did not allow for these sessions

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate juniors and seniors in social science diversity courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • questioning assumptions about racial categories
  • understanding race and ethnicity as socially constructed identities

Affordances¶

  • Racial Doubt Pedagogical Framework

Claims¶

  • All racial and ethnic subgroups in the treated class shifted toward uncertainty about racial classifications after the lessons [+W]
  • Students entered the courses highly confident they could identify their own and others' race and ethnicity [+W]
  • Students explained their national-origin racial classifications as derived from media depictions rather than direct knowledge [+W]
  • Treated students attributed their new uncertainty to personal and emotive engagement with classmates' self-identified racial narratives [+W]
  • Both male and female students in the treated class showed statistically significant increases in uncertainty about racial classifications [+W]
  • Before the lessons, the treated and non-treated classes did not differ significantly in how they classified the twenty-seven nationalities [+W]
