# Expand interactive simulation methods such as virtual patient and peer simulation in nursing education for communication-skill topics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (8 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 causal, 2 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that virtual patient, virtual-environment peer, and face-to-face peer simulation all reduced negative attitudes and produced comparable skill, satisfaction, and self-confidence outcomes, the authors recommend that "the use of interactive methods such as virtual patient simulation and peer simulation be expanded in nursing education programs for teaching topics that require communication skills". Online peer or virtual patient options are also offered as alternatives when face-to-face practice is not possible or limited.
