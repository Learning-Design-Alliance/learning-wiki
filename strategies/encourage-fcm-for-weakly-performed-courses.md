# Encourage teachers to use the flipped classroom model when teaching courses students perform badly in, while attending to self-control readiness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's authors advise encouraging teachers to use FCM in the classroom, arguing the findings "support the counterargument that FCM learning is more beneficial to students than CTMs". They specifically recommend the FCM strategy "when teaching courses students to perform badly, such as research methodology". The strategy requires preparing pre-class materials and facilitating interactive in-class sessions, and instructors should address self-control readiness given online-session distractions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must be prepared with the method; pre-class materials (audio PowerPoint) must be produced and distributed via a platform
- Instructors should attend to how to instill self-control readiness in learners while using this method
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one 8-week, single-course quasi-experiment with 64 students in one Nigerian region; the authors state the study should be broadened to more sample sizes, other student levels, other courses, and a full-semester intervention

### Target Learners
- Nursing undergraduates; tertiary students in courses where performance is substandard

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-directed learning readiness and autonomous learning in research methodology
