# Involve students in selecting the courses offered at an enrichment camp through a double-blind ranking process

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The camp runs a multi-stage course-selection process run in parallel with student applications: a call for courses is issued, proposals undergo a scientific and pedagogical check, and shortlisted students rank the shortlisted courses according to preference. This is done "in a double-blind way—students don't know who would teach each course", and organizers do not know individual preferences until places are assigned. The article reports that courses on Relativity/Cosmology have always been selected by students when offered, driven by student demand for topics not satisfactorily covered in school.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A call for courses with scientific and pedagogical checking of proposals
- A shortlist of courses that students rank before places are assigned
#### Constraints
- The process is tied to a residential camp with a limited number of courses; in the 2021 edition the shortlist included 6 courses, of which 3 were taught

### Target Learners
- pre-university students in the last three years of secondary education

### Target Learning Goals
- student choice and motivation in advanced science topics such as relativity
