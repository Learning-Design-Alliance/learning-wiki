# Targeted support for the small group of low internet-use students, who show the steepest performance drops at the end of assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators provide targeted support to low internet-use students because this group, though small, shows both lower baseline accuracy and steeper declines in accuracy over lengthy digital assessments. The authors state that "any interventions aimed specifically at low‐use students would be more narrowly focused, but potentially highly beneficial" and suggest future research explore underlying causes such as limited technology access or motivational profiles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires identifying the small low-use group, which the authors warn educators might otherwise overlook
#### Constraints
- The study is not causal and cannot speak to the merit of limiting or promoting internet use in schools

### Target Learners
- 15-year-old secondary students with low levels of internet use

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining accuracy during lengthy digital cognitive tasks
- digital task persistence
