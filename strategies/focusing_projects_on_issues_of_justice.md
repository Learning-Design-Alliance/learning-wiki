# Focusing Projects on Issues of Justice

## Description
Focusing projects on issues of justice means anchoring [Project-Based Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) in authentic social problems — inequity, environmental harm, access, representation — that students investigate and act on. Rather than treating justice topics as content to read about, the strategy positions students as investigators and change agents who produce work with real stakes for real audiences. It draws on culturally relevant pedagogy, which argues that academic learning is deepened, not diluted, when it connects to students' communities and to critique of social structures [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M].

## Design Implications

Justice-focused projects raise engagement by increasing perceived task value and authenticity — learners invest more effort when work matters beyond the grade [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]. But relevance alone is not sufficient: projects still require strong disciplinary structure, sustained inquiry, and explicit scaffolding of the complex skills (research, argumentation, collaboration) that justice work demands.

### Context
#### Requirements
- An authentic problem connected to students' lives or communities, identified with students rather than imposed on them ([Challenge Identification](../elements/challenge-identification.md))
- [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) — a real recipient or venue for the final product (council meeting, community organization, published report)
- Sustained [Collaboration](../elements/collaboration.md) structures and [Argumentation](../elements/argumentation.md) support, since justice issues are contested and require evidence-based reasoning
- Teacher preparation for facilitating discussions of inequity and identity, including norms for respectful [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)

#### Constraints
- Projects that treat justice as a theme bolted onto trivial tasks produce cynicism rather than engagement; the disciplinary work must be genuinely necessary to the project [-M]
- Poorly facilitated discussions of inequity can harm students from targeted groups — teachers must not rely on students from marginalized communities to serve as "living resources" or spokespeople for their groups [-M]
- Community-connected projects require relationships and logistics (partners, permissions, time) that exceed typical unit planning; without them, "authentic" projects collapse into simulation [-W]
- High-stakes emotional content without adequate [Check-Ins](../principles/check-ins.md) and opt-out pathways can create distress rather than productive [Cognitive Disequilibrium](../elements/cognitive-conflict.md) [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Local civic action**: students investigate a concrete local issue (park access, bus routes, school funding) and present proposals to decision-makers
- **Data-for-justice projects**: students use disciplinary tools (statistics, mapping, archival research) to document inequity — e.g., Gutstein's social-justice mathematics curriculum
- **Youth participatory action research (YPAR)**: students design and conduct original research on problems in their own schools and communities (Duncan-Andrade & Morrell's approach)
- **Choice within structure**: teachers set the justice frame while students select the specific issue, balancing [Personalization](../principles/adaptive-learning.md) with curricular coherence

### Target Learners
- Students from marginalized communities, for whom culturally relevant content strengthens identity, belonging, and persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Adolescents generally, who are developmentally primed for idealism and questions of fairness [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Students with low prior engagement in school, for whom authentic purpose can reframe academic work as meaningful [~M]
- Requires support for students new to open-ended inquiry; novices need more [Scaffolding](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) than experienced project learners [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Disciplinary knowledge and skills applied to authentic problems ([Application of Knowledge](../elements/application-of-knowledge.md))
- Civic agency: understanding how to research, argue, and act on public issues
- Critical literacy: analyzing how power and inequity shape texts, data, and institutions
- [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md) and perspective-taking across social positions

### Instructions
1. **Identify a genuine issue with students** — surface community problems students care about ([Challenge Identification](../elements/challenge-identification.md)); co-selection builds ownership and autonomy [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]
2. **Frame the driving question** — make it open-ended, disciplinary, and action-oriented (e.g., "How can we reduce food insecurity in our neighborhood?")
3. **Build knowledge and inquiry skills** — sequence [Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md), [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md), and data collection so students develop the expertise their action requires
4. **Support argumentation and collaboration** — establish [Collaboration](../elements/collaboration.md) roles and [Argumentation](../elements/argumentation.md) norms for working through disagreement
5. **Produce and present authentic work** — deliver products to real audiences ([Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md)), then reflect on impact and process ([Application of Knowledge](../elements/application-of-knowledge.md))

## Related Strategies
- Community-based learning — situates projects in ongoing partnerships with community organizations
- Service learning — a close cousin with a stronger emphasis on structured reflection on service experiences
- Youth participatory action research — the research-intensive variant in which students are co-researchers

## Examples
- **[PBLWorks (Buck Institute)](https://www.pblworks.org)** — publishes gold-standard PBL units, several explicitly focused on equity and community issues, with rubrics and scaffolds.
- **Gutstein's social-justice mathematics curriculum (Chicago)** — middle-school students used mathematics (data analysis, geometry, statistics) to investigate housing displacement, sweatshop wages, and neighborhood demographics, then took action on local issues.
- **YPAR programs (e.g., UCLA Council of Youth Research)** — high-school students conduct original research on educational inequity and present findings to district officials and academic audiences.
- A science class investigating local air-quality data and presenting remediation proposals to the city council, combining disciplinary standards with environmental justice advocacy.
