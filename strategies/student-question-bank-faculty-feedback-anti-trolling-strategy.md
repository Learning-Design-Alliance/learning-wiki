# Implement student-authored question banks with targeted faculty feedback and anti-trolling community measures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends supporting student question writing with faculty academics who give feedback on question "accuracy, relevance and whether the difficulty was appropriate for the course", and with interventions against trolling: defining clear rules for online communities, moderators enforcing standards, and persistent identifiers for moderators while maintaining anonymity to other users. Students and facilitators should also be encouraged to write positive comments to reinforce question-writing behaviour.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty volunteers to review and comment on student-written questions for their specialty.
- Clear community rules and moderation to reduce trolling, which students viewed as demotivational.
#### Constraints
- In practice the proportion of questions reviewed by faculty was relatively small (<5%).

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- improving quality of student-authored formative questions
- sustaining student engagement and question-writing behaviour
