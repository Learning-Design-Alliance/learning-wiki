# Use clinically contextualised online e-learning cases to support didactic teaching in content-heavy preclinical subjects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article supports supplementing lectures with online material that contextualises basic science in clinical scenarios, since students "struggle to see the clinical relevance of the fundamentals of the biomedical sciences". The resource paired question/answer activities with feedback, podcasts with quizzes, and pathogen-focused clinical cases, and engagement with it was associated with higher examination performance. The authors conclude that e-learning "when used effectively, could ensure this important clinical discipline maintains its prominence within these newly designed curricula".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Alignment of online content with face-to-face teaching and module learning outcomes
- Clear instructions on expectations and a virtual learning environment for access
#### Constraints
- A direct link between engagement with specific content and performance in the related examination question cannot be made
- Only one year post-introduction was examined
- Care is needed to avoid e-learning being seen as a substitute for hands-on clinical skills training

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in preclinical, content-heavy modules

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical contextualisation of basic science knowledge
- examination performance in content-heavy subjects
