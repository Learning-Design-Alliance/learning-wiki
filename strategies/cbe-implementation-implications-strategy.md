# When implementing computer-based exams, communicate benefits, invest in facilities and test-taking functionality, and prepare technical-failure protocols

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's practical implications for universities implementing computer-based exams: communicate to students that "Student performance on multiple choice question exams does not appear to vary across test mode" along with the benefits of CBE; "invest in good CBE exam facilities" including more test-taking functionalities; and maintain protocols so students can complete the exam on a different computer or on paper if technical failure occurs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Good CBE exam facilities; test-taking functionalities; backup protocols for technical failure
#### Constraints
- Derived from a single-course field experiment; the teacher/faculty acceptance perspective was outside the study's scope

### Target Learners
- higher-education students in proctored high-stakes exams

### Target Learning Goals
- fair and acceptable high-stakes assessment across administration modes
