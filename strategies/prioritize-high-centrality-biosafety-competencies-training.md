# Prioritize training in specimen collection, pathogen harm assessment, medical record management, and antimicrobial resistance to strengthen nurses' biosafety incident response.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using network centrality findings to focus training and curricula on the highest-influence and bridge competencies. It states: "Prioritizing training in specimen collection (P4), harm assessment of pathogens (M3), medical record management (D5), and antimicrobial resistance (K5) could directly improve patient outcomes, reduce nosocomial infections, and mitigate occupational exposure risks." It also suggests simulations or workshops on monitoring (M4), evacuation protocols (D8), and interdisciplinary collaboration (K1).

## Design Implications

#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on cross-sectional, self-reported, convenience-sampled data; the authors call for training programs and longitudinal studies to validate the targets

### Target Learners
- Clinical nursing staff
- Nursing managers

### Target Learning Goals
- Enhancing biosafety incident response competence, particularly specimen collection, pathogen harm assessment, medical record management, and antimicrobial resistance knowledge
