# Incorporate simulator training into cardiovascular surgery curricula with independent resident access

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "Simulator training should be incorporated into the curriculum of cardiovascular surgery programs and residents should be offered access to this modality to practice independently," arguing this "increases opportunities for learning and motor skill development." It is grounded in the study's finding that both junior and senior residents significantly improved skills and anastomosis times on the low-cost simulator, and in the authors' argument that the operating room no longer offers ideal early training opportunities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a low-cost portable simulator and recorded, mentor-scored practice sessions using a standardized OSATS modified scale
#### Constraints
- The article notes the transition from simulator to surgery on real patients "has not yet been extensively studied" in its setting for coronary anastomosis

### Target Learners
- Cardiothoracic surgery residents
- Newly appointed surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition of coronary anastomosis operative skills and reduction of anastomosis time
