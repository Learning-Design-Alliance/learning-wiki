# Use pseudoscientific movie scenes as the context for teaching physics concepts and scientific skepticism

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (3 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy presents science through the window of pseudoscience: instructors show clips from films depicting supernatural or pseudoscientific claims, then use the scene as a background for analyzing the relevant physics and for modeling skeptical, evidence-based reasoning. As the authors put it, "the common belief that ghosts live in cold places provides the opportunity to discuss temperature and heat transfer (among other physics topics)". In their Sixth Sense example, a scene showing a thermostat drop from about 72 oF to 56 oF leads into conduction, convection and radiation, and into the Hampton Court Palace investigation where concealed, non-draught-proof doors explained reported cold spots and apparition experiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Films whose topics contradict scientific facts, which the authors label pseudoscientific for course use
- Instructors who maintain a skeptic attitude on any extraordinary topic requiring extraordinary proof
- Selected popular readings on pseudoscience, such as The Demon-Haunted World and Why People Believe Weird Things
#### Constraints
- Within the schedule of a normal course only a limited selection of topics is possible

### Target Learners
- college non-science majors in general-education physical science courses

### Target Learning Goals
- physics concepts such as temperature and heat transfer
- critical thinking and the scientific method
- attacking widely held misunderstandings and misconceptions
