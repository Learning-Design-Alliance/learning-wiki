# Structure chatbot teacher-training activities as find a right and a wrong answer, guide improvement with questions, then reflect

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The pilot activity asked pre-service teachers to engage with ChatGPT as part of training coursework using a three-part task: "(a) find at least one physics question that the chatbot would get right and at least one that it would get wrong, (b) use guiding questions to make it improve the unsatisfactory responses, and (c) reflect on how the dialogue went". This structure fostered high engagement, collaborative sharing of strategies, and a natural progression from navigating chatbot responses to deeper reflective discussion comparing chatbot answers with those typically given by students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the chatbot (participants used freely available ChatGPT-3.5)
- Careful question selection, since the chatbot is stronger on factual than conceptual questions
#### Constraints
- Interactions can become overly exhausting when the chatbot repeatedly fails to grasp complex or abstract questions

### Target Learners
- pre-service physics and mathematics teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Socratic questioning
- critical evaluation of AI-generated physics content
- identifying productive and unproductive aspects of chatbot responses
