# Frame disclosure of AI involvement as an instructional prompt rather than a disclaimer

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper draws a practitioner implication from its finding that transparency operated as an instructional intervention: "transparency about AI involvement may be more than an ethical obligation. It may be a pedagogical strategy in its own right, one that prompts students to engage critically with feedback rather than accepting it passively." Instructors considering AI-assisted feedback are advised to "frame disclosure not as a disclaimer but as an instructional prompt." This positions disclosure as a trigger for metacognitive and ethical reasoning about evaluation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor must be using or considering AI-assisted feedback; disclosure must explicitly name the AI as the source of feedback and score
#### Constraints
- Grounded in one qualitative study of 13 students in a Saudi computing course; the authors call the interpretation tentative pending controlled designs

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- critical engagement with AI-generated feedback; metacognitive and ethical awareness of assessment
