# Provide Opportunities for Success

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy deliberately sequences and calibrates instruction so that learners experience authentic, earned success early and often. Success experiences are the most powerful source of self-efficacy — learners' beliefs about their own capability — which in turn predict persistence, effort, and achievement [Bandura, A. (1977). Self-efficacy: Toward a unifying theory of behavioral change. *Psychological Review, 84*(2), 191–215](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.2.191) [+S]. The strategy is not grade inflation or lowered standards: success must be *earned* through calibrated challenge, adequate support, and high expectations for work quality, or it produces no durable efficacy gain.

## Design Implications

Early success builds efficacy beliefs that sustain motivation through later difficulty, but only when the success is attributable to the learner's own effort and strategy rather than to task triviality or instructor generosity [Schunk, D. H. (1991). Self-efficacy and academic motivation. *Educational Psychologist, 26*(3–4), 207–231](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.1991.9653133) [+M]. The design problem is therefore challenge calibration: tasks must be within reach with support ([Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md)) yet require genuine work. Mastery-learning structures operationalize this by allowing time to vary while holding achievement standards constant [Kulik, C.-L. C., Kulik, J. A., & Bangert-Drowns, R. L. (1990). Effectiveness of mastery learning programs: A meta-analysis. *Review of Educational Research, 60*(2), 265–299](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060003265) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Diagnostic knowledge of each learner's entry point, so initial tasks sit in the zone of proximal development ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md))
- Task decomposition into achievable steps with [Practice](../elements/practice.md) at each stage ([Part-Task Practice](../elements/part-task-practice.md) reduces load for novices [Part-task practice reduces load for novices.](../claims/part-task-practice-reduces-load-for-novices.md) [+M])
- [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) that is specific and action-oriented, focused on the task and process rather than the person [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- A mechanism for revising and resubmitting work, so a first failure is a waypoint rather than a verdict

#### Constraints
- Success on trivially easy tasks does not build efficacy and can signal low expectations [-M] — learners discount successes they attribute to task simplicity rather than ability [Schunk, D. H. (1991). Self-efficacy and academic motivation. *Educational Psychologist, 26*(3–4), 207–231](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.1991.9653133)
- Unearned praise or inflated evaluation undermines the attribution link between effort and outcome, and learners rapidly detect it [-M]
- If support is never faded, learners attribute success to the scaffold rather than to themselves; [Fading](../elements/fading.md) is required for efficacy to transfer [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]
- Premature exposure to high-stakes failure before any success experience can produce entrenched low efficacy that is difficult to reverse [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Mastery learning**: hold the standard constant, vary time and support until each learner demonstrates criterion performance [Kulik, C.-L. C., Kulik, J. A., & Bangert-Drowns, R. L. (1990). Effectiveness of mastery learning programs: A meta-analysis. *Review of Educational Research, 60*(2), 265–299](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060003265)
- **Graded task sequencing**: begin with simplified or part-task versions and progressively increase complexity and independence
- **Micro-goals**: break large projects into short, completable units so success frequency is high ([Achievable Micro-Goals](../strategies/achievable_micro-goals.md))
- **Revision-based success**: allow drafts and resubmission so the "success" is the final improved product, not the first attempt

### Target Learners
- Struggling or anxious learners with low prior efficacy, for whom early success experiences are the strongest lever on motivation [Bandura, A. (1977). Self-efficacy: Toward a unifying theory of behavioral change. *Psychological Review, 84*(2), 191–215](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.84.2.191) [+S]
- Novices, who benefit from process-focused goals and calibrated early tasks [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]
- Learners needing [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md) — the same standard may require different pathways to reach it

### Target Learning Goals
- Motivational outcomes: self-efficacy, persistence, and willingness to attempt difficult tasks
- Procedural and skill fluency, where successive approximation through calibrated practice builds competence
- Long-term retention in mastery-oriented programs, where criterion-referenced success is required before progression [Kulik, C.-L. C., Kulik, J. A., & Bangert-Drowns, R. L. (1990). Effectiveness of mastery learning programs: A meta-analysis. *Review of Educational Research, 60*(2), 265–299](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060003265)

### Instructions
1. Diagnose entry points with a low-stakes [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) or diagnostic [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) before assigning tasks.
2. Decompose the first unit into steps the learner can realistically complete with support ([Part-Task Practice](../elements/part-task-practice.md)); ensure the first task is achievable but not trivial.
3. Provide [Scaffolding](../elements/scaffolding.md) and model the target performance ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)) so success is attainable through effort, not luck.
4. Give specific, action-oriented [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) at the task and process level [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S], and require revision until work meets the standard.
5. Fade support and increase challenge as competence grows, so later successes are clearly attributable to the learner [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].
6. Frame goals in terms of process and progress rather than comparison with others [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M].

## Examples
- **Mastery learning programs** (e.g., Bloom's "Learning for Mastery" implementations): learners continue with corrective instruction and parallel [Practice](../elements/practice.md) until they meet the criterion, guaranteeing a success experience before progression [Kulik, C.-L. C., Kulik, J. A., & Bangert-Drowns, R. L. (1990). Effectiveness of mastery learning programs: A meta-analysis. *Review of Educational Research, 60*(2), 265–299](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060003265).
- **Khan Academy** (https://www.khanacademy.org) — mastery-based progression gates new content behind demonstrated proficiency, with hints and [Adaptive Difficulty](../elements/adaptive-difficulty.md) keeping tasks in the achievable range.
- **Writing workshop models** (e.g., Lucy Calkins' Units of Study) — short daily writing with teacher conferencing and revision cycles, so every learner produces a polished published piece each unit.
