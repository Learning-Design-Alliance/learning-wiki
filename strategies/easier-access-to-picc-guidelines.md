# Hospitals should provide easier access to PICC care guidelines, such as purchasing clinical guideline databases and training on guideline use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article found that the presence of institutional PICC guidelines was associated with higher knowledge scores and recommends that "hospitals provide easier access to guidelines, such as purchasing clinical guideline databases and providing training on how to access and use guidelines effectively". Guidelines are described as serving not only as procedural references but also as educational tools that reinforce evidence-based practices through repeated exposure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional availability of PICC care guidelines in the workplace
#### Constraints
- In tertiary hospitals the guideline association was significant (B = 2.140, p = 0.047), but not in secondary/primary hospitals

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses in hospitals providing PICC services

### Target Learning Goals
- Evidence-based PICC care practices including aseptic technique and infection surveillance
