# Sequence ASD intervention design so that behavioral and emotional pattern understanding precedes skill development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends a staged intervention approach for supporting learners with ASD: first establish a baseline understanding of each child's behavioral and emotional patterns through longitudinal monitoring, then build applications, technical aids, and skill-development activities on that foundation. The authors state that effective skill development should rest on "a deep understanding of each child's behavioral and emotional landscape". In this article the strategy is a framing recommendation; the study itself operationalizes only the early identification step via emotion-recognition models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal monitoring of emotions and behaviors to establish an individual baseline
- Evidence-based mapping of identified needs before selecting interventions
#### Constraints
- The article provides the framework and rationale but does not report learner outcome data from implementing the full sequence

### Target Learners
- children with Autism Spectrum Disorder, particularly in Information Technology education contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- learning and soft-skill development built on accurately recognized emotional and behavioral needs
