# Use direct workplace observation with immediate feedback to evaluate and reinforce faculty supervision performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends assessing faculty supervisory performance through direct observation in real clinical settings rather than relying solely on self-report. Trained observers completed a checklist during clinical rounds without interfering, and "faculty members received feedback on the strengths and weaknesses of implementing the clinical supervision model" at the end of the rotation. The authors suggest "performance assessment tools and direct/indirect observation be used to evaluate program impact on supervisor and student performance" in future programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- trained observers with checklist training and a pilot to establish reliability
- consent and a randomized observation schedule so observations occur naturally
- a mechanism for delivering feedback to faculty after observed rounds
#### Constraints
- the article lists participants' workload, reliance on self-reported data, air pollution and viral disease prevalence, and faculty reassignments as limitations of the study context

### Target Learners
- clinical faculty members acting as supervisors in teaching hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- applying clinical supervision behaviors in real workplace rounds
- continuous improvement of supervisory performance through feedback
