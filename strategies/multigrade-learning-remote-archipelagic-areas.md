# Implement multigrade learning to address teacher shortages in remote and archipelagic areas, grouping similar grade levels

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Multigrade learning assigns one teacher to provide learning to two or more study groups from the same or different levels, with level similarity as the reference: lower grades 1, 2, and 3 can be combined, as can grades 4, 5, and 6. The article states that in a multigrade class "one teacher will provide learning to two study groups (groups) or more that come from the same or different levels". It is adopted in remote/archipelagic areas forced by limited teacher numbers, to secure equality of opportunity for educational services.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Priority for multigrade teachers to get guidance and training opportunities
- Guarantee of accurate teacher data in Dapodik to avoid data errors that can harm teachers
#### Constraints
- Teachers' abilities are not evenly distributed
- Requires more effective time management and greater teacher creativity
- Curriculum achievement levels are often incomplete
- Lack of respect for individual differences among students

### Target Learners
- Students and teachers in remote and island areas of South Bangka Regency

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining educational service access where teacher shortages prevent single-grade classes
