# Integrate planning, monitoring and reflecting strategies systematically into early-childhood lesson design

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that proactive control strategies "should be systematically incorporated into early educational practices". Concretely, teachers articulate learning objectives and demonstrate problem-solving plans on mini-maps at lesson start (planning); children create individual plans and discuss them in groups; a rotating monitor role checks commands and reflects on errors while placing stickers for completed subtasks. The control condition deliberately omitted these elements, allowing a direct test of the strategy package.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Roles and phases must be structured so monitoring and reflecting occur during activity (a monitor checks each command and marks completed subtasks)
#### Constraints
- The article tested this package in one kindergarten with 46 children; the small sample warrants validation with a larger sample

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten children aged 5–6 learning introductory coding

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained attention during learning activities
- Emotional stability during learning
- Coding skills (Assignment, Type, Loop, Decomposition, Function, Algorithm)
