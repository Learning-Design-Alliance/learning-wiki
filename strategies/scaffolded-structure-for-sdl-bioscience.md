# Provide proactive support and curricular structure for students with self-reported learning difficulties and for all students facing SDL overload

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For students with self-reported dyslexia or ADHD, the article recommends accessible learning materials, assistive technologies, and tailored SDL supervision including "use of planners, chunking of tasks, and shorter focused study intervals". Bioscience courses relying heavily on SDL should add structure through detailed study plans, intermediate milestones, regular formative feedback, and reflection opportunities; making core concepts explicit and sequencing content transparently may reduce overload.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Bioscience courses that rely heavily on SDL should incorporate more structure through detailed study plans, milestones, and formative feedback
#### Constraints
- The dyslexia and ADHD accounts are self-reported, unverified, and from a small number of students in one setting

### Target Learners
- Nursing students with self-reported dyslexia or ADHD, and first-year nursing students generally

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained engagement in self-directed learning of bioscience content
