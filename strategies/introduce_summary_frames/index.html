Introduce Summary Frames¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 12 studies (4 quant-synthesis, 3 causal, 3 review, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 3 of 12 report an effect size

Description¶

Learners read a section of text to themselves before verbally summarizing the passage to a partner or completing sentence frames. Frames are generic text-structure templates — e.g., "_ wanted , but __, so _" for problem/solution text, or " happens because __" for cause/effect — that prompt learners to identify main ideas and the logical connectives that hold a passage together. The strategy combines independent reading, structured oral rehearsal, and generative restatement, all of which force the learner to construct rather than merely recognize meaning.

Design Implications¶

Summarizing is a generative task: producing a condensed restatement requires selecting main ideas, deleting redundancy, and building a coherent macro-structure, which is exactly the processing that improves comprehension and retention Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding. [+M]. Frames scaffold this by supplying the rhetorical skeleton of a text type, so working memory is spent on content rather than on inventing a structure Chunking reduces working memory load. [+M]. Oral summarizing to a partner adds a comprehension check: breakdowns in the partner's understanding surface gaps the summarizer must repair.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A text chunked into summarizable sections, matched to learners' reading levels (Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax)
  • Sentence frames matched to the actual text structure of the passage (problem/solution, cause/effect, compare/contrast, sequence)
  • A partner or small-group structure for oral summaries, with norms for listening and questioning
  • Instructor modeling of a completed summary before learners attempt their own

Constraints¶

  • Over-reliance on frames produces formulaic summaries that list frame slots without genuine condensation; learners may fill in blanks mechanically rather than select main ideas [-M]
  • Frames matched to the wrong text structure distort comprehension — forcing a cause/effect frame onto a descriptive passage teaches a false macro-structure [-M]
  • For learners with strong summarization skill, frames add little and can constrain more capable restatements Guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M]
  • Frames alone, without feedback on summary accuracy, can entrench inaccurate main-idea selection; feedback should target the summary's content and structure Feedback is most effective at task and process levels. [+M]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Oral-first: partners alternate summarizing sections aloud; frames serve as speaking prompts
  • Written frames: learners complete sentence frames individually, then compare with a partner
  • Fading: begin with full frames, move to word-bank prompts, then to unframed summaries as skill develops
  • Reciprocal format: pairs alternate roles of summarizer and questioner, as in reciprocal teaching
  • Disciplinary variants: frames reflect discipline-specific structures (lab report claim–evidence–reasoning; historical cause–consequence chains)

Target Learners¶

  • Developing readers and multilingual learners who benefit from explicit language scaffolds for academic discourse [+M]
  • Novices in a discipline who do not yet know its conventional text structures
  • Less valuable for advanced readers who already summarize flexibly Guidance that helps novices can become redundant or counterproductive as expertise grows. [~M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading comprehension: identifying main ideas and text structure
  • Summarization skill: condensing, selecting, and organizing information
  • Disciplinary literacy: internalizing the rhetorical patterns of academic language
  • Oral academic language production through structured rehearsal

Instructions¶

  1. Select a text and divide it into coherent sections; identify each section's dominant text structure.
  2. Model a summary using the frame, thinking aloud about how you selected main ideas (Coaching).
  3. Learners read the next section independently (Practice).
  4. Learners summarize orally to a partner using the frame, or complete it in writing; partners ask clarifying questions (Class Discussion).
  5. Provide feedback on accuracy and completeness of main ideas, then fade the frame over successive sections.

Related Strategies¶

  • Reciprocal Teaching — pairs summary frames with prediction, questioning, and clarifying roles in a structured dialogue
  • Think-Pair-Share — the oral-summary-to-partner step is a summarizing variant of this routine
  • Annotating — margin notes made during reading supply raw material for the frame slots (Annotating)
  • Teach students to use frame slot questions as a reading, studying, and self-monitoring tool

Related Elements¶

  • Practice — each read-then-summarize cycle is a spaced practice opportunity
  • Coaching — instructor modeling and corrective feedback during frame completion
  • Class Discussion — whole-class comparison of summaries sharpens main-idea judgments

Patterns That Use This Strategy¶

  • Cognitive Apprenticeship — the modeling step makes expert summarizing visible before learners attempt it
  • Direct Instruction — frames function as the guided-practice scaffold between modeling and independent work

Examples¶

  • Reciprocal Teaching of reading (Palincsar & Brown, 1984) — seventh graders with poor comprehension learned to summarize paragraphs within a dialogue routine; comprehension gains of roughly two standard deviations on assessments were sustained at follow-up.
  • Sentence frames in sheltered/SIOP instruction — content-area teachers provide frames such as "The data show that _, which suggests ___" so multilingual learners rehearse disciplinary language while summarizing lab results.
  • Reading Apprenticeship (WestEd) — disciplinary teachers use structured summary routines and metacognitive conversation starters during content-area reading (https://readingapprenticeship.org).
