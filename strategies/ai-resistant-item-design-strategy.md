# Design AI-resistant assessment items targeting the Perception-Cognition Gap and Calculation-Conceptualization Discrepancy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "educators and assessment experts can actively utilize atypical “Schematic Misinterpretation (1.2)” items, such as Questions 13 or 18", which require interpreting scientific rules symbolized by signs or shapes beyond simple x-y graphs. It further suggests multi-step items where procedural calculation is only the first step and the scientific meaning of the result must be reapplied, and items where hasty application of background knowledge leads to incorrect answers so the correct answer requires strict verification of visual data. Such design aims to prevent AI misuse and ensure academic integrity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Items must require interpretation of symbolic rules in atypical schematics or reapplication of calculation results to scientific meaning, beyond information retrieval or simple calculation
#### Constraints
- Grounded in vulnerabilities observed for three specific LLMs on one exam subject; the article calls for verifying whether these error patterns appear universally across other science subjects and LLMs

### Target Learners
- upper-secondary science examinees
- educators and assessment experts

### Target Learning Goals
- multimodal scientific reasoning
- data interpretation and concept application
- academic integrity in the AI era
