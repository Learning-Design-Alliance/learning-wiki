Mediate cognitive strategies by encouraging systematic, reflective problem-solving behaviour during one-to-one mediation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

During the MLE intervention the mediator works one-to-one on the processes that impair performance: "the mediatees were encouraged to develop effective thinking strategies, refrain from impulsivity, be precise and systematic in gathering of data, clearly identify and define problems, devise a plan of action and avoid trial-and-error responses, look for logical evidence and reflect before responding." The approach is process-oriented rather than content-focused, and the mediator intervenes as much as needed for the learner to complete the tasks. Aspects covered include regulation of behaviour, developing accurate perception, production of reflective and analytic insight, and provision of appropriate verbal tools.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A mediator who observes the learner's cognitive deficiencies (impulsivity, failure to follow instructions, lack of planning) so intervention can target them
  • Addressing emotional issues first, since many learners were highly anxious and hesitant to respond

Constraints¶

  • The article states the intervention was of short duration (30 to 45 minutes per learner) and suggests a longer-term programme is needed for more permanent cognitive modifiability

Target Learners¶

  • Remedial school learners with learning difficulties in Grades 4 to 7

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Self-regulated, reflective problem-solving
  • Accurate perception and systematic data gathering
  • Analytic insight into task requirements

Affordances¶

  • Feuerstein Mle Dynamic Assessment Framework

Claims¶

  • The pre-test score gap between the experimental and control groups narrowed from pre-test to post-test in favour of the experimental group [+W]
  • Within the experimental group, RCPM scores improved significantly from pre-test to post-test following MLE intervention, while the control group showed no significant improvement [+W]
  • ANCOVA on post-test RCPM scores found no statistically significant difference between the experimental and control groups [-W]
