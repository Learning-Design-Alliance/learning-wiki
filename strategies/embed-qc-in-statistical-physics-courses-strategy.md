# Embed quantum computing modules into existing statistical physics courses rather than teaching quantum computing in isolation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend integrating quantum computing into the undergraduate curriculum through familiar statistical physics topics, so students "draw meaningful comparisons and deepen their conceptual understanding". The modules are adaptable: instructors may include them in a statistical mechanics course, a quantum computing elective, or a modern physics sequence, scaled up or down by available time and student preparation. Advanced extensions include the transverse-field Ising model for quantum annealing, connections to Grover's algorithm, and running circuits on real hardware via IBM's cloud interface.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Jupyter notebooks with Qiskit and the Aer simulator
- instructor guidance framing the transition from classical to quantum as a progression
#### Constraints
- Full 2D lattices exceed what most quantum simulators or teaching setups can handle without the layered simplification

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- quantum intuition and coding skills
- rediscovering core statistical physics ideas from a quantum perspective
