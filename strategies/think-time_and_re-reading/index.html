Think-Time and Re-Reading¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q3–q4 · 1 of 2 report an effect size

Description¶

Sufficient time is allowed for learners to process information and re-read materials if needed. In discourse, this takes the form of deliberate wait time — pausing several seconds after posing a question before calling on anyone. In study contexts, it means permitting and structuring re-reading of texts, problems, and feedback rather than forcing immediate responses.

Design Implications¶

Extended wait time after questions increases the length and quality of student responses and the number of voluntary contributions Wait time improves the quality of classroom discourse. [+S]. However, re-reading is one of the weaker study strategies on its own: rereading a text produces modest gains compared with retrieval practice or elaborative strategies Rereading is less effective than retrieval-based study strategies. [~M]. The strategy therefore works best when think-time is paired with an active task — a prompt to annotate, predict, or formulate an answer — rather than passive repetition Rereading is less effective than retrieval-based study strategies. [-M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A classroom or course norm that silence after a question is productive, not a failure
  • Genuinely open questions worth thinking about; wait time after low-level recall questions adds little
  • Access to materials for re-reading (texts, recorded explanations, worked problems)
  • A follow-up structure that converts thinking into a response (Class Discussion, written response, or Check-In)

Constraints¶

  • Unstructured extra time invites disengagement; some learners stop attending when nothing is required of them [-M]
  • Re-reading familiar text creates fluency-based illusions of knowing — learners mistake ease of re-reading for mastery Rereading is less effective than retrieval-based study strategies. [-S]
  • Extended time on timed assessments changes what is being measured; accommodations must be deliberate (Accommodations)
  • Whole-class wait time can feel uncomfortable for instructors, who tend to cut it short after under one second without training Wait time improves the quality of classroom discourse. [~M]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Wait time 1 (after the question) vs. wait time 2 (after a student responds, before the teacher reacts) — both matter
  • Think-pair-share: individual think-time followed by peer rehearsal before public response
  • Structured re-reading: second pass with a specific purpose (annotating, answering a guiding question) rather than verbatim repetition
  • Extended-time accommodations on assessments for learners with documented processing-speed needs

Target Learners¶

  • Learners with slower processing speed or working-memory constraints, for whom forced-pace questioning produces guessing rather than reasoning Cognitive overload degrades learning outcomes. [+M]
  • Second-language learners and anyone processing in a non-native language
  • Anxious learners, for whom immediate cold-calling suppresses performance
  • Less beneficial as unstructured re-reading for learners who already use stronger strategies like self-testing Rereading is less effective than retrieval-based study strategies. [~M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Deeper comprehension and reasoning rather than rapid recall
  • Quality of oral and written contributions in discussion
  • Self-regulated study habits — knowing when a second pass is useful and how to structure it

Instructions¶

  1. Pose a question and silently count 3–5 seconds before accepting any response; do not rephrase or answer your own question Wait time improves the quality of classroom discourse.
  2. After a student responds, pause again before evaluating or redirecting.
  3. For texts and problems, assign a purposeful second pass — Annotating or answering a guiding question — rather than "read it again."
  4. Replace some re-reading with self-testing or closed-book recall, which produces more durable learning Rereading is less effective than retrieval-based study strategies. [+S]
  5. Build extended-time options into assessments where processing speed is not the construct being measured (Accommodations).

Related Strategies¶

  • Activating Prior Knowledge — think-time gives learners the space to retrieve relevant prior knowledge before new input
  • Chunking — breaking material into units makes each think-time pause productive rather than overwhelming

Examples¶

  • Think-Pair-Share — a standard active-learning routine: 30–60 seconds of silent thinking, brief pair discussion, then whole-class sharing; the pair step converts private think-time into a low-stakes rehearsal.
  • Extended-time testing accommodations — most university disability services grant 1.5× time for documented processing-speed or ADHD-related needs.
  • Purposeful re-reading in reading instruction — a second read of a science text with the task of marking claims vs. evidence, converting repetition into analysis.
