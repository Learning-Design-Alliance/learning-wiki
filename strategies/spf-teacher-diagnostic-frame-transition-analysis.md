# Teachers use SPF as a methodological guide: analyze students' frame transitions and provide targeted feedback during problem-solving activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's implementation section recommends embedding SPF in classroom problem-solving activities as a metacognitive guide aligned with learning goals. "Instructors are encouraged to analyze students' reasoning and frame transitions, enhancing their ability to provide targeted feedback and scaffold expert -like strategies", with attention to why and how specific frames connect to preceding and following frames. Recordings of students' verbal and non-verbal work can be analyzed with SPF categories with researchers, and the framework is also used to cultivate students' self-assessment in a final check phase.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration with researchers to analyze recordings of students' verbal and non-verbal work using SPF categories
- Text-rich, real-world problems to strengthen the link between abstract concepts and lived experiences
#### Constraints
- The implementation guidance is presented before empirical validation of the framework

### Target Learners
- physics teachers
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- representational coherence across linguistic, pictorial, and symbolic registers
- metacognitive self-assessment of reasoning
