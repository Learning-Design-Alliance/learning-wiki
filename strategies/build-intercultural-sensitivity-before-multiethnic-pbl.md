# Develop students' intercultural sensitivity and use rational subgrouping before and during multiethnic PBL group learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends preparing students for multiethnic PBL by building intercultural sensitivity before small-group work, since sensitivity was a stronger positive predictor of interaction than composition, and by having tutors manage group design. It states "one effective strategy would be to develop students' cultural sensitivity before they engage in small group learning", noting formal intercultural competency training is scarce in Chinese medical schools. Tutors should apply subgrouping principles and handle culturally driven tensions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A formal intercultural competency curriculum or preparatory course for undergraduates, including international students, before entry into multicultural learning environments
#### Constraints
- Causal inference is not warranted by the study's cross-sectional design; the recommended training programs were described as scarce or absent, so effectiveness was not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- Second-year medical undergraduates in multiethnic PBL groups, including international students

### Target Learning Goals
- Quality of collaborative group interaction (exploratory questioning, cumulative reasoning, conflict handling) in problem-based learning
