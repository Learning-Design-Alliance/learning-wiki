Teachers introduce portfolios in language classes so students regularly self-assess and become aware of their learning process¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Drawing on the study's findings, the authors recommend that "Teachers can introduce portfolios in their classes to help students be aware of their learning process", noting that "Portfolio provides opportunities for the learners to assess themselves regularly". The strategy involves having learners collect writing samples using target language, obtain teacher and peer feedback, reflect, and revise, so that documented work shows improvement over time and gives learners greater control and ownership of their learning.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A portfolio collection routine with teacher feedback and peer assessment opportunities
  • Time across multiple sessions for learners to revise and archive revised work

Constraints¶

  • The authors call for further research to examine the effectiveness of using portfolio in teaching and learning idioms in different contexts

Target Learners¶

  • language learners
  • EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • idiom learning
  • writing development
  • self-regulated awareness of the learning process

Claims¶

  • Portfolio assessment is followed by a statistically significant posttest difference in idiom knowledge between Iranian upper-intermediate EFL learners who kept portfolios and those who received traditional instruction [+M]
