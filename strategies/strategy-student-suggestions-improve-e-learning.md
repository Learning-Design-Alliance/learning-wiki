# Collect student feedback via online surveys and act on it: prioritize live video-lectures over posted presentations, manageable task loads, and extended thesis deadlines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Thematic analysis of the survey's open-ended responses turned student feedback into concrete improvement measures for online teaching. Of 2520 students, 913 (36%) responded on how e-learning could be improved; the most common suggestion was to "provide more online lectures instead of posting the text of presentations (N = 118; 13.0%)", followed by fewer tasks and assignments, more teacher engagement, and online exams. On theses, students most commonly suggested extending deadlines and enabling online thesis defense.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions should interact with their students via online surveys to obtain feedback that enables them to improve delivery of online education and organizational matters, as the authors recommend.
#### Constraints
- Suggestions were self-reported by 36% of the sample (913 students) in the specific context of the pandemic emergency switch to exclusive e-learning.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate university health sciences students (nursing, obstetrical nursing, physical therapy, radiologic technology, diagnostic laboratory medicine)

### Target Learning Goals
- effective delivery of theoretical health sciences course content online
- timely completion of final/diploma theses during disrupted conditions
