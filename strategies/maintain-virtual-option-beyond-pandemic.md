# Maintain a virtual option for research programs and conferences beyond the pandemic to broaden accessibility

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that student support programs keep a virtual option open even after in-person activities resume, because remote formats enable participation by students excluded by personal, financial, or medical circumstances. They advise "programs keep a virtual option open, even beyond the pandemic when in-person functions can resume", including maintaining virtual-platform licenses and infrastructure for recording and archiving in-person events, which they say will broaden accessibility long-term.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Maintaining a license for virtual platforms such as Zoom
- Introducing infrastructure for recording and archiving in-person events
#### Constraints
- The authors note unique situations will still arise and their recommendations may alleviate but not remove all barriers to engagement by marginalized peoples

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate researchers from underrepresented and underserved groups in STEM

### Target Learning Goals
- access to research experiences and professional development
- retention of marginalized students in STEM career paths
