Discipline-based educational development initiatives for academics' advising of self-learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that disciplinary variation in explanation and advice-giving was substantial, the authors recommend that educational development initiatives address disciplinary considerations. They also argue that because student-focused academics advised more deep learning strategies, "development initiatives that focus on approaches to teaching might be of particular benefit," and that academics "need to undergo further educational development to become experts on how students learn," since advised strategies focused on resource management rather than deep learning strategies.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Initiatives must be targeted to the specific disciplines and their epistemological characteristics

Constraints¶

  • The article notes the effects were small and the study had low statistical power due to sample size

Target Learners¶

  • university academics across hard and soft disciplines

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving academics' advice on students' self-learning strategies, especially deep learning strategies

Claims¶

  • Soft-discipline academics give reasons for self-learning containing fewer external motivators than hard-discipline academics [+W]
  • Soft-discipline academics write longer email answers and advise more resource management strategies than hard-discipline academics [+W]
