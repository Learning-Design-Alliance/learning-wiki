# Virtual patient interventions should be repeated and sustained rather than delivered as one-off sessions, since empathy declines during clinical training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends moving beyond single-session delivery toward repeated and temporally distributed virtual patient interventions. It states that "Empathy in medical students tends to decline during clinical training, indicating the need for not one-off interventions, but rather, repeated and sustained educational strategies." It draws on evidence from other VR-based domains where distributed practice enhanced retention and skill acquisition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Repeated and sustained implementation of virtual patient-based interventions rather than single-session formats
#### Constraints
- Further studies are needed to determine the optimal frequency and duration of virtual patient-based programmes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained empathic engagement in clinical practice
