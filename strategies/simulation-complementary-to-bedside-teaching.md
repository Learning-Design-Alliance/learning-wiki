# Integrate simulation, case-based learning and self-directed methods as complements to bedside teaching rather than substitutes for direct patient contact

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that the demand for modern teaching methods observed among both students and lecturers be met through balanced integration: innovative forms such as simulation and case-based learning are valued but 'should be viewed as complementary rather than a substitute for bedside teaching, which remains essential for learning'. The observed demand for simulation is therefore interpreted as support for combining modern educational approaches with direct patient contact, not replacing it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clinical settings must retain direct patient contact and bedside teaching as the core of clinical education while adding simulation, case-based learning and self-directed approaches.
#### Constraints
- The article states further research is required to establish any direct relationship between innovative methods and relief of structural space pressures.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in clinical years

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical skills such as history taking, physical examination, clinical reasoning and decision making
