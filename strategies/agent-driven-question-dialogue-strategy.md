# Use agent-driven question dialogue to challenge mathematical thinking and role-model learner behaviour

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using an agent-driven question dialogue with three purposes: "to challenge students to reflect on why they play as they do; to support transfer of tacit game playing knowledge to articulate language; and to provide a role model of questioning to imitate." The agent acts as an ideal learner posing deep, explanatory questions under the pretext that it wants to know. Question difficulty follows the game performance level of the human tutor to challenge without demotivating.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions must be slightly above the agent's knowledge level
- Response alternatives prompt young tutors with ideas
#### Constraints
- If progression stops, the advancement of questions stops as well

### Target Learners
- primary school students grades 1-6

### Target Learning Goals
- deep-level reasoning
- self-explanation
- mathematical inquiry
