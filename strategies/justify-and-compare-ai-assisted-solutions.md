# Require students to justify AI-assisted solutions and compare AI-generated with self-developed code

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because self-directed GenAI use alone did not produce measurable gains on AI-free assessments, the article recommends instructional activities that promote deeper engagement, such as "requiring students to justify AI-assisted solutions or compare AI-generated and self-developed code". This fits its broader recommendation that educators encourage explanation-seeking, critical evaluation of AI outputs, and reflection on problem-solving processes rather than relying on unguided, task-oriented interactions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional support that promotes responsible, thoughtful, and ethical use of AI, since simply allowing access is unlikely to improve outcomes
#### Constraints
- The article's evidence is observational and self-reported, so the strategy's effectiveness is a recommendation, not a tested result in this study

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate programming students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical evaluation of AI-generated code and deeper problem-solving engagement in OOP
