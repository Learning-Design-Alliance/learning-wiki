# Quantum program enhancements: nationally funded projects that augment existing study programs with shared quantum content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Rather than creating new standalone master's programs, nations fund projects that "develop content that is then used to augment many different study programs with additional quantum experience", across bachelor's, master's, and PhD levels. The article names this approach "Quantum program enhancements" and presents DigiQ as the flagship example, developing shared QT courses used by 16 different master's programs. The authors argue such enhancements "can be a far more efficient solution to offer more interdisciplinary, hands-on QT education, while minimising expenses associated with setting up new programs entirely from scratch".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- National funding for community-led projects developing educational programs, courses, internships, and collaborative projects
#### Constraints
- Joint programs such as those in the Erasmus Mundus framework are limited in feasibility by costs and national accreditation procedures

### Target Learners
- bachelor's, master's, and PhD students in STEM disciplines

### Target Learning Goals
- acquiring Quantum Technology knowledge and skills within existing degree programs
