# Develop mandatory national airway-management training: advanced ETI courses for anesthetists and non-invasive airway courses for pediatricians

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a three-part national response to the skill gap: an advanced pediatric airway management course for general anesthetists, regional training facilities run by tertiary centers, and a national course for pediatricians focused on "non-invasive maneuvers to guarantee a free airway, mask-and-bag ventilation and the introduction of a supraglottic airway device." Both course types should be mandatory during residency and for re-registration, with local skill training and assessments to counter skill decay.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Attendance at national courses and local skill training programs and assessments to maintain adequate skill, since skills decay when not frequently used or refreshed
#### Constraints
- The article states it is of no use to train general pediatricians in performing ETI, since they cannot gain the practical airway-management experience needed

### Target Learners
- general anesthetists and anesthesia residents
- pediatricians and pediatric residents in general hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- neonatal and pediatric endotracheal intubation skill maintenance
- non-invasive airway management, bag-mask ventilation, and supraglottic airway device skills
