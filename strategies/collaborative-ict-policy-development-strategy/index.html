Strategies

Develop ICT school policy collaboratively with teachers, train principals, and separate technical from curriculum-focused ICT support¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that only teachers' perceptions of policy matter for classroom ICT use, the article recommends that principals develop a more collaborative approach when defining ICT policy, engaging teachers so they share the policy's values and understand its implications. It further recommends that "training of principals should be a priority", citing evidence that more principal training accompanies more school-level ICT integration, and that schools "distinguish between technicians providing schools and teachers with technical support, next to ICT coordinators that focus primarily in educational support".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must be aware of the ICT plan's content for it to influence classroom practice
  • Principals need training to develop and articulate a shared vision about ICT use in collaboration with the school community

Constraints¶

  • ICT integration in Flanders was not yet compulsory, which principals cited as limiting their directive role; ICT coordinators' time constraints restrict curriculum-related support

Target Learners¶

  • school principals
  • ICT coordinators
  • primary school teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • integration of ICT into classroom teaching and learning

Affordances¶

  • Five Areas Local Ict Policy Framework

Claims¶

  • Teachers' perceptions of school ICT policy predict classroom ICT use, whereas principals' perspectives show no significant effect [+M]
  • School-level ICT policy factors (ICT plan, support, training) are significantly associated with teachers' classroom use of ICT in Flemish primary schools [+M]
  • Local ICT school policies in Flemish primary schools are often underdeveloped and underutilised [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use the HeDiCom framework as a common reference and blueprint for ICT policy planning, with indicators to measure teachers' competencies
  • Use the four-pattern ICT-use taxonomy to design ICT training programs aligned with students' actual technology use
