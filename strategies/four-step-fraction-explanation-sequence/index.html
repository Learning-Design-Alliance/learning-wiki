Four-step scaffolded explanation sequence for comparing fraction magnitudes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 2 of 2 report an effect size

Description¶

In the explaining component, tutors modeled a 4-step problem-solving sequence, gradually transferring responsibility to students while scaffolding. "In the first step, students wrote whether the fractions had the same denominator (“same D”), the same numerator (“same N”), or different numerators and denominators (“both diff”)." Students then explained why a good drawing shows equal units correctly divided and shaded, labeled each drawing with its numerical value, and in the final step "wrote a short sentence or phrase to explain why the circled fraction was greater." Tutors provided corrective feedback as needed.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Tutor modeling with gradual transfer of responsibility, scaffolding, and corrective feedback; students need background knowledge of numerators, denominators, and equivalent fractions.

Constraints¶

  • Schema-based instruction was deliberately not incorporated: students did not classify problems into problem types prior to formulating explanations.

Target Learners¶

  • at-risk fourth-grade students learning fraction comparison

Target Learning Goals¶

  • writing high-quality conceptual explanations of fraction magnitude differences

Claims¶

  • Supported self-explaining produced higher-quality explanations of fraction magnitude differences than the word-problem condition (ES = 0.93) [+W]
  • At-risk fourth graders taught supported self-explaining outperformed the word-problem condition on fraction magnitude comparison accuracy (ES = 0.43) [+W]
