# Repeat clinical skills assessments with formative feedback to build self-efficacy and buffer the negative effects of assessment anxiety

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's closing recommendation: repeat performance-based clinical skills assessments followed by formative feedback, so students accumulate authentic success experiences and accurate feedback that raise self-efficacy. The authors conclude that "repeated exposure to clinical skills assessments followed by formative feedback may improve self-efficacy and moderate the negative effects of OSCE-related anxiety," and that despite being stressful, the OSCE can provide meaningful learning experiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formative feedback after the assessment so students learn which skills need improvement
#### Constraints
- The moderation mechanism itself was not statistically tested in this study; the strategy is the authors' interpretation, and excessive stress can interfere with demonstrating competence

### Target Learners
- physical therapy and health-professions students in clinical skills courses

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical communication and reasoning skills
- self-efficacy for clinical performance
