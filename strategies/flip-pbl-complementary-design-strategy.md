# Use flipped classroom pre-class materials to offset PBL's preparation burden and small-group PBL discussion to add structure to flipped classrooms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that flipped classroom and PBL complement each other and recommends combining them: "pre-class teaching materials in flipped classrooms provided by instructors can save students time for preparation and lay a good foundation for the development of teaching in PBL; on the other hand, the small-group, teacher-oversight module in PBL can also make teaching more efficient." The strategy operationalizes this by pairing instructor-curated pre-class media with in-class problem-based small-group discussion under teacher oversight, as implemented in the hyperthyroidism sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must prepare relevant lecture videos and supplementary materials before class
#### Constraints
- The article notes some students in flipped classrooms suggested reduced instructor contact could negatively affect them without increased faculty direction and support

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in clinical internships

### Target Learning Goals
- Strengthen clinical case-analysis and problem-solving skills alongside theoretical knowledge
