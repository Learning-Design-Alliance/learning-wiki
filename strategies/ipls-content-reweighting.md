# Re-weight IPLS content: de-emphasize projectile motion, constant-acceleration rotations, gravitation, and heat engines; give new prominence to fluids, optics, energy, and entropy; add scaling, strength of materials, and gradient-driven flows

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Based on inquiries to biologists, including an AAMC committee and the authors' own colleagues, the article recommends topics "should be de-emphasized including projectile motion, rotations with constant acceleration, Newton's law of gravitation, and heat engines," while fluids, optics, energy, and entropy gain new prominence and scaling, strength of materials, gradient-driven flows, and molecular-to-macroscopic descriptions are considered for inclusion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Know your students and what physics is likely to be useful to them; topics should have authentic biological applications and contribute to a coherent and cohesive story line for the physics
#### Constraints
- Biologists' needs are sub-discipline specific — cellular biologists need entropy and free energy, organismal biologists need forces and torques, population biologists need feedback loops — so the authors urge physicists to champion all students' needs

### Target Learners
- biology majors, increasingly concentrated in biochemistry, genetics, molecular biology, and cell biology

### Target Learning Goals
- physics content with authentic biological applications
