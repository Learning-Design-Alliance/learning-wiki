# Use validated simulators to widen access to Aquablation training where clinical cases and robotic systems are scarce

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating the validated simulator as a training station into structured basic Aquablation programs because "Considering the expense and limited clinical availability of the AquaBeam® system, validated simulators could enable wider access to training and assist in standardizing technique acquisition across various centers." The strategy positions simulation as a safe, standardized setting for residents to build confidence in fundamental technical aspects without patient-related risk, supported by CTA and expert consensus within ESU-led structured training and competency evaluation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Support by CTA and expert consensus, such as those led by the ESU, including structured training and competency evaluation
#### Constraints
- The simulation does not encompass intraoperative difficulties such as bleeding and variable tissue resistance, which the model is unable to demonstrate

### Target Learners
- Urology residents in early training stages

### Target Learning Goals
- Building confidence in fundamental technical aspects of Aquablation without patient-related risk
