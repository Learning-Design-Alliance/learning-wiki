# Implement structured mentorship programs and formal leadership development initiatives for healthcare professionals in LMIC settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends formal training initiatives, "such as structured mentorship programs," to foster collaborative and resilient healthcare systems in low- and middle-income countries. Leaders themselves called for "more structured mentorship programs that allow younger professionals to learn leadership skills in a supportive way." Leadership development should focus not only on individual competencies but also on creating the enabling environment for effective, collaborative healthcare delivery.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentorship should be structured and supportive rather than purely experiential
#### Constraints
- Initiatives must be tailored to the cultural and organizational realities of LMIC settings, where hierarchical norms persist

### Target Learners
- healthcare leaders and younger professionals in LMIC healthcare systems

### Target Learning Goals
- leadership competencies, participatory behaviors, and non-technical skills development
