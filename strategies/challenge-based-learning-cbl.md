# Challenge Based Learning (CBL)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 19 studies (9 review, 5 theoretical, 3 causal, 2 quant-synthesis), `q1`–`q4` · 3 of 19 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Challenge Based Learning (CBL) is a collaborative, inquiry-driven framework in which learners identify a real-world challenge, investigate it through guided research, and act by developing and implementing evidence-based solutions. The framework moves through three interrelated phases — **Engage** (moving from a big idea to a concrete, learner-owned challenge), **Investigate** (guided and student-directed inquiry), and **Act** (designing, implementing, and evaluating solutions) — with documentation and reflection throughout. CBL was developed and piloted by Apple with educators and is documented in the *Challenge Based Learning White Paper* (Nichols & Cator, 2009; Apple Inc., 2011).

## Design Implications

CBL situates learning in authentic problems, which raises perceived relevance and task value — a key driver of engagement and persistence [~M]. Because learners work on whole, complex problems rather than isolated subskills, CBL supports transfer of knowledge to novel situations [~M]. Its effectiveness, however, depends on scaffolding: unguided inquiry alone tends to underperform explicit guidance for novices, so CBL implementations must pair learner autonomy with coaching, structure, and just-in-time instruction [~S]. The productive-struggle character of the Engage and Investigate phases can also deepen conceptual learning when followed by structured consolidation [Productive failure improves conceptual learning relative to direct instruction followed by practice.](../claims/productive-failure-improves-conceptual-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- An authentic challenge connected to learners' world, framed during [Challenge Identification](../elements/challenge-identification.md)
- Collaborative team structures and norms with defined roles and individual accountability
- Access to information sources, experts, and tools for investigation
- Time — CBL typically spans weeks, not single sessions
- Ongoing instructor [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and formative checkpoints rather than front-loaded lecturing
- A genuine, open-ended challenge connected to a "big idea" that learners can act on locally
- Collaborative team structures with defined roles and shared artifacts ([Collaboration](../elements/collaboration.md))
- Access to real-world resources, data, experts, or community partners
- Sustained time blocks; CBL typically spans weeks, not single sessions

#### Constraints
- Novices without adequate scaffolding experience high extraneous load and flounder in open-ended problem spaces; guided inquiry outperforms pure discovery, especially for learners with low prior knowledge [~S]
- The time cost is substantial; coverage-driven curricula and high-stakes standardized assessment schedules frequently crowd out the Act phase, reducing CBL to a simulation of action [-W]
- Group work without structured roles and accountability can produce social loafing and uneven learning across team members [-M]
- Assessment systems built for individual content mastery are a poor fit; misaligned grading undermines the collaborative, iterative character of the work [-W]
- When the challenge is too far from learners' prior knowledge, initial engagement collapses; the big idea must connect to something learners can activate [~M]
- Weakly scaffolded versions collapse into unguided discovery, which is ineffective for novices [Minimal-guidance instruction underperforms scaffolded instruction for novice learners.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [-S]
- Deep content coverage can suffer; CBL must be deliberately aligned to curriculum standards or key concepts get skipped [~M]
- Group work without structure and individual accountability allows free-riding and uneven learning [~M]
- Assessment is difficult to standardize; success depends on authentic rubrics and process evidence, not exams alone

#### Implementation Variability
- **Scale**: from a single unit within a course to a whole-school program
- **Locus of the challenge**: teacher-provided big ideas with learner-derived challenges, or fully learner-identified challenges
- **Act phase depth**: awareness campaigns, prototypes, policy proposals, or implemented community interventions
- **Subject integration**: single-subject CBL or interdisciplinary implementations
- **Scale**: full-semester challenges vs. short 2–3 week mini-challenges
- **Locus of the challenge**: teacher-nominated big idea with learner-defined challenge questions (typical entry point) vs. fully learner-generated challenges
- **Domain**: science (environmental audits), civics (community campaigns), media/technology (app or prototype design)
- **Hybrid forms**: CBL phases interleaved with direct instruction on needed concepts, blending [Explicit Teaching](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) with inquiry

### Target Learners
- Middle school through adult learners; the framework was originally piloted in secondary and higher education
- Learners with enough prior knowledge to conduct guided inquiry productively; for complete novices, front-load direct instruction before opening the investigation [~M]
- Learners who benefit from autonomy and authentic purpose; autonomy-supportive designs strengthen intrinsic motivation [Autonomy support fosters intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]
- Learners whose confidence needs building — successful challenge completion and public action can strengthen persistence beliefs [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Learners with moderate prior knowledge who can conduct guided investigation without drowning in search [Whole-task practice improves transfer of complex skills.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [~M]
- Adolescents and adults; younger learners need heavily simplified challenge structures and more facilitator scaffolding
- Learners disengaged by abstract instruction — authentic purpose and audience increase persistence [Learners invest more effort when they perceive a task as relevant and valuable.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Less suitable as a first exposure for complete novices, who need foundational [Procedural Information](../elements/procedural-information.md) before open investigation

### Target Learning Goals
- Deep subject-matter knowledge applied to authentic contexts
- 21st-century competencies: collaboration, communication, critical thinking, and self-direction
- Problem identification and solution design — defining a problem well, not just solving a given one
- Civic and community engagement through implemented action
- Collaboration and communication: negotiating roles, producing shared artifacts
- Research and information literacy: gathering, evaluating, and synthesizing evidence

### Instructions
1. **Engage** — Present a big idea (e.g., water, health, sustainability); through essential questioning, learners narrow it to a specific, actionable challenge ([Challenge Identification](../elements/challenge-identification.md))
2. **Commit** — Teams adopt the challenge and develop a guiding question
3. **Investigate** — Conduct guided then self-directed research: background reading, data collection, interviews, and fieldwork; instructors provide [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and just-in-time content instruction
4. **Analyze** — Teams synthesize findings and identify evidence-based solution directions
5. **Act** — Design, implement, and document a solution; connect to [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) where possible
6. **Reflect and Assess** — Document the process, evaluate impact, and debrief learning against both content and competency goals
