# Implementing the predict-observation-explain (POE) sequence inside a virtual laboratory

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
POE is a constructivist strategy in which students first make a prediction about a prepared event, then observe the event while performing the experiment, and finally explain by resolving conflicts between predictions and observations. In the VCL this was implemented as: before beginning, students select a prediction about the subject and record the data; during the experiment they observe every detail while playing an active role and recording data; at the end, the software supports comparison of students' prior knowledge with the experiment result. The article reports this structure helped the experimental-group teacher create a constructivist learning environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher guides students instead of explaining contradictions directly and helps them make alternative interpretations
- Appropriate environment is prepared for students to take notes during observations
#### Constraints
- POE achieves its goal only when the teacher guides rather than explains, per the article's cited conditions

### Target Learners
- 9th-grade secondary school chemistry students

### Target Learning Goals
- Activating prior knowledge and resolving conflicts between predictions and observations
- Active participation in scientific experimentation
