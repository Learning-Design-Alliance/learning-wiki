# Anchor oral-health content longitudinally across the medical curriculum with a learning spiral and interdisciplinary formats

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the study's findings of low knowledge and short-term one-off effects, the authors recommend integrating more oral health topics across years, possibly as a learning spiral enabling repeated uptake and deepening from preclinical to clinical years, plus additional lectures, tutor training, and joint courses with dental students. They cite U.S. expert recommendations to “maximize touchpoints” throughout undergraduate education, include skills training, and focus on oral-systemic connections and bi-directional learning with dentists.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration with dental lecturers
- Careful weighing of costs and benefits against competing curricular demands
#### Constraints
- Oral health topics compete with a broad range of other demands in an increasingly complex medical world and may lead to cutbacks in the existing curriculum

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students
- Dental students in joint interdisciplinary formats

### Target Learning Goals
- Oral-systemic health knowledge
- Interprofessional collaboration competencies
