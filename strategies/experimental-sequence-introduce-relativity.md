# Sequence free-fall measurement, photocell timing, and incline analysis to introduce relativity concepts in elementary courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy begins with a student free-fall experiment using conventional clocks, followed by a photocell-detector laboratory measurement whose data deviations motivate the Hidden Time discussion, then an inclined-brick timing analysis from which time dilation and Lorentz contraction analogues are derived. The author reports the main topics of relativity "can b e treated in elementary undergraduate courses with considerable success", attributing this to the experimental approach plus the element of surprise.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A laboratory setup with passage photocell detectors connected to a chronometer
- Students submit a technical report after the first free-fall activity
#### Constraints
- The historical-experiments approach used elsewhere is stated to be out of scope for introductory courses because students lack background beyond secondary school physics

### Target Learners
- first-year university Electrical Engineering students
- potentially secondary school students, per the author's suggested extension

### Target Learning Goals
- understand time dilation, proper time and Lorentz contraction analogues
- connect experimental data analysis to theoretical models of motion
