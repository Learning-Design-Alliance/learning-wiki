# Teaching strategies to prevent and manage negative emotions in learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Participants offered practical strategies to reduce the onset of negative emotions, mostly within teaching staff responsibility. These included "the use of humor and other teaching methods to foster interest in the classroom," showing care and concern, encouraging peer connections and help-seeking, providing frequent and timely feedback to reduce anxiety and confusion, being available to students, giving clear instructions, and dispersing assessment tasks throughout semester to prevent overload. Students' self-regulation via reflective practice and cognitive reappraisal was also highlighted.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires teaching staff to take responsibility for emotional climate and provide timely feedback, availability and clear instructions
#### Constraints
- Strategies emerged from participant perceptions in one university context and have not been empirically tested in this study

### Target Learners
- university students
- academic faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- managing negative emotions to support learning
- reducing anxiety, confusion and boredom
- promoting student self-regulation of affect
