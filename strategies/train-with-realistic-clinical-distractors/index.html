Medical educators should account for incidental emotions during clinical training by incorporating realistic clinical distractors into simulation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that simulation training include realistic clinical distractors that elicit negative incidental emotions, since the studies finding emotion effects on performance focused on incidental rather than integral emotions. It cites Feuerbacher et al., who "employed four common clinical distractors during a cholecystectomy simulation" and found most errors occurred during distraction exposure. Incorporating such training could help trainees develop coping strategies for real-world settings.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Simulation scenarios must include external stressors or distractors unrelated to the clinical task, such as noise or challenging tasks.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • medical trainees
  • healthcare professionals in acute care

Target Learning Goals¶

  • managing incidental emotions during clinical performance
  • developing coping strategies for distracting conditions

Claims¶

  • Clinicians' emotional responses to patients impacted patient engagement and diagnostic decisions, with negative emotions associated with poorer quality care and positive emotions with more comprehensive care [+W]
  • Non-procedural skills scores during simulated CPR were significantly lower when external stressors were present [+W]
  • Conflict with patients' family members emerged as a common emotional trigger in end-of-life care, associated with negative emotions among physicians and nurses [+W]
  • Fear and clinical uncertainty were associated with more cautious clinical decisions, including more admissions, more diagnostic tests, and more antibiotic prescriptions [+W]
  • Paramedic trainees randomized to a low-stress condition calculated drug doses more accurately than those in the high-stress group [+W]
  • Negative emotions, most commonly stress, were inversely correlated with clinical performance in some simulated or educational acute care settings [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Future research should explore emotion management strategies and exposure to emotionally evocative scenarios to support clinician performance and well-being
