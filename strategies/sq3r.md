# SQ3R

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
SQ3R is a study-reading sequence developed by Francis Robinson (1946): **Survey** the text (headings, summaries, figures), **Question** (turn headings into questions), **Read** to answer those questions, **Recite** (self-explain or retrieve key points without looking), and **Review** (re-check and consolidate after a delay). It converts passive reading into an active, goal-directed cycle built around pre-questions and retrieval.

## Design Implications

SQ3R's active components align with well-supported learning science: generating questions before reading focuses attention and improves comprehension [~M], and reciting/reviewing functions as retrieval practice, one of the most robust techniques in the literature [+S]. However, the full five-step ritual is not uniformly superior to simpler active-reading variants; evidence for the complete package is weaker than for its parts [~W].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Expository text with navigable structure (headings, summaries) — surveying depends on it
- Time for the full cycle; SQ3R roughly doubles reading time versus straight reading
- Learner training in question-generation and self-recitation, ideally with modeling ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md))

#### Constraints
- Effects on comprehension are small and inconsistent when compared with reading plus any active task; much of the benefit comes from the added time-on-task rather than the specific sequence [~W]
- The full procedure imposes considerable overhead; for narrative text or short passages the survey and question steps add little [~M]
- Learners often abandon the multi-step routine without scaffolding or accountability; partial adoption (skipping Recite) strips out the most effective component [-M]
- For learners with strong prior knowledge, surveying and pre-questioning can be redundant [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **SQ4R** adds a "Reflect" step (relate to prior knowledge, elaborate)
- **PQ4R** (Thomas & Robinson, 1972) substitutes "Project" framing; functionally equivalent
- Reduced variants — survey + retrieval only, or question-generation only — often match the full sequence at lower cost [~M]
- Digital adaptations embed the steps as prompts in reading platforms (e.g., Perusall annotation prompts)

### Target Learners
- Middle school through college students reading dense expository material (textbooks, technical reports)
- Struggling readers who default to passive word-by-word reading; the structure externalizes study planning
- Less useful for early readers still decoding, or experts who already self-regulate reading effectively

### Target Learning Goals
- Comprehension and retention of expository text
- Study-skill and self-regulation development: planning, monitoring, and reviewing one's own reading
- Preparation for assessment on text-based material

### Instructions
1. **Survey** — skim headings, bold terms, figures, and summary before reading; functions as an [Advance Organizer](../elements/advance-organizers.md)
2. **Question** — convert each heading into a question to be answered; write questions down
3. **Read** — read actively, seeking answers to the questions; annotate selectively ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md))
4. **Recite** — close the text and state key points aloud or in writing from memory (retrieval)
5. **Review** — return after a delay (same day, then spaced), re-answer the questions, and check against the text

## Examples
- **College study-skills courses** (e.g., university learning-center curricula) teach SQ3R as the canonical textbook-reading routine for introductory survey courses.
- **Robinson's original context (1946, Ohio State)** — designed for military and college personnel reading technical manuals.
- **Khan Academy / MCAT prep guides** — structured preview-question-read-review cycles for dense science passages.
