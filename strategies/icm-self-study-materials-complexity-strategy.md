# Ensure self-study materials for inverted-classroom teaching are not too complex and avoid redundancies with classroom phases

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An implementable design guideline for inverted-classroom teaching drawn from the module's evaluation: materials made available before the attendance phase must be manageable for independent study, and content overlap between self-study and classroom phases should be minimized for efficiency. The article states "it should be ensured that the teaching materials made available beforehand are not too complex to be understood by the students on their own and that redundancies between the contents of self-study and classroom phases are avoided for reasons of efficiency". Otherwise, the authors warn, the ICM will not make much sense.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be able to acquire the necessary declarative knowledge on their own in the self-study phase
#### Constraints
- If students cannot acquire the declarative knowledge alone due to motivation deficits or overly complex or time-consuming materials, the ICM will not make much sense

### Target Learners
- higher-education students in blended or inverted-classroom courses

### Target Learning Goals
- independent acquisition of declarative knowledge prior to classroom application phases
