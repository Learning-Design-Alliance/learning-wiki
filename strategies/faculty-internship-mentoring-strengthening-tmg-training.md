# Strengthen TMG preparation through faculty improvement, enhanced internships, and post-graduation supervision and mentoring rather than curriculum revision alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (1 for, 5 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's stakeholder recommendations and discussion propose a multi-pronged improvement strategy: interventions "to improve faculty quality including specific efforts to hire full-time, highly qualified faculty members", improved internship components with faculty accompaniment at placement sites and reduced tutor-to-student ratios, plus continuing education, supervision strengthening, and workplace-based assessment with supportive feedback from mentors. The authors argue curriculum revision alone is unlikely to produce desired changes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty preparation and internship quality control must accompany curriculum content changes for training improvements to translate into practice
#### Constraints
- The article notes the Mozambique faculty preparation was limited to a 3-day workshop and few faculty were involved in the 3-year revision, which it judges inadequate compared to intensive efforts in Tanzania and Rwanda

### Target Learners
- Non-physician clinicians (Tecnicos de Medicina Geral) in pre-service training in Mozambique

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical knowledge retention
- Clinical reasoning and case management
- Physical examination skills
