# Use latent motivation signals from short admission responses to guide early mentoring support without extra student burden

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The article recommends that motivation signals extracted from short admission responses could guide early support in selective STEM pipelines. It states: "Practically, these signals could guide early support (e.g., mentorship matching) without extra student burden." The strategy reuses text applicants already produce, avoiding additional surveys or interviews, and is framed for resource-constrained settings and Spanish-speaking contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Short open admission responses already collected by the program
- Text-analysis pipeline (LDA plus small LM embeddings) to surface latent themes
#### Constraints
- Findings are exploratory; the study does not aim to establish predictive accuracy for high-stakes decisions
- Future fairness analyses across demographic subgroups are needed to ensure text-based signals do not amplify existing inequities

### Target Learners
- Secondary and early-undergraduate students in selective STEM pipelines

### Target Learning Goals
- Early identification of students for mentoring and support in rigorous STEM pathways

### Affordances
- Intrinsic Instrumental Motivation Axis Admission Text
