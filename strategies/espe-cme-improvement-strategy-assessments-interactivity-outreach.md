# Improve open-access CME e-learning by adding pre- and postmodule assessments, increasing interactivity and multimedia, and strengthening outreach in underrepresented regions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend a set of forward-looking actions to enhance the platform's impact: developing pre- and postmodule assessments to evaluate knowledge gain, increasing interactivity through multimedia content and case simulations, strengthening outreach in underrepresented regions by partnering with local societies and ambassadors, and exploring long-term impact on clinical practice and patient outcomes. These follow directly from the survey findings that satisfaction was high but no objective learning outcomes were measured and participation from some regions was lacking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Partnerships with international societies such as ESPE and ISPAD and their organizational infrastructure, which several authors emphasize as of critical importance
#### Constraints
- National CME accreditation policies vary widely; some countries may not recognize EACCME credits, reducing the incentive to engage with external platforms
- Language barriers, limited awareness, cultural preferences for in-person education, and technological access and digital literacy may still pose challenges in some areas

### Target Learners
- health care professionals in underrepresented regions
- global CME learners in pediatric endocrinology and diabetes

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring knowledge gain from e-learning modules
- improving engagement and accessibility of CME content
