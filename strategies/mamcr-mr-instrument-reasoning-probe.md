# Use module analysis of multiple-response reasoning instruments as a scalable probe of students' emerging mathematical reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose module analysis of a multiple-response mathematical reasoning instrument in physics contexts as a potential probe to fill the gap left by qualitative case studies, which give rich views of specific students but little characterization across large groups. MAMCR uses community detection algorithms on networks of individual correct responses, preserving the complexity of MR questions that dichotomous scoring ignores. The authors state the coupling of PIQL MR questions with MAMCR shows promise for finding patterns of emergent expertise on a scale qualitative methods cannot achieve.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A multiple-response instrument whose individual correct responses can form a response network; community detection algorithms run repeatedly to ensure module consistency
#### Constraints
- Applied here to networks of only correct responses, ignoring whether students also choose incorrect responses; InfoMap did not yield meaningful results, so multiple algorithms must be compared

### Target Learners
- large groups of introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- characterizing emerging expert-like mathematical reasoning across topics
