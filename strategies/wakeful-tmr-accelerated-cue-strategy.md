# Deliver task-congruent auditory cues during short wakeful rest intervals at a temporally accelerated rate to boost early motor learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy pairs motor practice with task-linked auditory cues replayed during brief wakeful rest intervals, with cue speed set faster than the learner's preceding practice speed. The article reports that "auditory stimuli presented at 1.3x the preceding practice speed during rest intervals improved early motor learning compared to stimuli presented at the same speed," and concludes that "optimizing sensory cue parameters during wakeful rest intervals actively promotes early motor learning." Cues must map onto the learned motor pattern, since random cues showed no benefit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Auditory cues must be congruent with the learned task sequence; random cues failed to induce TMR
#### Constraints
- The 1.3x rate was based on a prior somatosensory stimulation study, and the article notes the modality difference may explain why auditory stimuli at this speed did not produce optimal improvement
- No strictly matched control condition was included to isolate the specific effects of the TMR protocol
- Replay counts were not experimentally fixed and may have influenced outcomes

### Target Learners
- adult learners practicing motor skills

### Target Learning Goals
- early-phase motor skill acquisition / sequence learning efficiency
