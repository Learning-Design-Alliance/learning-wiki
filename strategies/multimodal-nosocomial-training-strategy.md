# Multimodal infection-prevention training combining theory with clinical practice, delivered by qualified internal experts with employer-supported time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that prevention and control of nosocomial infections be pursued with a multimodal strategy rather than knowledge transfer alone, stating that "Education focusing only on the transfer of theoretical knowledge is not very effective and insufficient to change behavior." The WHO-endorsed approach combines educational tools and programs, supervision and control, active staff participation, and leadership commitment. Training should combine theory with clinical practice, be delivered internally by qualified persons who understand nurses' problems, and be backed by employer-allocated time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Nursing staff should have time set aside for training and educational events and employer support in applying knowledge in practice
- Internal training should be carried out by qualified persons with medical education, experts in nosocomial infections, and nurses who understand nurses' needs
#### Constraints
- The article states education correlating with current staff needs is required; the strategy is grounded in the WHO 2009 hand-hygiene multimodal approach rather than tested in this study

### Target Learners
- hospital nursing staff

### Target Learning Goals
- prevention and control of nosocomial infections
- hand hygiene compliance
- post-exposure procedures
