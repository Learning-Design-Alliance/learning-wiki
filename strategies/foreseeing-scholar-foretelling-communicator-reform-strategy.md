# Embed foreseeing within the Scholar role and foretelling within the Communicator role when reforming competency frameworks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that competency framework reforms explicitly place the computation of prognostic estimates under the Scholar role and the disclosure of prognosis under the Communicator role, so that prognosis is articulated under more than one competency role. It states: "It is also recommended that foreseeing should be discussed within the context of the Scholar role while foretelling should be discussed within the context of the Communicator role." This follows from the finding that 80.3% of prognosis citations sat in the Medical Expert role alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Competency framework documents for all specialties and subspecialties must add prognosis content in advance of the CanMEDS 2015 launch
- Both quoad vitam and quoad sanantionem dimensions should be discussed, holding to the Hippocratic two-dimensional definition
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is grounded in a keyword content analysis of CanMEDS 2005 documents, not in an intervention study of curricular reform

### Target Learners
- physicians
- medical specialty and subspecialty trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- objective computation of prognostic estimates
- skillful disclosure of prognosis to patients and substitute decision makers

### Affordances
- Foreseeing Foretelling Prognosis Model
