# Adapt the grading system to rate explanation quality and detected errors, exchanging two unassisted problems for four worked examples

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 4 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 design), `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Where problem-solving performance counts toward grading or exam admission, the article suggests rating students on the quality of their self-explanations and the number of detected errors rather than only counting correct solution steps. The authors report good experience exchanging two problems without assistance for four worked examples of the described types, noting the number of problems usable for rating does not change much.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A rating system adapted to score explanation quality and error detection instead of only correct solution steps
#### Constraints
- If students do not solve all problems themselves and a rating is required, the rating system has to be adapted

### Target Learners
- university physics students in programs with graded problem sets or exam-admission problem quotas

### Target Learning Goals
- serious engagement with self-explanations and error finding within graded coursework
