# Tailor stress interventions by stress type: promote challenging stress and reduce impeding stress

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educational interventions differentiate stress types rather than applying uniform stress reduction. As printed, "educational interventions can focus on different types of stress to tailor strategies, refine existing general stress guidance approaches, promote 'challenging stress' positively correlated with academic achievement, and reduce 'impeding stress' negatively correlated with academic achievement." They also advise building a comprehensive support system covering stress prevention, identification, intervention, and transformation, and fostering Openness as a psychological resource.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires institutions to distinguish stressor types (e.g., professional study stress vs. scientific research, ecological, and employment stress) when designing support
#### Constraints
- Grounded in cross-sectional, self-reported data from one Chinese medical university; the authors note causality cannot be established from this design

### Target Learners
- medical master's (postgraduate) students

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing harmful academic stress and supporting academic achievement
