# Pursue enrollment growth through parallel steps: program information, mathematics-background support, scholarships, assistantships, internships and research involvement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends attacking low physics enrollment with several parallel actions rather than a single fix: "proper information about the program, help in developing a required mathematics background, offering scholarships, teaching assistantship or internships and involving them in research." It pairs these with making programs more interesting through interdisciplinary courses and electives, and with working on retention so enrollment is maintained "without compromising on standards." The strategy treats recruitment, preparation and retention as one coordinated plan.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Accurate program information at the admissions stage, with counselors knowledgeable about the physics program
- Support for students to develop the required mathematics background before or during the program
#### Constraints
- The author warns that enrollment must not be increased at the cost of long-term damage; standards and technical skills cannot be compromised

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science and engineering students
- prospective physics majors
- K-12 students preparing for college physics

### Target Learning Goals
- increasing physics program enrollment
- improving retention of physics majors
- developing mathematics background for physics study
