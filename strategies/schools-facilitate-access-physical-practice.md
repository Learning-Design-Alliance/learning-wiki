# Schools and sports organizations should create active lifestyles and facilitate access to physical practice for children and adolescents

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation is that institutions responsible for youth physical activity should actively enable practice rather than merely note healthy habits. The authors state that "those responsible for public and private organizations, schools, and sports clubs should focus on creating active lifestyles and facilitating access to physical practice at these ages", grounding this in the observed associations between fitness, attention, and academic performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular physical exercise opportunities that raise physical fitness levels, since the article links fitness rather than activity alone to the outcomes
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional and does not establish that increasing fitness causally improves attention or academic performance

### Target Learners
- children and adolescents aged 10–12

### Target Learning Goals
- selective attention and concentration
- academic performance
- health and integral development
