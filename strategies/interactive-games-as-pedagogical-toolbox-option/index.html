Offer interactive digital games like PàN as one option in teachers' pedagogical toolboxes for L2 vocabulary and engagement¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors conclude that interactive games such as PàN can enhance L2 vocabulary learning for some participants and increase student interest and engagement, so they recommend such games as "another viable option for teachers' pedagogical toolboxes". The recommendation is hedged: statistical results showed limited advantage over paper-based gamified activities, so games complement rather than replace existing activities.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Games should involve interactive, meaningful use of vocabulary connected to personal experiences, in a fun and competitive environment

Constraints¶

  • Statistically, digital game-playing showed limited pedagogical value for vocabulary acquisition compared with paper-based gamified information gap activities; benefits were concentrated in some individual learners

Target Learners¶

  • intermediate-level L2 French learners in classroom settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • L2 vocabulary acquisition
  • student interest and engagement in learning

Claims¶

  • Both experimental and control groups improved significantly in French vocabulary over time, with no time-versus-group interaction attributable to the digital game treatment [~W]
  • Descriptive posttest gains favored the PàN group (M=14.24 vs 9.23) but the difference was not deemed significant [~W]
  • Individual differences produced highly variable vocabulary gains among PàN players, with some participants benefiting far more than others [~W]
  • The article identifies creative drama, cooperative learning, educational games, and animations as particularly beneficial vocabulary-teaching methods [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Integrate digital games into the language curriculum to lower affective barriers and encourage L2 interaction
  • Offer high-interest text alternatives and choice alongside short stories to sustain engagement during strategy instruction
  • Pace and vary gamified activities, and offer self-directed options, to mitigate learner fatigue
  • Use gamified information gap activities with time pressure and forced output to promote L2 oral interaction practice
