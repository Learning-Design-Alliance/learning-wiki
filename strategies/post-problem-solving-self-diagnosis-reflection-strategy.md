# Use a written self-diagnosis reflection exercise after group problem solving to help students identify areas of struggle

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
After lab groups complete a context-rich problem and the instructor outlines the solution, students individually write reflections on the areas of the solution they struggled to understand, guided by a rubric adapted from a self-diagnosis study. The article states "A reflection exercise (Yerushalmi, Cohen, Mason, & Singh, 2012) is introduced to help students identify areas of struggle in solving physics problems." The reflections also served the researchers as corroborating data for identifying students' epistemic games and orientation categories.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A rubric adapted from a self-diagnosis study
- weekly lab problem-solving sessions with instructor-outlined solutions
- student willingness to reflect in writing
#### Constraints
- Studied with a single cohort of 39 complete-data students in one IPLS course section pair

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life science and health science majors in introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- metacognitive awareness of problem-solving difficulties
- attitudes toward physics problem solving
