# Use practice-authentic certainty descriptors and question-specific certainty-safety feedback matrices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Deliver MCQs with written certainty descriptors tied to practice behaviour (e.g. whether the student "would need to consult a colleague or references" before responding), and return feedback as a matrix showing each question's topic, certainty and safeness, with specific notice of high-certainty unsafe responses. The authors argue such descriptors are "more authentic to practice" than probabilistic confidence ratings, since clinicians ask whether they need extra information rather than estimating percent likelihood of correctness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Written descriptors for each certainty level must be displayed and practised in advance via the learning management system
- Feedback must be specific to each question's topic so the context-specific nature of certainty is recognised
#### Constraints
- The wording of certainty descriptors can alter responses and therefore meaning

### Target Learners
- medical students in clinical modules

### Target Learning Goals
- reflection-in-action self-monitoring of decision certainty
- recognising when to seek external cognitive support
