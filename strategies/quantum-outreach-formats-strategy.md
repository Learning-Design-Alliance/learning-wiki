# Use varied instructional formats — pictorial learning, games, hackathons, and summer schools — to introduce quantum computing to high-school students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 6 studies (2 design, 1 review, 1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 6 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper catalogues tested formats from open quantum education pilots: pictorial learning conveying abstract ideas intuitively, interactive games such as Quarks Interactive visualizing superposition and entanglement, hackathons blending "hands-on coding with peer collaboration and expert mentorship," and structured summer schools like Qubit by Qubit featuring live instruction, mentorship, and projects. These formats accommodate students from diverse academic backgrounds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to tools, mentors, or structured programs; for hackathons, real quantum devices and simulators
#### Constraints
- Equitable participation is limited by connectivity, time zones, and language barriers

### Target Learners
- High-school and early university learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of quantum principles
- Motivation and hands-on engagement with quantum computing
