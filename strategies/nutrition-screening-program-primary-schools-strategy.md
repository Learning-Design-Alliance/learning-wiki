# Implement nutrition screening programs and health–education sector collaboration to improve primary school academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation is that government should "paid attention to implement nutrition screening program and intervention strategy to improve academic performance at primary school children", and the Results state that "collaboration between the health and education sectors is required to alleviate the problem". The strategy pairs routine anthropometric screening of school children with joint health–education sector action on undernutrition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Government commitment to implement nutrition screening programs in primary schools
- Collaboration between the health and education sectors
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional and cannot establish cause and effect between nutritional status and academic performance

### Target Learners
- Primary school children (ages 7–10) in Debre Markos Town, northwest Ethiopia

### Target Learning Goals
- Improve academic performance among primary school children
