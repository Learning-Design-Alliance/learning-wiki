# Use truly longitudinal matched data when investigating instructional impact across multiple courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that studies of instructional impact spanning multiple courses collect matched longitudinal data rather than relying on pseudo-longitudinal course averages. Its central implication is "to underscore the importance of truly longitudinal data when investigating the impact of instruction across multiple courses," because pseudo-longitudinal averages can reflect selection rather than instruction. The study demonstrates the two designs diverging: pseudo-longitudinal averages rose while matched data showed no cumulative instructional effect.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Matched pre- and postinstruction responses for individual students across the courses being compared, as the authors obtained by matching via student ID and name.
#### Constraints
- The article notes previous studies found matching trends between longitudinal and pseudo-longitudinal data, so the divergence is not universal and should not be assumed in either direction.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics majors in required lab courses

### Target Learning Goals
- students' beliefs, attitudes, and expectations about experimental physics
