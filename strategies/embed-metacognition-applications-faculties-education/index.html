Embed metacognition-based applications across faculties of education during pre-service teacher training¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that applications based on metacognition be carried out at faculties of education, including all departments and levels, because training received as prospective teachers appears to shape teachers' later use of metacognitive skills. They state: "it is suggested that the applications ba sed on metacognition should be carried out at the faculties of education by means of including all departments and levels." They also call for longer-term experimental research and mixed qualitative-quantitative methods.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Extending the practice beyond a single course to all courses at the faculties of education

Constraints¶

  • The present study lasted only ten weeks and used a single course, so longer-term experimental researches are recommended

Target Learners¶

  • prospective teachers across departments and levels of faculties of education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • metacognitive awareness and metacognitive skill development in pre-service teachers

Claims¶

  • Under current training, the control group improved significantly only on the questioning sub-dimension, with no significant change on other sub-dimensions or the scale overall [+W]
  • Within the experimental group, metacognitive awareness increased significantly from pre-test to post-test on the planning, awareness and questioning sub-dimensions [+W]
  • A metacognition-based instructional practice produced higher post-test metacognitive awareness than current training in planning, awareness, regulation and questioning sub-dimensions and scale-wide among prospective teachers [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Integrate metacognitive strategy instruction across content areas and across before, during, and after phases of teaching
  • Foster autonomy and curiosity-driven learning by embedding metacognitive processes and self-regulation elements into education
