# Use interactive eBooks as an adjunct to traditional teaching rather than a substitute

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend combining eBooks with standard teacher-led classes so that classroom learning through an expert is complemented by "additional independent directed interactive learning outside the classroom". The eBook's interactive and test-based features give students more practice opportunities, automated marking reduces teacher load, and students can resolve learning deficiencies in their own time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- eBook materials with interactive tests and automated marking; institutional willingness to author eBooks (a 9-hour teacher workshop in this study)
#### Constraints
- The article states that in some cases eBooks could not substitute classical teaching; developing good e-learning materials can be resource intensive and the investment must be justified by the outcome

### Target Learners
- medical students and interns

### Target Learning Goals
- self-directed practice and mastery of domain-specific identification skills
