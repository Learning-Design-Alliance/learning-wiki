# Combine CL-based cognitive explanations with DDL concordance work when teaching polysemous spatial prepositions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating corpus linguistics with CL pedagogy rather than using either alone. It argues that "DDL by itself may not be sufficient as a pedagogical approach to effectively teach the senses of spatial prepositions despite the rich context in which learners are exposed to  various uses and can thus learn through discovery", while CL-only instruction on decontextualized sentences would be, in the article's words, a gross injustice to language as a living entity. The combination supplies both motivated semantic explanation and rich authentic context.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cognitive explanations grounded in a principled polysemy network
- Access to a corpus with a concordance interface and learner training in using it
#### Constraints
- The article notes corpus data alone lacks explanatory adequacy for preposition senses, and prior DDL-only work was criticized as lacking unifying insight about relations among uses

### Target Learners
- L2 learners of English, especially those whose L1 lacks one-to-one preposition correspondences

### Target Learning Goals
- acquiring and retaining the spatial and figurative senses of polysemous prepositions

### Affordances
- Principled Polysemy Model Tyler Evans
