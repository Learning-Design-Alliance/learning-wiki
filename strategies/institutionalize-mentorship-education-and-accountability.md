# Institutionalize mentorship education, accountability constructs, and environmental evaluation across the STEM ecosystem

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper's recommendations section synthesizes systemic actions: institutionalize formal, culturally aware mentorship education using evidence-based methodologies from NASEM, CIMER, and NRMN; evaluate environments with multi-stakeholder research-based tools beyond employee satisfaction surveys; and set specific constructs of accountability. It recommends to "consider asking mentors to develop a detailed mentorship plan to communicate objectives, incentivizing faculty to complete training on effective mentorship, and even tying promotion or tenure criteria to mentorship activities". Assessment of plan outcomes is suggested for department heads, deans, and provosts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated time, resources, and funds for mentors to develop mentoring plans; commitment from federal and private funding agencies
#### Constraints
- Accountability causes tension amongst mentors, though the paper argues it is necessary

### Target Learners
- STEM faculty mentors
- graduate students and early-career researchers underrepresented in STEM

### Target Learning Goals
- culturally aware mentoring
- structured, outcome-focused mentoring relationships
