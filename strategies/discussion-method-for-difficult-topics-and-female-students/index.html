Integrate discussion method into teaching of perceived difficult chemistry topics to support female students' performance¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proffers two linked recommendations: teachers should effectively employ the discussion method in a way that helps improve female students' performance in chemistry, since mean achievement scores significantly favoured males; and teachers should make conscientious efforts to integrate discussion method into the teaching of perceived difficult topics in chemistry, which the article says will help in diffusing the abstractness of most chemical concepts.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers adopting a facilitator role and encouraging learners to discover things for themselves

Constraints¶

  • The gender gap was measured only in this study's two groups; the article itself calls for more topics to be tested to compare findings

Target Learners¶

  • Female senior secondary chemistry students; students facing perceived difficult topics

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved chemistry achievement and reduced difficulty with abstract chemical concepts

Claims¶

  • Boys outperformed girls in chemistry achievement in both the discussion and lecture groups [-W]
  • Discussion method produced higher chemistry achievement than lecture method in a quasi-experiment with SSII students [+W]
