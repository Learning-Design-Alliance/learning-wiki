# Involve teenagers younger than 16 as peer teachers in particle physics outreach

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A strategy of involving teenagers younger than 16 as peer teachers in the outreach programme, building on peer teaching "defined as a learning situation in which a student from the same peer group as the learners functions as a teacher". The programme encourages teenage peer teachers to "revise presentations, deliver lectures, and provide assistance in masterclasses and workshops". The majority of peer teachers are young females, which the authors argue demonstrates strong potential to inspire and motivate other girls and address gender disparities in physics engagement, while showing fellow students that participation in particle physics does not depend on age or level of education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Motivated teenagers younger than 16 willing to serve as peer teachers, who revise presentations, deliver lectures and assist in events
- Facilitation by researchers or undergraduate instructors to maintain a non-judgmental atmosphere of academic collaboration
#### Constraints
- Success of the interventions often hinges on the presence of supportive STEM infrastructure within schools; schools lacking such support are less responsive

### Target Learners
- Teenagers younger than 16 as peer teachers
- Students aged 10-15 as workshop participants

### Target Learning Goals
- Spark early interest in physics and demystify particle physics before the age-15 subject choice, especially for girls
