Apply PBL selectively in Chinese radiology education, balancing its benefits against time and resource costs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), q1–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that PBL "should be appropriately applied according to the current situation of Chinese education, teaching contents of radiology subject, and students’ specific circumstance." It reports that most Chinese schools used PBL for less than 50% of total clinical curricular hours, and medical schools need to balance PBL's positive effects against the cost of teaching time and resources.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • balance between the positive effects of PBL and the costing of teaching time and resources

Constraints¶

  • PBL takes too much time; the teacher-oriented traditional system is hard to change in a short time; some students participate negatively or only superficially, limiting their knowledge acquisition

Target Learners¶

  • medical students in Chinese medical universities or colleges

Target Learning Goals¶

  • radiology theoretical knowledge and clinical skills

Claims¶

  • PBL improves radiology skill (film reading) scores more strongly than theoretical scores (pooled SMD = 2.10) [+W]
  • PBL improves theoretical knowledge scores in Chinese radiology education compared with traditional teaching (pooled SMD = 1.20) [+W]
  • Questionnaire surveys in most included studies indicate positive PBL effects on learning interest, scope of knowledge, team spirit, and oral expression [+W]
  • PBL score advantages hold in both probationer and intern subgroups, with larger effects among probationers [+W]
  • Applying PBL widely in Chinese pediatric medical education is constrained by time demands, student knowledge-structure difficulties, and faculty and financial resources [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use a combined teaching model mixing PBL with lecture-based and digital teaching, adapted to the local setting
  • Exercise caution when selecting a single teaching method; consider disciplinary characteristics and student demographics, and weigh combined approaches
