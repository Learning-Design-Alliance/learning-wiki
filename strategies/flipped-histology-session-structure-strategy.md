# Flipped-classroom histology session structure: pre-class electronic content followed by in-person group problem-based microscopy with the instructor as facilitator

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy operationalizes the flipped classroom for a laboratory-based basic-science course. "Electronic content for the histology course was initially provided to students before holding the in-person class"; in the laboratory, students then "collaborated in groups and engaged in problem-based activities using microscopes and glass slides", with the instructor serving solely as a facilitator and mentor. Each group received exactly 16 sessions of 90–120 minutes with the same instructor and textbook, and both groups sat an identical concurrent final exam.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Electronic content prepared and distributed before each in-person session
- In-person laboratory sessions with microscopes and glass slides for group problem-based activities
- Instructor role redefined as facilitator and mentor rather than lecturer
#### Constraints
- The authors note results should be interpreted with caution given the nature of the methods compared, and recommend pre-test/post-test designs in future research

### Target Learners
- Medical students in preclinical histology courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Histology concept understanding, self-directed learning, and collaborative application of knowledge in laboratory settings
