# Combine instructional video viewing with hands-on practice on a 3D-printed procedure-specific model before live performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's training recipe: all groups first viewed a standard instructional video in which "A senior attending surgeon performed and explained the surgical steps"; the 3D group then completed five hands-on sessions on the procedure-specific 3D-printed model. This combination of video instruction plus anatomically faithful hands-on practice produced the best transfer performance of the three tested regimens.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standard training video demonstrating the operative steps, and access to a 3D-printed model of the target procedure for repeated hands-on sessions
#### Constraints
- Tested only in surgeons without prior biliary-enteric anastomosis experience, at a single centre, with transfer assessed on swine organs rather than human patients

### Target Learners
- Junior or general surgeons learning a complex laparoscopic procedure

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition and transfer of procedural skills for laparoscopic biliary-enteric anastomosis
