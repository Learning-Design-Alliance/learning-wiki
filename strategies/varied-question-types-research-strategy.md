# Use varied research question types, including asking students about answers they would not naturally choose, to access knowledge not revealed by select-and-justify tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that researchers and instructors gather information about student ideas by varying the types of questions used rather than relying solely on asking for a correct answer and explanation. As the authors state, "By asking students to discuss answers they may not have chosen naturally, we can investigate students' abilities to explain something that is already established or to disprove an incorrect response." The strategy surfaces whether students can explain established answers and disprove incorrect ones, and how their responses shift across task formats.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coding of responses should be guided by prior published research on students' ideas about the content
- A traditional format question should be included as a reference point for comparison
#### Constraints
- In this trial each student answered only one question; the authors state that further investigation through interviews and weekly pretests is needed to understand how students interact with these questions

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnosing students' incoming ideas and reasoning about physics content
