# Combine experiential learning with intercultural communication training in a module with reflective practice to build doctors' awareness of their intercultural communication behaviour

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that achieving awareness of intercultural communication behaviour "requires a combination of experience and ICC training, preferably in a module with reflective practice". Because the study found that extensive experience alone did not make specialists aware of differences in their communication with native versus non-native patients, the authors propose training rooted in patient-centred communication that lets doctors confront and reflect on their own videotaped behaviour. Training modules should enable participants to master generic communication skills as well as ICC-specific skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the doctor's own videotaped consultations for reflective practice
- Training content covering both generic communication skills and ICC-specific skills
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is grounded in a single-hospital qualitative study whose participants all worked in one migrant-friendly hospital, which the authors state could limit transferability to other hospitals

### Target Learners
- medical specialists
- postgraduate medical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- awareness of differences in communication with native versus non-native patients
- intercultural communication skills
