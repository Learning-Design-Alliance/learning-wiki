# Pattern Recognition in Learning

## Description
Pattern recognition is a cognitive process where the brain identifies recurring structures, relationships, or sequences to assign meaning and enhance memory. By organizing information into contextual, event-oriented situations that include motor learning, location changes, music, rhythm, and novelty, learners can maximize their ability to remember and apply new knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning material to be organized into patterns, understanding of the key concepts and relationships, and creativity to develop memorable associations
#### Constraints
- May require time and effort to identify and create effective patterns, effectiveness depends on the relevance and memorability of the patterns, may not be suitable for learners who prefer linear or sequential learning

### Target Learners

- All learner levels, particularly useful for learners who benefit from contextual learning and those who struggle with rote memorization

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance memory recall, improve understanding of complex concepts, promote critical thinking skills, increase engagement with learning material

### Affordances
- Leverages the brain's natural tendency to seek patterns, enhances memory through contextualization, promotes critical thinking skills, increases engagement and motivation

### Personalization
- Learners can create their own patterns and associations based on their personal interests and experiences. They can also use different modalities (visual, auditory, kinesthetic) to reinforce the patterns.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learners' ability to recognize and apply patterns in new situations, assess their understanding of the underlying concepts, evaluate performance on tests and quizzes that require pattern recognition

## Impact
- Pattern recognition has been shown to enhance memory recall, improve understanding of complex concepts, and promote critical thinking skills. It leverages the brain's natural tendency to seek patterns and make connections.
