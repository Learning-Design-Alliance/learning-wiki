# Establish school-based health and wellbeing programs to prevent health-risk behaviors and improve academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that health problems and stress are associated with poor academic performance, the article recommends that "health and welfare programs must be established in schools to reduce the incidence of behaviors that could have a detrimental effect on the physical and mental health of young people, as this could result in improved academic performance." The strategy is grounded in the citation network's health- and stress-related clusters and in cited school programs, such as Van Loon et al.'s two school programs to improve anxiety control and social abilities. The article notes program content will differ across primary, secondary and university levels.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must be tailored to the educational level, as the article states older, more competitive students are more likely to suffer physical and mental damage.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on the association between health and performance found in the reviewed literature, not on a program tested within this article.

### Target Learners
- Primary, secondary and university students

### Target Learning Goals
- Preventing mental and physical health-risk behaviors that undermine academic performance
