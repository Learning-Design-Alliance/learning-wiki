# Implement GME coaching with coaches outside the learner's department to promote psychological safety and surface undisclosed concerns

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends structuring GME coaching so that coaches work with learners from outside their own department, which the authors say "promotes psychological safety that is needed for coaching success." In this safe environment, learners can "communicate concerns more openly than they might with their PD," allowing coaches to identify different or additional areas of focus not initially identified by the learner or program director. The authors propose that coaching in GME may provide an additional safe environment for learners to reveal areas of concern that otherwise would not be disclosed or addressed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coaches drawn from outside the learner's department
- A needs assessment conducted with the program director and then the learner
- Periodic longitudinal meetings to monitor progress on coaching goals
#### Constraints
- The article reports outcome data of coaching in graduate medical training is scarce, and larger studies over time with measurable outcomes are needed to adequately assess the efficacy of GME success coaching

### Target Learners
- Residents and fellows in graduate medical education

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-reflection and identification of areas for improvement
- Disclosure of concerns or difficulties affecting training completion
