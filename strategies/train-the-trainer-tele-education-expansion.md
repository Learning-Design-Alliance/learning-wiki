# Expand low-cost case-based tele-education to other emerging ICUs using a train-the-trainer model

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend expanding the weekly case-based tele-education program to other emerging ICUs in their region and other low- and middle-income countries "using a train-the-trainer model." They argue the results "provide proof of concept" that significant improvements are possible "with modest investments in time and technology," in contrast to the substantial cost of advanced tele-ICU command centers whose evidence for benefit they describe as limited.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A secure but affordable video-conference platform
- Expert educators, potentially developed locally through train-the-trainer progression
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted in a single center with motivated staff and leadership support, which the authors state may not be present in all limited resource settings

### Target Learners
- Critical care practitioners in emerging ICUs in low- and middle-income countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge translation and quality improvement in critical care
