# Screen students for elevated simulation anxiety with STAI and pupillometry, then support predisposed learners with pre-visit, presession-viewing, and breathing-relaxation interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that facilitators identify students with disabling anxiety during simulated clinical scenarios and then apply supportive interventions. It states that for learners "more predisposed to developing raised anxiety thresholds, different interventions could be performed to improve their response, e.g., a previous visit to the simulation laboratory, visualization of a presession, and relaxation exercises by breathing control". Screening uses the STAI questionnaire and the digital pupillometer together.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a digital pupillometer and a validated STAI version in the learners' language
#### Constraints
- The STAI requires unrestricted completion time and should not be repeated frequently, since participants might memorize the questions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in high-fidelity simulation

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing disabling anxiety to protect performance and decision-making during simulation
