# Design learning around social collaboration, crafted challenges, supportive feedback, ownership and practical application

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
From their findings the authors set out implications for educational practice: "Firstly, where possible, we should focus on the social dimension and dynamics of learning. Allowing students to learn together can foster a collective sense of ownership and engagement in their learning." Secondly, educators should craft sufficient but achievable challenges; feedback helps learners gain self-knowledge and reinforces ownership of their actions. Thirdly, supportive educator relationships enable engagement and professional identity development. Finally, "allowing learners to consolidate their learning through practical application can enable an embodied sense of taking such new knowledge to their future professional self."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educator training that raises the profile of agentic learning and promotes the conditions that enable it
#### Constraints
- Challenges must be provided within learners' capabilities; the article notes the study's findings may not be readily transferable to other contexts such as work-based education

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- promoting agentic learning, ownership of actions, and professional identity development
