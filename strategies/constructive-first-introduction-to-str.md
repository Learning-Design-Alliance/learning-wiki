# Begin a special relativity course with dynamical derivations of length contraction, time dilation and the Lorentz transformation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends opening a course on special relativity with the constructive, dynamical derivations based on the charge models before following the more traditional development. It states it "would be possible to begin a course of STR with the above dynamical derivations", and that afterwards the course would follow the traditional path, though it "would be more consistent to derive results using dynamical arguments wherever possible".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must either have met, or be given, the expressions for the electric and magnetic fields of charges in uniform motion, obtained directly from Maxwell's equations.
- The relativistic form of Newton's second law must be reached logically after the Lorentz transformation, not anticipated at the outset as in Bell's model.
#### Constraints
- The approach does not require assuming the invariance of the speed of light, which the article counts as an advantage for introducing other counterintuitive results.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students taking special relativity for the first time

### Target Learning Goals
- understand why moving objects contract and moving clocks run slow, not merely that they do
