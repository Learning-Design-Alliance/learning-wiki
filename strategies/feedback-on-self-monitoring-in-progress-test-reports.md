# Integrate task-specific feedback on self-monitoring accuracy into regular progress test reports

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that regular task-specific feedback on self-monitoring accuracy may enhance self-monitoring over time, for example by triggering an adequate level of further self-directed training. Such feedback could be integrated in regular progress test reports, ideally together with advice on how to practice topics for which students lack accurate self-monitoring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback must be task-specific and delivered regularly
#### Constraints
- The authors did not provide feedback in this study and call for future research to investigate this

### Target Learners
- Medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving self-monitoring accuracy and directing self-directed training
