# Use a historical ocean-navigation narrative as an entry point for teaching space and time concepts through stellar orientation and the calendar

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The first proposal teaches space and time concepts "through traditional examples of orientation by the stars and the use of the calendar, using the case of historical ocean navigation without advanced instruments". Students read an adapted text from Roa Bastos's 'Vigilia del Almirante' and answer questions such as whether it is possible to determine the place and time of year of the story. They then represent the Sun's movement, contrast their ideas with direct systematic observations, Stellarium, concrete analogues (flashlight, styrofoam spheres, globe), and ephemeris tables, and finally rework the narrative set in their own city as formative assessment.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prior knowledge of cardinal points, the daily movement of the Sun for a local observer, the horizontal coordinate system, common constellations, and equinoxes.
#### Constraints
- Designed primarily for secondary natural sciences but adaptable to teacher-training institutes.

### Target Learners
- secondary school students
- teacher-training institute students

### Target Learning Goals
- determining approximate date and location from celestial observations
- understanding the Sun's annual movement at different latitudes
- relating Earth's shape and axial tilt to the seasons
