Extend interactive workbook development to other grades and domains and train teachers in its use¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's suggestions section recommends preparing multimedia-supported web-based workbooks for other classes and learning domains, ensuring schools' computer and internet infrastructure, and preparing in-service trainings so teachers "use such materials efficiently." It also proposes future studies on permanence of effects, comparisons using the recorded answer database, and gathering teacher opinions about the software.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Schools must meet computer and internet necessities to practice the activities

Constraints¶

  • The software was developed and tested only for one 4th grade learning domain in one district

Target Learners¶

  • Primary school students
  • Teachers using web-based materials

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Social Studies content learning
  • Effective integration of web-based materials

Claims¶

  • Control group showed no significant pretest-to-posttest change except a decline in attitude toward the internet [+W]
  • Experiment group showed significant pretest-to-posttest gains in computer, internet, social studies attitudes and academic success [+W]
  • Experiment group students developed more positive attitudes toward computers and toward social studies than control group students [+W]
  • Fourth-grade students using the interactive web-based workbook scored significantly higher on the academic success test than the control group at posttest [+W]
