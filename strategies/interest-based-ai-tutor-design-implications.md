# Seven design implications for an interest-based AI tutor, organized across opening/rapport, instruction, and evaluation phases of tutoring dialogue

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper translates its findings into a design space for interest-based AI tutors, framed by three tutoring-dialogue phases: opening/rapport-building, instruction and explanation, and evaluation/formative assessment. Implications include to "prompt about interest" at session start, "frame concept with interest", "craft reflection prompts using interest", "adaptively switch depth of interest integration", use "interest-based analogies as conceptual bridges", "use interest-based examples for reinforcing learning", and "using interest-based scenarios for active reasoning".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the learner's stated interest or hobby and contextual details gathered at session start
- Verification step after analogy use to check that misconceptions were not introduced
#### Constraints
- Findings are specific to adult learners in one-to-one online tutoring in a controlled setting
- Scenarios are less frequent and harder to design under low knowledge of the hobby

### Target Learners
- adult learners in one-to-one online tutoring

### Target Learning Goals
- contextualizing instructional content around learners' interests to support engagement and understanding

## Claims

- [Tutors integrated interests either peripherally (inserted into prepared content at specific points) or centrally (interest as the lesson's organizing frame), with central integration used in most sessions](../claims/central-versus-peripheral-interest-integration.md) [+W]
- [Analogical bridges were the most frequently used mechanism of interest integration, with illustrative examples and contextualizing scenarios also employed](../claims/interest-integration-mechanisms-frequency.md) [+W]
- [Students and tutors perceived interest integration positively — students found lessons more casual, familiar, and engaging, while tutors valued the resulting interactivity](../claims/positive-perceptions-interest-integration.md) [+W]
- [Tutors struggled to design interest-based content when unfamiliar with the hobby, broadening interests or producing forced examples, and wanted more student involvement](../claims/tutor-challenges-low-interest-knowledge.md) [~W]
