Use systematic formative assessment before, during, and after instruction to uncover covert learning processes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The comment recommends formative assessment as the practical alternative to judging engagement from overt behavior: "systematic and regular assessment activities before, during, and after instruction". Repeated targeted assessments provide feedback that lets teachers and learners optimize teaching and learning, and meta-analyses on formative feedback support its effectiveness. Practitioners define learning goals, devise suitable assessment techniques, provide feedback, and check alignment of these steps alongside their instruction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Practitioners must think about students' cognitive activities and learning processes, define learning goals, devise suitable assessment techniques, and provide feedback with aligned guidance during instruction.

Constraints¶

  • Systematic use of formative assessment may put a higher demand on practitioners' shoulders than following the ICAP framework.

Target Learners¶

  • School students across classroom instruction

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Insight into students' covert learning processes, conceptions, and level of processing to adapt instruction

Claims¶

  • Overt student behavior is an unreliable proxy for covert cognitive engagement and instructional effectiveness [+W]
  • Identical overt behaviors can reflect different engagement modes: a video-watching example [+W]
