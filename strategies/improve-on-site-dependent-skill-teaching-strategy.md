# When clinical training is restricted, improve teaching methods so students can learn clinical judgment on-site, and develop methods for skills negatively affected by all-on-campus training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends preparing for future restrictions on clinical practice by targeting teaching at skills that depend on clinical settings. It states: "it will be necessary to improve teaching methods so that students can learn the subject matter and clinical judgment that can only be learned through on-site clinical training", and "for skills that were rated positively for all on-site training, it will be desirable to develop teaching methods that create situations closer to those experienced during clinical training." The article also suggests pooling digital learning resources, simulators, and role-playing as supplements, while cautioning they are no substitute for in-person clinical rotations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identify which nursing skills were positively rated for all on-site training so teaching methods can target them
#### Constraints
- "they are no substitute for the learning experience of in-person clinical rotations, as some techniques can only be learned in a clinical setting"
- The article notes "there is currently no alternative learning method to touch"

### Target Learners
- Final-year nursing students in basic nursing education

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical nursing skills attainment and clinical judgment
