# Explicitly address the visibility of laser light beams when using the jelly lens analogy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because some students think light beams can be seen from the side, the authors 'recommend addressing the visibility of laser light beams, which is easily demonstrated with a laser pointer traversing air'. A beam becomes visible only when it passes through a translucent medium such as jelly that scatters some light into the eye; likewise, an observer on Earth cannot see the trajectories of light rays.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A laser pointer demonstration traversing air, showing that beams become visible only in a translucent medium that scatters laser light into our eyes.
#### Constraints
- Many visualisations of gravitational lensing 'use a ray model of light suggesting that we can see the light ray from the side', which might reinforce this misconception.

### Target Learners
- high school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- correct understanding of the role of light in seeing
- interpreting gravitational lensing visualisations without optics misconceptions
