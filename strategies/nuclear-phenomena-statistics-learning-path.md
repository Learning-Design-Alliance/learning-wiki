# A four-part learning path on nuclear phenomena coupling classroom introduction, laboratory radioactivity measurement, statistics instruction, and computer-lab data elaboration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a learning path of "four different but correlated activities": a brief classroom introduction to nuclear phenomena, measurement of radioactivity in the physics laboratory with a Geiger counter and uranium sources, an introduction to statistical data analysis starting from the question of limits to measurement precision, and selected elaborations of the experimental data in a computer lab. It was planned for the last year of high school with about 16 hours scheduled, and a similar structure is proposed for introductory undergraduate physics courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a physics laboratory where background and weak sources can be monitored for long time, plus a computer lab for data elaboration
- Statistical topics presented in previous years need to be revised if the path is implemented in the final school year
#### Constraints
- The author judges the path "hardly feasible in last class due to graduation exam", calling it an excellent optional proposal for interested students

### Target Learners
- last-year high school students
- undergraduate students of disciplines different from physics in introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding statistical data analysis of real experimental data
- nuclear phenomenology and radioactivity
- interdisciplinary integration of statistics and experimental work
