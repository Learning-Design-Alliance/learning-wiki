# Use the elicit-confront-resolve strategy to structure tutorial questions that help students refine documented misconceptions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 against) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes the elicit-confront-resolve strategy as a common instructional strategy used in guided inquiry to support students in refining their conceptions to more strongly align with normative physics. The elicit phase draws out known conceptions using a physics task, the confront phase guides students to become consciously aware of specific flaws in their thinking, and the resolve phase provides scaffolding to support students in developing ideas aligned with normative physics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- the elicit phase should use a physics task, typically the same as the one used in the original research for identifying student conceptions
- the confront phase should guide students through a reasoning process that helps them become consciously aware of specific flaws in their thinking
- the resolve phase should provide scaffolding to support students in developing ideas and reasoning aligned with normative physics
