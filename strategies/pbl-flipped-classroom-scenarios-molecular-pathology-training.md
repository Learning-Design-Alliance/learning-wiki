# Use PBL with flipped classroom and scenario-based troubleshooting modules for specialized molecular pathology technique training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete implementation recipe the article tested: combine problem-based learning with a flipped classroom in three phases—pre-class self-directed learning with operational videos, probe-design materials, and guidelines; in-class group presentations with instructor feedback correcting procedural descriptions and interpretation logic; and structured laboratory practice validating hypothesized technical modifications. Students were "organized into six collaborative learning groups, with each group assigned one to two problem scenarios" drawn from ten real-world EBERs-ISH-focused clinical and experimental problems. The authors recommend standardizing and disseminating their multi-level assessment framework, including the Protocol Comprehension Examination, to other molecular pathology technique curricula.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners should be naive to the target technique's theory and practice, with prerequisite coursework completed
- Assessment should maintain expert blind-scoring mechanisms to ensure objectivity and validity
#### Constraints
- The authors note implementation challenges including resource constraints, teacher professional development requirements, and assessment complexity substantially impact pedagogical effectiveness
- Lacking longitudinal follow-up, the strategy's effects on skill retention and transfer to other techniques are undetermined

### Target Learners
- postgraduate pathology residents
- recent graduates of medical or biotechnology programs

### Target Learning Goals
- specialized molecular pathology technical proficiency
- troubleshooting of staining artifacts and sectioning defects
- theory-practice integration through iterative hypothesis testing
