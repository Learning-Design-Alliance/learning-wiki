# Deliver empirically-supported simulation-based education to train key procedural skills such as lumbar puncture where clinical opportunities are few

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (6 for, 2 mixed) · 5 studies (3 causal, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors argue that limited clinical opportunities to perform procedures justify simulation-based training: "Such data relating to limited clinical opportunities to perform procedural skills indicate the importance of delivering empirically-supported simulation-based education to train key skills such as lumbar puncture." The strategy trains procedures to behavioural fluency in the simulator, using frequency building, corrective feedback and celeration chart monitoring, so that competence is established despite scarce real cases. The article also recommends incorporating peer tutoring and economic evaluation in future implementations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated task analysis and expert-set fluency criterion
- Trained observers to score accuracy and deliver corrective feedback
- A task trainer replicating the relevant anatomy
#### Constraints
- The authors note it is not possible to state conclusively that the intervention leads to better outcomes than supervised clinical practice, and that generalisation assessment was limited by retrospective chart audit and few clinical lumbar punctures

### Target Learners
- Paediatric trainees and other medical trainees learning procedural skills

### Target Learning Goals
- Achieving behavioural fluency in infrequently performed clinical procedures

### Affordances
- Precision Teaching Behavioural Fluency Framework
