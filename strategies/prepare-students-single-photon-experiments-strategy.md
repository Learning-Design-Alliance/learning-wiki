# Prepare students for single-photon experiments with pre-lab resources and prior classical experiments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Instructors prepare students for the conceptually and technically complex single-photon experiments by providing resources that introduce both the theory and the apparatus. These included "videos made by the instructors, readings about the experiments," readings about statistics, homework assignments, papers from prior students, and lab manuals, with the expectation that students learn on their own and receive one-on-one help as needed. Some courses also required students to first complete classical optics or electronics work, such as using polarizers and wave plates or building a visible-laser interferometer, to build optics skills before progressing to the quantum experiments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need knowledge of both quantum concepts and how the experimental apparatus works before working with the experiments
#### Constraints
- Not all prerequisite courses covered the quantum optics concepts and math necessary to understand the single-photon experiments

### Target Learners
- beyond-first-year undergraduate physics, applied physics, and engineering physics majors
- junior and senior physics majors in quantum courses

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of quantum optics experiments
- optics and lab skills needed to perform the experiments
