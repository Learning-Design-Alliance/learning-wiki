Supervise students' online assessment habits and equip schools with wireless broadband before scaling cloud learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's forward-looking recommendations address two barriers to cloud-based learning. Because Internet access is required and students may be "distracted by online games or social networking websites", it recommends that "teachers and parents remind and supervise students in developing satisfactory habits of online assessment use before conducting online assessments or learning". For infrastructure, it recommends schools include wireless broadband Internet equipment in their computer-equipment budgets so ubiquitous learning can be realized.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • teachers and parents available to remind and supervise students
  • school budget processes for computer-related equipment

Constraints¶

  • stated for the Taiwanese context, where nearly all schools have broadband but wireless access is rare

Target Learners¶

  • junior high school students using online assessment systems

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustained engagement with online learning activities
  • ubiquitous learning via mobile devices

Claims¶

  • Students without home broadband or who rely only on a cell phone show a digital skill gap of roughly three grade levels and are less likely to plan for college [+W]
  • Individual internet penetration in rural Bangladesh (36.5%) is about half the urban rate (71.4%), which one case-study preprint argues puts cloud-dependent AI tools out of reach for rural learners [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Sustain broadband and device access beyond grant funds by inserting permanent upgrade clauses in ISP contracts and adopting 'device as service' procurement models at state scale
  • Improve UI consistency and network system before scaling VR classroom use
  • Set clear recording goals, budget for equipment, and plan technical support and consent before classroom video recording
