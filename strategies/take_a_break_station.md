# Take A Break Station

## Description
A designated space in the classroom where children can take a moment to decompress, reset, and self-regulate their emotions and behaviors. It's not a consequence (unlike a 'Time Out'), but a chance for students to recognize their emotions, understand the impact of their behavior on others, and learn self-management skills. The station includes a basket of materials (stress balls, timers, notebooks, etc.) and a comfortable place to sit and reflect.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designated space in the classroom, basket of calming materials (stress balls, notebooks, etc.), reflection prompts (written or visual), and consistent implementation. Teachers need to introduce the station and model its use.
#### Constraints
- If overused, may indicate deeper needs that require a different intervention. Requires consistent teaching and modeling to ensure students understand its purpose and use it effectively.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Primarily K–5 students, but adaptable for older students with social-emotional needs.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Help students recognize and manage their emotions, self-regulate their behavior, and understand the impact of their actions on others.

### Affordances
- Empowers students to self-regulate without constant adult intervention, reduces classroom disruptions, and promotes emotional intelligence. Provides a safe space for students to calm down and refocus.

### Personalization
- The station can be personalized with different materials based on student needs (e.g., visual prompts for non-readers). The reflection process can be guided by an adult until the student can work independently.

- Observe student behavior before and after using the station for changes in emotional state and behavior. Collect student reflections on their experience at the station. Track frequency of use to identify students who may need additional support.

## Impact
- Reduces repeat behaviors and classroom disruptions. Empowers students to take responsibility for their emotions and actions.
