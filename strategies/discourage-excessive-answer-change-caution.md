# Advise examinees that excessive caution about changing answers is unwarranted and review time has diminishing returns

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that examinees preparing for USMLE and similar credentialing examinations be informed that answer changing generally helps rather than hurts scores. It states "excessive caution is not warranted because some levels of answer changing have consistently been show to improve scores (on average)", while also warning that "there is a point of diminishing returns related to time invested in reviewing a single item."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examination format must permit returning to items within a block, as on the computer-administered Step 2 CK.
#### Constraints
- The size of the impact of answer changes is modest and does not importantly affect most examinees' scores; whether more answer changing would help further is unknown.

### Target Learners
- Medical students and graduates preparing for USMLE Step 2 CK and other medical credentialing examinations

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective test-taking strategy for multiple-choice credentialing examinations
