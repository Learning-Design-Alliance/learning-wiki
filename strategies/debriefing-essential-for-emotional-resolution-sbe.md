# Treat debriefing as an essential part of simulation-based education, providing time for reflection, clarification and resolution of confusion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review concludes that the emotional rollercoaster students experience during simulations highlights the importance of time for reflection and clarification during debriefing. "Not including adequate debriefing can mean that students leave the simulation with unresolved frustration and confusion. Therefore, debriefing should be considered as an essential part of SBE." Debriefing is also identified as the aspect in which most learning occurs, and where negative emotions can shift to positive through reflection and supportive feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient debriefing time and the opportunity for students to reflect on what worked and what did not, and to obtain supportive feedback
#### Constraints
- Without adequate debriefing, students may leave the simulation with unresolved frustration and confusion

### Target Learners
- Health professional students participating in simulation-based education

### Target Learning Goals
- Resolving confusion and frustration, building confidence, and consolidating learning from simulation scenarios
