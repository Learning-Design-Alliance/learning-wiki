# Prioritize more studies of direct dropout behavior, including objective enrollment data, before drawing firm retention conclusions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because only three of the reviewed studies measured actual dropout behavior and only one used objective enrollment data, the article concludes that "more studies on dropout behavior are needed before concrete conclusions can be made". It recommends that future work measure actual dropout (school/university records or enrollment status) rather than relying solely on intentions, and that further work integrate and test theory, including examining mechanisms underpinning the burnout–dropout relationship.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal designs with objective measures of dropout such as enrollment records
#### Constraints
- Actual dropout behavior is difficult and costly to measure, and difficult to disentangle voluntary leaving from course failure, which the article gives as reasons researchers use proxy measures

### Target Learners
- students in post-compulsory education

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate understanding of burnout's role in student retention and dropout
