# Augment traditional physics instruction with visuo-haptic simulations of abstract concepts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 4 studies (3 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends augmenting traditional classroom learning with haptic tools so students can observe and feel forces in abstract physics phenomena. It reports that "this research shows that by augmenting traditional classroom learning with the use of haptic tools, students were able to better understand abstract physics concepts," reflected in higher quiz scores for simulation groups. The authors also note advantages of repeatability and a large, effectively continuous range of customizable physical parameters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to haptic hardware and visuo-haptic simulation software; the article notes students may need familiarity with the device, though a tutorial made no quiz-score difference
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only on the Coriolis effect with undergraduates; the authors call for future work on long-term retention and additional disciplines

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students
- STEM students in disciplines with abstract force concepts

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding abstract physics phenomena difficult to convey with text and still images
