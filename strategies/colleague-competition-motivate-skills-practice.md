# Use competition between colleagues, more than between organisations, to motivate skills practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 5 studies (3 causal, 1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 5 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests framing training as a competition to motivate practice, with the competitive nature stated at the outset and a winner rewarded. Participants rated colleague competition more motivating (mean 6.0) than competition between countries (mean 5.8), and the authors conclude that "competition might have a useful role to help motivate staff to perform CPR training". Site co-ordinators mostly endorsed colleague-level competition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An objective, consistent performance score (the Overall CPR score) to rank competitors
- Participants informed of the competitive framing at the outset
#### Constraints
- Motivation ratings were modest overall and varied widely by site (Chur means 2.8–3.7); the authors state additional work is needed before firm conclusions

### Target Learners
- Professional pre-hospital EMS healthcare providers

### Target Learning Goals
- Motivation to practice CPR skills regularly
