# Districts use financial, personnel management, and working-conditions strategies to recruit and retain teachers, including leadership compensation, mentoring, and collaboration time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Districts report adopting financial strategies (74%), personnel management strategies (55%), and working-conditions strategies (40%). Financial moves include additional compensation for leadership roles (53%) and increased salaries (40%); working-conditions moves include mentorship or induction for all new teachers (36%) and common planning time (36%), since "More collaborative work environments, where professional learning and collective responsibility are emphasized, can have a positive effect on teacher retention."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration strategies require adequate time for planning and adequate teaching and learning resources
#### Constraints
- The report notes these policies have generally not been targeted to shortage fields

### Target Learners
- California K-12 students in districts with teacher shortages

### Target Learning Goals
- Recruiting and retaining qualified teachers to improve educational opportunities
