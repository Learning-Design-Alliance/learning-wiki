# Brief Instruction Steps

## Description
Breaking up instructions into short, concrete steps allows learners to pay attention to and remember one instruction at a time. When there are multiple steps, providing written or visual instructions for learners to refer back to is helpful in supporting their Working Memory and Speed of Processing. Combining audio and picture instructions can facilitate the use of both the visual and auditory channels of Working Memory to increase the likelihood of learners carrying out all the instructions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires careful planning to break down instructions into small, manageable steps. Visual or written aids may be needed. Audio recording equipment may be used.
#### Constraints
- May be time-consuming to prepare instructions in very small steps. Learners may become overly reliant on step-by-step guidance.

### Affordances
- Reduces cognitive overload, supports working memory, and improves comprehension by providing manageable, step-by-step guidance. Uses multiple modalities to engage visual and auditory learning channels.

### Personalization
- Use varied formats (written, audio, visual) to cater to different learning preferences. Adjust step complexity based on individual learner needs. Provide additional scaffolds such as demonstrations or examples.
