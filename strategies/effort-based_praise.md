# Effort-Based Praise

## Description
Praising learners' efforts and strategies—as opposed to their intelligence—cultivates a growth mindset and enhances motivation and performance. Focusing praise on the learning process helps learners view intelligence as malleable, encouraging them to embrace challenges and persist through setbacks. In contrast, praising intelligence can undermine motivation and performance, as learners praised for intelligence may view intelligence as a fixed trait and become less resilient in the face of failure. Effort-based praise highlights the value of hard work, effective strategies, and perseverance, reinforcing the idea that learning is a process of continuous improvement. Instructors should provide specific feedback that acknowledges the learner's effort, strategies used, and progress made, rather than making general statements about their intelligence or talent.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Observation of learner efforts and strategies. Ability to provide specific and meaningful feedback. A focus on the learning process rather than just the end result.
#### Constraints
- If praise is not genuine or specific, it may be ineffective. Overuse of praise can diminish its impact. Learners may become overly reliant on praise for motivation.

### Personalization
- Feedback can be tailored to individual learner efforts and strategies. Praise can be specific to the task and the learner's approach to it. Instructors can provide individualized encouragement to help learners persist through challenges.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track changes in learner motivation and persistence. Measure performance improvements following effort-based praise. Assess the extent to which learners adopt a growth mindset.

## Impact
- Research demonstrates that effort-based praise enhances motivation and performance, while intelligence-based praise can undermine it (reference Dweck's research on mindset). Learners who receive effort-based praise are more likely to embrace challenges and persist through setbacks (reference research on resilience).
- The specific mechanism: telling a student they are smart when they produce a good result implicitly commits them to that same explanation when they later do poorly — if a good result means "you're smart," a poor result on the very same logic means "you're not smart" (Dweck, 2000). Effort-based praise avoids setting up this trap because it never ties the outcome to a fixed personal trait in the first place.
