Implement multi-disciplinary threat assessment with reporting mechanisms and safe channels for students to come forward¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The brief recommends that schools train personnel in threat assessment, in which "a careful protocol implemented by a multi-disciplinary team" examines threats and deals with them, differentiating false alarms from real threats and providing appropriate interventions for real ones. It further recommends establishing a mechanism for making credible threats known, educating school and community members about warning signs, and creating safe spaces for students to report threats, since students often know about a classmate's threat.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Personnel trained in threat assessment
  • A mechanism for making credible threats known
  • Safe spaces for students to come forward and report threats

Constraints¶

  • Threat assessment requires knowing about the threat; in many cases red flags were ignored despite somebody knowing something about the perpetrator's violent intentions

Target Learners¶

  • School staff and administrators
  • Students
  • Parents and community members

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Identifying and reporting warning signs and differentiating real threats from false alarms before an attack occurs

Related Strategies¶

  • Prefer lockdown drills over highly realistic, unannounced active shooter exercises, embedding them in layered emergency preparedness
