# Strengthen ASHA capacity through gender and cultural sensitization in training, interpersonal-skills emphasis in monthly meetings, non-punitive lesson-sharing spaces, and community engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends capacity-building mechanisms for CHW programmes: "thorough sensitization about gender, religious, and cultural issues", strengthening monthly meetings so sensitization on gender and cultural issues is given equal importance with technical skills, giving ASHAs "a non-punitive space whereby they can share lessons learned", and enhancing community engagement to boost recognition and self-confidence. It also recommends timely incentives and supportive supervision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Refresher courses and monthly meetings must actually be implemented; the article notes refresher courses are not uniformly implemented across all states
#### Constraints
- The article states it is not clear whether the existing 3-day gender sensitization exposure or distributing a brochure without orienting ASHAs would address cultural norms and prejudices

### Target Learners
- community health workers (ASHAs) and their professional health-system counterparts

### Target Learning Goals
- interpersonal and communication skills, gender and cultural sensitization, and service-promotion competence

- [Reciprocal Determinism Chw Performance](../theories/social-learning-theory.md)
