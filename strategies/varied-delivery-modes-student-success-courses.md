# Offer student success courses in varied content, timing, and delivery modes matched to student needs rather than a one-size-fits-all model

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy addresses student criticism that student success courses can feel irrelevant, costly, and like wasted time. The report recommends that colleges "consider providing the courses in a variety of delivery modes so students can select the one that best fits their schedule," such as online versions, night classes, or selectable modules based on self-assessment. It also suggests different curricula based on students' fields of study, prior college experience, and work status, and timing some content in the second semester once students recognize where they struggle.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Colleges should review the content of any offerings they make mandatory to ensure they are engaging, high quality, and relevant to students' intended coursework and career plans.
#### Constraints
- The report states there is not a one-size-fits-all model for this type of course; students have different needs and enter college at different points along the education trajectory.

### Target Learners
- Community college students, including first-time, returning, and working students

### Target Learning Goals
- College success skills such as time management, study habits, and test taking; adjustment to college culture and expectations
