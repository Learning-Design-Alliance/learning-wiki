# Develop citizen science projects that go beyond pattern recognition to provide avenues for further learning and independent thought

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article identifies an area where India "can make a substantial diﬀerence" by "creating citizen science projects that go beyond just pattern recognition by the participant, and provide avenues for further learning and independent thought", noting very few such projects exist worldwide. It points to asteroid hunt and exoplanet naming campaigns as starting points to attract young minds, and urges mega-projects such as SKA, LIGO and TMT to devote resources to citizen science using their data, with precursor-telescope projects startable immediately.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Internet connectivity to offer scalable citizen science programs to the public
- Mega-projects should devote resources to developing and running citizen science projects with their data
#### Constraints
- The technology for AI-aided translation of Indian-language materials is described as still too nascent for error-free use, a separate limitation the article notes for multilingual content

### Target Learners
- general public
- young students

### Target Learning Goals
- participation in scientific data analysis
- further learning and independent scientific thought
