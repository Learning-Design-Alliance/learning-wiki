# Deploy VR lab activities through a buddy system and reserve time for unscripted exploration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's implementation recipe for VR labs pairs students so that while one wears the headset, the other reads exercise instructions, records results, and monitors the headset view on a large 2D display, with turns taken in series. A key part of the methodology was to "include time for unscripted activities to allow students to explore these difficult concepts on their own." This pairing keeps the headset-wearer supported while preserving student control of their vantage point, and it extends to practical supports such as a pre-lab video on the headset and controllers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Paired students with a large 2D display mirroring the headset view for the monitoring partner
- Scheduled time for unscripted activities beyond the scripted exercise
- Access to equipment outside scheduled lab time where possible
#### Constraints
- The buddy system changes classroom logistics: students must wait their turn and perform the activity in series rather than in parallel

### Target Learners
- General education introductory astronomy students working in lab pairs

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding difficult 3D astronomy concepts (Moon phases, eclipses, stellar parallax) through self-controlled exploration
