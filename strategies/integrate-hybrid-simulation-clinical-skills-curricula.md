# Integrate hybrid simulation into clinical skills curricula to concurrently train communication and technical skills in a safe, repeatable environment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend hybrid simulation as "a valuable complement to clinical skills training programs in nursing education", arguing its "integration into clinical skills curricula can enhance learning quality, foster professional competence, and provide a safe environment for repeated practice." The strategy pairs a standardized patient with a technical simulator so communication and psychomotor skills are practiced simultaneously rather than separately, with structured prebriefing and debriefing around the scenario.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained standardized patients with prepared scripts and guides, faculty-approved scenarios, dedicated assessors per skill, and instructor feedback during practice
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one quasi-experimental study of sixth-semester nursing students with moderate post-hoc power (0.648); long-term effects, broader populations, cost-effectiveness, and comparisons with other modalities remain untested

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- patient communication and interaction
- suturing and clinical procedural skills
