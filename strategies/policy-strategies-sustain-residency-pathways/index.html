Sustain and expand residency pathways through maintained state grant funding, financial aid, apprenticeship structures, and technical assistance¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends that California policymakers maintain funding for the Teacher Residency Grant Program beyond its 2026–27 expiration, renew or replace the Golden State Teacher Grant Program's $20,000 tuition support, support residencies in establishing apprenticeship funding streams under Department of Labor guidelines, and continue funding the SRTAC. Federal recommendations include expanding Teacher Quality Partnership grants, upgrading the TEACH Grant to cover full tuition costs, and covering teachers' monthly federal loan payments until their debt is retired.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • State and federal appropriations dedicated to residency initiation, growth, and continuous improvement

Constraints¶

  • TRGP funding is due to expire after the 2026–27 school year, and Golden State Teacher Grant funds were expected to run out in 2024–25

Target Learners¶

  • Prospective teachers, particularly diverse candidates preparing for high-need communities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Access to affordable, high-quality preservice teacher preparation
  • Recruitment and retention of effective teachers in shortage areas

Affordances¶

  • Teacher Residency Model Framework

Related Strategies¶

  • Sustain teacher residencies through blended federal and state funding, including TQP grants, ESSER funds, and dedicated state investments
  • Build high-retention, supportive pathways into teaching for candidates of color
  • Recommendations for sustainable and affordable residency programs: align with LEA strategy, engage system leaders, dedicate sustainability roles, and run affordability analyses
  • Underwrite comprehensive preparation, improve teaching conditions, raise compensation, and expand induction to retain teachers of color
  • Design service scholarships that cover a substantial portion of preparation costs, with stable multiyear funding and linked data systems
  • Offer targeted service scholarships, loan forgiveness, and 1-year postbaccalaureate residency programs to rapidly expand the supply of well-prepared teachers in shortage fields and locations
  • Build a diverse teacher pipeline through Grow Your Own grants, teacher academies, scholarships, MSI investment, and residency pathways
  • Policy recommendations for sustaining and expanding teacher residencies
  • State policy levers for sustaining yearlong, stipended clinical preparation: require or incentivize full-year stipended clinical experience, fund partnerships, and reduce testing fees
  • Use Teacher Residency Grant Program funds, braided with other sources, to recruit and retain a diverse teaching workforce
  • State actions to strengthen and sustain a funded residency pathway: clarify goals, upgrade data systems, adjust grant parameters, offer transition grants, and pair new LEAs with experienced IHEs
  • Use state completer survey data within accreditation to flag struggling programs and expand subsidized high-quality clinical pathways for underserved candidates
