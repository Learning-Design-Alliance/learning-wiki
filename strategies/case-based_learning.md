# Case Based Learning

## Description
Case based learning places learners in the role of analyst or decision-maker confronting a realistic narrative — a patient history, a business dilemma, a legal dispute, an engineering failure. Learners must identify the salient issues, apply disciplinary concepts to messy particulars, and defend a course of action, typically through structured discussion. The case functions as a situated proxy for experience: it carries the ambiguity, competing constraints, and incomplete information of real practice without its risks.

## Design Implications

Cases work because they situate abstract principles in concrete, storied contexts, forcing learners to retrieve and apply knowledge under conditions resembling transfer [Case based learning improves exam performance.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]. Their effectiveness depends less on the case text itself than on the discussion structure around it — learners must actively reason and commit to positions, not merely read [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. Because real cases are ill-structured and context-bound, revisiting multiple cases that share underlying principles supports abstraction and flexible application [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-written case with genuine decision points, not a thinly disguised illustration of a textbook answer
- Preparation structures (pre-reading questions, response submissions) that ensure learners arrive having committed to an analysis
- A facilitation plan: probing questions, board plans, and willingness to let discussion go somewhere unplanned
- Explicit connection back to course concepts during or after discussion ([Debrief](../elements/debrief.md))

#### Constraints
- Cases with an obvious single "right answer" collapse into recall exercises and forfeit the judgment-building benefit [-M]
- Novices without adequate domain knowledge can flounder in open discussion, encoding surface details rather than principles [~M] — some prior instruction or scaffolding is usually needed first
- Large classes without discussion infrastructure reduce the method to lecture with a story attached [-M]
- Poorly facilitated discussion can entrench misconceptions if errors go unchallenged [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Discussion-first (Harvard method):** cold-calling and board-driven whole-class analysis of a case learners have prepared in advance
- **Small-group analysis:** teams work a case then report out; scales better and adds peer explanation
- **Progressive disclosure:** the case unfolds in stages and learners commit to decisions before seeing consequences, mirroring real decision pressure
- **Multimedia/anchored cases:** video-based cases (e.g., the Jasper Woodbury series) embed data in rich narrative contexts

### Target Learners
- Advanced novices and intermediate learners who have foundational concepts but lack application experience [Case based learning improves exam performance.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]
- Professional students (medicine, law, business, teaching) preparing for ill-structured practice
- Complete novices benefit only with heavy scaffolding; pure discovery in an unfamiliar domain overloads working memory [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Applied reasoning: using concepts to diagnose, decide, and justify
- Transfer: recognizing when principles apply across surface-different situations [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M]
- Professional judgment and argumentation: weighing competing values and evidence
- Perspective-taking: understanding stakeholder positions within a situation

### Instructions
1. Select or write a case with a genuine dilemma and incomplete information; align it with target concepts ([Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md))
2. Assign the case in advance with preparation questions requiring a committed position or decision
3. Open discussion by surfacing learners' analyses before supplying any framing ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
4. Probe with questions that force application of concepts and confrontation between competing analyses ([Argumentation](../elements/argumentation.md))
5. Debrief: name the principles the case illustrates, contrast with other cases, and address lingering misconceptions ([Debrief](../elements/debrief.md))
6. Sequence multiple cases over time that vary surface features but share structure ([Comparing Cases](../elements/comparing-cases.md))

## Examples
- **Harvard Business School** — the canonical discussion-based MBA case method; every course session centers on student analysis of a prepared case ([hbs.edu](https://www.hbs.edu/mba/academic-experience/classroom-experience/Pages/the-case-method.aspx))
- **Harvard Business School Publishing case collections** — curated cases with teaching notes used across business schools worldwide ([hbsp.harvard.edu](https://www.hbsp.harvard.edu))
- **National Center for Case Study Teaching in Science (University at Buffalo)** — peer-reviewed science cases with teaching notes for undergraduate STEM ([nccsts.org](https://www.nccsts.org))
- **Anchored Instruction (Jasper Woodbury series)** — video-based adventure cases requiring mathematical problem solving embedded in narrative
