# Match the clinical teaching model to the encounter type and learner level

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 5 studies (3 review, 2 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends selecting among the seven models by encounter type and learner preparation: OMP for novice learners and higher-order learning, SNAPPS for advanced learners who can drive the encounter, MiPLAN for bedside teaching, ED STAT! for the ED environment, Aunt Minnie for classic presentations on busy shifts, SPIT for broadening differentials, and Activated Demonstration for skills. It states "Seven clinical teaching models were presented which target different types of clinical encounters, and require variable amounts of facilitator and learner preparation." Models need not be used exhaustively: the review notes not all OMP steps are needed every encounter and "It is not necessary to use the model with every single case."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Preceptors need familiarity with multiple models; the review says the review "can serve as a template to help educators become more adept and flexible when teaching within the ED."
#### Constraints
- SNAPPS and ED STAT! require training for learners and/or preceptors; Aunt Minnie and SPIT are unstudied; Activated Demonstration is "not flexible for other aspects of clinical teaching (e.g. differentials)".

### Target Learners
- medical students and residents on clinical rotations
- faculty and senior resident clinical teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- efficient learner-centered teaching in time-constrained clinical environments

### Affordances
- Seven Learner Centered Clinical Teaching Models Taxonomy
