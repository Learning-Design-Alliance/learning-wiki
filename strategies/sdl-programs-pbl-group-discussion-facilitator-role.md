# Incorporate SDL programs with problem-based learning and group discussion components, and shift teachers from didactic instructors to facilitators

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the survey findings, the article recommends that "SDL programs should be incorporated in all undergraduate medical curricula with improvised problem-based learning and group discussion components", that "Teachers' role should be shifted from traditional didactic instructor to more of a facilitator for more collaborative learning", and that first-year students be assessed for SDL at admission and followed up longitudinally. It further recommends attention to cultural and linguistic barriers and greater institutional support for public medical colleges.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional and administrative support, particularly in public medical colleges, to conduct seamless SDL programs
#### Constraints
- Students' socioeconomic and demographic characteristics should be taken into account before administering any SDL programs

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-directed learning readiness
- collaborative learning
- lifelong learning
