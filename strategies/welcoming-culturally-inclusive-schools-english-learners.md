# Create positive, culturally inclusive, welcoming schools using strengths-based practices such as newcomer welcome centers, attendance cafés, and home-language-as-asset approaches

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The brief advises schools to "use a strengths-based approach to help English learners feel seen, valued, and supported," establishing classroom routines and enrichment reflecting students' interests. Specific strategies include creating newcomer welcome centers, hosting "attendance cafés in families' home languages," running summer kindergarten-readiness camps such as Jumpstart, designing culturally and linguistically responsive lessons, using restorative circles to reduce bullying, treating home language as an asset (e.g., the Seal of Biliteracy), offering flexible schedules for students who work, and including parents of English learners in school and district decision-making bodies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be equipped to address and reduce anxiety in the classroom, especially for students learning a second language, with space created for peer-to-peer connection
#### Constraints
- Presented as promising practice recommendations, not as interventions evaluated in this brief

### Target Learners
- English learners from elementary through secondary grades, including working older students and newcomers

### Target Learning Goals
- School belonging, engagement, attendance, and language development
