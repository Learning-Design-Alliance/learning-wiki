# Evaluate GenAI declaration frameworks empirically and pair them with institutional guidance on which tools are in scope

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors propose three strands of future work: empirical evaluation "comparing student declarations against observed workflow data to assess whether declarations track actual behaviour"; pedagogical evaluation of whether the framework changes student reflection, perceived psychological safety around disclosure, and cohort-level patterns of use; and cross-disciplinary adaptation to identify the minimal generalising category set. They also recommend that the declaration "should, thus, be accompanied by clear institutional guidance on which tools are in scope", since IDE autocomplete, writing assistants, and AI-summarising search engines sit on a spectrum with conversational tools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional guidance defining which tools count as GenAI use must accompany the declaration
#### Constraints
- The framework does not itself detect misconduct, and pairing it with detection tools raises well-documented reliability concerns

### Target Learners
- Higher education students and educators in Computer Science and, prospectively, other disciplines

### Target Learning Goals
- Validating that declarations track actual GenAI behaviour
- Improving student reflection and psychological safety around disclosure
