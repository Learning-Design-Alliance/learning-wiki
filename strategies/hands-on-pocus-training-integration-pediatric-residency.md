# Integrate ongoing hands-on POCUS training into pediatric residency curricula to build resident competence and confidence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating POCUS training into pediatric residency programs because residents value it yet report poor comfort, confidence, and knowledge without training. It states that "Developing comprehensive curricula that emphasize hands-on experience could significantly improve both resident competence and patient care outcomes in pediatric settings." Residents' own most preferred proficiency intervention was dedicated practice with an ultrasound machine (40.2%).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured training opportunities during residency, since 68% of residents had received no formal or informal POCUS training
#### Constraints
- The article recommends further studies assessing the impact of educational interventions on pre- and post-knowledge, since its own design was cross-sectional and could not test interventions

### Target Learners
- Pediatric residents (Years 1–3) in tertiary teaching hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- Point-of-care ultrasound image acquisition and interpretation knowledge, comfort, and confidence
