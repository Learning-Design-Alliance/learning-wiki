# Design CKD health IT applications that are multi-platform, iteratively developed, CKD-tailored, and simple for low e-literacy users

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's concluding design recommendations for CKD-directed health IT: applications should be available on multiple platforms, "created using an iterative development process, and tailored closely to the specific demands of the CKD community to improve the likelihood of acceptance". Simplicity is named a vital element, particularly for users with low e-literacy, and the review stresses that health IT should be integrated with — never replace — the provider-patient relationship. It also suggests guidance and coaching by medical professionals will be necessary to establish health apps in patient care.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Iterative development process with the intended audience
- simplicity of the interface
- integration with the provider-patient relationship
#### Constraints
- Health IT can never replace the provider-patient relationship, per the review

### Target Learners
- adults with chronic kidney disease
- users with low e-literacy
- elderly and chronically ill users

### Target Learning Goals
- patient engagement
- self-management
- treatment adherence
