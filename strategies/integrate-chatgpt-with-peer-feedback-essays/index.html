Integrate ChatGPT as a feedback resource alongside peer feedback for essay writing in higher education¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (1 causal, 1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 design), q2 · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends combining ChatGPT-generated and peer-generated feedback for argumentative essay tasks in higher education, since the two sources show complementary strengths: ChatGPT provides extensive descriptive feedback while peers excel at identifying problems. The authors argue this combination "creates a synergistic relationship that could result in better feedback outcomes" and could alleviate teacher workload in large online courses.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to ChatGPT and a peer feedback process using the same feedback prompt for both sources

Constraints¶

  • The study did not investigate whether students actually utilize the feedback or whether essays improve in revision
  • ChatGPT is sensitive to prompt wording, so prompt-related factors should be controlled
  • ChatGPT can potentially generate inaccurate results

Target Learners¶

  • graduate students in higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • argumentative essay writing skills
  • giving and receiving quality feedback

Claims¶

  • ChatGPT produced more descriptive feedback than peers on argumentative essays [+W]
  • Essay quality correlates positively with the affective feature of ChatGPT feedback and negatively with the affective feature of peer feedback [+W]
  • No significant relationship exists between essay quality and the overall quality of feedback generated by peers and ChatGPT [~W]
  • Peers outperformed ChatGPT in identifying problems within essays [+W]
  • Students perceived peer group feedback as contextually rich but sometimes subjective, and ChatGPT-4o feedback as more objective and well structured but occasionally lacking contextual sensitivity [+W]
  • Business Writing students valued AI feedback for higher-order and sentence-level comments but found it sometimes too general, while appreciating peers' contextual knowledge [+W]
  • First-year EAP students prefer a hybrid feedback model combining AI-generated comments with instructor feedback over either source alone [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Combine AI-based feedback on early drafts with human feedback on later drafts, and teach students to evaluate AI feedback critically
  • Use hybrid human–AI feedback models that combine AI's and instructors' complementary strengths
  • Have students compare their peer-feedback revisions with AI-generated revisions to examine dialogic positioning
