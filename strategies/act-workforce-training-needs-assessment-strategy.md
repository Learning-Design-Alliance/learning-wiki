# Assess the current hospital workforce against the competency set to determine training needs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend using the agreed competency set as the reference for workforce development: "the identified clinical competencies provide a benchmark for the recruitment, training and support for ACTs". As a next step they advise "assessing the current hospital-based workforce against these competencies to determine the existing training needs of staff providing specialist care", and ensuring robust processes whereby skills can be acquired and clinical quality of ACTs assessed. The strategy treats the 72 competencies as a curriculum and appraisal framework for building specialist alcohol capability in general hospitals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Appropriate training and organizational support from the hospital management team for successful ACT implementation
#### Constraints
- Evidence largely comes from specialist addiction services, so validity in acute-setting patient groups with different clinical characteristics needs testing

### Target Learners
- senior clinical alcohol practitioners
- hospital staff receiving trust-wide alcohol education and training

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying and managing alcohol use disorders in hospitalized patients
- developing specialist clinical skills across a non-specialist workforce
