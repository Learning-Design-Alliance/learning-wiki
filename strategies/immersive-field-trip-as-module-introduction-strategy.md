# Place an immersive virtual field trip as the introductory experience of an instructional module

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's practical implication is that instructors should "incorporate lessons involving HMD experiences into their instructional programs, particularly as an introduction", because such experiences "can help create interest in the material that leads to better learning engagement and learning outcomes over the course of an instructional module". A virtual field trip as an initial step may create enjoyment that maintains short-term engagement and instill interest supporting long-term engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Subsequent lessons must provide application and reflection opportunities so the interest generated can be maintained across the module.
#### Constraints
- HMD use brings practical challenges in realistic classroom settings; the field trip content must be experienced as things "too expensive, dangerous, or impossible in the real world" to justify immersive delivery.

### Target Learners
- middle school students

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained engagement and long-term retention of science content across a multi-lesson module

### Affordances
- Cognitive Affective Model Of Immersive Learning
