# Teach each word as a schema within its textual context, eliciting its syntactic, semantic and discoursal relationships from learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
In schema-based instruction the teacher teaches entirely in English, pronouncing each schema, having students repeat it, and eliciting its syntactic, semantic and discoursal relationships with other schemata in the passage. The teacher builds on learner input, asks what type a schema is and why, and connects it to learners' personal lives with visual supports. The article reports this produced significantly better structure, vocabulary and reading comprehension than translation-based teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Authentic texts whose constituting words/phrases can be parsed into semantic, syntactic and parasyntactic schemata.
- A teacher who provides appropriate visual and textual contexts and takes an active role, as the article recommends.
#### Constraints
- Persian equivalents were offered only on rare occasions when English explanations were incomprehensible; the study was conducted in a foreign-language academic context with freshman medical students.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate EFL students in medical universities

### Target Learning Goals
- reading comprehension
- structural knowledge
- vocabulary knowledge
