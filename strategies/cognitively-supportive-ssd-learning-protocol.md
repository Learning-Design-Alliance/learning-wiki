# Accelerate sensory substitution learning with a cognitively supportive protocol: immediate feedback, structured forced-choice tasks, repeated exposure, and explicit mental imagery

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article attributes its markedly shorter learning time (above-chance performance within a single 30-minute session) partly to task design: "our learning protocol incorporated cognitively supportive elements, including immediate feedback, structured forced-choice tasks, repeated exposure, and an explicit focus on mental imagery". It states these features are known to enhance perceptual learning efficiency and that such strategies are being implemented for learning SSDs. The strategy is to embed these elements in SSD training regimes, which the authors say opens the door to tailored protocols adapting to individual users' needs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simplified, black-and-white stimuli restricted to cardinal orientations, as the authors note these likely reduced task difficulty in their protocol
#### Constraints
- The study only measured short-term learning effects; long-term retention, generalization, and automatization with extended practice remain untested
- Findings are based on sighted participants; generalizability to blind individuals is an open question the authors state

### Target Learners
- sighted adults learning a visual-to-auditory sensory substitution device
- visually-impaired and blind SSD users (proposed application stated by the authors)

### Target Learning Goals
- rapid recognition of soundscapes corresponding to simple visual shapes and letters
