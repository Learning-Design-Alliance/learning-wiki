Use ChatGPT as a supplementary educational tool alongside traditional study methodology rather than as a standalone resource¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 qualitative), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's included studies repeatedly characterize ChatGPT as a supplementary resource rather than a replacement for conventional study. One included study notes that "a high margin of error (4-16%) emphasizes that it should be used with traditional study methodology." The review supports targeted use of ChatGPT in medical education while cautioning against reliance on it as a sole resource.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Verification of ChatGPT-generated answers by learners or educators, given the reported margin of error

Constraints¶

  • Included studies report hallucinations and unreliable performance in some specialties, rendering it unreliable as a sole resource in some domains

Target Learners¶

  • medical students
  • medical residents

Target Learning Goals¶

  • medical licensing examination preparation
  • clinical knowledge acquisition

Claims¶

  • LLM use as an educational resource showed mixed effects on student learning outcomes compared with conventional methods [+W]
  • Engineering students come to view mastering GenAI as crucial as other engineering skills, driven by labour-market expectations, alongside widespread uncertainty about over-reliance [+W]
  • ChatGPT-4.0 and 3.5 cannot replace the comprehensive skill set required for effective medical practice [+W]
