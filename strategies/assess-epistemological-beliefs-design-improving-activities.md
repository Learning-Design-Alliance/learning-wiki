# Assess students' epistemological beliefs at course entry and design classroom actions aimed at improving them, avoiding practices that reinforce equation-plugging

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors argue that knowing students' epistemological state is a precondition for targeted teaching action: "Knowing the state of our students' epistemological beliefs is crucial in order to design activities aimed at improving them." They warn that everyday classroom practices are part of the hidden curriculum and can unintentionally reinforce novice views of science. The strategy is to administer an instrument such as CLASS, interpret category-level results, and design actions that change novice thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administration of an epistemological-beliefs instrument such as the CLASS to the target group before designing interventions
#### Constraints
- The article applied the test only once, so it documents entry beliefs rather than measuring change from an intervention

### Target Learners
- first-year university students in general physics courses, both physical-science and life-science orientations

### Target Learning Goals
- expert-like epistemological attitudes and problem-solving approaches toward physics
