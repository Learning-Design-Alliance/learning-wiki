# Design formal and informal STEM programs that recognize youths' STEM accomplishments while fostering personal interest in STEM

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend designing and developing formal and informal programs that "recognize youths' various STEM-related accomplishments while fostering personal interest in STEM fields", because their preliminary analysis indicated recognition alone, without personal interest, may not nurture STEM identity. They further suggest targeted interventions to facilitate underrepresented youths' achievements in STEM and call for research on reframing existing recognition approaches in formal and informal STEM learning contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recognition of STEM accomplishments paired with activities that build personal interest or positive disposition toward STEM
#### Constraints
- Grounded in preliminary analysis of five interviews; the authors present it as a suggestion for intervention design, not a tested strategy

### Target Learners
- underrepresented youth, particularly women and ethnic minorities in pre-college STEM settings

### Target Learning Goals
- foster strong STEM identity
- STEM career aspirations

### Affordances
- Stem Identity Precursors Framework
