# Use pre-recorded OSCE-style skills videos with pause and replay to build procedural knowledge before practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends providing students with step-by-step pre-recorded demonstrations of clinical procedures in a time-pressured OSCE-style format. The pre-recorded format "allowed students to focus on those intricate details in greater depth with added stop/start function, and re-watch sections that students found difficult". The authors position this as addressing the 'see one' aspect of skills learning, ensuring students do not practise incorrect technique when in-person opportunities arise.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Videos must be tailored to the undergraduate curriculum and OSCE format
- Quality review of content before delivery
#### Constraints
- Not intended as a substitute for in-person teaching; lacks tactile learning of clinical skills

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical procedural skills
- OSCE preparation
