Extend game-based financial literacy instruction to students with substantial debt and low-to-moderate income adults¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend extending their successful classroom experience beyond campus to groups who need financial knowledge most. They suggest school administrators target students with substantial student loans, and that off campus a good starting group is people seeking tax assistance from the IRS Volunteer Income Tax Assistance (VITA) program, since financial literacy is especially important for these groups.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Free, accessible games such as those used in the course

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on one small case study plus similarity to Tufano et al. (2010), a study of 84 low-income adults across six locations in the US

Target Learners¶

  • college students with substantial student loans
  • low-to-moderate income adults seeking VITA tax assistance

Target Learning Goals¶

  • financial literacy
  • informed financial decision-making

Claims¶

  • A Mann-Whitney U test also showed the game-based session's scores differed statistically from the traditional session's [+W]
  • Final exam scores were significantly higher for the game-based homework session (t = -2.5559, p = 0.0168) [+W]
  • College students assigned free online games as homework scored significantly higher on the midterm than students given traditional assignments [+W]
  • Students initially struggled with game questions but acquired the concepts through repeated play [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Integrate financial education as a requirement in regular high school rather than leaving it as optional summer camp enrollment
  • Extend EFL learning beyond the classroom with daily Web 2.0 interaction alongside traditional teaching
  • Mobilize community colleges to address low-income students' basic needs and financial stability to support completion
