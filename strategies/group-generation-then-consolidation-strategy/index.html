Use group-based generation and exploration followed by teacher-led consolidation and comparison of student-generated and canonical solutions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 4 studies (2 theoretical, 1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q2–q4 · 2 of 4 report an effect size

Description¶

This implementable recipe operationalizes PF in classrooms: assign students to small groups (triads) chosen by the teacher, have them design solutions to a complex problem on an unlearned concept during a two-period generation and exploration phase with no scaffolds, then run a whole-class consolidation phase in which groups share their RSMs and the teacher compares and contrasts them with canonical ways of solving before students practice isomorphic problems. Group work rests on vicarious failure: observing classmates' failed solution attempts also prepares students for learning.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Groups working on a complex problem targeting a not-yet-taught concept
  • A consolidation phase in which the teacher shares canonical solutions and contrasts them with student-generated RSMs

Constraints¶

  • The generation phase intentionally produces failure: none of the groups successfully solved the given problem during that phase

Target Learners¶

  • Seventh- and eighth-grade mathematics students in mainstream public schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Learning novel mathematical concepts (average speed, standard deviation) including procedural fluency, conceptual understanding, and near transfer
