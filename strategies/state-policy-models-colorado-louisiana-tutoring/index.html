State policy models for scaling high-impact tutoring: Colorado's non-competitive grant program and Louisiana's long-term Accelerate initiative¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The brief presents two state approaches as models. Colorado's HB 1234 establishes a statewide tutoring grant program targeting students from low-income families, students of color, and rural students; the bill "makes the grant program non-competitive, meaning any district that's interested and meets the application requirements is eligible for funds," with flexibilities for rural districts. Louisiana's Accelerate initiative provides school-day, just-in-time tutoring using effective teachers, with state-provided staffing and scheduling supports and free aligned tutoring curricula, designed as a long-term strategy to outlast relief funding.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Districts must submit proposals explaining how their tutoring programs align with research-based components of highly effective tutoring programs

Constraints¶

  • The brief notes some Louisiana guidance differs from the research-based guidelines, particularly regarding group sizes

Target Learners¶

  • students from low-income families, students of color, and students in rural areas
  • all students in statewide initiatives

Target Learning Goals¶

  • accelerated learning through personalized instruction during the school day

Related Strategies¶

  • Target tutoring equity by prioritizing underserved students and using culturally reflective curriculum and formative data
  • Expand the pool of potential tutors rather than expanding group sizes when scaling tutoring to all students
