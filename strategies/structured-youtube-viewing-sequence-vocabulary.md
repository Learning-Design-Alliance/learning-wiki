# Structured YouTube viewing sequence for vocabulary lessons: pre-questions, dual viewing with pausing, group discussion, and role-play follow-up

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's administration procedure, following Berk (2009), gives an implementable recipe: teachers write one or two general questions on the board before viewing, students watch a two-minute clip, discuss answers in groups with a representative reporting, then watch again while teachers 'pause and replay the clip to emphasize the new vocabulary'. Groups then prepare a role-play encouraging use of the newly learned vocabulary. The same steps were repeated for the second vocabulary activity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need explicit instructions and materials, and the article reports teachers unanimously agreed they should be trained to use YouTube effectively
#### Constraints
- The procedure was carried out over one module of seven weeks in a single institution with female pre-intermediate students

### Target Learners
- EFL pre-intermediate university students

### Target Learning Goals
- vocabulary recognition, comprehension, retention, and pronunciation
