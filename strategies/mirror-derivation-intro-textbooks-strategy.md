# Replace the electron Lorentz-force demonstration with the mirror-based derivation when introducing radiation pressure to first-year students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors propose that introductory instructors and textbook authors demonstrate radiation pressure using the reflection of a light wave off a near-perfect conducting mirror, a simplified version of Planck's calculation. Its advantage is that "it avoids consideration of the force acting on a point charge and can therefore be carried out at the purely class ical level". They state it "should be adopted by introductory textbook author s" and is accessible to students with basic exposure to Maxwell's equations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students have had a basic exposure to Maxwell's equations, as the derivation uses Faraday's and Ampère's laws in differential form.
#### Constraints
- The derivation rests on tacit assumptions, including that the magnetic field drops to zero inside the good conductor and that the field generated by induced currents exerts no net force on those currents.

### Target Learners
- first-year (freshman) physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding that light carries momentum and exerts radiation pressure in the direction of propagation
