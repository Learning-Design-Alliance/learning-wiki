# Self-Monitoring of Behavior

## Description
Self-monitoring is a strategy where learners self-assess their behavior and record the results, increasing or decreasing the frequency, intensity, or duration of existing behavior. It involves students keeping track of whether they are on-task, compliant, and following rules and routines, essentially evaluating their own behavior. This can be applied to various aspects, such as attention (SMA) or academic performance (SMP). Self-monitoring saves instructors time monitoring learner behavior.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a self-monitoring form or tally sheet, a method for signaling time intervals (e.g., timer, beep tape), and instructor guidance and support. Clear definitions of the behavior to be monitored are essential.
#### Constraints
- Learners may be tempted to rate their behavior more positively than is accurate, necessitating monitoring by the instructor. Requires initial training and ongoing support to ensure accurate and consistent implementation.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learner levels, including K–12, higher education, and adult learners, particularly those needing support in attention, task completion, and social skills.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Increase learner awareness of their own behavior, improve specific behaviors (e.g., attention, task completion, social interactions), and promote self-regulation

### Affordances
- Provides immediate feedback, engages learners actively, facilitates communication between learners and instructors, and promotes self-awareness. It also fosters a sense of responsibility and independence.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted to various behaviors and settings. The self-monitoring form can be personalized to reflect the learner's age, developmental level, and individual needs. Cues and prompts can be tailored to the specific behavior being monitored.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track changes in the frequency, intensity, or duration of the target behavior. Compare baseline data with post-implementation data to assess effectiveness. Monitor the agreement between learner self-assessments and instructor observations to ensure accuracy.

## Impact
- Studies have shown positive changes in social behaviors, completion of written work, ability to follow directions, and academic engagement. Improves on-task behavior, reduces disruptive behavior, and enhances listening skills.

## Examples
- A teacher implements SMA (self-monitoring of attention) with a student who has difficulty paying attention during instruction, using an audio tape with tones to signal time intervals. SMP (self-monitoring of performance) is used with a student who needs to monitor the rate at which they correctly complete class work or the overall accuracy of their performance.
