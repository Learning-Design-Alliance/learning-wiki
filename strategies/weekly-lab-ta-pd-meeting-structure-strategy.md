# Use weekly meetings with situational-interest reflections, role-playing on sabotaged experiments, and mid-semester wrap-up to build lab TA buy-in and skill

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy describes the concrete meeting structure the article used to build TA motivation and skill. Following the interest framework of Hidi and Renninger, meetings open with all TAs sharing a student interaction they found surprising, concerning, or encouraging, to "trigger situational interest in their work as TAs" and reinforce it through meaningful social reflection. Most of the meeting is dedicated to one or two learning activities, such as role-playing student-TA interactions around ‘sabotaged’ experiments in which the apparatus is miscalibrated or the analysis incorrectly done, followed by debriefing. Meetings wrap up mid-semester so TAs practice independently.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- a training leader moderating discussions using standard methods for establishing and maintaining positive interactivity
#### Constraints
- the program was developed via extensive discussions and iterations between three experienced physics educators at one large research university

### Target Learners
- graduate student teaching assistants teaching introductory physics labs

### Target Learning Goals
- confidence as educators, proficiency with apparatus, and motivation to support student learning

### Affordances
- Cognitive Apprenticeship Lab Ta Pd Framework
