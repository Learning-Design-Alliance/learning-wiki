# Use patient-specific 3D-printed phantoms to educate junior surgeons and preoperative patients and for preoperative rehearsal planning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend deploying the reusable patient-specific phantom across several educational uses: training inexperienced junior surgeons, explaining disease and surgery to patients scheduled for operations, pre-operative planning, and surgical guidance. "This phantom can also be used for various educational purposes, such as educating inexperienced junior surgeons or patients scheduled for surgery to provide them a better understanding of the disease and surgical procedures." Rehearsal simulation of complex anatomical variations in advance is said to reduce surgeons' effort, time, and burden.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A patient- and disease-specific internal model generated from the patient's medical images for the most accurate procedure
#### Constraints
- A patient- and disease-specific simulator based on the medical image of the patient could be expensive

### Target Learners
- inexperienced junior surgeons
- patients scheduled for surgery

### Target Learning Goals
- better understanding of the disease and surgical procedures
- pre-operative planning of complex anatomical variations
