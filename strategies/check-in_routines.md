# Check In_Routines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A check-in routine is a short, predictable opening activity in which every learner briefly reports on their emotional state, well-being, progress, or readiness to work. Formats range from a one-word mood share to a rating scale, a quick written prompt, or a round-robin question. The routine serves two functions simultaneously: it builds relational trust and belonging, and it surfaces information the instructor can use to adjust the day's plan.

## Design Implications

Check-ins operationalize social-emotional learning at low cost: brief structured SEL practices produce measurable gains in achievement and behavior when embedded consistently [Durlak et al.'s meta-analysis of school-based SEL](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2010.01564.x) [+S]. They also support belonging, which is a precondition for engagement and persistence, particularly for learners at risk of feeling marginal [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. Because check-ins give learners a voice in how the session begins, they function as an autonomy-supportive practice rather than a compliance ritual [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A predictable, low-stakes format (same time, same structure) so the routine itself does not consume working memory
- A mechanism for every learner to respond — verbal round-robin, digital poll, sticky note, or private form — so participation does not depend on willingness to speak publicly
- Instructor follow-through: the data must visibly influence the session, or learners disengage from the ritual

#### Constraints
- Publicly sharing emotional states can expose or embarrass learners who are struggling; anonymous or opt-out channels mitigate this [~M]
- If responses are never acted on, the routine becomes an empty ritual and loses its relational and informational value [-M]
- Poorly framed questions ("how is everyone?") invite social desirability answers rather than usable information [-W]
- Time cost is real: in short sessions, an unbounded check-in crowds out instructional time [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Mood scale** (e.g., 1–5 or emoji) — fastest; suited to large groups and digital delivery
- **One-word round** — builds vocabulary for emotional articulation; suited to cohort-based classes
- **Progress check** ("where are you on the task?") — shifts the routine toward [Formative Assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md) and informs pacing
- **Themed prompt** ("what's one thing blocking you?") — connects the ritual to the day's content
- **Game-based variants** such as ['What's My Emotion?' Game Check-In](../strategies/whats_my_emotion_game_check-in.md) — lower the social barrier for younger or reluctant learners

### Target Learners
- K–12 learners, where daily SEL routines show the strongest effects [SEL programs improve achievement and behavior.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2010.01564.x) [+S]
- Online and cohort-based adult learners, where check-ins substitute for ambient social cues and build [Community of Inquiry](../principles/community-of-inquiry.md) presence [+M]
- Learners experiencing transition or low belonging, who benefit most from structured signals of being seen [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Social-emotional: emotional literacy, self-awareness, self-regulation
- Metacognitive: surfacing readiness, confusion, and task progress before instruction begins
- Community-building: establishing psychological safety that supports later [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) and discussion

### Instructions
1. **Choose a fixed format and time** — the first 3–5 minutes of each session; predictability is what makes it low-cost ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md))
2. **Select a question matched to purpose** — emotional state, task progress, or content readiness; keep it answerable in one sentence
3. **Guarantee a response channel for everyone** — verbal, written, or anonymous; pair public rounds with a private option ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md))
4. **Acknowledge and act** — name patterns aloud ("several of us are stuck on step 2, let's revisit it") so learners see the routine has consequences ([Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md))
5. **Close the loop later** — follow up privately with learners who signal distress or persistent struggle ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))

## Examples
- **Responsive Classroom (Center for Responsive Schools)** — the "Morning Meeting" greeting and sharing rounds are a canonical whole-class check-in routine used across thousands of US elementary schools. [https://www.responsivereads.com](https://www.responsivereads.com)
- **Zoom/Slack mood polls in online cohorts** — instructors open synchronous sessions with a one-click emoji poll; results are visible to all and shape the session opener.
- **Advisory periods in secondary schools** — daily check-ins paired with goal-setting, consistent with the SEL structures catalogued by [CASEL](https://casel.org/fundamentals-of-sel/).
