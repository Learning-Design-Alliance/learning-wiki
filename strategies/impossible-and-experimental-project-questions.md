# Use deliberately impossible or experimentally answerable project questions to reveal student understanding and integrate theory with experiment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author proposes two future question-design possibilities for project-based exams. One is to set questions difficult to the point of impossibility, since the paths students attempt and the points at which they abandon them may demonstrate understanding better than a straightforward successful solution. The other is to include problems that can involve experimental measurements, allowing close integration of theory and experiment in a theoretical course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- If questions may be impossible, students should be warned beforehand of the possibility to avoid excess student anguish and stress.
#### Constraints
- These possibilities are stated as untested future directions in this paper, not as trialled results.

### Target Learners
- honours/masters level physics coursework students

### Target Learning Goals
- problem-solving approach and understanding
- integration of theory and experiment
