# Use continuing education as a mechanism to build subdiscipline expertise among anatomy teachers lacking graduate coursework

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends continuing education and on-the-job training as pathways for anatomy teachers to gain traditional subdiscipline expertise they did not obtain in graduate school, noting "continuing education represents a mechanism to prepare and develop anatomy faculty." In the survey, 11% of professors reported assigned time for continuing education and 16% reported spending time on it, and prior work cited by the authors found roughly half of anatomy teachers had recent anatomy-related continuing education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Availability of anatomy-related continuing education opportunities and, where used, on-the-job training such as participating in a course before teaching it
#### Constraints
- It is unclear whether there is a difference in the quality of subdiscipline education between graduate coursework and continuing education; effects of continuing education or on-the-job training remain unclear

### Target Learners
- US anatomy teachers lacking graduate coursework in one or more traditional anatomical sciences subdisciplines

### Target Learning Goals
- Building teaching-ready knowledge of gross anatomy, histology, neuroanatomy, and embryology
