# Use peer observers with profession-matched checklists during simulation scenarios to shift debriefing control from facilitators to participants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the MINTS-DR courses, colleagues not involved in a scenario observed the performance on a display screen and were given NTS checklists matching their own profession before the scenario began. After each scenario they filled in the checklists and provided feedback to their colleagues during debriefing, referring to their forms. The authors report this moved the locus of control from the facilitators to the observers, with facilitators supporting rather than leading the feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A separate quiet observation room with a display screen showing the scene from multiple points of view and clinical parameters; observers given checklists before the scenario and instructed to focus on colleagues' performance
#### Constraints
- One observer per scenario instead used the global team observation checklist rather than observing individual colleagues

### Target Learners
- multi-professional delivery room teams in simulation training

### Target Learning Goals
- peer feedback on non-technical skills and metacognitive reflection during debriefing
