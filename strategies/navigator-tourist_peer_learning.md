# Navigator-Tourist Peer Learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Navigator-Tourist Peer Learning assigns complementary roles within a dyad or small group: learners who have demonstrated mastery ("navigators") guide learners still developing the skill ("tourists"). Navigators explain directions, diagnose confusion, and keep the pair on task; tourists ask clarifying questions, attempt problems, and articulate their reasoning back. The metaphor makes role expectations concrete and gives both partners an explicit, teachable job.

## Design Implications

The strategy works through two mechanisms that operate in opposite directions for each partner. Tourists benefit from one-on-one explanation calibrated to their current understanding, while navigators benefit from the [learning-by-teaching](../elements/articulation.md) effect: preparing to teach and explaining aloud forces retrieval, organization, and gap-detection in the navigator's own knowledge [Explaining to another person improves the explainer's conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. Peer tutoring of this kind reliably outperforms individual work and, in structured formats, teacher-led instruction for both tutees and tutors [Same-age peer tutoring improves achievement for tutors and tutees.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.95.2.240) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Valid identification of navigators — mastery must be verified, not assumed, or tourists receive inaccurate instruction
- Explicit role scripts: what navigators should say when a tourist is stuck, what tourists should ask ([Articulation](../elements/articulation.md) prompts help)
- Training for navigators in explanation and questioning, since untrained tutors often simply give answers [Tutor effectiveness depends on tutor training and structured materials.](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543059003321) [~M]
- Teacher monitoring and a mechanism for tourists to flag unhelpful pairings ([Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md) or exit tickets)

#### Constraints
- Unstructured pairs drift off task or the navigator simply does the work for the tourist, producing no learning for either partner [Peer interaction without individual accountability produces uneven participation and unequal learning gains.](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543059003321) [-M]
- Navigators with fragile mastery can transmit misconceptions; errors that go uncorrected in the dyad are consolidated for both learners
- Fixed navigator/tourist roles can create status hierarchies that depress tourist engagement and motivation; rotating roles mitigates this
- Poor fit for content neither partner has access to — the strategy transfers knowledge, it does not introduce it

#### Implementation Variability
- **Cross-age tutoring**: older navigators with younger tourists, useful when no same-class mastery exists
- **Role rotation**: partners alternate navigator/tourist roles across tasks, converting the structure into reciprocal teaching
- **Materials-mediated**: navigator guides using a structured worksheet or [worked examples](../principles/worked-examples.md) rather than free-form explanation, reducing reliance on navigator fluency
- **Group format**: one navigator with two or three tourists, trading intimacy for coverage

### Target Learners
- Tourists: learners needing academic support who benefit from individualized, low-stakes explanation and questioning [Same-age peer tutoring improves achievement for tutors and tutees.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.95.2.240) [+S]
- Navigators: learners with verified mastery who consolidate and deepen understanding through teaching [Explaining to another person improves the explainer's conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]
- Less effective when the ability gap is very large — navigators far above the tourist struggle to calibrate explanations to the tourist's level [Tutor effectiveness depends on tutor training and structured materials.](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543059003321) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency and skill consolidation in well-structured domains (mathematics, reading, language learning)
- Metacognitive and communication skills: questioning, explaining, monitoring another's understanding
- Not well suited to introducing novel concepts or open-ended inquiry where no partner holds expertise

### Instructions
1. Verify mastery of candidate navigators with a short assessment before assigning roles.
2. Pair navigators and tourists; keep ability gaps moderate and consider personality and working relationships.
3. Train navigators in explanation moves: ask before telling, use [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md), check understanding with questions rather than "got it?"
4. Provide a structured task with materials both partners can see ([Practice](../elements/practice.md) items, worked examples, or a problem set).
5. Set interaction norms: tourists must attempt before asking; navigators must prompt before answering ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) rather than telling).
6. Circulate and monitor; intervene when navigators give answers instead of explanations or when tourists are passive.
7. Debrief with both roles — tourists report what helped; navigators identify what was hard to explain, which surfaces their own gaps.
8. Rotate roles periodically so tourists gain teaching experience and navigators receive support ([Fading](../elements/fading.md) of the role structure).

## Examples
- **Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS)** ([Vanderbilt Kennedy Center](https://vkc.mc.vanderbilt.edu/vkc/pals/)) — a researched peer-tutoring program in reading and math in which stronger and weaker students alternate coach/player roles; the role rotation is the key design difference from one-way tutoring.
- **Cross-age reading buddies** — upper-elementary navigators read with and question younger tourists weekly, a common structure in UK and US primary schools.
- **Recitation sections in introductory STEM courses** — students who completed the course with high marks lead problem-solving sessions for current students, combining navigator explanation with structured [practice](../elements/practice.md) materials.
