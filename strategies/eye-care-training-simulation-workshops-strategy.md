# Integrate comprehensive eye care training with evidence-based guidelines, hands-on workshops, and simulation-based learning into nursing education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that training programs for ICU nurses go beyond lectures by combining evidence-based guidelines, hands-on practice, and simulation. It states: "these training programs should incorporate evidence‐based guidelines, hands‐on workshops, and simulation‐based learning approaches." The recommendation rests on findings that only 14% of nurses had formal eye care education and that structured certification was associated with better practical competency. Hospitals are also advised to establish standardized protocols and routine audits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained instructors and institutional support for workshops and simulation-based sessions
- Integration into undergraduate, postgraduate, and in-service nursing curricula
#### Constraints
- The study's own null finding on existing formal training suggests training alone, without practical reinforcement and standardized protocols, may not improve competence

### Target Learners
- ICU nurses
- nursing students
- postgraduate nursing trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- eye care knowledge
- preventive eye care practices
- ocular complication risk recognition in critically ill patients
