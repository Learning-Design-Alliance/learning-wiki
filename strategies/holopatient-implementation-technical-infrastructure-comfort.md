# Address technical infrastructure and user comfort (Wi-Fi reliability, cybersickness) when implementing holographic simulation education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (1 for, 5 mixed) · 6 studies (4 qualitative, 1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 6 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that successful implementation of HoloPatient-based simulation requires attention to technical infrastructure and user comfort alongside evidence of educational effectiveness. Students reported that inconsistent Wi-Fi prevented the HoloPatient application from opening quickly on HoloLens, and some wearers experienced cybersickness symptoms such as nausea and dizziness. The conclusion states these practical insights offer guidance for future applications of holographic simulation in nursing education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reliable, consistent Wi-Fi connections capable of supporting simultaneous download and access of HoloPatient on MR devices
#### Constraints
- Cybersickness among HoloLens wearers limited some students’ ability to wear the device for more than a few minutes

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students in simulation-based courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained engagement and immersion in holographic simulation learning
