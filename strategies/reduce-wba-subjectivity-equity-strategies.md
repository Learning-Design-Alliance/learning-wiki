# Reduce subjectivity in workplace-based assessment through clear rating criteria, reduced performance pressure, and programmatic assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings that ethnic bias did not appear in first impressions but minority students may face greater scrutiny, the authors propose three solutions for equitable assessment. They recommend "clear rating criteria that are known to both students and assessors, minimizing the ambiguity and subjectivity of WBAs", mention eliminating grades and distinctions like 'cum laude' to reduce performance pressure and stress, and propose adopting programmatic assessment for its holistic view of student progress and promise of enabling equity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rating criteria must be known to both students and assessors
#### Constraints
- The authors note ethnic differences may manifest more subtly than direct visual observations, through accents, communication styles, social norms and values

### Target Learners
- medical students, particularly ethnic minority students in clinical traineeships

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable workplace-based assessment of clinical competence
