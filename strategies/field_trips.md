# Field Trips

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Field trips are structured excursions that take learners out of the classroom to observe, interact with, and make sense of authentic environments — museums, nature reserves, workplaces, historical sites, or (virtually) inaccessible locations. They situate learning in physical and cultural context, connecting abstract classroom content to concrete places, objects, and people. Effective trips are not outings but instructional designs: they include preparation before, structured engagement during, and consolidation after.

## Design Implications

Field trips work as learning events when they are integrated into instruction rather than appended to it. Research on museum and science-center visits shows that learning outcomes depend on the *contextual model* of learning — personal, sociocultural, and physical context interacting — so trips succeed when they activate prior knowledge, focus attention with clear tasks, and are followed by structured reflection [DeWitt & Storksdieck's review finds novelty and orientation strongly shape what is learned on trips.](https://doi.org/10.1080/10645570802355562) [~M]. Free-choice exploration alone produces engagement but unreliable content learning; guided tasks paired with autonomy produce both.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear curricular linkage: a stated learning goal the trip serves, not a generic "enrichment" rationale
- Pre-visit preparation that activates relevant prior knowledge and sets an observation agenda ([Activation](../elements/activation.md), [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
- Structured in-site tasks — observation prompts, sketching, data collection, [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) — that direct attention without eliminating choice
- Post-visit consolidation: [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), writing, or [Application](../elements/application.md) tasks that convert episodic experience into retrievable knowledge
- Logistical planning: transport, permissions, funding, accessibility, and safety; for virtual trips, devices and connectivity

#### Constraints
- Novelty of the setting can overwhelm instructional content — learners attend to the unusual environment rather than the target material unless orientation and tasks are provided [~S]
- Overly scripted trips suppress the curiosity and free exploration that make informal settings valuable; the effect is conditional on the balance struck [~M]
- Cost and logistics disproportionately exclude disadvantaged learners when funding is uneven; poorly subsidized trips can widen rather than narrow opportunity gaps [-M]
- Virtual field trips reduce sensory richness, embodied experience, and social interaction, weakening the episodic-memory advantage of physical presence [~W]
- A single trip without follow-up produces strong memories but little durable conceptual change; consolidation activities are not optional [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Physical trips**: museums, field sites, workplaces, community organizations; vary in structure from guided tours to open inquiry
- **Virtual trips**: VR tours and online museum collections extend access to inaccessible or costly locations at near-zero marginal cost
- **Community-based variants**: service learning and site-based projects embed trips in sustained local partnerships rather than one-off visits

### Target Learners
- K–12 learners, for whom novel, concrete experiences are especially effective at building schemas for abstract content [~M]
- Disadvantaged learners, who benefit most when trips provide experiences more affluent peers receive at home — the Crystal Bridges study found the largest gains in critical thinking and empathy among students from high-poverty and rural schools [Bowen & Kisida found art museum trips improved critical thinking, with larger effects for disadvantaged students.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X13512675) [+M]
- Adult and professional learners in site visits and workplace excursions, where the trip functions as observation of authentic practice

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual grounding: connecting abstract ideas to concrete instances and places
- Attitudes and motivation: increasing interest, curiosity, and cultural engagement — attitudinal effects are among the most consistently documented outcomes [+M]
- Social-emotional goals: empathy and tolerance, particularly for art and history trips [Bowen & Kisida found art museum trips improved tolerance and empathy measures.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X13512675) [+M]
- Observational and inquiry skills: noticing, describing, and questioning in authentic settings

### Instructions
1. Define the learning goal and select a site (or virtual environment) that authentically embodies it.
2. Run pre-visit activities that activate prior knowledge and set an observation agenda — [Activation](../elements/activation.md) tasks, [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md), and prediction prompts.
3. During the trip, assign light-structure tasks (observation prompts, sketching, question generation) while preserving learner choice and social interaction.
4. Immediately after, consolidate through [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), reflective writing, or artifact creation.
5. Apply the experience back to classroom content through an [Application](../elements/application.md) task that requires learners to use what they observed.

## Related Strategies
- Activating prior knowledge — the pre-visit phase is a high-leverage instance of this strategy
- Acting and role-play — on-site dramatization and docent role-play deepen engagement with historical or scientific settings

## Related Elements
- [Activation](../elements/activation.md) — pre-visit preparation determines what learners can extract from the experience
- [Application](../elements/application.md) — post-visit tasks convert episodic memory into usable knowledge
- [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) — the primary consolidation mechanism after the visit
- [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) — field notebooks and sketching direct attention during the visit
- [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) — give learners a structure for interpreting an unfamiliar environment

## Patterns That Use This Strategy
- [Anchored Instruction](../patterns/anchored-instruction.md) — the site functions as a real-world anchor for problem-based learning

## Examples
- **Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art (Arkansas, USA)** — randomized studies of school tours found gains in critical thinking, historical empathy, tolerance, and later cultural consumption, largest for disadvantaged students ([Bowen & Kisida, 2014](https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X13512675)).
- **Google Arts & Culture virtual tours** ([artsandculture.google.com](https://artsandculture.google.com)) — successor to the discontinued Google Expeditions VR program, letting classes explore reefs, ruins, and museum collections remotely.
- **School science field programs** (e.g., outdoor education centers and nature-reserve school programs) — pre-visit lessons, on-site data collection, and post-visit analysis of student-collected data.
