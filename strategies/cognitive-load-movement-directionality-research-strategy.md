# Future research should test whether physical tasks with heavier cognitive loads produce greater cognitive gains than low-cognitive-demand activity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The article recommends that follow-up work move beyond cross-sectional correlation to test directionality and the cognitive load of the physical task itself. It states: "Of particular interest in this regard would be the directionality of the academic relationship to complex tasks, to see if physical tasks with heavier cognitive loads also lead to greater cognitive gains than physical activity with a low cognitive demand." This strategy frames complex-movement tasks as a research lever for learning effects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal or experimental designs that allow cause-and-effect examination, since the cross-sectional dataset does not
#### Constraints
- The current single-cohort, single-school, pedometer-only, cross-sectional design cannot establish directionality

### Target Learners
- elementary school students (fourth graders)

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement across literacy, mathematics, social studies, science, and English
