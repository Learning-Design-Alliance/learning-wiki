# Balance optical-alignment practice with completing experiments through sequencing, shared setups, and instructor realignment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because fully setting up the apparatus from scratch takes more time than students have, instructors use strategies to let students practice optical alignment while still obtaining data. For shorter guided labs, some instructors "chose the order of the experiments such that students built on prior weeks’ work," so students could perform complicated experiments in less time by the end of class. Other approaches included rotating which student group performs the alignment for each experiment, leaving the alignment from a prior group in place for the next group, and having instructors step in on an as-needed basis to improve students' alignment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor time and availability to realign setups between or during lab sessions
#### Constraints
- When students set up the apparatus entirely from scratch, the experiments take more time to perform than students have during a course

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in quantum and beyond-first-year lab courses

### Target Learning Goals
- optical alignment skills
- completing the experiments and obtaining data within course time
