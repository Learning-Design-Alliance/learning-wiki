# Introduce practical life materials into non-Montessori classrooms to support fine motor skills and attention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review concludes that "the practical life materials can be usefully introduced into non-Montessori classrooms to support the development of young children's fine motor skills and attention". Practical life activities involve pouring, using utensils, cleaning, preparing snacks and arranging flowers, and aim to build gross and fine motor control, eye-hand coordination and the cycle of selecting, initiating, completing and tidying up an activity. Quasi-experimental studies reviewed support this transferable use without adopting other Montessori elements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time must be set aside in the timetable for using the materials, with additional free-choice availability
#### Constraints
- Attention benefits were observed only for girls, with boys showing no such benefits
- For fine motor control, benefit was securely attributable only when no other Montessori elements were introduced

### Target Learners
- Young children in conventional kindergarten classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- Fine motor control and eye-hand coordination
- Attention development
- Skills for independent living
