# Support multi-directional thinking activities and technology-adapted engaging lessons for abstract science topics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "Activities that allow students to think multi-directionally should be supported to ensure better comprehension of abstract science topics" and that "Students should be encouraged to think multi-directionally rather than thinking in a uniform manner by using methods and techniques such as the six hats thinking technique." It further recommends making lessons more fun by adapting methods to technology and developing activities that increase permanency of learning rather than memorization.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to technology or web-assisted materials
- Teacher willingness to move beyond traditional transmission-style teaching
#### Constraints
- Based on a single semi-experimental study with 50 students in one topic area

### Target Learners
- 7th-grade secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Comprehension of abstract science topics
- Permanent learning rather than memorization
