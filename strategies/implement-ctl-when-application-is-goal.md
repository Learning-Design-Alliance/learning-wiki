# Implement contextual teaching and learning where application of knowledge is the instructional goal, with confidence that basic content mastery is maintained

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educators wanting the benefits of contextualized teaching and learning can implement it "with more peace of mind that students will still learn the basics", since the contextualized curriculum performed comparably to the scientific-basis alternative on content mastery. They argue that if the goal is teaching students to apply scientific information in a contextual setting, the contextual approach provides a model for student learning and thinking, teaching students to think contextually while learning scientific concepts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Empirical support is currently limited to content mastery; the authors call for studies measuring students' ability to apply knowledge and think critically
#### Constraints
- Based on one quasi-experimental study with small classes at a single university; the authors recommend replication with larger class sizes, same-semester classes, and other universities

### Target Learners
- postsecondary students in science and agriculture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- application of scientific knowledge in real-world contexts
- mastery of scientific content

## Claims

- [Both treatment groups showed large pretest-to-posttest gains in scientific concept mastery, with the contextual group gaining 30.00 points and the comparison group 26.72 points](../claims/both-treatments-large-prepost-gains.md) [+W]
