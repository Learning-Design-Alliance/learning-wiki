# Design simulation scenarios around foreseeable crisis problems so participants practice troubleshooting skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend drafting simulation scenarios in consultation with education experts and deliberately including problems that might arise in a crisis, so participants can practice the skills required to troubleshoot them. Their four ECMO scenarios each targeted a specific failure: sweep gas not turned on, accidental circuit disconnection, line rattling from a volume-pressure mismatch, and air in the circuit. Debriefing after each scenario addressed both clinical and nonclinical skills in an interactive manner.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios should be drafted in consultation with medical education experts to maximize the potential to deliver specific learning outcomes.
#### Constraints
- Two delegates noted there were limitations as to how real a simulated ECMO scenario could be.

### Target Learners
- ECMO specialists in training
- intensive care nurses and consultants

### Target Learning Goals
- troubleshooting ECMO emergencies
- communication and leadership during crises
