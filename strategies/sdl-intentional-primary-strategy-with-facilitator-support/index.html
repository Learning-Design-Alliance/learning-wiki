Introduce SDL intentionally as the primary educational strategy with structured facilitator support balanced against learner autonomy¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that SDL "is most likely to succeed when introduced intentionally as the primary instructional strategy, when facilitator support is balanced with learner autonomy, and when the modality aligns with the targeted learning outcomes." They further suggest that "structured facilitator support could enhance SDL effectiveness, though this trend did not reach statistical significance." This strategy responds to their finding that SDL yielded significantly larger effects when used as the experimental intervention rather than a control condition, and that teacher-absent SDL may be limited for developing clinical or behavioral competencies.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • SDL must be introduced intentionally as the primary educational strategy rather than as a superficial comparator

Constraints¶

  • The facilitator-support trend did not reach statistical significance in the moderator analysis

Target Learners¶

  • health professions learners across undergraduate and postgraduate training

Target Learning Goals¶

  • knowledge acquisition, clinical skill development

Claims¶

  • Most SDL-labeled interventions lack authentic learner autonomy in resource selection and self-assessment [+W]
  • Adult learners expect AI to enhance autonomy through choices, flexible learning spaces, metacognitive awareness, and structured self-assessment [+W]
  • SDL modality and teacher role do not significantly moderate SDL effectiveness [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Label interventions as SDL only when learners have genuine autonomy in resource selection and self-assessment
