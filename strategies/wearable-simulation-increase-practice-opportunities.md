# Increase patients' practice opportunities with an inexpensive wearable simulation tool to overcome limited nurse training time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using simulation tools combined with operating videos and manuals as the standard process for insulin injection training, because the lack of opportunities to practice is an important reason for insufficient insulin injections and busy nurses leave patients few chances to repeat injections. The tool "can be used repeatedly, allowing patients to get enough practice to master insulin injection skills in a short time" and is economical enough for use in developing countries.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A wearable simulation tool with a large injection area that is easy to clean and maintain, plus videos and manuals for review.
#### Constraints
- The device only simulates abdominal injection; the article notes most trainings start at the abdomen, so practicing other sites after proficiency is not difficult.

### Target Learners
- Diabetic patients in endocrinology and other clinical departments; nursing and medical students as a teaching aid

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe, standardized insulin injection technique
