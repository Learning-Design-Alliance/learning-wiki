# Teach the integral form of Maxwell's equations as describing field correlations before introducing the differential form

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article notes the integral form of Maxwell's equations is usually taught first and endorses this sequencing with a specific framing: the integral forms "describe field correlations, and I agree that this is what we should teach students until they are ready for the locally causal theory". The differential form is then introduced when students are ready for locally causal explanations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must later encounter the differential form, since locally causal explanation of electrodynamics requires it
#### Constraints
- The integral form is not locally causal because it describes field behavior over extended regions

### Target Learners
- physics students progressing from introductory to advanced electromagnetism

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the causal structure of Maxwell's equations

### Affordances
- Local Causality Fields As Causal Agents
