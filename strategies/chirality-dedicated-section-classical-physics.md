# Introduce chirality with a dedicated section in classical physics, before quantum mechanics, to connect disparate topics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends teaching chirality much earlier than quantum mechanics, where parity operator formalism usually first appears. It argues a dedicated section provides "a connection between otherwise disparate topics such as the inversion of a plane mirror, solenoid handedness, various right-hand rules, circular polarizations, and threaded ropes and fasteners." Mirrors and knots are chosen because they visually display chirality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rotation matrices familiar to undergraduates, since group theory is generally not part of the standard undergraduate curriculum
#### Constraints
- The treatment uses only basic matrix operations, avoiding the full formalism of the three-dimensional point group

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understand chirality and handedness
- understand symmetries in physical law
