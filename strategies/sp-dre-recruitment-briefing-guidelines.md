# Action guidelines for recruiting, informing and briefing standardized patients for intimate examination (DRE) programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article derives a set of action guidelines from the SP interviews for the recruitment, informing and briefing of SPs willing to participate in a DRE program. The guidelines include "Exclusively appointing experienced SPs (previous experience in delivering physical examination skills)", conversation about motives and possible worries, clarifying possible mental strain and social bemusement, actively addressing the possibility to withdraw at any time, creating calm protected conditions, familiar team presence, action guidelines for inappropriate student behaviour, and informing students about respectful conditions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- SP programs need comprehensive information talks covering potential psychological reactions and social-environment reactions before, during or after participation
- First teaching assignments should occur only in the presence of and supported by personnel familiar to the SPs
#### Constraints
- Guidelines were derived from interviews with only four SPs in a single program; the authors note the exploratory nature of the research may restrict generalisability

### Target Learners
- standardized patients being prepared to teach intimate physical examination skills
- undergraduate medical students taught by these SPs

### Target Learning Goals
- digital rectal examination skills
- professional feedback and respectful patient-student interaction during intimate examination training
