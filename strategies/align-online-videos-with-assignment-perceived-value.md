# Align online video design and incentives with the concrete content students perceive as needed for assignment completion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors revise courses so videos better support student learning, noting students prioritize content explicitly required for assignments. Student survey feedback supports making applications explicit: "It would be helpful if the videos made it clearer which formulas were important," and requests for "more examples" were frequently found. Instructors should also recognize that without grade incentives, students may treat lecture videos as supplementary unless they see their value for coursework.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Videos tied to observable assignment requirements (actions, formulas, examples)
- Instructor awareness that ungraded videos depend on perceived value
#### Constraints
- Without direct grade incentives, students may gradually disengage from lecture-oriented videos
- Computer-based quizzes allowing many submissions may further reduce motivation to watch lecture videos

### Target Learners
- university students in blended courses

### Target Learning Goals
- completing laboratory assignments and problem-solving tasks
