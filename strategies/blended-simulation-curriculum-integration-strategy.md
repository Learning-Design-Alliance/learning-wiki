# Integrate multiple simulation modalities into a blended simulation-based surgical curriculum rather than using simulation as an adjunct

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends embedding simulation into the fabric of surgical education: "Simulation-based curricula often include a blend of theoretical instruction, practical skills training, and assessment." It further advises that "curricula may incorporate a variety of simulation modalities, each targeting different aspects of surgical competence", for example VR for initial anatomical familiarization and physical simulators for dexterity and technique. This multi-faceted approach is presented as ensuring trainees develop both cognitive and technical competencies, and the review argues widespread adoption requires simulation to become a core component rather than a supplement to traditional training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured curricula combining theoretical instruction, practical skills training, and assessment
- Institutional commitment to shift delivery toward simulation-based training and assessment
#### Constraints
- High costs, access disparities, lack of standardization, and resistance to change within the surgical education community hinder broader adoption, per the review

### Target Learners
- general surgery residents
- surgical educators and program directors

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical competence spanning cognitive and technical skills

### Affordances
- Surgical Simulation Technology Taxonomy
