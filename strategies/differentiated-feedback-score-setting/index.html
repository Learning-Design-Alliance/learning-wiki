Set feedback difficulty according to children's initial graphomotor level when using handwriting apps¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that future handwriting-app protocols differentiate the feedback children receive according to their initial graphomotor level, because a single feedback setting benefited only medium-level learners while low-level learners may have ignored overly demanding feedback. The authors state that "A differentiated score setting that allows feedback to be delivered according to the children's initial graphomotor level could be considered in future experimental protocols," and also suggest testing spatial and kinematic feedback separately for low-level writers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An app able to adjust feedback thresholds or composition per learner, based on assessed initial level
  • Knowledge of each child's initial graphomotor level

Constraints¶

  • Stated for low and high initial-level learners; the tested undifferentiated setting showed benefit only for medium-level learners

Target Learners¶

  • kindergarten beginning writers with low, medium or high initial handwriting levels

Target Learning Goals¶

  • cursive handwriting acquisition and transfer to paper

Claims¶

  • Kindergarteners with a high initial handwriting level showed no learning effect on the transfer task in either the tablet-app or the traditional condition [+W]
  • Kindergarteners with a low initial handwriting level improved significantly in both conditions, with no detectable advantage of tablet-app training over traditional instruction [+W]
  • Kindergarteners with a medium initial handwriting level made significantly greater paper-based progress when handwriting training partially used the tablet app than when it was entirely traditional [+W]
  • In a 12-week kindergarten trial, the benefit of tablet-app handwriting training over paper-and-pencil-only training depended on children's initial handwriting level, as shown by a three-way interaction between condition, initial level and time [+W]
