# Establish remote STEM mentoring for girls by matching tailored pairs, choosing proven communication tools, and educating both sides on role-specific challenges

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
From their retrospective analysis the authors draw lessons for organizations establishing remote mentoring: Skype, Google Hangout, and phone are "the popular and feasible choices for remote communications endorsed by a majority of our mentor-mentee pairs"; pairs should be matched on interests, maturity, availability, and expertise; and it is important "to educate both sides to collectively address the challenges" since mentors and mentees perceive different hurdles. Programs should anticipate programming-dominated skill offerings when mentors come mainly from computer science.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentors with the passion, time, and energy, recruited through rigorous selection
- Core-team support to help pairs resolve challenges
#### Constraints
- Skill palette is dominated by programming when most mentors have computer science backgrounds; challenges included time zones, infrastructure gaps, and lack of material on teaching advanced programming languages to children

### Target Learners
- Teenaged girls interested in STEM, including socio-economically underprivileged mentees

### Target Learning Goals
- STEM skill building, project completion, career awareness
