# Design flipped pre-class materials with limited video length, varied learning formats, and post-video assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend concrete design choices to mitigate study-load resistance in flipped classrooms: "the instructors must consider limiting the total combined length of video segments" (their online pre-class video was limited to 20 min), plan content to avoid prolonged self-learning in a single format by dividing objectives into parts previewed via video lectures, assigned reading, and presentation preparation, and note that "assessment after the video will play a key role in the success of FC". These choices aim to keep pre-class workload acceptable while sustaining engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Well-developed pre-class materials
- Careful course-content planning to vary pre-class formats
#### Constraints
- In this study the post-video assessment was not tailored, which the authors state may have lowered the actual performance of FC

### Target Learners
- medical residents in flipped pre-simulation preparation

### Target Learning Goals
- preparation for simulation-based crisis management training
