# Teachers and researchers can co-design storytelling-based programs, ending with a debate, to integrate contemporary physics into formal curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that interdisciplinary storytelling programs be implemented in class: "Teachers in collaboration with researchers can design specific programs to integrate formal scholar curricula to engage students in these fields." The activity recipe is a theatrical monologue with poems, followed by an expert explanation of the physics, and closing with a debate in which students ask questions. The authors emphasize the debate's role after science theatre for critical thinking and scientific literacy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A debate/minds-on phase after the storytelling so students re-elaborate contents in a personal way
- Collaboration between teachers and researchers to design the program
#### Constraints
- The authors suggest the monologue contents could be more adequate for students attending the last two years of high school

### Target Learners
- high-school students, including humanities students lacking science in their curriculum

### Target Learning Goals
- engage students in contemporary physics and STEM/STEAM, stimulate critical thinking and interest in science communication
