# Future predictive models for training outcomes should incorporate physical activity dose and recruit biomechanical attributes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extending prediction models beyond demographic and entry-fitness variables. They state that "the inclusion of physical activity dose in predictive models may yield higher levels of predictive accuracy" and that "measurement or estimation of the biomechanical attributes of recruits (where feasible) should be included in future predictive models". They also suggest complex modelling methods exploring nonlinear relationships may yield greater accuracy than the common statistical procedures used here.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to prospective data capturing exercise dose and biomechanical measures (e.g., q-angle, pes planus, ankle dorsiflexion) that were unavailable in the retrospective dataset used in this study.
#### Constraints
- The current dataset lacked prior injuries, smoking status, race/ethnicity, prior activity self-reports, exercise dose, joint flexibility, shoe age and mental health history, limiting the achieved accuracy.

### Target Learners
- Military recruits in basic training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Early identification of trainees at high risk of injury, fitness-test failure, or attrition
