# Pragmatic educational supports for IMG resident physicians: structured onboarding, Japanese-language support, longitudinal mentorship, and active experiential learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends pragmatic, non-assumptive supports for residents educated outside Japan, including structured onboarding to the Japanese medical system, tailored Japanese-language support, and longitudinal mentorship to facilitate cultural adaptation and help-seeking. It adds that instructional approaches grounded in "active, reflective, and experiential learning—such as simulation-based and flipped classroom learning" may enhance integration of clinical knowledge in multicultural environments, prioritizing domains where score differences were observed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Education systems need to deepen understanding of the Japanese medical system and support cross-cultural adaptation and language skills of resident physicians who are IMG.
#### Constraints
- Given the exploratory nature of the study and sample imbalance, no definitive conclusions regarding underlying educational or cultural mechanisms can be drawn.

### Target Learners
- Resident physicians who completed undergraduate medical education outside Japan in Japanese postgraduate training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Test-based clinical knowledge across GM-ITE domains
- Cross-cultural adaptation
- Japanese-language clinical communication
