# Add a service-learning element to a science course to connect learning to practical action for others

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This strategy implements the Action component of the IPP by pairing science coursework with community-engaged service learning. The article states that "Taking a science course and adding a service-learning element is a wonderful way to introduce students to practical, external action and to model how students can use what they are learning to become men and women with and for others." The author also describes a lighter-weight variant: linking course concepts to real-world applications, homework problems, and ethical discussions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A community partner or real-world scenario that students care about, so that learning connects theoretical knowledge to what it can accomplish
#### Constraints
- The article acknowledges that not every science course can include a service-learning component, offering real-world linkage as a more practical alternative

### Target Learners
- university STEM students
- local middle school science students in the toy-explanation example

### Target Learning Goals
- science communication skills
- awareness of privilege, poverty, and community impact
