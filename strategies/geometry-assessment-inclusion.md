# Include geometry assessment in psychoeducational evaluations of mathematical cognition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that geometry, currently neglected in standard psychoeducational assessment, be systematically included when evaluating mathematical cognition. It states "geometry is under- assessed in psychoeducational evaluations and should be included in the assessment of mathematical cognition", because geometric difficulties can be detected before formal instruction, affect use of mathematical symbols and interpretation of graphs, and appeared in 9.3% of the dyslexia sample as a distinct deficit profile.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks covering visuospatial processing (distances, directions), visuo-perceptual shape matching, and mental rotation of 2D and 3D objects
#### Constraints
- The geometry subgroup in the study was small (n = 7); the authors say further research is necessary to validate these findings

### Target Learners
- children with dyslexia or suspected mathematical learning difficulties in grades 2–8

### Target Learning Goals
- early detection of visuospatial and geometrical deficits affecting mathematics and science learning

### Affordances
- Four Domain Math Cognition Taxonomy
