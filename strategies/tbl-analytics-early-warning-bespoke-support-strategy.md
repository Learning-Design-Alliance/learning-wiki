# Use routine TBL readiness data as an early-warning system to trigger bespoke, targeted mid-semester support for at-risk students.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates a strategy in which routine iRAT data from the first four themes identifies the lowest-performing students, whose academic advisors are notified and who then receive bespoke support packages. The article states that "meaningful predictive information is available early enough to support targeted action" and that packages typically included "targeted study-skills coaching; library led sessions on evidence search and resource use; referral to the Disability Advisory and Support Service (DASS) for screening or adjustments; counselling referral where appropriate; and structured mentorship". This strategy operationalises programmatic assessment by translating frequent low-stakes evidence into timely, proportionate support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires frequent, low-stakes performance data generated through readiness assurance testing within a structured active-learning curriculum.
- Requires existing academic advising structures through which flagged students can be offered tailored support packages.
#### Constraints
- The bespoke nature of the intervention introduces heterogeneity in delivery without fidelity monitoring, making it difficult to isolate which components drove improvement.
- A Hawthorne or expectancy effect cannot be excluded.

### Target Learners
- Year-1 undergraduate medical students in large cohorts

### Target Learning Goals
- Early identification and support of students at risk of under-performance
- Improved semester readiness and summative examination performance
