# Evaluate infrastructures continually using process-tracking methods such as ethnography and embedded sensors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This sixth IMPROV action area addresses evaluating infrastructuring processes and outcomes. The article states such research "requires methods that are able to track processes, cope with complexity, and examine changes over time." The infrastructure literature heavily relies on ethnographic methods, including nexus analysis; because ethnography is labor-intensive, the article proposes rapid ethnographic techniques, stakeholder-as-ethnographer supports, and embedded sensors generating digital trace data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Research must be deeply integrated into infrastructuring processes and compatible with infrastructuring perspectives, treating failure as generative for recognizing new points of infrastructuring
#### Constraints
- Ethnographic methods are labor-intensive and time-consuming, and cannot always be implemented in educational settings to their full potential

### Target Learners
- learning scientists
- teachers and students as ethnographers of their own practices

### Target Learning Goals
- documenting failures and complex design dynamics to produce new knowledge about sustaining innovations
