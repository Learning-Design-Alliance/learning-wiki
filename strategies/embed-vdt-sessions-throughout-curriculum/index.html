Embed VDT sessions throughout the anatomy curriculum rather than restricting them to isolated modules¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review reports that VDT integration applied across entire academic curricula was associated with pass rates as high as 100% compared to 87.5% in controls, and statistically higher GPAs. The highest satisfaction levels were reported in studies evaluating VDT use across the entire anatomy curriculum over an academic year. This supports scheduling VDT sessions as a recurring curricular component rather than a one-off demonstration.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sufficient VDT access (optimal group size 8–15 students per session) and adequate interaction time per session

Constraints¶

  • Most institutions reported access to only a single VDT unit; 51% of students cited restricted training time and 39% insufficient access

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate medical and nursing students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Anatomy knowledge acquisition and course completion

Claims¶

  • VDT use is associated with improved academic performance in 86% of studies, with score increases of 8–31% over traditional teaching methods [+W]
  • Perceived VDT utility did not always correspond to measurable academic advantage [~W]
  • Student satisfaction with VDTs ranged from 64 to 95%, with preference for VDT-exclusive learning low (2.4–30.2%) [~W]
