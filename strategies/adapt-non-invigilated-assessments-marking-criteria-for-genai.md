# Adapt non-invigilated written assessments and their marking criteria in response to Gen-AI susceptibility

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that assessment design respond to demonstrated AI vulnerability rather than relying on detection alone. Educators should "require version control" via auditable evidence trails, require reproducible analysis documents, ask creative assessments for "more than incremental creativity", treat contextually irrelevant content as an integrity-review trigger, include referencing quality in marking, and "ensure that assessment passes are awarded for evidenced attainment of the intended learning outcomes, rather than on writing fluency alone".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Markers and institutions need protected time and updated marking guidance to implement integrity reviews and revised criteria
#### Constraints
- The article notes its findings assume a naive student making minimal effort, so susceptibility may be underestimated for sophisticated AI users

### Target Learners
- undergraduate psychology students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- academic integrity
- authentic demonstration of attained learning outcomes
- critical evaluation and referencing skill
