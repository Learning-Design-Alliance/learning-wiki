# A five-stage didactic sequence in which students analyze the Huaso jump with video tracking tools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a didactic sequence in which "students analyze the Huaso–Larraguibel jump using video tracking tools", structured in five stages: Contextualization (presentation of the historical event), Data acquisition (using Tracker to obtain position data), Modeling (applying kinematic equations), Analysis (comparing experimental data with theoretical predictions), and Discussion (interpretation from physical and biomechanical perspectives). This approach is stated to promote active learning and connect abstract concepts with real-world phenomena.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the Tracker software and an audiovisual record of the jump for frame-by-frame data extraction
#### Constraints
- All available records originate from the same historical video, with camera motion limiting analysis to vertical motion

### Target Learners
- science students in physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- kinematics and dynamics concepts
- modeling with kinematic equations
- comparison of experimental data and theoretical predictions
- interdisciplinary understanding linking physics, biomechanics and veterinary science
