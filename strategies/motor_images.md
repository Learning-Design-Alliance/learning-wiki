# Motor Images

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Motor images (sometimes called "muscle memory") involve encoding information through physical movement — air-writing letters, gesturing word meanings, tapping syllables, or physically manipulating objects. The strategy draws on the **enactment effect**: self-performed actions are recalled better than actions that are only observed or described [Cohen's review of enactment memory](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01326550) [+S]. Multisensory combinations of seeing, hearing, saying, and moving strengthen the memory trace through redundant encoding pathways [Dual coding theory and education](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01320076) [+M].

## Design Implications

Enactment improves free recall of action phrases and procedural content relative to verbal-only study [Cohen's review of enactment memory](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01326550) [+S], and gesture accompanying instruction supports retention of newly learned material [~M]. The mechanism is not a "kinesthetic learning style" — it is the additional, distinctive encoding produced by performing the action itself, which benefits learners regardless of their preferred modality [Learning styles: Concepts and evidence](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x) [X].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content that can plausibly be mapped to a movement, gesture, or manipulation
- Space and permission for physical activity; clear safety norms
- Explicit connection between the movement and the content, so the motor trace serves recall rather than becoming a separate, competing task
- Repeated, [spaced](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) practice so the motor pattern becomes automatic

#### Constraints
- Enactment benefits shrink for abstract, non-actionable content; the effect is strongest for concrete, action-referenced material [Cohen's review of enactment memory](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01326550) [~M]
- Elaborate movements can add extraneous load, competing with the content for working memory rather than supporting it [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [-M] — keep movements simple and automatic before combining them with new content
- Not suitable for learners with motor impairments without adapted alternatives; can be disruptive in whole-class settings without routines
- Tailoring movement to diagnosed "kinesthetic learners" has no evidentiary basis [Learning styles: Concepts and evidence](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x) [X]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Full enactment** — learners physically perform the action (air writing, finger calculation, manipulatives)
- **Gesture** — smaller-scale hand movements accompanying speech or reading, usable in seated settings
- **Motor imagery** — learners mentally simulate the movement when physical execution is impractical; weaker than enactment but retains some benefit [~W]
- **Observation-plus-enactment** — watch a [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md), then immediately perform it

### Target Learners
- Young children and beginning readers, for whom letter-formation movements reinforce grapheme identity [~M]
- Learners acquiring procedural or motor-adjacent skills (handwriting, sign language, instrument fingering, surgical technique)
- Learners who struggle with purely verbal rote memorization — enactment provides a distinctive second retrieval route [+M]
- Benefits accrue to all learners, not only those self-identified as kinesthetic [Learning styles: Concepts and evidence](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x) [X]

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of discrete items (letter names, spelling patterns, vocabulary, math facts)
- Procedural fluency: automating sequences of action
- Early literacy and numeracy foundations where form and meaning are physically instantiated

### Instructions
1. Identify the content element that can be mapped to a simple, repeatable movement.
2. [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) — model the movement while naming the content aloud.
3. Have learners perform the movement themselves ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)), pairing action with speech.
4. [Provide guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md) — correct the motor pattern early before errors become automatic.
5. Fade the movement as recall becomes reliable, and [Enhance retention and transfer](../elements/enhance-retention-and-transfer.md) by spacing practice and asking learners to recall without the gesture.

## Examples
- **Orton-Gillingham-based programs** (e.g., [Wilson Reading System](https://www.wilsonlanguage.com)) — air writing and finger-tracing letters while voicing sounds, combining motor, visual, and auditory channels.
- **"Fonzie fists"** — a fist-based hand cue for discriminating the letters *b* and *d*.
- **Finger calculation strategies** (e.g., TouchMath, https://www.touchmath.com) — touching touchpoints on numerals to ground arithmetic in motor action.
- **Total Physical Response (TPR)** in language teaching — learners respond to commands with whole-body movement, pairing comprehension with action.
