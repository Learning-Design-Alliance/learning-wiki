# Intimately intermingle general skills with subject-matter instruction instead of teaching thinking in segregated courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instruction call for "the intimate intermingling of generality and context-specificity in instruction," rather than segregated thinking-skills minicourses that make little effort to link to subject matter or nonacademic applications. This moves education beyond "educating memories to educating minds." The authors point to existing methodologies and educational experiments addressing this agenda and forecast that joining subject-matter instruction and the teaching of thinking will be a major development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- General heuristics must be taught so they make contact with students' knowledge base in the domain, as Schoenfeld's approach does.
#### Constraints
- Exclusive attention to particularized knowledge yields brittle competency serving mostly formulaic problems, while heuristics without knowledge base are weak.

### Target Learners
- school and college students across subject matters

### Target Learning Goals
- higher order thinking skills embedded in domain learning
- transfer of skills across contexts

### Affordances
- General Domain Knowledge Partnership Synthesis
