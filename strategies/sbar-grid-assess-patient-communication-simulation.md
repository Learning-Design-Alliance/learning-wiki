# Use an SBAR-inspired observation grid to structure and assess nurse-patient communication during simulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes an evaluation grid inspired by SBAR (Situation-Background-Assessment-Recommendation), adapted to evaluate patient-nurse communication. The grid contains 10 items scored on a three-point ordinal scale (0 for not done, 1 for done incompletely, 2 for done completely), with good internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha = 0.84). Within simulation-based education, SBAR helps nursing students organize clinical information, communicate assessments logically, and actively involve patients in the exchange of information.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessors trained to score the 10-item grid consistently across sessions
#### Constraints
- The adopted grid focused exclusively on nurse-patient communication rather than interprofessional communication

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- structured patient communication
- clinical information organization
