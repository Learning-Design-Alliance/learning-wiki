# Lateral Reading

## Description
Lateral reading is a digital literacy strategy that involves evaluating the trustworthiness of online information by leaving the original site and consulting other digital sources to see what they say about it. This method contrasts with vertical reading, which involves staying on a single webpage and trusting the source's self-description. Lateral reading utilizes resources like Wikipedia, news stories, and fact-checking websites to assess the credibility and reliability of a source.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the internet and digital devices. Structured activities and guidance from instructors or product developers. Examples of online sources to evaluate.
#### Constraints
- Requires time and effort to consult multiple sources. May be overwhelming for beginners without proper guidance. The reliability of the sources used for lateral reading also needs to be considered.
