# Run meta-debriefing clubs as communities of practice where debriefers critically review audiovisual footage of previous debriefings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A meta-debriefing club is an alternative venue for rich learning conversations: debriefers meet virtually or in-person "to critically review audiovisual footage of previous debriefings." The article frames clubs as "a community of practice that allow opportunities for legitimate peripheral participation and enculturation in a team with shared values," aligned with social constructivist and situated learning theory. Clubs widen access so more faculty contribute and learn from others' debriefing experiences, disseminating actionable learning points—especially important for remote faculty working in isolation or underresourced contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mutually convenient time and venue (virtual or in-person); facilitation using a debriefing assessment tool, a standard debriefing framework, or a combined approach
#### Constraints
- Immediate post-event meta-debriefing may be infeasible due to time constraints, competing faculty priorities, debriefer fatigue, and limited availability of meta-debriefing expertise; conducting meta-debriefings at the end of a simulation course may be counterproductive

### Target Learners
- Simulation faculty debriefers, including remote faculty and those in underresourced contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- Critical review of debriefing practice
- Sharing actionable learning points across a faculty community
