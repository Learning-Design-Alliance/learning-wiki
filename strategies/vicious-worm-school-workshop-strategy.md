# Deliver computer-based health education workshops to school students as future health change agents, with local tailoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends including tailored 'The Vicious Worm' workshops in integrated cysticercosis control programs in endemic sub-Saharan Africa, and focusing messages on school students so they can act as health change agents disseminating messages through their communities. Presenters should further tailor the program locally: assigning common local surnames to story characters "also proved popular and would be encouraged in future workshops."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A laptop, projector and small generator for rural delivery
- Questionnaires adapted and shortened for the age group (QS1 of 24 questions proved too long; QS2 of 15 questions took about 35 min)
#### Constraints
- Knowledge increases do not necessarily lead to transmission-related behaviour change, which may be driven by cultural, economic or practical factors

### Target Learners
- primary school students in endemic rural communities

### Target Learning Goals
- parasite transmission and prevention knowledge for disease control
