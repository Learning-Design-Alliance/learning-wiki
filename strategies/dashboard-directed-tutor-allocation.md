# Direct tutor effort toward the lowest-achieving students using a dashboard rather than a round-robin allocation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends replacing round-robin tutor allocation with dashboard-driven direction of tutor attention to students with the lowest initial math preparation. It found round-robin tutoring 'may have led to unneeded interruptions that distracted already-engaged students' and suggests 'a dashboard-directed system that pushes tutors away from a "round-robin" engagement with all students to even greater interaction with students below grade level could yield even stronger aggregate results and achieve greater educational equity.'

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A real-time tutor-facing dashboard providing learning process data such as idle time and progress to prioritize student support
#### Constraints
- At Site 2 no dashboard was available, so tutors used round-robin visits; Site 3 piloted only a prototype dashboard, and its site-wide treatment lacked a control group

### Target Learners
- middle school students from low-income backgrounds, especially those below grade level

### Target Learning Goals
- increased math software engagement and lesson completion for the students most in need
