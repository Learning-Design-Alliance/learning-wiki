# Use innovative, technology-based teaching methods—simulation, role-playing, videos, case studies, and debriefings—to train psychological safety competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Mentors recommended moving beyond lectures toward experiential and technology-supported formats for teaching trainees to communicate safety concerns, warn about unsafe behavior, and support second victims. The abstract notes "the mentors recommended innovative strategies, many of them based on technological tools or solutions, including videos, seminars, lectures, workshops, simulation learning or role-playing with or without professional actors, case studies" plus panel discussions, clinical sessions, and debriefings. Training should be ongoing and tailored to the health care context rather than one-off sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training content should cover patient safety, interpersonal communication, assertiveness, active listening, and social support skills
- Skills should be practiced in real-world scenarios including challenging conversations and conflict resolution
#### Constraints
- Proposals are mentor opinions from a convenience-sampled European mentor panel, not evaluated interventions

### Target Learners
- health care trainees (residents and students) during clinical internships

### Target Learning Goals
- communicating patient safety concerns
- assertively warning about unsafe behavior
- supporting second victims
