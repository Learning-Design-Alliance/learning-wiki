# Offer VR-modified 3D instructional video as a viable supplementary learning tool while retaining traditional 2D lecture for knowledge acquisition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (6 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 6 studies (3 review, 2 causal, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 6 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's own resolution is that virtual reality is "viable as a learning tool, but knowledge obtained is not necessarily as high as using traditional lecture," so VR video should complement rather than replace traditional lecture in oral placement therapy training. The strategy distributes identical audio and content in both 2D and 3D formats, lets learners use inexpensive plastic smartphone viewers, and gathers knowledge-test and Likert opinion feedback to judge reception. Because opinions were polarized, offering both formats lets learners and providers act on individual preference while further research on physically interacting with models proceeds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need smartphone access; distributing identical content across formats to isolate the 3D manipulation
- Feedback collection (knowledge and opinion measures) to judge each learner group's reception
#### Constraints
- Small subject pool, specificity of the field, and varying needs and preferences of individual subjects limit generalizability
- The video was immersive but not interactive; the author states further experimentation is required on physically interacting with a model

### Target Learners
- Professional oral placement therapists
- Professional trainees in specialized therapy or medical fields

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of factual instructional content
- Engagement with and preference for instructional format
