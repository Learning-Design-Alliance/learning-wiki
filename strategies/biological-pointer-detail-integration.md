# Integrate essential information into biological pointers by presenting likely-to-be-externalized content with vivid, multimodal detail

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because learners often retain only a pointer to externalized information, this strategy recommends presenting information likely to be externalized in a way that embeds key details into that pointer, e.g., linking monetary inflation inseparably to an increased money supply via multimodal visualization. The review states that "this strategy of integrating the most important information into pointers may inoculate people against false information and manipulation." It draws on embodied metaphor instruction and multimodal theories of mental representation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional design that identifies which information is likely to be externalized
- Multimodal or embodied presentation formats that bind essential details to the pointer
#### Constraints
- The exact contents of biological pointers are yet unknown, and the boundaries between pointers and gist memory may be hard to draw

### Target Learners
- learners who offload information to digital stores

### Target Learning Goals
- retaining essential conceptual content despite offloading
- resistance to memory manipulation
