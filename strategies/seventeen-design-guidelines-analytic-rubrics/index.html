Apply seventeen evidence-based design guidelines when developing formative assessment with analytic rubrics for complex skills¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q1–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article compiles seventeen guidelines for formative assessment with analytic rubrics, stating "Table 1 presents an overview of (seventeen) guidelines on analytic rubrics we encountered in literature. Guidelines 1–4 inform us how to use rubrics for formative assessment; Guidelines 5–17 inform us how to use rubrics for instruction, with Guidelines 5–9 on a rather generic, meso level and Guidelines 10–17 on a more specific, micro level." They cover preferring analytic over rating-scale rubrics, self- and peer-assessment, training teachers and students, authentic tasks of increasing complexity, modelling examples, rehearsal practice, high-quality feedback, and specific goal-setting. The study applied all seventeen in designing its classroom scenarios.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Rubrics must be tailored to specific learning objectives and target groups; teachers and students need training before use

Constraints¶

  • The guidelines were validated in secondary education for oral presentation; the authors note the design-based context limits exclusion of confounding factors

Target Learners¶

  • Secondary school students acquiring complex skills

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Complex skill acquisition supported by formative assessment with analytic rubrics

Claims¶

  • Formative assessment with validated analytic rubrics in the Viewbrics tool produced higher oral presentation performance than conventional rating-scale rubrics in Dutch secondary education [+M]
  • Oral presentation performance improved significantly over time, but only control-group students showed statistically significant growth between the two presentations [-M]
  • Video-enhanced analytic rubrics did not significantly outperform text-based analytic rubrics on oral presentation performance, though a marginal trend favored video-enhanced rubrics [~M]
  • Text-based rubrics alone provide insufficient contextual, dynamic and procedural information to support rich mental model development of complex skills [~W]
