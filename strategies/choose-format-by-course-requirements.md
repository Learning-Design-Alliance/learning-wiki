# Choose between online and face-to-face delivery based on the specific requirements of the course, and continuously review educational practices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advises that "The choice between online and face-to-face teaching should be based on the specific requirements of the course", and that it is essential to continuously review educational practices. For practical courses where hands-on experimentation matters, face-to-face delivery is favored by the exam-score findings; for content where flexibility dominates, online delivery offers advantages in location and time management. The strategy treats format choice as a per-course decision rather than a global one.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must assess the specific requirements of each course, including whether physical interaction with equipment and materials is part of the learning goals.
#### Constraints
- The article emphasizes that teaching effectiveness depends on many variable factors such as interaction, flexibility and engagement, and that its evidence comes from one physiology course at a single institution.

### Target Learners
- Higher-education students in medical and science practical courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Alignment of teaching format with course-specific learning requirements and exam performance
