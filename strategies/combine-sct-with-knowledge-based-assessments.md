# Combine SCTs with traditional knowledge-based assessments to capture clinical reasoning alongside lower-level knowledge outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because SCTs intentionally assess higher-level thinking in ambiguity while knowledge-based exams emphasize recall and single correct answers, the authors recommend using multiple assessment types across a curriculum. As they state, "utilizing multiple types of assessment instruments throughout curricula may assist in gaining a better understanding of a student’s overall performance." Adding SCTs may reveal reasoning in ambiguity that traditional examinations miss.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires curricular capacity to administer and score SCTs against an appropriately constituted reference panel.
#### Constraints
- The authors note further study is needed on SCT utility and validity in DPT education as either an assessment or pedagogical tool.

### Target Learning Goals

- comprehensive assessment of student performance
