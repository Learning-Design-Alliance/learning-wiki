# Address assessor discomfort, including screening with the modified PADS scale, as one avenue for reducing failure to fail

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that assessor discomfort be addressed alongside existing failure-to-fail strategies, arguing discomfort "represents a further avenue to pursue". They suggest the PADS scale, modified for local use, "may have a role identifying clinicians who are vulnerable to high levels of discomfort in assessment", and cite Cox and colleagues' suggestion of role play for those with high discomfort.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Further study of whether PADS scores are modifiable with variables such as training or experience, as the authors propose
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted in the Australasian College of Dermatologists context; the authors generalise on the grounds that assessment practices in specialist medical colleges tend to be similar
- Discomfort does not explain all instances of failure to fail

### Target Learners
- Clinical supervisors assessing medical specialty trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate delivery of assessment judgements and feedback to trainees
