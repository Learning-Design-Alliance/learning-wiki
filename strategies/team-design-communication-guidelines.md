# Guidelines for team designing: continuously introduce constraints alongside solutions and negotiate solutions rather than persuade

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The discussion translates observed patterns into action-oriented guidelines for e-learning team design practice. Designers should expect that "Designers continuously introduce new constraints and requirements alongside design solutions," and act as solution negotiators: the predominance of transformation-oriented design sequences and proposal-oriented communication sequences implies continuous epistemic negotiation until a satisfactory state of the design object is reached, with self-explanation and verification as basic communication techniques. Team deliberation promotes understanding and consensus rather than persuasion, valuing empathy and multiple personal expertise.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Team members must develop skills in self-explanation, verification, empathy, and drawing on multiple personal expertise
#### Constraints
- Guidelines derive from two highly experienced teams in the development phase; the authors assume their manifested patterns relate to efficient behavior based on experience and professional recognition

### Target Learners
- instructional designers and interdisciplinary e-learning project teams

### Target Learning Goals
- effective team design communication and decision-making in e-learning development
