# Design differentiated professional development resources targeting prioritized personas rather than one-size-fits-all materials

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing different aspects of professional development resources to appeal to different prioritized personas, instead of "providing one type of professional development resource that might appeal to only one persona or contain so little or so much information that it benefits no one at all". Stakeholders prioritize personas by likelihood of benefit and impact on scaling innovations, then build site features for each: a simple home page with a jump bar for novices, links to comparison databases for pragmatists, and detailed research-validation pages for seekers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interview-based personas with identified task flows and pain points
- Stakeholder team consensus on persona prioritization
#### Constraints
- The authors believe impact would be lessened if focused on convincing skeptical faculty to use innovations

### Target Learners
- University physics faculty adopting educational innovations

### Target Learning Goals
- Implementation and continuing use of research-based curricular and assessment innovations
