# Faculty development organized around authentic situations where autonomy support and authority are hard to distinguish

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that faculty development move beyond teaching autonomy-supportive behaviours as a list and instead help educators distinguish autonomy support from permissiveness and authority from psychological control. Learning should be "organized around authentic clinical situations in which these distinctions become difficult to maintain," such as supervising a struggling learner or responding to a patient-safety concern, with the aim of developing judgment and confidence in combining high structure with low control.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty development must also address the professional beliefs that support controlling practice, such as equating decisiveness with competence
#### Constraints
- Technique-only approaches may have limited durability when educators do not understand why control becomes compelling under pressure

### Target Learners
- Clinical educators and supervisors in medical education

### Target Learning Goals
- Distinguishing autonomy support from permissiveness and authority from psychological control
