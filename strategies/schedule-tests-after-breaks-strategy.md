# Plan standardized tests as closely after breaks as possible and account for fatigue in school-day and accountability design

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' policy recommendations: cognitive fatigue "should be taken into consideration when deciding on the length of the school day and the frequency and duration of breaks"; school accountability systems "should control for the influence of external factors on test scores"; and the most straightforward implementation is "to plan tests as closely after breaks as possible". They also suggest adjusting scores by 0.9% of an SD per hour later and downward by 1.7% of an SD after breaks, while noting this requires continuous monitoring and may not always be feasible.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of each school's break schedule so tests can be placed shortly after breaks end
#### Constraints
- The score-adjustment approach may not always be feasible in practice since it requires continuous monitoring and adjustments; the article says results should not be read as evidence for later school start times

### Target Learners
- students aged 8–15 taking computer-based standardized tests

### Target Learning Goals
- fair and accurate assessment of academic achievement on standardized tests
