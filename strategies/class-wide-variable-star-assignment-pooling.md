# Scale student research to a class by assigning each student a carefully-chosen variable star to solve, then pooling results as a team project

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For scaling archival research beyond one-on-one supervision, the author proposes assigning each student in a class a carefully-chosen variable star to analyze, then pooling the class results as a team project to draw overall conclusions. Alternatively, students can individually study groups of variable stars from massive surveys and identify ones worthy of further study. He notes the Galaxy Zoo project's popularity suggests many people are motivated by classifying objects as normal or peculiar.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A carefully-chosen set of variable stars or survey archives suited to individual analysis
#### Constraints
- The author's program's future is limited since he has been retired for ten years; the proposal is prospective, not evaluated

### Target Learners
- class-level undergraduate or high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- data analysis
- scientific classification and judgement
- collaborative synthesis of results
