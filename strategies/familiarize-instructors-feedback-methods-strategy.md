# Increase instructors' familiarity with feedback methods through informational materials, best-practice examples and training aligned to course format and learning objectives

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend making feedback methods, their relevance and opportunities for use more familiar to instructors, since unfamiliarity was the most cited reason for non-use of checklists. They propose printed flyers like the MERLIN information cards, informational materials, manuals and films describing selected methods with their potential uses, strengths and weaknesses, and compiling a list of best-practice examples to boost motivation. Method choice should suit the course format and learning objectives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must be informed about the advantages, disadvantages, and potential situations in which individual methods can be used
- familiarity with the basic rules for giving feedback described in the literature should be increased
#### Constraints
- The selected feedback methods must be suitable for a specific course format (e.g. audience response systems for lectures) and depend on the learning objectives to be met

### Target Learners
- medical instructors at university faculties teaching in the first and second study phases and the final practical year

### Target Learning Goals
- effective use of feedback methods in competency-based medical teaching
