Implement a structured PFB template and foresee multiple practice occasions when involving students as assessors and assessees¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), q2–q4 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

As an implication for practice, the authors recommend implementing a structured PFB template guiding students to "provide feedback and feed forward on a list of pre-specified, or preferably mutual discussed criteria", and to "foresee multiple practice occasions or feedback cycles" when involving students as assessors and assessees in the PFB process.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • a structured PFB template including feedback, feed forward, and evaluation of received feedback

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • first-year higher education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • peer feedback quality
  • academic writing

Claims¶

  • Students perceived the content checklist as a helpful instructional intervention for formulating peer feedback [+W]
  • Peer feedback quality and product quality increased over time for all conditions, with no significant differences between conditions at time 2 and time 3 [+M]
  • The peer feedback request condition showed a significant PFB score increase from time 1 to time 2 (11.88 points), but only at the initial stage of performance [~M]
  • Students perceived the PFB request as useful for formulating feedback, with request and content conditions rating it significantly more useful than the combination condition [+M]
  • The quality of feedback students give peers predicts gains in their own work, but no recorded study isolates a causal benefit of the assessor role [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use adaptable collaboration scripts that fade structure in or out over time according to students' needs
  • Design peer assessment to encourage specific problem-identifying feedback, suggestions, and affective support rather than grades alone
  • Promote interaction between provider and receiver of peer feedback to stimulate elaboration on judgments
