# Set case-mix-aware proficiency expectations and personalized therapeutic endpoints: count procedures toward proficiency only within the nodule-size stratum being trained, and for large nodules target symptom relief rather than maximal volume reduction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends judging procedural proficiency within the nodule-size stratum actually treated, since a learning curve achieved on small or medium nodules does not guarantee performance on large ones. It also recommends reframing the goal for large nodules as personalized treatment: "in patients with large nodule sizes, we should look at RFA as a technique providing personalized treatment, in that, we may actually fix a given end-point for each patient", aiming at relief of symptoms rather than maximal VRR. Training counts and outcome expectations should therefore be anchored to the difficulty of the cases performed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A documented case log distinguishing treated nodules by baseline volume, and a defined technical efficacy threshold (here VRR ≥50% at 6 months) against which per-stratum proficiency is assessed
#### Constraints
- The article states that in nodules with baseline volume >25 ml, suboptimal VRR has to be expected, so proficiency targets should not be applied to large nodules
- Evidence base is a single-center retrospective design, which the authors note restricts external generalizability

### Target Learners
- Physicians in procedural training (endocrinologists performing thyroid RFA)

### Target Learning Goals
- Reaching proficiency appropriate to the case difficulty being treated; setting clinically meaningful (symptom-relief) endpoints for difficult cases
