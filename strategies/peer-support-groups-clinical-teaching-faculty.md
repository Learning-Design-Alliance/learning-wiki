# Implement peer support groups in which small groups of clinical teaching faculty collectively reflect, debrief, and share teaching strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
As a cost-effective alternative to individual teaching consultation, the review proposes bringing together small groups of clinical teaching faculty "to collectively reflect, debrief, and share strategies for improving their teaching". Faculty discuss their self-assessments and SETs within the group, which is anticipated to allow deeper self-reflection and a more concrete plan for modifying teaching. An integrative review of 34 papers found most peer support was collaborative rather than evaluative, with positive outcomes including changes to teaching skills and behaviors and greater awareness of how to promote student self-assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time for faculty participation, which the review identifies as a logistical challenge
#### Constraints
- Most clinical educators receive no compensation for their time, and training sites are not always within academic health centers, so programs may struggle to ask faculty for additional work

### Target Learners
- clinical teachers and supervisors in genetic counseling and other small health professions programs

### Target Learning Goals
- improvement of clinical teaching practice
- deeper critical reflection on teaching
