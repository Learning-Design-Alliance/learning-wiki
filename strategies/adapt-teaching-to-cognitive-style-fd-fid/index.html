Adapt L2 teaching approaches to learners' field dependence-independence after assessing their cognitive style¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teachers learn their students' cognitive style of field-dependence/independence and adapt instruction accordingly, because input flood benefited the two styles differently. As the authors put it, if teachers "become aw are of their students' cognitive style of field-dependence/independence" they "can adapt their teaching approaches to the different needs of the learners more effectively". Field-independent learners can enhance collocation knowledge through input flood alone, while field-dependent learners appear to need more explicit treatment types.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must assess learners' cognitive style and know ways to support both groups' learning processes

Constraints¶

  • The article's evidence base is one quasi-experiment with upper-intermediate Iranian EFL adults; the durable benefit of implicit treatment was limited to field-independent learners

Target Learners¶

  • EFL learners of varying cognitive styles, especially field-dependent learners needing explicit support

Target Learning Goals¶

  • collocational knowledge development

Affordances¶

  • Field Dependence Independence Cognitive Style

Claims¶

  • Input flood benefits field-dependent learners over control only on the immediate post-test, not the delayed post-test [+W]
  • Field-independent learners in the input flood group outperform field-independent control learners on both post-tests [+W]
  • Field-independent learners outperform field-dependent learners in the input flood group on both immediate and delayed collocation post-tests [+W]
  • Input flood treatment has a durable long-term effect on field-independent learners' collocation knowledge despite some decay [+W]
  • Input flood treatment has no durable long-term effect on field-dependent learners' collocation knowledge [+W]
  • Cooperative learning benefited all three cognitive style groups equally in mathematics, with no interaction between methodology and cognitive style [~W]
  • In language, a significant methodology-by-cognitive-style interaction emerged, apparently favoring field independent students [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Identify students' cognitive style before choosing between inquiry and conventional teaching
