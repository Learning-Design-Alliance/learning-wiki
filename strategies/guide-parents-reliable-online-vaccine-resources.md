# Guide parents toward reliable, evidence-based online resources to strengthen digital health literacy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Given the pervasive influence of digital misinformation, the article recommends actively guiding parents "toward reliable, evidence-based online resources, thereby strengthening their digital health literacy and resilience against misleading content". This responds to the finding that the internet was the primary source of vaccine-related information for 64.0% of participants, which the authors suggest may increase susceptibility to misinformation and contribute to negative vaccination attitudes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Healthcare professionals able to identify and recommend reliable, evidence-based online vaccine resources
#### Constraints
- Recommended in the context of NICU parent education; broader applicability not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- Parents relying on the internet for vaccine-related information

### Target Learning Goals
- Strengthen digital health literacy
- Build resilience against vaccine misinformation
