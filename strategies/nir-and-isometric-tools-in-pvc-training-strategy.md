# Use NIR light visualization technology and isometric exercise training tools in nursing PVC training before graduation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that vein-prominence enhancement techniques be incorporated into nursing PVC training: "The findings support the importance of using NIR light visualization technology and isometric exercise training tools for successful PVC placement" and "Strategies must be evaluated to enhance the PVC insertion technique before graduation. It can be recommended that different teaching techniques be continued under instructor supervision for students in the procedure of PVC placement." The NIR group's first-attempt success advantage grounds the recommendation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor supervision during practice; access to an NIR visualization device (Accuvein) or stress balls for isometric exercise
#### Constraints
- Results come from a single facility, healthy student participants, and immediate/short-term outcomes, and cannot be generalized to clinical PVC attempts

### Target Learners
- first-year nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- successful first-attempt peripheral venous catheterization

## Claims

- [Nursing students trained with NIR light visualization achieved the highest first-attempt PVC success rate (67.3%) compared to isometric exercise (40%) and control (29.2%) groups](../claims/nir-visualization-highest-first-attempt-pvc-success.md) [+W]
