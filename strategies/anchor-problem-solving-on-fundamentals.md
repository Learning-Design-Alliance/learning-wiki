# Anchor career development in problem-solving on a foundation of physical fundamentals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A career strategy directing early-career scientists to become genuinely good at solving problems on top of a deeply understood foundation, acquiring skills as a by-product of pursuing important problems rather than learning tools first. The authors write that "the tools will keep evolving, but the ability to formulate a problem, decompose it, and apply good judgment will remain valuable." They warn that generative AI producing working solutions before understanding the problem is "useful for shipping but corrosive for learning."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Completed or in-progress disciplinary degree providing quantitative reasoning about a complex physical system under uncertainty
#### Constraints
- The authors note mathematics and physics are hard to acquire later, so the foundation must be protected deliberately during degree programs

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate and graduate students in weather and climate science
- Early-career professionals and postdoctoral researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- Problem formulation and decomposition
- Physical intuition for atmospheric behavior
