# Until more gender-representative research exists, identity-based equity efforts should purposefully consider feminist frameworks in STEM

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The review recommends that efforts to boost engineering identity, when based on a quantitative literature drawn largely from male-majority samples, may support their equity goals by "purposefully considering frameworks of feminism in STEM (e.g., Heybach & Pickup, 2017)". This is a forward-looking recommendation the authors draw from their gender-composition findings, intended for researchers and program designers concerned with gender disparities in engineering. It is paired with their call for more gender-representative research on engineering undergraduates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recognition that current instruments and identified identity factors derive largely from male-majority samples
#### Constraints
- Positioned as an interim measure until more gender-representative research on engineering undergraduates can be conducted

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students
- learners who do not identify as male

### Target Learning Goals
- engineering identity development
- gender equity in engineering education
