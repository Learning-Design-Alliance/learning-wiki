# Use the drawable-conductor exercises to teach measurement uncertainty through device errors, repeated-measurement agreement, sample preparation and error propagation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes that the conducting-line activities offer multiple opportunities to learn measurement uncertainty, "in line with previous work that advocates for measurement uncertainty as one of the key concepts students should grasp through laboratory experiments". Students progress from device measurement errors (ohmmeter, ruler, protractor), to agreement among repetitive resistance measurements (mostly from probe-line contact), to sample-preparation variation, to error propagation when multiple measurements are combined (Fig. 3 Middle and Fig. 7).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A digital ohmmeter and drawn conductors are needed so students can compare repeated measurements and propagated errors.
#### Constraints
- The article states the key source of error in most measurements is the probe-line contact, and that errors always increase when multiple measurements are combined.

### Target Learners
- high school students
- early undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- estimating and propagating measurement uncertainty
- understanding the scientific method through systematic measurement
