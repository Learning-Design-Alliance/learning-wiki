# Before applying a normal-distribution stabilization model, agencies should thoroughly explore indicator data distributions with visual and numeric checks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy directs education agencies to check whether indicator distributions align with a stabilization model's assumptions before applying it. The article recommends that "agencies should thoroughly explore data distributions, supplementing visual checks with numeric ones, to understand how well indicator distributions align with model assumptions of normality." Recommended checks include histograms at the subgroup–indicator level, Q–Q plots, scatterplots, and supplementary normality tests such as D'Agostino's K-squared test, with visual inspection treated as most useful because statistical tests are sensitive to sample size.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to subgroup–indicator-level score distributions for each performance indicator under consideration for stabilization
#### Constraints
- Statistical tests of normality are sensitive to sample size and point estimates provide little information about distribution shape, so visual inspection may be most useful

### Target Learners
- State education agencies
- Student subgroups including small demographic groups within schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Reliable measurement of school and student-group performance for accountability and diagnostic decisionmaking
