Implementation recommendations for adopting project-based learning in higher education classrooms¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that "PoBL strat egy as a pedagogical approach should be made a part of a routine which is readily -available for teachers to use in a classroom setting", while listing prerequisites such as teachers' professional development, classroom management ability, evaluation techniques, and technology integration. Specific recommendations include providing students with computers and the Internet, avoiding very large classes, setting up facilities for cooperating with the outside environment, designing lesson plans that encourage problem-solving and cooperative skills, and preparing students for group cooperation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers' professional development and classroom management ability
  • Classrooms equipped with computers and the Internet
  • Class sizes small enough that supervision quality is not diminished
  • Learner cooperation, with instruction in group-working skills

Constraints¶

  • For classes with larger numbers of students, the use of PoBL is not advised
  • Formal procedures for outside cooperation can be time- and energy-consuming

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students
  • educational technology students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-directed learning skills
  • problem-solving skills
  • critical thinking
  • cooperative and communicative skills

Claims¶

  • Pairwise comparisons show significant self-directed learning gains from pre-test to both post-tests, but no significant change between post-test one and post-test two [+W]
  • The increase in self-directed learning depends on treatment group, with a greater rate of increase for the PoBL group (group × measurement interaction) [+W]
  • Educational technology students taught with project-based learning show significantly higher self-directed learning than students taught with conventional teaching [+W]
  • Self-directed learning scores increase significantly across the three measurements over the semester [+W]
