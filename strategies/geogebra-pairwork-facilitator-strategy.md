# Use GeoGebra-supported pair work with teacher-as-facilitator scaffolding in circle lessons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "teachers be encouraged to use GeoGebra software in teaching Mathematics", implemented through an initial instructional intervention introducing the tools, followed by pair work with a step-by-step guide while the teacher acts as facilitator. Students construct diagrams, make conjectures, and reach shared understanding through peer interaction, with GeoGebra acting as the primary scaffold toward the ZPD.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Initial teacher introduction of the software tools; pairs of students with a step-by-step guide; monitoring to balance input from both group members
#### Constraints
- Students reported low confidence with the software, attributed to insufficient time to familiarize themselves with the keystrokes; the study covered only circles over one week and the authors call for research on broader mathematical concepts

### Target Learners
- Year 9 (Form 3) secondary mathematics students in mixed-ability classes

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of circles through exploration and visualization
