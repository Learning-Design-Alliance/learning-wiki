# Policies improving school attendance, supporting low-income households and expanding early childhood education to raise academic outcomes in low-income urban settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends intervening on the factors its adjusted model identified as significant predictors of academic performance. It concludes that "policies aimed at improving school attendance, supporting low-income households and expanding early childhood education may have the greatest impact on improving academic outcomes in the study population", and calls for collaboration among stakeholders, teachers, parents and the community. This follows from its findings that absenteeism, monthly income and preschool attendance predicted achievement while anthropometric undernutrition did not.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions implemented in collaboration with stakeholders, teachers, parents and the community, as the article states
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a cross-sectional study of urban public primary schools only; the article notes it excluded private schools and rural children and cannot support causal inferences

### Target Learners
- Primary school children aged 7–14 in low-income urban public schools

### Target Learning Goals
