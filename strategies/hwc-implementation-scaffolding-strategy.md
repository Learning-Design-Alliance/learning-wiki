# Offer homework corrections with explicit scaffolding and reflective-comment patterns to motivate reflection in upper-division physics courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Homework corrections are implemented by posting worked solutions shortly after the deadline and inviting students to make reflective comments on their original submissions for credit. In Instructor A's version, students use the Canvas commenting tool to make "a reflective statement about what they did wrong and write a description of what the correct approach would be," with example patterns provided for comments that would and would not get credit. The authors conclude HWCs can be useful but that more scaffolding may be needed to support students in identifying and learning from mistakes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have reasonably attempted the problem on the initial submission to be eligible for corrections, and worked solutions must be posted promptly after the deadline.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the implementation as described essentially eliminates the possibility of students submitting late work, and instructors raised concerns about grading load, grade inflation, and students gaming the system.

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics majors

### Target Learning Goals
- reflection on problem solving
- learning from errors
- metacognitive skills
- content understanding
