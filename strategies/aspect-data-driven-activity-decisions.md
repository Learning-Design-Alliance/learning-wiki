# Instructors use ASPECT data to decide whether to continue, modify, or discontinue an activity and to compare activities or populations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends instructors administer ASPECT after an activity and use the engagement data for data-driven decision making. "Instructors can use these data to inform their decisions to revise or discontinue activities," and can compare results between activities, student populations, or potentially instructors—for example, checking whether a struggling demographic group's engagement is also stunted, or comparing activities with different instructor framing techniques.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administration of ASPECT after implementing a strategy or teaching an activity, so engagement data exist for the decision
#### Constraints
- Further field-testing is needed to assess whether ASPECT can differentiate activities differing in elements other than length and instructor feedback

### Target Learners
- college instructors teaching large active-learning STEM courses

### Target Learning Goals
- informed selection and revision of active-learning activities based on student engagement data

### Affordances
- Three Construct Engagement Model Aspect
