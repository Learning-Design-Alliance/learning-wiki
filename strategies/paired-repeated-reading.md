# Paired Repeated Reading

## Description
Paired repeated reading is a fluency-building technique in which two learners take turns reading the same passage aloud several times — typically three to four readings — while the partner listens, follows along, and gives feedback on accuracy and expression. The reader rereads until a criterion (e.g., 85–100 words correct per minute with few errors) is reached, then partners switch roles. It adapts Samuels' (1979) repeated reading method for classroom use by removing the need for one-on-one teacher time.

## Design Implications

Repeated reading works because rereading familiar text reduces word-recognition effort, freeing attention for comprehension and prosody [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]. The pairing structure adds accountability and immediate error correction that silent rereading lacks, and peer feedback keeps accuracy practice from drifting into practicing errors.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional-level texts (roughly 90–95% word accuracy on first read) short enough to reread within a session
- Trained partners who know how to follow text, note errors, and give specific praise and corrections ([Feedback](../elements/feedback.md))
- A simple timing and progress-recording method so learners can see growth across readings
- A model of fluent reading at some point in the routine ([Audiobooks](../elements/audiobooks.md) or teacher read-aloud) so learners have a prosody target

#### Constraints
- Rereading text that is too difficult produces frustrated, error-laden practice rather than fluency gains [-M]
- Gains are largely text-specific; transfer to new passages requires wide reading across many texts, not just repeated reading of a few [~M]
- Untrained partners often fail to catch errors, allowing mispronunciations to be rehearsed and consolidated [-M]
- For readers already fluent, rereading consumes time with little benefit — the approach is remedial, not universal [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Partner reading with error correction**: listener interrupts on errors, prompts correction, then the reader rereads the sentence
- **Timed repeated reading**: reader graphs words-correct-per-minute across readings, adding self-monitoring and motivation
- **Radio reading / performance reading**: repeated practice culminates in reading to an audience, adding purpose and prosody pressure
- **Paired with a model first**: partner or audio reads the passage once before the learner rereads it (modeled repeated reading), which helps the weakest readers [~M]

### Target Learners
- Struggling readers in grades 2–8 whose decoding is slow but not severely impaired — the population with the strongest meta-analytic gains [+S]
- English learners, who benefit from hearing and rehearsing natural phrasing [~M]
- Less appropriate for readers with very low decoding accuracy, who need explicit phonics first; rereading text they cannot decode does not build the needed skill [-M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Oral reading fluency: rate, accuracy, and prosody [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Automaticity at the word level as a precondition for comprehension ([Automaticity](../elements/automaticity.md))
- Reading confidence and stamina for longer texts

### Instructions
1. Pair learners and give each the same short instructional-level passage ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) longer texts into 100–200-word sections).
2. Optionally play or perform a fluent model of the passage once ([Audiobooks](../elements/audiobooks.md)).
3. Reader A reads aloud while Reader B follows, timing the reading and noting errors ([Feedback](../elements/feedback.md)).
4. Reader A rereads the same passage two to three times, aiming to beat their own rate and error count; B graphs the results.
5. Partners switch roles and repeat with a new passage.
6. End sessions with wide independent reading of new texts so fluency generalizes beyond the practiced passages ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)).

## Related Strategies
- **Choral Reading** — whole-group version; lower feedback precision but useful for building prosody before pairing
- **Reader's Theater** — performance-based repeated reading with authentic audience and expressive-reading goal
- **Echo Reading** — teacher-led modeling variant for the weakest decoders

## Examples
- **Read Naturally** (https://www.readnaturally.com) — commercial program combining audio modeling, timed repeated reading, and graphed progress monitoring; built directly on the repeated reading evidence base.
- **Partner reading routines in PALS (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies)** (https://vkc.mc.vanderbilt.edu/pals/) — structured paired reading with trained roles and error-correction scripts, validated in numerous RCTs.
- **Tier 2 fluency interventions** in many MTSS frameworks schedule 15–20 minutes of paired repeated reading three to four times per week with weekly progress graphing.
