# Walk through plots slowly: axes first, then legend, then scientific meaning, privileging the paper's own dataset

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because plot quality and clarity are highly variable, the guide recommends presenting important plots slowly in a fixed order: first review the X and Y axes and their quantities, then the legend identifying which color, line, or symbol is this paper's sample, and only then the plot's scientific meaning. Literature comparison datasets are reviewed but the paper's own dataset takes precedence, and subfield-specific axis notation should be explained for cross-subfield audiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time in the presentation for deliberate plot walkthrough; awareness that audience members may be from other subfields
#### Constraints
- Aimed at astronomy seminar audiences where y-axis labels may use subfield-specific notation incomprehensible to outsiders

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students presenting plots to interdisciplinary astronomy audiences

### Target Learning Goals
- explaining figures clearly
- interpreting scientific plots for an audience
