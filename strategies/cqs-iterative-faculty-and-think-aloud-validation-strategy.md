# Iterative development and validation of clicker question sequences via faculty review and student think-aloud interviews

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes an iterative validation process for a clicker question sequence: after initial development using the learning goals and guided sequences of a validated tutorial, the sequence was refined with physics faculty and then tested with student think-aloud interviews. "We then conducted individual think-aloud interviews with advanced students who had learned these concepts via traditional lecture-based instruction" to ensure students interpreted the questions as intended and that the sequencing provided appropriate scaffolding support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to physics faculty for feedback on wording and sequencing
- Advanced students who have learned the concepts via traditional instruction for think-aloud interviews
#### Constraints
- Development of a research-validated learning tool is described as an iterative process requiring further refinement after in-class implementation

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- organizing knowledge structure of quantum mechanics concepts via scaffolded question sequences
