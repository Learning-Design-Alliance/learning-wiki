Design IcPAB interventions on theoretical underpinnings such as the COM-B model and use curriculum-related activity breaks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 10 claims (4 for, 2 mixed, 4 against) · 4 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q3 · 3 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends grounding classroom activity-break interventions in behaviour-change theory and aligning breaks with lesson content. It states that "theoretical underpinnings such as the COM-B behaviour model [1] can be integrated to these intervention designs to obtain positive results", and that curriculum-related breaks respond to teachers' concerns about tight schedules. It also recommends cluster-randomised designs and moderation analyses of intervention intensity.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interventions should include well explained process evaluation and fidelity methods, such as facilitator-completed daily logs for intensity and accuracy of delivery.

Constraints¶

  • Classroom teachers report difficulties implementing activity breaks due to high curriculum demands, and pure educational time might be shortened by IcPAB.

Target Learners¶

  • primary school children aged six to thirteen

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic performance
  • executive function and attention
  • physical activity levels and health outcomes

Claims¶

  • Few IcPAB interventions are theory-based or include process evaluation and fidelity monitoring [-W]
  • In-classroom physical activity breaks significantly improve primary school children's foreign language learning [+W]
  • In-classroom physical activity breaks significantly improve primary school children's spelling performance [+W]
  • In-classroom physical activity breaks produce a large significant increase in primary school children's moderate-to-vigorous physical activity levels [+W]
  • In-classroom physical activity breaks show no significant effects on children's executive function and attention outcomes [~W]
  • In-classroom physical activity breaks show no significant effect on primary school children's mathematics performance [-W]
  • In-classroom physical activity breaks show no significant effect on children's test anxiety [~W]
  • In-classroom physical activity breaks show no significant effect on primary school children's reading performance [-W]
  • Classroom physical activity increases children's on-task behaviour, with no consistent gain on attention or other cognitive tests [+M]
  • Classroom active breaks show no reliable effect on mathematical fluency [-W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Refocus physical activity research on study quality, transparent reporting, fidelity monitoring and differentiation of intervention characteristics
