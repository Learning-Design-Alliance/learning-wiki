# Use structured Cooperative Learning activities — Think-Pair-Share, Numbered Heads Together, Jigsaw, and Circle the Sage — to maximize learner interaction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents four common Cooperative Learning activities as concrete structures for interaction. Think-Pair-Share is "a multi-mode discussion cycle" of individual thinking, pair talk, and sharing with the larger group; Numbered Heads Together has students number off, answer a high-consensus question as a team, and random numbers respond for team points; Jigsaw sends students to expert groups then back to teach their portion; Circle the Sage has knowledgeable students explain to classmates who then compare notes as teams.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Numbered heads is used to exclude hitch-hiking and remind students about individual accountability
- Groups should be small when learners are just beginning to work together
#### Constraints
- Jigsaw models were developed for narrative materials in content areas where the goal is concepts rather than skills

### Target Learners
- first-year EFL university students

### Target Learning Goals
- reading strategy use
- peer interaction
- individual accountability
