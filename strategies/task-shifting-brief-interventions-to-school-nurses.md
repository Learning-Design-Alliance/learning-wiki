# Task shifting brief mental health interventions to school nurses as a first-line response for anxious students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends task shifting responsibility for delivering brief mental health interventions to school nurses, who are positioned to identify anxious youth because somatic symptoms and avoidance lead these students to visit the school nurse. The authors state that "Task shifting responsibility for delivering brief mental health interventionsto school nurses is feasible and shows promise for reducing anxiety and related impairment" and note the approach "may also be integrated within a response to intervention model used in schools." Nurses in the study delivered an average of about six brief sessions per child over 8 weeks after a 1-day training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Nurses receive a 1-day training in the assigned intervention, intervention materials, and some degree of consultation until they achieve a set criterion for adherence and competence, as the authors recommend for future tests
#### Constraints
- Not all nurses or school systems view mental health interventions as part of their job; more than 50 school districts were approached to recruit 30 nurses, and school systems may need cost-benefit data before adopting

### Target Learners
- elementary school students ages 5–12 with excessive anxiety identified by school nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing anxiety symptoms, somatic complaints, and related impairment to improve functioning and classroom participation
