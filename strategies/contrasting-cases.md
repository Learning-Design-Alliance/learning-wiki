Now content for Contrasting Cases:

Description: Learners are presented with two or more cases that differ on one or more critical dimensions (target features) and are asked to compare them, which makes the deep structural features visible. Contrasting cases can precede instruction ("time for telling" / invention tasks) to prepare learners to learn from subsequent exposition, or follow instruction to refine discrimination. Rooted in Marton's variation theory — discernment requires variation; Schwartz & Bransford's PFL (preparation for future learning).

Design implications: Comparison directs attention to differences rather than surface features; per Alfieri et al. (2013) meta-analysis, guided case comparisons outperform studying cases individually, especially when cases are contrasting (vs. merely similar) and when learners receive guidance. Contrasting pairs must share surface features but differ on target concepts, so learners don't waste resources on irrelevant variation (cognitive load).
