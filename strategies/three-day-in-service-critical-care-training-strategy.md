# Deliver a three-day in-service critical care training using existing hospital structures, local facilitators, and supervised skill practice with return demonstrations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete recipe: run one three-day session per hospital, facilitated by local staff, using available resources (manikins, monitors, a portable ventilator). Teaching combined lectures, group discussions, skill sessions and return demonstrations, with participants divided into supervised groups during practice and daily participant note-takers reporting back for recap. The article concludes the programme "could be used for upskilling nurses in critical care settings in Malawi and other developing countries with a similar context".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Negotiation with nurse leaders so staffing and hospital services are not disrupted
- A preparatory meeting with facilitators a week before training
#### Constraints
- Skills are rapidly lost when training is not repeated, so ongoing implementation is imperative per the authors

### Target Learners
- in-service critical care nurses in ICUs and HDUs in resource-poor settings

### Target Learning Goals
- practical critical care competencies such as suctioning, mechanical ventilation care, CPR, and interpretation of arterial blood gases
