# Assign students misleading and well-explained web articles and videos for comparison, discussion, and presentation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that after introducing QIST concepts, educators assign homework and classroom activities in which "students must read (watch) some articles (videos) on the web with misleading headlines related to those concepts" alongside articles that elucidate the concepts well. Students then compare both types in class discussion, search for misinformation-propagating and well-teaching sources themselves, and present findings in small groups before whole-class discussion, with educator support as needed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must first have been introduced to the relevant QIST concepts before comparing sources
- Educators provide appropriate support to reinforce concepts and help students differentiate sources
#### Constraints
- The article proposes this activity as a recommendation; it reports no evaluation of the activity's effectiveness

### Target Learners
- introductory college students and early learners at pre-college levels

### Target Learning Goals
- critical evaluation of QIST sources
- learning to go beyond flashy headlines to understand QIST concepts
