# Develop a centralised, strategically governed SBE framework with embedded leadership and faculty development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that the organisation develop a strategy, governance and operation model aligned with organisational principles, including a governance and leadership model for simulation that is embedded and supported across all levels of the organisation and is centralised to increase fiscal efficiencies and sustainability. This model positions simulation not primarily as a technology-based asset but as an organisationally driven, educational support system enabling curriculum integration, faculty development, evaluation and sustainable practice. It should incorporate outcome measures at multiple levels to demonstrate value and return on investment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clearly articulated rationale for SBE defining its intended contribution to student learning, workforce preparedness, interprofessional collaboration, patient safety and educational quality.
- A mandated leadership team overseeing reporting functions, curriculum integration, faculty development initiatives and simulation programmes across the organisation.
#### Constraints
- Regardless of the level of readiness, unless sufficient resources are devoted to SBE, it will not be successful or sustainable.

### Target Learners
- Nurse education leaders
- Nursing simulation programme managers
- Academic nursing managers

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustainable integration of simulation-based education across multidisciplinary, multi-campus health programmes
