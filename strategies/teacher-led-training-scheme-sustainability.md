# Train interested teachers to run the astronomy discovery sessions themselves to make the project sustainable

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
To continue and expand the project beyond the pilot's reliance on local astronomers, the authors propose a training scheme in which astronomers transfer knowledge of the software and required skills to interested teachers, who then conduct the sessions in the same format. The article states this "would make the project more sustainable, as it previously required a large time commitment from local astronomers, including travel time to the schools". Running the club at the same school for successive years would also build knowledge of the IT setup.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A training scheme to transfer software and skills knowledge from astronomers to teachers; some ongoing astronomer support
#### Constraints
- The pilot format required a couple of hours of preparation per week from astronomers, which could strain workload

### Target Learners
- Key Stage 3 pupils across a wider range of schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained access to authentic astronomical research experiences
