Design recommendations for inclusive-pedagogy simulations: nuanced options, emotion-surfacing prompts, structured feedback¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

From the study's findings, the authors recommend that simulation developers strengthen candidate learning by improving scenario design. Specifically, they advise that developers "include more nuanced response options (beyond binary decision pathways), embed reflective prompts that explicitly surface candidates' emotions and reasoning at each decision point, and provide structured feedback that models inclusive, stakeholder-responsive communication across multiple audiences." These recommendations respond directly to the binary-decision and complexity limitations teacher educators reported.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Simulation scenarios with decision points at which reflective prompts and feedback can be embedded

Constraints¶

  • Recommendations address design of future simulations; the current version's binary pathways limited nuance, per teacher educator feedback

Target Learners¶

  • teacher candidates in preparation programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • inclusive stakeholder-responsive communication
  • professional reflection and decision-making

Affordances¶

  • Situated Simulation Framework Inclusive Pedagogy

Claims¶

  • The simulation increased teacher candidates' confidence and self-efficacy for addressing sensitive topics, but did not alleviate concerns about parent and administrator backlash [+W]
  • Teacher candidates moved from abstract inclusivity ideals to specific instructional and relational strategies after the simulation [+W]
  • Teacher educators found the simulation fostered critical thinking and reflection but faced integration, accessibility, and binary-decision limitations [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Include debriefing with reflective questions after simulation sessions before receiving feedback
  • Sequence virtual simulation before authentic video across skill-development stages in teacher observation training
  • Future research should position students and other stakeholders as partners in the design of online simulation approaches
