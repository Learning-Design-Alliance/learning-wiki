# Use extended task-specific rating scales for formative OSCE feedback despite lower psychometric efficiency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends reserving extended, task-specific rating scales for formative purposes: their precise descriptors give detailed feedback on individual performance even though they rate less efficiently. The authors state that "more detailed feedback on performance - guided by precise, extensive rating scales - might be supportive in formative OSCE, despite lower psychometric efficiency." This positions scale choice by assessment purpose: generic scales for summative decisions, extended scales when the goal is detailed feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Raters need task-specific descriptors precise enough to guide feedback on individual performance across the assessed domains.
#### Constraints
- Lower psychometric efficiency: the extended scale showed lower internal consistency (Cronbach's α 0.699 vs. 0.803) and needed 13 stations to reach a projected Phi ≥ 0.65 versus ten for the generic scale.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students assessed in abdominal ultrasound OSCEs

### Target Learning Goals
- detailed formative feedback on basic abdominal ultrasound performance domains
