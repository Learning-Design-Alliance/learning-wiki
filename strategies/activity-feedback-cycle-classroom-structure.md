# Classroom implementation structure: preparation, introduction, activity, feedback cycle

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Each class runs a preparation–introduction–activity–feedback progression: after a 2-3 minute introduction, "students are allowed to self-organize into groups numbering between two and ﬁve members" and work on activities without books or computers, while "the ratio of students to in structors/TAs can be as high as 35:1"; feedback sessions follow, and the sequence repeats through the class period.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A pre-class meeting between the instructor and TAs to review the activities and discuss anticipated questions/difficulties is necessary
- Students complete targeted reading and pre-class online quizzes before each class
- The classroom needs movable tables and chairs to facilitate group work
#### Constraints
- With a class size of 35 students or less, no additional TA help is necessary compared to the traditionally taught course
- Not every group is visited by the instructor or TA during a given activity

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- active engagement
- collaborative problem solving
- immediate feedback

### Affordances
- Deliberate Practice Framework Upper Division
