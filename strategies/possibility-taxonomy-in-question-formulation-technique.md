# Incorporate the finer possibility-oriented question taxonomy into the Question Formulation Technique

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extending classroom question-posing practices such as the Question Formulation Technique (QFT), which typically distinguishes only open and closed questions, by adding the possibility-thinking dimension. They write that "Incorporating this finer taxonomy into QFT can inform efforts by teachers to more deliberately cultivate questions that go beyond surface information-seeking and into the realm of deeper forms of inquiry and imagination." Possibility-oriented questions are less common among students yet have been argued to be especially valuable for fostering exploration, autonomy, and creativity.

### Target Learning Goals
