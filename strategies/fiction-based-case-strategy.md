# Use fiction-based cases — short stories with correct or deliberately mixed science, dialogues, and novel-versus-movie comparisons — to teach physics and astronomy content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The article recommends fiction as case material in several variants: a short story with correct science only that guides students to learn the science behind it; a story that "intentionally includes both correct and incorrect science" so students identify which is which; staged dialogues in Galileo's style read aloud in sections with discussion; and comparing the science in Sagan's Contact novel with the movie adaptation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Fiction cases must be supplemented with readings and accompanied by discussion questions for groups and the whole forum
#### Constraints
- The variants are described from the author's own astrobiology course use; no outcome data specific to fiction cases are reported

### Target Learners
- introductory astrobiology students
- non-science majors fulfilling general education science requirements

### Target Learning Goals
- space weather and the Earth's magnetosphere
- radioactivity
- origin-of-life topics
- distinguishing correct from incorrect science
