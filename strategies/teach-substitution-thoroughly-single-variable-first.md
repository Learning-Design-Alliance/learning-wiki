# Teach substitution thoroughly in single-variable calculus, including its conditions, before multivariable change of variables

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The author argues the multivariable change-of-variables method is much easier to learn if its one-variable ancestor is treated thoroughly in Calculus I, including checking that substitutions are C1 and one-to-one and changing the limits. He writes that teaching substitution in several variables "would help greatly" if the method "were thoroughly taught in the case of one variable (usually in calculus I)", and says he cannot overemphasize this point.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Single-variable courses must cover the conditions on substitutions (C1, one-to-one) and limit changes, not just the mechanical formula
#### Constraints
- Stated as the author's recommendation from exam-error analysis; the article reports no experiment testing it

### Target Learners
- undergraduate calculus students

### Target Learning Goals
- change of variables
- Jacobian
- multiple integration
