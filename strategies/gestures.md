# Gestures

## Description
Gestures are physical movements, typically of the hands or arms, used to communicate meaning or support learning. Teachers and learners can use hand signals to communicate or support their learning. Research shows that students are more accurate in counting when they point to each item. Visual gestures that complement spoken language allow for the processing of information in multiple ways, supporting greater retention of information in Working and Short-term Memory. Providing a visualization or animation in addition to audio or text activates visual Working Memory and supports deeper comprehension. Developers can also use these visualizations or enactments to capture and maintain Attention on key content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- No specific materials required, but effective implementation depends on clear, intentional use of gestures and visualizations.
#### Constraints
- Overuse of gestures can be distracting. Some learners may not benefit from or understand certain gestures.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learner accuracy in tasks involving counting or memory recall. Measure comprehension through formative assessments following instruction with and without gestures.

## Impact
- Research indicates that gesturing can significantly improve learning outcomes, particularly in math. Gestures can help students transfer learning to new contexts, such as transferring knowledge learned in an addition-based equation to a multiplication-based equation.
