# Design activities that make students' AI-supported learning processes visible through reflection prompts and process documentation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because submitted code provides limited evidence of what students did after receiving AI-generated assistance, the article recommends reflection prompts and process documentation asking students to explain how they used AI, what they accepted or rejected, what they modified, and what they learned. Such practices shift attention from detecting AI use to understanding how students learn with AI, helping educators distinguish copy-and-paste submission from meaningful AI-supported learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must design and collect reflection prompts or process documentation alongside code submissions
#### Constraints
- Grounded in one student's self-reported strategies; the article notes these may not represent all learners

### Target Learners
- undergraduate programming students

### Target Learning Goals
- making learning processes visible
- distinguishing meaningful AI-supported learning from answer copying
