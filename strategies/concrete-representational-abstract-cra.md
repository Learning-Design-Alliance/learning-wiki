# Concrete-Representational-Abstract (CRA)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
CRA is an instructional approach that uses concrete materials, visual representations, and abstract symbols to teach math concepts. Learners first manipulate concrete objects to understand a concept, then draw pictures representing the objects, and finally use abstract numbers and symbols. As instructors move through the sequence, abstract symbols should be used *in conjunction with* concrete materials and drawings — not after them — to promote association between symbolic notation and the underlying quantities. The approach descends from Bruner's enactive–iconic–symbolic sequence and is a staple of special education mathematics instruction.

## Design Implications

CRA manages intrinsic cognitive load by giving abstract notation a referent learners can see and touch, reducing the risk that symbols are manipulated procedurally without meaning [Overloaded working memory degrades learning outcomes.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+M]. Pairing representations with symbols also exploits dual channels of processing [Dual coding of verbal and visual information improves recall.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-recall.md) [+M]. However, concrete materials are not automatically helpful: poorly chosen manipulatives can distract from the target structure rather than illuminate it [Manipulatives support learning only when they make the target structure salient.](../claims/example-problem-sequences-reduce-cognitive-load.md) [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Concrete materials matched to the concept (e.g., base ten blocks for place value, fraction tiles for equivalence), drawing materials, and explicit teaching methods
- Instructor modeling and description at each level, with ample [practice](../elements/practice.md) and [feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) before transitioning
- Deliberate overlap between levels: abstract symbols introduced alongside, not after, concrete and pictorial work
- A mastery criterion at each level before fading to the next

#### Constraints
- Transitions that move too quickly leave learners with symbols but no underlying concept; learners with weak fact fluency or memory difficulties may stall at the abstract level
- Concrete materials that are themselves complex (e.g., money for teaching decimals) can add extraneous load [Overloaded working memory degrades learning outcomes.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-M]
- For learners with strong prior knowledge of a concept, the concrete phase is redundant and slows progress [Guidance and support become less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Requires more instructional time than symbol-first teaching; impractical as a universal default for every topic

#### Implementation Variability
- **Gradual vs. simultaneous**: classic CRA fades each level out before the next; a simultaneous variant presents all three representations side by side throughout
- **Virtual manipulatives**: apps such as Brainingcamp and The Math Learning Center's [Number Frames](https://www.mathlearningcenter.org/apps) replicate concrete materials digitally with comparable outcomes in several studies
- **CRA for algebra**: Witzel's CRA sequence uses concrete cups and counters for integers and equation solving, extending the approach beyond arithmetic

### Target Learners
- Learners with mathematics learning difficulties and disabilities — the strongest evidence base [CRA instruction improves math outcomes for students with learning disabilities.](../claims/example-problem-sequences-reduce-cognitive-load.md) [+M]
- Young learners encountering a concept for the first time, for whom symbols alone carry no meaning
- Less beneficial for advanced learners who already operate symbolically [Guidance and support become less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: why a procedure works, not just how
- Procedural fluency grounded in meaning, supporting retention and transfer
- Representational flexibility: connecting symbols, pictures, and quantities

### Instructions
1. Activate relevant prior knowledge and preteach prerequisite skills ([Direct Instruction](../elements/direct-instruction.md), [Supportive Information](../elements/supportive-information.md))
2. **Concrete phase**: model the concept with physical materials while narrating the mathematics; learners manipulate and explain ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
3. **Representational phase**: replace objects with drawings or diagrams of the same objects, explicitly linking each mark to the material it stands for
4. **Abstract phase**: introduce symbols *alongside* the drawings, then fade the supports ([Procedural Information](../elements/procedural-information.md), [Simple-to-Complex Sequencing](../elements/simple-to-complex-sequencing.md))
5. Provide immediate corrective [feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) at each level and [assess performance](../elements/assess-performance.md) against a mastery criterion before advancing

## Related Strategies
- [Cognitively Guided Instruction (CGI for Math)](../patterns/cgi-for-math.md) — shares CRA's commitment to grounding symbols in learners' own models of quantities
- [Explicit Teaching](../patterns/explicit-teaching.md) — CRA is typically delivered through explicit modeling, guided practice, and independent practice
- [Manipulative-Based Instruction](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md) — the concrete phase generalizes beyond mathematics to science and other domains

## Examples
- **Place value**: learners build 342 with base ten blocks, then draw flats/longs/units, then write the numeral — with the numeral introduced while blocks are still visible
- **Fraction equivalence**: fraction tiles compared physically, then bar diagrams, then the symbolic procedure for finding common denominators
- **Witzel's CRA algebra curriculum**: solving *x* + 3 = 5 with cups and counters before pictorial sketches and finally symbolic manipulation
- **[Number Frames / Number Pieces apps](https://www.mathlearningcenter.org/apps)** — free virtual manipulatives supporting the concrete and representational phases digitally
