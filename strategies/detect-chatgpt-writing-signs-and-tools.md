# Detect machine-generated writing by checking citations, originality, factual errors, grammar, contextual awareness, and with language-analysis tools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article enumerates signs that work was written by a chatbot: repetitive or odd language patterns; missing or improper citations, since chatbots "are not capable of conducting original research or producing new ideas"; similarity to existing sources; factual errors or inconsistencies; unnaturally error-free grammar and spelling; and generic, less contextually aware text. It also recommends language analysis tools such as the GPT-2 Output Detector Demo.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Manual review or plagiarism detection tools, plus in some cases natural language processing or machine learning algorithms trained to recognise AI-generated content
#### Constraints
- Grammar and spelling cues vary depending on the quality of the AI language model and its training data; the article cautions detection will be an arms race

### Target Learners
- higher education students

### Target Learning Goals
- academic integrity
- accurate assessment of student understanding
