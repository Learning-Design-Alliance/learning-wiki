# Incorporate simulation-based training, situational judgment tasks, and case analysis to develop higher-order applied competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the BOPPPS-based blended teaching model and traditional lecture-based teaching did not differ significantly on true/false or case-based essay questions, the authors recommend that future courses should incorporate more simulation-based training, situational judgment tasks, and case analysis to support the development of higher-order applied competencies. This addresses the selective advantage observed on single-choice items and the lack of differentiation on applied reasoning formats.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires course assessment frameworks that include diverse question formats beyond single-choice items
#### Constraints
- The current assessment tools included relatively few items addressing Learning Objective 4 on long-term postdisaster care and rehabilitation needs

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- Higher-order applied competencies in disaster nursing
- Applied reasoning and decision-making in disaster scenarios
