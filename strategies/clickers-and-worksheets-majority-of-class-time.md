# Devote the majority of class time to active learning via clicker questions and small-group worksheets

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course spends only a small amount of time lecturing, with the considerable majority of each period devoted to active learning. The article reports that "The most signiﬁcant tools to aid active learning that we have employed are “clicker”-based questions about scientiﬁc concepts, graphs, or in-class demonstrations, and in-class worksheets." Worksheets break lessons into questions and discussion topics that small groups work on while instructors circulate, then the class reconvenes. The author argues active learning is especially important for general education students less interested in science.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teaching assistants and instructors walk through the class offering advice and asking questions during worksheet work
#### Constraints
- The article notes active learning in general education courses "has been much less explored" compared to introductory courses for science majors

### Target Learners
- non-science-major undergraduates

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement with scientific material
- conceptual understanding
- confidence in scientific inquiry
