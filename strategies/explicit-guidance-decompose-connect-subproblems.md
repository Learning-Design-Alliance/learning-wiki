# Provide explicit guidance and sustained practice on decomposing multi-step problems and connecting sub-problems when using solved examples for analogical reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that analogical-reasoning activities with solved examples include explicit guidance on dividing a multi-step problem into sub-problems, connecting consecutive sub-problems (e.g., recognizing that the final speed of one sub-problem becomes the initial speed of the next), and organizing information from the solved example. The authors hypothesize that “students will gradually develop expertise” if such activities are “sustained and rewarded throughout the course,” helping students learn the applicability of physics principles in diverse situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must work with a solved example and a target problem sharing the same underlying principles but differing in structure, and guidance must direct attention to the principles involved in both problems.
#### Constraints
- In this study, even intervention 3 students who received an explicit hint about applying conservation of mechanical energy twice had great difficulty figuring out the correct solution process, so a single hint session was insufficient.

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students in calculus-based and algebra-based courses

### Target Learning Goals
- multi-step problem solving via systematic decomposition into sub-problems
- analogical transfer between problems with the same underlying principles
- correct selection and application of physics principles (conservation of momentum and mechanical energy)
