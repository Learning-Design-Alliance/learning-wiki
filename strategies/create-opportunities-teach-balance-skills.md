# Create opportunities for trainees to learn the skills of balancing competing professional values and work-life demands

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators deliberately create opportunities for trainees to practise resolving conflicts among competing values — continuity of care, compliance with regulations, the need for sleep, professional identity — and to learn the life-long skills of balancing work with personal and other professional interests. It warns that enforced compliance with duty-hour rules may make learners' conflict resolutions less visible over time, closing teaching opportunities, and challenges attendings to reflect on what they value and the attributions they make about trainees' behaviours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators must reflect on what they value in trainees and the attributions they make regarding their behaviours before designing these opportunities
#### Constraints
- The article acknowledges there may be more questions than answers, including whether a new professionalism is good or bad and for whom, and how to assess professionalism without risking misattributions in either direction

- residents
- medical students

- work-life balance
