# Embed explicit AI ethics instruction early and use scenario-based, guidance-oriented teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on the finding that students' ethical judgments form early in the transition to higher education and that uncertainty persists around modified AI content, the authors recommend institutions "intentionally embedding explicit AI ethics instruction early in the curricula of academic programs". At the course level they suggest structured scenario-based discussions, guided decision-making prompts, structured reflection, transparency assignments such as disclosure statements, and requiring students to justify AI use decisions. They also recommend moving beyond rigid binary prohibitions toward nuanced, guidance-oriented policies and faculty development on modeling ethical AI use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional policies that clearly articulate boundaries between AI-assisted learning and AI-enabled academic misconduct
#### Constraints
- The authors frame these as prospective directions; experimental studies are needed to test whether such instruction improves ethical judgment

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students, especially first-year students in their transition to higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- ethical judgment and responsible use of generative AI
