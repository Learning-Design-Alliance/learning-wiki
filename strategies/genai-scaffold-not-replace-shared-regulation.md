# Design educational GenAI to scaffold rather than replace shared regulation during collaboration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that GenAI availability shifted regulation away from socially shared forms towards hybrid co-regulatory forms, the authors recommend that educational GenAI be designed to scaffold rather than replace shared regulation during collaboration. The article's agent model illustrates one approach: prompts that elicit explanation, invite perspective-taking, and guide task progression without supplying solutions, so that regulatory responsibility remains with the group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- GenAI interventions should target how groups coordinate and regulate joint activity rather than providing direct solutions
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a small, demographically skewed sample with many second-language English speakers in text-only interaction; the authors call for research in more diverse and multimodal settings

### Target Learners
- university students in AI-supported collaborative learning

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining socially shared regulation of learning during group work
