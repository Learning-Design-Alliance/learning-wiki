# Use this single-session expressive writing study as a pilot for future trials varying writing form, administration mode, and session frequency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future research treat this study as a pilot and evaluate expressive writing's efficacy "relative to forms of expressive writing (i.e. writing about life goals and writing about positive experience)", and relative to administration mode (individually or in groups) and frequency (single instance or consecutive periods). They also recommend proposing a theoretical framework for why expressive writing does not work for certain age groups and exploring its boundary conditions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Future studies should compare writing forms, individual versus group administration, and single versus repeated sessions
#### Constraints
- The present null results came from a single 15-minute session with an unselected, relatively healthy sample, so they may not generalise to multi-session or selected samples

### Target Learners
- female university students
- potentially older age groups such as the elderly

### Target Learning Goals
- body satisfaction
- positive affect
- psychological well-being after thin-ideal exposure
