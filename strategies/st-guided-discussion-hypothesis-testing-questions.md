# Guide students through hypothesis-testing discussions instead of providing experimental recipes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course runs as three six-hour classes a week apart in which students develop the tracking software, acquire data, and write a report. Rather than recipes, instructors "guide the students through the discussions that emerge in the laboratory as they perform the experiments", orienting the analysis around questions that test the Brownian hypotheses, such as "can the motion of these beads be described as Brownian?". This opens a rich discussion on physics and challenges students toward critical thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Before the first experimental class, students are asked to read the provided material and associated topics on stochastic trajectories, Langevin dynamics, Brownian motion, and the experimental setup.
#### Constraints
- The reported implementation took three classes of 6 hours each, separated a week apart, within a full semester experimental course.

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical thinking through testing Brownian-motion hypotheses
- data analysis and scientific report writing
