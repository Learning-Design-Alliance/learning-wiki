# Social Emotional Learning Curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Social Emotional Learning (SEL) curricula are structured, sequenced instructional programs that explicitly teach five interrelated competencies: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, and responsible decision-making (the CASEL framework). Delivery typically combines direct instruction of skills, structured practice through role-play or discussion, and integration into academic content and classroom routines.

## Design Implications

Well-implemented SEL programs produce measurable gains in social-emotional skills, attitudes, and academic achievement, with effects persisting years after program completion [Durlak et al.'s meta-analysis of 213 school-based programs.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2010.01564) [+S]. Effectiveness depends on the SAFE features — Sequenced, Active, Focused, Explicit — rather than on program branding alone; programs using active skill practice outperform those relying on lecture or discussion alone [+S]. Skills must be modeled and rehearsed, not merely described, consistent with observational learning mechanisms [Social learning theory.](../theories/social-learning-theory.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A sequenced scope-and-lesson sequence rather than isolated activities
- Active practice formats: role-play, rehearsal, feedback ([Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md), [Acting-Role Play](../strategies/acting-role-play.md))
- Adult modeling — teachers demonstrating the skills they teach, since students learn from observed behavior [+M]
- Integration into classroom management and academic tasks, not a stand-alone block disconnected from the rest of the day
- Implementation support: training and coaching for teachers, since effects shrink sharply under poor implementation fidelity [+M]

#### Constraints
- Effects are substantially weaker when implementation fidelity is low or when teachers lack buy-in and training [-M]
- One-off workshops or assemblies without sequencing and practice show negligible effects [-M]
- Curricula that teach skills without addressing classroom climate or [Belonging](../elements/belonging.md) show weaker transfer to real peer interactions [-W]
- Overly scripted delivery can crowd out responsive discussion and reduce student [Autonomy](../principles/autonomy.md), undermining internalization of the skills [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Universal (Tier 1)** classroom curricula (e.g., PATHS, Second Step, RULER) delivered to all students
- **Targeted (Tier 2/3)** small-group interventions for students with elevated needs
- **Embedded approaches** that integrate SEL into academic instruction and discipline practices rather than a separate period
- Brief targeted interventions can be surprisingly durable — a 2-session empathic discipline program for teachers cut suspensions significantly [Brief empathic discipline intervention reduces suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- K–12 students generally, with the strongest academic effects in elementary grades [+S]
- Students experiencing adversity or low [Belonging](../elements/belonging.md), for whom explicit social-skills instruction compensates for less modeling at home [~M]
- Students with behavioral or self-regulation difficulties benefit most from targeted small-group formats [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-regulation and emotion management skills
- Interpersonal skills: cooperation, conflict resolution, help-seeking
- Responsible decision-making and ethical reasoning
- Prerequisites for academic learning — attention, persistence, and classroom engagement [+S]

### Instructions
1. **Establish a safe climate first** — use [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) routines and community-building so students are willing to practice vulnerable skills publicly.
2. **Name and model the skill** — the teacher demonstrates the competency (e.g., a calm-down routine, an "I-statement"), consistent with [Social Learning Theory](../theories/social-learning-theory.md).
3. **Rehearse actively** — students practice through structured role-play ([Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md)) with peer and teacher feedback, not just discussion.
4. **Debrief and generalize** — connect the skill to real classroom situations and academic work; prompt students to plan when they will use it.
5. **Reinforce in context** — cue and praise the skill during authentic moments throughout the day rather than confining it to the SEL lesson block.
6. **Offer choice in application** — letting students select practice scenarios or goals supports ownership [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M].

## Examples
- **[PATHS (Promoting Alternative Thinking Strategies)](https://www.pathseducation.com)** — sequenced elementary curriculum teaching emotion vocabulary and self-control; among the most heavily evaluated universal SEL programs.
- **[Second Step](https://www.secondstep.org)** (Committee for Children) — K–8 curriculum with scripted lessons, puppet/video modeling, and skill rehearsal cards.
- **[RULER](https://ei.yale.edu)** (Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence) — an approach-based curriculum built around emotion "anchors" (Charter, Mood Meter) integrated into school climate rather than isolated lessons.
- **[CASEL](https://casel.org)** — the framework and program-clearinghouse organization; its SAFE criteria summarize the design features that predict effectiveness.
