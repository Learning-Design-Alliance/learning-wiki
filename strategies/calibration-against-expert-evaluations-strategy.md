# Use calibration against expert evaluations to develop and assess student peer-evaluation ability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course assessed and trained students' evaluation abilities by comparing their evaluations to expert evaluations: students evaluated common instructor-evaluated videos for practice and for calibration credit, received a "calibration grade" dependent on how well their evaluation aligned with the expert evaluation, and were shown the detailed instructor evaluation after each practice and calibration video. A hidden calibration video embedded in the peer-video pool provided an unprimed measure of alignment. This design let the authors track changes in evaluation behavior across the semester.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A corpus of instructor-evaluated common videos for each lab
- Instructional videos including a step-by-step example evaluation of two actual video lab reports
#### Constraints
- Students were never shown the instructor evaluation for the hidden calibration video

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduates learning to evaluate scientific presentations

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating scientific arguments
- aligning evaluation judgments with expert norms
