# Target high-importance, low-preparedness procedures for curricular interventions, with simulation as a promising modality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that procedures with high perceived importance but low perceived preparedness be prioritized for curricular interventions, and suggest simulation as a suitable teaching modality for these skills. They argue simulation "offers a chance for residents to attempt well-defined tasks with the appropriate level of difficulty and ample opportunity for feedback, repetition, and correction of errors", and cite a meta-analysis suggesting simulation-based curricula with deliberate practice may be superior to traditional clinical education for specific skill acquisition. Focused training may help residents gather the volume of repeated, deliberate practice needed for competence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires identifying procedures with high importance and low preparedness ratings to focus limited curricular resources
#### Constraints
- Simulation use for less acute skills is less well described than for resuscitation and acute care skills; the relationship between perceived and true competence remains questionable

### Target Learners
- pediatric residents

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural skills competency in required pediatric procedures
