# Practical considerations for mentors and trainees to foster telementoring literacy: expectation management, situational awareness, intertwined feedback and mentorship, and scaffolding complexity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Derived from the study's empirical data, the article presents four practical considerations in Table 1 to guide future telementoring program designs: early expectation management (acknowledging mentors and trainees may need to adapt to the technology before meaningful learning), enhanced situational awareness (explicit attention to the situation during real-time telementoring and the broader healthcare system), intertwined feedback and mentorship (a common language and a trusting, motivating relationship), and scaffolding complexity (jointly adapting to coinciding technological, situational and task complexities).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentors and trainees must check iteratively whether instructions are understood in the same way and discuss jointly what is needed to make telementored practice relevant and efficient.
#### Constraints
- When task complexity exceeds what can be scaffolded through telementoring alone, the article recommends considering a blended learning design.

### Target Learners
- Clinician trainees and mentors in telementored programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Fostering telementoring literacy in clinical skill training
