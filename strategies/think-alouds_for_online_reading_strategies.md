# Think-Alouds for Online Reading Strategies

## Description
Teachers model their online reading strategies through think-alouds. This involves verbalizing the thought processes behind finding relevant information and choosing which links to click. By explicitly modeling these processes, teachers support students' self-regulation and inhibition while building their background knowledge for effectively navigating non-linear texts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to online reading materials, a platform for modeling (e.g., projector, interactive whiteboard), and time for explicit instruction and guided practice.
#### Constraints
- May require significant preparation time for the teacher to identify appropriate online resources and plan the think-alouds. Students may need additional support to internalize and apply the modeled strategies independently.

### Target Learners

- Middle school, high school, and adult learners who need to improve their digital literacy skills.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance self-regulation, build background knowledge, improve online reading comprehension, develop information literacy skills

### Affordances
- Provides a clear model of expert thinking, reduces cognitive load by demonstrating effective strategies, and promotes metacognition by making thinking visible. Supports learners in developing self-regulation skills for online reading.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to different reading levels and content areas. Teachers can adjust the complexity of the online reading tasks and the explicitness of the think-alouds based on learner needs.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe students' online reading behaviors and strategies. Collect student reflections on their own information-seeking processes. Compare pre- and post-intervention scores on online reading comprehension assessments.

## Impact
- Research supports the use of think-alouds for improving reading comprehension. Explicit modeling and metacognitive reflection have been shown to enhance self-regulation and strategic learning.
