# Peer feedback against success criteria: students locate where peers are in their learning and give actionable clues, hints, suggestions, or wonderings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The brief defines peer feedback as "the practice of students analyzing evidence of learning (what another student says, does, makes, or writes) in relation to an interpretive framework, i.e., the success criteria, and then giving actionable feedback." Students locate where other students are in their learning and give clues, hints, suggestions, or wonderings about next steps. Teachers support the process by designing structured occasions to learn how to give and receive feedback, providing practice and reflection with protocols, and communicating why peer feedback benefits learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An interpretive framework such as success criteria; structured occasions and protocols for practicing giving and receiving feedback; clear communication about why students engage in peer feedback.

- 

### Target Learners
- Early elementary students, a majority designated as English learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving work (e.g., reading fluency) against learning goals and success criteria
