# School counselors implementing cross-age peer mentoring as a research-based school-climate intervention in rural, high-poverty schools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends school counselors deliver cross-age peer mentoring—pairing older students with younger (seventh grade and under) students—as a research-based intervention targeting school climate via school connectedness, aligning with state school-improvement reporting requirements. The authors state this "postured school counselors within pertinent school improvement activities" and suggest counselors conduct such investigations through collaboration with state school counseling associations and university counselor-preparation faculty.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School administration interest and IRB approval
- Trained mentors and a structured activity schedule
- A recurring meeting period for weekly sessions
#### Constraints
- Whole-sample effects may be non-significant (as here); PK-12 logistical challenges (competing initiatives, scheduling) can constrain implementation and evaluation

### Target Learners
- middle school mentees at risk of disengagement
- students in high-poverty rural schools

### Target Learning Goals
- school connectedness
- school climate improvement
