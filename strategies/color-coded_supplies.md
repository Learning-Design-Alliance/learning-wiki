# Color-Coded Supplies

## Description
Color-coding school supplies can make organization easier and more streamlined for kids and the adults in charge. Assigning different colors to various supplies can help kids quickly identify what they need for each subject or task. Assign different colors to various supplies to help quickly identify them. Use the first color for papers that need to come home but that don’t need to go back to school. Use the second color for papers that need a caregiver’s attention and have to be returned to school. And use the third color for homework, labeling one pocket “To Do” and the other “Completed.”

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School supplies (folders, notebooks, etc.), colored labels or markers
#### Constraints
- Requires initial setup and consistent use. May not be effective for learners with color blindness. Dependent on the learner maintaining the color-coding system.

- Colors can be assigned based on the learner's preferences and needs. Color-coding system can be adapted to different subjects or tasks.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learner's ability to quickly identify and retrieve supplies. Track the organization of learning materials. Gather feedback from the learner on the usefulness of the color-coding system.

## Impact
- Visual cues, such as color-coding, have been shown to improve organization and memory. Color-coded systems can reduce cognitive overload and promote efficiency.
