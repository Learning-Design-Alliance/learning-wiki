Balance entertainment and educational elements when designing or selecting educational games¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In its recommendations, the article advises that when "Designing or selecting computer-assisted educational games, it should be attentive to balance the proportion of entertainment and educational elements", because lacking that balance makes achieving the objectives difficult. It further recommends considering students' levels and developmental features, avoiding a sexist approach, and ensuring games let students practice and evaluate what they have learned beyond knowledge transfer.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Software support from experts may be desired; student feelings and opinions might be obtained via questionnaires during design

Constraints¶

  • Games under the level of students could be boring for them

Target Learners¶

  • 6th-grade middle school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Turkish grammar (parts of speech)

Claims¶

  • Computer-assisted educational games raised 6th-grade grammar achievement more than the existing curriculum over 12 weeks [+W]
  • Computer-assisted educational games improved attitudes toward grammar more than the existing curriculum [+W]
  • Computer-assisted educational games improved attitudes toward the Turkish course more than the existing curriculum [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Select games that maximize participation and match proficiency, and use games as complementary activities
  • Design teaching material to embody content, arouse interest, sustain attention, activate students, and feel game-like
