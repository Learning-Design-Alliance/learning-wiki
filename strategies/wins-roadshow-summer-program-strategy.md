# WINS: combine school science roadshows with a broader single-sex summer day program

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author proposes Women Into the Natural Sciences (WINS) as a two-part initiative: traveling all-school assembly roadshows featuring science demonstrations with well-represented female scientists, followed by an application-based summer day program of about 50 students with four instructors, structured in units across many science fields. The single-sex format is proposed because surveyed students "felt the presence of boys in the classroom would inhibit their learning", with the expectation of converting to a mixed-sex program once equity indicators improve.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A host university with instructors, demonstrators, and laboratory facilities
- Funding via grant or minimal tuition
- A generated script and handouts for the roadshows
#### Constraints
- The author presents the single-sex format as a short-term solution justified while schools remain inequitable, to be converted to a mixed-sex program later

### Target Learners
- High school girls, reached through all-school assemblies and a summer application process

### Target Learning Goals
- Excitement about science across multiple fields
- Career awareness including non-academic science careers
- Presentation skills via poster projects and oral presentations
