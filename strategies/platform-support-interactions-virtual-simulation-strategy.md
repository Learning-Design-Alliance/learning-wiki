# Improve platform technical support and academic-related interactions, and use blended learning with virtual simulation for students in higher learning phases

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that online medical education should target its weakest-perceived aspects: platform service and learning-related interaction. It states that "more attention needs to be paid to improving technical support for platforms and academic-related interactions between instructors and students", and that for students in higher learning phases, blended learning combining online platforms with virtual simulation tools allowing practice and immediate feedback would appeal to advanced students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Investment in platform infrastructure and instructional design; development of suitable virtual clinical experimental systems
#### Constraints
- The authors note the study did not investigate the teaching modes used in ongoing courses and used single items for PU and satisfaction, which may affect reliability

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students, especially those in clinical medical education and clerkship rotation phases

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining engagement and satisfaction in formal online medical education; developing practical clinical skills online
