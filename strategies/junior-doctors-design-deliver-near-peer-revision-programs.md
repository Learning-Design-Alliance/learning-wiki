# Use junior doctors as near-peer tutors in roles beyond teaching delivery, including program design and organisation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that recent medical graduates act not only as deliverers of teaching but as designers, developers and co-ordinators of structured revision programs: "We therefore support the use of junior doctors as near-peer tutors in variety of roles aside from teaching delivery including program development and organisation." The authors' own program demonstrated this full-cycle role, with all three authors planning, co-ordinating and producing materials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enthusiastic and dedicated recent graduates with personal knowledge of the examination system
- A well-organised structure utilising appropriate teaching styles applied according to topics
- Senior support is very beneficial, as near-peer teaching should not have negative effects on preparation
#### Constraints
- Success may not be transferable to other situations; it relies on the motivation and enthusiasm of tutors and participating students

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- OSCE examination preparation
- confidence and expectation alignment

### Affordances
- Cognitive Congruence Near Peer Teaching
