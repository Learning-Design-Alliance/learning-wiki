# Model Receiving Feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Modeling feedback reception means the instructor deliberately demonstrates the *recipient* side of feedback: reading or hearing feedback, managing the emotional reaction, interpreting what it means, deciding what to act on, and revising work accordingly — all narrated aloud. This is a form of [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) applied to the feedback process, making the usually invisible work of a skilled feedback recipient observable and imitable. It treats feedback literacy as a teachable competency rather than expecting learners to infer it from comments alone.

## Design Implications

Feedback only improves learning when recipients actively process and use it, yet most feedback research and practice focuses on what the giver writes, not what the receiver does [Feedback improves learning when learners act on it; recipient engagement is the active ingredient.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Modeling reception closes this gap: by watching an expert respond to critical feedback — including initial defensiveness, confusion, and repair — learners acquire both the interpretive strategies and the emotional regulation that feedback demands. Social modeling of coping responses is well established: observing a model persist through difficulty increases observers' own persistence and self-efficacy [Observing a model cope with difficulty raises observer self-efficacy and persistence.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_learning_theory) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Authentic feedback artifacts (real instructor comments, peer reviews, rubric evaluations) to model against
- Instructor willingness to demonstrate genuine reactions, including not knowing an answer or disagreeing with a comment
- A follow-on task in which learners apply the same reception process to their own feedback ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
- A shared vocabulary for feedback response (e.g., clarify → prioritize → plan → revise)

#### Constraints
- Modeled receptiveness that is visibly performative ("I love this feedback!") undermines credibility; learners detect and discount inauthentic modeling [-M]
- If the instructor models reception but assessment still rewards only final products, learners learn that acting on feedback is optional [Feedback without opportunity to use it produces minimal improvement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [-S]
- Modeling on sanitized or artificially positive feedback does not transfer to real critical feedback; the model must include negative and ambiguous comments
- Whole-class modeling may not match individual learners' actual emotional responses to critique; some learners need private coaching instead [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Live demonstration**: instructor receives real (anonymized) student work feedback and processes it aloud in class
- **Retrospective walkthrough**: instructor revisits feedback they once received (e.g., rejected manuscript reviews) and shows how they responded
- **Contrast cases**: side-by-side comparison of a defensive vs. a receptive response to identical feedback, sharpening discrimination between productive and unproductive reactions
- **Peer modeling**: a student who successfully revised based on feedback narrates their process to the class — often more credible than instructor modeling [~M]
- **Dialogic modeling**: two instructors model a feedback conversation, including asking clarifying questions and respectfully pushing back

### Target Learners
- Novices who have received little critical feedback and lack scripts for responding to it
- Learners with low feedback literacy who read comments as verdicts on ability rather than information for improvement [Learners who treat feedback as information for action improve more than those who treat it as judgment.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M]
- Students experiencing their first high-stakes critique (thesis drafts, design critiques, clinical performance reviews)
- Less necessary for learners who already demonstrate self-regulated response to feedback

### Target Learning Goals
- Feedback literacy: interpreting, evaluating, and acting on comments
- Self-regulated learning: using external information to monitor and adjust one's own work
- Affective regulation: managing defensiveness and anxiety in response to critique
- Revision skill: converting feedback into concrete improvement plans

### Instructions
1. Select an authentic feedback artifact with mixed positive, critical, and ambiguous comments.
2. Model the reception process aloud ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)): initial emotional reaction, then deliberate re-reading, sorting comments into "clear / unclear / disagree."
3. Demonstrate interpretation moves: asking what evidence would resolve an ambiguous comment, connecting a comment to the rubric or task criteria.
4. Demonstrate prioritization: choosing which one or two comments to act on first and why.
5. Model the revision itself, showing the before-and-after of the changed work.
6. Have learners repeat the same process on their own feedback ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)), ideally with a structured response template ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) or written response plan).
7. Follow up: revisit the revised work so learners see the payoff of acting on feedback ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) for learning, not just of learning).

## Examples
- **Writing center conferences** (e.g., the University of North Carolina Writing Center model): tutors narrate how they would respond to instructor comments on a draft before the student attempts their own response ([https://writingcenter.unc.edu](https://writingcenter.unc.edu)).
- **Design studio critiques** in architecture and art education: instructors model receiving critique from colleagues, demonstrating how professionals ask clarifying questions rather than defending immediately.
- **Graduate "responding to reviews" workshops**: advisors walk students through real peer-review reports they received, showing how they triaged reviewer comments for a resubmission.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** hint sequences implicitly model a receptive stance — treating an error as a prompt to try a next step rather than a verdict.
