# Use simulations, provided or student-directed data collection, and open-ended projects to preserve lab learning goals when hands-on equipment is unavailable

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article documents a set of strategies instructors used successfully during emergency remote lab instruction: switching to simulations (PhET, SPICE, Fritzing, MultisimLive, Pivot Interactives), sending equipment home or using smartphone measurement apps, having students analyze instructor-provided or published data, having students direct instructor data collection, and shifting toward open-ended, project-based work. Instructors reported these approaches met learning goals and, in some cases, "The level of student engagement was much higher in the remote format." The article presents these as approaches worth continuing beyond the pandemic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need time to develop and align new remote activities with course goals, which the article identifies as the most important resource
#### Constraints
- Simple simulations may not replicate the complex aspects of performing real experiments
- Home lab kits may not allow work with complex measurement apparatus important in beyond-first-year labs
- Using previously collected data may diminish student understanding of how data was acquired and interpreted

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in introductory and beyond-first-year lab courses

### Target Learning Goals
- reinforcing physics concepts
- developing experimental skills such as data analysis, communication, and troubleshooting
