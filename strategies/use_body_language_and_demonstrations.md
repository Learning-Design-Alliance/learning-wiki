# Use Body Language and Demonstrations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy pairs spoken or written instruction with non-verbal channels: gestures, facial expression, posture, and — most importantly — physical demonstration of the task itself. Instead of describing what to do, the instructor shows it, making the action observable and imitable. It draws on social learning theory: much procedural knowledge is acquired by observing a model rather than by decoding verbal descriptions [Bandura, A. (1977). Social learning theory.](../theories/social-learning-theory.md) [+S].

## Design Implications

Observation of a modeled performance reduces the working-memory and comprehension burden of translating verbal instructions into action, particularly for procedural and motor tasks [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) [+M]. Gesture research shows that speech-accompanying gestures add representational information that learners integrate with speech and later use in their own reasoning [Goldin-Meadow & Alibali, 2013] [+M]. Demonstrations are most effective when the instructor makes reasoning visible while performing — a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) overlay — and when learners immediately attempt the action themselves rather than only watching.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A visible, accurate model of the target performance (live, video, or animated)
- Gestures and demonstrations that are congruent with the verbal message; mismatched or decorative gesture adds noise [Clark & Mayer, 2016] [~M]
- Clear sightlines or camera framing so the critical movements are actually observable
- A follow-on opportunity for learners to imitate and receive [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) or [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md)

#### Constraints
- Watching without doing produces illusions of competence; observation alone rarely transfers without practice [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [-S]
- Complex demonstrations can overload novices when every movement is visible at once; segmenting or highlighting the critical action is needed [Chunking reduces extraneous load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [-M]
- Fine-grained or invisible processes (internal reasoning, sub-microscopic phenomena) cannot be demonstrated physically and require other representations
- Demonstrations can anchor learners to one way of performing; contrasting variants mitigates this [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Live modeling** — instructor performs in real time, pausing to narrate decisions
- **Video or animation** — allows replay, slow motion, and close-up framing of critical movements
- **Gesture-supported talk** — for conceptual content, using depictive gestures (e.g., tracing a graph's shape) rather than full performance
- **Peer modeling** — a near-peer demonstrates, which can raise observer [self-efficacy](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+W]
- **Erroneous demonstration** — deliberately showing a common mistake for learners to diagnose [Erroneous examples build conceptual knowledge.](../claims/erroneous-examples-build-conceptual-knowledge.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Novices and young learners, who rely more heavily on observed models than on verbal description [+M]
- Learners with limited language proficiency or reading difficulty, for whom demonstration bypasses the verbal channel [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [~M]
- Less beneficial for experts, who can extract what they need from verbal or written instructions alone [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural and psychomotor skills: techniques, tool use, laboratory and craft procedures
- Behavioral routines and classroom norms
- Conceptual support: gestures that embody relationships (e.g., symmetry, directionality, change over time)

### Instructions
1. Identify the exact observable performance learners must acquire; if it cannot be shown, choose a different strategy.
2. Plan the demonstration: segment it, and decide which movements or decisions to highlight [Chunking reduces working-memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M].
3. Model the performance while narrating reasoning aloud ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)), using gestures that map onto the content rather than idle motion.
4. Have learners imitate immediately with [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and corrective [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md), fading the model as competence grows ([Fading](../elements/fading.md)).
5. Where misconceptions are predictable, contrast a correct demonstration with a flawed one [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M].

## Examples
- A chemistry instructor demonstrates proper titration technique — hand position, swirl motion, reading the meniscus at eye level — while narrating each decision, then circulates as students replicate it.
- **YouTube-style video modeling in physical education and music** (e.g., slow-motion serve demonstrations in tennis instruction apps) lets learners replay and zoom on critical movements.
- An elementary teacher uses hand gestures tracing a number line while explaining addition with regrouping, then has students perform the same gestures as they solve — gesture production supports abstraction of the underlying concept [Goldin-Meadow & Alibali, 2013] [+M].
