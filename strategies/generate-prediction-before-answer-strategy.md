# Ask learners to generate a prediction before telling them the correct answer to stimulate curiosity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends prediction generation as an efficient, curiosity-stimulating instructional strategy: "a good strategy is to ask learners to generate a prediction before telling them the correct answer to a question." It notes the boost "is not achieved through extrinsic rewards but through an intrinsic desire to know whether a prediction holds true," and that the strategy can be used quickly, repeatedly, and simultaneously with large groups, including via mobile devices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must make and commit to a prediction before the correct answer is revealed
#### Constraints
- Curiosity is not the only factor determining learning performance; generating examples may compensate via prior-knowledge connection

### Target Learners
- school and university students, particularly in science curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- stimulating curiosity and promoting memory for factual content

### Affordances
- Knowledge Gap Relevance Account Prediction
