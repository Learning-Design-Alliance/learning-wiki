# Guided Play

## Description
Guided play combines the child-directed aspects of free play with intentional learning goals through light adult scaffolding. It involves exploration within a context designed to foster specific learning outcomes while maintaining child agency and gentle adult guidance to progress towards those goals. Adults can create educational settings for children to explore freely or engage in children's natural play, interjecting questions, comments, and explanations to enhance learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Play materials relevant to the learning goals, a space conducive to play, and an adult facilitator who can observe, ask guiding questions, and provide explanations without taking over the play activity.
#### Constraints
- The success of guided play depends on the adult's ability to strike a balance between providing guidance and allowing child-led exploration. Overly directive adults may stifle creativity and motivation. Requires preparation and thoughtful integration of learning goals into play scenarios.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Foster language development, enhance mathematics and spatial skills, promote cognitive development, encourage social and emotional growth

### Affordances
- Guided play leverages children's intrinsic motivation by allowing them to lead the activity while subtly incorporating educational elements. It is a flexible and engaging method that promotes active participation, discovery, and experimentation. The approach supports both academic and social-emotional growth through playful interaction.

### Personalization
- Guided play can be adapted to various settings and content areas by adjusting the learning opportunities within the play environment. Adults can tailor their level of involvement and the types of questions or prompts they provide based on individual learner needs and interests.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe children's engagement and interactions during play to assess understanding of concepts. Evaluate their ability to apply new vocabulary, solve problems, and demonstrate spatial skills within the play context. Track the connections children make between play and real-world applications.

## Impact
- Research indicates that guided play is an effective method for enhancing learning across various domains, including language, mathematics, and spatial skills. It supports the development of executive functions, social skills, and proactive control mechanisms. It has been shown to be more effective than direct instruction in some contexts, fostering creativity, emotional well-being, and social development.

## Examples
- In a 'restaurant' scenario, encourage creating menus, writing orders, and calculating bills to reinforce numeracy and literacy skills. During block play, ask questions about shapes and sizes to promote geometric understanding. While climbing a tree, discuss different tree characteristics and their role in the ecosystem to integrate science concepts.
