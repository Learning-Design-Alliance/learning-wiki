# Combine multiple interactive educational methods (simulation, role-play, workshops, seminars) to develop nursing students' triage competency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because a three-week role-playing program alone did not improve overall triage competency, the authors recommend that "to improve nursing students’ triage competency, it is necessary to develop a program that combines various educational methods such as simulation, role-play, workshops, and seminars." They argue these methods improve not only screening knowledge but also communication, physical assessment, and clinical reasoning, and that KTAS education should be embedded in undergraduate courses addressing critical thinking disposition, problem-solving ability, and clinical judgment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must address critical thinking disposition, problem-solving ability, and clinical judgment ability, not only KTAS knowledge and algorithm application
#### Constraints
- Short-term theoretical education alone cannot improve these competency factors, per the authors

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students
- triage nurses in emergency departments

### Target Learning Goals
- triage competency
- communication
- physical assessment
- clinical reasoning
