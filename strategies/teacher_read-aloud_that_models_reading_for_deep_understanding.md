# Teacher Read-Aloud That Models Reading for Deep Understanding

## Description
A teacher read-aloud involves a teacher fluently reading a text aloud to learners, intentionally modeling strategies of understanding that can be reinforced in shared, guided, and independent reading. The teacher engages learners at the edge of their speaking vocabulary, selecting texts a grade or two above their grade level. Read alouds should include think-aloud or interactive elements and focus intentionally on the meaning within the text, about the text, and beyond the text.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher-selected text, open-ended questions, and a plan for introducing vocabulary. The read-aloud requires preparation and practice, including selecting relevant texts and planning for discussion.
#### Constraints
- Can become a passive activity if learners are not actively engaged. Requires careful planning and selection of appropriate texts. If the discussion is not properly guided, it may not lead to deeper understanding.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Build vocabulary, develop understandings of story structures, support developing connections between print elements, encourage high levels of understanding, teach the reading process in a meaningful context, model fluency, motivate learners to read.

### Affordances
- Provides a model of fluent reading, helps learners practice reading with expression, supports comprehension monitoring, offers opportunities for individualized instruction, improves reading accuracy, and encourages peer feedback and correction.

### Personalization
- Select books and discussion topics that meet the learners' needs, cultural backgrounds, and experiences. Ask guiding questions tailored to the learners' needs, cultural backgrounds, and experiences. Practice reading aloud to build fluency.

## Impact
- Teachers model the ways that involvement with text engages us, and we give them energy for learning how reading works. By showing them how to search for meaning, we introduce strategies of understanding we can reinforce in shared, guided, and independent reading.
