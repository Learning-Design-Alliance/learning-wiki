Use real-life examples within constructivist English teaching, while preparing for students who are not ready for independent learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 causal), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's conclusion recommends attending to students in reform efforts and reports that real-life examples were well received in the constructivist classes, while cautioning that "some of them may not be prepared to learn independently" and that a few students were concerned about course standard, completeness of interpretation, and topic coverage. The strategy is to embed real-life examples in constructivist lessons and to scaffold students toward independent learning.

Claims¶

  • After constructivist teaching, overall difficulty in English fell from 58% to 42%, with comprehension difficulty falling to 44% [+W]
  • Pre-test difficulties in English were concentrated in comprehension (77%) and writing (70%), with overall difficulty of 58% [+W]
  • Students report low motivation for English writing due to lack of real-life application and formulaic compositions [+W]
  • Students want curriculum connected to real life, careers, and life skills, and report that the majority of the high school curriculum lacks real-world application [+W]
