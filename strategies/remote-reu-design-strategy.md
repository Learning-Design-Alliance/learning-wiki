# Design remote research programs with structured communication, explicit norms, shipped equipment, and dedicated workspaces to expand access to research experiences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's design recommendations for remote undergraduate research programs center on deliberate structuring of communication and expectations. It reports that "Weekly check-ins were vital to support mentees' research goals and help them develop more open communication with their mentors," that norms should be made explicit to avoid unproductive behavior, and that students need "specific resources such as a separate workspace, fast internet, and a personal computer" for remote work to succeed. Mentors shipped experiment kits home and used tools like Slack for rapid feedback. The authors suggest remote research programs could expand access to research experiences even after COVID-19 restrictions are lifted.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must provide structured regular meetings, explicit communication norms, and adequate home resources such as a separate workspace, fast internet, and a personal computer
#### Constraints
- The article notes virtual interactions may be more challenging for some students, and professional development activities that imparted knowledge did not support community

### Target Learners
- undergraduate research mentees in remote programs

### Target Learning Goals
