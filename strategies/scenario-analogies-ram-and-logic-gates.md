# Use extended real-life scenario analogies for RAM versus permanent storage and for logic gates

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article gives two extended verbal analogies. For storage, a school workbench represents RAM and a cupboard the hard drive: materials on the workbench allow quick work but space runs out and are cleared when leaving, showing that "all information in RAM will be removed when the computer is shut down and that RAM is a temporary storage area". For logic gates, AND and OR gates are analogized to a bus crossing bridges A and B to reach school, with codes 0 and 1 as bridge states. These detailed, narrated scenarios make unobservable mechanisms concrete.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Analogies should be tested by taking experts' opinions on their reliability before use
#### Constraints
- Students sometimes interpreted the analogies improperly, requiring instructor assistance to change wrong analogies

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students learning computer hardware and electronics basics

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguishing RAM from permanent storage; understanding AND and OR logic gate behavior
