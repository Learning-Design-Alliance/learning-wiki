# Use a modified Delphi expert-consensus process to identify, define, and weigh the steps of a procedure-specific assessment score

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (3 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy builds a scoring system by drafting steps and definitions from the literature, revising them in an expert pre-round, then running iterative anonymous surveys in which experts rate each substep's importance for patient outcomes with controlled feedback. Here, "A modified Delphi approach was chosen to identify and weigh key components of a minimally invasive stapled intestinal anastomosis with hand-sewn closure of the enterotomy", with consensus set at more than 80% agreement per category.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An identified international expert panel with judged clinical and research expertise and an online survey tool; consensus was defined as > 80% agreement on one category.
#### Constraints
- A limit of one pre-Delphi questionnaire and four Delphi iterations was set to prevent participant fatigue.

### Target Learners
- surgical education researchers and assessment developers creating procedure-specific scores

### Target Learning Goals
- development of reliable, consensus-based assessment scores for procedural technical skills
