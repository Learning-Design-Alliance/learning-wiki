# Make self-regulation support features optional and learner-initiated rather than imposed

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
From participants' negative reactions to enforced self-control and sharing features, the article recommends that functionality in personal learning contexts "should remain optional and user-initiated". Although opt-out mechanisms existed, they were initially enforced and were received negatively, with participants expressing concerns about privacy and autonomy. The authors conclude that self-regulation cannot be effectively supported through imposed constraints, but rather through carefully designed, learner-driven mechanisms, and plan to move self-control and sharing features into a dashboard.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Self-control and sharing functionality must be opt-in and user-initiated, with genuine control over privacy and autonomy
#### Constraints
- The article states this recommendation derives from a small-sample (n = 15), short-term study; imposed constraints were received negatively in this trial

### Target Learners
- informal programming learners using personal self-regulation tools

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining self-regulation without undermining learner autonomy
