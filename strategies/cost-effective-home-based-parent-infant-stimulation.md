# Cost-effective, home-based parent-delivered early stimulation for infants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that a cost-effective intervention programme could enable parents to provide educational stimulation for their infants in a home environment, since intervention at such an early age does not form part of the formal education system. It suggests that support from private and non-government organisations to parents and primary caregivers would imply a potentially broader implementation of the Numbers in Nappies programme, which it describes as intellectually and economically accessible across socioeconomic sectors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Parents or primary caregivers undergo training in the programme and deliver the daily sessions themselves
#### Constraints
- The study recommends a more structured evaluation of the implementation of the programme by the parents, and notes results should not be generalised without replication on a larger and more representative sample

### Target Learners
- infants aged 3–12 months in home settings

### Target Learning Goals
- cognitive processing potential
- social–emotional functioning
