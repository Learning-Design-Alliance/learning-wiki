# Assess curricular transformation with an array of instruments covering both performance and student perception

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that instructors and administrators examine the instruments they use to assess student opinion, because traditional end-of-course evaluations may not adequately assess the student-centered nature of a course or correlate with learning gains. During the IBIS transformation the authors paired pre/post concept assessments with SALG surveys, attitudinal Likert items, and sentiment analysis of open-ended responses to gauge both learning and buy-in. This array revealed increased buy-in that performance measures alone would have missed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruments targeting different facets of the classroom experience, including measures of student opinion, self-efficacy, and perceived learning gains
#### Constraints
- The article notes end-of-course evaluations may not accurately correlate with student learning gains, potentially increasing negative perceptions of curricular changes

### Target Learners
- undergraduates in transformed introductory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating curricular effectiveness in terms of both student performance and student buy-in
