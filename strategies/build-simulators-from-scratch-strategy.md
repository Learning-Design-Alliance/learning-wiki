# Have students build simulators from scratch instead of using established frameworks that abstract away implementation details

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course's core teaching move is constructivist: rather than demonstrating algorithms with IBM Quantum Learning, Qiskit, or Cirq, which the authors say are "good for demonstrating quantum algorithms" but "often abstract away implementation details", students implement the simulation methods themselves. The authors state that "By implementing the simulation methods themselves, students develop a more in-depth understanding of fundamental concepts in quantum computing, including superposition, entanglement, and the effects of noise on quantum systems." Supervisors are present throughout and students are encouraged to collaborate.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need prior grounding from an introductory lecture; supervisors must be present to provide guidance and expert knowledge.
#### Constraints
- The approach is positioned as building on, not replacing, an introductory lecture; it targets students who already have the basics.

### Target Learners
- graduate computer science students

### Target Learning Goals
- deepened understanding of quantum computing fundamentals
- research readiness in quantum computing
