# Use nonresponse to generic tier-two preschool behavioral intervention as a trigger for formal ADHD diagnostic referral and more intensive tier-three intervention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that preschool teachers watch for children with externalizing behaviors who show "an overlay of especially inattentive, impulsive, or hyperactive behaviors" and who "may not always respond even to the best of such generic tier-two behavioral interventions". Such nonresponders "may indeed necessitate referral for a formal diagnostic workup for ADHD, possibly leading to more intensive tier-three intervention involving enhanced parent collaboration and classroom management strategies specific to ADHD". The strategy positions a generic tier-two program such as PFS as both an effective first step for most children and a source of "more compelling evidence of further need for more intensive and focused interdisciplinary treatment for nonresponders".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A screened, implemented tier-two behavioral intervention such as PFS with fidelity monitoring so that nonresponse is interpretable
- Access to qualified professionals for formal ADHD diagnostic workup and, where indicated, multimodal treatment including possible medication management
#### Constraints
- The article states it cannot yet recommend PFS as either a comprehensive or specific treatment for preschool ADHD, pending a priori randomized clinical trials

### Target Learners
- preschool children with externalizing behaviors who do not respond to tier-two intervention

### Target Learning Goals
- reduction of externalizing and ADHD-related problem behaviors
- appropriate escalation to intensive, diagnosis-specific intervention
