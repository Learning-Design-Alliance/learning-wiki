Tailor reward timing to learners' existing interest: post-learning unexpected rewards for high-interest material, promised performance-linked rewards for low-interest material¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 7 studies (3 review, 2 causal, 2 quant-synthesis), q2–q4 · 2 of 7 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article derives a practical approach from its findings: "when students already possess a high level of interest in a course, pre-learning extrinsic rewards may be unnecessary. Instead, unexpected rewards given after learning could help further improve their performance." Conversely, for students lacking intrinsic motivation, promising performance-linked rewards before learning could boost engagement. The strategy exploits the temporal dynamics of reward and interest rather than adding rewards indiscriminately.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Knowledge of learners' current interest level in the material to select before- versus after-learning reward timing

Constraints¶

  • Effects were demonstrated with short film clips in controlled memory experiments and emerged only at delayed tests, not immediately

Target Learners¶

  • students
  • adult learners in workplaces

Target Learning Goals¶

  • enhancing memory and performance through motivationally tailored reward scheduling

Affordances¶

Claims¶

  • Rewards attenuated forgetting for low-interest clips when cued before stimuli but for high-interest clips when cued after, per the forgetting-rate analysis [+W]
  • When reward cues follow stimuli, rewards improve memory for high-interest clips at 1 day, increasing the interest effect (additive effect) [+W]
  • When reward cues precede stimuli, rewards improve memory for low-interest clips at 1 day, decreasing the interest effect (undermining effect) [+W]
  • The timing of reward cues moderates the reward-by-interest interaction on memory over time (four-way interaction), with effects emerging only after consolidation [+W]
  • Rewards decreased the interest effect on memory when cued before clips but increased it when cued after clips, at 1 day but not 10 minutes [+W]
  • Expected tangible rewards reduce later free-choice engagement with a rewarded task, while verbal praise does not [+W]
  • Extrinsic rewards deplete intrinsic motivation for already-motivated activities once the rewards are removed [+W]
