# Cultivate Multiple Ways of Knowing and Making Meaning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 14 studies (5 causal, 4 quant-synthesis, 4 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q4` · 3 of 14 report an effect size

## Description
This strategy designs learning so that content can be accessed, understood, and expressed through multiple modalities, cultural frames, and forms of representation. Learners encounter material through varied means (text, visual, oral, embodied, narrative) and demonstrate understanding through varied means (writing, art, performance, discussion, building). The goal is an inclusive environment where diverse backgrounds are treated as assets and expression is not limited to a single sanctioned format.

## Design Implications

Multiple representations strengthen learning by giving learners redundant, complementary codes for the same idea [Presenting words and corresponding pictures improves recall over words alone.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-learning.md) [+S], and examining the same concept through multiple cases and perspectives builds flexible, transferable knowledge [Examining multiple varied cases builds flexible knowledge structures.](../claims/cognitive-flexibility-theory-multiple-cases.md) [+M]. Offering meaningful choices in how to engage and express understanding supports autonomy and intrinsic motivation [Supporting learner autonomy increases intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]. Critically, this strategy is about multiple *modalities and perspectives* — not matching instruction to a diagnosed "learning style," which has been repeatedly discredited [Teaching to matched learning styles does not improve outcomes.](../claims/learning-styles-matching-does-not-improve-learning.md) [-S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content represented in at least two modalities with a clear instructional purpose, not decoration ([Dual Coding](../theories/dual-coding-theory.md))
- Authentic materials and examples drawn from learners' cultural contexts, treated as objects of study rather than tokens
- Multiple assessment/expression options that hold constant the learning goal while varying the format ([Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md))
- A psychologically safe climate for sharing personal and cultural perspectives ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
- Source materials and examples drawn from more than one cultural, disciplinary, or representational tradition
- Instructor knowledge of learners' communities and funds of knowledge, gathered rather than assumed

#### Constraints
- Matching instruction to self-reported "learning styles" does not improve achievement and wastes design effort [Teaching to matched learning styles does not improve outcomes.](../claims/learning-styles-matching-does-not-improve-learning.md) [-S] — offer multiple representations, but do not sort learners by style
- Unrestricted choice can overload novices, who lack the knowledge to select productively; guided or constrained options work better for them [~M]
- Superficial inclusion of cultural examples ("heroes and holidays") can stereotype rather than affirm; deep integration of perspectives requires substantive redesign [~M]
- Multiple formats multiply design, facilitation, and assessment workload; rubrics must assess the shared learning goal, not production polish, or creative options become inequitable [~M]
- Superficial "multicultural add-ons" (holidays, food, flags) without epistemic substance do not improve learning and can read as tokenism [-W]
- Openly inviting personal perspectives without structure can expose learners to stereotype threat or discomfort in mixed-status classrooms [~M]
- Treating "multiple ways of knowing" as license to abandon accuracy or evidence standards degrades conceptual learning [-M]
- Matching instruction to self-reported "learning styles" does not improve outcomes and wastes design effort [X]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Representation-side:** teach one concept through text + diagram + demonstration + narrative ([Analogies](../elements/analogies.md), [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md), [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md))
- **Expression-side:** fixed goal, variable product — essay, podcast, concept map, performance, model
- **Perspective-side:** analyze the same event or problem through multiple cultural or disciplinary lenses
- **Participation-side:** vary between individual, pair, and communal modes of knowing (reflection, dialogue, [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md))
- Curriculum-level: alternate case sources, authors, and knowledge traditions across a unit
- Activity-level: offer parallel pathways (narrative, visual, manipulative, dialogic) into the same concept
- Assessment-level: allow multiple demonstration formats against a common rubric
- Community-level: invite elders, practitioners, or community experts as co-teachers of local knowledge

### Target Learners
- Multilingual learners, who benefit from complementary verbal and visual representations [Presenting words and corresponding pictures improves recall over words alone.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-learning.md) [+S]
- Learners from cultural backgrounds underrepresented in canonical curricula, who engage more deeply when materials connect to community knowledge [~M]
- Novices, who need constrained choice rather than open-ended option sets [~M]
- All learners benefit from multiple representations; none benefit from style-matched instruction [Teaching to matched learning styles does not improve outcomes.](../claims/learning-styles-matching-does-not-improve-learning.md) [-S]
- Learners from cultural or linguistic backgrounds underrepresented in standard curricula, who benefit when their funds of knowledge are treated as instructional assets [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding that transfers across contexts [Examining multiple varied cases builds flexible knowledge structures.](../claims/cognitive-flexibility-theory-multiple-cases.md) [+M]
- Perspective-taking and empathy [Building empathy improves intergroup attitudes.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M]
- Creative and communicative expression
- Retention of core content through dual verbal/visual encoding [Presenting words and corresponding pictures improves recall over words alone.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-learning.md) [+S]
- Epistemic flexibility: recognizing that disciplines and cultures have different valid standards for warranting claims

### Instructions
1. Identify the non-negotiable learning goal, then design at least two representations of it (text + visual, narrative + data, concrete + abstract) ([Analogies](../elements/analogies.md), [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md))
2. Audit examples and materials for whose knowledge and experience they assume; add authentic perspectives from the learners' communities
3. Offer a bounded set of expression options for the same goal ([Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md)), with a shared rubric focused on the learning goal
4. Open with identity- and experience-connecting routines ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), [Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md)) so learners' ways of knowing enter the room
5. Use structured dialogue to surface multiple interpretations ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md))
6. Assess against the goal, not the format; provide [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md) so expression options remain genuinely accessible

## Description
This strategy designs learning so that knowledge can be built, expressed, and validated through multiple epistemic channels — narrative, demonstration, dialogue, embodied practice, visual representation, and cultural tradition — rather than through a single privileged mode. It draws on culturally responsive pedagogy and [Constructivism](../theories/constructivism.md): learners arrive with different funds of knowledge, and instruction treats those as assets to build on rather than deficits to overcome. Importantly, "multiple ways of knowing" refers to varied *sources and modes of knowledge construction*, not to the discredited notion that individuals have fixed learning styles that instruction should match [X].

## Design Implications

Presenting concepts through multiple representations and inviting learners to connect content to their own cultural and experiential knowledge deepens understanding and supports transfer [Multiple cases and representations across contexts support flexible knowledge transfer.](../claims/cognitive-flexibility-theory-multiple-cases.md) [+M]. ### Instructions
1. Audit the unit: identify whose knowledge, examples, and modes of expression are represented, and where a single tradition dominates.
2. Open with [Activation](../elements/activation.md) — elicit learners' prior experiences and community knowledge relevant to the topic [Activation improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M].
3. Present the core concept through at least two representational modes (e.g., [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md), [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md), visual models, [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md)) [Dual coding improves recall.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-learning.md) [+M].
4. Structure [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or [Argumentation](../elements/argumentation.md) so learners compare how different traditions or disciplines frame the same question.
5. Offer [Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md) or equivalent options for how learners demonstrate mastery, assessed against a shared rubric.
6. Close with [Articulation](../elements/articulation.md) — learners name what their own perspective contributed and what they learned from others'.
