# Teach learners two loud task-focusing questions to pose when feeling overwhelmed by stress in emergencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A brief (10-minute) stress-coping instruction in which learners are told that feeling overwhelmed can impair decision-making and are taught to ask two questions aloud: "what is the patient's condition?" and "what immediate action is needed?". The strategy aims to redirect attention to task priorities and break a stress-performance vicious cycle. In the trial, 74% of intervention participants actually posed the two questions aloud during the scenario.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A short instruction explaining that perceived stress may interfere with decision-making and that focusing on the situation's basic conditions and immediate actions can counter feeling overwhelmed
#### Constraints
- The intervention reduced perceived stress but did not significantly improve performance; the authors state it may not have been intense enough and recommend studying more intense interventions

### Target Learners
- medical students with no prior CPR training in their curriculum

### Target Learning Goals
- managing acute stress during emergency resuscitation
- maintaining task focus under pressure
