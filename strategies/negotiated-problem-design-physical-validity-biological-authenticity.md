# Negotiate interdisciplinary problems jointly, seeking both physical validity and biological authenticity, and make modeling assumptions explicit.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' recommended practice is to design physics problems for biology students through extended negotiation between disciplines, "seeking both physical validity and biological authenticity". The negotiated worm scaling problem retained physics-style representational translation tasks (numbers, graphs, equations) while being reworked "to emphasize the biological realities of the situation", demonstrating value for phylogenetic development of gills and lungs, and made "the modeling assumptions that go into the analysis" explicit, which both disciplines valued.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Extended negotiation between physicists and biologists over problem content and framing
- Attention to both the physics tasks and the biological insight the problem yields
#### Constraints
- The authors note each interdisciplinary exploration is bound to be unique to the individuals and institutions involved

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life-science majors and pre-medical students in physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- use general-purpose physics tools (scaling, dimensional analysis, multiple representations) to gain deeper understanding of biological systems

- Disciplinary Worldview Heuristics Biology Physics
