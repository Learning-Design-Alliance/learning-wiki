# Multiple Writing Surfaces

## Description
Multiple writing surfaces involve providing learners with a variety of spaces where they can write and express their ideas, such as walls, tables, or portable whiteboards. This strategy aims to promote active engagement, collaboration, and creative thinking by making writing more accessible and flexible. Learners can use these surfaces to brainstorm, solve problems, take notes, or share ideas with peers. This approach can enhance motivation and cater to different learning styles by offering diverse ways to interact with the material.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires writable surfaces (e.g., whiteboards, painted walls, tables) and writing tools (e.g., dry erase markers, chalk). May need classroom redesign or additional materials.
#### Constraints
- Requires careful planning to manage space and resources. May be disruptive if not implemented with clear guidelines. Potential for misuse or defacement of surfaces.

### Affordances
- Enhances engagement by providing diverse and accessible writing spaces. Supports collaboration by allowing learners to work together on shared surfaces. Fosters creative thinking by encouraging free-form expression. Improves memory retention through visual and kinesthetic learning.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to various subject areas and learning environments. Surfaces can be customized with prompts, templates, or graphic organizers. Can be integrated with digital tools for blended learning experiences.

## Examples
- An elementary school used smart wall paint to allow students to write on the walls and tables with dry erase markers. Schools equip new classrooms with Apple TV and multiple writing surfaces to give students and teachers the flexibility to move around the room and write anywhere on the walls. Post-it Super Sticky Dry Erase Surface allows students and teachers to quickly transform any surface into a whiteboard.
