# Replicate the same public astronomy survey at venues with lower science interest, income, and degree attainment, and expand degree categories

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' stated future-work recommendation is to collect more surveys in the exact same manner to increase the statistics, and to use the exact same survey at a different location where visitors have lower science interest, lower household income, and fewer degree holders. They also recommend editing future surveys to include individuals who have some college or other types of degrees, since the original instrument lacked those categories.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The same six-item instrument administered in the same manner so results are comparable across venues
#### Constraints
- The original sample's demographics (58% very interested in science, 40% of visitors with household income over $100,000) limit generalization of the current percentages to the broader public

### Target Learners
- Adults from the general public, including those with some college or non-traditional higher degrees

### Target Learning Goals
- Baseline measurement of public astronomy literacy to inform strategies that build on the public's current knowledge base
