# Physical Education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Physical Education (PE) is a curricular domain and instructional strategy in which learners acquire motor skills, tactical understanding, and health-related knowledge through structured movement tasks. Effective PE sequences [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md), guided [Practice](../elements/practice.md), and [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) within progressively modified game or performance contexts, rather than relying on drill alone.

## Design Implications

Motor skill acquisition follows the same cognitive principles as other domains: novices benefit from observing a model before attempting performance, and from practice conditions that manage working-memory load [Bandura's social learning framework positions observation of a model as the primary route to motor skill acquisition.](../theories/social-learning-theory.md) [+S]. Because early skill attempts consume working memory on movement mechanics, breaking complex skills into parts reduces load for beginners [Part-task practice reduces load for novices.](../claims/part-task-practice-reduces-load-for-novices.md) [+M]. Feedback should target technique and strategy rather than scores or personal traits [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adequate, safe activity space and equipment scaled to learners' bodies and skill levels
- A clear skill model for [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md), whether live instructor modeling or video
- High rates of active practice time — instructional designs should maximize motor engagement and minimize waiting in lines or listening to extended talk
- A [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) routine that names the specific movement adjustment needed, delivered close in time to the attempt

#### Constraints
- Extended verbal instruction during activity time reduces practice minutes and depresses skill gains; explanations should be brief and delivered during natural pauses [~M]
- Whole-skill practice of complex, high-load skills (e.g., a gymnastics sequence) can overwhelm novices; unmanaged, it produces error-ridden repetitions that consolidate poor technique [-M]
- Fitness-only or lecture-only formats that exclude skill practice fail to build competence, and low perceived competence undermines continued participation [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [-M]
- Highly competitive elimination formats remove practice opportunities precisely for the lowest-skilled learners who need them most [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Sport Education** (Siedentop): seasons-long team affiliation with student roles (captain, referee, coach) embedding skill practice in authentic competition
- **Teaching Games for Understanding (TGfU)**: tactical problems first, skill development as needed to solve them — reverses the technique-first sequence
- **Mastery/individualized formats**: skill stations with [Adaptive Difficulty](../elements/adaptive-difficulty.md) so learners progress on criteria, not time
- **Video-based modeling**: slow-motion and expert-novice contrast footage for skills too fast or complex to observe live

### Target Learners
- Novices of all ages, who need observation plus part-task practice before whole-skill performance [Part-task practice reduces load for novices.](../claims/part-task-practice-reduces-load-for-novices.md) [+M]
- Low-skilled or low-confidence learners, who benefit most from process-focused goals and feedback rather than outcome comparisons [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]
- Advanced performers, who profit more from variable, game-like, whole-task practice than from further part drills [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Motor skill acquisition: fundamental movement skills and sport-specific techniques
- Tactical understanding: decision-making within game structures
- Physical literacy and lifelong activity habits: confidence, competence, and motivation to be active

### Instructions
1. Open with a brief, activating warm-up that embeds the lesson's movement theme ([Activation](../elements/activation.md)).
2. [Demonstrate](../elements/demonstration.md) the target skill with a live or video model, highlighting one or two critical features — not the whole technique at once ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md)).
3. Set up [Practice](../elements/practice.md) as part-task drills or simplified games, beginning with a manageable version of the whole skill [Part-task practice reduces load for novices.](../claims/part-task-practice-reduces-load-for-novices.md) [+M].
4. Circulate and deliver process-level [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md): name the movement error, show the correction, let the learner retry immediately [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
5. [Scaffold](../elements/scaffolding.md) toward full game or performance conditions, progressively removing constraints as competence grows [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].
6. Close with reflection on tactics and effort rather than scores, reinforcing process goals [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M].

## Examples
- **Sport Education** (Siedentop): a 12–18 lesson "season" in which teams practice, officiate, and coach each other, culminating in a tournament; reviews report improved skill and engagement, with effects strongest for team-sport units [~M]
- **TGfU** (Bunker & Thorpe): a volleyball unit begins with a modified 3-vs-3 game exposing the tactical problem of court coverage; overhead passing is then taught as the solution
- **[SHAPE America](https://www.shapeamerica.org)** standards-based curricula: grade-level outcomes specifying skill competencies (e.g., dribbling with control in dynamic contexts by grade 5) that anchor station-based mastery practice
