# Training programs for distributed rural emergency teams should build role clarity, team cohesion, and technology fluency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that training for distributed teams focus on three areas. First, "Strengthening role clarity: Training should emphasize task delegation strategies and well-defined roles". Second, building cohesion by "fostering open and transparent communication, collaborative decision making, and opportunities for team members to build shared mental models, relationships, and trust". Third, guiding effective technology use to minimize communication delays. These recommendations respond to the co-located teams' advantage in timeliness and adaptability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teams are ad hoc and short-term, so the authors state they "would benefit from introductory sessions, level setting, and more specific and focused training"
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from one simulation study in which only 29% of participants had prior distributed-team experience, which the authors flag as possibly affecting performance and generalizability

### Target Learners
- on-site rural cottage hospital staff (nurses, assistant nurses) and remote on-call general physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- role clarity and delegation
- distributed communication and shared mental models
- effective telemedicine technology use
