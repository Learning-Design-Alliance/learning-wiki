# Use SVVR as a standardized, repeatable preparation strategy for nurses' delirium care education, with attention to implementation resources

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend SVVR as a workforce development and continuing education strategy: it "may address this challenge by providing standardized, repeatable, and realistic clinical scenarios without involving actual patients", particularly for newly graduated nurses. They advise nurse managers to consider repeated training, unit-level reminders, and workflow integration for attitudes, and note implementation requires consideration of equipment availability, costs, technical support, and staff familiarity with virtual reality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Equipment availability, initial development and maintenance costs, technical support, and staff familiarity with virtual reality technology
#### Constraints
- Conventional video-based education requires fewer technological resources and may be easier to implement at scale
- Future studies should evaluate implementation outcomes, resource requirements, and cost-effectiveness

### Target Learners
- Newly graduated nurses
- Registered nurses in ICUs, general wards, and postanesthesia recovery rooms

### Target Learning Goals
- Delirium early recognition and assessment skills
- Continuing education and workforce development in delirium care
