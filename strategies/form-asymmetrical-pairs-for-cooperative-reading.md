# Form asymmetrical pairs and build pair work into reading instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that teachers form asymmetrical pairs instead of symmetrical pairs, implement scaffolding regularly in classroom settings, and create conversational contexts that extend one another's learning. They state: "It is suggested that teachers form AS pairs instead of S pairs." Materials developers should include substantial pair work in textbooks, and curricula should emphasize interaction between learners and tasks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher who introduces the purpose of scaffolding, monitors pair interaction, and provides assistance to pairs needing more support
#### Constraints
- Findings come from one institute in Mashhad and cannot be safely generalized to other situations, per the authors' stated limitations

### Target Learners
- Intermediate EFL learners in pairs of differing proficiency

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension and peer-mediated learning strategies
