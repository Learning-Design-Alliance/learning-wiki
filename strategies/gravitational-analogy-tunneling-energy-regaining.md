# Use a gravitational analogy of a ball rolling over a hill to resolve student confusion about how a tunneling electron regains kinetic energy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
When transformed-course students understood that the electron loses kinetic energy entering the air gap but could not explain how it regains kinetic energy reentering the second wire, the authors found that an analogy of "a ball rolling over a hill, in which the kinetic energy is lost as the ball rolls up the hill and regained as the ball rolls down the hill, quickly resolves this confusion." They argue it is preferable to address the strengths and limitations of the analogy directly rather than avoid its use, since it is already inherent in phrases such as "potential step" and "potential barrier."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A physical context in which the electron visibly loses and regains kinetic energy, such as the electron-in-a-wire with an air-gap barrier example used in the course.
#### Constraints
- Some researchers avoid gravitational analogies for fear they may suggest tunneling involves a particle traveling through a physical barrier and exacerbate the belief that energy is dissipated; the authors report seeing no evidence of such a link.

### Target Learners
- students in transformed modern physics courses for engineering majors

### Target Learning Goals
- explaining energy conservation in quantum tunneling
- understanding how an electron regains kinetic energy after tunneling through a barrier
