# Gamification

## Description
Gamification is the integration of game elements like point systems, leaderboards, badges, or other elements related to games into “conventional” learning activities in order to increase engagement and motivation. Examples include awarding badges for participation in online discussion forums or earning points for completing tasks, which can be tracked via digital gradebooks or shared online.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration of digital tools (e.g., learning management systems, Google Drive). Design of a point or badge system aligned with course objectives. Ongoing tracking of student progress.
#### Constraints
- Can be demotivating for students at the bottom of leaderboards. May not lead to improved learning outcomes if not thoughtfully integrated. Over-emphasis on extrinsic rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Increase engagement, sustain motivation, reward task completion, track progress, foster comradery or competition

### Affordances
- Leverages learners' familiarity with game mechanics. Provides immediate feedback through points and badges. Fosters motivation through competition and reward systems.

### Personalization
- Activities can be tailored to individual learning preferences by offering choices in how to earn points. Point systems can be supplemented by academic rewards to suit different needs.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track student participation and engagement with gamified activities. Compare learning outcomes between gamified and non-gamified courses. Monitor student feedback regarding motivation and engagement.

## Impact
- Research shows gamification can increase student engagement and motivation. Studies indicate that leaderboards can increase time-on-task and academic performance, although caution is needed to avoid discouraging low-performing students.
