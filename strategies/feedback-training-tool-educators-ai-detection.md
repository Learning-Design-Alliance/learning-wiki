# Adapt the feedback-based detection experiment into a training tool for educators

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends turning its experimental software into practice training: learners judge side-by-side pairs of human and AI-generated texts, indicate confidence, and receive immediate correctness feedback identifying which text was written by whom. The authors state that "our experimental software can be easily adapted into a practical tool for educators, enabling them to assess and enhance their ability to recognize AI-generated content." The experiment showed this feedback loop improves both accuracy and confidence calibration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A corpus of topically and stylistically comparable human and AI-generated text pairs, as generated from Koditex texts with GPT-4o
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a Czech-language experiment with GPT-4o-generated texts; the article notes English dominates language-model training data, making other languages potentially different

### Target Learners
- educators
- adult learners

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguishing AI-generated from human-written texts
- calibrating confidence in authorship judgments
