# Weekly pre-lab context-rich problem-solving exercise with metacognitive self-diagnosis rubric

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
In place of recitations, the instructor used the first 50-60 minutes of lab for an exercise presenting "a context-rich problem that was conceptually connected to the lab experiment that would follow," worked for about 45 minutes, ideally in coordinated groups. Students then reviewed the solution via Socratic dialogue and completed a self-diagnosis rubric identifying strengths and weaknesses. Course points rewarded active participation rather than completion to avoid grade-dependence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- instructor availability during the exercise to keep all group members working toward a solution
- measures to prevent one student dominating group workload, e.g. assigned roles or individual work with consultation allowed
#### Constraints
- group work often reflected individual efforts with some students helping others as needed

### Target Learners
- introductory algebra-based physics students, primarily life science majors

### Target Learning Goals
- problem-solving framework mastery
- metacognitive self-diagnosis
- connection of physics topics to lab experiments
