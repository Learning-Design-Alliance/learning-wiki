Plan for multiyear implementation support because outcome trajectories are U-shaped, with short-term declines preceding long-term gains¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report warns that in some restorative implementation studies, outcomes over time are U-shaped, so institutions should secure funding structured to support multiple years of implementation and communicate that funding is not tied to near-term results. It states that outcomes "are 'U-shaped,' meaning there are short-term declines followed by long-term gains." It also recommends giving caregivers firsthand experience with restorative practices to mitigate apprehension and garner long-term support.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Multiyear investments in implementation support, with resources and ongoing training to sustain educators' restorative practices

Constraints¶

  • Short-term declines in outcomes such as academic performance may occur before long-term gains, which can tempt staff to abandon the practices

Target Learners¶

  • school and district leaders
  • caregivers and community members

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustained restorative practice implementation

Related Strategies¶

  • Invest in supportive-community strategies—mental health staffing, SEL, restorative practices, and relationship-centered structures—rather than expanded physical security
