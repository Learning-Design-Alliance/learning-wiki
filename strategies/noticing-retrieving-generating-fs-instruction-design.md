# Design explicit FS instruction around noticing, retrieving, and generating psychological conditions with in-class presentation, practice, and after-class reinforcement.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The five-week intervention taught 10 FSs per week through presentation of meaning, usage, and functions with example sentences, followed by in-class gap-filling, sentence writing, and paragraph writing activities mapped to "noticing, retrieving, and generating" processes. After-class supplementary materials on a course management system and homework paragraphs (70-100 words with 3-5 taught FSs) created additional encounters and productive use of the target FSs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-class time for presentation and practice of target FSs each week
- A course management system or similar channel for distributing supplementary materials and collecting homework
#### Constraints
- In-class teaching activities were regarded as more useful than after-class practice by participants, as the latter was designed as supplementary

### Target Learners
- EFL college students
- English majors in academic writing courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic formulaic sequence knowledge
- Academic writing proficiency
