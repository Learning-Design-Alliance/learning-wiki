# Rapid feedback system: de-identified student responses emailed to the resident within 3 days of each teaching session

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
After each Friday teaching session, the Director of Medical Student Education emails students a four-question free-text feedback form, with responses due by Sunday evening. The article states: "The DMSE de-identifies all student responses and emails them in a group format back to the resident within a 3 day time period, i.e. no later than 5 PM on Monday." The timing is designed so residents can adjust the next week's session. Students answer about strengths, one thing to do differently, confusions, and two things learned.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A program structure with sequential weekly teaching sessions so feedback can inform the next session
- A designated administrator (the DMSE) to collect, de-identify, and forward student responses
- Four specific free-text questions posed to student learners
#### Constraints
- Students were not formally trained to give feedback
- Students knew responses would be de-identified and grouped, but one resident commented that students may have lost anonymity, possibly making critical feedback less likely

### Target Learners
- PGY-2 pediatrics residents acting as teachers
- third-year medical students providing feedback

### Target Learning Goals
- residents' development of small-group teaching skills
