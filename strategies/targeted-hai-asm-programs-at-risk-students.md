# Provide at-risk students with targeted programs combining varying levels of human–animal interaction and academic stress management content, matched to the targeted academic outcome

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the trial's finding that different HAI dosages benefited different outcomes — combined HAI and content for WILL and SELFREGULATION, HAI-only for delayed SKILL gains — the article recommends that "University administrators should thus consider providing at-risk students with targeted programs with varying levels of HAI and ASM content, depending on academic outcomes targeted." This positions animal-assisted programming as a targeted rather than purely universal prevention tool.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs require registered therapy handler-dog teams, trained facilitation staff, and structured four-week sessions with consistent delivery across conditions
#### Constraints
- Benefits were demonstrated only for at-risk students; typical students showed no significant improvements, possibly due to higher baseline study skills and less impairment in mediating domains

### Target Learners
- at-risk college students with higher-than-average depression, anxiety, worry, or perceived stress

### Target Learning Goals
- learning and study strategies (motivation, attitudes, self-regulation, study skills)

### Affordances
- Gee Hai Direct Indirect Learning Framework

## Claims

- [At-risk college students receiving equal combinations of human–animal interaction and content show higher SELFREGULATION scores than at-risk students receiving content only, at posttest and follow-up, while HAI-only shows no effect](../claims/hai-enhanced-improves-selfregulation-at-risk.md) [+W]
- [At-risk college students receiving equal combinations of human–animal interaction and academic stress management content show higher WILL (anxiety, attitude, motivation) scores than at-risk students receiving content only, at posttest and six-week follow-up](../claims/hai-enhanced-improves-will-at-risk-students.md) [+W]
- [Human–animal interaction effects on SKILL for at-risk students emerge only at six-week follow-up in the HAI-only condition, with no significant effects immediately posttest](../claims/hai-only-improves-skill-at-followup-at-risk.md) [+W]
- [At-risk college students receiving human–animal interaction only (100% HAI, no content) also show higher WILL scores than at-risk students receiving academic stress management content only, at posttest and follow-up](../claims/hai-only-improves-will-at-risk-students.md) [+W]
