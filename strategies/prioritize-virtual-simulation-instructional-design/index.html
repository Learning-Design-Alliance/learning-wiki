Prioritize virtual simulation methods in teachers' instructional design, especially for practical worship topics¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on its findings, the article recommends that "virtual simulation methods should be given priority in the design process of teaching teachers", and specifically suggests that Islamic Education teachers be provided with teaching materials using virtual simulation mode, especially for the topic of Worship, because worship is a practical practice that needs to be simulated in the classroom so students can understand and practice it in real life. Materials should also account for students' cognitive levels across high, medium and low achievement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need ICT skills and training on the use of web-based virtual simulation teaching materials before classroom implementation

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from one quasi-experiment on a single topic (pilgrimage) in two Malaysian schools; the authors call for larger samples and other topics before generalizing

Target Learners¶

  • Malaysian secondary school students, particularly Islamic Education students at high and moderate achievement levels

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding and performing practical worship practices such as the Hajj pilgrimage

Claims¶

  • High-achievement students taught with web-based virtual simulation significantly outperformed high-achievement controls on the pilgrimage topic [+W]
  • Web-based multimedia virtual simulation embedded in PowerPoint teaching increases Form Four students' achievement on the Islamic Studies pilgrimage topic compared with PowerPoint teaching without simulation [+W]
  • Moderate-achievement students taught with web-based virtual simulation significantly outperformed moderate-achievement controls on the pilgrimage topic [+W]
  • The authors interpret web-based virtual simulation as shifting the pilgrimage lesson from teacher-centred to student-centred learning consistent with constructivist learning theory [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use virtual simulations to enhance cognitive learning while prioritizing in-person laboratory sessions for psychomotor and technical skills
