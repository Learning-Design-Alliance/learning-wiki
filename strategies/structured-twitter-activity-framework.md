# Organize Twitter-mediated activities with weekly structured questions, peer-commenting requirements and tutor feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article implements and evaluates a framework of Twitter-mediated educational activities in which students answer weekly laboratory questions via the course hashtag, comment on at least two peers' answers, and submit course questions, while tutors post information and give feedback. The authors argue that "proper Twitter mediated activities could enhance the learning experience." The framework follows the design proposed by Junco, Elavsky and Heiberger (2013), whose organized, compulsory use showed benefits that optional, less organized use did not.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need access to Twitter and a course hashtag; weekly questions are posted and students have 3 days to answer with up to two tweets per question, comment on at least two peers, and submit at least two course questions per semester.
#### Constraints
- Participation in the reported studies was optional and the most active students received up to 1.5 bonus grade points, which the authors state should be controlled in future work; results are reported as not generalizable.

### Target Learners
- higher education students

### Target Learning Goals
- course engagement and participation
- communication with peers and tutor
- familiarity with social networks for learning

## Claims

- [Students' Twitter activity concentrated in comments on peers' tweets, indicating preference for peer interaction over tutor-directed questions](../claims/peer-commenting-dominates-twitter-activity.md) [+W]
- [In all three case studies, students who participated in the Twitter activities received higher laboratory and final grades than students who did not participate](../claims/twitter-participants-higher-lab-grades.md) [+W]
- [Twitter activity was related to the final grade only in the first study (r=.49), with no significant relationship in the second and third studies](../claims/twitter-activity-final-grade-first-study.md) [~W]
- [Students' attitudes toward learning via ICT shifted after the activities in the first study and toward learning via social media in the second, with no shifts in the third study](../claims/attitude-shifts-social-media-ict.md) [~W]
