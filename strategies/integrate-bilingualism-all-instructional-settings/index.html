Integrate bilingualism into all instructional settings through districtwide professional learning and in-service coaching¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

This recommendation directs education leaders to promote the use of primary languages in instructional settings beyond formal bilingual programs, "through districtwide professional learning and in-service coaching." It rests on the report's finding that instructional coaches already employed by most LEAs can help all teachers integrate home language and English language development into their practice. A foundational step the report describes is making space for the bilingualism students already have as an asset for learning and sense-making, even if universal bilingualism is not the initial goal.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Existing instructional coaches and program coordinators, supported by leadership, to drive consistent expansion of bilingual opportunities

Constraints¶

  • Survey and interview samples cannot be assumed to reflect the region overall, and the report notes these staffing roles are vulnerable to funding reductions

Target Learners¶

  • English Learner students across all instructional models, bilingual or not

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Home language use in academic instruction and English language development

Related Strategies¶

  • School and district leaders' recommendations for implementing effective classroom coaching programs
  • Invest in program coordination, instructional coaching, and bilingual paraprofessional career pathways to improve English Learner program design
  • Teach in students' home language in primary, assess learning levels and differentiate instruction, and align teacher training with language-of-instruction policy
