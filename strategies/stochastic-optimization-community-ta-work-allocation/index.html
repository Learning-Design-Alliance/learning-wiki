Apply stochastic optimization to divide and schedule community teaching assistants' work at scale¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends applying data-driven algorithmic management based on stochastic optimization to "scale up and make ef ﬁcient utilization of the collective productivity of community TAs", dividing work dynamically, scheduling rest-breaks and adjusting incentives based on game theory. It motivates this with reported small scale (typically fewer than 10 community TAs per course) and poor retention. Learner ratings of TA performance would supply the data such mechanisms need.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Mechanisms allowing learners who interacted with a community TA to provide ratings on the TA's performance

Constraints¶

  • Most community TAs (about four out of five) declined invitations to perform community TA duties for a second time, limiting interactions and attention available to learners

Target Learners¶

  • community teaching assistants
  • large-population MOOC learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • scaling perceived personal attention and learner interaction opportunities in MOOCs

Claims¶

  • AI scheduling systems reportedly reduce administrative workload by up to 40% and enrollment prediction exceeds 85% accuracy (as reported in the reviewed literature) [+W]
  • Iterate averaging with step sizes decreasing slower than O(1/n) converges at an optimal rate [+W]
  • Diminishing step sizes in stochastic approximation induce implicit averaging that eliminates noise effects in the long run [+W]
  • Many learning algorithms, including neural network training and Q-learning, have recursive forms of the stochastic approximation type [+W]
