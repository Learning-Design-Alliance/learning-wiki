# Incorporate OEND routinely into justice diversion addiction treatment intake rather than targeting by justice involvement subgroup

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy operationalizes the article's conclusion that "OEND should be routinely incorporated into justice diversion addiction treatment". At intake, programs deliver overdose education — recognizing an overdose, responding, and Good Samaritan Law information — and distribute naloxone to every client, without screening by arrest history, incarceration time, or prior overdose. The article's latent class analysis showed no subgroup differences in overdose exposure or naloxone knowledge that would justify targeting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Program staff able to deliver overdose education and supply naloxone at treatment entry
- Clients who use opioids entering via justice diversion pathways
#### Constraints
- The article studied one residential program with 60–90 day typical treatment duration in Michigan; it could not distinguish clients diverted after arrest, incarceration, or parole/probation

### Target Learners
- Adult people who use opioids in justice diversion residential addiction treatment

### Target Learning Goals
- Overdose recognition and response, naloxone knowledge and use
