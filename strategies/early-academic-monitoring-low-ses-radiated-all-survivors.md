# Monitor and intervene early on academic performance in ALL survivors with low socio-economic status or radiation exposure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that ALL survivors facing socio-economic disadvantage or radiation exposure receive early academic monitoring and intervention, because these two factors were the only significant predictors of reduced reading achievement. The authors state that "survivors with low socio‐economic status and those exposed to radiation during treatment could benefit from early monitoring and intervention to maximize academic success." The recommendation rests on the study's school-based achievement data rather than on a tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of survivors' socio-economic status and radiation exposure, e.g., via school lunch-program participation and medical records
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is based on observational associations in a single-institution sample of 63 survivors, not on a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- pediatric acute lymphoblastic leukemia survivors in grades 3–11

### Target Learning Goals
- reading achievement
- academic success monitoring
