# Rotate the camera 90 degrees so the first assistant avoids the mirror-image condition during left colonic surgery

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A tactical modification for laparoscopic left colonic surgery in which the camera direction is rotated 90 degrees left or right depending on the approach direction, letting the first assistant view a side or pelvic-direction monitor instead of a mirror image. Approaching "in a cranial direction to the common iliac artery", the camera is rotated "90 degrees to the left"; approaching caudally, 90 degrees to the right, so the assistant avoids the mirror-image condition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An additional monitor positioned on the side of the head or in the pelvic direction so the assistant can look away from the mirror-image display
#### Constraints
- Demonstrated by the authors for the external approach in typical left colonic surgery; the article does not report a formal evaluation of this tactic

### Target Learners
