# Formative Assessment Loops

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A formative assessment loop is a repeating cycle: elicit evidence of learning (a quick check, task, or question), interpret that evidence against the intended learning goal, and act on it — the teacher adjusts instruction while learners adjust their strategies. The loop closes only when the *action* step occurs; collecting evidence without responsive follow-up is measurement, not formative assessment [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S].

## Design Implications

Formative loops work because they surface misconceptions while they are still correctable and because feedback arrives when learners can still use it [Feedback is most effective when it answers where am I going, how am I doing, and where to next.](../claims/feedback-answers-three-questions.md) [+S]. The design burden falls on the *act* step: each loop must specify in advance what evidence will be collected, what decision rule triggers a change, and what the change will be. Loops should be short and frequent rather than long and summative in disguise.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clearly stated learning goals against which evidence is interpreted ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md))
- Low-stakes elicitation tasks that reveal thinking, not just right answers ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
- A pre-planned menu of instructional responses so the teacher can act immediately
- Feedback that names the gap and the next move, not just a score ([Action-Oriented Feedback](../strategies/action-oriented-feedback.md))

#### Constraints
- Grading loop artifacts converts them into summative events; students then hide confusion and the evidence dries up [-S]
- Feedback without learner opportunity to act on it produces negligible gains [Feedback is most effective when it answers where am I going, how am I doing, and where to next.](../claims/feedback-answers-three-questions.md) [-S]
- Loops fail when the elicitation task is answerable without understanding (recall-only checks), producing false confidence in progress
- Over-frequent checking without instructional response consumes time and erodes trust in the process [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Whole-class loops**: exit tickets or hinge questions with branching next-day plans
- **Individual loops**: [Adaptive Learning](../principles/adaptive-learning.md) systems that route learners to remediation based on item responses
- **Peer loops**: structured peer review where students apply criteria to each other's work [~M]
- **Self-loops**: students judge their own work against criteria, building evaluative judgment

### Target Learners
- Struggling learners benefit most, since early detection prevents compounding gaps [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]
- Novices, who cannot yet self-diagnose misconceptions and depend on external evidence
- Less automatic benefit for advanced learners, for whom frequent low-level checks can feel like busywork [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: exposing and repairing misconceptions before they consolidate
- Procedural fluency: catching error patterns during early [Practice](../elements/practice.md)
- Self-regulation: learners internalize the loop itself — monitoring, judging, adjusting

### Instructions
1. Define the learning goal and the evidence that would demonstrate it ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md)).
2. Design a short elicitation task — hinge question, exit ticket, or one-item probe — targeting the most likely misconception ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)).
3. Collect and rapidly interpret the evidence against a decision rule (e.g., "fewer than 70% correct → reteach via a different representation").
4. Act: adjust instruction, regroup, or assign targeted follow-up ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md), [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).
5. Give learners feedback that specifies the gap and the next action, and time to act on it ([Action-Oriented Feedback](../strategies/action-oriented-feedback.md)).
6. Re-elicited evidence in the next loop verifies whether the adjustment worked; repeat until the goal is met.

## Related Strategies
- [Action-Oriented Feedback](action-oriented-feedback.md) — the feedback step of the loop must specify next moves to close the gap
- [Activating Prior Knowledge](activating-prior-knowledge.md) — the first loop often serves as a diagnostic for what learners already hold, including misconceptions

## Examples
- **Dylan Wiliam's "hinge questions"** — a single multiple-choice question mid-lesson, with each distractor mapped to a specific misconception and a pre-planned reteaching route; the whole class's response set determines the next five minutes.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — mastery exercises continuously assess and route learners to prerequisite content or hints, enacting the loop algorithmically.
- **Retrieval-practice routines** (e.g., two-question openers in [Peer Instruction](https://www.perusall.com) / Eric Mazur's ConcepTests) — students answer individually, discuss with peers, re-answer, then the instructor addresses the residual confusion.
