Strategies

Employ experimental and longitudinal designs with standardised measurement tools in future pressure injury prevention research.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends moving beyond descriptive KAP surveys toward more robust and theory-informed study designs, specifically calling for "randomised controlled trials and longitudinal studies" to evaluate multilevel interventions such as structured training combined with leadership engagement, mentorship models, and digital decision-support tools. It also highlights the value of mixed-method approaches for unpacking how contextual, cultural, and organisational factors shape preventive practice.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Use of standardised and validated assessment tools to improve comparability across studies.

Constraints¶

  • Scarcity of experimental and longitudinal studies currently restricts assessment of intervention effectiveness and sustainability.

Target Learners¶

  • Nursing researchers and clinical educators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improving research methodology for evaluating pressure injury prevention interventions

Claims¶

  • Nurses' knowledge of pressure injury prevention is frequently inadequate, particularly in prevention-specific domains. [~W]
  • Preventive practices for pressure injuries are often suboptimal despite positive attitudes. [~W]
  • Common barriers to pressure injury prevention include staff shortages, high workload, limited resources, and insufficient institutional support. [~W]
