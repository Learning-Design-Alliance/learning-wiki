# Expand diagnostic access and train primary care doctors in surrogate airflow-obstruction measurement where spirometry is unavailable

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend policy-level actions to close chronic cough diagnostic gaps in resource-limited primary care: expanding access to essential diagnostic tools, mobile diagnostic units, telemedicine consultations and portable spirometers, plus practical clinician training. They state that "training PCPs in peak expiratory flow (PEF) measurement as a surrogate for airflow obstruction can serve as a practical alternative where spirometry is unavailable". These actions target practising primary care physicians managing persistent cough.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Primary care clinicians trained in PEF measurement as a surrogate for airflow obstruction
- Availability of portable spirometers, mobile diagnostic units or telemedicine consultation channels
#### Constraints
- Recommended where spirometry is unavailable; the article notes CXR reliance alone may miss functional disorders such as asthma or GERD

### Target Learners
- primary care physicians in resource-limited settings

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnosing and managing chronic cough
- distinguishing asthma, COPD and other respiratory conditions
