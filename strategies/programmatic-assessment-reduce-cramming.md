# Align assessment with long-term learning by spreading low-stakes evaluation moments over time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy responds to the article's finding that exam proximity, not intention, drove effective strategy use: students crammed and favoured recognition-friendly approaches when multiple-choice exams rewarded short-term performance. The authors recommend that "alignment between learning and assessment methods is necessary, which could be achieved by implementing programmatic assessments that spread out low-stakes evaluation moments over time". Spreading evaluation across a course is expected to reduce reliance on reactive, performance-oriented studying and foster consistent use of effective learning strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment programs must prioritize understanding and recall rather than recognition alone for the strategy to promote long-term learning.
#### Constraints
- The article notes cramming can be more efficient for the immediate goal of passing an exam that rewards short-term performance.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate university students

### Target Learning Goals
- consistent use of effective learning strategies such as distributed practice for long-term retention
