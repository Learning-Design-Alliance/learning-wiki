# Advance micro-credentialing policy across five domains: controlling for quality, counting what matters, finding time, leveraging expertise, and designing for effectiveness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The brief proposes five major policy domains for the future of micro-credentialing: controlling for quality, counting what matters, finding time, leveraging and recognizing expertise, and designing for effectiveness. Teams of educators, administrators, and researchers should review which micro-credentials meet standards for which purpose; decisions are needed on what recognition credentials earn; policies must account for teachers' time; policymakers should fund recognition of earned credentials; and policy must meet educators' felt needs and build engagement and ownership in design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- State, district, or network-level review teams to judge which micro-credentials meet standards for what purpose
#### Constraints
- The brief states much more policy work is needed on which incentives are better for what kind of micro-credentials and on balancing choice with coherence

### Target Learners
- PK-12 teachers, administrators, and paraprofessionals

### Target Learning Goals
- Quality-controlled, competency-based professional learning that spreads teacher expertise and drives school improvement
