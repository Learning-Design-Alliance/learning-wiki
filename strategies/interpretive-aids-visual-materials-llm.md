# Enhance visual materials with interpretive aids such as labels or arrows to support both AI performance and human learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the error analysis, the article recommends using standardized assessments and augmenting visual exam/learning materials with interpretive aids. The authors write that the findings "demonstrate the importance of using standardized assessments and enhancing visual materials with interpretive aids such as labels or arrows, and demonstrate how analyzing LLM errors can inform educators on teaching clinical reasoning".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Visual materials supplemented with labels or arrows; use of standardized (internationally standardized) assessments rather than region-specific frameworks alone
#### Constraints
- The Japan-specific Brunnstrom framework, not internationally standardized, posed a major barrier for LLMs trained primarily on English-language data

### Target Learners
- occupational therapy students
- occupational therapy educators

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- visual-spatial interpretation
