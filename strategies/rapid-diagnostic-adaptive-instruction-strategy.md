# Use rapid first-step diagnostic tests before and during instruction to adapt faded worked-example sequences to learner expertise

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a three-step procedure: establish the sequence of main intermediate solution steps corresponding to the task's subgoal structure; design representative subtasks for each step and arrange them in an ordered series; and present the series for a few seconds per subtask, requiring learners to "quickly indicate the next step toward a complete solution". Scores place learners at appropriate stages of faded worked examples and monitor progress in real time, as implemented in the computer-based Experiment 4.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task domain with well-defined, predictable sequences of solution steps
- A computer-based or otherwise rapid delivery environment for real-time monitoring
#### Constraints
- Validated only in narrow domains (linear algebra equations and coordinate geometry) with Year 9–10 students

### Target Learners
- secondary school mathematics students with varying levels of domain expertise

### Target Learning Goals
- efficient matching of instructional guidance to current learner expertise in mathematics problem solving
