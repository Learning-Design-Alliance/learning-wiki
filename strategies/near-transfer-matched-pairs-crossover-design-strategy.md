# Design two-stage exam studies with matched near-transfer question pairs and a randomized crossover design to remove question-recall confounds

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's methodological recipe: pairs of near-transfer questions are used for the initial test and retest "to remove the confounding factor of question recall from the measure of learning", and a randomized crossover design is used "where each participant was in both the control and treatment groups". Six questions per exam were developed to match six end-of-course diagnostic questions, and three group-exam versions each missing two questions randomly assigned students to treatment or control questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Question pairs rated by content experts for targeting the same application of a given concept; item analysis via the discrimination index
#### Constraints
- The crossover design was chosen as fair to students; the diagnostic was administered in lab sessions at fixed times relative to the midterms

### Target Learners
- introductory calculus-based physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring retention and near-transfer of concepts after collaborative testing
