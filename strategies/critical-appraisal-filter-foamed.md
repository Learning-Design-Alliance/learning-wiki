# Teach residents a filter for assessing the quality and validity of free open access medical education resources

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because most residents rarely appraise the evidence behind online materials, the article argues educators' primary role is teaching critical appraisal. It states that "a growing body of free and open access educational materials means that students must develop a filter that allows them to assess the quality of information" and that "our job is to teach residents how to critically assess the quality of resources and the validity of their evidence."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators acting as facilitators of knowledge who guide appropriate use and critical appraisal of multimedia and Internet-based resources
#### Constraints
- The article notes peer-reviewed content of textbooks and journals is generally superior in quality to that found on many blogs, podcasts, and online resources

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine residents
- Millennial Generation learners in medical education

### Target Learning Goals
- Critical appraisal of evidence quality in online educational resources
