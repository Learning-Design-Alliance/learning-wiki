Align teaching method with competency gap and prioritize TBL for communication-focused courses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that nursing educators adopt a strategic, outcome-driven approach rather than a "one-size-fits-all" model: program directors should "align the teaching method with the specific competency gap identified in the curriculum." Because TBL yielded the largest communication-skill effect (SMD = 1.94), they recommend TBL be "prioritized in courses focusing on leadership, handoffs, and interprofessional collaboration."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Programs must identify the specific competency gap in the curriculum before selecting a method

Constraints¶

  • Recommendation rests on comparative effect sizes from heterogeneous quasi-experimental evidence; the authors caution findings should be interpreted with caution due to small-study effects and methodological limitations

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate and postgraduate nursing students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • communication skills
  • problem-solving abilities
  • self-directed learning

Claims¶

  • Merely modifying teaching methods in medical and dental schools has limited merit for steering students' learning approaches; no one-size-fits-all teaching method exists [+W]
  • Modern teaching methods significantly improve nursing students' communication skills (SMD = 1.80) [+W]
  • Communication-skill effects of modern teaching methods are significant only for short durations (≤5 weeks) and second-year students [~W]
  • PBL and TBL significantly improve nursing students' self-directed learning [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Scaffold method choice by student progression: CBL early, transitioning to PBL and TBL
