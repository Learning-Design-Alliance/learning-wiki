# Use findings on gender and GPA among two-year black transfers to inform debates on the two-year transfer pathway in engineering

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors position their results as input to policy discussions about the two-year transfer route into engineering for black and other minority students. They state the findings "should inform debates regarding the effectiveness of the two-year transfer pathway in engineering for black and other minority students." The strategy is to ground such debates in evidence distinguishing transfer origin, gender, and academic achievement rather than treating transfers as a single group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal data that can distinguish transfers from two-year versus four-year institutions and track engineering GPA and graduation outcomes
#### Constraints
- The analysis concerns black engineering transfer students; the authors note the earlier persistence finding does not hold for students of other ethnicities

### Target Learners
- black or African American undergraduate engineering students who transfer from two-year colleges

### Target Learning Goals
- persistence and six-year graduation in engineering majors
