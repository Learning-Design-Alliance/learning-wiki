# Design online courses to fit learners' psychosocial characteristics and include social interaction, self-regulation, and frequent assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on Cercone (2008) and Ren et al. (2017), the review states that quality online learning should include "a significant component of social interaction and collaboration with peers, a connection between new knowledge and past experience, self-reflected and self-regulated learning, an immediate practical application of exercises and frequent assessment". Course design should fit learners' psychosocial characteristics, needs and expectations, which the review says produces more motivated and engaged students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online course design must account for learners' individual characteristics such as learning styles, motivation, and prior knowledge
#### Constraints
- The review frames this as a design recommendation for the transition from traditional to online learning models, not a tested result

### Target Learners
- higher education students
- online learners

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement and motivation in online learning
- self-regulated learning
