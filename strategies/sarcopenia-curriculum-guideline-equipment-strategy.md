# Expand sarcopenia coverage in medical curricula, add primary-care guidelines, and provide equipment support for screening and diagnosis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building sarcopenia awareness and competence in primary care through pre- and post-graduate training, guideline development, and practical diagnostic tools. It states that "Expanding the subject of sarcopenia in the medical school curriculum, including sarcopenia management in guidelines for primary health care providers and providing equipment support" may be a rational public-health approach, and that family physicians "should be supported by guidelines suitable for primary care and by equipment support for screening and diagnosis."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must reach family physicians both before and after graduation, since one-time training without feedback and continuous reinforcement is judged insufficient by the authors
#### Constraints
- The article notes recommended diagnostic tools such as DXA and BIA are impractical in primary care due to high cost, accessibility difficulties, and requirement of trained personnel

### Target Learners
- family physicians in primary care
- medical students and residents

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of sarcopenia definition and EWGSOP2 diagnostic criteria
- competence in sarcopenia screening and management
