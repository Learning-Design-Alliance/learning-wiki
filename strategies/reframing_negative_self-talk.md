# Reframing Negative Self-Talk

## Description
Reframing involves rephrasing negative thoughts, statements, or situations into more positive alternatives. Students use their self-talk logs or notes to identify negative messages and restate them into positive messages, often using the name they chose for their negative voice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Self-talk logs or notes containing negative messages. Examples of negative self-talk to reframe. A supportive environment for practicing and sharing reframes.
#### Constraints
- Students may struggle to identify positive alternatives. Some reframes may feel forced or insincere. The activity's success depends on students' willingness to practice and apply reframing.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- K-12 students who are learning to manage negative thoughts and develop a more positive self-perception.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- To help students transform negative thoughts into positive alternatives, promoting a growth mindset.

### Affordances
- Promotes self-awareness by identifying negative thoughts. Supports emotional regulation by changing negative thoughts into positive ones. Fosters a growth mindset by seeing challenges as opportunities for growth.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to different age groups and learning styles. Students can use their own self-talk logs or pre-made examples. Educators can provide varying levels of support and scaffolding.

## Assessment Evidence
- Review students' reframes for accuracy and positivity. Assess students' ability to articulate the impact of reframing on their emotions. Observe students' use of reframing in real-life situations.

## Impact
- Research shows that cognitive reframing has a positive impact on mindset. Reframing can foster stress resilience and promote problem solving when facing adversity.

## Examples
- Restating the fixed mindset phrase 'Everyone knows I can’t win this challenge' into a positive message like 'Good try, Ruthless Ralph. Even if I don’t win, I will learn something from this challenge!' Practicing with a variety of negative messages and reframing them into positive alternatives. Using a journal to track and reframe negative self-talk over time.
