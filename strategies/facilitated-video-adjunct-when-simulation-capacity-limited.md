# Use facilitated interactive video as a scalable BLS training adjunct when simulation capacity is limited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, within a BLS curriculum that includes a shared structured lecture, a well-prepared facilitated interactive video component "warrants further investigation as a pragmatic and scalable adjunct when simulation capacity is limited". Video can be prepared once and delivered to many students with the same standard, whereas simulation laboratories require substantial infrastructure and instructor availability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery with facilitation and real-time instructor feedback, not passive viewing
- A shared structured lecture preceding the modality-specific component
#### Constraints
- The findings should not be interpreted as showing that stand-alone video can replace stand-alone simulation
- The trial was not designed as an equivalence study

### Target Learners
- medical students in large undergraduate programs

### Target Learning Goals
- basic life support knowledge and skills
