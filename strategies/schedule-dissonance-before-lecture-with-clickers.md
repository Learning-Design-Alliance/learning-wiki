# Strengthen misconception remediation by scheduling dissonance-inducing activities before lecture and checking emerging understanding with clickers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that the intervention produced dissonance for only about 30% of targeted students, the authors recommend greater effort at promoting cognitive dissonance, perhaps by requiring students to correctly answer questions and recognize the shortcomings of their misconceptions before moving to the next task. They further recommend scheduling the intervention immediately before lecture instruction on the concepts, so dissonant students are more ready to accept the scientific explanation presented during lecture, and using personal response systems (clickers) so students can ask questions and have their emerging understanding checked during lecture.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Intervention activities must explicitly challenge student misconceptions and provide cognitive dissonance
- Lecture instruction on the concepts should follow the intervention
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is offered for similar instructional interventions in introductory science courses; the authors note budget misconceptions are difficult to overcome even with careful targeting

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory science students

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing pattern-matching misconceptions about stock-flow systems
- accurate understanding of budget and mass-balance concepts
