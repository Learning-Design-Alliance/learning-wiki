# Instructor trust-building strategies for active-learning courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that faculty build student trust through responsive actions and course-design choices that signal care, acceptance, and understanding. It lists specific strategies used by the instructor in this course: "1) being transparent about the purpose and goals of active learning; 2) highlighting the empirical evidence in support of these pedagogies"; 3) ensuring consistent alignment between activities and assessments; and 4) encouraging a growth mindset toward learning. Responsive practices such as referring to students by name, providing nonevaluative opportunities to work through problems, frequent feedback, and encouraging collaboration are also named.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must support trust-building statements with responsive actions, including soliciting formative student feedback and being available before, during, and after class.
#### Constraints
- The strategies are drawn from one course whose instructor had demonstrated high competency with active learning in a large classroom; the article calls the findings preliminary.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large active-learning science courses

### Target Learning Goals
- student trust in the instructor, commitment to active learning, and engagement
