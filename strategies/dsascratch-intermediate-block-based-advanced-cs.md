# Use DSAScratch as an intermediate block-based means of teaching advanced CS curricula in K-12 before students move to textual languages

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends positioning DSAScratch as "an intermediate means of teaching advanced CS curricula using a block-based programming environment such as Scratch", letting educators introduce abstraction, logic, conditionals, loops, and basic algorithms without technical syntax. Because Scratch is open-source and free, the authors argue the approach makes these concepts accessible to a wide audience with minimal investment, and prepares students for textual languages with some familiarity with advanced concepts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators divide subject matter into abstraction, logic, conditionals, loops, and writing basic algorithms as part of introductory K-12 computer science curricula; the extension must run within Scratch, which is transpiled into ES6
#### Constraints
- The evaluation is preliminary, with a small sample of ten students; the authors state it is difficult to draw statistical inferences and that further evaluation with more diverse student groups is needed

### Target Learners
- K-12 high school students in introductory computer science, particularly novices before transitioning to Python or other textual languages

### Target Learning Goals
- Advanced data structures and algorithms concepts
- High-level abstraction capabilities and problem decomposition
