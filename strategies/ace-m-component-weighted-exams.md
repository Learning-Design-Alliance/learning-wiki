# Structure exams around ACE-M components with points attached to Analyze and Create a Plan to signal the value of the whole process

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
On exams, students separate their thoughts into the appropriate ACE-M component, and each component is associated with points so students see the value the instructor places on the entire process. "Typically the Analyze the Task and Create a Plan components are worth 40% each", which differs from students' initial view emphasizing calculations. Monitoring is not a required component, but students can write questions and observations for partial credit under any of the three required components.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Exams must ask students to separate their thoughts into the appropriate component, with points assigned per component
#### Constraints
- Monitoring does not factor into the required components; it earns partial credit only as written questions and observations

### Target Learners
- lower-division undergraduate STEM students

### Target Learning Goals
- problem-solving process
- valuing analysis and planning over calculation alone

## Claims

- [Calculus students who explicitly cited ACE-M earned course grades almost a full letter grade higher than those who did not mention it](../claims/ace-m-citation-higher-course-grades.md) [+W]
- [ACE-M Calculus and Organic Chemistry students reported statistically significant gains in their value and use of monitoring, unlike comparison-course students](../claims/ace-m-monitoring-use-significant-gains.md) [+W]
- [Students in ACE-M Calculus and Organic Chemistry courses reported statistically significant gains in problem-solving self-efficacy, while comparison-section students did not](../claims/ace-m-self-efficacy-significant-gains.md) [+W]
- [Large majorities of students reported ACE-M was the most important process they learned (85% of Calculus 1) or helpful (87% of General Physics)](../claims/ace-m-student-reported-helpfulness.md) [+W]
