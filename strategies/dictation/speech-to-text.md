# Dictation/Speech-to-text

## Description
Dictation, also known as speech-to-text or voice recognition, involves using technology to convert spoken words into written text. This enables users to focus on expressing their thoughts verbally, rather than struggling with the physical act of writing. It can be integrated into various devices like computers, tablets, and smartphones, often utilizing built-in speech recognition software. Users speak into a microphone, and the software recognizes their speech, converting it into written text. The accuracy can vary depending on the user's speech patterns and the software's capabilities, with more advanced programs offering improved recognition and error correction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a device with a microphone and speech recognition software (e.g., Dragon NaturallySpeaking, Microsoft Speech Recognition). A quiet environment is beneficial for accuracy. Training and practice are needed to become proficient in using the software.
#### Constraints
- Accuracy can be affected by background noise, speech impediments, and variations in pronunciation. Younger children may find it challenging to use due to inconsistent speech patterns. Requires error correction and monitoring to ensure accuracy. Dependence on technology may hinder development of handwriting skills.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Individuals with dysgraphia, ADHD, executive functioning deficits, learning disabilities, physical disabilities, vision impairments, limited motor skills, or those learning a second language. Particularly beneficial for middle school, high school, and college students.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Facilitate writing for individuals with dysgraphia, ADHD, or executive functioning deficits, improve writing production, reduce anxiety around writing, and enhance core reading and writing abilities.

### Affordances
- Enables individuals to bypass handwriting difficulties and focus on content creation, improves writing mechanics and legibility, promotes independence by reducing reliance on human transcribers, decreases anxiety related to writing, and enhances phonemic awareness by visually connecting spoken words to their written form.

### Personalization
- Users can adjust settings for language, accent, and speaking style to improve accuracy. Software can be trained to recognize individual voices and speech patterns. Integrates with various applications like word processors and email programs.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track improvements in writing length, complexity, and mechanics. Observe student confidence and engagement in writing tasks. Compare dictated text to handwritten work for legibility and accuracy. Monitor the frequency and types of errors made during dictation.

## Impact
- Research indicates improved writing quality and quantity for students with learning disabilities. Studies show enhanced phonemic awareness and error correction skills. Assistive technology can significantly improve academic performance and reduce anxiety related to writing.
