Intensify standard reading interventions by adjusting dosage, alignment, transfer scaffolding, behavior supports, and data-based individualization¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Drawing on the Taxonomy of Intervention Intensity, the article recommends concrete ways schools can intensify a standard protocol such as Passport to Literacy for students with the most intensive needs. Recommended moves include increasing "dosage" via "more intervention in terms of duration or frequency, or in terms of a smaller group size", aligning the intervention to the specific skills students lack, explicitly scaffolding transfer of strategies to new genres and text levels, adding behavior supports such as self-monitoring or graphing of oral reading fluency progress, and using data-based individualization with frequent progress monitoring and diagnostic information.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need progress-monitoring data and diagnostic information such as phonics inventories and observations of engagement to make ongoing adjustments

Constraints¶

  • The article notes tension between implementing with fidelity and moving toward data-based individualization; smaller, more homogeneous groups are suggested as a condition for easier alignment and feedback

Target Learners¶

  • Fourth-grade students with very poor reading comprehension receiving Tier 2 intervention

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading comprehension and word reading skills, including transfer of comprehension strategies across genres and text levels

Affordances¶

  • Taxonomy Of Intervention Intensity Framework

Claims¶

  • Quantile regression revealed larger intervention effects on GMRT reading comprehension at the .25–.30 quantiles (g = .35, ~9 points) and the .50 quantile (g = 0.22, ~5 points), but not at other quantiles or on WJ-III Passage Comprehension [+W]
  • Effect sizes for the intervention were less than g = .03 on most outcomes, with small comprehension effects of g = 0.15 and g = 0.19 [+W]
  • A standard Tier 2 multi-component reading intervention produced no statistically significant differences versus business-as-usual on any of seven reading outcomes for fourth graders at or below the 15th percentile in comprehension [+W]
  • Despite an average of 94 well-implemented intervention sessions, students remained about one standard deviation below national norms in reading comprehension with low vocabulary [+W]
