# Frame science's exclusion of supernatural explanations as a methodological self-limitation, not a metaphysical claim

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
When teaching the nature of science, the author carefully explains that science's restriction to natural explanations is a self-limitation motivated by principle (the supernatural is not observable) and by experience, and stresses that "it is a methodological limitation, not a metaphysical one." This framing is intended to let students hold religious beliefs while understanding why science excludes supernatural explanations, drawing on Gould's Nonoverlapping Magisteria.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful prior definition of terms such as theory, fact, hypothesis, model, and law
- Explicit discussion of how science interacts with other areas of knowledge, including religion
#### Constraints
- The author notes some students still objected to the word natural despite careful statements that methodological naturalism does not preclude the reality of the supernatural

### Target Learners
- non-science majors
- elementary education majors

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding methodological naturalism
- distinguishing methodological from metaphysical claims
- understanding the relationship of science and religion
