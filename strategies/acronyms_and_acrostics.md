# Acronyms and Acrostics

## Description
Acronyms use the first letter of each word in a list or phrase to create a new, memorable word or abbreviation. Acrostics use the first letter of each item to form a sentence or phrase. These devices can aid in recall by providing a simple, easily remembered cue.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- List of items to be remembered. Ability to create words or phrases from the first letters of those items.
#### Constraints
- May not work well for complex or abstract concepts. The acronym or acrostic must be easily remembered itself.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learners, especially useful for those who need to memorize lists or sequences.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve recall of lists, names, or sequences; simplify complex information into easily remembered cues

### Affordances
- Reduces cognitive load by condensing information into a single, easily remembered word or phrase. Activates pattern recognition to facilitate recall.

### Personalization
- Learners can create their own acronyms or acrostics tailored to their learning style and the specific information they need to remember.
