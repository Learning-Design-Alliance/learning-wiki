# Design hybrid learning models that integrate LLMs with critical reflection, and establish secondary validation mechanisms for AI-driven clinical decisions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that LLMs be embedded in medical education and clinical workflows only within safeguards rather than as primary knowledge sources or standalone diagnostic tools. It states: "we recommend designing hybrid learning models in medical education that integrate LLMs, encouraging students to engage in critical reflection under AI assistance." In clinical practice it calls for "establishing secondary validation mechanisms for AI-driven decisions," with healthcare professionals reviewing AI-supported decisions to protect patient safety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Healthcare professionals must conduct necessary reviews of AI-driven decisions to ensure clinical decisions benefit patient safety
#### Constraints
- The article states LLM limitations, including knowledge inaccuracies, output randomness, risk of dependency, and ethical and privacy concerns, preclude their use as primary knowledge sources in medical education or standalone tools in clinical diagnostics

### Target Learners
- medical students
- clinicians in professional training

### Target Learning Goals
- medical knowledge acquisition
- critical reflection and independent thinking
- clinical diagnostic reasoning
