# Have students generate specific learning objectives from resource material and compare them with facilitator-generated objectives during the SDL session

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete SDL recipe the article describes: the facilitator briefly introduces the topic for 10 minutes, students then "make the Specific Learning Objectives (SLOs) using the resource material and study the topic accordingly for 30 min", and in the second part "the SLOs made by each student of each group were compared to the SLOs made by the facilitator of that group, and the topic was discussed for 10 min". This turns self-study into an explicit goal-setting and feedback cycle within one hour.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A separate facilitator assigned to each group
- Resource material supplied or brought by students
- About one hour of session time
#### Constraints
- Article tested this only with first-year MBBS students on two clinical anatomy topics

### Target Learners
- First-year MBBS students in group-based anatomy sessions

### Target Learning Goals
- Setting specific learning objectives for a clinical anatomy topic
- Studying topic content independently and resolving doubts through discussion
