# Two-instrument assessment: AI-free coding quizzes plus verification-gated oral defenses of comment-stripped AI-assisted work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's practical response to proxy collapse pairs weekly AI-free coding quizzes, which assess the white-box phase, with ten-minute one-on-one oral defenses of AI-assisted, comment-stripped project work, which assess orchestration. The author argues "if coding fluency is a learning goal, AI-free conditions are now the only conditions under which it can be validly measured," and the defense is scored on a five-dimension rubric with a gate: the verification dimension must reach the functional level to pass. Live adaptive probing, not comment-stripping, is what certifies, since the probing does not exist in advance and cannot be rehearsed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires small class sizes: ten minutes per student per defended assignment, roughly twenty-eight contact-hours per semester at the projected fifteen-student cohort; degraded modes (spot-defenses, TA-led defenses, paired defenses) each carry named costs.
#### Constraints
- The comment-stripping, published rubric with verification gate, and sophomore-level population are genuinely untested until Fall 2026; the rubric thresholds are one examiner's unvalidated defaults, and the format does not scale to a three-hundred-seat course, raising an equity concern.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students, sophomore-level computational course and upper-level courses

### Target Learning Goals
- explaining and defending computational artifacts in the language of the discipline; calibrated reliance on AI

- Five Pillar Tool Invariant Computational Framework
