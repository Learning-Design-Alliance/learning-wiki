# Professors should proactively clarify exam form and content expectations before students begin to struggle

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article states that "the study aims to provide professors with a tool for predicting which students may be vulnerable to miscommunication so that the professors may clarify their expectations before the student begins to struggle in the classroom." Interviews showed struggling students studied hard but prepared for the wrong style of questions based on prior assumptions about science exams, so explicit clarification of exam format and expected thinking style targets this failure mode.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must communicate the exam's format (e.g., many short problems vs. long-form problems) and the style of thinking required
#### Constraints
- The study is correlational at one institution; the AM correlation was significant only in the non-majors class

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate expectations about exam form and content; effective exam preparation

- Academic Miscommunication Construct
