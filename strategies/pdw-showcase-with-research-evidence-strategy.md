# Use professional development workshops that pair hands-on curriculum activities with a summary of classroom trial results to build teacher support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's PDW format gives teachers direct experience of the student curriculum activities plus a discussion of research intervention results, then surveys their attitudes. It is grounded in the article's argument that teachers without program experience are likely to be daunted, "incorrectly believing that it requires high mathematical skill and exceptional intelligence". Following the activity summary, teachers discussed "how the activities presented could be used in their classrooms".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be given experience of the Einstein-First program itself so they feel comfortable with the concepts they may be asked to teach
#### Constraints
- Most teachers (88%) still believed they would require additional training and resources before feeling sufficiently prepared to teach the content

### Target Learners
- primary and secondary school science teachers, from early childhood to grade ten

### Target Learning Goals
- teacher understanding of Einsteinian concepts of light and gravity and preparedness to teach them
