Strategies

Train learners in explicit monitor strategies for pronunciation, including identifying risky words during reading, covert rehearsal, post hoc monitoring, action replay, tape self-recording, and peer-monitoring¶

Description¶

The article recommends a set of teachable strategies for strengthening learners' monitor use after initial instruction. Learners first identify possibly mispronounced words from their reading, then practise through activities such as "covert rehearsal", "post hoc monitoring", "action replay", tape self-recording, and group peer-monitoring. The authors argue monitor capability "can be strengthened through strategy training" and that learners must take responsibility for monitoring their own speech inside and outside the classroom.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Initial instruction on target-sound pronunciation rules so learners have rules to monitor with
  • Conscious attention to learning strategies, which the article assumes is beneficial

Constraints¶

  • Improvement may be limited if learners depend exclusively on teachers, since sufficient individual teacher time is not guaranteed

Target Learners¶

  • adult EFL learners, especially pre-service English teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • pronunciation accuracy
  • self-monitoring of speech

Claims¶

  • The control group showed no significant improvement in pronunciation accuracy without instruction or monitor training [+W]
  • Combining pronunciation instruction with monitor-strategy training greatly improves pronunciation accuracy in adult English majors [+W]
  • Instruction plus monitor training produced positive transfer of pronunciation knowledge to uninstructed words [+W]
  • Pronunciation gains from instruction plus monitoring are smaller in spontaneous speech than in vocabulary and sentence reading [~W]
