# Online Videos for Family Learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy uses short, freely accessible online videos as shared learning objects that family members — adults and children together, often across generations and language backgrounds — can watch, discuss, and act on jointly. Videos serve as a common reference point that does not privilege any family member's literacy or fluency in the dominant language, allowing learning conversations to start from a shared observation rather than from text.

## Design Implications

Video combines visual and auditory channels, which supports comprehension for viewers with varied language proficiency and reduces the reading demands that often exclude family members with limited literacy in the instructional language [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [+M]. Because families learn together, the video functions less as direct instruction and more as a stimulus for joint attention and conversation — the adult is positioned as a co-explorer rather than a homework enforcer, which supports autonomy and positive learning dispositions at home [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]. Effectiveness depends on the video being short, captioned, and paired with a concrete follow-up activity; video alone, watched passively, produces little durable learning [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Short videos (ideally under 5–6 minutes) matched to a single, concrete idea, so working memory is not overloaded [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]
- Captions or subtitles in family languages, and visuals that carry meaning independently of narration
- A suggested shared activity or discussion prompt that follows viewing ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
- Free, mobile-friendly access — most families will watch on a phone, not a desktop

#### Constraints
- Passive co-viewing without prompts or follow-up activity yields shallow engagement [-S]; the video must be a springboard, not the whole lesson
- Videos in only the dominant language can reproduce the same exclusion the strategy is meant to remove [~M] — monolingual narration shifts the burden back onto the most fluent family member
- Production quality and pacing designed for individual learners (fast cuts, dense narration) can overwhelm intergenerational co-viewing; segmenting and learner pacing controls are needed [~S]
- Home internet access and device sharing are unevenly distributed; assuming availability excludes precisely the families most targeted [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Co-viewing prompts:** pause points with a question for the family to discuss ("What do you think happens next?")
- **Multilingual captioning:** same video, multiple subtitle tracks, so each family member can read in their stronger language
- **Activity kits:** video paired with physical materials (e.g., a science video plus a take-home experiment), turning observation into joint [demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) and hands-on practice
- **Family-created videos:** families produce their own videos explaining a family practice or skill, reversing the direction of transmission and building on home funds of knowledge

### Target Learners
- Multilingual families where adults and children have different proficiency levels in the instructional language [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [+M]
- Families with adults who have limited print literacy but strong oral-language engagement
- Young children learning alongside caregivers, where joint attention to a shared screen scaffolds conversation
- Caregivers who want to support school learning but lack confidence; successful co-viewing builds caregiver self-efficacy, which predicts persistence in supporting learning at home [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Shared vocabulary building across languages and generations
- Conceptual understanding of science, health, numeracy, and literacy topics introduced at school
- Family engagement dispositions: normalizing learning talk at home

### Instructions
1. Select or produce a short video on a single concrete idea, with visuals that carry the meaning and captions in family languages.
2. Add one or two pause-point questions so the family discusses during viewing, not just after ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md)).
3. Attach a simple joint activity that applies what was seen ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)) — a recipe, experiment, counting game, or walk-and-look task.
4. Provide the same content in multiple languages or with minimal text so no family member is locked out.
5. Invite families to respond — a photo, a short video, or a drawing — so the exchange is two-way rather than broadcast.

## Related Strategies
- Family Literacy Workshops — in-person counterpart that can introduce and model the co-viewing routine
- Take-Home Activity Kits — the physical follow-up component that pairs naturally with video
- Multilingual Home Messaging — distribution channel for video links in family languages

## Examples
- **[Sesame Street in Communities](https://www.sesamestreetincommunities.org)** — free video-based toolkits for caregivers on health, resilience, and early learning, with printable activities designed for co-viewing.
- **[Colorín Colorado](https://www.colorincolorado.org)** — videos and bilingual resources for families of English learners, showing caregivers how to support reading at home in any language.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — narrated video lessons with captions that families can watch together, followed by practice exercises; used in family math nights where caregivers and children solve alongside the video.
- **[Peep and the Big Wide World](https://peepandthebigwideworld.com)** — short science videos with accompanying bilingual (English/Spanish) family activity guides designed for co-viewing and hands-on exploration.
