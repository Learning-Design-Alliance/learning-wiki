Apply seven principles for blending positive aspects of web-facilitated, hybrid, and online environments in undergraduate education¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article cites Babb and colleagues (2014) offering "seven principles for successfully enhancing undergraduate education by blending positive aspects of all three learning environments." These include "increasing faculty and student interaction; encouraging students' collaborative learning; engaging students in active learning activities; giving prompt feedback; emphasizing the importance of time management skills; clearly communicating with students their responsibilities and the proposed learning outcomes; respecting students' opinions and providing them with alternative assignment options."

  • Enhancing engagement, motivation, and academic performance across delivery modalities

Claims¶

  • Approximately 82.1% of web-facilitated section students completed an extra credit assignment compared to about 37.5% of online section students [+W]
  • More than 92.3% of web-facilitated students earned an overall grade of A compared to 83.3% of hybrid and 62.5% of online students [+W]
  • No section consistently maintained a higher assignment completion rate than the others [-W]
  • No statistically significant differences in common activities shared between web-facilitated and hybrid or hybrid and online sections [+W]
  • No statistically significant differences among the three sections on mid-term exam, online journal entries, or combined totals [+W]
  • Web-facilitated section students earned significantly higher final exam means than both hybrid and online section students in an undergraduate exercise psychology course [+W]
  • Web-facilitated section students earned significantly higher final course grade and final letter grade means than online section students [+W]
