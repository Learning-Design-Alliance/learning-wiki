# Offer school-based educational support for learning difficulties and frequent sickness, and reduce clinic time burdens, to interrupt cascades to poorer educational outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend supporting adolescents with physical, emotional and cognitive difficulties through school-based services without requiring disclosure of HIV status, and improving overall mental, physical and emotional health to interrupt negative educational pathways. They note clinic-side innovations — fast-track clinic queues for school learners, adolescent-specific opening hours, and public transport subsidies — that may reduce time burdens for adolescents balancing care and school.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educational systems must be strengthened and capacitated; support should target observable difficulties rather than HIV status
#### Constraints
- Cross-sectional design limits causal determination; school-based large-scale testing programmes in limited-resource settings may not lead to strong linkages to care

### Target Learners
- Adolescents living with HIV in school in resource-limited settings

### Target Learning Goals
- School retention and attendance
- Scholastic achievement despite chronic illness and cognitive difficulty
