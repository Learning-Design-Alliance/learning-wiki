# Use simulation-based learning with scaffolding to train intervention reasoning skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends simulation-based learning as a promising approach for fostering intervention reasoning, stating that "A highly promising educational approach is simulation-based learning, which has been demonstrated to be an effective approach for developing complex skills such as (collaborative) diagnostic reasoning in medicine and beyond." The authors propose enhancing simulations with scaffolding such as reflection prompts, external collaboration scripts, or worked examples, and raise the concept of representational scaffolding aligned with professional practice demands.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation effectiveness can be enhanced by integrating additional scaffolding for the learning process, such as reflection prompts, external collaboration scripts or worked examples
#### Constraints
- The article notes that the effectiveness of representational scaffolding in learning intervention reasoning has not yet been empirically investigated, and it is open whether simulations used for diagnostic reasoning training suffice for intervention reasoning

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- health professions students

### Target Learning Goals
- intervention reasoning skills
- complex clinical skill development
