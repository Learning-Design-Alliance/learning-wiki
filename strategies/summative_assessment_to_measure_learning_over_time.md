# Summative Assessment to Measure Learning Over Time

## Description
Summative assessment evaluates learner achievement against a set of standards at the conclusion of an instructional period, while interim assessments administered between summative points show individual progress toward those same standards. Together they produce a longitudinal record of learning: interims diagnose trajectory, summatives certify attainment, and the comparison between them measures growth and instructional efficacy.

## Design Implications

Assessment itself is a learning event: retrieving knowledge on an assessment strengthens retention more than restudying [Testing produces durable retention gains over restudying.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S], so well-designed summative and interim assessments contribute to learning rather than merely measuring it. Their measurement function depends on alignment — items must map explicitly to the standards being claimed, and results are only actionable when they feed back into instruction at the task and process level [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear, explicit standards or learning outcomes against which performance is judged
- [Performance assessment](../elements/assessment.md) tasks that require learners to demonstrate skills in authentic or near-authentic contexts, not just recall facts
- A schedule of interim checkpoints leading to the summative point, so growth is observable
- Attention to technology access when assessments are delivered digitally, so measurement reflects learning rather than infrastructure

#### Constraints
- Summative instruments are limited to what can be measured in the available time; narrow formats (e.g., multiple-choice only) systematically under-measure complex skills [-M]
- High-stakes summative use can narrow instruction toward test content (teaching to the test), distorting the curriculum [-M]
- Digital delivery may confound results when learners have unequal access to devices or connectivity [~M]
- Summative results arrive too late to change instruction for the cohort assessed; without interim checkpoints the data has diagnostic value only for future cohorts [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Interim benchmarks** (e.g., quarterly district assessments) track individual progress toward standards between summative windows
- **Performance-based summatives** (portfolios, capstone projects, exhibitions) assess integrated skill in authentic contexts
- **Cumulative review formats** — distributing earlier material across later assessments exploits retrieval practice and spacing effects [Spaced retrieval improves long-term retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S]
- **Competency-based models** replace time-bound summatives with mastery demonstrations on demand

### Target Learners
- Adult learners in online or blended environments, where interim data substitutes for the informal progress signals a classroom instructor would otherwise observe [~W]
- Learners in standards-based or competency-based programs who need documented progress toward defined outcomes
- All learners benefit when assessment results are used formatively rather than only for grading [Formative use of assessment information produces substantial learning gains.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Measuring learning over time and documenting individual progress toward standards
- Certifying attainment by comparing performance against standards
- Evaluating the efficacy of instruction at the program or course level
- Long-term retention, when assessments require cumulative retrieval rather than unit-isolated testing [+S]

### Instructions
1. Define the standards and success criteria learners will be held to, and make them visible in advance ([Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md))
2. Design a performance assessment that requires learners to apply skills in a realistic context rather than reproduce content ([Application of Knowledge](../elements/application-of-knowledge.md))
3. Schedule interim checkpoints that sample the same standards, so individual trajectories are visible before the summative point ([Continuous Review](../elements/continuous-review.md))
4. Deliver the summative assessment, including cumulative items that require retrieval of earlier material ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
5. Analyze results at both the individual and group level: progress over time for learners, standard-level patterns for instructional evaluation
6. Feed results back into instruction — the assessment only improves learning if the information changes what happens next [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]

## Related Strategies
- Formative Assessment Loops — the complementary strategy; summative data is only trustworthy when instruction has been continuously adjusted by formative evidence
- Standards-Based Grading — reporting structures that make interim-to-summative growth interpretable to learners and stakeholders
- Mastery Testing — retesting until standard attainment, replacing single-point summatives

## Patterns That Use This Strategy
- [Formative Assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md) — interim checkpoints are the bridge between daily formative practice and summative judgment
- [Competency-Based Learning](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md) — replaces fixed summative windows with assessment on mastery demonstration

## Examples
- **NWEA MAP Growth** (https://www.nwea.org) — adaptive interim assessments administered 2–3 times per year, reporting individual growth trajectories toward standards between state summative tests.
- **Advanced Placement examinations** — summative performance assessments with published standards and released items, allowing instruction to align to the assessed constructs.
- **Portfolio capstones in teacher education programs** (e.g., edTPA, https://www.edtpa.com) — a performance-based summative in which candidates demonstrate teaching skill against explicit rubric standards.
