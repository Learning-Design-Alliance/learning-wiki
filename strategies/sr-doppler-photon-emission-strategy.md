# Treat the Doppler effect for light via photon emission/absorption with energy and momentum conservation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A teaching strategy addressing a topic the article says is often overlooked in textbooks: the emission or absorption of a photon by an atom or nucleus. After introducing basic relativistic dynamics formulae without proof, teachers have students "deal with the emission or absorption of a photon by an atom or a nu cleus, thus obtaining the formulae of the Doppler eﬀect for photons and discussing their relation with the corresponding formula for light waves." The Newtonian treatment of photon absorption is also used to make the rest energy mc² emerge, and the wave description is retained for cases with statistically significant photon numbers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Basic formulae of relativistic dynamics are introduced without proof before the photon treatment
#### Constraints
- The wave description of light yields correct predictions when the number of photons involved is statistically significant

### Target Learners
- students in elementary physics courses or upper high school

### Target Learning Goals
- understand the Doppler effect within a corpuscular description of light
- connect wave and corpuscular descriptions of light
- understand rest energy mc²
