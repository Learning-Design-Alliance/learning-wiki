Enrich secondary school Business Studies curricula with an extended curriculum using active learning in a business-simulated, cooperative set-up¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes developing additional curriculum activities that use active learning in a business-simulated set-up, executed in combination with a cooperative learning strategy, to enhance entrepreneurial problem recognition and problem-solving. It states: "It is consequently strongly recommended that secondary school curricula for Business Studies are enriched by these strategies, not only in South Africa , but world -wide." The intervention comprised 28 contact sessions of 40 minutes each with scaffolding (mediated learning) until learners could identify and solve problems unaided.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Continuous implementation integrated with the curriculum, since the effect might not be sustainable otherwise
  • Group work rather than individuals, since the curriculum promoted social interaction between learners

Constraints¶

  • The teaching framework was applied to relatively small groups of learners in one school and not tested on all race and socio-economic groups in society

Target Learners¶

  • Grade 11 Business Studies learners
  • secondary school learners in South Africa and worldwide

Target Learning Goals¶

  • entrepreneurial problem recognition and problem-solving competencies
  • positive approach towards Business Studies

Affordances¶

  • Five Step Problem Solving Process

Claims¶

  • The same intervention significantly improved learners' problem-solving, with a medium practical effect (d = 0.6) [+W]
  • Applying the intervention to the control group produced statistically and practically significant gains in both problem recognition (d = 1.5) and problem-solving (d = 2.2) [+W]
  • Performance depended on group-by-time interaction: a highly significant interaction effect (p < 0.00001) was obtained for both problem recognition and problem-solving [+W]
  • The experimental group's gains were not sustained: at post-test 2 its problem recognition was lower than both the control group and its own post-test 1, and problem-solving showed no significant change [-W]
  • Qualitative observations and interviews indicated learners learned to identify and solve problems and experienced the intervention positively [+W]
