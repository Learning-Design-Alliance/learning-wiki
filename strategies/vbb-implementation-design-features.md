# Tentative design features for implementing video-based benchmarking in OSCEs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's recommendations for practice, offered as tentative given the single study. They specify presenting examiners with "two or three videos of performances (depending on their duration) depicting varied levels of performance (perhaps good and borderline)", asking examiners to score each performance before receiving the panel score and justification, using a credible panel with clear explanations tied to the marking scheme, allowing discussion among examiners, and timing benchmarking within 24 h of the OSCE but not immediately before. Institutions with resource limits may apply VBB strategically where variability is most problematic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Benchmark content must be specific to the target OSCE station; the benchmark panel must be sufficiently and currently clinically experienced in the question domain
#### Constraints
- The authors state it is premature to make firm recommendations from a single study; VBB may not align with the purpose of constructivist-orientated assessment programmes where legitimate difference of perspective is valued

### Target Learners
- OSCE examiners and assessment teams in medical schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Standardisation and alignment of examiner scoring across parallel OSCE circuits

### Affordances
- Vbb Realist Middle Range Theory
