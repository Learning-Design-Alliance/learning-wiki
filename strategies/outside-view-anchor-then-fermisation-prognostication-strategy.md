# Teach prognostication via an outside-view-then-inside-view sequence using epidemiological anchors and Fermisation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's training programme teaches a two-step approach to prognostic estimation: first take an outside view using actuarial anchor numbers from epidemiological data, then personalise with an inside view adjusting for the individual patient. As the article states, "prognostic accuracy can be improved by first taking an 'outside view' on a situation—for example, by using anchor numbers derived from the brochure ('actuarial' approach)", followed by adjustment using the principle of Fermisation. It was delivered as a teaching lecture with a brochure and repeated before each trained phase.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A brochure of epidemiological data on the clinic's most common diseases, with median survival and Kaplan-Meier curves for patient subgroups
- A teaching lecture introducing the general approach to prognostication
#### Constraints
- The effect was shown for prognostication up to 6 months; the authors state the teaching effect is unlikely to be present beyond this time, especially beyond 1 year
- The training effect was not demonstrated by a randomised comparison

### Target Learners
- physicians in training (residents)
- board-certified oncologists

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical prognostication accuracy
- use of epidemiological anchor data in prediction
