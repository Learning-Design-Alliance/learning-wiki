# Use peer observation of random differences for history-taking training and predesigned DPs for procedural skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends matching the DP mode to the skill: for history-taking, where process presentation is diverse, trainees observe peers and record random differences on a shared scoring sheet, receiving all feedback items at class end; for procedural skills, teachers predesign DPs into flawed videos and trainees mark and verbally correct them. The authors state "The diversity of process presentation determines that the random DPs from trainees seem to be more appropriate for history-taking training and testing than predesign DPs."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A printed scoring sheet identical to the preclass PDF for each observer
- Standardized patients rehearsed with scripts, and a standard history-taking video recorded on a condition outside the scripts
#### Constraints
- Random-DP peer observation was confirmed for history-taking; predesigned DPs were used for the procedural skills

### Target Learners
- first-year medical or surgical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- history-taking
- basic surgical and procedural clinical skills
