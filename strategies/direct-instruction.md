# Direct Instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Direct Instruction (DI) is a highly structured, teacher-led strategy in which content is broken into small sequential steps, modeled explicitly by the instructor, practiced with high rates of learner response, and followed by immediate corrective feedback. It encompasses both the formal Engelmann-style DI programs (e.g., *Reading Mastery*, *Corrective Mathematics*) and the broader family of explicit teaching routines described by Rosenshine — daily review, presentation in small units, guided practice, and independent practice.

## Design Implications

Direct Instruction manages working memory load by sequencing content in small steps and providing explicit models before independent work, reducing unguided search that overwhelms novices [Novices learn more from explicit guidance than minimal-guidance approaches.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+S]. Its effectiveness depends on active learner responding — choral responses, questioning, and checking for understanding — rather than passive lecture; unidirectional transmission without response opportunities is a degraded form that loses most of the benefit [Active learning improves exam performance relative to lecture-only formats.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Carefully sequenced, small-step content with a defined skill progression
- Explicit modeling with [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) and [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) narration of expert reasoning
- High-frequency learner responses with choral or individual answering
- Continuous checking for understanding and immediate [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) with error correction
- [Practice](../elements/practice.md) distributed until responses are fluent and automatic

#### Constraints
- Ineffective when delivered as passive transmission; without learner response and checking, it reduces to lecture [-S]
- Less effective for learners with strong prior knowledge, for whom explicit guidance becomes redundant and can impose unnecessary load [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]
- Weak fit for open-ended, ill-structured goals (creative writing, design, inquiry) where no single correct procedure exists to model [~M]
- Scripted implementations can suppress teacher responsiveness to individual learner needs and reduce adaptivity [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Programmed DI** (Engelmann): fully scripted lessons, fast pacing, homogeneous skill grouping — used at scale in programs such as *Reading Mastery* and *Corrective Reading*
- **Explicit teaching routines** (Rosenshine): flexible principles — daily review, small steps, questioning, guided then independent practice — adaptable across subjects
- **Hybrid forms**: explicit modeling followed by [Fading](../elements/fading.md) into guided discovery, combining structure with learner exploration

### Target Learners
- Novices and struggling learners, who lack the schemas to benefit from unguided exploration [Novices learn more from explicit guidance than minimal-guidance approaches.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+S]
- Early literacy and numeracy learners, where DI programs show consistent effects [DI programs improve achievement across reading and math.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S]
- Less beneficial for advanced learners, per the [Expertise Reversal Effect](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency and automaticity in well-structured domains (decoding, arithmetic, grammar)
- Foundational declarative knowledge and vocabulary
- Skill generalization when instruction includes varied examples and [Fading](../elements/fading.md)

### Instructions
1. Begin with a short review of prerequisite skills ([Activation](../elements/activation.md)).
2. State the goal and model the new skill in small steps using [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) with [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) narration.
3. Conduct guided practice with high response rates, using [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and immediate corrective [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md).
4. Check for understanding with frequent questions; re-teach on errors before proceeding.
5. Release to independent [Practice](../elements/practice.md), [Fading](../elements/fading.md) support as fluency develops.
6. Assess performance and [Fading](../elements/fading.md)-adjust pacing based on mastery data ([Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md)).
