# Racial Equity in PBL - Build Community Through Peer Feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
When students are given the opportunity during projects to frequently share feedback and revise their work, it creates moments to witness growth and progress — their own and their peers'. Providing meaningful feedback to each other allows students to contribute to a strong, supportive classroom culture, building the relationships and connection they need. In racially diverse classrooms, this strategy deliberately positions every student as both a contributor and a critic, distributing intellectual authority rather than concentrating it in the teacher or in historically high-status students.

## Design Implications

Peer feedback functions as both an academic move and an equity move: it improves work quality through iterative critique [Feedback that answers "Where am I going, how am I doing, and where to next?" improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S], and it builds the sense of belonging that underpins persistence for students whose identities are underrepresented or stereotyped in academic settings [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. For feedback to build community rather than status hierarchies, it must be taught, structured, and normed — unstructured peer critique tends to reproduce existing classroom status orders.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A classroom environment that values feedback as care, not criticism; norms co-authored with students
- A facilitator skilled in teaching feedback protocols and mediating conflicts
- Protected time in the project calendar for feedback *and* revision — feedback without revision reads as performative
- Feedback protocols (e.g., "I like… I wonder… I suggest…", gallery walks, critical friends) that make participation roles explicit

#### Constraints
- Requires substantial time for feedback and revision cycles; compressing them undermines both quality and trust
- Without taught norms, feedback can devolve into either empty praise or status-reinforcing critique, with students of color disproportionately receiving lower-quality or harsher comments [-M]
- Public critique can threaten belonging for students already uncertain of their standing; anonymous or small-group formats may be needed early [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [~M]
- Facilitator must be prepared to address interpersonal conflict and bias in feedback directly, not avoid it

#### Implementation Variability
- **Critical Friends protocols** (Adaptive Schools / School Reform Initiative): structured, timed rounds with the presenter silent during feedback
- **Gallery walks and silent critique** via sticky notes: lowers social risk, useful early in community-building
- **Digital peer review** (e.g., Peergrade, Peerceptiv, Flipgrid video feedback): asynchronous formats give multilingual students time to compose
- **Feedback ladders**: begin with anonymous written comments, progress to face-to-face structured critique as trust develops

### Target Learners
- All grade levels; protocols must be scaled to developmental readiness
- Students from historically marginalized groups, for whom visible contribution to peers' work builds academic standing and belonging [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Emergent multilingual learners, when feedback formats allow writing, drawing, or oral alternatives
- Less beneficial when imposed on classrooms without established trust — premature public critique can depress participation [-M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Classroom culture: building relationships, trust, and collective responsibility for quality
- Self-regulated learning: using feedback to revise, and internalizing evaluative criteria [Feedback that answers "Where am I going, how am I doing, and where to next?" improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]
- Critical thinking: analyzing peers' work against criteria deepens understanding of quality itself
- Intergroup competence: working across difference with respect and candor [Building empathy improves intergroup attitudes among students.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Co-construct norms.** With students, define what helpful, respectful feedback sounds like; anchor to a protocol such as "kind, specific, helpful."
2. **Teach feedback as a skill.** Model critique on anonymous or teacher-authored work before students critique each other; use [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) routines to surface how critique is landing emotionally.
3. **Structure the exchange.** Run feedback rounds using [Peer Interaction](../elements/peer-interaction.md) structures and a timed protocol; the presenter listens without defending.
4. **Require revision.** Every feedback cycle must end with [Revision](../elements/revision.md) time so students witness growth — the equity payoff is seeing that ability is built, not fixed.
5. **Make growth visible.** Compare drafts publicly (portfolio, wall of drafts) so the community sees iteration as the norm, not remediation.
6. **Rotate roles.** Ensure every student gives and receives feedback across the project; monitor participation patterns for status effects and rebalance grouping accordingly.

### Personalization
- Let students choose feedback modality — written, oral, video, or annotated — and whether to receive feedback anonymously first
- Offer sentence stems and translated stems for multilingual students
- Allow choice of critique partner early on, moving toward assigned mixed groupings as norms solidify

### Assessment Evidence
- Quality and specificity of feedback given (against a rubric)
- Evidence of substantive revision between drafts
- Student-reported belonging and psychological safety (surveys, [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) data)
- Relationship and connection indicators: cross-group feedback pairings over time

### Impact
- More supportive classroom culture and stronger relationships among students
- Improved work quality through iterative improvement [Feedback that answers "Where am I going, how am I doing, and where to next?" improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]
- Increased sense of agency: every student experiences shaping others' work and being shaped by peers
- Growth mindset norms: revision makes improvement observable, countering fixed-ability narratives that disproportionately harm students of color

### Affordances
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — peer feedback enacts this principle by making success criteria visible and actionable mid-project, when students can still use them
- [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) — feedback rounds give every member a defined, interdependent role rather than free-riding group work
- [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md) — critiquing a peer's work requires perspective-taking about their intentions and effort
- [Communities of Practice](../principles/communities-of-practice.md) — shared critique norms and public drafts make the classroom function as a novice community with common standards of quality

## Examples
- **EL Education (Expeditionary Learning)** — EL's PBL model builds "kind, specific, helpful" critique and multiple draft cycles into every expedition, with public galleries of drafts ([https://eleducation.org](https://eleducation.org))
- **High Tech High (San Diego)** — project critique sessions and exhibition rehearsals where students give structured peer feedback before public presentation ([https://www.hightechhigh.org](https://www.hightechhigh.org))
- **School Reform Initiative Critical Friends Groups** — trained facilitators run protocols in which presenters receive timed, normed feedback from peers ([https://www.schoolreforminitiative.org](https://www.schoolreforminitiative.org))
