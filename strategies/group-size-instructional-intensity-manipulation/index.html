Manipulate instructional group size to vary treatment intensity in intervention research¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study manipulates group size as a proxy of instructional intensity to examine hypothesized mechanisms of change, using a treatment intensity framework centered on specifying the "active ingredients" of an intervention. ROOTS groups with a 5:1 ratio were treated as low intensity and 2:1 groups as high intensity, while holding materials, lesson pacing, and implementation plan constant except for group size. This allows direct examination of intensity effects within a single study.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Identical intervention materials and implementation plan across intensity conditions
  • A low-inference observation instrument to measure instructional interactions

Constraints¶

  • Few, if any, prior mathematics intervention studies have directly manipulated instructional intensity within a single study, per the authors

Target Learners¶

  • kindergarten students at risk for mathematics difficulties

Target Learning Goals¶

  • increasing intensity and frequency of student-teacher instructional interactions

Claims¶

  • An earlier preliminary investigation of ROOTS found small-to-medium positive effects on kindergarten mathematics achievement measures [+W]
  • Preliminary COSTI observations suggest students in ROOTS-small (2:1) groups received rates of practice opportunities greater than or equal to peers in ROOTS-large (5:1) groups [+W]
  • Year 1 of the ROOTS efficacy trial used a classroom-blocked RCT assigning at-risk kindergartners to ROOTS-large, ROOTS-small, or no-treatment control conditions [~W] (the trial design behind these findings)
