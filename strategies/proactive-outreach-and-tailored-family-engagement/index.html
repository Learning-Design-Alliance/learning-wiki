Schools use proactive outreach, flexible tailoring for elevated hyperactivity, and family feedback on intervention acceptability¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Drawing on its participation findings, the article recommends that schools counter assumptions that families are disinterested by using proactive outreach, since most families offered the FCU voluntarily completed a feedback session. It states "Schools may wish to use flexible and responsive approaches such as the FCU so they can tailor their approach with families", particularly for children with elevated hyperactivity, and suggests seeking family feedback through dialogue such as family focus groups and promoting home-to-school planning and parent-teacher communication during sessions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interventions families find acceptable, since treatment acceptability significantly predicted participation

Constraints¶

  • The school-engagement effect was not significant at one-year follow-up, so the authors frame school-focused strategies as avenues to encourage future change rather than demonstrated effects

Target Learners¶

  • families of kindergarten and early elementary school children, especially families of children with elevated hyperactivity behavior

Target Learning Goals¶

  • family-school engagement at home and school and family participation in family-centered interventions

Claims¶

  • Most families offered the FCU participated through feedback (87%), but fewer than half completed follow-up treatment sessions (49%) [+W]
  • Child hyperactivity behavior and caregiver perception of the FCU predicted treatment time, with hyperactivity the stronger predictor [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Leverage culturally-specific engagement strengths: support school participation for Spanish-speaking families and oral storytelling for English-speaking families
