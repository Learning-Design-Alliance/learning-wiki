# Use a justify-every-option prompt on multiple-choice questions as a candidate activity for helping students access productive alternative ideas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A specific implementable activity drawn from the article's stated future direction: "One future direction i s to examine whether our prompt to provide a justification for each multiple -choice option could enhance learning by helping students access these productive ideas." The strategy asks students, for each multiple-choice option, to generate the most convincing explanation someone selecting it might hold, regardless of their own belief, so that productive resources become accessible for learning rather than remaining uncaptured.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must attempt a convincing justification for every option, including options they do not believe, with follow-up questioning to ensure all options receive explanations.
#### Constraints
- The article frames learning benefits as untested: it proposes to examine whether the prompt could enhance learning, and the reported study measured generated alternatives and beliefs, not learning gains.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- accessing productive alternative ideas and correct reasoning elements during conceptual physics learning
