# Foster Self-Advocacy and Self-Determination

## Description
This strategy develops learners' capacity to understand their needs and rights, communicate them effectively, set and monitor goals, solve problems, and make decisions about their own learning. It is carried out through explicit instruction in self-advocacy skills, structured opportunities to practice them in authentic settings (e.g., leading IEP meetings, negotiating accommodations), and gradual transfer of decision-making responsibility from teacher to student. Self-determination theory provides the motivational grounding: autonomy, competence, and relatedness are basic needs whose support predicts intrinsic motivation and engagement [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S].

## Design Implications

Self-advocacy instruction works because it builds both the motivational conditions for engagement (autonomy and perceived competence) and the self-regulatory skills that sustain it [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]. The strategy is not simply "giving students choice" — effective programs pair autonomy with explicit skill instruction and supported practice, then fade adult direction as competence grows [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit instruction in component skills: self-awareness (understanding one's disability, strengths, and needs), knowledge of rights and accommodations, goal setting, and communication scripts
- Authentic practice settings where advocacy has real stakes — IEP/transition meetings, accommodation requests, work-based learning placements
- A supportive environment in which adults genuinely cede decision-making authority rather than performing consultation
- Progress monitoring structures so students experience the competence that sustains motivation [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Autonomy support without skill instruction can leave struggling learners adrift; unguided choice benefits students with weak self-regulation less than structured autonomy [~M]
- One-off lessons or assemblies do not produce durable self-determination; effects depend on sustained, embedded practice across settings [-W]
- Environments that punish disclosure (e.g., stigma around accommodations) suppress advocacy behavior even when skills are taught [-M]
- Students with significant support needs may require extensive scaffolding and communication supports; a "sink or swim" interpretation of empowerment is counterproductive [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Student-led IEPs** (e.g., the *Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction*, SDLMI): students prepare and lead their own transition planning meetings
- **Embedded advocacy curricula**: programs such as *I'm Determined* (Virginia DOE) integrate goal setting and decision-making into general coursework
- **Postsecondary preparation**: explicit instruction in disclosing disability and requesting accommodations before college entry
- **Peer mentoring**: older students with disabilities model advocacy for younger ones

### Target Learners
- Adolescents with disabilities preparing for transition to postsecondary education, employment, and independent living [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Students with weak self-regulation, who benefit from explicit goal-setting and self-monitoring instruction [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- All learners benefit from autonomy support, but effects are strongest for those whose motivation is currently controlled rather than intrinsic [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-awareness and metacognition: understanding one's needs, strengths, and rights
- Self-regulation: goal setting, monitoring, and strategy adjustment [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Communication and problem-solving: articulating needs and negotiating support
- Motivational outcomes: task value and engagement through perceived autonomy and relevance [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Build self-knowledge.** Guide students through structured reflection on their strengths, needs, and rights (individual reflection, portfolios).
2. **Teach component skills explicitly.** Model goal setting, communication scripts, and problem-solving steps; use [Practice](../elements/practice.md) with role-play before real-world application.
3. **Set goals with students, not for them.** Use process goals for novices rather than distant outcome goals [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M].
4. **Provide authentic advocacy opportunities.** Have students lead IEP/transition meetings, request accommodations, and make real choices about learning tasks.
5. **Fade adult direction.** Transfer decision-making responsibility progressively as students demonstrate competence [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].
6. **Reflect and monitor.** Build in regular [Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md) where students evaluate progress toward self-set goals and adjust strategies.

## Related Strategies
- Goal-setting instruction — the self-regulatory engine of self-determination
- Self-monitoring and self-evaluation — the monitoring loop that sustains goal pursuit
- Student-led conferencing — a structured authentic advocacy setting

## Examples
- **[Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction (SDLMI)](https://www.ou.edu/education/zarrow/sdlmi)** — a teacher-implemented model in which students set, act toward, and self-evaluate goals; validated across disability categories in secondary settings.
- **[I'm Determined](https://www.imdetermined.org)** (Virginia Department of Education) — state-level initiative with student-led IEP templates, "good day plans," and youth summits.
- **Student-led IEP meetings** — students present their strengths, needs, and goals at their own transition planning meetings, a widely replicated practice in secondary special education.
