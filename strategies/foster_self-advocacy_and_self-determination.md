# Foster Self-Advocacy and Self-Determination

## Description
This strategy emphasizes the importance of developing self-advocacy and self-determination skills in students, particularly those with disabilities. It involves teaching students to understand their needs and rights, communicate them effectively, solve problems, set goals, and engage in active reflection. The goal is to empower students to become the primary agents in their learning and lives, improving their educational and life outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit instruction on self-advocacy and self-determination skills, opportunities to practice these skills in real-world settings, a supportive and inclusive environment, and a commitment to empowering students to make choices about their learning and lives.
#### Constraints
- Requires a shift in mindset towards valuing student agency and empowerment. Some students may need significant support and encouragement to develop these skills. Success depends on consistent reinforcement and a commitment to creating a learning environment where students feel valued and respected.

### Target Learners

- Students with disabilities, students preparing for postsecondary education and the workforce, and learners who benefit from self-directed learning

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance understanding of individual needs and rights, improve communication and problem-solving skills, promote goal setting and monitoring, foster self-reflection, and empower students to take control of their learning and lives.

### Affordances
- Empowers learners to understand their needs and rights, promotes effective communication and problem-solving, fosters self-reflection and goal setting, enhances self-determination, and improves educational and life outcomes.

### Personalization
- The specific skills and strategies taught can be tailored to the individual needs and goals of the students. Educators can provide differentiated support and scaffolding to ensure that all students can participate effectively.
