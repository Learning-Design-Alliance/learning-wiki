Use multisensory audio-visual games to train transversal skills alongside curricular content¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends structuring activities as multisensory (audio-visual) games so children can use their preferred sensory modality, promoting inclusion of sensory-impaired students. As printed: "We structured the virtual archaeological area activities around multisensory (i.e., audio-visual) educational experiences to enhance engagement and develop transversal skills." The four Scratch games target visual and auditory short-term memory, eye-hand coordination, rhythm training inspired by Stambak's methods, and logical reasoning, while reinforcing classroom concepts such as the golden ratio.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Games must provide audio-visual sensory feedback
  • Supervision by a teacher or experimenter to prevent frustration among children who might struggle

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • Primary school children, including blind or deaf students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Visual and auditory short-term memory, eye-hand coordination, rhythm, logical reasoning, teamwork
