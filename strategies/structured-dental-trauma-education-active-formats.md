# Incorporate structured dental traumatology content into the medical curriculum using active, practical formats such as simulations, interactive case studies, and workshops

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend systematically incorporating dental trauma education into the medical curriculum, arguing current coverage is "marginal and insufficiently structured" despite the critical need for prompt intervention. They call for integrated educational approaches that blend theoretical knowledge with clinical practice, specifically naming simulations, interactive case studies, and workshops, and for utilizing active learning methods. The TDI-KNOW Index can be used to assess the impact of such targeted training interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curriculum time must be allocated to dental traumatology content within medical education programs
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a single-faculty cross-sectional study; attendance at existing classes was self-reported and showed no significant score difference

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- medical doctors from various specialities

### Target Learning Goals
- First aid for dental traumatic injuries, including replantation decisions and transport media for avulsed teeth
