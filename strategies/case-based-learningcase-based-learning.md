# Case Based Learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Case based learning places learners in the role of analyst or decision-maker confronting a realistic scenario — a patient presentation, a business dilemma, a legal dispute, a classroom incident. Learners work through the case individually or in groups, applying concepts to diagnose problems, weigh options, and justify decisions. It is carried out through structured case discussion, written case analysis, or sequenced case sets that reveal information progressively.

## Design Implications

Cases situate abstract principles in concrete, messy contexts, forcing learners to retrieve and apply knowledge rather than recognize it [Case based learning improves exam performance in health professions education.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]. Learning depends on the quality of the discussion and analysis, not the case document itself: cases must be paired with prompts that elicit reasoning and [Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md), since a case read passively produces little transfer [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. Because real problems are ill-structured, multiple contrasting cases are more effective than a single case for building flexible, adaptable knowledge [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-documented, realistic case with genuine decision points and no obvious single answer
- Preparation materials that establish the prerequisite concepts before analysis begins
- Structured discussion protocols or analysis frameworks (e.g., issue → analysis → recommendation)
- A facilitator skilled at probing reasoning rather than lecturing ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md), [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
- Closure: an expert debrief or model analysis so learners can calibrate their reasoning

#### Constraints
- Cases without closure or expert comparison leave novices with unresolved misconceptions — learners may encode flawed reasoning as readily as sound reasoning [-M]
- Novices without sufficient domain knowledge can flounder in unstructured case discussion, engaging in trial-and-error search rather than principled analysis [~M]
- A single memorable case can induce overgeneralization; learners may treat one context's solution as universal [-W]
- Group discussion dominated by a few voices reduces the retrieval practice that drives the effect [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Progressive disclosure** (Harvard method): information is released in stages; learners must commit to decisions before seeing consequences
- **Contrasting case sets**: several short cases varying one dimension, supporting abstraction of underlying principles
- **Erroneous or flawed cases**: learners diagnose what went wrong, leveraging error analysis
- **Student-authored cases**: learners construct cases from their own experience, shifting the goal toward synthesis

### Target Learners
- Advanced novices and intermediate learners who have foundational concepts and need to learn application [Case based learning improves exam performance in health professions education.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]
- Professional learners (medicine, law, business, teaching) preparing for ill-structured real-world decisions [Whole-task performance improves transfer.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [+M]
- Pure novices benefit less without scaffolding; the [expertise reversal effect](../theories/expertise-reversal-effect.md) means heavy case-based formats can outstrip what beginners can extract [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Applied reasoning and diagnosis: connecting principles to ambiguous situations
- Decision-making under uncertainty: weighing trade-offs and justifying choices
- Flexible knowledge representation: seeing how the same concept manifests across contexts [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M]
- Professional identity and judgment: practicing the role, not just the content

### Instructions
1. Assign preparatory readings or a mini-lecture establishing the concepts the case will exercise ([Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md), [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
2. Present the case with an explicit analysis framework and clear deliverable (recommendation, diagnosis, plan) ([Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md))
3. Have small groups analyze the case, articulating reasoning at each decision point ([Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md), [Articulation](../elements/articulation.md))
4. Facilitate whole-class comparison of group analyses, probing for underlying principles rather than verdicts ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
5. Close with an expert debrief or model analysis so learners compare their reasoning to expert reasoning ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))
6. Sequence a second, contrasting case to test whether learners transfer the principle to a new context ([Application of Knowledge](../elements/application-of-knowledge.md))

## Examples
- **Harvard Business School case method** — MBA students analyze a new case daily in section discussion, with cold-calling to ensure broad participation; the canonical implementation of progressive-disclosure case teaching
- **[National Center for Case Study Teaching in Science](https://www.nsta.org/national-center-case-study-teaching-science)** — Peer-reviewed science cases (e.g., "The Case of the Tainted Taco Shells") with teaching notes, used across undergraduate STEM
- **Problem-based medical curricula** (e.g., [McMaster University](https://mdprogram.mcmaster.ca)) — Patient cases drive tutorial-based learning of basic and clinical sciences
