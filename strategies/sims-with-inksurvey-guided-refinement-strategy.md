# Pair interactive computer simulations with InkSurvey probe questions to guide student re-exploration of the sim without over- or under-scaffolding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In advanced undergraduate chemical engineering and physics courses, students first explore an interactive simulation independently, learning all they can on their own. The instructor then uses a series of InkSurvey questions in class to probe student understanding, identify points of difficulty, and guide students back to the sim for further exploration or testing of concepts. This avoids both the too-little-guidance failure, where students may not construct correct understandings, and the too-much-guidance "cookbook" atmosphere that discourages self-exploration. Because the same devices serve both purposes, the guided refinement is seamless.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have the same pen-enabled devices for both exploring simulations and submitting responses
- A curated set of simulations relevant to the course concepts

### Target Learners

### Target Learning Goals
- deep conceptual understanding that transfers to application in other situations
- self-exploration of difficult concepts
