# Institutions should implement structured perinatal bereavement education, tailored by department, alongside institutional resources such as bereavement suites and CuddleCots

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the identified gaps, the article recommends that institutions implement evidence-based educational programs for frontline staff, including perinatal bereavement workshops and simulation training, and ensure access to institutional resources such as mental health services for staff, bereavement care pathways, and bereavement suites located external to the maternity unit. It also suggests collaborative approaches involving nursing, social workers, mental health professionals, and chaplaincy, and reports the hospital is exploring a bereavement suite and values tools like CuddleCots and its bereavement committee.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Data-driven needs assessment to identify educational gaps before formalizing protocols or workflows
- Education should be tailored to the unique demands of each clinical setting, since confidence differed between L&D and ED staff
#### Constraints
- Evidence for the recommended programs comes from cited external studies and staff open comments, not from an intervention tested in this study

### Target Learners
- Frontline hospital staff (L&D, ED, Maternity), including residents and attendings

### Target Learning Goals
- Perinatal bereavement care knowledge, skills, confidence, and self-awareness
