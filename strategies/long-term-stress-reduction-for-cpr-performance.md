# Test long-term stress-reduction interventions and HRV-informed support rather than brief pre-task breathing for CPR performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because 3 minutes of slow-paced breathing showed no benefit, the authors recommend that "In future studies, it is recommended to test the effect of long-term stress reduction interventions on the emergency teams' CPR performance". They also suggest health systems may develop AI tools combining HRV monitoring with routine high-quality training, and that "health professionals with low HRV could benefit from increasing their HRV by biofeedback or by having more training to improve their performance".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions of longer duration than the 3-minute breathing task used in this study, since the article cites evidence that breathing durations longer than 5 minutes showed marginal effects on subjective outcomes
#### Constraints
- As a simulator study, caution is needed in drawing conclusions about real-life CPR, where stress levels would plausibly be higher

### Target Learners
- emergency medical teams
- paramedic students and medics

### Target Learning Goals
- improving pediatric CPR performance under stress
