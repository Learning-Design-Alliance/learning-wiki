Identify students' cognitive style before choosing between inquiry and conventional teaching¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that elementary science teachers first diagnose students' cognitive-style tendency and then adapt their teaching model accordingly: "The teacher should always identify the tendency in the cognitive style, that the students have before starting to teach. In this way the teacher can modify the method or model of teaching based on the student cognitive styles." The MFFT is the instrument the study used for this diagnosis, classifying students as reflective or impulsive from response time and accuracy.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A cognitive-style instrument such as the MFFT must be administered before instruction
  • Teachers must be able to switch between guided inquiry and more teacher-centered formats

Constraints¶

  • The study's evidence base is fifth-grade science in Singaraja City; the authors call for replication with larger samples, other science topics, and other psychological variables

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students
  • fifth-grade science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • science learning achievement

Claims¶

  • Guided inquiry's advantage over conventional teaching holds for reflective but not impulsive students [+M]
  • Guided inquiry learning yields higher fifth-grade science achievement than conventional teaching [+M]
  • Reflective students outperform impulsive students in science learning achievement [+M]
  • Teaching model and cognitive style interact significantly in their effect on science achievement [+M]
