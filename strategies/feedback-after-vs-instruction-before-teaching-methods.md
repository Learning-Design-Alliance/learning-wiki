# Choose between feedback-after-response and instruction-before-response teaching methods based on participants' background

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The review identifies two teaching methods for delivering evacuation knowledge in IVR SGs. Feedback after response lets participants learn from mistakes, in immediate in-game form or as post-game feedback; instructions before response tell participants what to do ahead of events, which can reduce fear and stress. The framework states "There are two teaching methods that are suitable for different target groups , which are feedback after response and instr uctions before responses . Both methods are proven effective." Selection criteria are participants' background and the game narrative method.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback is fundamental to enhancing the knowledge acquisition of participants because it allows them to learn from mistakes and rectify responses
#### Constraints
- The difference of the efficiency between each method is still lacking in the literature; in the reviewed papers both teaching methods were applied only in accordance with participants' background

### Target Learners
- building occupants including children aged 7–11 in fire-safety training

### Target Learning Goals
- evacuation knowledge acquisition
- self-protection skills
- spatial knowledge
