# Use the two-challenge rule to support nurses in respectfully asserting patient-safety concerns with physicians

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The two-challenge rule is a communication technique taught in the PRONTO curriculum for respectfully asserting disagreement with superiors when there is a patient safety concern. Mentors described it as a way for nurses to advocate for evidence-based treatment despite hierarchy; the article reports that "some mentees are doing [the] two challenge rule with doctors," for example arguing for nifedipine rather than only Lasix. Clinical discussions provided a formal setting that fostered teamwork and institutional support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Physician buy-in and a setting for clinical discussions between doctors and nurses
#### Constraints
- Strict hierarchy between nurses and doctors was perceived by all mentors as a significant barrier in this setting

### Target Learners
- Nurse mentees and physicians at primary health centers

### Target Learning Goals
- Teamwork, communication, and advocacy for evidence-based PE/E management
