Institutions and their representatives should work to build and repair adolescents' institutional trust to reduce dropout risk¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that institutional trust predicts dropout, the authors recommend that scientific and societal conversations address not only students' institutional trust but also what institutions and their representatives can do to increase and possibly repair adolescents' trust. The article states that "Trust develops when (representatives of) institutions are trustworthy, when they show honesty, reliability, and competence". Investigating the factors that facilitate or undermine trust may inform policymakers and potential interventions to reduce dropout.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutions and their representatives must demonstrate honesty, reliability, and competence, since the article states trust develops when institutions are trustworthy

Constraints¶

  • The study's evidence is correlational and specific to tertiary vocational schools in the Netherlands; the article notes it is unclear whether results generalize to other educational levels and countries

Target Learners¶

  • Adolescents in tertiary vocational education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • School retention and educational attainment

Claims¶

  • Financial scarcity is indirectly associated with dropout through lower institutional trust [+W]
  • Experienced financial scarcity, but not perceived family wealth, predicts school dropout among tertiary vocational students [+W]
  • Perceived family wealth shows a small indirect negative effect on dropout via institutional trust, with no total effect [+W]
  • The indirect effects of family wealth on dropout via institutional trust are robust to controlling for mental health problems [+W]
  • System justification does not mediate the family wealth–dropout association once institutional trust is accounted for [+W]
