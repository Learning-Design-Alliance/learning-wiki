# Integrate case-based learning, supervised clinical simulations and e-learning modules aligned with WHO and CDC standards into infection control curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends strengthening infection control education in Sudanese medical schools through combined theoretical and practical formats. It states that "The integration of case-based learning, supervised clinical simulations and e-learning modules aligned with WHO and CDC standards could bridge current knowledge and practice gaps." The strategy targets the observed gaps in hand hygiene, PPE sequencing and sterile field management, and pairs curricular change with improved supervision and resource access.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Alignment with WHO and CDC standards
- Both theoretical and practical training formats
- Adequate access to PPE and infection control supplies
#### Constraints
- The article notes current clinical training environments may not adequately reinforce positive attitudes toward infection prevention, and self-reported data limit assessment of long-term behavioral change

### Target Learners
- Clinical-year medical students (4th–6th year) on surgical rotations in LMIC settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Infection prevention and control competency in surgical settings, including hand hygiene, PPE use, sterile technique and post-exposure response
