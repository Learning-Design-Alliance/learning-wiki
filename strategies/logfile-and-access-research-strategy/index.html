Use log-file data and stakeholder-access research to refine learning-analytics feedback¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's future-research recommendations are concrete next steps for learning-analytics feedback design. "Besides using questionnaires, log-file data (e.g., mouse clicks representing visits of the JIT and VIZ-modules) can be used to examine whether a specific module or a combination of both modules is most beneficial for learning." Further research should examine which kinds of curricula and work domains are amenable to analytics-enhanced E-portfolios, and whether and in what cases a supervisor should have maximum access to feedback messages in order to stimulate learners' self-assessment and reflection.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Research designs must account for differences in program duration and the resulting amount and type of data that can be gathered per student

Constraints¶

  • The article states future research should reveal whether E-portfolios enhanced with learning analytics actually can stimulate learning at the workplace; this has not yet been shown

Target Learners¶

  • learners, peers and supervisors in professional education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-assessment, reflection, and learning from personalized workplace feedback

Claims¶

  • Students perceived the automated personalized feedback as too general and lacking personal response [+W]
  • Supervisor experts considered the visualization module more useful than the just-in-time feedback module, but endorsed a revised JIT design after redesign [+W]
  • Students and supervisors in medical, veterinary and teacher education perceived the learning-analytics-enhanced E-portfolio as useful for monitoring professional development [+W]
