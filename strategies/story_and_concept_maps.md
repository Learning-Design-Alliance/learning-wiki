# Story and Concept Maps

## Description
Story and concept maps are visual representations used to organize information and enhance comprehension, particularly of narratives and complex relationships. Story maps focus on key story elements like characters, setting, problem, and solution. Concept maps show relationships between words and phrases. These maps support prewriting and postreading activities, summarizing main ideas, and identifying key details in both fiction and nonfiction texts. Learners create, write, draw, and organize information from a text into meaning with a dynamic graphic organizer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Story or informational text, blank story or concept map templates (paper or digital), colored pencils/markers (optional)
#### Constraints
- May be time-consuming, requires explicit instruction and modeling, can be overwhelming for learners if not properly scaffolded, effectiveness depends on the quality of teacher guidance and support

## Assessment Evidence
- Evaluate the completeness and accuracy of the story or concept map, assess the depth of understanding demonstrated by the connections made between elements, track learner progress in identifying key details and summarizing main ideas

## Impact
- Research indicates that story maps improve comprehension by helping learners organize story elements and summarize main ideas. (Reutzel, 1985). Graphic organizers, in general, support cognition and literacy skills by helping learners visualize how ideas fit together.
