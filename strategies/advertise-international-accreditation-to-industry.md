# Accredited programs in developing countries should actively advertise international accreditation to industry and encourage graduates to promote the distinction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that academia in developing countries work to publicize the international accreditations their programs attain, so that the labor market learns the value of hiring graduates from accredited programs. The authors state: "we believe that academia, in the particular case of the developing countries, should work better to advertise the international accreditations attained". Participants suggested low-cost channels such as social network postings and professional networks like Linkedin.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must have attained an international accreditation to advertise
#### Constraints
- One participant noted advertising costs could be very expensive and the university may lack the financial resources for it

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students and recent graduates in developing countries

### Target Learning Goals
- employability of graduates
- industry awareness of program quality
