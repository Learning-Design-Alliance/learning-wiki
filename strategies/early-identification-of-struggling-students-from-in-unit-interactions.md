# Identify struggling students early from in-unit assignment interactions and provide timely personalized support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends using students' interactions with in-unit assignments to identify struggling students before they take the final unit assignment. Because struggling students requested more hints, answers and explanations while showing lower engagement, the authors suggest that "if teachers provide them with suitable support (e.g. explanations and tutoring) they could potentially answer the final assignments more successfully", and recommend timely personalized support and interventions, self-directed learning strategies, and online tutorials or educational apps.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to students' in-unit assignment clickstream actions (hint, answer and explanation requests, engagement actions) before the end-unit assignment
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from middle school math students on the ASSISTments platform during 2019-2023; the article notes generalizability to other courses and datasets should be checked

### Target Learners
- middle school mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- improving performance on end-unit math assignments
