Credit-point assignment complex with student-selected deliverables in a projective-recursive history of computer science course¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

In the history of computer science course at KSPU and KazNPU, learning followed the projective and recursive strategy described as "I create a didactic means and I study by means of it", combined with individual learning roadmaps. Students accumulated credit points from a menu of activities: lecture attendance and digital notes, seminar work, creating an author website on the history of computer science, building multimedia exhibits for a virtual museum, authoring test assignments in the Testosfera system or digital form, presentations, and analytical reports, each with printed point values. A student needs at least 100 points to get a credit, and lectures were broadcast online and recorded for offline viewing.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A credit threshold of at least 100 points, with point values printed for each assignment type
  • Online-broadcast, recorded lectures uploaded to the university network so students can choose their form of classes

Constraints¶

  • The strategy was applied with 4th-year bachelor's students of the physics and mathematics profile in a history of computer science course, studied within an international inter-university cluster

Target Learners¶

  • senior pedagogical-university students of the physics and mathematics profile

Target Learning Goals¶

  • history of computer science content mastery
  • creating didactic means such as websites, tests and museum exhibits
  • self-planned progress toward a credit threshold

Affordances¶

  • Methodological System Blended Learning Computer Science

Claims¶

  • In the projective-recursive history of computer science course, 90% of senior students overcame the 100-point credit limit two weeks before the deadline, though only 5% initially planned the credit-securing website route [+M]
