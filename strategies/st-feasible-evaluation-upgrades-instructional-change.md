# Adopt minimal, feasible evaluation upgrades—COPUS documentation, student-level covariates, and cluster-aware analysis—when evaluating instructional change

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
For departments continuing an instructional change initiative, the article recommends a short list of low-cost evaluation improvements for future terms: routine COPUS-based classroom documentation, collection of a few administrative student-level covariates, a simple cluster-aware analysis plan once enough AL sections exist, and moving beyond pass/fail to concept inventories or common item-level measures. The article states that "Minimal upgrades that would substantially improve interpretability include: (i) routine COPUS-based documentation of classroom practice across a larger sample of meetings and sections to provide a clearer fidelity record". A qualitative study of the change process using Reinholz's four frames is also underway.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Data collection kept feasible within routine departmental reporting
- Enough AL sections available before between-section variability can be estimated credibly
#### Constraints
- Aggregated section-level counts alone cannot adjust for student self-selection, clustering, or instructor and scheduling effects

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in gateway STEM courses

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting passing outcomes to conceptual learning and documenting implementation fidelity
