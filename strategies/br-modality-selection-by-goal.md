# Select bellringer modality by examination goals: 2D images to reduce cost, VR for remote testing, physical specimens for fine structures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 unmarked) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's future-directions section recommends that educators choose examination modalities based on their goals, since neither modality nor preference affected performance. It states that "testing using 2D images may help reduce expenses compared to VR and physical modalities, while having no adverse impact on testing performance," and that VR "may also be a viable alternative testing option for remote education." It further proposes that physical specimens may better represent fine structures whose details are diminished by VR rendering or 2D imaging.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Alignment between the chosen modality and the examination's goals, per the authors' recommendation to consider examination goals when employing novel evaluation modes
#### Constraints
- The fine-structure suitability suggestion is conditional: the authors write it holds "If trends seen among poorly performing questions hold true," and the study did not examine modality performance within specific anatomical themes or organ systems

### Target Learning Goals
