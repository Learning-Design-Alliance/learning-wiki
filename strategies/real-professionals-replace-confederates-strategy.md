# Use unbriefed real professionals as supporting team members to reduce pedagogical staffing in interprofessional simulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete staffing recipe drawn from the Control condition: instead of trained confederates, staff the simulation with working Emergency Department nurses and medical support workers who were "not informed beforehand of the outcome of the scenario enabling a spontaneous attitude as a real life". The authors suggest "Not using confederates could reduce the pedagogical staff mobilized in full-scale simulation and improve the implementation of interprofessional simulation but still preserve participants' performances."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supporting professionals need at least equivalent clinical experience (in the study, at least 5 years of practice)
- Participants must not know the professionals in advance or be able to identify whether they are confederates
#### Constraints
- The impact of confederates' presence during the debriefing phase was not studied
- Real professionals could take initiatives and suggest therapeutics, unlike the scripted confederates

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine residents in interprofessional team simulation

### Target Learning Goals
- Team management in crisis situations and CRM skills
