# Use an independent community of practice approach with multiple methods to develop a national competency framework

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Rather than a top-down 'policy community' process or translating an existing foreign framework, the article recommends a community of practice approach: "we chose a community of practice approach in which a dedicated research group analyzed various resources, including data from interviews and FG sessions with representatives of relevant stakeholders". A voluntary, pluriform research group of practitioners and experts worked 2.5 years across 34 sessions under principles of autonomy, neutrality, pluriformity and topic expertise, with national associations preparing the framework's 'welcome landing'. The authors offer this as a transferable route map while cautioning that other approaches may be more effective and that cultural differences warrant tailored, hybrid designs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Close involvement of national medical associations
- a nationally active community of practice
- voluntary work of diverse researchers and practitioners
- a multi-method research design to circumvent reverberation of established opinions
#### Constraints
- Whether this approach is more effective than alternatives remains an open question requiring further research; access to national published sources and cultural differences may affect transferability

### Target Learners
- Physician researchers
- medical professional associations
- framework developers in other countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Developing a national professional competency framework
- avoiding reinforcement of dominant professional positions
