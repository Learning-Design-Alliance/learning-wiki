# Coach residents in adaptive, problem-focused coping strategies for perceived over- and underentrustment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, where ideal staffing and supervision are unrealistic, clinical educators should coach residents to use the most adaptive coping strategies. The authors state that "clinical educators should help residents use the most effective coping strategies to maintain their well-being, thrive as learners, and provide safe patient care," and that coaching strategies should include organizational aspects so residents can use problem-focused coping. Context-specific coaching in postgraduate training should be provided.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coaching strategies should include organizational aspects to ensure residents can use problem-focused coping strategies, as the article states.
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a fallback when staffing cannot be improved, not a substitute for adequate staffing and supervision.

### Target Learners
- clinical residents in postgraduate medical education

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining resident well-being while learning in the clinical workplace
- safe patient care under entrustment
