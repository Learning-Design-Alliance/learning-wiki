# Future cooperative learning studies should be longitudinal and qualitative, using attitude questionnaires, interviews, and objective computerized pronunciation measures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that future studies investigate the affective and incubated benefits of cooperative learning that short-term quantitative designs miss. It proposes "pre- and post-intervention questionnaires with both Likert-scaled and open-ended questions regarding subjects' attitudes toward phonetic instruction," plus interviews, focus groups, class journals, and in-class audio recordings focusing on social skills. It also recommends computerized instruments such as Audacity and an additional pair of evaluators to increase objectivity of data collection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A longitudinal qualitative design to capture incubated outcomes not observable within a short-term experiment
#### Constraints
- Academic improvement from cooperative learning may not last longer than the incubated affective benefits; subsequent outcomes may not be evident in the short term

### Target Learners
- English language learners in ESL pronunciation settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Oral proficiency and pronunciation of ESL learners
- affective outcomes such as lowered anxiety and caring relationships
