# Frame collaborative learning as a game and build trust in teammates' ability to increase collaboration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In its educational suggestions, the article recommends treating collaboration through a game-theoretic lens: "Viewing collaborative learning as a game, where the whole team must collaborate to win the game, can be beneficial in a context similar to the one presented in the paper." It also states that "Collaboration increases if students have trust in each other's ability to solve the problem at hand," and that game theory can be used to assess collaboration between students. This draws on the literature point that cooperation relies on "thick trust" among agents who know each other.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have trust in each other's ability to solve the problem at hand
- Instructors must assess collaboration, e.g., via game-theoretic modeling of member contributions
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is offered for contexts similar to the one presented in the paper, a freshman course with biweekly group sessions

### Target Learners
- university students working in small collaborative groups

### Target Learning Goals
- increasing collaboration and individual contribution in group work
