# Combine qualitative and quantitative methods with theory-based analysis when evaluating exchange programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that evaluations of international exchange programs be grounded in sociocultural learning theory and use mixed methods. They state: "We recommend that future studies combine qualitative and quantitative methods and consider the theory-based analysis of learning experiences." In their own study, the qualitative interviews shaped which Tuning competencies the quantitative questionnaire assessed, and the two datasets were combined to answer the mediation question.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A theoretical framework (here Vygotsky's sociocultural theory) to ground the evaluation
- An initial qualitative phase to identify which competencies to measure quantitatively
#### Constraints
- Without a theoretical background, the authors argue, evaluations are without scientific grounding or implications

### Target Learners
- medical students participating in international exchange programs

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating gains in job-specific and intercultural competencies from exchange participation
