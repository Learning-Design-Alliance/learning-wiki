# Divide the class into four groups, with teachers forming balanced groups for primary pupils

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For one-off hands-on STEM sessions with a single educator, the article recommends dividing the class into four groups: "dividing the class into four groups is optimal, as it allows for more focused and effective learning while allowing a single educator ... to monitor the progress of each group". Groups work as teams exchanging and comparing approaches. For primary pupils, teachers who know the students should form balanced groups; secondary students may choose their own groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A single educator per class; teacher knowledge of students to balance primary-school groups
#### Constraints
- Smaller groups or one-to-one approaches are limited by the need to engage the whole class with a single educator

### Target Learners
- Primary and secondary school students in whole-class hands-on STEM sessions

### Target Learning Goals
- Collaboration, problem-solving and focused engagement during one-off laboratory sessions
