# Balance assessor selection and clarify the developmental purpose of multisource feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
When administering multisource feedback, administrators should seek a balanced sample of assessors across job roles and make the developmental purpose of peer assessment explicit. The article states "A balanced sample of assessors should be sought when conducting MSF" and suggests "Inviting a third party to select assessors may be one solution to reduce this bias". It also recommends emphasising that feedback will not impact employment but supports professional development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessees asked to select at least 10 assessors with 2 from each job role category; actual selection ranged from 2 to 13, so third-party selection may be needed
#### Constraints
- Third-party assessor selection may not be without its own challenges
- Organisational cultures in which doctors feel uncomfortable assessing each other pose a challenge, particularly in Japan

### Target Learners
- Hospital-based doctors and the administrators running their peer assessment

### Target Learning Goals
- Reliable, low-bias multisource feedback for professional development
