# Train the trainers in learning-promoting feedback approaches, supported by quality circle meetings sharing standards of good outpatient care

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because implementation of observation-based feedback is challenging, the article recommends that the medical specialists serving as trainers be specifically trained in which approaches promote learning effectively. Table 1 summarizes recommended formats for training trainers and mentors who can support a learning-focused feedback culture, and the article states the criteria function "can be significantly supported through the regular sharing of information on standards of good outpatient care in quality circle meetings for trainers". Trainers should not act like irrefutable role models but use supervision as a source of inspiration for their own practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainers willing to scrutinize their own practices and show openness to learning
- A no-blame culture that encourages and promotes learning
#### Constraints
- The aspect of what is required of trainers should certainly be the focus of further study, per the article

### Target Learners
- medical specialists serving as trainers in post-graduate pediatric practices

### Target Learning Goals
- giving learning-focused, criteria-guided feedback
- conducting productive reflection on videotaped consultations
