# Relate specific teaching cases to the general theory to counter functional fixedness on standard examples

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's teaching implication is that instruction which highlights only specific cases — such as the electric current in a very long straight wire, or the standard charging capacitor used to introduce displacement current — fosters functional fixedness that blocks transfer to new contexts. The authors observe that students' reasoning "consistently reflect[s] reasoning based on functional fixedness associated with teaching that highlights specific cases (electric current in a very long straight wire) but do not relate the specific cases to the general theory". Teaching should therefore connect worked standard cases to the general framework of Ampère-Maxwell's law.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must present the general theory alongside the specific cases so students can recognise the displacement current and variable electric fields in non-standard contexts

### Target Learners
- undergraduate Physics and Engineering students in introductory electromagnetism

### Target Learning Goals
- recognising the displacement current in contexts beyond the standard capacitor example
- associating magnetic fields with time-varying electric fields
