Adopt automatic (opt-out) enrollment policies placing qualifying students in advanced coursework¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends districts automatically enroll students with qualifying grades or test scores in advanced coursework, with students free to opt out. It argues an "opt-out" policy rather than "opt-in" process "reduces the risk of gatekeeping practices." Evidence cited includes Washington's Tacoma schools doubling advanced enrollment and Texas's 2023 statewide math automatic enrollment law.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students with qualifying grades and/or test scores are identified for automatic placement
  • State technical support and pilot grant funding to equip administrators, counselors, and teachers
  • Extensive parent communications about the policy

Constraints¶

  • Students retain the choice to take the regular course instead
  • Notification letters alone still place the self-advocacy burden on families

Target Learners¶

  • High school students, particularly students of color and students from low-income backgrounds

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Access to and success in advanced coursework including AP, IB, and accelerated math pathways

Related Strategies¶

  • Adopt automatic enrollment and open enrollment policies for advanced courses to expand equitable access
  • State policy package to expand equitable access to advanced coursework: credit transparency, automatic enrollment with opt-out, fee removal, disaggregated data reporting, and belonging supports
  • Automatically enroll high-performing sixth graders in advanced math, with parent opt-out, to circumvent educator bias in referral
  • Automatic enrollment into advanced coursework with an opt-out option
  • Adopt automatic-enrollment policies that default students into higher-level math based on prior achievement or demonstrated proficiency
  • State leaders should eliminate dual credit fees for all students and strengthen automatic enrollment policy implementation
  • Equitable access to rigorous and culturally sustaining curricula, including automatic advanced-coursework enrollment
  • Use multiple measures, not single test cut scores, to determine eligibility for dual credit courses
