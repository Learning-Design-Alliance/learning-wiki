# Assess and Differentiate

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Assess and Differentiate is a strategy in which teachers gather evidence not only of students' content understanding but also of their academic language skills — vocabulary, syntax, and discourse functions — and use that evidence to adjust instruction. Differentiation can target the *process* (how students work and what scaffolds they receive), the *product* (how they demonstrate learning), or the *content* itself, with the goal of keeping all learners within a productive zone of challenge.

## Design Implications

Assessment only improves learning when the results actually change subsequent instruction; feedback and assessment data are most effective when they operate at the task and process levels rather than as global judgments [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. Separating language proficiency from content knowledge prevents teachers from misreading limited English proficiency as limited understanding, and allows scaffolds to be targeted to the actual bottleneck [Contingent scaffolding, calibrated to observed learner needs, improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment tools that distinguish content mastery from language proficiency (e.g., separate rubrics, language objectives alongside content objectives)
- Formative assessment routines that produce usable evidence during instruction, not only after it ([Formative Assessment](../elements/formative-assessment.md), [Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md))
- A repertoire of instructional alternatives — varied texts, grouping structures, output modes — so differentiation is possible when data reveal a need
- Reassessment to verify that differentiated support actually closed the gap ([Reassessment](../elements/reassessment.md))

#### Constraints
- Differentiation without accurate assessment becomes guesswork and can mis-scaffold, either over-supporting competent learners or under-supporting struggling ones [Contingent scaffolding, calibrated to observed learner needs, improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [-M]
- Differentiated tasks that reduce cognitive demand too far for struggling learners lower the ceiling of the curriculum rather than raising support [Reducing task difficulty for novices can remove the productive struggle needed for learning.](../claims/example-problem-sequences-reduce-cognitive-load.md) [~M]
- Time-intensive: without streamlined routines (shared rubrics, quick checks), teachers cannot sustain assessment of both content and language across a full class
- Over-differentiation can fragment the classroom community and stigmatize lower-proficiency groups if grouping is visible and static

#### Implementation Variability
- **Process differentiation**: same content, varied scaffolds — sentence frames, glossaries, [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md), or structured [Collaboration](../elements/collaboration.md)
- **Product differentiation**: same objectives, varied output — oral presentation, written report, diagram, or demonstration
- **Tiered tasks**: same essential concept at different levels of abstraction or language demand
- **Flexible grouping**: groups re-formed based on current evidence rather than fixed proficiency labels

### Target Learners
- Multilingual learners and English language learners, for whom content and language demands must be diagnosed separately
- Students with uneven profiles (strong content knowledge, weak academic language, or the reverse), who are systematically mis-served by uniform instruction
- All students benefit when assessment informs pacing and scaffold removal [Fading support as competence grows promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Content objectives: ensuring conceptual understanding is not blocked by language demand
- Academic language objectives: vocabulary, sentence structures, and discourse functions of the discipline
- Self-regulation: involving students in interpreting their own assessment data builds ownership of growth [Self-monitoring improves self-regulated learning.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Set dual objectives.** For each lesson, define the content objective and the language objective (e.g., "students will *explain* causes using causal connectives").
2. **Assess both dimensions.** Use quick diagnostic tools — entrance slips, sorting tasks, oral checks — that reveal whether errors stem from content gaps or language gaps ([Formative Assessment](../elements/formative-assessment.md), [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)).
3. **Analyze the evidence.** Sort students by need: who needs content reteaching, who needs language scaffolds, who needs both, who needs extension.
4. **Differentiate the process or product.** Assign targeted scaffolds — sentence frames, [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md), tiered texts, or choice of output mode — while keeping the essential learning goal constant.
5. **Reassess and fade.** Check whether the scaffold closed the gap, then remove it as proficiency grows so support does not become permanent ([Reassessment](../elements/reassessment.md)).

## Examples
- **WIDA-supported instruction (U.S.)** — Teachers use WIDA ACCESS proficiency scores alongside classroom formative assessment to differentiate tiered tasks and language supports ([https://wida.wisc.edu](https://wida.wisc.edu)).
- **Reading workshop with conferring** — Teachers confer one-on-one, assess a student's oral retell and use of academic vocabulary, then assign differentiated texts and sentence starters for the next cycle.
- **Tiered science labs** — All students run the same investigation; groups receive different lab-report templates (full frames, partial frames, open response) based on a quick pre-assessment of writing samples.
