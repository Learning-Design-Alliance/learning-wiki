# Adopt a stand-alone POGIL-style Bayes' theorem lesson using hand-computable two-way tables and natural frequencies instead of simulation or posterior distributions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends introducing Bayesian reasoning through two-way tables and natural frequencies students compute by hand, framing belief updating as ordinary conditional probability over hypotheses. The activity was designed "using hand-computable probabilities, two-way tables, and structured group roles so that instructors could adopt the lesson without specialized software or advanced probability prerequisites." This makes Bayesian ideas feasible in a course with limited time and minimal technical prerequisites, and the stand-alone unit can be inserted anywhere in the course sequence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need the provided student and instructor activity versions and facilitation notes; brief introductory slides on conditional probability and the frequentist-Bayesian contrast precede the activity
#### Constraints
- The lesson was implemented toward the end of the semester after data collection, description, and frequentist inference; it can serve as a springboard or a stand-alone introduction

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory statistics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Bayesian reasoning without difficult computation or simulation
- conditional probability and Bayes' theorem
