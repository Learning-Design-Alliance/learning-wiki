# Randomize subject matter to reinforced or unreinforced status within a shared quiz as its own control

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Rather than randomizing learners to an intervention, the article randomizes individual learning points to reinforcement or no reinforcement and embeds questions on both in the same anonymous quiz, undistinguished for respondents. The authors state this "eliminated unintended crossover between groups and also allowed each quiz to serve as its own control, increasing the efficiency of the design" while letting the whole housestaff participate.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each quiz must contain both intervention and control questions, and content questions must not be repeated across surveys
#### Constraints
- The authors note they had no means of determining who read the weekly or monthly Pearls

### Target Learners
- medical residents and interns in a large residency program

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring retention of didactically taught medical knowledge
