# Use pragmatic implementation and dissemination strategies to boost e-learning usage and sustainability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The ACoRD project employed a set of strategies to improve RLO usage: identifying early adopters and educator champions, integrating RLOs into existing curricula, sending reminders to educators, monitoring RLO performance and feeding back to educators, and running a dedicated dissemination team. The article reports that "RLOs addressing more general subjects such as literature review, prescription writing, and smoking cessation attracted a larger user base" than discipline-specific topics, and that collaborative partnerships boost visibility and long-term use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Engagement of institutional faculty, students, and stakeholders, which the article calls critical for successful adoption of e-learning
#### Constraints
- Strategies were implemented pragmatically, so the authors were unable to identify and measure the effectiveness of each implementation strategy

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical, pharmacy, nursing, and biomedical science students

### Target Learning Goals
- integration of digital learning resources into health care curricula
