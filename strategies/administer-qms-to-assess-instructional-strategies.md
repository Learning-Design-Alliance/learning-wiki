# Administer the QMS after instruction to evaluate courses, as a background diagnostic for graduate students, and to assess instructional strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The QMS is intended as a reusable assessment instrument: it can be given to upper-level undergraduates after instruction, used as a preliminary test for graduate students entering graduate QM courses, and used to evaluate the effectiveness of different instructional strategies, since "The survey can be administered to assess the effectiveness of various intructional strategies." Its easy administration and objective scores allow comparison of methods and populations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should have completed at least a one-semester junior-senior level QM course for the survey to be meaningful
#### Constraints
- The survey is confined to 1D quantum systems and excludes spin and Dirac notation, so it assesses only the targeted conceptual core

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate QM students
- graduate students preparing for graduate-level QM courses

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing conceptual understanding of quantum mechanics and the effectiveness of instructional strategies
