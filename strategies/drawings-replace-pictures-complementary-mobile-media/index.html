Replace on-device pictures with drawings of real objects to make mobile-device media complementary to the objects¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A concrete implementation of the complementary-media recommendation: when learners observe real objects alongside a mobile device, present schematic drawings rather than photographs on the device. The article suggests "the use of drawings to replace pictures to make the two media (drawings and objects) convey different information to students," reducing the overlap that produces redundancy effects.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A mobile learning setting in which learners simultaneously observe real objects and read on-device material about them.

Constraints¶

  • This is an authors' recommendation for future design; drawings replacing pictures were not tested in the reported experiment, and the authors note media configurations "need to be investigated before reliable conclusions can be drawn".

Target Learners¶

  • Primary school students learning with tablet-based materials in physical environments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Learning object characteristics (e.g., leaf morphology) without redundant processing of similar information

Claims¶

  • Redundancy effect on comprehension: TP and TO conditions outperformed the TPO condition in a mobile learning experiment [+M]
  • No split-attention difference in comprehension or learning efficiency between text+picture on a mobile device and text on a mobile device plus real objects [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Illustrate Through Multiple Media
  • Teachers and intervention designers should use mobile devices thoughtfully alongside other teaching approaches, ground designs in existing evidence, and evaluate rigorously
