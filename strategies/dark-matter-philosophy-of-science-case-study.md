# Teach the philosophy of science through the dark matter problem and galaxy rotation curves in mechanics courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a presentation in which students examine the dark matter problem — specifically spiral galaxy velocity rotation curves — as a case study in Bacon's induction, Popper's falsifiability, and the Duhem-Quine thesis. Because the topic is "just enough removed from everyday experience to be analyzed from a fresh perspective," students new to physics can analyze it epistemologically without the burden of misleading preconceptions they carry about terrestrial mechanics. The presentation states hypotheses clearly, makes a prediction, and asks which auxiliary hypotheses need modification.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need the requisite mathematical background for the junior-level version, or the discussion is simplified to fit at the end of a first physics course after coverage of Newton's laws and Kepler's laws
#### Constraints
- The analysis is restricted to Newtonian gravity for mathematical accessibility, even though any solution must be consistent with general relativity

### Target Learners
- students in a junior-level classical mechanics course
- students at the end of a first introductory mechanics course

### Target Learning Goals
- understand important issues in the philosophy of physics including falsifiability and the Duhem-Quine thesis
- not be afraid to challenge accepted dogma and recognize current knowledge as the current best hypothesis

- Duhem Quine Thesis
