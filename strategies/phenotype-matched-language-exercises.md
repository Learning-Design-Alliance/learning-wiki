# Match language exercise difficulty to the child's assessed receptive and expressive phenotype level

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends selecting language therapy exercises at the level just above a child's current phenotype rather than far beyond it. It states that a nonspeaking child benefits from Single-Word-expression exercises while sentence-level exercises may be counterproductive, and that a Command-level comprehender benefits from Modifier-level exercises while Syntactic-level exercises may be overly demanding. A two-dimensional classification "could better guide the selection of individualized language interventions" and support progress tracking, e.g. reporting movement from Single-Word to Single-Sentence expression.
