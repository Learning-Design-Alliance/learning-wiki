# Draw magnetic fields as decorated vectors (traditional arrow with a curved orientation arrow) as a transitional bivector-inspired representation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For instructors who find full bivector tiles too big a leap, the paper recommends drawing magnetic fields as the traditional pseudovector arrow decorated with a curved arrow indicating the corresponding bivector's orientation. These decorated vectors look distinct from electric field vectors, retain a visual relationship to current-loop sources, and remind students that force directions involve rotation in the curved arrow's plane. The author reports students respond positively to them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must supply the right-hand-rule framing connecting the decoration to the traditional vector direction
#### Constraints
- Students using the cross product with decorated vectors would in principle need to track whether results are pseudovectors

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students
- possibly high school students for planar problems

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguish magnetic from electric field representations
- determine magnetic force directions
