# Integrate simulation-based learning early into undergraduate medical curricula, particularly where clinical exposure is limited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend formally embedding simulation-based learning into undergraduate medical curricula rather than treating it as an isolated tool, arguing that "Early exposure to structured orthopedic skill development has been associated with increased specialty interest and improved preparedness for surgical clerkships." They state the findings "support the formal integration of simulation-based learning into undergraduate medical curricula, enabling universities to better introduce this approach into teaching," and suggest remote and 3D-model-based methods (IoST, image-guided surgery principles, 3D printed models) as future extensions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured curricular integration with standardized assessment methodologies rather than isolated implementation; instructor feedback within the hands-on format
#### Constraints
- Further research is required to evaluate long-term retention and transfer to clinical performance; the study did not assess cost-effectiveness

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- medical universities designing orthopedic curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- technical and non-technical orthopedic competency development
- preparedness for surgical clerkships

## Claims

- [In one uncontrolled pre–post study, scores across all evaluated competency domains among medical students rose from pre-test to post-test after a structured simulation-based orthopedic workshop, with statistically significant differences and large effect sizes](../claims/orthopedic-simulation-workshop-prepost-gains-all-domains.md) [+W]
- [Perceived importance of practical workshops increased after participation, with the proportion giving a maximum score rising from 34.28% to 61.42%](../claims/perceived-workshop-importance-increased.md) [+W]
- [Confidence in managing musculoskeletal trauma showed the greatest pre–post gain, with the median rising from 4 to 9](../claims/trauma-management-confidence-greatest-gain.md) [+W]
- [Theoretical knowledge scores rose from pre-test to post-test in most domains in one uncontrolled pre-post study, while anatomical-considerations scores (Q4) fell from 55 correct before to 53 after the workshop](../claims/mcq-knowledge-improved-except-anatomy-domain.md) [~W]
- [No significant gender differences were found across most variables, except procedural risk recognition, where male participants reported higher confidence](../claims/gender-differences-only-risk-recognition.md) [~W]
