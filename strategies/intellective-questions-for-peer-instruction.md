# Use peer instruction questions that allow argumentation and reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends selecting question types for peer instruction based on the domain and kind of question: for simple factual or perceptual questions, partner interaction may amplify wrong answers when most students answer incorrectly, whereas for intellective tasks, interaction can produce knowledge gains. It states that "Peer instruction questions that allow for student argumentation and reasoning, therefore, may have the best benefits in student learning." Instructors should pose conceptual questions that test whether students can apply ideas to new settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions challenging enough that students can construct and compare explanations during discussion
#### Constraints
- For factual or perceptual questions where most students answer incorrectly, peer interaction may bolster wrong answers

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students
- high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding
- application of ideas to new contexts
