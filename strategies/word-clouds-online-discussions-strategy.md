# Incorporate word clouds into online discussions to stimulate critical thinking and engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends presenting discussion texts as word clouds—visual representations displaying word frequency by size—rather than linear text, within asynchronous online discussions. Students analyze the clouds to speculate about authorship, historical context, and purpose, then relate course concepts to their analyses and reply to peers with differing interpretations. The authors state this strategy 'can be applied to a wide range of educational environments to stimulate critical thinking and engagement.'

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors should clearly articulate the nature of word clouds and the origins of the particular word cloud before proceeding with the assignment
- Educators should engage students in word cloud analyses as a whole group prior to incorporating it as an individual assignment
#### Constraints
- Most effective when the process is more important than right-or-wrong answers
- Certain words should be kept together to preserve phrases crucial for understanding if the software allows
- The analysis of word clouds is, and should be, challenging

### Target Learners
- undergraduate college students in online or blended courses

### Target Learning Goals
- critical thinking
- engagement
- peer interaction
