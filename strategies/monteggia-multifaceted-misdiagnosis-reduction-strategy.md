# Multifaceted safeguard strategy for reducing missed pediatric Monteggia diagnoses: structured training, senior review, standardized imaging, and early follow-up

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a bundle of institutional and educational safeguards against missed Monteggia diagnoses, since "Early recognition depends not only on imaging but also on clinician awareness and suspicion." Components include structured early training on radiographic assessment and pitfalls, use of simulations, senior oversight with prompt attending review, short-interval follow-ups within 1–2 weeks for uncertain cases, standardized imaging with dedicated elbow views, contralateral comparison, and team-based double reading or early specialist consultation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured instruction on radiographic assessment and common diagnostic pitfalls for early-career clinicians
- Senior oversight so attending physicians or radiologists promptly review junior trainees' interpretations
- Standardized imaging protocols including dedicated elbow views for isolated ulnar fractures
#### Constraints
- The study's own data did not demonstrate a significant correlation between radiographic quality and diagnostic accuracy, so the imaging-protocol component rests on prior literature the authors cite
- Recommended for institutions, especially those lacking on-site pediatric orthopedic expertise

### Target Learners
- Orthopedic residents, particularly first-year residents
- Emergency and general radiologists
- Junior physicians in high-volume emergency settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate radiographic recognition of pediatric Monteggia fracture-dislocations
- Calibrated index of suspicion for radial head dislocation accompanying ulnar injury
