# Incorporate hands-on clinical simulation with low-fidelity task trainers into basic science curricula to bridge theoretical knowledge and clinical application

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests that incorporating simulation into basic science curricula may help narrow the gap between theoretical knowledge and clinical application. The intervention used three 120-min sessions with low-fidelity simulators covering hemoglobin, hemostasis, and acid–base balance, incorporating "scenarios involving communication skills, technical procedures, and clinical interpretation" with immediate feedback at every station.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires curricular restructuring to balance theory and practice, as well as substantial investment in equipment, faculty development, and preparation time.
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted at a single institution, so generalizability should be considered in relation to different contexts and differences across curricula.

### Target Learners
- first-semester undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- theoretical biochemistry knowledge
- clinical interpretation of laboratory findings
- technical skills in blood sampling
- communication skills including rapport and informed consent
