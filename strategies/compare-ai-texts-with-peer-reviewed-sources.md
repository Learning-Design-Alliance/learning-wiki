# Embed ethical inquiry into AI-supported instruction by having learners compare AI-generated texts with peer-reviewed sources

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy positions AI use inside a critical-comparison activity: learners evaluate AI-generated output against peer-reviewed sources to build critical awareness and reduce misuse. The review reports that Wood and Moss (2024) "called for embedding ethical inquiry into AI-supported instruction by encouraging learners to compare AI-generated texts with peer-reviewed sources, thus promoting critical awareness and reducing the risk of misuse". It addresses the review's broader finding that ethics and academic-integrity concerns are rarely addressed in AIHE studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to both generative AI tools and credible peer-reviewed source materials
- Instructor framing that makes the comparison an explicit ethical-inquiry task
#### Constraints
- Recommended by a cited study (Wood and Moss, 2024) rather than tested within this review

### Target Learners
- higher education students using generative AI tools

### Target Learning Goals
- critical awareness of AI output, academic integrity, ethical use of AI
