# Project-Based Learning (PBL)

## Description
Project Based Learning is a teaching method in which learners gain knowledge and skills by working for an extended period of time to investigate and respond to an engaging and complex question, problem, or challenge. Learners tackle authentic problems in the world and accomplish tangible change while learning. Research indicates positive gains in achievement and content knowledge, self-confidence, collaboration, and engagement. PBL helps learners develop skills for living in a knowledge-based, highly technological society, brings new relevance to the learning at hand, lends itself to authentic assessment, promotes lifelong learning, and accommodates learners with varying learning styles and differences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires careful planning and preparation, access to resources and materials, and a supportive learning environment. Instructors need to facilitate and guide learners through the project, providing support and feedback as needed.
#### Constraints
- Can be time-consuming and resource-intensive. May require significant upfront planning and preparation. Learners may struggle with self-direction and time management.

### Affordances
- Provides learners with a way to tackle authentic problems in the world and accomplish tangible change while learning. Learners see the relevance of the learning, as PBL helps them develop skills for living in a knowledge-based, highly technological society.

### Personalization
- Projects can be designed to align with learners' individual interests, needs, and learning styles. Flexible project topics and assessment methods allow for personalized learning experiences.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track learner progress on project milestones, assess the quality of their final product, and evaluate their ability to apply knowledge and skills to real-world problems. Measure gains in content knowledge, self-confidence, collaboration, and engagement through pre- and post-assessments.

## Impact
- Research indicates positive gains in achievement and content knowledge, self-confidence, collaboration, and engagement. Improved learner outcomes and increased motivation. Increased ability to apply knowledge and skills to real-world problems.

## Examples
- My Restaurant Project: Students remedy a food desert situation in their neighborhood by planning out a healthy restaurant, including nutritional facts for all the dishes, food costs, grant proposals, and cash flow models. Geodesic Greenhouses: Learners design a geodesic greenhouse, to allow people to grow fresh vegetables, flowers, fruit and herbs year-round, with minimal extra heating or cooling.
