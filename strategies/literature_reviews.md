# Literature Reviews

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A literature review asks learners to locate, appraise, and synthesize multiple published sources on a question, producing an organized account of the state of knowledge rather than a summary of individual texts. The core cognitive work is *integration*: identifying agreements, disagreements, and gaps across sources and organizing the review around ideas or controversies rather than source-by-source. It is carried out through structured search, critical reading with [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md), and synthesis writing organized by theme or argument.

## Design Implications

Literature reviews develop disciplinary reasoning because learners must weigh conflicting evidence and construct a defensible claim from sources [Argumentation improves reasoning quality.](../claims/argumentation-improves-reasoning.md) [+M]. The task is working-memory and comprehension intensive; scaffolds that reduce source-management load — matrices, synthesis tables, thematic outlines — prevent overload from degrading the quality of integration [Cognitive overload degrades learning outcomes.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S]. Reviews organized around *contrasting cases* (studies that disagree) produce deeper understanding than reviews that merely list findings [Comparing and contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a real corpus (library databases, curated reading sets) with enough sources to permit genuine synthesis
- An explicit organizational scheme — thematic, methodological, or chronological — taught before writing begins
- Source-evaluation criteria (currency, method quality, bias) made explicit, as in [A Finder's Guide to Facts](a_finders_guide_to_facts.md)
- A synthesis scaffold such as an evidence matrix (source × claim × method × finding) to externalize comparisons

#### Constraints
- Weak search skills produce biased or shallow corpora; without instruction in database use and keyword strategy, learners review an unrepresentative slice of the literature [-M]
- Source-by-source "annotated bibliography" writing defeats the purpose — learners who organize by author rather than by idea show little gains in integrative reasoning [-M]
- For novices with little domain knowledge, evaluating methodological quality is unreliable; they cannot distinguish strong from weak studies without [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) supplying disciplinary criteria [-W]
- Very large corpora induce [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) failures — learners who try to hold dozens of sources in mind without external organizers produce superficial summaries [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Narrative review**: prose synthesis organized by theme; standard in capstone and thesis work
- **Systematic review**: explicit search protocol, inclusion criteria, and PRISMA-style documentation; teaches reproducibility
- **Mini-review with curated corpus**: instructor supplies 5–10 deliberately conflicting sources so the synthesis work, not the search, is the focus — appropriate earlier in a curriculum
- **Annotated synthesis matrix**: learners fill a table before writing, making cross-source comparison visible

### Target Learners
- Advanced undergraduates and graduate students writing theses, capstones, or research proposals
- Novices benefit most when the corpus is curated and the synthesis scaffold is provided; open-ended search plus open-ended writing overwhelms them [Cognitive overload degrades learning outcomes.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S]
- Professionals updating practice knowledge (e.g., evidence-based medicine, teaching) — reviews are the core skill of evidence-based practice

### Target Learning Goals
- Integrative reasoning: reconciling conflicting findings into a coherent account
- Disciplinary epistemology: judging what counts as good evidence in a field
- Research gap identification: locating what is not yet known as a basis for new inquiry
- Academic writing genre knowledge: citation practice, hedging, and attribution

### Instructions
1. Frame a focused, answerable question and define inclusion criteria for sources.
2. Teach search strategy: databases, keywords, citation chaining; model it with a live demonstration ([Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) modeling phase).
3. Have learners read with [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md), extracting claim, method, and evidence quality per source into a synthesis matrix.
4. Group sources by theme or controversy, deliberately pairing studies that disagree [Comparing and contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+M].
5. Draft the review organized by ideas, not authors, with an explicit argument about the state of knowledge [Argumentation improves reasoning quality.](../claims/argumentation-improves-reasoning.md) [+M].
6. Peer-review drafts against a rubric checking synthesis (not summary), then revise.

## Examples
- **Graduate thesis proposals** — the required literature review chapter; strongest when advisors require a synthesis matrix before drafting.
- **Evidence-Based Practice assignments in nursing and medicine** — students conduct structured searches (e.g., via PubMed Clinical Queries) and appraise studies against explicit hierarchy-of-evidence criteria ([University of Oxford CEBM levels](https://www.cebm.ox.ac.uk/resources/ebm-tools/)).
- **CIVIC online reasoning curriculum ([Stanford History Education Group](https://cor.stanford.edu))** — a K-12 adaptation: learners evaluate multiple sources on a question, an entry-level form of cross-source synthesis [Civic online reasoning instruction improves evaluation skill.](../claims/civic-online-reasoning-instruction-improves-evaluation.md) [+S].
