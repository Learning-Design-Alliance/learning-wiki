# Mitigate extraction errors by requiring high-resolution PDFs, equation-editor equations, and legible graph axes, units, and legends

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because extraction errors in equations, figures, tables, and graphs can generate plausible but insufficiently supported feedback or invalid assessments, the article recommends submission practices that improve automated retrieval: students submit reports as high-resolution PDF files avoiding low-quality scans or photographs; equations are written with the word processor's equation editor rather than inserted as images, since fractions and symbols embedded as images may be difficult to read automatically; and graphs include clearly legible axes, units, and legends within the figure itself.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors should pay particular attention to AI observations depending on mathematical expressions, numerical values and units, graphs and labels, data tables, experimental diagrams, and images.
#### Constraints
- When feedback is classified as doubtful or invalid, the article recommends a focused conversational second review rather than discarding the tool altogether.

### Target Learners
- higher education students submitting laboratory reports

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate automated retrieval of evidence from student reports

### Affordances
- Evidence Present Versus Effectively Available Distinction
