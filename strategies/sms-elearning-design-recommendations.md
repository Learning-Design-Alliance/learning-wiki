# Design e-learning for self-management support with dedicated work time, authentic scenarios, short self-paced sessions and rigorous evaluation built in from inception

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's forward-looking design recommendations: e-learning for SMS should provide high interactivity, opportunities for practice and application, and time for reflection; use authentic real-world scenarios and patient-interaction videos; allow flexible, self-paced completion with the ability to save progress; and build rigorous effectiveness evaluation into the design from its inception. The review cites Lewis et al.'s systematic review of 72 studies recommending "well-defined goals and objectives determined by a clear understanding and assessment of learners' needs" and careful consideration of situational factors such as learner characteristics, resources and infrastructure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated time and space for e-learning in the work environment, integrated with existing professional development activities and opportunities to practice skills learned online
#### Constraints
- Completely online delivery poses limitations on the scope and types of SMS capabilities that can be taught exclusively and effectively; high creativity with discussion boards and audio-visual resources would be needed to replicate the depth of learning

### Target Learners
- Health professionals in primary care and community settings learning chronic condition self-management support

### Target Learning Goals
- Communication and person-centred care skills, behaviour change facilitation, goal setting and action planning with patients
