# Magnetism Investigations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Magnetism investigations are hands-on science activities in which learners test which objects a magnet attracts, explore how magnetic force acts at a distance and through materials, and map how a compass needle responds to nearby magnets and magnetic fields. The cycle of predicting, testing, observing, and explaining drives the learning: learners commit to a prediction, confront the outcome, and revise their mental model of magnetic properties.

## Design Implications

Hands-on investigation makes an invisible force observable and testable, which is especially valuable for abstract physical-science concepts young learners cannot directly perceive [Active learning improves exam performance relative to lecture alone.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. The strategy works best when prediction precedes testing — committing to a prediction creates cognitive disequilibrium when results contradict it, which motivates conceptual change [Cognitive disequilibrium motivates conceptual change.](../claims/cognitive-disequilibrium-motivates-conceptual-change.md) [+M]. Investigations should conclude with explanation and discussion, not just manipulation; activity without sense-making produces engagement but shallow learning [Hands-on activity alone does not guarantee conceptual learning without reflection and explanation.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Bar magnets, compasses, and a mixed set of test objects (steel, aluminum, copper, plastic, wood, paper) — including non-magnetic metals so learners confront the "all metal is magnetic" misconception
- A prediction sheet or recording structure so learners commit before testing ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) can frame the recording format)
- A debrief in which learners articulate rules ("magnets attract iron and steel, not all shiny things") and connect compass behavior to Earth's magnetic field
- Controlled environment: keep stray magnets and electronics away from compass stations

#### Constraints
- Requires physical materials and per-group supervision; not feasible at scale without kits
- Young learners frequently overgeneralize from a single trial (e.g., "magnets stick to metal" after testing only steel) — multiple contrasting examples are needed, and a single hands-on session rarely displaces entrenched misconceptions [~M]
- Compass readings are easily confounded by nearby metal furniture or hidden magnets, producing observations learners cannot explain [-W]
- Safety: small magnets pose swallowing hazards for young children; strong neodymium magnets can pinch

#### Implementation Variability
- Prediction-first vs. explore-first: prediction-first produces stronger conceptual change; explore-first suits learners with no prior exposure
- Add complexity progressively: attraction → force through materials (paper, wood, water) → distance effects → compass field mapping
- Pair with [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) (iron filings as "footprints" of the invisible field) to make the field visible
- Older or advanced learners can extend to plotting field lines with iron filings or building an electromagnet

### Target Learners
- K–5 learners encountering physical science for the first time; magnetism's immediate, dramatic feedback suits concrete operational thinkers
- Learners holding common misconceptions (all metals are magnetic; magnets attract everything shiny) who benefit from direct refutation through testing [Cognitive disequilibrium motivates conceptual change.](../claims/cognitive-disequilibrium-motivates-conceptual-change.md) [+M]
- Less effective as pure free exploration for novices; guided structure with focused questions is needed [~S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: magnetic vs. non-magnetic materials, action at a distance, Earth as a magnet
- Science practices: prediction, fair testing, observation, evidence-based explanation
- Representational skill: mapping invisible fields via compass deflection or filings

### Instructions
1. Activate prior experience: "Where have you seen magnets work?" ([Activation](../elements/activation.md))
2. Present the object set and have each group record predictions for every object before touching a magnet ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
3. Groups test each object, recording attract/repel/no effect, and re-sort objects by result ([Application](../elements/application.md))
4. Introduce the compass: learners move a magnet around a compass and record needle deflection at different positions and distances ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
5. Debrief: groups state a rule supported by their evidence; the class tests edge cases (aluminum coin, nickel) against the rule ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
6. Assess with novel objects and a "explain the compass" transfer prompt ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
