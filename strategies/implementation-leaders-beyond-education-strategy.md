# Pair guideline-implementation education with dedicated implementation leaders who identify local barriers, in-service new staff and audit advice delivery

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors state they did not explore strategies beyond the education piece and propose that dedicated implementation leaders could support guideline implementation. Such leaders "could identify hospital‐specific enablers and barriers to implementation; location‐specific solutions to these barriers; in‐servicing solutions to support education for new staff; mechanisms to ensure educational materials are readily available, and could complete auditing of whether pregnant women are given CMV prevention advice". The article says new research is required to test whether these strategies work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated implementation leaders within health services
- New research to investigate whether these strategies are useful for CMV prevention messaging
#### Constraints
- The article explicitly notes this is untested: new research is required to investigate whether these strategies would be useful and whether they reduce cCMV infection

### Target Learners
- Maternity health professionals including obstetricians and general practitioners

### Target Learning Goals
- Implementation of congenital CMV prevention guidelines in clinical practice
