Use adaptive thresholding to equate component-task difficulty at ~80% accuracy before multitasking training and testing¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Before training, each participant completes an adaptive staircase procedure that individually tunes tracking speed and discrimination response window so both component tasks run at approximately 80% accuracy. The article states this was done "to ascertain that multitasking deﬁcits are not due to individual differences in task skills", with difficulty adjusting whenever accuracy rose above 82.5% or fell below 77.5%. This implements equal-difficulty calibration across participants and conditions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An adaptive staircase with predefined criterion accuracy (~80%) and adjustment thresholds (82.5%/77.5%) applied per participant before group comparisons

Constraints¶

  • Thresholding was applied to these laboratory tracking and discrimination tasks; transferability of the calibration approach to other task types is not tested in the article

Target Learners¶

  • Healthy adults aged 18–40 performing visuomotor tracking and perceptual discrimination tasks

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Comparing dual-task versus single-task performance at matched individual difficulty levels

Claims¶

  • Simultaneous performance of tracking and discrimination tasks produces large multitasking costs at baseline [+W]
  • Multitasking training reduces dual-task cost relative to single-task component training, with gains specific to the multitasking group [+W]
