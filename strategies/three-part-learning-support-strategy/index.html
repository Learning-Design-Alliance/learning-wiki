Three-part learning support: contextualized real-world problems, hot topics, and question-and-answer¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The FOCUS learning support strategy divides weekly one-hour sessions into three sections: connections between mathematics and the real world using technology and culturally relevant problems, mini-instruction of hot topics addressing common misconceptions via alternative approaches, and Socratic-style question-and-answer time. The article states "The learning support model was a three-part approach generalizable to any mathematics course". Time was allocated roughly 50% connections, 30% hot topics, and 20% question and answer.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors who facilitate labs and provide personal attention to students' needs
  • A set of lesson plans and activities aligned with program objectives

Constraints¶

  • Time allocation percentages and weekly one-hour format as implemented at this university

Target Learners¶

  • Developmental mathematics students in co-requisite college coursework

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Connecting abstract mathematical concepts to real-world relevance
  • Addressing misconceptions and critical concept attainment

Affordances¶

  • Focus Three Legged Stool Model

Claims¶

  • FOCUS Intervention students earned significantly more College Algebra grade points than the baseline comparison group, but the effect was small (η²p = .01) [+W]
  • FOCUS Intervention students were significantly less likely to withdraw from College Algebra than a baseline comparison group (OR = 0.22, a large effect) [+W]
  • FOCUS Intervention students made a statistically significant increase in THEA mathematics scores from pretest to posttest (d = .61) [+W]
  • THEA score gains did not differ significantly across the five semester/year administrations of the FOCUS Intervention [+W]
