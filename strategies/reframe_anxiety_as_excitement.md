# Reframe Anxiety as Excitement

## Description
This technique involves changing one’s mindset from anxiety to excitement, leveraging the similar physiological responses (faster heartbeats and cortisol surges) associated with both emotions. Excitement puts people in an opportunity mindset (“I can do something about this”), whereas anxiety can put people in a threat mindset. By reframing anxiety as excitement, learners may experience better math performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simple prompt or message before starting a task. Requires some awareness of one's emotional state.
#### Constraints
- May not work for everyone; some may find it difficult to shift from anxiety to excitement. The Atlantic published a video about this strategy, and some find this difficult to do.

### Affordances
- Helps learners approach challenges with a positive mindset, improves focus and problem-solving abilities, avoids the need for significant physiological shifts required to achieve calmness.

### Personalization
- The reframing technique can be personalized with different encouragement messages and opportunities mindset prompts. The written message, “Try to get excited,” before completing a task.

## Assessment Evidence
- Improved math performance, increased problem-solving focus, positive mindset

## Impact
- Studies show that showing participants the written message, “Try to get excited,” on their computer screens before giving them difficult math problems to do resulted in better math performance than showing the message, “Try to remain calm,” or the neutral message, “Please wait a few moments.
