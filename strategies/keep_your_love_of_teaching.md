# Keep Your Love of Teaching

## Description
This strategy treats the teacher's own enthusiasm, humor, and authentic enjoyment of students and subject matter as an instructional resource to be deliberately maintained rather than left to chance. It involves protecting the conditions that generate genuine enthusiasm — autonomy, connection with students, intellectual engagement with content — and expressing that enthusiasm visibly in instruction.

## Design Implications

Teacher enthusiasm is measurably linked to student engagement and achievement: observed enthusiastic instructional behavior predicts student enjoyment and achievement in large-scale studies [Kunter et al. found enthusiastic teaching predicts student enjoyment and achievement.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031289) [+M]. Enthusiasm operates partly through emotional contagion and modeling — students catch and mirror the teacher's expressed interest in the content [Teacher enthusiasm is a distinct construct linked to student engagement via emotional transmission.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-014-9272-z) [+M]. Because enthusiasm is fragile under chronic workload and depleted resources, sustaining it requires structural attention to burnout risk, not just individual willpower [Burnout arises from mismatches between person and workplace that erode energy and engagement.](https://doi.org/10.1080/02678373.2016.1217725) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular, genuine contact with the aspects of teaching that energize the individual teacher (favorite topics, student interactions, intellectual challenge)
- Instructional autonomy sufficient to teach in ways that feel authentic [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]
- Expressive channels: vocal variety, movement, humor, and personal connection to content that students can perceive
- Workload management that prevents the chronic exhaustion underlying burnout

#### Constraints
- Performed or feigned enthusiasm without underlying enjoyment reads as inauthentic to students and can undermine credibility [~M]
- Enthusiasm cannot compensate for poor structure or unclear explanations; it amplifies competent instruction rather than substituting for it [~M]
- High emotional labor in unsupportive environments accelerates rather than prevents burnout [Burnout arises from mismatches between person and workplace that erode energy and engagement.](https://doi.org/10.1080/02678373.2016.1217725) [-M]
- Overly high-arousal delivery can overwhelm content for some learners and cultures; enthusiasm must not crowd out thinking time [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Enthusiasm for *students* (relational warmth) versus enthusiasm for *content* (displayed subject passion) are partially separable and can be cultivated through different practices
- In online and asynchronous settings, enthusiasm shifts toward voice modulation, video presence, and personalized feedback rather than physical expressiveness
- Department- and school-level practices (collaborative planning, celebrating student growth) sustain enthusiasm collectively rather than individually

### Target Learners
- K–12 and undergraduate learners, who are especially responsive to teacher affect and emotional display [Teacher enthusiasm is a distinct construct linked to student engagement via emotional transmission.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-014-9272-z) [+M]
- Low-motivation or anxious students, for whom a teacher's visible confidence and enjoyment can support persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Less effective as a lever for advanced, purely self-directed learners who rely less on instructor affect

### Target Learning Goals
- Motivational and affective goals: fostering enjoyment, interest, and willingness to engage
- Sustained engagement in long or difficult units where initial novelty has faded
- Modeling disciplinary passion — showing what it looks like to care about a subject

### Instructions
1. Identify your "energy sources" — the topics, activities, and student interactions that genuinely energize you — and schedule them regularly rather than letting them be crowded out by administrative demands.
2. Express enthusiasm visibly in instruction: vary vocal delivery, share why you find the material interesting, and connect content to your own curiosity. Enthusiasm only benefits students when they can perceive it [Kunter et al. found enthusiastic teaching predicts student enjoyment and achievement.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031289) [+M].
3. Build relational enthusiasm through brief personal contact routines such as a [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) at the start of class, which sustains the teacher–student connection that feeds enjoyment of teaching.
4. Protect autonomy by negotiating instructional decisions where possible; self-determined teaching is more sustainable and more motivating for students [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S].
5. Monitor your own depletion using workload and emotion checks; treat rising exhaustion as an instructional-design problem (reduce scope, share planning, restore recovery time) rather than a personal failing [Burnout arises from mismatches between person and workplace that erode energy and engagement.](https://doi.org/10.1080/02678373.2016.1217725) [+S].
6. Pair enthusiasm with strong structure — clear goals, [Practice](../elements/practice.md), and feedback — so that displayed passion amplifies rather than substitutes for effective instruction.

## Related Strategies
- [Activate Background Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md) — enthusiasm for student ideas makes activation routines feel genuine rather than procedural
- [Action-Oriented Feedback](../strategies/action-oriented-feedback.md) — feedback conversations are a high-yield site for relational enthusiasm

## Examples
- **Responsive Classroom** (https://www.responsiveclassroom.org) — its daily [morning meeting](../elements/check-in.md) structures give teachers a reliable, low-cost routine for positive student contact that sustains relational enjoyment.
- **National Writing Project** (https://www.nwp.org) — summer institutes deliberately reconnect teachers to their own writing and intellectual passions, a professional-development model built on the premise that teacher enthusiasm for the discipline transfers to students.
- A secondary science teacher who opens each unit with the question or phenomenon that first hooked *them* into the field, narrating their own curiosity aloud before introducing the curriculum standard.
