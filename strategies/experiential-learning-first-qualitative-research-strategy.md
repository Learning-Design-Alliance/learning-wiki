# Provide procedural and technical information before philosophical and theoretical information, and incorporate experiential learning at the outset of qualitative research courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy implements the article's grounded theory of inductive qualitative research education. The authors recommend providing students with procedural and technical information they require in order to conduct qualitative data collection prior to delving into philosophical and theoretical information, which they say will enhance the positive affective and cognitive experiences of learning qualitative research and minimize the negative aspects. Once students have some confidence in the skills required, they will be more receptive to considering relevant theoretical aspects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must sequence courses so that experiential learning and procedural or technical skills come before philosophical and theoretical instruction.
#### Constraints
- The authors state the current typical structure of qualitative research courses (i.e., philosophy, epistemology, followed by skills) should be reversed or at least reconsidered, framing this as a reconsideration rather than a tested optimum.

### Target Learners
- graduate students learning qualitative research

### Target Learning Goals
- conducting qualitative research
- gaining confidence in qualitative data collection skills
