# Frequent Student Reflections

## Description
This strategy involves prompting learners to reflect on their learning more often, allowing them to better understand their strengths and weaknesses, make connections between topics and disciplines, and grow as they self-assess their knowledge and understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reflection prompts, a method for collecting reflections, and time for learners to respond.
#### Constraints
- Requires time to adjust classroom routine to make time for reflections, learners must act on their reflection advice.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learner levels, particularly those who need to improve their self-assessment and metacognitive skills.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve self-assessment skills, enhance understanding of strengths and weaknesses, foster connections between topics and disciplines, and promote a growth mindset.

### Affordances
- Enables learners to identify gaps in their knowledge, make time to determine if they understand before it is too late to seek help, and practice growth mindset comments.

### Personalization
- Collecting student reflections through various mediums (e.g., GrokSpot, OneNote, online forms). Asking students to reflect at the beginning/end of the year, during learning, and after assessments.

## Assessment Evidence
- Honesty and detail in learners’ responses, evidence of growth in self-assessment skills, and connections made with other classes or in the community.

## Impact
- Helps learners to realize when they don’t fully understand a topic or idea, provides a chance to practice growth mindset comments, and enables learners to find connections they may not have noticed otherwise.
