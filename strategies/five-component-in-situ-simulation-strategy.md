# Run the simulation-debrief-revise cycle with five key components: in situ setting, real clinical team makeup, meaningful scenario, expert-plus-management debriefing, and repetition to saturation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article prescribes an implementable recipe for simulation aimed at systems improvement rather than education alone. It states the key components of simulation are: "1) simulation should take place in situ (within the real workplace with normally available equipment and drugs) to re-create the work environment accurately", with working healthcare workers as participants, a meaningful clinical event as the scenario, structured debriefing by simulation experts and senior management, and repetition until further useful system observations cease. Each cycle ends with instituted guideline and workflow changes tested in the next simulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A pre-designed management focused feedback rubric for debriefing
- Changes to improve the guideline and workflow instituted after each debriefing and tested in the subsequent simulation
#### Constraints
- The article notes time constraints of an imminent outbreak did not allow a more formal evaluation of the methodology

### Target Learners
- working healthcare workers (doctors, nurses, and supporting staff)

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating and improving clinical guidelines, systems, and workflow
