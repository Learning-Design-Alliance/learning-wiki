Offer pre-grading with corrective comments and resubmission before the due date, supported by an automated grader¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends letting students submit skill-teaching projects early, receiving prompt automated grading with comments that point to what needs correction (not the solution), and resubmitting for final grading before the due date. This implements the response-feedback-response cycle of behavioral learning theory, with rapid feedback assumed since "all projects were graded within 48 hours of submission". The authors note it is particularly valuable for online web-based courses that lack direct observation of learning during physical lab meetings.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An automated grading tool to keep feedback lag short and grading effort manageable
  • Comments that indicate what needs correction without giving the solution
  • Submission a reasonable time prior to the due date to allow feedback and resubmission

Constraints¶

  • Effect on test scores was significant only when test concepts closely matched the pre-graded project (Excel Solver); mismatched content (Excel IF's versus Scenarios) showed no significant effect

Target Learners¶

  • students in introductory computer literacy or information systems courses, particularly online

Target Learning Goals¶

  • hands-on software skills and related conceptual understanding

Affordances¶

  • Gagne Nine Conditions Responding Reinforcement

Claims¶

  • Implementing the AGLS automated grader reduced grading time and allowed the instructor to require twelve assignments instead of five [+W]
  • Across all assignments combined, the pre-grading group's hands-on-versus-multiple-choice score difference (5.72) exceeded the control group's (4.22), but not significantly [~W]
  • Pre-grading/resubmission was associated with a statistically significant learning difference only for the Excel Solver assignment, not for Access or Excel IF assignments [~W]
  • Uptake of the pre-grading option was high but declined over the semester, from 85% on the first assignment to 70% on the last [~W]
