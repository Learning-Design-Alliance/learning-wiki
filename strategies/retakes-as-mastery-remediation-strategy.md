# Implement assessment retakes as mastery-oriented remediation to build a growth-mindset culture and spread equitable grading school-wide

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes Kayla's gradual implementation of assessment retakes with remediation, in which 'retakes were not treated as a second chance for points, but as an opportunity for students to demonstrate their eventual mastery of content.' She started in physics, expanded to other courses as student success became visible, and the practice eventually gained school-level acceptance despite initial collegial resistance. The strategy pairs retakes with growth-mindset messaging so students learn it is acceptable not to succeed immediately.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher autonomy to allow retakes and revisions; framing retakes around eventual mastery rather than points; gradual expansion as evidence of student success accumulates
#### Constraints
- The article reports criticism from colleagues ('Why are you doing the test retakes') and notes the practice challenged existing norms around rigor and grading; school-level adoption also depended on other teachers reading the same equity literature

### Target Learners
- high school students, including female students and students from minoritized backgrounds experiencing test anxiety

### Target Learning Goals
- mastery of physics content
- growth mindset around assessment
- equitable grading practices
