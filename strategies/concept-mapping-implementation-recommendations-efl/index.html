Strategies

Introduce concept mapping through direct training, attend to computer ability, and start with familiar topics¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends EFL teachers incorporate the concept map strategy as a brainstorming exercise or pre-writing activity, with careful introduction: "a period of direct training is required before students can successfully construct their own concept maps." Teachers should give particular emphasis to students' computer ability before any use of concept maps, and students' first map should be created on a simple, familiar topic. Learners' needs, wants, expectations, and cognitive abilities should be taken into account.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Direct strategy training following a modeled sequence before independent map construction

Constraints¶

  • Mixed computer ability in the class led this study to adopt paper-and-pencil mapping; the article warns computer ability may hinder effective utilization of maps

Target Learners¶

  • EFL university students learning academic writing

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Argumentative essay writing and independent learning strategy use

Claims¶

  • Paper-and-pencil concept mapping at the pre-writing stage significantly improved EFL students' argumentative essay performance across five writing aspects [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Teach concept map construction through a graduated briefing sequence before independent use
  • Use teacher-student interactive map construction so the teacher can correct misinformation and introduce new ideas
  • Integrate mind and concept maps into preclinical curricula with structured training, weekly review use, and collaborative mapping
  • Concept Mapping
  • Implement brainstorming sessions in EFL writing lessons through small monitored groups with clarified problems, open expression, deferred judgment and fixed timing
  • Apply mind mapping in mathematics teaching through overview, preview, inview, and review stages
