Participatory design sessions with educators to gather usability feedback on educational robot prototypes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors ran two sessions with educators from two districts — one build-and-test session and one play-and-discuss session — to involve intended users in the design process. The article describes "This exploratory participatory design study investigated educators from two different school districts initial impressions of the robot" to leverage educators' assets and contextual knowledge to inform design. Feedback was deductively coded with the ERA Framework, and findings underscored the importance of participatory design with teachers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sessions should include educators with relevant roles (technology support, CS leads, language specialists) and guiding questions about robots in education

Constraints¶

  • Most participating educators had prior robotics experience and district CS support, so views of inexperienced educators were not represented

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 educators implementing or considering classroom robotics

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Usability and curricular integration of educational robots for CS instruction

Affordances¶

  • Era Framework Educational Robot Design Principles

Claims¶

  • Educators provided feedback across all five ERA Framework principles and engaged positively with Roversa [+W]
  • Educators perceived the micro:bit basis as extending Roversa's use across grades PreK-12 and beyond simple directional programming [+W]
  • Physical buttons on Roversa were perceived to help students build directional programming concepts through physical manipulation [+W]
  • Adjustable calibration of movement distance and turning radius was perceived as an advantage over fixed-distance robots like the Bee-Bot [+W]
  • Roversa's Spanish language capabilities were perceived to increase engagement in dual language immersion classrooms [+W]
