# Photo elicitation assignment inviting students to photograph and reflect on their identities in STEM

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes a classroom project adapted from a social work practice in which students capture five original photographs representing facets of their experience, choosing from prompts such as "Your sense of belonging (or exclusion) in STEM" and "Your support systems". For each image students write a short reflection of approximately 200 words connecting it to their identity in STEM. Students are encouraged to take new photos rather than use existing images, an act the author calls intentional noticing, and may substitute poetry, drawing, collage, or layered text and image.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students take original photographs and write short reflections of approximately 200 words per image
- Students are encouraged to take new photos for the project rather than using existing images
#### Constraints
- Participation in any class discussion or presentation should be fully voluntary
- Students may opt out of sharing their work to respect privacy

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students
- students in any STEM course

### Target Learning Goals
- STEM identity reflection
- sense of belonging
- connection and inclusion
