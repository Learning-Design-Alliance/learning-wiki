Embed ethics framing into technical accounting courses using disguised, ambiguous scenarios rather than obvious ethical dilemmas¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends preparing students early in their academic careers for settings with few direct indicators of unethical behavior by measuring and developing sensitivity to subtle cues. Its instrument used a multi-phase scenario with "substantial additional information ... to distract the participant from the ethical dimension of the case," so that recognition of the developing dilemma had to be inferred. The authors suggest their educational and measurement processes "may provide a basis for advancing the ethics education dialogue."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A multi-phase scenario that progressively discloses information, with distractor content disguising the ethical dimension
  • Expert-panel review and pilot testing of the instrument before use

Constraints¶

  • The authors note the unexpected RQ2 results may be specific to their sample and encourage replication at multiple universities with more than one instructor

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate accounting majors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • recognizing developing ethical dilemmas in ambiguous professional situations

Claims¶

  • Students in an ethics-framed educational process demonstrate greater ethical sensitivity than students in an accounting-framed process when evaluating an ambiguous scenario [+W]
  • Ethical sensitivity increases as progressively more information about the developing dilemma is disclosed across scenario phases [+W]
