# Fidget Contracts

## Description
Fidget contracts are agreements between a learner and an educator or caregiver that outline specific rules and guidelines for using fidget tools. These contracts define when, where, and how fidgets can be used to support focus, attention, and self-regulation rather than as toys. By setting clear expectations and promoting mindful use, fidget contracts help prevent misuse and distractions while maximizing the benefits of sensory tools for learners who struggle with attention or sensory processing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires open communication and collaboration between the learner and educator/caregiver. A printed or digital contract that both parties agree to and sign is essential.
#### Constraints
- May not be effective if the learner does not understand or agree with the contract. Requires consistent enforcement and follow-up to ensure compliance.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Learners with ADHD, sensory processing issues, or difficulty focusing; useful for K–12 students and adults

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote mindful fidget use, improve focus and attention, prevent misuse and distractions, support self-regulation

### Affordances
- Provides structure and accountability for fidget use, helps learners understand the purpose of fidgets as tools rather than toys, and promotes self-awareness of their own needs and behaviors.

### Personalization
- Contracts can be customized to fit individual needs, preferences, and contexts. Rules can be tailored to specific environments, such as classrooms, homes, or workplaces.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe changes in fidget use behaviors, such as frequency, purpose, and compliance with contract rules. Monitor learner focus and attention levels during activities where fidgets are used.

## Impact
- Improved focus and attention: Fidget contracts help learners use sensory tools more effectively, leading to increased focus and attention during tasks. Reduced distractions: By setting clear expectations, contracts minimize the potential for fidgets to distract others.
