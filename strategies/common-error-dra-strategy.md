# Identify common errors in the first dynamic assessment and have learners correct them in the second assessment and a written DRA

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The study added a development-referenced assessment to the post-CRA by determining "the common errors that emerged in the initial DSA and having learners attempt to correct them in the second DSA". A common-error written DRA was then included in the post-CRA, and 86.4% of the common errors were corrected on it, which the authors read as a quantifiable indication of development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recording and transcribing both DSAs to identify common errors
- A common-error written DRA added to the post-CRA
#### Constraints
- It is necessary to determine exactly which learners made which errors in DSA1 and their ability to correct them in DSA2 and on the DRA

### Target Learners
- First-year university L2 learners

### Target Learning Goals
- L2 grammar correction
- Declarative written knowledge development
