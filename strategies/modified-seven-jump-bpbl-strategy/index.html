Use a modified Seven Jump PBL process with technology-supported flexibility for blended delivery¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study employed a modified "Seven Jump" PBL process consisting of six steps: define the problem, brainstorm ideas, develop learning issues, research individually, reconvene to discuss findings, and complete a reflective journal entry. In the B-PBL condition, steps 1-4 were completed in class at the group's own pace, step 5 was conducted via e-campus chat room outside class, and step 6 was a collaborative group reflection submitted as homework.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A virtual learning environment with chat room and discussion forum capabilities
  • Course content organized into weekly problems aligned with textbook chapters
  • Instructor availability as facilitator rather than lecturer

Constraints¶

  • Individual tutors were not provided during online chat or in-class discussions, which the authors note as a possible limitation

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate nursing students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • problem-solving skills
  • collaborative learning
  • course content mastery

Claims¶

  • Blended problem-based learning produced significantly higher posttest academic achievement than PBL and traditional lecture instruction [+M]
  • All three instructional groups showed higher academic achievement scores from pretest to posttest [+M]
  • No significant differences in critical thinking dispositions were found among PBL, B-PBL, and traditional lecture groups at pretest or posttest [~M]
