Courseware development suggestions: 3D Max with UV mapping, moderate file sizes, and music instead of voice¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors close with three implementable suggestions for developers of similar 3D courseware. First, use 3D Max for fast and simple modeling and achieve high-fidelity imagery via Photoshop and/or UV Mapping, since Virtool is "too complicated and time-consuming". Second, keep graphics files moderately sized so they download easily on household computers with narrow bandwidth. Third, the literature review suggested voice may distract learners, so easy-listening music replaced verbal messages, though future researchers are advised to include verbal instructions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An upgraded CPU, increased main memory capacity and an independent graphics card to prevent lags from affecting learning results and interest

Constraints¶

  • 3D Max alone does not deliver images closely resembling the original; the fidelity gap must be solved with Photoshop and/or UV Mapping

Target Learners¶

  • courseware developers
  • college and industry training designers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • developing effective 3D instructional courseware for equipment-operation skills

Claims¶

  • Lecture plus 3D courseware produced significantly lower cognitive load than lecture alone and lecture plus video instruction [+W]
  • Lecture plus 3D courseware produced significantly better learning results than lecture alone, lecture plus video, and lecture plus image instruction [+W]
  • Interviewed students in the 3D courseware group reported greater independency, interactivity and control than the other groups [+W]
  • Instructional method significantly affected both learning results and cognitive load in color management and inspection instruction (MANCOVA omnibus) [+W]
