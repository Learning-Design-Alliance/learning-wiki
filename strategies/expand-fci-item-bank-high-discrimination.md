# Expand the FCI item bank with highly discriminative items exhibiting sufficient difficulty variability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend enlarging the item bank available to Chain-CAT by developing new items or incorporating items from other research-based assessments. They write that items should be "highly discriminative, i.e. that can better distinguish respondents with different levels of proficiency" and should "display suﬃcient variability in their diﬃculty parameters." This follows directly from their simulation finding that the 30 FCI items constrain item selection so severely that constrained 5-item Chain-CAT loses its efficiency advantage.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- New or incorporated items must have high discrimination parameters
- New or incorporated items must exhibit sufficient variability in difficulty parameters
- The reliability of statistical grouping and the content balancing algorithm should be re-examined when new items are added
#### Constraints
- The article notes that item quality involves multiple perspectives beyond IRT metrics, and whether items performing well under IRT are regarded as high quality by experts needs additional investigation

### Target Learners
- University students in introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate and precise estimation of students' conceptual understanding of Newtonian mechanics in frequent short assessments
