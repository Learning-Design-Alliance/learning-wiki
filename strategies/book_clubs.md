# Book Clubs

## Description
Book clubs are small groups of learners who choose, read, and discuss a shared text. Learners take ownership of their reading experience by selecting books, establishing group rules, pacing their reading, and guiding their conversations. Instructors provide choice in books, group membership, rules, conversation topics, and final products. The emphasis is on fostering a shared reading experience where learners share thoughts, analyze texts, and critically think together. Structures may vary, including discussions, projects, and written reflections, to suit diverse learner needs and objectives. This approach promotes engagement, critical thinking, and social awareness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a variety of books. Class time for reading and discussion. Facilitation by the instructor to guide discussions and provide support.
#### Constraints
- Requires careful planning to ensure meaningful discussions and prevent off-topic conversations. Some students may be unprepared or dominate discussions, requiring instructor intervention. Assessment can be challenging due to varied reading materials and group dynamics.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve reading comprehension, develop critical thinking skills, foster collaborative discussions, increase student engagement

### Affordances
- Encourages student ownership and motivation through choice. Provides a social context for learning and discussing texts. Promotes deeper engagement with reading material and critical thinking skills. Enhances vocabulary and communication skills.

### Personalization
- Offer diverse book choices catering to different interests and reading levels. Allow learners to set their reading pace and discussion topics. Provide options for final projects to accommodate different learning styles.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor participation and engagement in discussions. Review written reflections and project submissions for evidence of critical thinking and comprehension. Assess group dynamics and collaboration through observation.

## Impact
- Research supports that student choice in reading and independent reading are significant factors in helping students improve as readers. Learning in social contexts has been shown to have significant effects on comprehension of material and retention of new information.
