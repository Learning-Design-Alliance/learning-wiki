# Whole Class Self-Monitoring

## Description
Whole class self-monitoring involves the entire class participating in self-monitoring techniques, adapted from individual self-monitoring methods. This approach aims to increase self-control and constructive introspection in all students, rather than targeting just a few at-risk individuals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular, evenly spaced behavior checks; individual tracking sheets for students; an audible or visual cue; an operant response to the cue; and a plan for withdrawal from the system. A short discussion period is also needed for objective self-assessment.
#### Constraints
- Requires a structured classroom environment and may initially disrupt the instructional day. Requires consistent modeling and reinforcement of the language and concepts of self-monitoring. Inappropriate behavior may initially increase as students test the limits of the system.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Elementary and secondary classrooms where the teacher wants to promote self-regulation and responsible behavior in all students.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote self-control, introspection, and responsible behavior in the entire classroom, fostering a positive and supportive learning environment.

### Affordances
- Prevents at-risk students from feeling singled out, provides positive role models for self-control, and creates a structured classroom environment that benefits all students. The language of self-monitoring and introspection can be taught and modeled.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted to different grade levels and classroom schedules. The specific behaviors to monitor can be chosen based on class needs.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor the frequency and accuracy of student self-reports. Observe changes in on-task behavior, classroom interruptions, and overall classroom climate.

## Impact
- Increases on-task time, decreases classroom interruptions, and promotes a positive classroom climate. Students become more aware of and honest about their behavior.
