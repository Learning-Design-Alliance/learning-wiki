# Have learners draw their own mental images of idioms to build durable recall

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
After the teacher clarifies idioms through examples, stories and definitions, learners draw their own mental image of each idiom on paper and may show and explain their drawings to peers in small-group work. The article recommends teachers "apply this technique for teaching idioms" because, although drawing may not boost immediate recall, it has a significant effect on delayed recall, and offers students an enjoyable alternative to rote memorization.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher first clarifies idiom meanings through examples, stories and definitions
- Learners produce their own drawings rather than viewing ready-made pictures
#### Constraints
- The study compared drawing only with definitions and stories, not with ready-made pictures or etymological elaboration

### Target Learners
- Intermediate-level Iranian EFL learners (ages 14-18)

### Target Learning Goals
- delayed recall of English idioms
