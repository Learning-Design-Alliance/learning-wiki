# Use beneficiary framing and algorithm transparency interventions to shape educators' trust and behavioral intentions toward AI systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends experimentally testing interventions that vary the framing of AI systems and their algorithm transparency and literacy support. Beneficiary framing draws on persuasion research showing a message resonates more when relevant to the audience's perspective — educators care about educator benefits versus student benefits, as when doctors practiced better hand hygiene with patient-safety signs. Algorithm transparency draws on evidence that even a simple algorithm explanation increased learners' trust in a peer grading system that violated their expectations, and that an explanation improving transparency improved student attitudes toward Bayesian Knowledge Tracing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should be tested causally, building on theoretical models of technology acceptance and academic resistance.
#### Constraints
- The article states the goal should not be to maximize educators' trust, which may cause overreliance and an inadvertent loss in trust, but to create conditions for trust to build over time through experience.

### Target Learners
- Educators (teachers, faculty) using or considering AI-powered education technology

### Target Learning Goals
- Building calibrated educator trust in and effective use of AI systems such as predictive learning analytics

### Affordances
- Tam Perceived Usefulness Ease Of Use
