# Design AI companions as co-learners that cannot provide immediate solutions to preserve learner cognitive activity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing AI support so that the AI learns alongside the learner and does not know level solutions in advance, in order to mitigate cognitive offloading and unreflected acceptance of AI output. The authors conclude that "AI companions designed as co-learners can support allocation of cognitive resources between learners and AI while preserving learners' responsibility for conceptual understanding."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The AI must not know the solution at the start of each level and must learn with the learner via reinforcement learning
#### Constraints
- The authors call for generalizing the effects to other settings and ensuring cognitively activating use; the finding comes from one game and one content domain

### Target Learners
- school students
- university students
- general public learners using AI-supported games

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding while preventing cognitive offloading to AI
