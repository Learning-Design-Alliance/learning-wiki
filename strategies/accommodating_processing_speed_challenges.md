# Accommodating Processing Speed Challenges

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Accommodating processing speed challenges means adjusting pacing, task volume, and response formats so that learners who process information slowly can demonstrate knowledge without speed acting as a confound. Processing speed — the rate at which simple cognitive operations are executed — varies widely across learners, develops through childhood and adolescence, and declines with age [Salthouse, T. A. (1996). The processing-speed theory of adult age differences in cognition. *Psychological Review, 103*(3), 403–428](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.103.3.403) [+S]. The strategy is carried out through extended time, reduced item volume, chunked tasks, extended wait time in discussion, and scaffolds that reduce the amount of novel information a learner must hold and manipulate at once.

## Design Implications

Processing speed is a bottleneck resource: slow execution of basic operations consumes working memory that would otherwise go to reasoning and comprehension [Sweller, J., van Merriënboer, J. J. G., & Paas, F. (1998). Cognitive architecture and instructional design. *Educational Psychology Review, 10*(3), 251–296](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1022193728205) [+M]. Effective accommodations therefore do two things — give more time and reduce unnecessary load — rather than lowering the intellectual demand of the task. The goal is to separate speed from competence: assess what learners know, then build fluency deliberately so the accommodation becomes less necessary over time.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear picture of *where* the bottleneck occurs (reading rate, note-taking, oral processing, written output), gathered through observation and [assessment](../elements/assessment.md)
- Materials structured for low-load delivery: [chunking](../principles/chunking.md) of content, [clear structure](../principles/clear-structure-presentation.md), and [advance organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) so learners can orient before high-speed input begins
- Explicit norms that time pressure is not part of the learning goal, so extended time is actually used rather than abandoned under peer pressure

#### Constraints
- Extended time alone produces modest and inconsistent gains; it helps most when paired with strategy instruction, and it can inflate scores for students who do not need it, threatening validity [Lovett, B. J. (2010). Extended time testing accommodations for students with disabilities: Answers to five fundamental questions. *Review of Educational Research, 80*(4), 611–638](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654310364063) [~M]
- Reducing pace and volume for learners who have already automatized the component skills is redundant and can depress engagement [Expertise reversal: guidance becomes less effective as expertise grows.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Removing *all* time pressure can eliminate retrieval effort that supports retention [Spaced repetition improves retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [~W] — some timed practice remains valuable once accuracy is established
- Over-accommodation without fading can undermine self-efficacy and self-advocacy; supports should be contingent and progressively withdrawn [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [~M]
- Accommodations applied uniformly without diagnosis can mask the actual bottleneck — a slow reader given extra time on a listening task gains nothing

#### Implementation Variability
- **Assessment:** extended time (typically 1.5×), reduced item sets, or separate quiet settings
- **Instruction:** increased wait time after questions — extending teacher wait time from ~1 second to 3+ seconds lengthens and improves student responses [Rowe, M. B. (1986). Wait time: Slowing down may be a way of speeding up! *Journal of Teacher Education, 37*(1), 43–50](https://doi.org/10.1177/002248718603700110) [+S]
- **Materials:** guided or partially completed notes, audio versions of text ([audiobooks](../principles/audiobooks.md)), text-to-speech tools
- **Output:** speech-to-text, scribing, or oral responses in place of timed written output

### Target Learners
- Students with identified disabilities involving processing speed (ADHD, specific learning disabilities, autism spectrum conditions) — chronic time pressure erodes the confidence these learners need to persist [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+S]
- Second-language learners processing in a non-dominant language, for whom slower lexical access mimics low processing speed
- Older adult learners, given the robust general slowing of processing with age [Salthouse, T. A. (1996). The processing-speed theory of adult age differences in cognition. *Psychological Review, 103*(3), 403–428](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.103.3.403) [+S]
- Less beneficial for learners with high automaticity in the relevant skills, for whom extra time adds little [Expertise reversal: guidance becomes less effective as expertise grows.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- **Knowledge and comprehension goals** where speed is incidental to the objective — the default case for most content learning
- **Strategy acquisition**, supported by [coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and explicit strategy instruction that makes slow processing more efficient over time
- **Fluency goals** (reading rate, math fact retrieval) — accommodated during acquisition, then addressed directly through [practice](../elements/practice.md) rather than permanently bypassed

### Instructions
1. **Diagnose the bottleneck.** Use observation and [assessment](../elements/assessment.md) data to determine whether the difficulty lies in input (reading, listening), processing (reasoning under load), or output (writing, producing).
2. **Reduce input load.** Pre-teach structure with [advance organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md), supply guided notes for [annotating](../principles/annotating.md), and segment content into manageable units [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S].
3. **Adjust pacing in interaction.** Extend wait time after questions, slow delivery of novel information, and repeat key points across modalities [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [~M].
4. **Accommodate output.** Allow extended time, speech-to-text, or oral responses on tasks where the goal is reasoning rather than writing fluency.
5. **Build fluency deliberately.** Schedule short, frequent, timed [practice](../elements/practice.md) on component skills so automaticity develops and the accommodation becomes less necessary [Part-task practice reduces load for novices.](../claims/part-task-practice-reduces-load-for-novices.md) [+M].
6. **Fade and monitor.** Use [check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md) and [assessment for learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) to review which accommodations remain necessary, withdrawing supports as automaticity grows [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M].

## Examples
- **Khan Academy** (https://www.khanacademy.org) — pauseable, self-paced video with transcript controls lets learners set their own processing rate rather than the instructor's.
- **Microsoft Immersive Reader** (https://www.microsoft.com/immersive-reader) — text-to-speech, syllabification, and line-focus tools that slow and structure text input for readers with slow decoding.
- **CAST UDL Guidelines** (https://udlguidelines.cast.org) — the "timing" and "executive function" guidelines operationalize this strategy as universal design rather than individual exception.
- **College Board accommodations** (https://accommodations.collegeboard.org) — documented extended-time (50% or 100%) accommodations on SAT/AP testing, the most widely researched formal implementation of this strategy [Lovett, B. J. (2010). Extended time testing accommodations for students with disabilities: Answers to five fundamental questions. *Review of Educational Research, 80*(4), 611–638](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654310364063).
