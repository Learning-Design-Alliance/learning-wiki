# Pre-college seedbed research program measuring solar radio activity with Radio Jove, without lectures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Young Talent Program of Mathematics, Science and Technology at Fundación Universitaria Konrad Lorenz runs yearly since 2012 for 30 selected high-school participants, including some from disadvantaged communities. Instructors propose a main objective and answer questions "with no lectures at all"; in 2014 participants measured solar activity via radio waves using NASA's Radio Jove antennas without NASA's main software, writing their own analysis in Python with Numpy, Matplotlib and SciPy. Data collection was done in public parks to engage the general public.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An admission exam selecting participants by problem-solving ability; two instructors supporting participants for one year; Radio Jove receiver kit and antenna hardware
#### Constraints
- The JOVE setup typically costs under 350 USD including receiver kit, antenna hardware, software and assembly tools, which the authors list as an advantage of low cost

### Target Learners
- high-school students from Bogotá and nearby cities, including disadvantaged communities

### Target Learning Goals
- scientific computing techniques
- data collection and analysis
- presenting science to the public
