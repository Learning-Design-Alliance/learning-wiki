# Use dynamic testing with graduated prompts scenarios as part of psycho-educational assessment to tailor instruction to individual needs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adding dynamic test procedures with graduated prompts scenarios to the school psychologist's assessment repertoire, because such procedures reveal children's learning processes during complex cognitive task solving and specify at which step in the problem solving process different children need help. The authors state: "we strongly recommend adding dynamic test procedures more often to the school psychologist's assessment repertoire", noting the procedure fits with needs-based assessment, planning interventions and response to interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trained tester who can choose the right protocol in response to each action of a child and score prompt frequencies
#### Constraints
- The multiple protocols were rather difficult for testers to learn, apply and score; the authors suggest a computerized training such as a tangible table would help support administration and scoring

### Target Learners
- second-grade children with arithmetic difficulties

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying individual need for instruction in multi-step problem solving and early math skills
