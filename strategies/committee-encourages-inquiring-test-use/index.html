A test-standards committee should encourage the continued inquiring use of every possible form of test or scale rather than force decisions among systems¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

This strategy directs any coordinating committee in mental testing away from adjudicating among competing test systems and toward pluralistic encouragement of standardization work. Hollingworth writes that if there is to be a committee, "its work should consist in the encouragement of the continued inquiring use of every possible form of test or scale which anyone has the genius to formulate and the patience to standardize in a preliminary fashion." The strategy follows from his judgment that settling on universal scales or scoring methods is premature.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Test forms and scales formulated by workers willing to standardize them in a preliminary fashion must be available for the committee to encourage.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • individuals assessed with mental tests across age ranges and school grades

Target Learning Goals¶

  • standardization and continued improvement of mental tests and scales

Claims¶

  • Settling on final age-range or universal test scales is premature while competing test systems correlate over 90 per cent [+W]
