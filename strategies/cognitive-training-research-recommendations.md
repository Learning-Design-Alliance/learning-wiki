# Redirect cognitive-training research toward near transfer, computational modeling, and process-level analysis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend that because reliable far-transfer effects are unlikely, research efforts should be redirected to near transfer or other methods for improving cognition. Concrete recommendations include putting data and analysis code online, using "well-specified theories (e.g., computational models) to derive predictions," testing "interventions in silico before testing them in vivo," collecting fine-grained data such as eye movements and response times, understanding participants' strategies, and carrying out task analyses of training and test tasks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Data, analysis code, and other relevant information placed online
- Computational models that simulate both training and transfer tasks with the same stimuli as human experiments
#### Constraints
- Blind large-scale search of the intervention space is impractical without strong theoretical constraints

### Target Learners
- cognitive-training researchers
- adults
- children

### Target Learning Goals
- working memory
- fluid intelligence
- transfer of learning
