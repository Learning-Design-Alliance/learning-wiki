# Use computer textual glosses as a short-term, autonomous vocabulary episode supplemented with pushed-output tasks for retention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends treating textual glossing as an interim solution: glossing unfamiliar words acts as "an autonomous vocabulary episode" to be complemented later with direct teaching or other vocabulary activities. For retention, the authors state that more robust "pushed output" tasks must be designed to sustain the initial gains, since gloss-only use and the study's tests did not give learners sufficient opportunities to produce output.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Gloss use must be followed by tasks creating more instances of noticing and processing to enable long-term gains
#### Constraints
- Textual glossing alone enhances vocabulary learning only in the short term; gains were not sustained after three weeks

### Target Learners
- Second language learners at varying proficiency levels

### Target Learning Goals
- Short-term and sustained second language vocabulary knowledge
