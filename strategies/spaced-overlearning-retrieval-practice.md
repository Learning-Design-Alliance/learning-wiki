# Achieve automaticity through spaced overlearning via retrieval practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that fundamentals needed frequently in physics and chemistry be memorized to automaticity — fast, implicit, automatic retrieval from long-term memory — and that this requires overlearning, defined as "sustained practice, beyond the point of mastery." Overlearning begins with retrieval practice activities such as flashcards, writing tables of relationships from memory, mnemonics, sequence recitation, and clicker questions, which apply the testing effect. To promote lasting wiring in LTM, overlearning should be spaced over several days and periodically thereafter (distributed practice), since crammed knowledge tends to require intensive re-study while spaced overlearning supports savings in relearning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Practice at recall must be repeated over several days and periodically thereafter rather than massed in one day
#### Constraints
- Gaining automaticity in recall of appropriate facts and procedures requires time and effort; daily physiological capacity for LTM wiring change is limited, so instructors should identify which fundamentals to overlearn

### Target Learners
- students in introductory physics and chemistry courses

### Target Learning Goals
- automatic recall of fundamental facts, procedures, and relationships for problem solving
