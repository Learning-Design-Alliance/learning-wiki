# Use a weekly commit-then-feedback ECG reading program with a validated Accuracy score to provide objective feedback and rank trainee interpretation skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends addressing competency gaps in ECG interpretation through specialized training programs such as pECGreview, in which participants commit to a specific free-text interpretation each week, then receive the official interpretation with teaching points. The validated Accuracy score can be applied to as few as five interpretations per participant to give "objective feedback on their ECG interpretation skills" and rank participants, providing a framework for future descriptive and interventional studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An experienced pediatric electrophysiologist assigns an official interpretation to each ECG as the scoring benchmark
- Participants submit independent free-text interpretations before receiving feedback
#### Constraints
- The Accuracy score is subjective and shows demonstrable systematic bias between evaluators; the format tests interpretation outside a true clinical context and without computer-generated interpretation

### Target Learners
- Pediatric cardiology fellows
- Other trainees and faculty in pediatric cardiology

### Target Learning Goals
- Pediatric ECG interpretation accuracy and competency
