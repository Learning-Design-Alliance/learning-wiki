# Teach vocabulary by explicitly instructing learners in cognate structures between their first language and English

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers make cross-language similarity explicit: before vocabulary work, learners receive instruction about structural similarities between a language they know and English, so they can recognize cognate words. The author states that "we need to give education about cognate words and structures in order to recognize the similarities between languages", and that cognate studies can be used in regions where Kurdish is widely spoken.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The learner's existing language must share cognate words with English (shared etymology or orthographic similarity)
#### Constraints
- The study covered only one group of cognate words, which the author names a limitation; grammatical-structure similarities remain to be studied systematically

### Target Learners
- Kurdish-Turkish bilingual secondary students
- learners whose first language is related to English

### Target Learning Goals
- English vocabulary acquisition and retention
