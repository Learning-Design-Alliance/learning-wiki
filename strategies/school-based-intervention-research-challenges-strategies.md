# Plan school-based intervention research around recruitment, scheduling, dosage, and fidelity challenges

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's discussion of challenges yields implementable guidance for classroom-based efficacy research: expect teacher preferences about random assignment and apprehension about control-group participation during recruitment; anticipate diverse middle-school intervention schedules and mid-year student movement that limit intervention time; add a dosage variable to control for differing completion times and absences; use audio recordings transcribed with software as evidence of implementation fidelity; and analyze control teachers' instructional approaches when their curricula differ, which becomes especially valuable when there are no discernable performance differences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional approval, district permission and access, and informed consent procedures
#### Constraints
- Requiring all teachers to record their lessons deters some teachers from participating; assessing control teachers' fidelity is not feasible when curricula differ

### Target Learners
- sixth-grade mathematics intervention classes

### Target Learning Goals
- valid evaluation of classroom-delivered instructional interventions
