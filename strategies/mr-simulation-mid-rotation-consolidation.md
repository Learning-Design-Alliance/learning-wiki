# Integrate a mixed reality simulation session as a mid-rotation consolidation supplement to clinical teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents EyelearnMR as a mid-rotation consolidation session that can be integrated into an existing rotation timetable without displacing core teaching: the 2-hour session and assessment were scheduled on days 8 and 9 of a 2-week posting. Because the hybrid curriculum was noninferior to traditional teaching with less clinical exposure, the authors conclude it "is an effective supplementary teaching tool in ophthalmic education and may confer additional learning benefits."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to MR headsets and funding for deployment; a structured and guided session consistent with pedagogical best practices
#### Constraints
- Results apply to a single examination skill (ocular motility) with tightly defined parameters; the observed improvement cannot be attributed solely to the MR software because the structured session also included guided demonstration, self-paced exploration, and repeated practice with immediate feedback

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in specialty rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical examination skills
- diagnostic competencies

## Claims

- [Medical students using EyelearnMR plus traditional teaching scored significantly higher on a video-based ocular motility assessment than students receiving traditional teaching alone, despite less clinical exposure time](../claims/eyelearnmr-higher-video-assessment-scores.md) [+W]
