# Align interprofessional pediatric emergency team training with a needs assessment of profession-specific roles, learning behaviors and training needs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educational designs for team trainings be aligned to participants' needs to increase effectiveness, using a survey of experience, prior training and individual needs as the first step in developing an in-house program. It states that "Differences of profession-specific roles and requirements, as well as in learning behaviors must be taken into account when developing interprofessional team trainings." The authors further suggest a blended learning approach with tailored preparation for each profession to address differing learning behaviors and enhance acceptance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A needs assessment identifying frequent and critical in-house emergency situations, supporting and impeding aspects, and individual training objectives before designing the training
#### Constraints
- The survey data reflect subjective perceptions of staff of one tertiary pediatric center in Germany and may not be fully applicable to other pediatric emergency care sites; Likert means provide only exploratory insights

### Target Learners
- physicians and nurses in pediatric hospital settings

### Target Learning Goals
- management of pediatric in-hospital emergencies including basic life support, emergency algorithms, and team communication
