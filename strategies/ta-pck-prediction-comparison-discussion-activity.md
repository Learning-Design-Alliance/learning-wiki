# Professional development activity: TAs predict students' most common incorrect answers on a validated conceptual survey, then compare with actual student data and discuss

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 11 claims (11 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes and recommends a professional development activity in which TAs complete a PCK task on a validated conceptual survey: they identify the correct answer, predict the most common incorrect answer introductory students would select after lecture-based instruction, and explain their reasoning. TAs are then shown quantitative student data on which incorrect options were most common, followed by discussion of the student reasoning behind those choices. The article states such an activity "can be quite valuable in enhancing TAs' knowledge of student difficulties" and that this reflection process can promote robust understanding of student thinking patterns.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a validated conceptual multiple-choice survey and quantitative post-instruction response data from introductory students
#### Constraints
- The article notes the activity targets alternate conceptions persisting after traditional lecture-based instruction, since those are most important for TAs to be aware of

### Target Learners
- first-year physics graduate teaching assistants in a pedagogy-oriented professional development course

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of common introductory student alternate conceptions and difficulties with physics content
