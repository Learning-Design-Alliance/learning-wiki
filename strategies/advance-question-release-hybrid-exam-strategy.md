# Distribute exam questions one week in advance with in-class independent written solutions to reduce test anxiety without losing academic-integrity controls

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementable assessment recipe: give students the actual exam questions a week early so they can engage in group-oriented problem solving and practice writing and rewriting concise solutions, then require independent in-class written solutions under integrity controls. The article reports that groups of up to 10 students discussed the problems, and that students "should practice writing and rewriting solutions to the exam questions in order to develop a concise pathway from the problem statement to the realized answer."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must have the next exam nearly ready as soon as one is graded and returned, for a semester with multiple such exams
- Significantly greater professor time for developing test questions and grading
- Integrity measures: prohibit programmable calculators and warn that numerical values may be changed trivially
#### Constraints
- The authors found these integrity measures necessary based on the results of the first exam of the semester, which was excluded from the analysis
- Instructor gender differed between cohorts (a man taught control, a woman taught experimental), a potential confounder the datasets were too small to assess

### Target Learners
- Early college students in introductory physics experiencing high test anxiety

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing test anxiety while measuring student knowledge and promoting self-reported mastery
