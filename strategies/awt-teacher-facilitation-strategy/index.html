Teachers facilitate AWT by supporting peer correspondence, collecting papers, and guiding error correction without direct interference¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's teacher recommendations for running AWT: the teacher "should support everyone in the classroom to correspond with each other, but must be cautious not to interfere too much," maintain a relaxed environment where learners are not afraid to make mistakes, collect papers each lesson, write mistakes on the board, and help via guided discovery when students cannot find the errors.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A seating arrangement allowing students to see each other and exchange papers
  • End-of-lesson paper collection and board-based error listing

Constraints¶

  • Guided to Turkish-as-a-foreign-language writing lessons in the article, though the authors suggest the method could be improved upon and used for teaching different languages

Target Learners¶

  • foreign language learners at B2 level

Target Learning Goals¶

  • writing skills
  • error self-correction
  • communication

Affordances¶

  • Ask Write Throw Technique

Claims¶

  • Learners attributed AWT's greatest benefits to socialization and vocabulary learning, and all 18 experimental participants found the technique helpful [+W]
  • After 8 weeks, the AWT experimental group scored significantly higher than the control group on all five writing criteria, with lower error count [+W]
  • Before the intervention, the AWT experimental group and the traditional-instruction control group showed no significant difference on any of the five writing-skill criteria [+W]
  • The control group's writing skills also improved significantly over the 8 weeks under traditional instruction [~W]
