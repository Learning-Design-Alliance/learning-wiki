# Goal Setting With Learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Goal setting with learners is the practice of jointly establishing specific, measurable, and appropriately challenging targets for learning, rather than imposing them unilaterally. Goals may be set for a lesson, unit, or longer arc, and are most effective when learners participate in defining them, monitor progress toward them, and revise them based on feedback. The strategy converts diffuse intentions ("get better at writing") into concrete commitments ("write a five-paragraph essay with a clear thesis by Friday").

## Design Implications

Goal setting works because specific, difficult goals focus attention and energize effort more reliably than vague "do your best" exhortations [Locke & Latham's goal-setting theory](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705) [+S]. Proximal, sub-goals are especially powerful for learners: they provide frequent success experiences that build self-efficacy, which in turn sustains engagement with distal goals [Proximal sub-goals raise self-efficacy and performance.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.41.3.586) [+S]. Involving learners in setting their own goals also supports autonomy and ownership, strengthening intrinsic motivation [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Goals that are specific and, where appropriate, learning-focused (mastery) rather than only performance-focused (outperforming others)
- A baseline or diagnostic so goals are set relative to current performance ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md), [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md))
- A visible mechanism for tracking progress and revisiting goals ([Action Planning](../strategies/action_planning.md), [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md))
- Feedback loops so learners can tell whether they are on track ([Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md))

#### Constraints
- Goals framed as external performance comparisons can undermine intrinsic motivation and produce shallow strategies [Overly controlling goal imposition can crowd out intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [-M]
- Goals set beyond the learner's perceived reach can trigger avoidance and disengagement, particularly for low-efficacy learners [~M]
- Overly numerous or competing goals fragment attention; each additional goal dilutes effort on the others [~S]
- For open-ended exploratory learning, tightly specified goals can narrow search prematurely and reduce incidental learning [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Proximal vs. distal:** break long-term goals into short-cycle sub-goals (daily, weekly) to build efficacy through frequent success [Proximal sub-goals raise self-efficacy and performance.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.41.3.586) [+S]
- **Teacher-set vs. co-constructed vs. learner-set:** younger or novice learners typically need teacher-scaffolded goal setting; self-set goals become feasible as self-regulation develops
- **Mastery vs. performance framing:** mastery goals ("understand X") support deeper processing; performance goals can motivate but risk surface strategies [~M]
- **Individual vs. shared goals:** team goals in [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) contexts add accountability

### Target Learners
- Learners with weak self-regulation benefit most from externally structured, proximal goals [Goal setting supports self-regulated learning.](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) [+M]
- Low self-efficacy learners, for whom achievable micro-goals provide early success experiences [Proximal sub-goals raise self-efficacy and performance.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.41.3.586) [+S]
- Adolescents and adults, who can participate meaningfully in co-constructing goals; young children need concrete, immediate, teacher-framed targets [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-regulation: planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's own learning
- Skill development over extended time horizons (writing, mathematics fluency, language learning)
- Motivation and persistence in long or effortful learning projects

### Instructions
1. Establish a baseline with a diagnostic task or [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) so goals are grounded in current performance.
2. Co-construct a specific, challenging but attainable goal with the learner; convert vague aims into concrete targets using [Achievable Micro-Goals](../strategies/achievable_micro-goals.md).
3. Break the goal into proximal sub-goals with short timelines, recorded in an [Action Plan](../strategies/action_planning.md).
4. Schedule regular [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) to monitor progress, give feedback, and adjust goals that prove too easy or too hard.
5. Have learners reflect on goal attainment and set the next goal, gradually transferring ownership ([Academic Choice](academic-choice-planning-working-reflecting.md)).

## Examples
- **Writing portfolios (elementary):** students set a weekly writing goal ("use two transition words"), track it on a goal card, and review it in teacher conferences — the proximal-goal structure shown to raise self-efficacy in Bandura and Schunk's studies [+S]
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — learners set mastery goals per skill and see progress meters; the platform's goal and streak features externalize progress monitoring
- **Morisano et al. (2010) goal-setting intervention:** struggling university students who elaborated and reflected on personal goals improved GPA relative to controls [+S] ([doi:10.1037/a0019472](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019472))
