# Self Assessment Rubrics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Self assessment rubrics are explicit scoring guides listing quality criteria and performance levels that learners apply to their own drafts, performances, or solutions. The learner, not the instructor, performs the evaluative act: judging work against criteria, identifying gaps, and planning revisions. The strategy is typically carried out before submission (draft-stage self-scoring), at submission (reflective cover sheets), or after feedback (comparing self-scores with instructor scores).

## Design Implications
Self assessment with rubrics develops *evaluative judgment* — the capacity to recognize quality in one's own work — which is a precondition for self-regulated improvement [Assessment for learning positions learners as active evaluators rather than passive recipients of grades.](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) [+M]. Rubrics also make feedback actionable: when feedback is tied to visible criteria, learners can locate it at the task and process level, where feedback has its strongest effects [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels rather than the self.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. The rubric must be taught, not merely distributed — learners need practice applying criteria to samples before their self-ratings align with expert judgments.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A rubric with a small number of specific, observable criteria and clearly differentiated performance levels
- Orientation activities: scoring anonymized sample work (strong and weak) and discussing discrepancies before self-scoring ([Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) and [Non-Examples](../elements/non-examples.md) work well as calibration samples)
- A follow-up action: revision, reflection, or a plan, so the self-judgment feeds back into work rather than ending as a score
- Instructor or peer scores for comparison, so learners can calibrate their judgment over time

#### Constraints
- Untrained self-assessment is systematically inaccurate: novices tend to overestimate quality, and self-grading without calibration can inflate confidence without improving work [-M]
- Holistic or vague rubrics ("well-organized, insightful") produce unreliable self-ratings; criteria must be concrete and exemplified [-M]
- When self-assessment is graded or perceived as high-stakes, learners score strategically rather than honestly, destroying the calibration signal [-M]
- Rubric-driven self-assessment can narrow attention to listed criteria and suppress creative risk-taking if the rubric is treated as exhaustive [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Draft-stage self-scoring:** learners score a draft and revise before submission; strongest form for improving the artifact
- **Calibration comparison:** learners score their work, then compare with instructor scores; discrepancies become discussion points
- **Co-constructed rubrics:** the class negotiates criteria, deepening ownership of the standards [+W]
- **Single-point rubrics:** criteria listed once with blank space for evidence of meeting/falling short; reduces box-ticking and prompts specific self-commentary
- **Exemplar-anchored rubrics:** each level illustrated with authentic samples, which supports abstraction of quality criteria [Comparing multiple contrasting cases helps learners abstract the dimensions that define quality.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Intermediate learners who have enough domain knowledge to apply criteria but not yet the judgment to self-correct without support
- Novices benefit only after calibration training with exemplars; unguided self-rating by novices is unreliable [-M]
- Advanced learners may find detailed rubrics constraining; single-point or criterion-light formats suit them better [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Quality criteria knowledge: knowing what distinguishes strong from weak performance in a domain
- Self-regulation: monitoring, judging, and revising one's own work ([Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md))
- Metacognitive accuracy: closing the gap between perceived and actual performance

### Instructions
1. Select or co-construct 3–6 criteria aligned with the learning goals; write concrete descriptors for each level ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
2. Gather 2–3 authentic samples at different quality levels; have learners score them in groups and discuss discrepancies to calibrate judgment before touching their own work
3. Have learners score their own draft, citing specific evidence from the work for each rating — require written justification, not just a checkmark
4. Pair self-scores with [Peer Assessment](../elements/peer-assessment.md) or instructor scores; treat large discrepancies as the teaching moment
5. Require a revision or action plan based on the lowest-rated criteria, so judgment converts into improvement
6. Repeat across multiple assignments; fade rubric detail as learners internalize the criteria

## Related Strategies
- [Peer Assessment](../elements/peer-assessment.md) — same criteria applied to others' work; often easier to judge honestly and calibrates self-judgment indirectly
- [Portfolio Assessment](portfolio-assessment.md) — self-assessment rubrics give portfolios their evaluative spine across artifacts
- [Error Analysis](../principles/error-analysis.md) — self-identified errors against criteria build the same monitoring skill
- [Reflective Journals](reflective-journals.md) — a qualitative complement that captures the reasoning behind self-ratings

## Examples
- **[Calibrated Peer Review](https://calibratedpeerreview.org)** — learners first score instructor-seeded calibration essays; only after their scores align with expert scores do they review peers and themselves, directly operationalizing rubric calibration.
- **Single-point rubrics in writing workshops** — teachers list criteria once; students annotate their drafts with evidence of meeting each criterion and flag one criterion to strengthen in revision.
- **Design studio crits (architecture, engineering capstones)** — students present work against a published rubric, self-rate before the crit, then compare their ratings with panel feedback.
