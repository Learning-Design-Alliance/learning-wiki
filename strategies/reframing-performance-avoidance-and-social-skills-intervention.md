# Intervene by reframing performance-avoidant students' motivations toward mastery and teaching them relationship-initiation strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that teachers design interventions addressing both motivation and social behaviour: an intervention targeting students with high performance avoidance goals "could focus on reframing their academic motivations towards mastery and providing strategies to initiate and maintain social relationships". They also suggest teachers "could implement collaborative work strategies that encourage students to focus on personal improvement and learning", for example via TARGET guidelines, to foster mastery approach goals linked to more central network positions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers able to implement mastery-promoting goal structures (e.g. TARGET guidelines) and to deliver social-skills support alongside motivational reframing
#### Constraints
- Grounded in cross-sectional findings, so the direction of effects between goals and positioning is unknown; the article states future longitudinal work is needed to inform such interventions

### Target Learners
- high-school students, especially those with high performance avoidance goals

### Target Learning Goals
- adaptive (mastery approach) achievement goals and effective, reciprocated peer social connections

## Claims

- [Performance avoidance goals are negatively associated with in-degree but positively associated with out-degree centrality, suggesting ineffective social outreach](../claims/performance-avoidance-goals-ineffective-social-outreach.md) [+M]
