# Mechanical Binet testers should report findings as tables of related chronological and mental ages, not as judgments of normality or abnormality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (5 for, 3 mixed) · 5 studies (3 theoretical, 1 review, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy directs examiners who can do good mechanical Binet testing without being clinical psychologists to report their examinations of children or groups in tables of related chronological and mental ages rather than in terms of normality or abnormality. The article argues this is the satisfactory way of reporting Binet results, since only the expert can evaluate lesser degrees of retardation for diagnosis or prognosis. Within this format, examiners may state with high certainty that children showing retardation of more than 3 years are feeble-minded.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examiners must be capable of doing good Binet testing of the mechanical sort and report both chronological and mental ages for each case examined.
#### Constraints
- Examiners using this reporting format should not say that children testing less than 3 years retarded are backward or normal, since only the expert can evaluate lesser degrees of retardation.

### Target Learners
- children examined with the Binet Scale

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate classification of children's intellectual development
