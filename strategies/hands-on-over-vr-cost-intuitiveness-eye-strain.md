# Favor hands-on simulation over VR when cost, intuitiveness, and eye strain matter

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The discussion notes that "hands-on simulation demonstrates advantages over VR simulation in terms of cost, intuitiveness, and the avoidance of eye strain" and cites a case where VR underperformed hands-on training on resuscitation flow time (Issleib et al.), "underscor[ing] the need to carefully consider the differences between VR simulation and real-life scenarios". This supports choosing physical hands-on models for some anatomical teaching contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Consider the teaching context when choosing between VR and physical simulation
#### Constraints
- The cited VR disadvantage evidence comes from other studies (Issleib et al.; Einloft et al.), not from this study's own data

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- three-dimensional anatomical concepts requiring physical manipulation
