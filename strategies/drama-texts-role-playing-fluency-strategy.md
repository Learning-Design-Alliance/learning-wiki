# Use modern English drama texts with role-playing tasks to develop speaking fluency in EFL courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends teaching speaking through modern English drama texts combined with task-based and role-playing activities, because such texts "bridge the gap between course-book dialogues and natural usage" and can "become a main aid in the acquisition of communicative competence". The study implemented this as a ten-week treatment with pretest, oral interview and voice recording, analyzed with Praat software.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must justify why dramatic methods suit the class, considering curriculum relevance, linguistic accessibility, intrinsic interest and practicality
#### Constraints
- Because the texts are based on informal oral communication with little grammatical content, the strategy supports fluency more than accuracy

### Target Learners
- University EFL/ELT students

### Target Learning Goals
- Oral fluency
- communicative competence
