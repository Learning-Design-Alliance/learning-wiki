# Improve CVD prevention decision aids by explaining CVD risk basics, including medication and lifestyle options, balancing icon-array risk presentation, and adding action support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose four concrete improvements to make CVD prevention decision aids suitable for low health literacy populations: "a basic explanation of what CVD is and what CVD risk means"; inclusion of both medication and lifestyle options; balanced presentation of risks and benefits using visual communication aids such as icon arrays; and more support for what actions to take based on the decision made. These follow directly from the evaluation deficits found.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Decision aid developers should present all options, risks and benefits in a balanced and unbiased way, with visual representation of numerical information
#### Constraints
- The article notes IPDAS rating items did not cover health literate design issues such as use of white space, images consistent with text, and clear direction for next steps

### Target Learners
- patients with low health literacy considering CVD prevention medication in primary care

### Target Learning Goals
- informed shared decision-making about CVD prevention options
- understanding of CVD risk and the benefits and harms of medication and lifestyle options
