# Strategies for implementing upper-division conceptual assessments: early faculty involvement, simple rubrics, multiple-choice versions, and low-stakes framing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a set of implementation strategies for overcoming barriers to upper-division conceptual assessment use. To reduce faculty resistance, the authors "solicited faculty involvement early in the development process"; to reduce logistical barriers they offered grading help, designed the CCMI and CURrENT with simple rubrics, and developed multiple-choice and multiple-response versions. Student resistance is addressed by "Framing the tests as valuable, but low stakes measures of students' understanding" and providing individualized feedback and review office hours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must dedicate a full 50-minute class period to administer the assessment
- Assessments must be kept secure, so solutions are not provided to students
#### Constraints
- Variable content coverage between institutions makes one-size-fits-all assessment difficult; some external institutions argued the instruments favor CU's content and teaching styles

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics majors
- physics instructors and departments

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring conceptual understanding of upper-division physics content
- sustaining use of transformed curricular materials
