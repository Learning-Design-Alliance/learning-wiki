# Worked Example Routine

## Description
This routine provides a structured approach to using worked examples in teaching to prevent passive or superficial engagement. It sequences five steps — Get ready (present the problem), Read (students study the worked example), Discuss (partners talk until everyone understands each step), Explain (students answer explanation prompts about why steps work), and You Try (students solve a similar problem independently). The routine operationalizes the finding that worked examples benefit learning only when learners actively process the reasoning, not merely view the solution [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M].

## Design Implications

The routine addresses the central failure mode of example-based learning: learners skim a completed solution, experience fluency, and mistake recognition for understanding [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [-S]. By inserting discussion and self-explanation prompts between reading and independent practice, the routine forces learners to reconstruct the reasoning behind each step, which builds schemas more effectively than silent study [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A high-quality worked example with steps that are individually meaningful — each line should be annotatable with a "why" ([Worked Examples](../principles/worked-examples.md))
- Explanation prompts targeting the rationale for steps, not restatement of what was done (e.g., "Why was this equation set up this way?")
- A structurally similar "You Try" problem matched to the example so transfer is near enough to succeed but not identical
- Structured partner discussion with norms that require both students to explain, not one to copy

#### Constraints
- Skipping the Explain step reduces the routine to example-plus-practice, losing most of the self-explanation benefit
- Poorly matched "You Try" problems (too hard, too different) reintroduce unguided search and negate the example's load reduction [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [-M]
- For learners with substantial prior knowledge, the full routine becomes redundant and slows progress [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M] — fade the routine as expertise grows ([Fading](../elements/fading.md))
- Discussion pairs without accountability norms can produce answer-copying rather than explanation

#### Implementation Variability
- **Completion problems**: replace "You Try" with a partially worked example learners finish, easing the example-to-practice transition
- **Alternating pairs**: alternate worked example / problem to solve across a problem set, rather than batching all examples first
- **Faded routine**: for experienced learners, drop Read and Discuss, keeping only explanation prompts on critical steps
- **Error analysis variant**: present a worked example containing a deliberate error for partners to find during Discuss

### Target Learners
- Novices in a domain, particularly in mathematics and science where solution procedures are step-structured [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]
- Learners with low prior knowledge who would otherwise engage in inefficient means-ends search [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]
- Less appropriate for advanced learners, who benefit more from independent problem solving than from studying full solutions

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency: acquiring and correctly sequencing solution steps
- Conceptual understanding: connecting procedure to underlying principles via explanation prompts
- Schema construction: recognizing which problem type a solution method applies to

### Instructions
1. **Get ready** — Present the problem statement and activate relevant prior knowledge before showing the solution ([Activation](../elements/activation.md))
2. **Read** — Students study the worked example individually, attending to each step ([Worked Examples](../principles/worked-examples.md))
3. **Discuss** — Partners explain each step to one another until both can justify it ([Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md))
4. **Explain** — Students respond in writing to prompts asking *why* each key step was taken ([Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md))
5. **You Try** — Students solve a structurally similar problem independently ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)); check before assigning further problems

## Examples
- **[Use Worked Examples](../strategies/use_worked_examples.md)** — the parent strategy: present a fully solved problem with step-by-step reasoning, then a similar problem for independent solution.
- **Mathematics classrooms** — a teacher presents a two-step equation solution, pairs discuss why the inverse operation was applied at each line, students write explanations, then solve a matched problem; the sequence repeats with a faded (partially worked) example next.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — video worked examples followed by practice with on-demand hints, approximating the Read → You Try sequence digitally, though the Discuss and Explain steps must be added by the teacher.
