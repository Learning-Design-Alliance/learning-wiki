# Instructors should adhere to assessment best practices: rubrics, grading by category, item-bias analysis, and universal design

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that individual instructors, though constrained by academic freedom and grading autonomy, can and should adhere to best practices when creating quizzes and exams. These include conducting their own reliability studies such as finding coefficient alpha, analyzing and discarding biased items, using "more rigorous and standardized rubrics" or grading by category, and designing assessments that follow principles of universal design to reduce construct-irrelevant variance and bias. Administrators are urged to encourage such practices through teacher evaluations and promotion decisions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Individual instructors must take time to conduct their own reliability and item-bias analyses, which may be rather cumbersome
- Administrators should encourage these practices informally and through direct incentives like teacher evaluations and promotion decisions
#### Constraints
- None of these suggestions can be enforced in most cases because of academic freedom and grading autonomy
- Some teachers consider behavior promoting achievement part of achievement and may discount attempts to separate components on principle

### Target Learners
- university physics instructors designing quizzes and exams
- K-12 teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- valid and reliable measurement of student physics understanding through course assessments
