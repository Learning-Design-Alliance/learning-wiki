# Scaffold student problem generation gradually: vote on homework problems first, then modify the most-voted problems, then create new ones

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy stages student involvement in exam-problem creation across a semester. Students first vote anonymously on homework problems they want on the exam, then are given the opportunity to modify the most-voted problems, with some mods being straightforward such as "backwards treatment of variables (inverting given and wanted)" and others elaborate creations. In the Fall 2014 study the voted problems formed a test bank worth 80% of the exam grade, with instructor-chosen complementary questions worth the remaining 20%.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Appropriate scaffolding was implemented to ensure students were capable of creating questions
- An anonymous voting platform (Piazza) and a small points incentive for voting
#### Constraints
- Only about 10% of the Fall class made modifications even when given the opportunity, and motivation was a limiting factor in Spring 2015

### Target Learners
- first-year algebra-based introductory physics students, mostly non-majors

### Target Learning Goals
- deeper engagement with problem concepts, familiarity with exam problems, improved exam performance
