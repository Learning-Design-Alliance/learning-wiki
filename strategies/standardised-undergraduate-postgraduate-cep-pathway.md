# Develop a standardised and accredited training pathway from undergraduate through to postgraduate level for CEP competency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Stakeholders identified that variation in undergraduate taught content produced gaps in knowledge and skill on entry to CEP master's courses, with students feeling set up to fail in screening and prescription. The article recommends that "A standardised and accredited training pathway from undergraduate through to postgraduate level should be explored to allow student competency to be developed over a longer period, to enhance knowledge, skills and competency on graduation and registration." Australia's ESSA multi-tier accreditation framework is cited as a potential model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Accredited curriculum frameworks spanning undergraduate and postgraduate levels
- Undergraduate programmes focused on transferable skills and, ideally, undergraduate placements
#### Constraints
- Framed as requiring future exploration; no clinical placement hours are currently obtained at undergraduate level in the UK

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate and postgraduate exercise science and clinical exercise physiology students

### Target Learning Goals
- Baseline standardisation of practical competencies such as exercise screening, prescription and delivery before master's study
