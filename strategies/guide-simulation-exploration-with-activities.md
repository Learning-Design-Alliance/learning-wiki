# Pair simulations with guided-inquiry activities rather than relying on undirected exploration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that simulations be used with activities that direct exploration toward learning goals. Its interviews show that "students can usually learn some important concepts from undirected exploration of simulations, but they can learn much more from using the simulations in conjunction with activities that guide their exploration." With undirected exploration students often give correct explanations but "do not necessarily recognize that they have learned." In interviews on Quantum Tunneling and Wave Packets, students who could explain the measurement button in wave-packet mode could not explain plane-wave mode until the interviewer asked how far a plane wave extends, suggesting a guided activity including that question would help. The authors provide guidelines for developing guided inquiry activities and a database of activities.
