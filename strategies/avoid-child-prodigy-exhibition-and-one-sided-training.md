# Protect gifted children from public prodigy exhibition and one-sided premature training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Stern condemns the "child-prodigy monstrosities": infant prodigies exhibited in public — reading babies, child mnemonists, lightning calculators, and above all musical prodigies — who are often merely precocious and mature early. He argues that public dragging before audiences, pampering, one-sided development of sensational performances, and lack of quiet, harmonious general development mean "the ruin of their whole lives," and calls for public indignation against parents' avarice and the populace's enthusiasm.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Quiet and harmonious general development instead of one-sided training for virtuosity
#### Constraints
- Applies with special force to rare children bearing the germ of real creative greatness, who suffer bodily and mental injury from a wandering childhood life

### Target Learners
- precociously developed children and child prodigies

### Target Learning Goals
- preservation of whole-life development over early sensational performance
