# Explore computer-based training as a more effective method for retail food safety education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on cited findings that computer-based instruction improved participants' skills and that it can help optimize cognitive load, the authors recommend computer-based curriculum as a new method for more than half of participants. They state: "Based on findings of this study, computer-based curriculum will be a new method for more than half of participants." The recommendation responds to their conclusion that current training methods are not meeting the needs of hot and cold self-service food bars.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Computer-based curriculum must be accessible to employees; the study notes a paper booklet was designed to accommodate individuals without internet access
#### Constraints
- The cited support for computer-based training comes from other studies (Palvia and Palvia 2007; Macaulay and Pantazi 2006; Van Gerven, Paas, and Tabbers 2006), not from an intervention tested in this study

### Target Learners
- managerial and non-managerial grocery store food bar employees

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of understanding of food safety concepts and their importance
