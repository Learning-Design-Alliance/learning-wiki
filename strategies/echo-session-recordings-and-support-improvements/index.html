Strategies

Record ECHO sessions for later viewing and add clinical shadowing and non-physician staff involvement to address time barriers¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on participant-reported barriers of competing time demands, the review recommends recording ECHO sessions for later viewing, noting that "recording ECHO sessions to be viewed at a later time or date could solve this issue". The synthesis table also lists participant feedback for improving ECHO including "archiving recordings of sessions, clinical shadowing of buprenorphine providers, and increasing the involvement of non-physician clinical staff". These are implementable program-design adjustments drawn from participant interviews and feedback.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • ECHO programs must have the technical capacity to record and archive sessions

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation responds to time and availability barriers reported in a 12-week rural primary care ECHO study; the review does not report testing whether recordings improve outcomes

Target Learners¶

  • Primary care physicians and clinical staff in rural primary care clinics

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Sustained participation in OUD tele-mentoring and application of MOUD treatment knowledge

Claims¶

  • ECHO session attendance is variable, higher among prescribers than non-prescribers, with time demands and clinic leadership support as reported barriers [+W]
  • Most teacher programs required significant time, effort, and resources, with mean program attrition of 12.69% and rates reaching 66.67%, suggesting time demands may limit real-world translation [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide recorded live virtual classroom sessions that students can access at any time
