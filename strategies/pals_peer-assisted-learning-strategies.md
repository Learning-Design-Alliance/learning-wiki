# PALS_Peer Assisted Learning Strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Peer Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS) is a classwide peer-tutoring program, developed at Vanderbilt University by Douglas and Lynn Fuchs, in which students work in pairs and alternate between the roles of "coach" and "player." Sessions follow a predictable script — in reading, partner reading with retells and paragraph shrinking, then prediction relay; in math, coached practice with point-earning for correct answers and explanations. The teacher trains students in the routines, monitors pairs, and awards team points, but the bulk of instructional time is spent in structured peer interaction rather than whole-class teaching.

## Design Implications

PALS converts practice time into high-frequency, individualized responding: each student reads, solves, and answers far more often than in whole-class formats, while the coach provides immediate corrective feedback [Active learning improves exam performance relative to lecture-only formats.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. The scripted routines and point system supply the structure that makes peer work effective — unstructured peer talk alone does not reliably produce learning gains. Role reversal matters: serving as coach requires the tutee to articulate, monitor, and evaluate performance, which consolidates the coach's own skill.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated PALS manual and materials for the grade band (K–6 reading, grades 2–6 math, high school variants)
- Teacher-led training in coaching behaviors: prompting, praising, correcting errors without giving answers
- Carefully matched pairs — typically a stronger and weaker student, with ability ranking done privately
- A predictable session schedule (e.g., 2–3 sessions per week, 25–35 minutes) sustained over many weeks
- Ongoing teacher monitoring and point-awarding to keep pairs on task

#### Constraints
- Gains depend on fidelity to the scripts; loosely adapted "peer tutoring" without training and structure shows much weaker effects [-M]
- Weaker students may simply imitate or comply without understanding if the coach lacks the skill to explain errors [-W]
- Requires sustained implementation (typically 12–35 weeks); short trials rarely show measurable effects [~M]
- Not a substitute for explicit, teacher-led instruction on new content — PALS is a practice and consolidation structure, not a first-teaching method [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Reading PALS (K–6):** sounds/words activities in kindergarten; partner reading, paragraph shrinking, and prediction relay in grades 2–6
- **Math PALS (2–6):** coach-and-player practice on computation and concepts with point-earning for correct answers and explanations
- **High School PALS:** adapted routines for struggling readers in secondary content classes
- **CS-PALS / content variants:** adaptations for specific subjects and for students with disabilities within inclusive classrooms

### Target Learners
- Students in grades K–6, with the strongest documented effects for struggling readers and students with learning disabilities [+S]
- Inclusive classrooms, where the dyad structure lets students with disabilities receive individualized practice without pull-out
- Less appropriate for students who need intensive one-to-one expert tutoring beyond what a peer can provide [-W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Fluency building: oral reading fluency, math fact and computation fluency [+S]
- Comprehension strategies: summarizing (paragraph shrinking) and prediction
- Procedural accuracy and self-explanation in mathematics
- Social skills and cooperative behavior through structured role exchange

### Instructions
1. Rank students by skill privately and pair each student with a partner of moderately different ability; assign who starts as coach.
2. Train both roles explicitly — model coaching behaviors with a student demonstration, then have pairs rehearse the routines ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md), [Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
3. Run scripted sessions: players perform (read aloud, solve problems) while coaches prompt, praise, and correct; award points for correct responses and good coaching.
4. Reverse roles each session so every student experiences both performing and coaching ([Reciprocal Teaching](../elements/reciprocal-teaching.md) uses a similar role-exchange logic).
5. Monitor pairs, award team points, and periodically re-assess to re-pair students as skills grow ([Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md)).

## Related Strategies

## Examples
- **Reading PALS (Grades 2–6)** — pairs alternate 10 minutes of partner reading with retell, 5 minutes of paragraph shrinking (summarize each paragraph in 10 words or fewer), and 10 minutes of prediction relay, earning points for accurate summaries and predictions. Materials and manuals are distributed by the [Vanderbilt IRCA PALS project](https://iris.peabody.vanderbilt.edu/).
- **Math PALS (Grades 2–6)** — 30-minute sessions twice weekly in which players solve computation and concept problems while coaches award points for correct answers and for explaining why an answer is right.
- **IRIS Center modules** — the [IRIS Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies module](https://iris.peabody.vanderbilt.edu/modules/) provides training on implementing PALS in inclusive classrooms.
