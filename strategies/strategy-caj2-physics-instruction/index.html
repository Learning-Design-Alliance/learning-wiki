Use computer-assisted Jigsaw II cooperative learning to teach secondary school physics.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that physics teachers use a computer-assisted Jigsaw II strategy in which students study basic concepts, divide lesson content among home group members, meet in expert groups, and return to teach their portion to home group teammates, all supported by a computer-assisted learning package. The authors state that "Teachers should expose physics students to computer-assisted Jigsaw II cooperative instructional strategy so as to promote social interact ion, active learning, discovery learning, motivation, learning by doing and learning by experience among students." This strategy is grounded in the study's finding that the approach outperformed individualized computer instruction on achievement and retention.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Heterogeneous home groups of three members with assigned responsibilities
  • A computer-assisted learning package installed on standalone computer systems
  • Physics teachers trained in the use of the package and cooperative learning strategies
  • Group processing forms completed after each lesson, with high-scoring teams recognised and rewarded

Constraints¶

  • The study was conducted with senior secondary school physics students in Minna, Niger State, Nigeria, using purposively sampled schools with computer laboratories under the SchoolNet programme

Target Learners¶

  • Senior secondary school (SSII) physics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Physics achievement and retention of concepts

Claims¶

  • Both Jigsaw II and ICI groups improved from pre-test to post-test, indicating both benefited from computer-assisted instruction. [+W]
  • Computer-assisted Jigsaw II cooperative learning produced significantly higher physics post-test achievement than individualized computer instruction. [+W]
  • Achievement level significantly influenced performance within computer-assisted Jigsaw II, with high achievers outperforming medium and low achievers. [+W]
  • Computer-assisted Jigsaw II students retained physics concepts significantly better than ICI students on a delayed test. [+W]
