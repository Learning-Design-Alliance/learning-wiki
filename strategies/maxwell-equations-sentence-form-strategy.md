# Present Maxwell's equations as enunciative sentences instead of mathematical expressions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
To make electromagnetic theory accessible to high-school students, the article recommends presenting Maxwell's equations intuitively: "pode-se apresentar as equações de Maxwell de maneira intuitiva e acessível a estudantes de ensino médio, substituindo-se as expressões matemáticas, envolvendo derivadas e integrais, por suas interpretações físicas em forma de sentenças enunciativas". Gauss's law is stated as constant electric flux proportional to the enclosed charge, and the magnetic analogues are built by asking students what the sources of magnetic fields could be. The article ties the strategy to interactive PhET simulations on radiating charges and radio waves.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Guidance so students derive the magnetic-field analogues themselves, given only that magnetic monopoles have never been observed
#### Constraints
- The strategy replaces derivatives and integrals with sentence-form interpretations rather than presenting the full mathematical formalism

### Target Learners
- High-school students studying electromagnetism

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding the generation and propagation of electromagnetic waves
- Understanding Maxwell's equations conceptually
