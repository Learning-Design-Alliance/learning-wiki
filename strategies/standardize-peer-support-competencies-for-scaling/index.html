Strategies

Standardize peer support around a small universal competency set and invest in training and supervision best practices to enable scale-up¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's authors recommend that the consistently deployed competencies be treated as a basis for standardization and scaling of peer support services, since findings confirm "the salience of a relatively small set of competencies across peer support interventions which could be considered universal for standardization and scaling up, as well as best practices for training and supervision." They further recommend future research identify additional competencies that are contextually sensitive or address the needs of vulnerable groups. This follows their finding that heterogeneity in role definitions, training quality, and supervision limits scaling.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training large numbers of front-line providers, ensuring quality of care, and supporting the providers themselves

Constraints¶

  • Current training programs necessitate in-person training and supervision, which restricts scalability and accessibility and tends to be resource-intensive

Target Learners¶

  • Peer supporters delivering services to adults with serious mental health challenges
  • Mental health systems and program designers scaling peer support services

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Peer supporter competency development and certification
  • Consistent quality of peer support delivery

Claims¶

  • Collaboration and care is the most frequently demonstrated peer support competency across peer support RCTs, followed by providing support and communication [+W]
  • Mental health diagnosis and psychoeducation is the most frequently demonstrated clinical competency in peer support RCTs [+W]
  • Peer supporter supervision was most often weekly and consisted of reviewing fidelity ratings and discussing problems, with supervisors including experienced peers, clinicians, and research staff [+W]
  • Peer supporter training duration ranged from a few hours to 192 hours, was most often in person, and intensive six-week training led all recruited peers to resign in one trial [+W]
  • Peer supporters in peer support RCTs were primarily recruited from community organizations and required lived experience and a similar diagnosis to service recipients [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Implement formal peer tutor training programs under faculty supervision within simulation curricula
  • School counselors set up peer-led small groups where trained students teach STAC strategies to their peers
