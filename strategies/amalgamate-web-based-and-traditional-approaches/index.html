Blend web-based and traditional approaches rather than replacing classroom instruction with web-based instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article concludes that web-based language teaching is not necessarily the best approach for all language components and recommends combining modes. As the authors state, "the best and most expedient way might be an amalgamation of both web based and traditional approaches to the teaching and learning of foreign languages." The recommendation follows from their finding that each mode favored different skills.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Web-based instruction requires learners trained to use chat rooms and email to communicate with the teacher, fellow learners, and native speakers, with monthly instructor meetings to resolve difficulties

Constraints¶

  • The article states web-based approaches might not be as useful for teaching all components and subcomponents of language, based on a single six-month study of elementary learners

Target Learners¶

  • elementary learners of English in language institutes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English reading comprehension
  • writing
  • speaking
  • structural knowledge

Claims¶

  • Traditionally taught elementary EFL learners significantly outperform web-based learners in speaking performance [+W]
  • Traditionally taught elementary EFL learners significantly outperform web-based learners in structural knowledge [+W]
  • Elementary EFL learners taught through web-based instruction significantly outperform traditionally taught learners in reading comprehension [+W]
  • Elementary EFL learners taught through web-based instruction significantly outperform traditionally taught learners in writing [+W]
  • Web-based advantages concentrate in skills practiced through the medium: the web group's interaction occurred mostly through reading and writing [+W]
