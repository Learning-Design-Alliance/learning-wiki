# Three-hour exoplanet transit analysis activity using differential photometry of public telescope images

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A classroom activity in which students download time-sequenced telescope images of the star WASP-2 from the Las Cumbres Observatory Global Telescope Network and use free digital imaging software to plot a transit light curve and calculate exoplanet parameters. The article states it 'can easily be completed within 3 hours in a high school or undergraduate university classroom setting by the students themselves'. Students record time and brightness of the target and a reference star for each image and plot the host divided by the reference star over time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a computer with spreadsheeting software, internet access, and the ability to download and install free digital imaging software
- Educators provide students with a basic overview of AstroImageJ, which comes with a detailed manual
#### Constraints
- The sample comprises only 10 time-sequenced images, so the article recommends each image be inspected individually rather than using multi-aperture automation

### Target Learners
- high school students
- undergraduate university students

### Target Learning Goals
- fundamental concepts in astrophysics
- the transit method
- digital photometry
- plotting and characterising a light curve
