# Provide workshops, training, and counseling sessions to help students overcome communication apprehension, and train instructors to handle stress and CA during educational activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends institutional support for students with high communication apprehension: "There is a need to conduct a series of workshops, training sessions or counseling sessions for the students to overcome CA." It further recommends that instructors be trained to handle stress and CA during class presentations and discussions, and that during emergency outbreaks educational interventions focus on students with low self-esteem and poor communication. This strategy is grounded in the study's finding that 28.2% of students reported high communication apprehension.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment to running a series of workshops, training sessions, or counseling sessions for affected students
- Instructor training capacity for handling stress and communication apprehension during presentations and discussions
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a cross-sectional, single-gender, single-institution survey; the authors note the female-only sample affected generalizability and that the cross-sectional design does not support causal relationships

### Target Learners
- female health sciences college students during and after pandemic-era remote learning

### Target Learning Goals
- reduce communication apprehension in group discussions, meetings, interpersonal conversations, and public speaking
- protect psychological wellbeing and academic performance
