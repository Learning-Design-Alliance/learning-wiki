# Use a varied repertoire of engaging instructional strategies in intensive math intervention: hands-on activities, real-life applications, structured practice, daily homework, feedback, and high expectations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (8 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
JEMP administrators and teachers reported that effective mathematics teaching combined multiple strategies: "engaging in hands-on and creative activities, providing practical applications, spending approximately 30% of the time practicing the material with two hours of daily homework, teaching students how to be thinkers, giving feedback, and emphasizing acceleration as well as remediation/high expectations." Formative and summative assessment and using data to inform instruction were common confirmed practices. The strategy positions student-centered classrooms, discourse, and academic language against traditional teacher-talk, paper-and-pencil approaches the article calls ineffective for underserved urban students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers trained to run student-centered classrooms where students do most of the talking and often collaborate in groups
- Classroom tutors (college mathematics majors) who support individual and small-group help, immediate feedback via grading assistance, and classroom management
#### Constraints
- The article identifies these strategies within an intensive six-week summer program for predominantly low-income, minority urban middle school students

### Target Learners
- urban secondary students who have not been successful in mathematics

### Target Learning Goals
- mathematics achievement and readiness for pre-algebra and algebra
- student engagement and mathematical discourse
