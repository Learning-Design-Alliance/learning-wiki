# Teach math-thinking skills as threads woven through content rather than as added content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors teach general scientific thinking skills with math by weaving them through existing content rather than adding new topics. The skills act like "the warp and weft in a weave," appearing in all content areas with general utility, so a packed curriculum need not grow. Figure 1 illustrates the interleaving of content with general strategy learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must make the strategies explicit across multiple contexts rather than relying on physics majors' years of incidental learning
#### Constraints
- Aimed at introductory courses whose curricula the author acknowledges are already full; non-major students may be focused on just making it through

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students, particularly non-majors and life science majors

### Target Learning Goals
- general scientific thinking skills with math
- using math as a way of thinking across topics
