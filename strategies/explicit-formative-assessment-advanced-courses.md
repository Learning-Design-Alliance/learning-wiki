# Use explicit formative-assessment interventions to help advanced physics students develop learning and self-monitoring habits of mind

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that the kind of explicit interventions shown to help introductory physics students develop learning and self-monitoring skills be extended upward: "Similar explicit interventions involving formative assessment might also prove useful in advanced courses and can help advanced physics students in developing habits of mind". The recommendation rests on their finding that advanced students do not routinely exploit their mistakes as learning opportunities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be explicit rather than relying on students independently learning from mistakes, since the study found most advanced students do not do so automatically
#### Constraints
- The article offers this as a suggestion based on prior introductory-level research and its own small case study, not as a tested intervention in advanced courses

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduate physics students in honors-level courses

### Target Learning Goals
- self-monitoring and reflection skills
- learning from mistakes in problem solving
- building a robust knowledge structure
