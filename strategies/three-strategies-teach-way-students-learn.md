# Three-part instructional strategy: build community, scaffold inquiry-based instruction while reducing cognitive load, and explicitly develop skills, habits, and mindsets

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report describes three key strategies Bronxdale teachers employ to teach the way students learn best: "They build community that allows adults and students to know each other well; they design and implement inquiry-based instruction with strong scaffolding and with attention to reducing cognitive load while teaching cognitive strategies; and they incorporate explicit development of social, emotional, and academic skills, habits, and mindsets." The strategy links relational trust, scaffolded inquiry pedagogy, and explicit executive-function and growth-mindset development, as students plan projects, receive feedback, revise, and see competence grow with effort.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must know students well, design scaffolded inquiry tasks, and provide feedback-and-revision cycles so students experience competence growing with effort.
#### Constraints
- The report notes strategies providing student voice, such as instructional targets and self-advocacy opportunities, were unevenly practiced across the school, motivating team-based work toward common practices.

### Target Learners
- High school students in inclusive classrooms, including students identified for special education

### Target Learning Goals
- Inquiry and proactive learning, clear communication, executive functioning, growth mindset, intrinsic desire for lifelong learning
