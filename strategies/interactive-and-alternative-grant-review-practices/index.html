Interactive and alternative review practices: applicant response rounds, sandpits, bibliometric scoring, decision aids, lotteries, and crowdfunding¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report catalogs practices that modify or supplement traditional peer review. Some funders allow more interaction between applicants and reviewers: "NWO allows applicants to respond in writing to comments after the first round of review and requires an oral presentation as a part of the funding process," and UKRI's Engineering and Physical Research Council sometimes brings proposers, research users, and other experts together in interactive "sandpits" where proposals can be developed with real-time feedback. The Hungarian Scientific Research Fund "uses an automated bibliometric scoring system that adjusts scores to account for disciplinary differences and career level." Researchers have proposed decision aids to help integrate sub scores into an overall proposal score; alternative methods like partial lotteries and crowdfunding are in early conceptual development or being piloted.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interactive practices require funder capacity to manage multiple review rounds and applicant-reviewer interaction

Constraints¶

  • Evidence about the efficacy of these ideas is limited; the decision aid's impact on proposal decisions has not been evaluated; partial lotteries and crowdfunding are in early phases of conceptual development or piloting

Target Learners¶

  • Research funders, program officers, and grant applicants

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improving the reliability, validity, or efficacy of grant proposal review processes
