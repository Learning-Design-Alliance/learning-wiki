# Teach meta-literacy as a core learning objective in courses using AI in education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper argues that co-authoring an ongoing understanding of oneself is a skill not currently acquired as part of students' education, and draws on Mackey and Jacobson's (2011) metaliteracy: the ability to evaluate, correct, and shape information in systems that adapt to how they are used. It recommends that "For courses that use AI-in-education, meta-literacy should be a core learning objective." Students without this training interact with AI as a search engine and get less from the architecture.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction that teaches students to distinguish a learning observation from a psychological claim, propose patterns the system missed, reject wrongly inferred ones, and read and revise the record.
#### Constraints
- The paper warns of a digital-divide problem: students who acquire co-authorship by accident enter AI-mediated learning with an advantage that grows over time.

### Target Learners
- Students in courses using AI-in-education systems

### Target Learning Goals
- Competent, critical participation in AI-mediated learning; distinguishing learning observations from psychological claims
