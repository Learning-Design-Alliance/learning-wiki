# Focus additional instruction on items that proved resistant to change or where students still scored low

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that future educators spend additional instructional time on knowledge, self-efficacy and behavior items that were resistant to improvement or where students scored low at post-test. Teachers are encouraged to focus on which food groups should be eaten most often, grain servings, food group nutrient sources, the meaning of 'lite' on packaging, soda/pop consumption, and dairy consumption, using strategies such as school-wide messaging, cross-curricular efforts, take-home assignments with parents, and additional curricular supports.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation of the constructivist curriculum as described, so that low-scoring or resistant items can be identified from post-test results.
#### Constraints
- The article notes dairy consumption remained unchanged and students reported almost no additional efficacy for drinking less soda/pop after the unit.

### Target Learners
- middle school students in health education

### Target Learning Goals
- nutrition knowledge
- dietary self-efficacy
- healthy eating behaviors
