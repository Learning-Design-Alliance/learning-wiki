# Use questionnaire-driven iteration between flipped sessions to adjust difficulty and teacher scaffolding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete implementation recipe drawn from the article's test phase: administer a post-class questionnaire after the first flipped session, analyse pain points within days, and redesign the next flipped session accordingly — for example adding explanations of key points, reducing how thoroughly the design "did not depend on students as thoroughly as it had the last time", and arranging pre-class tasks a week ahead. This lowered difficulty, improved participation, and produced more positive student feedback in the second session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Post-session questionnaires must be collected and analysed promptly
- The teaching team must retain design authority to iterate the next session's materials and tasks
