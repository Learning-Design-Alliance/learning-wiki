Use interactive whiteboard technology to support interactive, multimodal, student-centered whole-class teaching¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on their findings, the authors recommend using IWB-integrated multimodal presentation to promote a learner-centered pedagogy in which both teacher and students are learners, for example generating common topics and finding answers jointly from the internet. They report that "the more the students interacted with the MPS, the more they became adept in using its functions and features", and that teacher-student interactivity promotes attention to instruction and participation in classroom discussions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers with adequate subject matter content knowledge, instructional material production capability, instructional competency, and classroom management skills, since the tool cannot compensate for deficits in these

Constraints¶

  • The MPS "is not a magic tool for improving ESL student academic success"; it was "simply a tool that improved the teacher's innate and teaching capability in the ESL classroom"

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students learning English as a second language

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English vocabulary learning
  • student engagement and participation in whole-class lessons

Claims¶

  • Experimental-group students reported significantly higher overall learning satisfaction with the MPS-based English course than control-group students [+W]
  • Elementary ESL students taught vocabulary with the MPS on an interactive whiteboard achieved significantly higher post-test scores than students taught with a blackboard [+W]
  • Experimental-group students perceived the MPS as increasing interactivity, supporting multimodal-integrated learning, and making instruction more student-centered [+W]
  • Interactive whiteboard lessons supported by visual materials produce higher listening comprehension achievement than regular 2018 Turkish Course Curriculum teaching in middle school students [+W]
  • Learning from interactive whiteboard lessons supported by visual materials persisted, with only a small significant decline four weeks after the post-test [+W]
  • Interactive whiteboard groups showed more adaptive decision-making behavior earlier than traditional whiteboard groups in simulated medical emergencies [+W]
  • Experimental-group students' listening comprehension scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test after interactive whiteboard instruction with visual materials [+W]
