# Assign participants strategically so new facilitators build confidence on straightforward cases before taking more complex ones

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a triaging process in which new facilitators of complex behaviour-change interventions are first assigned seemingly more straightforward participants, with more complex cases allocated once they have built experience and confidence. The authors state "there might be value in assigning participants strategically", noting this mirrors the NHS Talking Therapies approach of giving new practitioners less complex presentations and smaller caseloads to allow time for reflective practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated, funded time for facilitator learning and reflective practice, since the authors state it is hard to envisage this work being absorbed within existing workloads
#### Constraints
- The authors note such a triaging process is unlikely to be failsafe

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals newly trained as facilitators of complex behaviour-change interventions

### Target Learning Goals
- Developing competence and confidence in facilitating behaviour-change conversations
