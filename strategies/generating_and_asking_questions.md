# Generating and Asking Questions

## Description
This strategy involves readers asking themselves questions throughout the reading process. The ability to ask relevant questions helps readers integrate information, identify main ideas, and summarize effectively. Asking the right questions allows readers to focus on the most important information and address any comprehension problems they encounter. By posing questions, readers actively engage with the text and monitor their understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need to be prompted to ask questions before, during, and after reading. Instructors should provide models of effective questioning and facilitate discussions based on learner-generated questions.
#### Constraints
- Learners may struggle to generate relevant or insightful questions if they lack background knowledge or critical thinking skills. Poorly formulated questions may lead to superficial comprehension.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learners, especially those in upper elementary, middle, and high school, as well as adult learners looking to improve reading comprehension and critical thinking

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve reading comprehension by integrating information, identifying main ideas, summarizing content, and focusing on key details through self-questioning

### Affordances
- Enhances active reading by encouraging learners to pose questions and seek answers, promotes critical thinking by focusing on important information and potential comprehension issues, and improves self-monitoring skills through metacognitive awareness.

### Personalization
- Learners can generate questions based on their individual interests and knowledge levels. Instructors can provide question stems or examples to guide learners in generating effective questions.
