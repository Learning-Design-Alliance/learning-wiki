# Incorporate camera metrics into existing RAMIS proficiency-based curricula and automated feedback systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend using validated endoscope-control metrics to improve surgeon training: "camera metrics might be helpful for discriminating surgeon skill or setting proficiency standards if incorporated into existing RAMIS simulation training exercises" such as the dVSS, dV-Trainer, RobotiX Mentor, and RoSS. They further propose that automated and objective measures of endoscope control could be used in intelligent tutoring systems to deliver formative feedback during surgeon training without requiring an expert instructor to be present, and that metrics could be replicated on any RAMIS task via on-screen camera-control icons or image processing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to endoscope movement data, either directly from a RAMIS platform or extracted from camera-control icons or image processing algorithms
#### Constraints
- Additional validation studies are needed before using camera metrics to evaluate clinical procedural performance; simulation exercises contain different visual information than live tissue

### Target Learners
- RAMIS surgeon trainees in simulation-based and clinical training

### Target Learning Goals
- Optimal endoscope control, visualization of the operative field, and surgical proficiency
