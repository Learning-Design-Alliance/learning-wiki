# Combine anonymous clicker questions with small-group discussion of conceptual questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size

## Description
The article recommends that instructors prepare CPS activities that promote active learning, critical thinking, and group discussions, in addition to straightforward questions that keep students alert and give an instant perception of understanding. Anonymity and group learning are identified as the two main factors making the technology effective in increasing participation, since anonymity decreases stress and embarrassment. Critical-thinking questions ask students to form small groups, discuss using notes and the textbook, and submit answers individually before receiving immediate feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors should be computer and internet literate and well trained to use the CPS, including embedding PowerPoint presentations, attaching pictures and videos, converting Word documents to .pdf, entering data in Excel, and plugging USB cables
#### Constraints
- The quality of student participation may not be ensured because some students may just randomly hit any button on the clicker

### Target Learners
- college students, including multicultural classes where students' first spoken language is not English

### Target Learning Goals
- participation quality, critical thinking, attendance, and achievement
