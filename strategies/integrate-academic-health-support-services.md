# Integrate academic and health support services rather than delivering them independently, targeting psychological distress in particular

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that because lower self-perceived academic performance co-occurs with psychological distress, fatigue, pain, and positive TMD screening results, academic and health support services should be integrated rather than delivered independently. The article states "academic and health support services should be integrated rather than delivered independently with psychological distress representing a particularly relevant target for assessment and support".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Support services must have mechanisms for identifying students with psychological distress and academic difficulties
#### Constraints
- Cross-sectional design cannot establish whether psychological distress precedes or follows perceived academic underperformance
