# Reform medical statistics teaching through blended, case-based, tiered instruction with diversified competency-based assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a reform strategy for medical statistics education built on its survey findings. It recommends that teachers incorporate "practical clinical and medical research cases PBL and CBL teaching methods", adopt blended online-offline delivery, build a medical statistics case library, and divide "statistics teaching into basic and advanced classes for separate instruction". Assessment should become a diversified, multi-dimensional evaluation system using case and scenario analysis and hands-on software operation rather than theory-only exams.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires specialized statistics instructors and clinical-research-experienced teachers teaching in synchronization; the article notes only a small portion of statistics professionals enter medical colleges.
#### Constraints
- The article recommends a phased approach: pilot testing in select medical schools, faculty certification programs, and policy dialogs with accreditation bodies; reforms balance immediate feasibility with long-term systemic change.

### Target Learners
- Chinese medical students (undergraduate, master's, and doctoral) in medical statistics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Selecting and correctly applying statistical methods in medical research and clinical practice
- Interpreting statistical analysis results professionally
- Hands-on statistical software operation
