# Incorporate business administration and management content into medical curricula to address the business-competence deficit

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adding business administration and management content to both PBL-based and conventional medical curricula, because graduates rated 'Business competence' as demanded by the clinical job but insufficiently taught. The authors note that joint MD/MBA programs at US universities increased from six programs in 1993 to 33 programs in 2002.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curricula must identify business competence as a job requirement not currently covered by teaching, as measured by graduate ratings of required versus taught competencies
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on self-perceived ratings from a survey with a low overall response rate (14%) and differing response rates between groups

### Target Learners
- medical students and medical graduates in Germany

### Target Learning Goals
- business competence for clinical practice
