# Guide and time generative learning activities to overcome barriers to sense-making

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because many learners do not spontaneously explain, visualize, or enact learning materials, the review recommends explicit prompting and guidance for GLAs, and instructors can use external GLA products as formative assessments to adapt instruction. When learners' products 'reveal knowledge gaps or misconceptions, instructors can provide targeted feedback to support subsequent internal sense-making processes'. The review also proposes overcoming barriers 'by appropriately guiding and timing GLAs', with timing including implementing any GLA as a self-testing opportunity after instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need explicit prompting and guidance, since many will not spontaneously engage in sense-making
#### Constraints
- GLAs do not guarantee sense-making; learners may struggle to generate high-quality representations without sufficient background knowledge or guidance

### Target Learners
- Students learning conceptual science and mathematics material

### Target Learning Goals
- Higher-quality explanations, visualizations, and enactments supporting comprehension and transfer

### Affordances
- Generative Sense Making Framework
