# Share Teacher's Written Work

## Description
Instructors share their written work with learners, soliciting feedback and criticism to model the value of continuous improvement. This approach fosters a culture of openness and demonstrates that everyone, including the teacher, benefits from constructive feedback. The shared work can include current drafts, polished pieces, or even writing from the instructor's earlier education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor willingness to share their work, structured opportunities for feedback, guidelines for providing constructive criticism. Requires a classroom culture that values openness and collaboration.
#### Constraints
- Instructor vulnerability can be challenging. Learners may be hesitant to provide critical feedback to the instructor. Potential for superficial or insincere feedback if not managed carefully.

### Target Learners

- All learner levels, particularly useful in secondary and higher education to foster a collaborative learning environment

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Model continuous improvement, encourage feedback seeking, promote a growth mindset, build confidence in learners

### Affordances
- Builds trust and rapport between instructor and learners, demonstrates the value of feedback, enhances learners' critical analysis skills, promotes a growth mindset by showing that even experts seek improvement

### Personalization
- Instructors can share different types of writing based on the course content and learner interests. The level of detail and complexity of the feedback sought can be tailored to the learners' skill levels. Learners can provide feedback through various channels, such as written comments, verbal discussions, or online forums.

## Assessment Evidence
- Increased learner participation in feedback activities, improved quality of learner feedback, changes in learners' attitudes towards receiving feedback on their own work.

## Impact
- Sharing instructor work and soliciting feedback helps learners feel more comfortable receiving criticism, viewing it as an opportunity for growth rather than an affront. This approach enhances learners' writing by encouraging them to take the time and care to consider and respond to reader insight.
