# Two-stage prebriefing strategy: advance written communication plus a short in-person prebrief on the day of the simulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because the simulation mimicked the spontaneity of a real fall and aimed to minimize time away from duties, prebriefing could not occur immediately beforehand. The strategy instead sends general written communication through routine organizational pathways outlining objectives, scenario, roles, and etiquette, re-sent roughly every 2 months, followed by a short in-person prebrief at the start of the shift on the simulation day. The article states a prebriefing "provides instructions to participants in order to establish a psychologically safe environment."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Organizational communication pathways to distribute advance written prebrief materials
- Supervisor and manager messaging supporting expected participation during the planning period
#### Constraints
- The specific time of the simulation is not revealed in advance, so participants cannot prepare for the exact moment

### Target Learners
- All nursing team members on units where in situ simulations occur

### Target Learning Goals
- Preparedness for responding to a resident fall
- Psychological safety in simulation-based training
