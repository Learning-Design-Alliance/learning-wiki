# Run in-class group assignments with rotating member roles, ID-integrated problems, and JEDI ground rules reinforced by role holders

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's group-work design assigns students to 3-4 member groups via a Google sign-up sheet within the 18 lab-section groups, meets in preassigned Zoom breakout rooms, and rotates a four-role list (coordinating roles, attendance/time management with JEDI-rule checking, explaining assignment rules, and writing/submission) for each new assignment so all members have equal opportunities. Problems integrate the last digits of all group members' school IDs to maintain academic honesty and are built to mitigate bias by name, gender, age, body shape, ethnicity, origin and diversity. Eight JEDI ground rules, from "One person should speak at a time" to non-biasing across demographics, are reinforced by the responsible role holder.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Groups of 3-4 students formed by choice within the 18 lab-section groups
- Rotating role assignment for each new assignment, with combined roles distributed when members are absent
- All members agree on the final product and include all names and roles on it
- A grading rubric distributing points for structure, content, organization, illustrations and mechanism, with penalties for late work, unfollowed requirements and unauthorized resources
#### Constraints
- All group members receive the same grade with detailed feedback regardless of individual contributions
- Around 5% of students moved to individual work mid-semester due to group discrepancies such as lack of contribution and carelessness

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- communication and collaboration skills
- academic honesty and respect for class requirements
- justice, equity, diversity and inclusion within groups
- time management and work-submission habits
