# Create a narrated expert video library of specific surgical techniques coupled with existing workshops and lectures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors propose filming, editing and narrating real interventions with patient consent — such as appendectomies, cholecystectomies and bowel resections — to build "A video library ... for residents". Videos are short (the expert video took about 45 minutes to produce, ran about 5 minutes), available at the resident's convenience, and could "be coupled with current technical workshops and lectures". This follows the trial's positive resident reception despite a non-significant skill effect.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Videos must be made by a reliable source with expert voice-over narration explaining key steps, and filmed with patient consent for real interventions.
#### Constraints
- The trial found no statistically significant skill benefit from self-directed video learning, so the library is justified by resident appreciation, not by measured skill gains.

### Target Learners
- general surgery residents (PGY-1 to PGY-5)

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of specific surgical technical procedures
