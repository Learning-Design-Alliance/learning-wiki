# Teach stress-management and self-care skills to medical students facing examination periods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article endorses introducing structured stress-management and self-care instruction for medical students during examination periods, noting that "teaching stress management and self-care skills for medical students may prove to be beneficial" and that "there is a need to look at the applicability of such measures" in their own medical school setting. It is motivated by the finding that all four WHOQOL-BREF domains were stress sources before exams, attributed to time constraints for self, family, friends and entertainment under a demanding curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Applicability and feasibility of the measures must be assessed in the local medical school setting, as the article states.
#### Constraints
- Stress in examinations cannot be eliminated; individual students should be identified and helped.
- The recommendation is drawn from a United States study and had not been tested in the Sri Lankan setting reported here.

### Target Learners
- fourth-year undergraduate medical students preparing for a mandatory examination

### Target Learning Goals
- manage examination stress
- maintain quality of life and wellbeing during exam preparation
- support examination performance
