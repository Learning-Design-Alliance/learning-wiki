# Use a 1-2 hour activity in which small groups define 'threshold' from shared examples for the purpose of helping younger kids

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a short (1-2 hour) activity where students are asked to define threshold as an ideal starting point for many physics courses. A class is shown examples such as "bending a pencil until it breaks" and a popping balloon, students brainstorm additional examples, and "Small groups of students then work together to define threshold for the purpose of helping younger kids decide what counts." A full lesson plan is linked.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A set of physical threshold examples shown to the class, plus time for brainstorming and small-group definition work
#### Constraints
- The article frames the activity as workshop-like and 1-2 hours in length; it does not claim it replaces extended definitional work integrated throughout a year-long course

### Target Learners
- high school and college physics students across a range of backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- crafting, refining and debating definitions; connecting physics concepts across topics and to everyday life
