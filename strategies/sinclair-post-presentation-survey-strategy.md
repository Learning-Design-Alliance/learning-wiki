# Use post-presentation survey questions adapted from Sinclair to elicit and code learners' metacognitive justifications

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students answered eight questions adapted from Sinclair (1999) immediately following every oral presentation, covering why they chose the topic, how it went, what problems they had, and what they would have done differently. Responses were collected after the first presentation (third week) and the final presentation (last week), piloted before administration, and coded into the three metacognitive levels. The final question, "What else would you have done differently?", permitted responses offering alternatives to be categorized as level 3.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions piloted prior to actual administration
- Data collection in the learners' L1 so autonomy rather than interlanguage is analyzed
- Two raters categorizing responses with disagreements resolved through discussion
#### Constraints
- Applied in a graduate CLIL/CBI Spanish course; the authors suggest future work operationalize autonomy in other aspects, including the sociocultural aspect

### Target Learners
- postgraduate foreign language students giving oral presentations

### Target Learning Goals
- metacognitive knowledge
- ability to justify decisions about learning
