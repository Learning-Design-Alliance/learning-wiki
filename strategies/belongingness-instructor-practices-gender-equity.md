# Instructor practices that foster belongingness and a growth-mindset classroom culture to support gender equity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Drawing on Kreutzer and Boudreaux's gender-equity framework and Steele's wise schooling, the article lists concrete instructor practices: instructors "learned all of their students' names", encouraged students to present alternative ideas and justifications, explained the instructional value of student questions, intuition, mistakes, and incorrect answers, relayed stories of former students who mastered the material after hard work, explained that the interactive-engagement pedagogy was chosen because they wanted students to do well, and scheduled office hours around student availability. The article frames these as fostering a growth-mindset, belongingness-oriented classroom culture.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors willing to explicitly discuss pedagogical rationale, growth mindset, and the value of student ideas and mistakes throughout the semester
#### Constraints
- The article presents these practices as part of an existence proof in a specific context, not as methods guaranteed to produce the same results

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics majors, including women whose belongingness the practices aim to affirm

### Target Learning Goals
- domain belongingness in physics
- growth mindset about learning physics
- gender equity in physics learning outcomes
