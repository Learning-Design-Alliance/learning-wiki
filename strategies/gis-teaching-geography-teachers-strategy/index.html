Adopt GIS-based teaching as an innovative method Geography teachers can use to stimulate mastery goal adoption, noting it does not require high-level technical skills¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's conclusion recommends GIS-based teaching as an innovative method Geography teachers can utilise to stimulate students to adopt mastery goals and to enhance underachieving students' achievement. It stresses feasibility: "the GIS integrated method used in this study did not require a high level of technical skills, for the teachers were able to learn and use t he GIS software within two weeks of training." The recommendation rests on the study's quasi-experimental gains in mastery goal and achievement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need about two weeks of training to learn and use the GIS software
  • Computer laboratory access and GIS lesson materials for the topic taught

Constraints¶

  • Findings come from a small sample in two Smart Schools in Sabah; the authors state a larger study is needed to generalise the effect

Target Learners¶

  • Underachieving upper secondary Geography students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Mastery goal orientation
  • Achievement in Geography topic content

Claims¶

  • GIS-based teaching produced significantly higher post-test achievement scores than traditional teaching among underachieving Geography students, with a large-sized between-group effect [+W]
  • GIS-based teaching produced significantly higher post-test mastery goal scores than traditional teaching among underachieving Geography students, with a medium-sized between-group effect at post-test [+W]
  • Student interviews were consistent with the quantitative findings: experiment group students reported excitement and goal achievement, while two control group students reported being unable to achieve their learning goal [+W]
  • Both groups improved from pre-test to post-test, but the experiment group's within-group gains in mastery goal and achievement were larger than the control group's [+W]
