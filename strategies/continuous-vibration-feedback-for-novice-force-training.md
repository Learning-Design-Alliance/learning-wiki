# Use continuous, linearly increasing vibration feedback when training surgical novices in laparoscopic force control

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (3 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends vibration feedback integrated into laparoscopic instrument handles as support for learning adequate tissue handling, with the signal increasing linearly with applied force rather than firing only at thresholds. The trial showed this modality produced the lowest forces, and "Surgical novices benefitted in all tasks from this system, whereas experienced surgeons benefited only in more advanced laparoscopic tasks."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruments equipped with vibration effectors driven by force-sensing hardware and thresholding software; novices should first reach a predefined proficiency level in a standardized basic laparoscopic skills course
#### Constraints
- Benefit for experienced surgeons was limited to mean non-zero force in complex tasks; clinical translation requires further research on integrating force sensors into instrument tips

### Target Learners
- surgical novices and students in laparoscopic training

### Target Learning Goals
- appropriate force application and tissue handling during laparoscopic tasks
