# Training institutes articulate normative expectations about direct observations and translate them into ongoing supervisor training and coaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because normative beliefs emerged as a significant determinant of supervisors' intention to perform direct observations, the authors recommend that training institutes make their expectations explicit and embed them in continuing development. They write: "By translating their normative expectations into ongoing training and coaching of supervisors, training institutes may effectively contribute to the enactment of supervisor intentions into actual performance of DOs." Such training should cover both conducting DOs and fostering shared responsibility between learner and supervisor for planning them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training institutes must articulate expectations regarding supervisors' engagement in DOs "more clearly, explicitly and consistently".
- Training should include guidance on fostering the shared responsibility of learner and supervisor in planning observations.
#### Constraints
- The article notes transferability may be restricted: in hospital-based settings with short-term supervisor–trainee contacts, normative beliefs may have a less significant effect on intention.

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate GP supervisors

### Target Learning Goals
- Increasing the frequency of direct observations of trainee clinical performance and fostering a shared mental model on their use

- Extended Tpb Model Supervisor Direct Observation Intention
