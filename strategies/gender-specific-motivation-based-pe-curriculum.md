# Develop gender-specific, motivation-based curricula that support autonomy and increase intrinsic motivation in PE teacher education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q3` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that health educators build university curricula for future PE teachers around motivational processes, explicitly tailored to gender differences in motivation. It states that "health educators should develop a gender-specific, motivation-based curriculum in universities aimed at increasing enjoyment, interest, and passion for PA among future PE teachers", and that increasing intrinsic motivation through adequate feedback plus emotional and autonomy support may powerfully influence curriculum outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Motivational processes should be taken into account as a crucial factor in PE curriculum development, per Kusurkar et al. as cited by the authors
#### Constraints
- Recommendations rest on a cross-sectional study of PE undergraduates at one Polish and one Ukrainian university and cannot be generalized to the whole student population

### Target Learners
- University students in physical education departments
- Future PE teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement (GPA)
- Intrinsic motivation for physical activity and study
