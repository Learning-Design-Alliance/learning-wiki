# Develop AI algorithms to automatically annotate surgical gestures and provide targeted feedback, with substantial testing before clinical implementation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation is that machine learning will likely enable automatic annotation of surgical videos, so "it could be possible to annotate surgical videos with surgical gestures automatically". AI algorithms could detect patterns human evaluators miss, provide performance levels and targeted feedback, and, for small tasks like suturing, give simple feedback such as needle handling or video comparisons with an expert.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Substantial testing of the AI algorithms before they can be implemented for clinical assessment
- A much better understanding of the patterns of surgical gestures relevant to each procedure for feedback to be targeted and correct
#### Constraints
- For full-length or real-life surgeries, a small deviation in the surgical pattern in one part of the procedure might not influence the surgery or patient outcome

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees
- practicing robotic surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- targeted feedback on surgical performance
- assessment of surgical skill level
