# Give learners enough time to work through case examples and guard against cognitive overload

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the evaluation survey results the authors draw the practical recommendation "that learners should be given enough time to work through case examples, and that caution should be taken to prevent cognitive overload". In the trial, KF arm students facing four cases in 13 minutes each reported more time pressure and higher workload, and the authors suggest the additional cognitive task of extracting key features under time pressure was too demanding for some students. They also suggest exploring virtual patients that are key feature-based by design rather than post-hoc reductions of detailed cases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness of learners' prior familiarity with content and method, since both were new to these students
#### Constraints
- Whether the failure is due to this specific implementation of the KF principle or to KF-based instruction itself is not clear, per the authors

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students encountering new content and new computer-based methods

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning skill acquisition through case-based learning
