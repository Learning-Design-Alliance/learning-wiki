# Combine required written justification with explicit metacognitive scaffolding prompts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the justification task alone produced superficial engagement and did not direct students' metacognitive processes, the article recommends pairing it with explicit scaffolding. "Future implementations should therefore combine written justification with more explicit metacognitive scaffolding, such as prompts asking students what aspects of a suggestion they should evaluate, what criteria they should use, and what evidence they should seek before accepting it." Designers should also increase the perceived value of the justification task and balance cognitive engagement against sustainable effort.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors or tool designers must embed the justification step directly into students' interactions with AI suggestions
#### Constraints
- Implementation should account for the perceived cognitive and time demand of the task and avoid overwhelming students with excessive justification requirements

### Target Learners
- college students using AI writing tools

### Target Learning Goals
- metacognitive monitoring during AI-assisted revision
- critical evaluation of AI suggestions
