# Use structured asynchronous online peer discussion of authentic critical appraisal papers to support learning in the clinical workplace

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing an online discussion as a framework to support students' learning in existing courses: students write an individual CAT paper on a self-selected clinical problem encountered in the workplace, then discuss it with peers in a structured asynchronous forum addressing three prescribed critical appraisal topics, after which they may revise the paper. The authors report that "a CSCL environment can support medical students in the execution and critical appraisal of authentic tasks in the clinical workplace."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A CSCL environment enabling structured, asynchronous discussion independent of place and time
- Discussion structured by the three critical appraisal topics
- An instruction manual and schedule provided to students
#### Constraints
- Such collaborative activities do not automatically result in positive learning outcomes; success depends on, among other factors, the intensity of the online activity within groups
- A controlled study comparing intense and limited discussion remains future research

### Target Learners
- medical students in clerkships dispersed over multiple training locations

### Target Learning Goals
- critical appraisal skills
- clinical problem solving
- knowledge construction through peer discussion
