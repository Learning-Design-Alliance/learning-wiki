# Target executive function skills through early intervention at school transition to support academic and social development in autistic kindergartners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 5 studies (4 associational, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on findings that EF predicted concurrent and later math achievement and that parent-reported EF difficulties predicted peer play challenges, the authors recommend targeted EF interventions for young autistic children at school transition. They state "targeted EF interventions could be a powerful way to optimize academic and social development in autistic kindergarteners" and recommend early comprehensive EF assessment to identify children at risk before gaps accumulate. They also note existing school-based EF programs show promise but generalization and long-term effects are less explored.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Early, comprehensive multimodal EF assessment to identify children at risk for academic and social difficulties
#### Constraints
- The article notes evidence on generalization of EF intervention improvements to real-life settings and long-term effects has been less explored

### Target Learners
- Autistic kindergartners at the transition from preschool to formal schooling

### Target Learning Goals
- Math and reading achievement; peer play interaction and social skills
