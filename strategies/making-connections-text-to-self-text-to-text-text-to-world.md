# Making Connections (Text-to-Self, Text-to-Text, Text-to-World)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 14 studies (6 review, 3 quant-synthesis, 2 causal, 2 theoretical, 1 associational), `q1`–`q4` · 3 of 14 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Making Connections is a comprehension strategy in which readers deliberately relate a text to three referents: their own experiences (text-to-self), other texts they have read (text-to-text), and events or knowledge in the wider world (text-to-world). It is usually introduced through instructor modeling — a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) in which the teacher verbalizes a connection — followed by guided practice with prompts ("This reminds me of…"), and independent application through journals, annotation, or discussion. The strategy is a staple of comprehension-strategies instruction in the tradition of [Reciprocal Teaching](../elements/reciprocal-teaching.md) and transactional reading theory (Rosenblatt, 1978).

## Design Implications

Connections work because they activate and integrate prior knowledge with new text content, supporting inference and elaboration [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. However, the *quality* of the connection matters more than its presence: connections must be elaborated and text-relevant to aid comprehension, not merely stated [Relevancy of emphasis directs attention.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A text with genuine points of contact for learners' experience and prior knowledge
- Instructor modeling of each connection type before independent use ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md))
- Guiding questions or sentence stems for each connection type (e.g., "This reminds me of… because…")
- A recording structure: double-entry journal, connection web, or [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) routine
- A forum for sharing connections ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or peer pairs)
- A text worth connecting to, with enough thematic or experiential surface for all three connection types
- Instructor modeling of what a *meaningful* connection looks like, contrasted with a trivial one

#### Constraints
- Superficial or tangential connections ("I have a dog too") consume instructional time without deepening comprehension; learners need explicit criteria for *relevant* connections [-M]
- Text-to-self connections can pull attention away from the text itself, especially for narrative texts, shifting readers toward personal association rather than text-based inference [~M]
- Unmodeled, the strategy tends to produce formulaic responses; benefits depend on quality of demonstration and feedback [-W]
- For learners with little relevant background knowledge, forced connections add load without benefit [Prior knowledge not related to performance.](../claims/prior-knowledge-not-related-to-performance.md) [~W]
- Superficial connections ("I have a dog too") do not improve comprehension and can consume reading time without benefit [~M] — without modeling, learners default to low-effort personal associations
- Text-to-self connections can pull attention away from the text itself; readers who over-personalize may substitute their own experience for the author's meaning, distorting comprehension [~W]
- For learners with little relevant experience (e.g., reading about unfamiliar cultures or historical periods), text-to-self prompts may frustrate rather than activate [~M]
- The strategy targets engagement and elaboration, not decoding or vocabulary; it will not remediate weak word recognition [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [-W] — learners still struggling at the word level need decoding support first

#### Implementation Variability
- Oral think-aloud modeling → shared whole-class charting → small-group discussion → independent journaling (gradual release)
- Written forms: connection logs, sticky-note annotation, double-entry journals
- Digital forms: social annotation platforms (e.g., [Perusall](https://www.perusall.com)) where learners post and reply to each other's connections
- Emphasis can shift by discipline: text-to-text dominates in literature study; text-to-world dominates in social studies and science
- **Timing:** pre-reading activation, during-reading annotation, or post-reading reflection and discussion
- **Format:** oral turn-and-talk, written journals, graphic organizers, or digital annotation tools
- **Weighting:** literature classes often emphasize text-to-text (intertextual themes); content-area classes emphasize text-to-world (application to current issues)
- **Choice-based:** learners select which connection type to pursue, supporting autonomy [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Elementary through secondary readers (grades 3–12), especially developing readers who read decodably but do not yet integrate text with background knowledge [+M]
- Multilingual learners, when connections are invited from their full linguistic and cultural repertoire [+W]
- Less useful for advanced readers who already integrate fluently; strategy instruction shows diminishing returns with expertise [~M]
- Upper elementary through secondary readers who have fluent decoding but need support constructing meaning
- Reluctant readers, for whom personal relevance increases engagement and persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension: integration of text content with prior knowledge and inference-making [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]
- Engagement and motivation: personal relevance increases task value [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Intertextual reasoning: comparing themes, structures, and claims across texts
- Critical literacy: situating texts in social and historical context (text-to-world)
- Reading comprehension: integrating text with prior knowledge to build a situation model
- Critical literacy: text-to-world connections link texts to social and civic issues
- Intertextual reasoning: text-to-text connections build genre awareness and thematic comparison
- Reflective thinking: text-to-self connections support metacognitive and affective engagement

### Instructions
1. **Select and preview the text.** Identify likely connection points and prepare prompts for each type ([Activation](../elements/activation.md) of prior knowledge before reading).
2. **Model with a think-aloud.** Read a short passage aloud and verbalize one connection of each type, explaining *why* it helps understanding — not just that it exists ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)).
3. **Co-construct criteria.** With learners, distinguish deep connections (elaborated, text-relevant) from surface ones; chart examples of each.
4. **Guided practice.** Learners mark connections while reading using sticky notes or an annotation code ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md)), prompted by stems such as "This reminds me of…" and "This is like the article we read about…".
5. **Share and elaborate.** Peers exchange connections and push each other to explain how the connection clarifies the text ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), [Peer Discussion](../elements/peer-discussion.md)).
6. **Apply independently.** Learners write a short response built on their strongest connection, then evaluate which connection type deepened their understanding most ([Application](../elements/application.md)).

## Examples
- **Mosaic of Thought comprehension curriculum (Keene & Zimmermann, 1997)** — the source framework that popularized the three connection types in workshop-model reading instruction.
- **[Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org)** — published lesson sequences and think-aloud scripts for modeling text-to-self, text-to-text, and text-to-world connections in grades K–8.
- **[Perusall](https://www.perusall.com)** — social annotation platform where university students post connections as margin comments and respond to peers', making text-to-text and text-to-world links visible and discussable.
- **IB Middle Years Programme language and literature units** — formally assess students' ability to connect texts to personal, cultural, and global contexts.
- **Reading workshop (grades 6–12):** Students keep a three-column connection log while reading a novel; columns feed small-group discussion and a reflective essay.
- **[Newsela](https://newsela.com)** — Leveled news articles paired with prompts that invite text-to-world connections to current events.
- **[Facing History and Ourselves](https://www.facinghistory.org)** — Humanities curriculum that systematically connects texts to historical and contemporary world issues (text-to-world as the organizing move).

## Description
Text-to-Text, Text-to-Self, Text-to-World (often abbreviated T-T, T-S, T-W) is a comprehension strategy popularized by Keene and Zimmermann's work on proficient reader thinking. Learners are prompted to connect a text to other texts they have read (comparing themes, structures, or characters), to their own lived experience (relating events and emotions to personal memory), and to broader world issues and current events. Connections can be prompted before, during, or after reading — to activate relevant knowledge, sustain engagement, or consolidate understanding.

## Design Implications

The strategy works by activating prior knowledge and prompting elaboration: articulating a connection requires learners to relate new information to existing schemas, which is a form of self-explanation [Eliciting self-explanations improves understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. However, the quality of the connection matters more than its presence — connections must be genuinely elaborative rather than superficial associations.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A text worth connecting to, with enough thematic or experiential surface for all three connection types
- Guiding questions or sentence stems for each connection type (e.g., "This reminds me of… because…")
- A recording structure — journal, two-column chart, or sticky notes — so connections are externalized and can be discussed ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md))
- Instructor modeling of what a *meaningful* connection looks like, contrasted with a trivial one

#### Constraints
- Superficial connections ("I have a dog too") do not improve comprehension and can consume reading time without benefit [~M] — without modeling, learners default to low-effort personal associations

#### Implementation Variability
- **Timing:** pre-reading activation, during-reading annotation, or post-reading reflection and discussion
- **Format:** oral turn-and-talk, written journals, graphic organizers, or digital annotation tools
- **Weighting:** literature classes often emphasize text-to-text (intertextual themes); content-area classes emphasize text-to-world (application to current issues)
- **Choice-based:** learners select which connection type to pursue, supporting autonomy [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Upper elementary through secondary readers who have fluent decoding but need support constructing meaning
- Reluctant readers, for whom personal relevance increases engagement and persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Less useful for beginning readers still building [Automatic word recognition](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md), or for advanced readers who already make connections spontaneously [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension: integrating text with prior knowledge to build a situation model

- Intertextual reasoning: text-to-text connections build genre awareness and thematic comparison
- Reflective thinking: text-to-self connections support metacognitive and affective engagement

### Instructions
1. Select and assign the text ([Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md)); optionally preview it with an [Advance Organizer](../elements/advance-organizers.md) framing the three connection types.
2. Model one connection of each type aloud with a short mentor text, contrasting a deep connection with a superficial one.
3. During or after reading, have learners record connections using stems ("This reminds me of…", "This is like…", "This connects to what's happening in…"), optionally with [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) directly in the text.
4. Facilitate [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or peer sharing in which learners explain *why* a connection matters to understanding the text — the explanation step is where elaboration happens [Eliciting self-explanations improves understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S].
5. Extend with an [Application](../elements/application.md) task, such as writing a text-to-world argument or comparing two texts on a shared theme.
