# Making Connections (Text-to-Self, Text-to-Text, Text-to-World)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Making Connections is a comprehension strategy in which readers deliberately relate a text to three referents: their own experiences (text-to-self), other texts they have read (text-to-text), and events or knowledge in the wider world (text-to-world). It is usually introduced through instructor modeling — a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) in which the teacher verbalizes a connection — followed by guided practice with prompts ("This reminds me of…"), and independent application through journals, annotation, or discussion. The strategy is a staple of comprehension-strategies instruction in the tradition of [Reciprocal Teaching](../elements/reciprocal-teaching.md) and transactional reading theory (Rosenblatt, 1978).

## Design Implications

Connections work because they activate and integrate prior knowledge with new text content, supporting inference and elaboration [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. However, the *quality* of the connection matters more than its presence: connections must be elaborated and text-relevant to aid comprehension, not merely stated [Relevancy of emphasis directs attention.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A text with genuine points of contact for learners' experience and prior knowledge
- Instructor modeling of each connection type before independent use ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md))
- Guiding questions or sentence stems for each connection type
- A recording structure: double-entry journal, connection web, or [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) routine
- A forum for sharing connections ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or peer pairs)

#### Constraints
- Superficial or tangential connections ("I have a dog too") consume instructional time without deepening comprehension; learners need explicit criteria for *relevant* connections [-M]
- Text-to-self connections can pull attention away from the text itself, especially for narrative texts, shifting readers toward personal association rather than text-based inference [~M]
- Unmodeled, the strategy tends to produce formulaic responses; benefits depend on quality of demonstration and feedback [-W]
- For learners with little relevant background knowledge, forced connections add load without benefit [Prior knowledge not related to performance.](../claims/prior-knowledge-not-related-to-performance.md) [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Oral think-aloud modeling → shared whole-class charting → small-group discussion → independent journaling (gradual release)
- Written forms: connection logs, sticky-note annotation, double-entry journals
- Digital forms: social annotation platforms (e.g., [Perusall](https://www.perusall.com)) where learners post and reply to each other's connections
- Emphasis can shift by discipline: text-to-text dominates in literature study; text-to-world dominates in social studies and science

### Target Learners
- Elementary through secondary readers (grades 3–12), especially developing readers who read decodably but do not yet integrate text with background knowledge [+M]
- Multilingual learners, when connections are invited from their full linguistic and cultural repertoire [+W]
- Less useful for advanced readers who already integrate fluently; strategy instruction shows diminishing returns with expertise [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension: integration of text content with prior knowledge and inference-making [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]
- Engagement and motivation: personal relevance increases task value [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Intertextual reasoning: comparing themes, structures, and claims across texts
- Critical literacy: situating texts in social and historical context (text-to-world)

### Instructions
1. **Select and preview the text.** Identify likely connection points and prepare prompts for each type ([Activation](../elements/activation.md) of prior knowledge before reading).
2. **Model with a think-aloud.** Read a short passage aloud and verbalize one connection of each type, explaining *why* it helps understanding — not just that it exists ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)).
3. **Co-construct criteria.** With learners, distinguish deep connections (elaborated, text-relevant) from surface ones; chart examples of each.
4. **Guided practice.** Learners mark connections while reading using sticky notes or an annotation code ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md)), prompted by stems such as "This reminds me of…" and "This is like the article we read about…".
5. **Share and elaborate.** Peers exchange connections and push each other to explain how the connection clarifies the text ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), [Peer Discussion](../elements/peer-discussion.md)).
6. **Apply independently.** Learners write a short response built on their strongest connection, then evaluate which connection type deepened their understanding most ([Application](../elements/application.md)).

## Examples
- **Mosaic of Thought comprehension curriculum (Keene & Zimmermann, 1997)** — the source framework that popularized the three connection types in workshop-model reading instruction.
- **[Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org)** — published lesson sequences and think-aloud scripts for modeling text-to-self, text-to-text, and text-to-world connections in grades K–8.
- **[Perusall](https://www.perusall.com)** — social annotation platform where university students post connections as margin comments and respond to peers', making text-to-text and text-to-world links visible and discussable.
- **IB Middle Years Programme language and literature units** — formally assess students' ability to connect texts to personal, cultural, and global contexts.
