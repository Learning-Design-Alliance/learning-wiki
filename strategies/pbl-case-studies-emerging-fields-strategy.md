# Use PBL-structured case studies to teach emerging, rapidly changing fields like nanotechnology

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The courses used case studies and problem-based learning as teaching methods, chosen because they focus on "cooperative sharing of ideas as well as healthy discussion and resolution of problematic issues". During case-study modules, students were presented with a selected case to resolve the core issue by critically evaluating researched information, connecting latest developments to current social issues. The authors argue this approach overcomes the weakness of lecture-based modules that quickly become out of date in rapidly changing areas, promoting learning at the cutting edge of the discipline.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work cooperatively to answer challenging questions or evaluate complex ethical issues
- Students investigate and learn necessary content to understand the context of a case
#### Constraints
- The collaboration inherent in case studies presents a challenge for students who excel as individuals in lecture courses

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate STEM students in emerging interdisciplinary fields

### Target Learning Goals
- Higher-order learning skills such as application, analysis, synthesis, and evaluation
- Cooperative problem solving as practiced by professional scientists and engineers
