Strategies

Monitor tutor performance, increase consistency of tuition, and involve classroom teachers in planning and managing tuition¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The evaluation's concluding recommendation is that "the Tutor Trust continues to monitor tutor performance and identifies mechanisms to increase the consistency of tuition", arising from reported variation in tutor quality and fidelity of delivery. It further recommends that "schools should be active participants in this process and classroom teachers need to be involved in the planning and management of tuition to ensure that in all tuition complements work in the classroom" — ensuring tuition complements rather than duplicates or diverges from classroom teaching.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Schools act as active participants in monitoring; classroom teachers are involved in planning and management of tuition

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation arises from a process evaluation whose qualitative sample was small and could not be analysed by tuition type

Target Learners¶

  • Secondary pupils receiving small-group or one-to-one tuition in English and mathematics

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Consistent, high-quality tuition aligned with classroom teaching in English and mathematics

Claims¶

  • Teachers and tutors perceived tuition as most effective when tutors had strong pedagogical skills and subject knowledge, engaged successfully with pupils, and targeted sessions appropriately [+W]
  • Schools perceived Tutor Trust tuition as most effective when tutors had strong pedagogical skills, subject knowledge, and could engage pupils and target sessions appropriately [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Monitor tutor performance and conduct, with schools implementing their own oversight mechanisms, to secure pupil benefit
  • Involve classroom teachers in planning and managing tuition so it complements classroom work rather than being a bolt-on
  • Pair chess tutors with actively engaged class teachers and strengthen tutor classroom-management training
