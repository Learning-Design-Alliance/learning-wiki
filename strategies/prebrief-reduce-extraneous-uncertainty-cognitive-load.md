# Reduce extraneous uncertainty and cognitive load through a thorough pre-brief, clear simulation plan and educator support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A session-design strategy for healthcare simulation: because uncertainty is inherent at the start of simulation, the article recommends that “reducing extraneous uncertainty as far as possible while still maintaining curricular objectives may minimise detrimental impacts”. It is operationalised through a pre-brief covering learning aims and expectations, a clear simulation plan, and educator support, which the article links to reduced extraneous cognitive load and improved simulation experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A pre-simulation briefing covering learning aims, expectations around simulation and the fictional nature of the scenario
- A clear simulation plan and educator support before, during and after the scenario
#### Constraints
- Uncertainty cannot be eliminated entirely because it is inherent at the beginning of healthcare simulation, and curricular objectives must still be maintained

### Target Learners
- medical students undertaking simulation-based learning
- healthcare trainees in simulation courses

### Target Learning Goals
- managing acute clinical emergencies under uncertainty
- reducing extraneous cognitive load during simulated scenarios
