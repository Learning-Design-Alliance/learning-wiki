Common themes for effective FL teaching: teacher proficiency, rich varied input across media, careful primary-to-secondary transition, balanced implicit and explicit instruction, appropriate technology, and strategy instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 quant-synthesis), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review distils common themes from its synthesis of updated seed-review studies on vocabulary, grammar, reading, writing and speaking/listening. These "revolve around the importance of teacher proficiency, using rich and varied methods to provide students with experience of language through a variety of media, a careful transition from primary to secondary levels of education, the careful use of both implicit and explicit instructional approaches, appropriate use of technology, and strategy instruction". Teachers and curriculum designers can use these themes as checkpoints when planning FL programmes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Delivery must attend to teacher practitioner skill and FL proficiency, which the review identifies as key to success

Constraints¶

  • Evidence relevance is limited by the English primary and secondary MFL context of much of the reviewed research

Target Learners¶

  • School-aged foreign language learners (4-18 years)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Foreign language proficiency across vocabulary, grammar, reading, writing, speaking and listening

Claims¶

  • Programme characteristics and practitioner skills matter more for successful foreign language learning than the specific teaching method used [~M]
  • Focus on Form (FonF) has a place within meaning-oriented FL teaching but its effectiveness depends on teacher and learner characteristics [~M]
  • Judicious use of technology, video, film and TV with captions can support FL learning, but technology use for its own sake is not advantageous [+M]
  • Meaning-oriented approaches providing rich, authentic input with high involvement load tend to be more successful in FL learning [+M]
  • Explicit grammar teaching outperforms implicit teaching; one review found the advantage only for complex rules, but a meta-analysis found it for simple rules too [~M]
