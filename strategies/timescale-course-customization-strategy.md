# Customize the timescale course along audience level, hours, and historical content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends adapting the timescale course to the audience and available time. About one third of the proposed syllabus deals with history of physics and technology, which "experience shows" the audience appreciates; an 18-hour version could skip lengthy historical excursions and reduce examples while keeping major areas and main techniques. For graduate audiences more technical detail can be added; for high-school popular lectures the focus shifts to visual content, interactive discussions, demonstrations and games, while still teaching basic estimation skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor willingness to select vivid timescale-related illustrations appropriate to the audience level
#### Constraints
- Advanced physics topics are not meant to be taught systematically since students may not have had quantum mechanics

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science-major students
- high-school students
- physics graduate students and researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- introduction to modern physics
- numerical estimation skills
