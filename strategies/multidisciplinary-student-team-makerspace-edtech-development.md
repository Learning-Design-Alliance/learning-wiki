# Form a multidisciplinary, faculty-led student team in a Makerspace to develop needed educational software

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because available virtual and augmented reality applications lack pedagogical or accessibility focus for many disciplines, the poster recommends establishing a multi-disciplinary, in-house team of students, led by faculty, to develop the needed educational software. The team is modeled like a professional software team, with each member contributing according to expertise: two computer science students build 3D models and code, an electrical engineering student serves as subject-matter expert, an anthropology student designs user experience and consults on accessibility, and a faculty member leads. The approach also gives students career-relevant skill-building experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a Makerspace with the needed hardware (the VITaL Laboratory); students at junior and senior standing in their disciplines; a faculty project lead
#### Constraints
- The software produced is discipline-specific and the project is a summer effort with a projected end-of-summer completion

### Target Learners
- junior and senior undergraduate students on the development team, across computer science, engineering, and anthropology

### Target Learning Goals
- gaining skill sets for students' future careers through project-based software development
