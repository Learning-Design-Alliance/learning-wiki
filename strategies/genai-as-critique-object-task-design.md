# Design GenAI-integrated tasks that cast the tool as an object of critique rather than a solution generator

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends creative task designs in which generative AI is integrated into the learning experience while engaging all four SELF factors. Examples include tasking GenAI to 'generate the ini tial computer coding for a speciﬁc task, embedding speciﬁc types of coding errors within it' for students to debug, and having GenAI 'writes the same essay from multiple perspectives' for students to critically assess, synthesize, or extend with their own background. A second GenAI assistant can grade reasoning or provide hints.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration must consider students' internal and external characteristics, not only the tool's internal character (factor I)
#### Constraints
- The article notes asking generative AI to write essays for students 'would be unethical and thwart learning'; hallucination and misinformation risks require students to develop the ability to recognize misinformed or biased responses

### Target Learners
- Higher education students in STEM disciplines developing problem-solving, debugging, and critical thinking skills

### Target Learning Goals
- Debugging, critical assessment of AI output, reflection, ethics, and creative thinking
