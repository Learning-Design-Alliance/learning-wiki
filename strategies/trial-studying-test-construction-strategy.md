# Construct trial-studying admission tests by analyzing the study program and mimicking its representative courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that institutions wanting a proximal, work-sample-based admission instrument build a trial-studying test that simulates a representative course, as was done by mimicking the first course, Introduction to Psychology, with two book chapters and a 40-item multiple-choice exam. "When constructing trial-studying tests for other programs, we recommend to start with an analysis of the study program and to identify representative courses for the program that show a high relationship with performance in the rest of the program." The strategy suits admissions aimed at student-program fit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A program analysis identifying representative courses with a high relationship with performance in the rest of the program, and a unique test per program, preferably with new items each administration
#### Constraints
- For programs oriented to practical skills such as medical school or teacher training, the relevant skills are more complicated to assess; the article notes a risk of unsatisfactory test-quality, so difficulty, item quality, and reliability need close attention

### Target Learners
- Applicants to undergraduate programs with direct program-based admission, such as European university programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Predicting academic performance and student-program fit in admission selection
