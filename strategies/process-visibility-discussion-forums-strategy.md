# Make student process visible in online science courses through discussion forums and process-sharing assignments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because online instructors "cannot see the development of conclusions. Only the conclusion itself", the review recommends designing avenues for students to share their process. In Jaber et al.'s (2018) online PD course, participants posted photos of home experiments and revised explanations in a discussion forum, letting instructors design instruction from posted ideas. In Jang's (2009) blended unit, online homework with open-ended questions let the instructor track conceptual development, and presentations emphasized sharing process, not just results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must consciously foster a culture that explicitly values student process and provide clear avenues for sharing process and receiving feedback before conclusions
#### Constraints
- In fully online settings the teacher cannot intervene in students' process in real-time as in face-to-face instruction

### Target Learners
- online and blended secondary science students
- elementary and middle school teachers in online professional development

### Target Learning Goals
- making student thinking visible
- responsive teaching
- development of inquiry skills

### Affordances
- Constructivist Learning Theories Knowledge Construction
