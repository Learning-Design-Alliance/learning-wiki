# Sequence professional development for lecturing faculty: show active classrooms and establish that pedagogy choice exists before offering training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that advocates of student-centered learning first show P&A faculty what active or mixed classrooms can look like, and convince them that a choice exists, before professional development and training. The authors state that "nudging faculty to understand their place in choosing pedagogy is vital" and that "convincing them, in turn, comes before professional development and other training." They also suggest researchers "ameliorate their sense of powerlessness by discussing techniques and tools that could be used within large classes of students and lectures in all spaces."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- PD providers must be willing and have sufficient time to reach out to faculty who do not usually attend PDs and listen to and meet them
#### Constraints
- The article notes that even if faculty are correct about powerlessness, the full social context of academia cannot be influenced directly through professional development

### Target Learners
- post-secondary physics and astronomy faculty who primarily lecture

### Target Learning Goals
- faculty empowerment to choose student-centered pedagogies within their own constraints and settings

### Affordances
- Pedagogical Powerlessness Grounded Theory
