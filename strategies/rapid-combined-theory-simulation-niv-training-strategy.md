# Deploy a combined 3-h theoretical-practical session plus 1.5-h simulation as rapid emergency training for NIV management

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a compact training recipe: a 3-h theoretical-practical session covering respiratory failure, NIMV modes and nursing care, and NIV indications, followed a week later by a 1.5-h simulation with a standardised patient in a simulated ICU, adhering to the Healthcare Simulation Standards of Best Practice. The authors argue this "rapid and effective training strategy" suits urgent workforce needs, and that "Hospitals and educational institutions should consider developing and implementing similar rapid training programmes to address urgent workforce needs."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivery by experienced clinical professionals to ensure practical relevance and clinical accuracy
- Simulation scenarios reviewed and approved by research and clinical teams, with clear learning objectives aligned to theoretical content
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single-site quasi-experimental study without a control group; the authors call for comparative studies against other NIV training approaches

### Target Learners
- Newly hired ICU nurses
- Fourth-year nursing students in critical care clinical practice

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge of NIV objectives, indications, ventilator programming and nursing care
- Self-perceived clinical competence in NIMV management
