# Incorporate structured AI-literacy, including prompt formulation and critical evaluation of AI output, into STEM education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends fostering AI literacy so students engage with generative AI as "active, reflective learners rather than passive recipients of answers." Its findings motivate this: effectiveness depended on how students formulated prompts, interpreted AI responses, and applied critical thinking, and students who verified AI output caught its errors. The authors' future work will examine training in prompt engineering and evaluation of AI-generated outputs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need guidance in formulating complete, contextual, conceptual prompts rather than copying and pasting the question
#### Constraints
- The article notes incorrect AI-generated responses occur, so evaluation of output is necessary; findings come from a small single-course study with self-selection bias

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory physics students at a community college

### Target Learning Goals
- physics problem solving
- critical evaluation of AI-generated responses
- conceptual understanding of kinematics

## Claims

- [Students who provided complete, contextual, conceptual prompts to ChatGPT received more accurate responses and showed greater gains than students who gave incomplete, procedural prompts](../claims/conceptual-prompts-better-chatgpt-outcomes.md) [+W]

- [Most students used ChatGPT to support procedural steps or clarify concepts, while a smaller group used it to verify their work and demonstrated critical thinking by correcting AI errors](../claims/supporting-versus-verifying-ai-use.md) [+W]
