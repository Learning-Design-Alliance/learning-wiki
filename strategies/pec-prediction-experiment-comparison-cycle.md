# Prediction-experiment-comparison (PEC) cycle built cooperatively with simulation software and the interactive whiteboard

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A classroom strategy in which the teacher and students cooperatively build a simulation in an open environment such as Interactive Physics, using the interactive whiteboard and following modeling steps, then develop the prediction-experiment-comparison (PEC) cycle. The worked example starts from observing the trajectory of a ball in a football match, representative of the broader phenomenon of projectile motion: students identify the useful quantities, describe the phenomenon (with stroboscopic visualization), determine relations between variables with qualitative graphs, and then move to mathematical formalization.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An open simulation environment (Interactive Physics), a LIM for cooperative construction, and everyday phenomena as starting points
#### Constraints
- Presented via a demonstration of the falling-body case in the TIC Laboratory; no measured learning outcomes are reported

### Target Learners
- secondary-school physics students
- TFA teacher candidates

### Target Learning Goals
- modeling physical phenomena
- linking everyday experience to mathematical formalization of motion
