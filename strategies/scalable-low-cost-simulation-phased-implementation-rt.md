# Prioritize scalable, low-cost simulation solutions and phased implementation so resource-limited settings can adopt the RT competency framework

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A forward-looking recommendation for making the proposed competency framework feasible worldwide: because "High-cost technologies such as ECMO simulators and advanced ultrasound modules may limit feasibility in low- and middle-income countries," the article advises low-fidelity simulation models, virtual reality platforms, and regional shared simulation centers, alongside faculty development programs and a phased implementation strategy beginning with critical competencies. It was not tested in this study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty development programs supported by international respiratory societies
- Regional shared simulation centers or open-access training repositories for resource-limited settings
#### Constraints
- High-cost technologies such as ECMO simulators and advanced ultrasound modules may limit feasibility in low- and middle-income countries

### Target Learners
- RT students and newly employed respiratory therapists in low- and middle-income countries
- RT educators and training institutions

### Target Learning Goals
- High-risk, low-frequency procedural competencies such as ECMO and lung ultrasound
- Simulation-based procedural skill development under resource constraints
