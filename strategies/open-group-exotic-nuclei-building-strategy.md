# Open-ended group exploration: let students build the largest and most exotic nuclei and submit their discoveries

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation for classroom use is open exploration: "The goal is to let students explore on their own and in groups to create the largest and/or most exotic nuclei they can." Students then answer guiding questions about why nuclei favor neutrons, how excited states destabilize nuclei, and when different decay types occur. The authors further encourage students to use the companion website and, with proper permission, submit their own nuclear discoveries, positioning learners as contributors to a shared discovery record.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The demonstration materials and rules from the Supplemental Material
- Proper permission for website submissions
#### Constraints
- Because nuclear physics is abstract, the authors note it can be difficult to include in course curriculum, which this exploration addresses

### Target Learners
- students in courses where nuclear physics is included in the curriculum

### Target Learning Goals
- explaining why nuclei favor more neutrons than protons
- explaining how excited states make nuclei less stable
- predicting which decay types occur in which circumstances
