# Where Are the People Like Me?

## Description
Learners examine media examples to consider who is represented and who is not, then explore the impact of this lack of diversity and how it might affect those who are misrepresented or not represented. This activity raises awareness of media representation and its effects, promoting critical media literacy and social awareness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Media examples (films, TV shows, music, fashion), a space for discussions and reflections, and facilitation by an instructor to guide analysis.
#### Constraints
- Learners may be resistant to analyzing media critically or may lack awareness of the issues involved. It requires careful guidance from the instructor to ensure thoughtful and respectful analysis.

### Target Learners

- All learners, particularly those who want to understand the impact of media on society and culture

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote media literacy, raise awareness of representation, encourage critical thinking, improve communication skills

### Affordances
- Enhances media literacy by analyzing representation in media. Raises awareness of the impact of misrepresentation and underrepresentation. Improves communication skills through discussions and reflections.

### Personalization
- Learners can choose media examples that resonate with their interests and cultural backgrounds. The level of analysis and reflection can be adjusted to their abilities.

- Assess the depth of analysis, the ability to identify issues of representation, and the level of critical thinking demonstrated in the analysis. Monitor changes in learners' attitudes towards media and social issues.

## Impact
- Media literacy has been shown to promote critical thinking, awareness of bias, and responsible media consumption. Raising awareness of representation also fosters social justice and inclusion.
