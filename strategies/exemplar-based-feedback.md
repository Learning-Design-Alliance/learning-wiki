# Exemplar Based Feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Exemplar based feedback replaces (or supplements) verbal and rubric-only feedback with concrete samples of work — strong exemplars, weak exemplars, or a graded range — that learners compare against their own drafts. The learner's task shifts from decoding abstract criteria to judging the distance between their work and a visible target, which is how assessment standards are actually communicated in practice communities.

## Design Implications

Exemplars work because they externalize quality standards that criteria lists leave tacit; learners consistently misinterpret rubric language until they see what "coherent argument" or "adequate evidence" looks like in a real artifact [Formative feedback improves learning when learners understand quality standards.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. The mechanism is comparative judgment: placing one's own work beside a model makes gaps salient in a way that generic comments do not. Comparing multiple exemplars — especially contrasting a strong and weak one — outperforms studying a single exemplar, because variation reveals which features carry the quality rather than the exemplar's surface content [Comparing contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Authentic exemplars at clearly differentiated quality levels, ideally annotated to explain *why* each is strong or weak
- An activity structure that forces comparison — learners must evaluate exemplars or self-assess against them, not just read them ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
- A follow-up revision cycle in which learners apply the calibration to their own work ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))

#### Constraints
- Copying: learners may imitate surface features (topic, structure, phrasing) rather than the underlying quality criteria, especially when only one exemplar is given [~M]
- Exemplars of exceptional quality can demotivate learners who see the gap as unbridgeable rather than as a target [~W]
- If exemplars are not annotated, learners often attend to irrelevant features; unguided exposure to models produces weak calibration [-W]
- Less effective for highly novel or creative tasks where no prior exemplar legitimately exists

#### Implementation Variability
- **Graded range (ladder):** three to five samples spanning quality levels; learners order and justify the ordering before seeing marks
- **Contrasting pair:** one strong and one weak exemplar on the same task, maximizing feature salience
- **Exemplar + self-assessment:** learners grade their own draft against exemplars before submitting, then receive confirmation or correction
- **Progressive disclosure:** full exemplar first, then partial exemplar with sections removed for learners to complete ([Fading](../elements/fading.md))

### Target Learners
- Novices who lack the tacit standards of a discipline and cannot infer quality from criteria language alone [+S]
- Returning or first-generation students unfamiliar with academic work conventions
- Less beneficial for advanced learners who have already internalized quality standards; for them exemplars add little beyond the criteria themselves [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Calibration of self-assessment: accurate judgment of one's own work quality
- Understanding of criteria and standards in complex, holistic tasks (essays, designs, code reviews)
- Transfer of quality concepts across tasks, via comparison of multiple cases

### Instructions
1. Select 2–4 authentic exemplars on the target task, spanning a deliberate quality range; anonymize and, if needed, adapt to the current topic.
2. Annotate each exemplar to make the quality-relevant features explicit — or have learners do the annotating ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md)).
3. Ask learners to evaluate the exemplars against the rubric *before* receiving marks, then discuss disagreements ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).
4. Have learners compare their own draft to the exemplars and identify the largest gap ([Self-Assessment](../elements/self-assessment.md)).
5. Return to the exemplars after revision to check that the gap closed; repeat with harder exemplars as competence grows.

## Examples
- **Comparative judgement platforms** such as [No More Marking](https://www.nomoremarking.com) present learners and judges with pairs of authentic student work; the pairwise comparisons themselves constitute exemplar-based calibration at scale.
- **Writing across the curriculum programs** commonly distribute annotated A/B/C-range sample essays at the start of a term; students rank them and justify rankings before their first submission.
- **Code review practice** in software engineering courses uses exemplar pull requests — strong and flawed — that students critique before reviewing peers' code.
