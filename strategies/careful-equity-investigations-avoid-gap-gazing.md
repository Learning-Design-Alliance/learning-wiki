# Conduct equity investigations of reform outcomes carefully, avoiding gap-gazing that marginalizes underrepresented groups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that education researchers examine whether their reforms truly serve all students, while cautioning that looking for performance differences between groups "should be done carefully, because it risks problemat izing already marginalized stu- dents." It suggests a constructive approach: examine successful curricula and learning environments so lessons may be drawn from positive examples, and expand research beyond classroom pre- and post-tests to the learning networks and communities that transmit information and expectations to future students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple measures of success are needed rather than taking any one test score as the solitary measure of a student group
#### Constraints
- Gap-gazing risks problematizing already marginalized students and reinforcing a deficit model

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable distribution of instructional benefits across gender and ethnic groups
