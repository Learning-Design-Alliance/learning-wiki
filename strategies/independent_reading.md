# Independent Reading

## Description
Independent reading involves students selecting and reading books on their own, within their reading level and interests. It provides opportunities to practice reading strategies, develop reading interests, and improve fluency, comprehension, and vocabulary. Effective implementation includes a classroom library with a diverse collection of books, explicit instruction on selecting and caring for books, regular dedicated reading time, and conferences to support individual guidance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A classroom or school library with a diverse collection of books, designated reading time, and teacher support for book selection and reading strategies.
#### Constraints
- May not improve skills if students are consistently challenged by difficult texts. Requires teachers to provide guidance and support to ensure students choose appropriate texts.

### Affordances
- Allows students to practice reading skills, build confidence, and develop a lifelong love of reading by choosing books that match their interests and reading levels. Provides access to a wide variety of books, increasing exposure to different genres and topics.

### Personalization
- Students select books based on their interests and reading levels, allowing for personalized learning experiences. Teachers can provide guidance and support to ensure students choose appropriate texts.

## Assessment Evidence
- Increased reading achievement, engagement, and motivation. Reduced summer reading loss and prevention of diminished reading habits at the secondary level.

## Impact
- Studies have shown a correlation between time spent reading and reading achievement. Independent reading programs with guided choice and teacher conferences yield positive results (Kuhn et al., 2006; Moss & Young, 2010).
