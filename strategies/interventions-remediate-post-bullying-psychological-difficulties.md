# Interventions for bullied students should address the psychological difficulties that follow victimization, not only prevent bullying

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools tackle the emotional and behavioural consequences of bullying in order to protect attainment. It observes that anti-bullying programmes "often fail to address the impact of the consequences of bullying for the victim", and that "focus should fall on addressing the knock on effects of bullying if it does occur". Existing interventions can be built upon by continuous remediation of these difficulties.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be able to identify and remediate emotional and behavioural difficulties occurring as a result of bullying, since the mediator targeted is psychological difficulty measured over time.
#### Constraints
- The article notes existing anti-bullying interventions have been shown to reduce prevalence and help victimization but typically do not address victims' post-bullying psychological consequences.

### Target Learners
- Primary and secondary school students who have experienced bullying victimization

### Target Learning Goals
- Protecting numeracy and literacy attainment following victimization; improving psychological well-being
