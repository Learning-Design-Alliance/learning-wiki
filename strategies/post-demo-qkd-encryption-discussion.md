# Follow the demonstration with a qualitative discussion encoding a shared encryption key in measurement outcomes and detecting an eavesdropper via Bell test failure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
An immediate post-demonstration discussion connecting the Bell test to secure key distribution. The instructor explains that "it is possible to encode a shared key in the measurement outcomes of the spin projection measurements carried out by the emitter (Alice) and the receiver (Bob)," and that an eavesdropper who intercepts and measures a particle destroys entanglement, so the correlation analysis no longer passes the Bell test and communication is aborted.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The demonstration must be completed first, and students should already be familiar with shared-key encryption from previous in-class discussions
- AV support such as slides or a whiteboard presents the key-encoding example (Fig. 3).
#### Constraints
- The authors note "the analogy with quantum cryptography proposed in here is not intended to be accurate," and "several quantum communication protocols do not rely on entanglement to guarantee security."

### Target Learners
- 9th-grade Chilean high-school students
- high-school students in other contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting quantum entanglement to secure key distribution
- understanding eavesdropper detection in quantum cryptography
