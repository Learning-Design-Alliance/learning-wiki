Use intensive recasts repeatedly focused on a single structure as an implementable alternative to explicit feedback¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article suggests that intensive recasts, repeatedly directed at one pre-selected structure during oral production, can serve as an effective corrective strategy. Because the recasts in this study were "intensively directed at a single structure, simple past tense", their corrective intent was transparent through rising intonation or emphasis, and they proved as effective as delayed explicit and metalinguistic feedback. Teachers can apply this during communicative tasks by recasting errors on one targeted form each session.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Recasts must repeatedly target the same single structure (intensive, not extensive)
  • Learners must recognize the corrective function of the recast

Constraints¶

  • The article's equality finding applies to intensive recasts on a partially acquired structure; extensive recasts aimed at many structures are treated as implicit and were not tested here

Target Learners¶

  • low-intermediate EFL learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • greater control over a partially mastered grammatical structure in oral production

Claims¶

  • Recasts on simple past tense errors outperform no feedback on oral accuracy in the final session (p = .040) [+W]
  • Intensive recasts and delayed explicit/metalinguistic feedback are equally effective: no significant difference between the two feedback types on oral accuracy [+W]
  • Planned focus on form via corrective feedback on simple past tense errors improves Iranian EFL learners' oral accuracy by the final treatment session [+W]
  • Delayed explicit and metalinguistic feedback outperforms no feedback on oral accuracy in the final session (p = .044) [+W]
  • Corrective feedback effects on partially acquired structures may emerge only on delayed measures, not immediately [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Pair form-focused instruction with prompts that push learners to self-repair rather than recasts
  • Embed metalinguistic corrective feedback briefly within communicative tasks without disrupting communicative flow
