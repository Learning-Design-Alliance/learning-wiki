# Visual Supports

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Visual supports are images, diagrams, graphic organizers, illustrations, and spatial layouts used to represent information alongside or instead of verbal text. They work by leveraging the visual processing channel — pairing words with pictures so learners can distribute processing across two input channels rather than one.

## Design Implications

Well-designed visuals improve learning because presenting words and corresponding pictures engages both verbal and visual processing channels, yielding better recall and transfer than words alone [Dual coding theory: combining verbal and visual codes improves memory.](../theories/dual-coding-theory.md) [+S]. The benefit is largest when the visual is integral to the content — showing structure, relationships, or processes — rather than decorative [Irrelevant graphics and seductive details reduce learning.](../claims/coherence-principle-irrelevant-material-hurts-learning.md) [-S]. Visuals also offload working memory by holding information externally, which matters most when material is complex [Chunking and external representation reduce working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Visuals that correspond directly to the verbal content, presented contiguously with it
- Simple, coherent design — no decorative clutter, background music, or extraneous detail [Irrelevant graphics and seductive details reduce learning.](../claims/coherence-principle-irrelevant-material-hurts-learning.md) [-S]
- Labels, captions, or brief narration that direct attention to the relevant features
- For complex visuals, segmentation or progressive disclosure so learners are not overwhelmed [Cognitive overload from too much simultaneous information degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S]

#### Constraints
- Decorative or tangential images ("seductive details") actively depress learning and recall [Irrelevant graphics and seductive details reduce learning.](../claims/coherence-principle-irrelevant-material-hurts-learning.md) [-S]
- Overly complex or dense visuals presented all at once overload working memory, especially for novices [Cognitive overload from too much simultaneous information degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S]
- Redundant on-screen text duplicating narration can hurt learning in multimedia settings [Redundant presentation of identical text and narration impairs multimedia learning.](../claims/redundancy-hurts-multimedia-learning.md) [~M]
- Learners with low spatial ability may need additional scaffolding to interpret schematic diagrams

#### Implementation Variability
- **Graphic organizers** (concept maps, flowcharts, comparison matrices) structure relationships among ideas before or during instruction
- **Advance organizers** provide a brief visual framework before new material [Advance organizers improve learning and retention.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]
- **Process diagrams and annotated illustrations** depict dynamic systems and procedures
- **Visual schedules and cue cards** support learners who benefit from predictable, externally represented routines

### Target Learners
- Novices, who lack the mental models to generate their own internal visualizations from text alone [Dual coding theory: combining verbal and visual codes improves memory.](../theories/dual-coding-theory.md) [+S]
- Learners with limited working memory capacity or language processing demands, for whom visuals offload the verbal channel [Chunking and external representation reduce working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Less beneficial for experts, who already hold sufficient mental models and may find added visuals redundant

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: representing structures, hierarchies, and relationships
- Procedural knowledge: depicting sequences and processes
- Retention: creating dual memory codes that support recall [Dual coding theory: combining verbal and visual codes improves memory.](../theories/dual-coding-theory.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. Identify the core content that describes structure, process, or relationships, and design a visual that represents it directly — not decoratively.
2. Present the visual contiguously with the corresponding words, integrating labels rather than separating them.
3. For complex visuals, introduce them incrementally with [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) or progressive disclosure.
4. Connect the visual to learners' existing knowledge using [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) or [Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md).
5. Have learners generate or complete visuals themselves ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md), concept mapping) to deepen processing.

## Related Strategies

## Examples
- **Khan Academy** (https://www.khanacademy.org) — narrated video lessons pair step-by-step worked problems with synchronized visual annotations, following multimedia design principles.
- **Thinking Maps** (https://www.thinkingmaps.com) — a published set of eight visual graphic organizers (circle map, flow map, tree map, etc.), each mapped to a specific cognitive process, used across K–12 curricula.
- **PhET Interactive Simulations** (https://phet.colorado.edu) — interactive visual simulations of physics and chemistry phenomena that let learners manipulate variables and observe visual consequences.
