# Maintain a difficulty-classified dynamic case bank and rotate group roles to counter implementation challenges

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
To address foreseeable hurdles, the article recommends building a dynamic case bank and rotating roles. It states: "Establishing a dynamic case bank: Regually collect real difficult cases (diagnostic difficulties, treatment problems, ethical dilemmas) and classify them by difficulty (basic standardized type, controversial speculative type, multidisciplinary integrated type)." Role rotation requires trainees to take different roles across cases, and mentoring pairs experienced teachers with new ones.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experienced teachers such as pathology experts to mentor new facilitators
- Ongoing collection of real difficult cases with privacy protection
#### Constraints
- The article notes case selection is difficult because knowledge updates rapidly, old cases may become outdated, and cases must protect privacy without losing authenticity

### Target Learners
- Lymphoma subspecialty trainees in heterogeneous groups of 4-6

### Target Learning Goals
- Balanced participation across ability profiles and exposure to varied case difficulty
