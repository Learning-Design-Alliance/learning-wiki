Deliver in-service teacher professional development that is closely linked to the books and lesson plans in use and supported by frequent classroom observation, through existing ministry structures¶

Description¶

The article recommends an in-service professional development model in which training content is tied directly to the instructional materials teachers use daily and is reinforced by frequent observational support. PRIMR's research showed "teachers can be sensitive to in-service teacher professional development (ITPD) if that ITPD is closely linked to the books and lesson plans used in schools, and if teachers are observed and supported frequently." Training delivery is delegated to TAC tutors and instructional coaches so the model runs within existing MOE structures and available financial resources.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • TAC tutors and instructional coaches receive 15 days of training so they are capable of guiding teachers implementing the program

Constraints¶

  • The article notes future research should examine how enhanced teacher support can change classroom behaviors, indicating the classroom-behavior mechanism is not yet established

Target Learners¶

  • Early-grade primary teachers and head teachers in Kenyan government and nonformal schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved teacher literacy instructional practices leading to pupil reading fluency and comprehension

Claims¶

  • PRIMR increased the percentage of pupils meeting the Ministry of Education's oral reading fluency benchmarks after one year [+W]
  • PRIMR's first-year effects on reading comprehension were positive but inconsistent across language, grade, and school type [~W]
  • One year of PRIMR produced statistically significant gains in oral reading fluency in grade 1 formal and nonformal schools and grade 2 nonformal schools, in both English and Kiswahili, but not in grade 2 formal schools [+W]
  • The authors caution that PRIMR's first-year effects may not generalize beyond the study's disproportionately peri-urban setting [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Reflection with Others
  • Brief initial training with extensive follow-up coaching and on-site facilitation for whole-school program implementation
  • Layered implementation support: initial on-site training plus follow-up modeling, observations, coaching, and monthly virtual sessions
  • Provide comprehensive professional development and ongoing coaching when implementing a structured literacy program
