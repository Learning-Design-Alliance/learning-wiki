# Guided Notes

## Description
Guided notes are instructor-prepared handouts that outline a lecture or lesson while deliberately omitting key information — blanks, prompts, or incomplete diagrams — that learners must fill in during instruction. Cues (asterisks, numbering, headings) signal what is important to record, converting passive listening into structured, generative note-taking.

## Design Implications

Guided notes work by reducing the transcription burden of note-taking — learners spend less working memory on deciding what to write and more on processing content [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Their benefit depends on learners actively completing them: notes handed out pre-filled, or completed without engagement, lose the generative processing that makes note-taking effective. Blanks should target the content most important for later assessment, and the notes should function as a study scaffold afterward, not just an in-class activity.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A lesson structure stable enough to anticipate what learners will record
- Blanks placed at conceptually important points, not trivial details
- Explicit instructor pacing — pauses that give learners time to write
- A follow-up use (review, quiz preparation) that makes completion worthwhile

#### Constraints
- If blanks are too frequent, learners copy verbatim without comprehension and miss subsequent content [~M]
- Pre-completed or photocopied "answer" versions circulate easily; the generative benefit disappears when learners obtain filled-in copies [-M]
- Less effective for discussion-based or emergent content where the instructor cannot predict what will be said
- Over-scaffolding can suppress learners' own note-taking judgment; benefits fade as learners develop independent note-taking skill [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Partial outlines (headings only) for advanced learners; near-complete notes with word blanks for novices
- Cornell-format guided notes: cue column, notes area, and summary section completed after class
- Digital versions with embedded checks (polls, LMS quiz items) replacing written blanks
- Diagram-based variants where learners label or complete a visual model

### Target Learners
- Students with weak or untrained note-taking skills, who otherwise record too little or copy indiscriminately [+M]
- Learners with attention difficulties or slower processing speed, for whom transcription load is the primary barrier [+M]
- Less necessary for skilled note-takers, who may find the imposed structure constraining [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of factual and conceptual lecture content
- Identifying the structure and relative importance of ideas (advance-organizer function) [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]
- Building study materials for later retrieval practice

### Instructions
1. Identify the 5–10 key points learners must retain; these become the blanks or prompts.
2. Draft an outline with headings and cues (asterisks, numbering) marking importance.
3. Delete key terms, definitions, or steps, leaving blanks sized to the expected response.
4. During instruction, pause after each blank and signal when to write; do not fill blanks while speaking continuously.
5. End with a summary prompt learners complete in their own words — this generative step drives retention more than the blanks themselves.
6. Assign a follow-up use: quiz the next session, or use the notes in a [Practice](../elements/practice.md) activity.

## Related Strategies
- **Advance Organizers** — guided notes function as a concrete advance organizer delivered at the point of note-taking
- **Retrieval Practice** — completed guided notes become the material learners later retrieve from, not re-read
- **Direct Instruction** — the natural pairing; guided notes structure the "present content" phase

## Examples
- **Cornell Notes** (Cornell University Learning Strategies Center) — the canonical two-column format with cue, notes, and summary sections; widely adopted in K–12 AVID programs ([https://lsc.cornell.edu](https://lsc.cornell.edu/notes/))
- **AVID (Advancement Via Individual Determination)** — uses structured Focused Note-Taking across secondary schools as a core college-readiness practice ([https://www.avid.org](https://www.avid.org))
- Large-enrollment university lectures commonly distribute partial-outline slides (e.g., via Top Hat or LMS handouts) with blanks for definitions and worked-example steps
