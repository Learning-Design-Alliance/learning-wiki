# Implement a multi-faceted approach to mitigating digital distraction rather than relying on device bans alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends combining strategies from its three prevention categories: classroom environment regulations (policies/rules, classroom management, engaging instructional strategies, redirection), technology controls (banning devices, deactivating/silencing alerts, replacing devices), and personal behavioral interventions (informal conversations, accountability, self-control tools, self-correction). It states that "Rather than considering banning devices to be the only solution, various strategies can be implemented", and that understanding causes and informing students of consequences also helps reduce distraction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors and administrators must select strategies matched to the identified causes of distraction, since the review found causes span technology, personal, and environmental factors
#### Constraints
- The review synthesizes proposed strategies from 18 of 26 studies; it calls for future research to identify the most effective interventions, particularly for high school students, rather than asserting tested effectiveness
