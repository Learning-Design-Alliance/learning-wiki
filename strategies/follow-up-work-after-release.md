# Follow-up work after release, including probationary periods and environment change, secures social adjustment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The outline makes follow-up work one of the most important activities for securing proper social adjustment after institutional training. A probationary period is frequently most valuable, during which the individual is studied in reactions to the new environment, and a change into a different environment from that where the delinquencies occurred is often needed after release.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Follow-up must also ascertain the real merits and efficiencies of what has been done in the institution and satisfy standards the individual has acquired
#### Constraints
- The authors warn that nothing is so uneconomical as to have a breakdown after years of educational treatment

### Target Learners
- released young offenders

### Target Learning Goals
- normal social adjustment after institutional training
