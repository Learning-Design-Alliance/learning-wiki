# Eleven sequenced classroom activities for developing the sense of chronological and historical time in early childhood

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article lists eleven activities the author reports having "developed in a school context with positive results": talking about effects of the passage of time; reconstructing a story's plot to make temporal sequence explicit; playing the goose game to experience waiting, order and before/after; planting seeds and observing growth; situating life events on a horizontal axis; writing the previous year's events on an axis; using a family album; building a genealogical tree of cut-out names; viewing episodes of Once Upon a Time... Man; handling Flintstones and The Land Before Time illustrations and modelling past, present and future objects in clay for comparison and classification; and contrasting the child's personal timeline with a parallel axis of historical periods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher guidance, with the teacher emphasizing ideas such as before and after and commenting on each generation in the genealogical tree
#### Constraints
- The article recommends establishing chronological-time activities first, because children experience exclusively chronological temporal experiences earlier

### Target Learners
- children in the second cycle of early childhood education
- elementary education students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding temporal sequence and before/after
- awareness of a past prior to one's own life
- initial classification of objects by historical epoch
