Target PCP education at managing physical effects and recurrence surveillance, incorporating adult learning theory principles¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that future educational programs for PCPs focus specifically on managing physical effects and on prevention and surveillance for recurrences, where knowledge and confidence gaps are largest. It further emphasizes that "the educational programs that tackle the gaps in PCPs knowledge and confidence incorporate adult learning theory principles, including behavior change, in order to promote more tangible and sustainable changes in clinical practice". It also suggests interventions targeting communication, using electronic health records or survivorship care plans, may enhance PCP knowledge and confidence.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educational programs should incorporate adult learning theory principles, including behavior change

Constraints¶

  • The review notes prior educational programs tended to focus on physical and psychological effects and recurrence, yet gaps remain, so education alone may be insufficient

Target Learners¶

  • Primary care physicians providing or expected to provide cancer survivorship care

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Managing physical effects of cancer treatment and surveillance for recurrences

Claims¶

  • PCP confidence in managing physical effects varies by symptom: 79% felt confident managing fatigue versus 16% for chemobrain [+W]
  • Most PCPs report inadequate formal survivorship training (up to 72%) and a desire for education, especially on physical effects (76–86%) [+W]
  • PCPs report stronger skills in detecting cancer recurrences (62–78%) than confidence to screen for recurrence (6–42%) [+W]
  • A greater proportion of primary care physicians report confidence in managing psychosocial effects of cancer (24–47%) than physical effects (10–37%) [+W]
  • Only 9–12% of PCPs felt at least somewhat familiar with surveillance guidelines, and lack of guideline knowledge was a reported barrier [+W]
  • Qualitative studies consistently describe PCPs' lack of knowledge for survivorship care, with confidence depending on experience, sex, and survivor population [+M]
