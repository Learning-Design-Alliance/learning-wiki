Faculty development for GenAI-era teaching innovation should address professional identity, AI literacy, and teaching self-efficacy alongside knowledge¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's practical implication is that faculty development and AI integration efforts in higher education should not target AI-related knowledge alone but also the psychological factors the study found associated with innovation behavior. The authors state the study "highlights the roles of professional identity, AI literacy, and teaching self-efficacy in promoting innovative teaching and offers practical implications for faculty development and AI integration in higher education." Because PI and AIL partially mediated competence effects, development programs that build identity and literacy may support the translation of knowledge into innovative practice.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Programs should address professional identity, AI literacy, and teaching self-efficacy in addition to AI-TPACK knowledge domains

Constraints¶

  • The study is cross-sectional and correlational, so the article's support for these factors is associative rather than causal; several significant paths were small in magnitude

Target Learners¶

  • college teachers, including early-career instructional staff such as teaching assistants

Target Learning Goals¶

  • GenAI-supported teaching innovation behavior

Affordances¶

  • Ai Tpack Seven Domain Competence Framework

Related Strategies¶

  • Enhance students' AI literacy while alleviating excessive academic stress to reduce over-dependence on generative AI
  • Redesign structurally: grade the reasoning behind AI-assisted work and teach AI-use rules and AI literacy explicitly
  • Differentiated interventions integrating AI literacy training, psychological capital cultivation, and professional identity reinforcement
