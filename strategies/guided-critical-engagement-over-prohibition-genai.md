# Institutions should respond to generative AI in education with guided critical engagement rather than prohibition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors argue that because public sentiment remains positive while structural concerns grow, students and the public "are not rejecting AI but are looking for frameworks to engage with it responsibly." They recommend integrating AI into teaching practices and updating misconduct policies, developing critical digital literacy frameworks, and shaping engagement to foster critical thinking rather than restricting access.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions must move beyond reactive governance and develop proactive frameworks equipping users to engage critically with new tools
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on observational sentiment analysis of one platform's discourse, not on tested institutional interventions

### Target Learners
- students in educational institutions using generative AI
- educators and institutional policymakers

### Target Learning Goals
- responsible and ethical use of generative AI in learning
- critical digital literacy regarding AI's ethical and societal dimensions
