# Explicitly address the applicability of the 'more data is better' heuristic, distinguishing standard deviation from the standard uncertainty of the mean

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that lab instruction more explicitly address when the "more data is better" heuristic applies, through careful consideration of "the difference between standard deviation and standard uncertainty of the mean, with explicit learning goals and activities focused on this distinction." Instruction should build on the heuristic's productiveness while distinguishing uncertainty in individual measurements from uncertainty in the mean. Because the heuristic appeared equally across scenarios, a purely statistical treatment may be sufficient, though companion work motivates linking it to the experiment's physical and measurement properties.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit learning goals and activities focused on the standard deviation versus standard uncertainty of the mean distinction
- Building on students' existing productive resources rather than treating the heuristic as an error
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is motivated by the prevalence of Statistics-coded reasoning in this study's more-data results, not by a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduate physics students in laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- reasoning about measurement uncertainty and variability in experimental data
