Adopt PBL in OB/GYN undergraduate curricula to enhance clinical competence and satisfaction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the review's synthesis, the authors recommend PBL as an interesting tool for OB/GYN education "due to its potential benefits and its importance in the curriculum of medical schools". They expect PBL methodology to evolve, adapting itself to ever-changing social and educational contexts, allowing its strengths to be maximized and its weaknesses addressed. The recommendation rests on favorable findings for academic performance and student satisfaction compared with conventional education.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Faculty familiarization with PBL process and dynamics, since faculty usually is not familiar with this form of active methodology

Constraints¶

  • The authors note further well-designed studies are necessary to confirm findings and guide educational strategies
  • Some authors criticize small-group variability, self-directed learning, and the facilitator role
