# Reading Conferences

## Description
A reading conference is a short (typically 3–7 minute) one-on-one conversation between teacher and student, usually during independent reading time, in which the student talks about what they are reading and how they are reading it. The teacher researches the reader — asking about comprehension, strategy use, and engagement — then offers one piece of instruction or coaching and records a goal to follow up on in a later conference. The structure is commonly described as *research–decide–teach* (Calkins) or *listen–assess–teach*.

## Design Implications

Reading conferences operationalize [formative assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md) at the individual level: the teacher gathers real-time evidence of a student's reading processes and adjusts instruction to it, which is most effective when feedback targets the task and process rather than the person [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. Because the instruction is contingent on what the teacher hears in the moment, conferences enact responsive scaffolding rather than a fixed script [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]. The one-goal-per-conference rule keeps working memory demands low for both parties and makes the follow-up check meaningful.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A predictable independent reading time with a classroom culture that permits brief, quiet teacher–student talk
- A record-keeping system (conferring notebook, spreadsheet, or app) tracking date, observed evidence, teaching point, and goal per student
- A rotation schedule ensuring every student is conferenced regularly (roughly weekly for elementary, biweekly for secondary)
- Teacher knowledge of reading processes and a repertoire of [coaching](../elements/coaching.md) moves and prompts

#### Constraints
- Conferences consume large amounts of instructional time; with 25+ students, weekly individual conferring may be infeasible, and coverage of other content suffers [-M]
- Effectiveness depends on teacher expertise in diagnosing reading behavior on the fly; poorly trained conferrers default to generic "sounds good" praise, which carries little instructional value [-M]
- Students not being conferenced must sustain independent reading; classrooms without established reading stamina lose the rest of the class to off-task behavior [-M]
- Brief snapshots of oral reading or retelling can misrepresent a reader's overall ability, especially for multilingual learners or students with processing differences [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Research conferences** (assessment-focused): teacher gathers data on the reader's process before deciding on instruction
- **Coaching conferences** (strategy-focused): teacher demonstrates or prompts a specific strategy, e.g., monitoring meaning or [annotating](../principles/annotating.md)
- **Goal-setting conferences**: student-led, connecting to self-regulated reading habits
- **Partnership or peer conferences**: students confer with each other using protocols, scaling the format when teacher time is scarce
- **[5-minute writing conferences](5-minute_writing_conferences.md)** apply the same conferring structure to writing instruction

### Target Learners
- Developing readers in K–8 who benefit from individualized, contingent support [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Struggling and disengaged adolescent readers, for whom personal accountability and book-match conversations increase engagement [+W]
- Less effective as a stand-alone intervention for students needing systematic code-based instruction; conferences supplement but do not replace explicit phonics [Phonics instruction outperforms whole-word approaches for generalization.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [+S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Comprehension strategy use: inferring, summarizing, monitoring, and repairing meaning
- Self-regulated reading: goal-setting, self-monitoring, and book selection
- Reading engagement and volume: building sustained independent reading habits
- Oral language and talk about texts

### Instructions
1. Establish independent reading routines and stamina before beginning conferences; the rest of the class must work without the teacher.
2. Keep a conferring record with one line per student: date, evidence heard, teaching point, goal.
3. Open with a research question ("What's happening in your book right now?" "Show me a part that was tricky") and listen to the student read or retell briefly.
4. Decide on one high-leverage teaching point based on the evidence, not a scripted sequence.
5. Teach it — through a quick [demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md), a think-aloud, or [coaching](../elements/coaching.md) prompts — and have the student try it immediately on their own text.
6. Name the goal explicitly, leave the student reading, and open the next conference with that student by checking the previous goal.
7. Rotate systematically so all students are seen; use patterns in your records to plan small-group instruction for students needing the same teaching point.
