# Redesign in-person peer tutoring for lockdown conditions by combining synchronous tutor-led sessions with asynchronous questionnaires, exercises and recordings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable recipe for continuing peer tutoring under social distancing: keep the Learning-Assistant-style structure of advanced students tutoring small groups, but move interaction into videoconference chatrooms and Telegram channels, and wrap the sessions in an asynchronous weekly cycle of diagnostic multiple-choice questionnaires, targeted exercises, and recorded sessions with solutions posted on Moodle. The authors note these activities "aimed to facilitate the students' learning process and, also, to mimic the classroom interactions that were not allowed", and repeated the same questionnaire after each session to measure impact.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A platform accessible to all students, recruited student-tutors working in pairs, staff coordination, and recording infrastructure so asynchronous students can reuse sessions
#### Constraints
- Participation was voluntary and synchronous attendance averaged 385 students per week; the authors note further improvements are needed, for instance to stimulate greater participation

### Target Learners
- University students in large introductory STEM courses during remote or lockdown teaching

### Target Learning Goals
- Revision of taught content, addressing common errors and misconceptions, and exam preparation
