# Use e-content packages as supportive material for teaching critical units in science courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend deploying e-content packages alongside or in place of conventional teaching for difficult, critical units of a course. They report that "e-content act as a good supportive material for teaching various critical units" and that teaching and learning were more effective through technology-centric instruction. The strategy is grounded in their finding that e-content teaching produced higher post-test and retention scores than conventional methods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A developed and validated e-content package aligned to the syllabus unit
- Technology infrastructure for presenting digital content to students
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one quasi-experimental study of a single organic chemistry unit with non-equivalent groups

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate science students

### Target Learning Goals
- Achievement in difficult conceptual units such as chemical nomenclature
