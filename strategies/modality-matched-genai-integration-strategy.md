# Integrate text-based GenAI into science curricula while reserving visual and video GenAI for creative, exploratory contexts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends differentiating GenAI integration by modality: text-based tools are well-suited for college-level science curricula where conceptual clarity and verbal explanation are central, while graphical and video-based tools are best suited to creative, exploratory educational contexts such as speculative modelling of extraterrestrial environments, where "imaginative engagement is prioritized over factual accuracy." This modality-matched approach avoids the risk of disseminating misinformation from inaccurate visual outputs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators provide explicit instruction in critical evaluation skills
- Students verify AI-generated content against authoritative sources
#### Constraints
- Broader application of image and video GenAI in science education remains constrained pending future advancements in GenAI capabilities or targeted pedagogical frameworks

### Target Learners
- college students in science courses
- non-science majors in general astronomy

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of science content
- creative and interdisciplinary thinking
- critical information analysis
