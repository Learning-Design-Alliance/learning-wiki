# Under limited course time and heterogeneous ability, organize strategy training as a separate module or use implicit instruction rather than deducting time from language teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the null results, the article recommends that "the organization of a separate module is an alterative solution when course time constraints may hinder the effectiveness of training", and that a second alternative is implicit instruction in learning strategies, "which does not take away time from language teaching yet may still have powerful effects" (Chamot, 2008). It also suggests focusing on a limited number of strategies linked to a single language skill, prioritizing cognitive strategies given their stronger association with language progress.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A decision about explicit versus implicit or modular organization should consider available course time and the heterogeneity of learners' language competence
#### Constraints
- The article states the hypothesis that implicit instruction enhanced learning equally cannot be confirmed without a three-group design (explicit, implicit, no strategy instruction), which no study has yet explored

### Target Learners
- higher-education students in mixed language ability groups

### Target Learning Goals
- efficient foreign language learning through strategy use under time constraints
