# Focus communication strategy training on maintenance strategies for meaning-focused discussion tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on Nakatani (2010), the authors suggest that training maintenance strategies (e.g., active responses and shadowing) may yield stronger discussion-performance gains than the clarifying and extending strategies taught here. They write that "Future studies may find more powerful results by focusing on maintenance strategies, or simply focusing on a wider gamut of conversation strategies in general over a longer period of time," and note strategy training is best reserved for tasks focused on meaning and maintaining conversation flow.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks whose primary goal is meaning negotiation and conversation flow rather than grammatical accuracy
#### Constraints
- Based on Nakatani's (2010) cited finding, not tested in this study; the article also reiterates that strategy training is not automatically conducive to improving accuracy

### Target Learners
- university EFL students

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining conversation flow and reducing miscommunication
