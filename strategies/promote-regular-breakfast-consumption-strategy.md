# Promote regular student breakfast consumption as a mechanism to strengthen academic achievement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (8 for, 1 against) · 6 studies (5 associational, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 6 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that education stakeholders and policymakers support regular student breakfast consumption as one lever for improving literacy and numeracy outcomes, building on its finding that skipping is associated with increased risk of low achievement on all five NAPLAN tests. It states "Supports to promote regular student breakfast consumption may be one mechanism through which education stakeholders and policymakers can strengthen academic achievement." The strategy is framed alongside existing government and donor investment in school breakfast provision in Australia.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires identifying and addressing the drivers of breakfast skipping, which the article says were not captured and may vary among children and adolescents
#### Constraints
- The study was limited to government schools in South Australia; reasons behind breakfast skipping were not captured, so strategies to counter skipping will differ depending on the driver

### Target Learners
- School students in grades 5, 7 and 9 (aged 8–16 years) in government schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Literacy and numeracy achievement on standardised tests (NAPLAN national minimum standards)
