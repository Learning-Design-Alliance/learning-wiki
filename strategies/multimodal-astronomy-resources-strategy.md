# Use a multi-modal, multi-sensory approach combining sound, touch and visual elements when designing astronomy education and outreach resources

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends engaging as many sensory modes as possible when presenting astronomy, rather than relying on images alone. Interviewee NB states that "A multi-modal approach is proba- bly always the best way to go" and notes that his tactile resources "include the visual element as well". Interviewee GF similarly suggests "A multi-modal approach, with a combination of sound and haptic feedback, would be a great way forward."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Resources must combine multiple sensory modes, for example tactile resources that also include the visual element, and sound paired with context.
#### Constraints
- Interviewee GF notes that almost no two people's accessibility needs are the same, making it very difficult to develop a single set of tools that works for everyone.

### Target Learners
- blind and visually impaired learners in astronomy education and outreach
- sighted learners in astronomy education and outreach

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding astronomical phenomena and data through non-visual senses
- inclusive participation in astronomy education and outreach
