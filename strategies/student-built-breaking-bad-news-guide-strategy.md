# Use a student-built guide to good practice covering setting, meeting, transition, announcement and perspectives when breaking bad news

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
During the workshop, students collectively drew up a guide to good practice in breaking bad news (Table 1) organised into five steps: setting/environment and preparation, meeting the patient, the transition step, breaking the bad news, and perspectives. It specifies concrete actions such as "A quiet place, sitting down, telephone off", checking what the patient has understood, a summary sentence and a pause before the news, clarity about how the information was obtained, and offering psychological support and a follow-up appointment. Copies of the S-P-w-ICE-S guidelines were distributed at the end.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multidisciplinary facilitators (psychologist, social worker, physicians) and an expert patient to scaffold the collective intelligence phase
#### Constraints
- The guide was developed for cancer announcement in the OSCE; the authors believe the method may apply to other conditions such as chronic diseases, ICU management or accidental death

### Target Learners
- fifth-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- breaking bad news
- structuring difficult conversations
- patient support
