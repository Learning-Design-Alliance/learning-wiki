Strategies

Teach equivocal words (false friends) by embedding each target term in a short humorous story that makes the false-friend reading nonsensical¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The strategy presents each confusing L2 term inside a joke or humorous anecdote in which interpreting the word with its Spanish false-friend meaning produces an absurd utterance, as when a learner orders "rape in the sailor's way" expecting adventure and receives fish. The control condition presented the same ten pairs through direct explanation without humour. The article uses this strategy as the experimental manipulation whose delayed-retention advantage it reports.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The teacher must first verify, as the authors did in a pilot with native-speaker colleagues, that the group responds to the kind of humour generated by the linguistic items

Constraints¶

  • Tested only with Spanish B1 (pre-intermediate) learners of English and German on ten pairs of equivocal words per language

Target Learners¶

  • Spanish pre-intermediate (B1) learners of English and German as foreign languages

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Correct meaning and use of equivocal words / false friends in the L2

Claims¶

  • Spanish B1 learners taught equivocal words through joke-telling retained more vocabulary on a two-week delayed post-test than learners taught the same items conventionally [+M]
  • The authors report that jokes helped learners pay more attention and increased concentration during class [+W]
  • Within the experimental group, female participants responded better to humour-based teaching than male participants [~W]
  • Within the experimental groups, the humour-based memory strategy operated more efficiently with older participants than with younger ones [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Add humorous cartoon visuals to verbal vocabulary instruction targeting confusing lexical items
