Strategies

Use controversial political speeches as CDA analysis materials after students master the ten-question model¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

After a first stage of ten sessions in which students learned critical analysis on ideologically loaded coursebook lessons, the teacher offered two political speeches — Hillary Clinton's and Donald Trump's acceptance speeches — for full CDA analysis. The article's rationale is that "their speeches are controversial" and cover diverse national and global issues, and that political speeches are highly constructed pieces of discourse containing most linguistic components fruitful for analysis. This sequencing moved students from guided description-level practice to independent interpretation and explanation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must first be "well equipped with the critical analysis method" before the speech-analysis stage

Constraints¶

  • The article notes the study's reading samples were limited to political and social issues, and a broader range of genres may lead to more reliable outcomes

Target Learners¶

  • intermediate and advanced undergraduate EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • analyzing ideological load, foregrounding, and deictic pronoun use in authentic discourse

Affordances¶

  • Fairclough Three Dimensional Cda Model

Related Strategies¶

  • Keep CDA instruction focused on the reading-comprehension goal and simplify the ten-question model's difficult concepts
