# Integrate puzzle-based techniques regularly into applied nursing curricula and train instructors in their use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that 'puzzle‐based teaching techniques be used regularly in applied areas such as nursing education', that puzzles and other game-based methods be integrated into nursing undergraduate curricula, and that 'instructors should be informed about puzzle‐based techniques and integrate them into their courses'. They also call for studies of long-term effects on retention and professional skill development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor familiarity with puzzle-based techniques
- Supporting educational materials alongside the puzzles
#### Constraints
- Generalizability limited to first-year nursing students at a single university; student interaction between groups may not have been fully prevented

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge retention, learning motivation, academic self-efficacy in applied nursing topics
