# Provide higher education instructors as little as five hours of group UDL instruction to increase implementation of UDL strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that brief, group-delivered professional development can change teaching practice: "as little as ﬁ ve hours of group instruction for higher education instructors on the use of UDL principles and teaching strategies effectively increases the implementation of those strategies." The training was embedded in existing weekly Teaching Fellow meetings, used supplemental readings, and was adjusted mid-semester based on first questionnaire results, such as exploring effective uses of classroom response technology after students reported it increased engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training delivered during existing instructor meetings with supplemental readings on UDL principles and applications
- A comparison condition is needed to attribute perceived changes to the training rather than semester rapport
#### Constraints
- Results may not generalize to full-time faculty, since the trained instructors were doctoral students in a different stage of professional development

### Target Learners
- higher education instructors, specifically graduate teaching fellows

### Target Learning Goals
- implementation of UDL teaching strategies, especially multiple means of representation
