# Multi-level institutional response to problematic internet use: digital wellbeing policies, psychoeducational curriculum programs, peer-led initiatives, and screening services

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The review recommends that interventions "extend beyond general awareness and incorporate structured, evidence-informed strategies at the individual, peer, and institutional levels." At the institutional level, universities should develop digital wellbeing policies with guidelines on device use and screen time management. At the educational level, structured psychoeducational programs in the curriculum should teach self-regulation skills, time management, healthy coping strategies, and critical awareness of online behaviors. Peer-led initiatives using trained student mentors and strengthened counseling services with routine screening are also recommended, along with app-based monitoring and voluntary digital detox campaigns.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Combining policy development, educational interventions, peer support, and accessible services for a sustainable, context-sensitive approach
#### Constraints
- The review identifies no interventional studies evaluating the effectiveness of such strategies, so their effectiveness is untested in this population

### Target Learners
- Health sciences students, particularly younger students and those at risk of problematic internet use

### Target Learning Goals
- Balanced and purposeful technology use, self-regulation, time management, and protection of academic engagement and performance
