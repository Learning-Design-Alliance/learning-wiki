# Quantum Straw activity sequence: hands-on smartphone spectroscopy with collaborative data pooling and a low-cost spectrometer

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A five-hour classroom sequence in which each group receives an identical straw and "uses a smartphone app to analyze the frequencies produced; the collaboratively collected data, entered into a shared spreadsheet, yield reliable estimates of the system's first resonant frequencies". Students measure the first five modes, compare means to theoretical values, repeat measurements with straws of increasing length to see level crowding, observe atomic emission spectra with a self-built card-stock spectrometer and fluorescent lamp, and discuss wavefunction interpretation. The sequence was implemented in three courses involving roughly 100 students in 32 teams.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identical straws (L = 19.9 cm), a free spectrum-analysis app on students' smartphones, a shared spreadsheet, and blowing at roughly 90° to the straw axis for best excitation of all modes
#### Constraints
- Some students raised concerns about mathematical prerequisites and pacing; the author suggests additional time for the formalism, an optional slower-paced session, or a pre-course refresher on sinusoidal functions

### Target Learners
- upper-secondary students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding energy quantization in bound systems
- linking theoretical principles to empirical observations
- fostering positive attitudes toward quantum physics
