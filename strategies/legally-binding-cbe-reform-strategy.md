# Develop a well-planned, legally binding, long-term CBE reform strategy with cooperation among the MoH's three human resource departments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends accelerating the slow OBE-to-CBE transition through a legally binding, long-term reform strategy. The 2005 ministerial decree "was not legally binding, and the enforcement thereof was left to the provincial governments in a decentralised system". Recommendations include abolishing national standardised final examinations for OBE-trained students by a certain year, strengthening legally binding accreditation, and cooperation among the three MoH departments managing human resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Strong MoH leadership and synergy among the Department of Human Resources for Health, the Department of Health Science Education, and the Department of Continuing Education
#### Constraints
- Implementation is currently slow: fifteen years after the decree, only 21% of nursing education institutions have introduced CBE, and seven of 26 provinces have not introduced it at all

### Target Learners
- secondary nursing education institutions
- provincial health departments

### Target Learning Goals
- expanding competency-based pre-service nursing education
- achieving standardised nursing care
