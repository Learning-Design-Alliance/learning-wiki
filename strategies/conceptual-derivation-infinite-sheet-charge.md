# Motivate the infinite-sheet-of-charge toy model with a conceptual derivation showing why the field is constant and when the model applies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Rather than offering only dimensional analysis, the author describes showing how the constant perpendicular field arises from adding vectorially the 1/r^2 contributions of bits of charge: on larger rings the fields angle more steeply and cancel, while ring circumference grows as R but distance falls as 1/(d^2+R^2), so the two effects cancel. The derivation also yields the model's applicability condition: "As long as the edges are further away from the point below the point we are considering by about 20d, we can treat a finite flat sheet of charge as if it were infinite."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must present the cancellation mechanism conceptually, since the full vector calculus is beyond introductory students
#### Constraints
- The article states the dimensional-analysis argument alone can strengthen students' belief that toy models are ridiculous and useless

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- making toy-model results physically plausible
- learning when a toy model applies
