# Present animations as distinct logical chunks separated by short pauses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that, because learners rarely take advantage of self-paced flow control in animations, designers should pre-structure the material instead. As the authors state, "We therefore suggest presenting animations with distinct logical chunks, separated by a small pause, to help students in building a mental representation of the information." This operationalises Rieber's chunking finding as a design default when learner control goes unused.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Applies when learners do not actively use flow control to chunk the animation themselves
#### Constraints
- The article notes giving learners control of information chunking may be suboptimal when they do not use it; it does not claim chunking maximises outcomes

### Target Learners
- Adult novices learning dynamic process content (e.g., human immunology)

### Target Learning Goals
- Building mental representations of dynamic processes from animated presentations
