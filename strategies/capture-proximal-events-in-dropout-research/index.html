Study dropout with designs and analyses that capture proximal precipitating events alongside long-term risks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends combining theoretical and methodological developments to study the interplay of early risks and proximal precipitants. It revives Singer and Willet's call for event history analysis to model "distal and proximal predictors of dropout," and argues prospective studies need more frequent assessments and broader coverage of between-wave events, while retrospective studies need credible comparison groups of similar students who do not drop out. Researchers should also examine configurations and accumulation of stressors rather than isolated events.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Study designs that record circumstances in the months or weeks before dropout
  • Credible comparison groups of non-dropouts otherwise similar to dropouts
  • Statistical tools such as event history analysis

Constraints¶

  • The article notes infrequent assessments (12 or 24 months or more) and attrition exceeding 30% by age fifteen limit existing longitudinal studies

Target Learners¶

  • high school students
  • dropouts and at-risk youth

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding and preventing high school dropout

Claims¶

  • A substantial share of dropouts show no clear signs of disengagement or major problems in the years before dropping out [+W]
  • The association between proximal events and dropout is moderated by event severity and students' prior vulnerability [+W]
  • Proximal events emerging during high school are associated with dropout over and above pre-existing vulnerabilities [+W]
  • Dropouts' own accounts point to recent precipitating events, with pivotal events present in almost 50% of cases in interview studies [+W]
  • The authors hypothesize a possible vicious cycle in which stress-induced self-control losses generate further stressful life events [+W]
