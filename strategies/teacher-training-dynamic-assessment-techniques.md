# Prepare teachers and school support specialists in the balanced use of static and dynamic assessment within teacher training programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that dynamic assessment is slowly embraced because "teachers are still unfamiliar with dynamic techniques and procedures", having remained in the care of diagnosis specialists. It endorses Lidz's earlier call for training both teachers and school psychologists in dynamic techniques for classroom as well as cognitive assessment, within teacher training institutions and programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher training institutions and programs willing to include dynamic assessment content
#### Constraints
- Practitioners have viewed these procedures as time consumers and therefore dispensable

### Target Learners
- teachers and school support specialists in training

### Target Learning Goals
- classroom use of dynamic assessment techniques to improve school performance and achievement
