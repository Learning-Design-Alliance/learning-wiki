# Pair project-based take-home exams with written reports and oral presentations to assess understanding and expose plagiarism

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Each take-home exam required a written report and an in-class oral presentation, with no specific format requirements beyond clear communication of methods and results. The author argues the oral presentation, with the opportunity to question the student, is an important part of determining the student's level of understanding and is useful for detecting plagiarism, since a student who has not done the work is less likely to understand it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear communication of the ethical standards expected of students, the extent to which they can use others' work, and how to acknowledge it.
- Strict time limits on presentations; with a recommended 15 minutes, some students still gave 40-minute presentations.
#### Constraints
- In large classes there may not be enough time for every student to present for every exam; the author suggests one or two presentations per student per semester, eliminating presentations, or group-work with rotating presenters, noting grade fairness issues for group reports.
- Presentation time occupies a significant portion of class contact hours as exams are increased.

### Target Learners
- honours/masters level physics coursework students

### Target Learning Goals
- oral scientific communication
- written scientific communication
- academic integrity
