# Stimulate reading and writing habits throughout adulthood and aging to mitigate the impact of low education and increasing aging rates

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that "the stimulation of RWH could mitigate the impact of low education and increasing aging rates mainly in low-income and low-literacy adulthood and aging." It grounds this in its finding that reading and writing habits remained associated with speech connectedness after age correction and that RWH's coefficient compensated for the aging effect, and in prior longitudinal evidence that reading activity prevents long-term cognitive decline.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to reading and writing activity across adulthood, particularly in low-income and low-literacy populations
#### Constraints
- The article states the protective association "could not be taken as a causality effect"

### Target Learners
- adults and older adults with low education and low to middle-low SES

### Target Learning Goals
- maintenance of connected speech and cognitive efficiency in typical aging

### Affordances
- Cognitive Reserve Stimulating Activities Resilience
