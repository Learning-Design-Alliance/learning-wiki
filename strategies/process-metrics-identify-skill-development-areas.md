# Use objective process metrics such as motion smoothness and bimanual dexterity to identify specific areas for skill development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (7 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend complementing error-based end-product scoring with quantitative process metrics that point to where a trainee should improve. They report that "metrics such as motion smoothness and bimanual dexterity can be good candidates to enable the identification of specific areas for development," unlike ALI's emphasis on error scoring, giving trainees actionable feedback for future training rather than only a summative error count.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sensor-based recording of instrument kinematics and hand coordination during task performance
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that bimanual dexterity alone should be interpreted cautiously because simultaneous hand movement does not necessarily enhance the overall outcome

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees
- microsurgery residents

### Target Learning Goals
- targeted improvement of psychomotor microsurgical skills
