# Adopt the sticky-note support-and-feedback system in any course with in-class exercises

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend transferring course design aspects to other settings: "the sticky note system is not just applicable to computer programming, and can indeed be applied to most courses that involve the completion of in-class exercises and require one-to-one help from the lecturer/GTAs." Each student receives a red and a green sticky note used as a help-status indicator during exercises and as exit tickets carrying one positive and one negative comment. This gives the lecturer immediate, actionable feedback each session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-class practical exercises
- One-to-one help availability from lecturer or teaching assistants
#### Constraints
- The technical environment must match the domain: for non-programming exercises a different practical setup (e.g. a CAD package or symbolic algebra package) may be needed

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in courses with in-class practical exercises

### Target Learning Goals
- timely formative feedback and rapid resolution of student difficulties

## Claims

- [The red/green sticky-note system for in-class support requests and exit-ticket feedback was popular and coincided with a rise in the Feedback criterion score from 0.47 to 1.32](../claims/sticky-note-formative-feedback-system.md) [+W]
