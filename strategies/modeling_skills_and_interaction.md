# Modeling Skills and Interaction

## Description
Modeling involves demonstrating the skills you expect from your students, both in terms of interaction and analysis. This can be done through providing examples of high-quality work, demonstrating effective communication strategies, and modeling appropriate contributions to discussions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires instructors to be skilled communicators and analysts, and to provide clear and relevant examples.
#### Constraints
- May not fully address individual student needs without personalized feedback. Requires careful planning and preparation to ensure clarity and relevance.

### Target Learning Goals

### Affordances
- Provides clear expectations, offers practical examples, and supports the development of essential skills.

### Personalization
- Can be personalized by providing varied examples, offering different perspectives, and allowing students to choose which skills to focus on.

## Assessment Evidence
- Improved student performance on assessments, increased quality of student work, and positive feedback on the clarity of the instruction.

## Impact
- Demonstrating the skills expected from students helps them understand what is required and how to achieve success (Cormier & Siemans, 2010).
