# Modeling Skills and Interaction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Modeling means the instructor (or a peer, or a recorded expert) performs the target skill — an analysis, a discussion move, a problem-solving procedure, an academic interaction — while learners observe. Effective modeling goes beyond showing correct output: the modeler narrates decisions, monitors their own understanding, and revises course, making expert reasoning visible rather than leaving learners to infer it from finished products. Modeling applies both to *cognitive* skills (how to analyze a text, evaluate a claim) and to *interactional* skills (how to disagree productively, question a peer, build on others' ideas).

## Design Implications
Modeling draws on social learning theory: learners acquire behaviors and standards by observing a competent model [Bandura, 1977]. Its effectiveness depends on making reasoning explicit — silent demonstration of the final product produces far weaker learning than narrated modeling of the process — and on pairing observation with guided practice, since watching alone yields illusory competence [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/example-problem-sequences-reduce-cognitive-load.md) [+S]. Modeling also manages cognitive load for novices by replacing unguided search with a studyable reference model [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/example-problem-sequences-reduce-cognitive-load.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A model whose performance is visible and accurate, ideally with the modeler's reasoning verbalized (a think-aloud protocol)
- Decomposition of complex skills into observable segments, so learners can attend to one component at a time [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]
- An immediate opportunity for learners to attempt the skill with support ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md), [Application](../elements/application.md))
- For interactional modeling: authentic discussion or collaborative tasks where the modeled moves are actually needed

#### Constraints
- Observation without subsequent practice creates an illusion of mastery and transfers poorly [-S] — learners consistently overestimate what they learned from watching
- Overly polished expert modeling can hide the struggle and error-recovery that learners most need to see; showing initial flawed attempts and revisions is often more instructive [~M]
- Modeling a single canonical approach can anchor learners and reduce flexibility on open-ended tasks; contrasting multiple models mitigates this [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M]
- For learners with substantial prior knowledge, explicit modeling becomes redundant and can depress performance [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Cognitive modeling:** instructor think-alouds while analyzing a text, solving a problem, or evaluating evidence
- **Interactional modeling:** instructor deliberately enacts discussion norms — rephrasing a peer's point, asking for evidence, respectfully disagreeing — before [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or [Argumentation](../elements/argumentation.md) tasks
- **Peer modeling:** a near-peer performs the skill, which can raise self-efficacy by making the model's competence feel attainable [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- **Faded modeling:** full demonstration → partial demonstration with learner-completed steps → independent performance [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+S]

### Target Learners
- Novices encountering a skill or discourse practice for the first time, who would otherwise search unproductively for a strategy [+M]
- Learners with low self-efficacy for the task, for whom a credible model (especially a peer model) builds confidence [+M]
- Less beneficial for experts or near-experts, for whom modeling is redundant and can interfere [~S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural and strategic skill acquisition (how to carry out an analysis or procedure)
- Norms and practices of academic interaction (discussion, critique, collaboration)
- Metacognitive and self-regulatory habits — what monitoring, checking, and revising look like in expert performance

### Instructions
1. Identify the target skill or interaction norm precisely, and decide what expert reasoning must be made visible.
2. Plan the modeling episode: perform the skill while verbalizing decisions, uncertainty, and self-checks ([Articulation](../elements/articulation.md)).
3. Where useful, model a flawed attempt and its repair, or contrast two approaches, so learners discriminate good from weak performance.
4. Follow immediately with structured learner practice — [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) during [Application](../elements/application.md) — while fading support as competence grows.
5. Debrief: name the moves that were modeled so learners can label and transfer them.

## Examples
- **Reciprocal Teaching (Palincsar & Brown)** — the teacher models predicting, questioning, clarifying, and summarizing while reading, then transfers each role to student groups; a canonical faded-modeling design.
- **[Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md)** — positions modeling as the first phase, followed by coaching and articulation.
- **Writing conferences and mentor texts** — instructors model revising a paragraph live, verbalizing evaluation of word choice and structure, before students revise their own drafts.
- **Discussion norm modeling** — before a seminar, the instructor and a colleague (or the instructor role-playing both sides) model evidence-based disagreement, then students rehearse the moves in [Case-Based Learning](../elements/case-based-learning.md).
