# Employ standardized, validated UX questionnaires and commercial HMDs to strengthen and scale XR education research

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review recommends that "standardized questionnaires (e.g., SUS, NASA TLX, and others) need to be consistently employed" and that novel neurosurgery-specific questionnaires be developed, since custom author-designed items without theoretical foundations undermine comparability and validation. It further argues that promoting low-cost commercial head-mounted displays could let departments worldwide, especially in developing countries, overcome socioeconomic barriers to advanced simulation systems and enlarge participant cohorts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to commercial off-the-shelf stereoscopic displays such as HMDs
#### Constraints
- Only one included study employed standardized questionnaires; static monitors with haptic devices currently dominate and are associated with more thorough UP assessment

### Target Learners
- medical students
- neurosurgical residents
- neurosurgery residency programs in developing countries

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical skill acquisition and assessment in cranial neurosurgery
