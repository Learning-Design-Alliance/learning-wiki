# Target teachers' bullying attitudes in preservice training and in-service professional development to shape intervention responses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the review finds antibullying attitudes associated with several intervention responses and with hypothetical intervention likelihood, it discusses implications for "preservice training and in-service teachers' professional development to target bullying attitudes." Attitudes are characterized as malleable cognitions that can be targeted through training during preparation programs or professional development for in-service teachers. The strategy rests on the meta-analytic associations, with the strongest link found for hypothetical intervention likelihood.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training efforts must treat attitudes as malleable cognitions targetable through intervention, per the Theory of Planned Behavior framing the review adopts.
#### Constraints
- The retrospective combined association was not significant, and attitude–response links varied by response type, so attitude change alone may not uniformly change all responses.

### Target Learners
- preservice teachers
- in-service PK-12 teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- strengthening antibullying attitudes to support appropriate intervention responses to bullying
