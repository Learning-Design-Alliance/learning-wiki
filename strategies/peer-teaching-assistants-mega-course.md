# Staff mega-course collaborative work with trained undergraduate peer teaching assistants recruited from former high-performing students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
To support 700+ students working on Lecture-Tutorials simultaneously, the authors hired approximately 8 former high-performing Astro 101 students as peer teaching assistants (PTAs), selected through mock-teaching interviews and trained weekly in pedagogical content knowledge via a situated apprenticeship modeling student difficulties and Socratic questioning. The authors state that "having a well trained cadre of PTAs proved to be absolutely essential for the successful implementation of the Lecture-Tutorials in the mega-course." Unlike Learning Assistant programs, recruitment targeted non-majors rather than future K-12 STEM teachers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- PTAs must pass an in-person interview with mock teaching scenarios and receive weekly training meetings with the instructor
#### Constraints
- The program differs from the physics Learning Assistant model in recruiting almost exclusively non-majors and not focusing on creating future K-12 STEM teachers

### Target Learners
- mega-enrollment introductory astronomy students, including first-generation and at-risk populations

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining collaborative group discourse and access to help at scale
