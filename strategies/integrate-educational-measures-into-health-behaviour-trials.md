# Integrate measures of educational performance into trials of interventions promoting breakfast consumption and other health behaviours

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future intervention trials collect educational outcome measures alongside health outcomes, so that trials can determine whether health-behaviour interventions also produce educational benefits. They argue such results "could be vital in helping to align health improvement with the 'core business' of schools" and support communicating educational benefits to schools to enhance buy-in to health improvement efforts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trials of health-behaviour interventions must collect validated measures of academic performance (the authors note studies have often relied on self-report or acute cognitive tests instead).
#### Constraints
- The authors note the evidence base linking health behaviours to educational outcomes is underdeveloped, with little prospective evidence and few interventions evaluated for educational impacts.

### Target Learners
- Primary school children aged 9–11 years

### Target Learning Goals
- Educational attainment (Key Stage 2 SATs performance in English, Maths and Science)
