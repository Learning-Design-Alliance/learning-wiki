# Two-step intervention: first decrease victimisation and build motivational factors, then increase achievement and engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the meta-analytic findings, the authors recommend that educational interventions targeting academic success address bullying and motivation together. "The first step of an intervention program should therefore be decreasing victimisation and particularly focusing on improving motivation, self-esteem, self-efficacy and self-concept. The second step is to increase students' academic achievement and enhance their educational engagement." They add that family and friend support and positive teacher–child relationships can protect bullied children from poor academic outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should include components of positive reinforcement, encouragement, and programs for enhancing academic engagement and achievement amongst children and adolescents.
#### Constraints
- The article notes mediation studies are few, mostly from a few countries, and that interventions may need to differ in each country as cultures define bullying differently.

### Target Learners
- children and adolescents involved in or at risk of bullying victimisation

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement
- academic engagement
- motivation, self-esteem, self-efficacy and self-concept
