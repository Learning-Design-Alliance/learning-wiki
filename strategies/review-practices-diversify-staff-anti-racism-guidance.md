# Review institutional practices, diversify staff and student populations, and develop guidance on reporting and tackling racism

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that stakeholders of graduate-entry medical education reflect on institutional practices affecting student–peer and student–staff relationships, review proposals to diversify student and staff populations, and develop guidance on reporting and tackling racism. It states that "Increasing BME representation among medical school staff is likely to help students feel more supported" and that anti-racism training should go beyond knowledge competency toward fostering critical awareness of self and others.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment to reviewing diversity proposals and evaluating guidance on tackling racism
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a small single-institution qualitative study, so replication elsewhere is not established

### Target Learners
- graduate-entry medical students from Black and minority ethnic backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable attainment and supportive learning relationships in undergraduate medical education
