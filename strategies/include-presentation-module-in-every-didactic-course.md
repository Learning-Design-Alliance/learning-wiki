# Include a video-feedback presentation module in every didactic course for skills lab tutors, and continue offering Peyton's Method despite time concerns

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that a presentation module with video feedback be included in every didactic course for skills lab tutors, since it was rated most helpful, most transferable, and most useful. They also retain Peyton's Method in the training, considering it "a very effective teaching method for practical skills and as a feasible approach for PAL teaching", and call for further randomized and prospective studies with objective evaluation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to video recording and 1:1 video analysis, structured peer observation sheets, and experienced instructors
#### Constraints
- A complete day of training represents a remarkable amount of time, which the authors appraise as warranted given the number of tutors benefiting; Peyton's Method transfer is limited by perceived curriculum time constraints

### Target Learners
- student peer tutors in medical skills labs

### Target Learning Goals
- effective presentation and explanation
- teaching practical skills via Peyton's 4-Step Approach
