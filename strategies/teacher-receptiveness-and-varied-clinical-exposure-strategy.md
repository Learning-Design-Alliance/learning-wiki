# Ensure teacher receptiveness and organise clinical practice so students encounter varied situations before graduating

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article identifies the learning environment — particularly teacher receptiveness — as important for reducing student stress during the preclinical-to-clinical transition. It recommends that clinical practice be organised so students "encounter as many different situations as possible before graduating", avoiding first occurrences in work environments where help is harder to get than in a learning environment with a teacher present. Teachers should support students in the most problematic steps identified by the study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers available and perceived as cooperative during clinical training
#### Constraints
- The study measured confidence in discussing cases with teachers but did not test an intervention on teacher receptiveness

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students in clinical training

### Target Learning Goals
- reduced stress during emergency management
- confidence in discussing cases with teachers
