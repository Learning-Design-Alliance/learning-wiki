# Teaching Social Awareness Through Current Events and Fictional Characters

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 2 causal), `q3` · 3 of 5 report an effect size

## Description
This strategy uses two kinds of narrative material — real events in the news and the inner lives of fictional characters — as anchors for teaching social awareness: the ability to take others' perspectives, recognize emotions, and understand how circumstances shape behavior. The instructor models empathetic reasoning aloud ("Why might this person have acted this way? What are they feeling?"), then structures discussion or role-play in which learners practice generating and comparing multiple perspectives.

## Design Implications

Narrative provides low-stakes psychological distance: learners can analyze a fictional character's motives or a news actor's situation without the social risk of analyzing peers, which makes it a safe rehearsal space for empathy before applying it to real relationships. Perspective-taking exercises built on rich narratives improve empathic concern and social inference, and reading literary fiction in particular is associated with short-term gains in theory-of-mind performance [Kidd & Castano, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1239918) [+W]. The effect depends on active engagement — guided discussion, role-play, or structured response — rather than passive exposure to the story [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Narratively rich material with psychologically complex characters or real stakeholders (news reports, literature, documentary media)
- Instructor modeling of empathetic reasoning — naming emotions, hypothesizing motives, acknowledging multiple valid perspectives ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) style narration)
- Structured response activities: discussion, writing in a character's voice, or role-play ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), [Acting-Role-Play](acting-role-play.md))
- Ground rules that make disagreement about perspectives safe and productive
- Age-appropriate, well-vetted news stories or narratives with genuine social or ethical content
- Structured discussion protocols or prompts that direct attention to characters' emotions, motives, and constraints — not just plot
- A facilitator skilled in managing discussion of sensitive or contested topics
- A bridge activity connecting the case to learners' own experience ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), reflective writing, or [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md))

#### Constraints
- Learners who have not developed introspective vocabulary may default to judgment ("they were wrong") rather than perspective analysis; explicit emotion vocabulary and sentence stems are needed [-W]
- Single-perspective news coverage or flat characters give learners nothing to contrast; material must contain genuine ambiguity or multiple stakeholders [-M]
- Discussion dominated by a few voices produces conformity rather than perspective exploration; use structured turn-taking or written pre-discussion responses [-M]
- Fictional empathy gains measured in the lab are small and may not persist without repeated practice across varied contexts [~W]
- Effects of fiction on empathy are small and depend on transport and quality of reflection; passive consumption without guided discussion produces little measurable gain [Reading literary fiction improves empathy and perspective-taking.](../claims/reading-literary-fiction-improves-theory-of-mind.md) [~W]
- Poorly framed current events can heighten anxiety, stereotype threat, or polarization, particularly when events involve learners' own communities [-M]
- Fictional exemplars that are one-dimensional or stereotyped can reinforce rather than reduce biased social schemas [-M]
- Discussion dominated by a few voices limits perspective-taking benefits for the rest of the group [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Current events variant: analyze a news event from the perspectives of multiple stakeholders, then compare with actual reported accounts
- Fiction variant: hot-seating (learners answer questions in character), diary writing from a character's viewpoint, or predicting character reactions at decision points
- Media variant: film clips or documentary footage with paused "what is happening inside this person?" prompts
- Cross-cultural variant: pairing the same event as reported by outlets from different countries to make perspective itself visible
- **Fiction-first:** use novels, picture books, or film characters (e.g., the [Reading With Relevance](https://readingwithrelevance.org) curriculum) as the anchor, then connect to real issues
- **Events-first:** start with a curated news story, then use role-play or writing from the perspectives of people involved
- **Structured programs:** embed both in a sequenced SEL curriculum such as [Second Step](https://www.secondstep.org) or [Facing History and Ourselves](https://www.facinghistory.org), which pairs historical/current events with narrative testimony
- **Media analysis:** have learners critique how news coverage portrays different groups, adding a media-literacy dimension

### Target Learners
- K–12 learners developing social-emotional competencies, especially in advisory or SEL blocks
- Adolescents, who are developmentally primed for perspective-taking and identity work [+W]
- Learners with strong narrative engagement; reluctant readers benefit from visual or current-events variants
- Less effective as a one-off activity for learners with limited emotion vocabulary — needs recurrence across the year
- K–12 learners, with picture books and read-alouds effective from early elementary [Dialogic reading improves expressive language.](../claims/dialogic-reading-improves-expressive-language.md) [+S]
- Learners who resist direct social-skills instruction but engage through story and character
- Mixed-perspective groups, where differing interpretations of the same event or character become the discussion material

### Target Learning Goals
- Social awareness: recognizing others' emotions and situational influences on behavior
- Perspective-taking: generating and weighing multiple interpretations of the same event
- Empathy and prosocial reasoning: connecting understanding to compassionate response
- Critical media literacy: noticing whose perspective is represented and whose is missing
- Perspective-taking: inferring others' emotions, motives, and constraints
- Ethical reasoning: evaluating choices and consequences in social dilemmas
- Social awareness: recognizing group norms, inequities, and community issues

### Instructions
1. Select material with genuine perspective complexity — a news event with multiple stakeholders or a story with a morally ambiguous character.
2. Model empathetic analysis aloud, naming emotions and hypothesizing motives before inviting learner analysis ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)).
3. Have learners generate at least two distinct perspectives, in writing or small groups, before whole-group discussion ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).
4. Deepen engagement through embodiment or voice — role-play, hot-seating, or writing in character ([Acting-Role-Play](acting-role-play.md)).
5. Debrief by connecting the analyzed perspectives to learners' own experiences and to prosocial action, consistent with the [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md) principle.
6. Provide feedback on the quality of perspective-taking (evidence cited for another's viewpoint, emotional accuracy), not on whether learners reached a "correct" interpretation.

## Related Strategies
- [Acting-Role-Play](acting-role-play.md) — embodiment of perspectives is the most immersive variant of this strategy
- [Case-Based Discussion](../strategies/case-based-discussion.md) — the current-events variant is essentially a social case discussion
- [Check-In Activities](../strategies/check-in-activities.md) — builds the introspective vocabulary this strategy depends on

## Examples
- **Facing History and Ourselves** (https://www.facinghistory.org) — published curriculum using historical events and primary accounts to teach perspective-taking and ethical reasoning in secondary classrooms.
- **Second Step** (https://www.secondstep.org) — SEL curriculum using scripted scenarios and character vignettes for emotion recognition and perspective-taking practice in elementary grades.
- **Newsela** (https://newsela.com) — leveled current-events articles at multiple reading levels, enabling the same event to be discussed by mixed-ability classes.
- **[Facing History and Ourselves](https://www.facinghistory.org)** — pairs historical documents, survivor testimony, and current events with structured reflection to teach civic and social awareness in secondary classrooms.
- **[Second Step](https://www.secondstep.org)** — elementary SEL curriculum using scripted scenarios and character vignettes for perspective-taking practice.
- **[The Choices Program](https://www.choices.edu) (Brown University)** — curriculum units in which students role-play stakeholders in current policy debates.
- **Read-aloud plus character interview** — after a picture book, students "interview" a character about their feelings and choices, then write a journal entry from that character's perspective.

# Use Current Events And Fictional Characters To Teach Social Awareness

## Description
This strategy teaches social awareness — perspective-taking, empathy, and recognition of social norms and inequities — by anchoring discussion and analysis in two kinds of concrete material: current events drawn from news and community life, and fictional characters from literature, film, or media. Learners examine what characters or real people experienced, felt, and chose, then connect those cases to their own lives. It is typically carried out through structured discussion, role-play, and reflective writing rather than direct instruction about social skills.

## Design Implications

Narrative material is effective for social-emotional learning because characters provide psychologically safe, low-stakes targets for perspective-taking: learners can discuss a fictional character's dilemma or a distant news event without the interpersonal risk of disclosing personal experience [Reading literary fiction improves empathy and perspective-taking.](../claims/reading-literary-fiction-improves-theory-of-mind.md) [+W]. Current events add authenticity and perceived relevance, which supports engagement and transfer to real social contexts [Authentic purposes increase motivation and learning.](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) [+M]. The strategy enacts [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md) by giving learners repeated, structured practice adopting others' viewpoints.

### Instructions
1. Select a current event or fictional narrative with a clear social dilemma and multiple stakeholder perspectives.
2. Establish discussion norms and activate relevant background knowledge ([Activation](../elements/activation.md)) so learners enter the case with needed context.
3. Present the material, pausing at decision points to ask "What is this person feeling? What are their options?" ([Anchored Instruction](../elements/anchored-instruction.md)).
4. Facilitate structured [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or role-play ([Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md)) in which learners argue from assigned perspectives ([Assigned Positions](../elements/assigned-positions.md)).
5. Bridge to learners' lives: reflective writing or a [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) connecting the case to their own social experiences.
6. Close with an application or action step — a class project, letter, or community connection — to support transfer.
