# Inquiry Based Learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Inquiry based learning positions learners as investigators: they pose or explore questions, collect and evaluate evidence, and construct explanations, with the instructor acting as guide rather than transmitter. It spans a continuum from structured inquiry (the question and procedure are given; learners generate the explanation) to open inquiry (learners generate questions and design investigations themselves) [Banchi & Bell's four-level framework](https://www.nsta.org/science-and-children) [~M].

## Design Implications

Inquiry can produce strong conceptual learning, but only when it is scaffolded — pure discovery without guidance consistently underperforms guided instruction [Alfieri et al. (2011) meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024848) [-S]. The critical design work is in the scaffolds: question prompts, evidence-organization tools, and instructor feedback during the investigation [Hmelo-Silver et al. (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520701263368) [+M]. Structured and guided inquiry reliably outperforms unguided discovery, and unguided inquiry can actively harm learning for novices [Kirschner, Sweller & Clark (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1) [-S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A driving question or problem that is genuinely problematic for learners but tractable with available resources
- Scaffolds for each phase: question formulation, data collection, evidence evaluation, and explanation construction ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md), [Argumentation](../elements/argumentation.md))
- Instructor monitoring with feedback targeted at the reasoning process, not just the final answer [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- Activation of relevant prior knowledge before investigation begins ([Activation](../principles/activation.md))

#### Constraints
- Unguided or minimally guided inquiry overloads working memory for novices, who lack schemas to organize the search [Kirschner, Sweller & Clark (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1) [-S]
- Inquiry benefits diminish and can reverse as expertise grows; experts learn better from direct information than from rediscovery [The expertise reversal effect.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Open inquiry without structure can leave learners practicing and encoding errors [Erroneous examples can build conceptual knowledge when errors are made explicit.](../claims/erroneous-examples-build-conceptual-knowledge.md) [~M]
- Assessment misalignment: inquiry develops reasoning and explanation skills that traditional recall tests do not capture, undermining perceived value

#### Implementation Variability
- **Structured inquiry**: question and method given, learners generate explanations — appropriate entry point for novices
- **Guided inquiry**: learners design methods within instructor-set questions, with prompts and tools
- **Open inquiry**: learners pose questions and design full investigations — best for learners with substantial domain knowledge
- **Productive failure variants**: learners attempt problems before instruction, then receive canonical teaching [Productive failure improves conceptual learning.](../claims/productive-failure-improves-conceptual-learning.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Learners with moderate prior knowledge benefit most; complete novices need heavier scaffolding, and advanced learners may find guided inquiry redundant [The expertise reversal effect.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Learners developing scientific practices — hypothesis generation, evidence evaluation, argumentation — rather than only content recall
- Learners who benefit from [Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md) prompts during investigation [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding and mental-model revision, especially where prior misconceptions must be confronted ([Cognitive Disequilibrium](../principles/cognitive-disequilibrium.md))
- Epistemic practices: formulating questions, evaluating evidence, constructing and defending arguments
- Transferable inquiry skills and dispositions (curiosity, tolerance of ambiguity) rather than efficient acquisition of large bodies of factual content

### Instructions
1. Activate prior knowledge and surface initial ideas or predictions ([Activation](../principles/activation.md), [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
2. Present a driving question or phenomenon that creates a gap between what learners know and what they need to explain
3. Support investigation with structured tools — data tables, question prompts, evidence organizers — fading support as competence grows [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+S]
4. Coach during investigation, using process-level feedback and [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) to monitor reasoning [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
5. Have learners construct and defend explanations, comparing cases to support abstraction [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M]
6. Consolidate with instructor-led synthesis that names the canonical concepts and corrects residual misconceptions — the phase most often omitted and most needed by novices

## Examples
- **[BSCS 5E Instructional Model](https://bscs.org/bscs-5e-instructional-model/)** — Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate; a widely adopted structured-inquiry sequence for science classrooms that pairs exploration with explicit explanation.
- **[IQWST (Investigating and Questioning our World through Science and Technology)](https://iqwst.org)** — Middle-school inquiry curriculum with heavily scaffolded investigations and explanation construction.
- **[Problem-Based Learning at Maastricht University](https://www.maastrichtuniversity.nl)** — Guided inquiry in small groups with trained tutors, illustrating the scaffolding infrastructure inquiry requires at scale.
