# Use LLMs as supportive educational tools and complements, not replacements, in medical education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that LLMs be positioned as supportive educational tools that complement conventional learning methods in medical education, given their strong but variable examination accuracy. It states they "should be used cautiously as complements rather than replacements for traditional learning methods," guided by ethical principles and scientific standards. This applies particularly to resource-limited settings where access to expert instructors is constrained.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration must proceed with careful awareness of LLM limitations, ensuring that AI supports rather than replaces expert human judgment
#### Constraints
- Due to variability and the risk of errors, LLMs are not yet ready to replace traditional educational resources or to be used independently in formal assessments

### Target Learners
- medical students
- health professions students

### Target Learning Goals
- medical knowledge assessed by licensing examinations
- clinical decision-making support
