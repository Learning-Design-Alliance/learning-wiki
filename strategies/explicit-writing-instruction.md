# Explicit Writing Instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Explicit writing instruction teaches the components of skilled writing — planning, drafting, revising, sentence construction, and genre structure — through direct explanation, [modeling](../elements/act-it-out.md), guided practice, and independent application. The instructor makes invisible expert decisions visible (e.g., thinking aloud while planning a paragraph), then supports learners as they apply the same moves to their own text. It contrasts with immersion-only approaches that assume students will absorb writing skill from reading and frequent writing alone.

## Design Implications

Explicit writing instruction is among the most consistently supported approaches in writing research: teaching writing strategies, summarization, and sentence-combining produce measurable gains in text quality across grade levels [Graham & Perin, 2007 meta-analysis of adolescent writing instruction](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.99.3.445) [+S]. Its effectiveness depends on making expert reasoning observable and then fading support — modeling without guided application produces recognition without production. Because writing imposes heavy simultaneous demands on working memory (generating ideas, transcribing, monitoring), explicit instruction reduces the load by automating sub-skills and sequencing them [Cognitive overload degrades learning when demands exceed working memory capacity.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear model of the target genre or skill, with the instructor's reasoning made visible ([articulation](../elements/articulation.md))
- Guided practice with feedback before independent writing ([application](../elements/application.md))
- Instruction sequenced from sentence-level to whole-text skills, respecting working memory limits [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Mentor texts that exemplify the target structure, ideally compared and contrasted [Comparing contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Overly scripted, formulaic instruction (e.g., rigid five-paragraph templates) can produce writing that satisfies the formula but transfers poorly to authentic tasks and audiences [~M]
- Whole-class explicit lessons pitched at one level misfit learners far above or below it; effectiveness drops when instruction ignores prior skill [~M]
- Explicit strategy instruction without extended opportunities to write and revise shows weak maintenance; strategies must be practiced in the context of real writing purposes [~M]
- Sentence-level drill disconnected from meaningful composing does not improve overall text quality [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Strategy instruction (SRSD)**: teaching self-regulated strategy development — planning strategies plus self-monitoring statements — the best-evidenced variant [+S]
- **Sentence combining**: learners combine short kernel sentences into complex ones; strong effects on syntax and quality [+S]
- **Text-structure instruction**: teaching genre schemas (narrative, argument, compare-contrast) with graphic organizers [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]
- **Process writing with explicit minilessons**: brief direct teaching embedded in a workshop model, rather than a fully scripted sequence [~M]

### Target Learners
- Struggling writers and students with limited prior knowledge, who benefit most from modeled strategies and reduced search [Graham & Perin, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.99.3.445) [+S]
- Elementary and adolescent learners across content areas; effects hold in second-language writing contexts [+M]
- Proficient writers need less explicit structure; excessive scaffolding can suppress their own planning and voice [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Genre and text-structure knowledge: knowing how a genre is organized
- Procedural fluency: planning, drafting, and revising routines that become automatic [Automaticity frees resources for higher-level processing.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M]
- Self-regulation: goal setting, self-monitoring, and revising one's own composing process
- Sentence-level craft: syntax variety, cohesion, and vocabulary use

### Instructions
1. **Set a purpose with a mentor text.** Analyze an exemplar of the target genre, ideally contrasting strong and weak versions [Comparing contrasting cases improves learning.](../claims/comparing-contrasting-cases-improves-learning.md) [+M]
2. **Model the strategy.** Think aloud while planning or drafting a short piece, verbalizing decisions ("I'll state my claim first because…") — the writing analogue of [articulation](../elements/articulation.md) in [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md)
3. **Guide practice.** Co-construct a text with the class or have students complete a partially written model ([application](../elements/application.md)) with prompts and feedback
4. **Fade support.** Move from shared writing to independent writing as competence grows, removing organizers and sentence frames
5. **Teach self-regulation.** Add goal setting and self-checking so students run the strategy without the teacher — the core of SRSD [+S]
6. **Assess and reteach.** Use writing samples to identify which sub-skill broke down, and reteach that component [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M]

## Examples
- **SRSD (Self-Regulated Strategy Development)** — the most researched explicit writing framework; e.g., POW+TREE for persuasive writing, with strong meta-analytic support ([ThinkSRSD resources](https://srsdresearch.org)) [+S]
- **The Writing Revolution (Hochman & Wexler)** — explicit sentence-level instruction (because/but/so activities, sentence combining) embedded across content areas ([thewritingrevolution.org](https://www.thewritingrevolution.org))
- **Graham & Perin's Writing Next recommendations** — explicit strategy instruction, summarization, and sentence combining top the effect-size rankings for adolescent writing ([Carnegie Corporation report](https://carnegie.org/media/filer_public/9c/e5/9ce573d5-06a0-4442-9237-88be5f74cb2e/ccny_report_2007_writing.pdf))
