Design reading comprehension activities that require students to collaborate with the teacher on joint tasks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teachers design instructional activities in reading comprehension that require students' collaboration in accomplishing reading tasks jointly with the teacher, naming dramatization, demonstration, miming and sketching as activity forms. It further recommends organising learners in varied groupings such as mixed academic ability, reading ability and interest to promote meaningful interaction, and embedding such activities in textbooks and the English curriculum for basic education.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Activities should be carried out jointly by teachers and learners in order to obtain meaningful results

Target Learners¶

  • second language learners at basic education level

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading comprehension in English as a second language

Claims¶

  • Students taught reading comprehension with Joint Productive Activity scored higher on post-tests than students taught by the traditional method [+W]
  • Six weeks of Joint Productive Activity instruction significantly improved Nigerian Junior Secondary II students' reading comprehension performance from pre-test to post-test [+W]
  • JPA lessons showed increased learner involvement and participation compared with passive traditional lessons [+W]
  • Traditional reading comprehension instruction produced no statistically significant pre-test to post-test improvement in the control group [+W]
