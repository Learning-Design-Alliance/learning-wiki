# Proximity Management

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Proximity management is a classroom strategy in which the teacher circulates among students during individual or group work rather than remaining at the front of the room. Physical closeness serves three functions simultaneously: it allows rapid detection and redirection of off-task behavior, it creates natural opportunities for individual [check-ins](../elements/check-in.md) and feedback, and it signals availability and attention to every learner.

## Design Implications

Proximity works because teacher presence lowers the threshold for both behavioral self-regulation and help-seeking: students near the teacher are more likely to stay on task and to ask questions when stuck [~M]. It converts monitoring from a distant, reactive activity into a continuous, low-cost diagnostic stream — the teacher sees emerging misconceptions in real time and can intervene before errors consolidate [Formative feedback during practice improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. The strategy is most powerful when circulation is purposeful — planned routes that reach struggling students first — rather than random wandering.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task students can work on independently or in groups ([Practice](../elements/practice.md), collaborative work, stations)
- A physical layout that permits movement between desks or groups
- Clear routines so students know what to do when the teacher is not beside them
- Brief, focused interactions — proximity check-ins should last seconds, not minutes, or the rest of the class loses monitoring

#### Constraints
- Effectiveness drops sharply if the teacher stops to tutor one student or group for an extended period; prolonged stops create a monitoring vacuum elsewhere in the room [-M]
- Can foster dependence: students who expect the teacher to come to them may wait rather than attempt work independently [~W]
- In large lecture halls, online, or hybrid settings, physical proximity is unavailable; equivalent presence must be engineered through tools (dashboard monitoring, chat presence) with weaker evidence [-W]
- Overuse can feel like surveillance to adolescents, undermining [autonomy](../principles/autonomy.md) and intrinsic motivation [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Circulating scan:** continuous slow movement with brief glances at work, no verbal interaction
- **Targeted proximity:** positioning near known off-task students or during transitions
- **Feedback sweeps:** planned route that reaches every student's work once per session for a 10–20 second check
- **Digital analog:** live dashboards in platforms such as Google Classroom or Desmos that let the teacher "stand next to" every student's work simultaneously

### Target Learners
- Younger students and students with attention or self-regulation difficulties, who benefit most from external structure [~M]
- Reluctant help-seekers, for whom a teacher passing by lowers the social cost of asking a question [~W]
- Less necessary for highly self-regulated learners working on well-matched tasks

### Target Learning Goals
- Any goal involving independent practice: procedural fluency, writing, problem sets
- Behavioral and self-regulation goals: staying on task, transitioning between activities
- Formative assessment goals: gathering real-time evidence of understanding

### Instructions
1. Launch the task with clear instructions and a visible success criterion so students can work without the teacher ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md)).
2. Begin circulating immediately — start near students most likely to struggle, and give a brief [check-in](../elements/check-in.md) at each stop.
3. Keep individual interactions under ~30 seconds; if a student needs more, note it and return during a later sweep or schedule follow-up [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md).
4. Use nonverbal redirection (standing near, pointing at the task) before verbal correction for minor off-task behavior.
5. Collect patterns across the room — if several students share a misconception, stop circulation and address it whole-class.

## Examples
- **KIPP and other "sweep the room" no-excuses school models** — teachers circulate constantly during independent work, using proximity and nonverbal correction as the first tier of behavior support ([Teach Like a Champion](https://teachlikeachampion.com) technique "What to Do" and "Sweeping").
- **Writing workshops (Lucy Calkins / Units of Study)** — the conferring component is structured proximity: the teacher moves desk to desk for brief one-to-one conferences while the class writes ([Heinemann](https://www.heinemann.com)).
- **Desmos Classroom** — the teacher dashboard provides a digital form of proximity, letting the instructor see every student's work live and pause the class to address shared errors.
