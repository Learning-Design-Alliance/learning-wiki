# De-emphasize high-stakes assessments and use specifications (mastery) grading to reduce instructor workload and clarify assignment relevance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors implementing student-centered pedagogies manage assessment and grading workload by shifting assessment structure. It states instructors "can use alternative strategies such as de-emphasizing high-stakes assessments (exams, large term papers), and increasing low-to-medium-stakes assignments or using specifications (mastery) grading." This provides clearer direction on assignment relevance and rubrics and engages students in their educational path, while universal design for learning in course development saves instructor time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need rubrics and clearly specified assignment expectations so that specifications grading provides a clear direction to the relevance of assignments
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a response to the grading time that case study-based and student-centered courses require; it does not report outcome data for the strategy itself

### Target Learners

### Target Learning Goals
- sustainable implementation of student-centered course design
- student engagement with assessment
