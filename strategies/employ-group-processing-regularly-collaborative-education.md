# Employ group processing regularly in collaborative and team-based education without subordinating it to content-based learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends extending group processing beyond enquiry-based learning into other collaborative and team-based educational contexts. The authors conclude that "If implemented regularly and not subordinated in time and priority to content-based learning, group processing can have a salutary effect on both academic achievement and the personal qualities of listening, receiving feedback, and collaborating". Implementation requires careful facilitation so that public feedback does not suppress constructive comments or promote tension, and facilitators must continue modelling feedback delivery and reception.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular implementation with protected time and priority relative to content-based learning
- Careful and intentional facilitation so public feedback does not suppress constructive feedback or promote tension
- Facilitator modelling of how to deliver and receive feedback effectively
#### Constraints
- Public feedback may counterproductively promote tension and disrupt peer relationships if not handled appropriately
- Students' perceived inability to assess peers appropriately may require facilitator intervention

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate students
- students in collaborative and team-based education

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement
- listening and receiving feedback
- collaboration skills
