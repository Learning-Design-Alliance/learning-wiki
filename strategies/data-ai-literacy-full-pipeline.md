# Build data and AI literacy spanning the full observational-to-model pipeline

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A strategy recommending that early-career scientists work with open-access datasets and standard analytical tools, and understand the entire data pipeline from in-situ measurements to climate model ensemble biases. The authors advise to "take advantage of the existing open-access datasets, popular analytical tools (e.g., Python, Jupyter, and Xarray)" and to be curious about the collectors and users of data. This literacy, combined with mathematics and physics skills, underpins evaluating work from oneself, others, and models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to open-access datasets and cloud infrastructure
- Continued expertise of scientists who build, collect, and curate observational datasets
#### Constraints
- Access to compute, fast networks, and good mentorship remains uneven within and between countries, per the authors citing prior work

### Target Learners
- Graduate students and early-career professionals entering AI-augmented research teams

### Target Learning Goals
- Evaluating model and forecast output quality
- Recognizing bias and misinformation laundered through AI outputs
