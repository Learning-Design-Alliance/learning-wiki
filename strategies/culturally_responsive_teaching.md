# Culturally Responsive Teaching

## Description
Culturally responsive teaching involves engaging students in the learning process, both cognitively and emotionally, through an understanding of how their identity and perspective is shaped by their culture and community experiences. It encompasses different aspects of a person's identities, including race, language, gender, and ability, and uses this knowledge to promote equity and foster a sense of belonging for each student.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An understanding of diverse cultural frameworks, awareness of implicit bias, and a commitment to disrupting dominant narratives that have historically and systematically excluded students.
#### Constraints
- Requires a shift in mindset for both students and teachers to view struggles positively. Students may initially resist embracing challenges or feel discouraged by failures.

### Affordances
- Provides a lens through which educators can identify practices that promote equity and foster a sense of belonging for each student. Understanding different cultural frameworks can provide insight into how and where implicit bias shows up in classrooms and curricula.

### Personalization
- Instructors can create learning experiences that connect with student's backgrounds and cultural experiences and connect learning to their own experiences and future applications.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe educators' behavior in the classroom, their interactions with students, and their responses to issues of race and equity. Measure students' sense of belonging and their perceptions of fairness and inclusion.

## Impact
- Research suggests that culturally responsive teaching can improve student academic outcomes, increase engagement, and promote positive social-emotional development.
