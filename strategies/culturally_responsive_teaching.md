# Culturally Responsive Teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Culturally responsive teaching (CRT) uses students' cultural knowledge, prior experiences, and frames of reference as conduits for learning rather than obstacles to it. The teacher learns how students' identities — race, language, gender, ability, community — shape how they interpret instruction, then designs learning tasks, examples, and interactions that connect to those frames while building the independent cognitive capacity students need to succeed beyond the classroom (Gay, 2010; Hammond, 2015). Ladson-Billings (1995) framed the closely related construct of culturally relevant pedagogy as resting on three pillars: academic success, cultural competence, and sociopolitical consciousness.

## Design Implications

CRT is not primarily about engagement or climate; its strongest formulations treat culture as a processing resource — activating familiar knowledge structures so working memory is freed for new learning [Activation of prior knowledge improves learning outcomes.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]. Belonging itself is a lever for achievement: brief interventions that affirm students' social belonging produce lasting gains for marginalized students [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+S], and empathy-building practices improve intergroup attitudes in diverse classrooms [Building empathy improves intergroup attitudes.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Systematic knowledge of students' backgrounds, languages, and community practices gathered through [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md), family/community contact, and asset-based framing — not assumptions from demographic labels
- Instructional examples, texts, and problems that reflect students' experiences while still teaching the target content ([Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) drawn from familiar contexts, [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) set in recognizable communities)
- A stance of high expectations paired with intellectual scaffolding; CRT without rigor becomes "warm demanders" without the demands
- Teacher self-examination of implicit bias and of whose knowledge the curriculum treats as default

#### Constraints
- Surface-level implementation — swapping examples or celebrating heritage months without changing instructional interaction — shows no measurable academic benefit and can read as tokenism [~M]
- "Culture" treated as static group traits risks stereotyping; within-group variation typically exceeds between-group variation, so individual knowledge of students must override cultural generalizations [-M]
- Effects on achievement are mediated by instructional quality; CRT that lowers cognitive demand in the name of relevance reduces learning [~M]
- Teachers who lack deep content knowledge may use cultural connection as a substitute for explicit teaching rather than a supplement [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Information-processing orientation** (Hammond): identify students' "learning edges" and scaffold across them, treating culture as cognitive architecture
- **Critical orientation** (Ladson-Billings, Gay): center sociopolitical consciousness and critique of dominant narratives alongside academics
- **Asset/deficit framing in language**: shifting from intervention-speak ("at-risk") to asset-speak changes teacher expectations and student self-perception
- In higher education and online settings, CRT adapts through inclusive syllabus design, varied examples, and structured [Class Discussions](../elements/class-discussion.md) that distribute voice beyond dominant participants

### Target Learners
- Students from historically marginalized groups whose home knowledge is systematically unrepresented in standard curricula [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+S]
- Multilingual learners, for whom connecting new vocabulary and concepts to home-language knowledge reduces unnecessary cognitive load
- All students benefit from relevance, but effects are strongest where the default curriculum has signaled exclusion [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic content mastery achieved through, not despite, students' cultural frames
- Belonging and identity safety as preconditions for risk-taking and persistence [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+S]
- Sociopolitical consciousness: analyzing how power and inequity operate in content areas
- Independent learning capacity — the goal is scaffolding students toward self-directed learning, not permanent accommodation

### Instructions
1. **Learn the learners.** Gather asset data on students' experiences, languages, and interests through [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md), surveys, and community engagement before designing instruction.
2. **Audit the curriculum.** Identify where examples, texts, and problem contexts assume a default cultural frame; replace or supplement with [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) and [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) drawn from students' worlds.
3. **Activate and connect.** Open units by [Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md) so new content attaches to familiar structures [Activation of prior knowledge improves learning outcomes.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M].
4. **Scaffold the learning edge.** Pair high expectations with contingent support — release responsibility as competence grows [Contingent scaffolding improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M].
5. **Build belonging deliberately.** Use norm-setting, [Peer Discussions](../elements/peer-discussion.md), and feedback that communicates high standards plus belief in the student [Feedback is most effective at the task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
6. **Assess inclusively.** Use [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) formats that measure the target construct rather than cultural familiarity with the context.

## Examples
- **Zaretta Hammond's "Culturally Responsive Teaching and the Brain" (2015)** — widely used professional-learning framework organizing CRT around information processing and "warm demander" pedagogy.
- **The Algebra Project** (https://www.algebraproject.org) — Robert Moses' curriculum that grounds algebra in students' physical and community experiences, demonstrating CRT applied to rigorous mathematics.
- **Native American culturally based education programs** (e.g., the Sealaska Heritage "Voices on the Rampage" and language-nested schooling in Alaska and Hawai'i) — integrate Indigenous knowledge systems into standards-aligned instruction.
- **Responsive Classroom** (https://www.responsiveclassroom.org) — morning meeting and [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) structures that surface student experience as instructional input.
