# Clear Online Communication

## Description
Clear online communication means publishing, at the start of a course, which channels learners should use for which purposes (announcements, questions, private concerns), what response times they can expect, and how synchronous and asynchronous contact will be used. It is carried out through a structured LMS presence, a stated communication policy, and consistent instructor behavior that matches the stated norms. Timely, predictable instructor responses are a core component of [Teaching Presence](../principles/community-of-inquiry.md) and social presence in online environments [+S].

## Design Implications

Ambiguity about where and when to communicate imposes extraneous load and erodes learners' sense of instructor presence; published norms and rapid, predictable responses increase satisfaction and perceived learning in online courses [+S]. Communication design should reduce the number of decisions a learner must make to get help — one primary question channel, one escalation path, one announcements channel — consistent with [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md). Response quality matters as much as speed: feedback tied to the task and process supports learning more than generic praise [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A single, well-organized LMS course hub where resources, announcements, and the communication policy live
- A published communication policy: which tool for which purpose, expected response window (e.g., 24 business hours), and synchronous availability
- Instructor capacity to actually meet the stated response times — under-promising and over-delivering beats the reverse
- A visible Q&A channel (e.g., a discussion forum) where answers benefit all learners, not just the asker

#### Constraints
- Response-time promises that instructors cannot sustain during busy periods damage trust more than modest promises kept [-M]
- Over-reliance on private email fragments the conversation, produces duplicate questions, and hides common confusion from the instructor [-M]
- Asynchronous-only communication can stall learners who are stuck; without a synchronous or rapid-response option, frustration compounds [-W]
- High message volume without triage (FAQ, pinned answers) makes channels unusable and learners stop reading them [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- A "hotline" or office-hours channel for urgent issues, alongside slower asynchronous forums for non-urgent questions
- Weekly announcement videos or posts that anticipate common questions, reducing inbound volume
- Peer-supported Q&A forums (e.g., Piazza, Stack-style) where students answer each other, with instructor endorsement
- Team-taught or large courses can route questions through TAs with an instructor escalation path

### Target Learners
- Fully online and hybrid learners who cannot resolve ambiguity by raising a hand [+S]
- Novice learners in the discipline, who are least able to judge whether their question is "worth asking" and benefit most from explicit norms
- Learners with low academic self-confidence, for whom visible instructor responsiveness supports persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Learner satisfaction, belonging, and persistence rather than specific content objectives [+S]
- Self-regulation: knowing when and how to seek help is itself a learnable strategy
- Course logistics and task comprehension, where timely clarification prevents compounding confusion

### Instructions
1. Set up a central LMS hub with a syllabus, weekly modules, and a pinned communication policy before the course opens.
2. Publish the communication policy: channel-per-purpose table, response-time commitments, and netiquette expectations ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md)).
3. Create a public Q&A forum and model its use by answering the first questions there, redirecting email queries to it ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).
4. Respond within the stated window, using [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) that addresses the task and process, not just the answer [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
5. Use brief [Check-Ins](../principles/check-ins.md) or early-week polls to surface confusion proactively rather than waiting for questions.
6. Maintain a growing FAQ or pinned answers so recurring questions are self-served ([Practice](../elements/practice.md) forums with instructor endorsement).

## Examples
- **Piazza** (https://piazza.com) — Q&A platform built for courses; students can post anonymously, peers answer first, and instructors endorse correct answers, scaling responsiveness in large enrollments.
- **A published "communication contract"** — many online programs (e.g., Penn State's online faculty guidance, https://teaching.psu.edu) recommend stating a 24-hour response window and a single questions forum in the syllabus.
- **Weekly announcement video** — a 3–5 minute instructor video each Monday previewing the week and answering last week's common questions, a common practice in quality online courses aligned with the Community of Inquiry framework.
