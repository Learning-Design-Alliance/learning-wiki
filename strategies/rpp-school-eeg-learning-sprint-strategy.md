# Research-practice partnership with an in-school EEG recording studio enables causal classroom learning-sprint studies embedded in the school day

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study was conducted through an enduring research-practice partnership between a university group and an independent transitional kindergarten-8th grade school with a permanent EEG recording studio. Researchers and teachers co-designed the study over nearly a year; during the two-week sprint teachers led ~15 min per school day of activities focused on phonemic awareness, phonics, and writing, using display boards, flashcards, spelling games, and Kahoot quizzes organic to classroom practices. The design randomized word-to-class assignment while reserving other words as controls, positioning the study to investigate a causal link between specific learning activities and neural changes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An existing research-practice partnership with the school, co-design time with teachers (nearly a year), and a dedicated EEG laboratory inside the school
#### Constraints
- The partner school is an independent, non-profit institution with a student-to-teacher ratio smaller than average public schools; the authors argue the instructional dynamics are nonetheless representative of effective, generalizable approaches

### Target Learners
- elementary school students in first and second grade

### Target Learning Goals
- new vocabulary acquisition and neural representation of novel written words
