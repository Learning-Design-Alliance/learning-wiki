# Institutional debiasing: give teachers time to reflect on biases, reduce cognitive load and provide clear evaluation rubrics, and use values affirmation to lower defensiveness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's recommendations for educational institutions and researchers target the conditions under which biased judgements arise. Teachers and trainees "should be given the time and support to explore their attitudes, beliefs and biases towards marginalized groups"; schools should "debias the learning environment" because biased judgements are more likely under high cognitive load and without a clear evaluation rubric; and values affirmation is proposed as a future-research avenue to make teachers less defensive when confronted with evidence of their own bias. The strategy treats bias reduction as a joint individual-and-institutional project rather than an individual failing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both the individual teacher and the institution must be invested in the debiasing process, per the article's second recommendation.
#### Constraints
- The article notes teacher attitudes and biases may be somewhat resistant to change, and that teachers' responses to confronting personal bias may be defensive in nature.

### Target Learners
- in-service and trainee teachers in primary and secondary education

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable assessment and grading of students from lower-SES and ethnic minority backgrounds
