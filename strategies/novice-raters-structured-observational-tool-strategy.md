# Employ novice raters with a structured observational tool such as TEAM when expert raters are unavailable

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that clinical studies and real-life teams employ less clinically experienced raters, such as residents, using a structured observational tool like TEAM to inform performance review and improvement, because experts are often unavailable due to clinical obligations. Novices with rater training produced ratings similarly distributed to experts', with "a consistent, but slightly more lenient rating behaviour."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rater training covering the instrument, common rating errors, and frame-of-reference training
- A structured observational tool with known psychometric properties
#### Constraints
- Novices rate slightly more leniently, especially on the global rating scale, so raw scores may not be directly comparable to expert ratings

### Target Learners
- medical emergency teams
- simulation course participants

### Target Learning Goals
- teamwork performance assessment and debriefing

## Claims

- [The German translation of TEAM shows psychometric properties comparable to the English and French versions, with high internal consistency and a single dominant component](../claims/german-team-psychometrics-validated.md) [+W]
- [Inter-rater reliability between novice and expert TEAM raters is moderate (ICC = 0.66), with consistent identification of best- and worst-performing teams in 75% of cases](../claims/novice-expert-icc-moderate-agreement.md) [+W]
- [Novice and expert raters show no significant difference on 7 of 11 TEAM items and on the TEAM sum score, but novices rate higher on 4 items and on the global rating scale](../claims/novice-expert-team-ratings-mostly-comparable.md) [+W]
- [Rater status accounts for 11.1% of variance in TEAM global rating scores, while the case-by-team interaction accounts for 43.2%, the largest share](../claims/rater-status-and-case-team-variance-components.md) [~W]
- [Team performance in simulated emergencies is highly case-specific: teams varied considerably across cases, with no superb or incapable teams per se](../claims/team-performance-context-specificity-simulation.md) [~W]
