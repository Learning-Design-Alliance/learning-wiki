# Support faculty adoption of active learning with peer communities, framing, incentives, and iterative refinement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends strategies to sustain EBAE implementation: addressing student misconceptions about learning by framing the instructional design at the beginning of the term, providing incentives for engagement, and building faculty support structures. The authors observe that "The faculty members who persist are usually the ones who get support from their peers (e.g., developing faculty learning communiti es, working with instructional designers at local teaching and learning centers)". They also recommend treating effective teaching as an iterative pursuit, refining materials and implementation after each use using feedback from students and TAs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need peer support structures such as faculty learning communities or teaching and learning center feedback to persist with EBAE strategies
#### Constraints
- Implementation difficulties may be specific to a particular university even if an EBAE approach was effective elsewhere; large class sizes and content coverage pressures are stated impediments

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large-enrollment introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained implementation of evidence-based active engagement instruction
