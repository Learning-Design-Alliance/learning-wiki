# Avoid simplistic good/bad food narratives and weight-control-centered framing in school nutrition education to protect psychosocial well-being

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review cautions that health promotion messaging can backfire: "Simplistic narratives that frame foods as inherently “good” or “bad,” or that emphasize weight control as a primary goal, may inadvertently encourage dietary restriction, anxiety around eating, or stigmatizing attitudes toward body size". It argues the broader educational discourse warrants critical examination so that nutritional guidance supports both physical and psychosocial well-being. Programs aiming at food literacy and empowerment should therefore frame messaging around autonomy and long-term healthy food relationships rather than weight control.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must frame nutritional guidance to support both physical and psychosocial well-being, per the review's stated goal of healthy, autonomous food relationships
#### Constraints
- The review presents this as a critical examination of discourse in childhood health promotion; it offers no empirical test of the proposed harms in this review's own data

### Target Learners
- children and adolescents in school settings

### Target Learning Goals
- healthy, autonomous food relationships and psychosocial well-being alongside nutrition knowledge
