Investigate the quality of learning acquired through the internet in future studies¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that future studies investigate the quality/performance of learning acquired through the internet by informal caregivers. It states: "For future studies, it is suggested to investigate the quality of learning acquired through the Internet." This addresses the gap that current evidence on caregiver self-directed learning via mobile health is largely limited to access, willingness, and preference data rather than learning outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Future studies should measure the quality or performance of learning acquired through the internet

Constraints¶

  • Current included studies were all observational and cross-sectional

Target Learners¶

  • Informal caregivers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Health education
  • Self-directed learning

Claims¶

  • Caregivers want information about the disease, specific patient care, and how to be a caregiver [+W]
  • Smartphone access among informal caregivers ranges from 61% to 99% depending on country [+W]
  • Informal caregivers tend to seek more health information on the internet than non-caregivers [+W]
  • Internet access and trust in information are the main barriers to caregiver self-directed learning via mobile health [+W]
  • Caregivers with low confidence in their own health are more likely to seek health information online [+W]
  • Knowledge and self-efficacy in COVID-19 caregiving increased after a self-learning software intervention [+W]
  • Being a woman, married, higher education, stable employment, and high income are associated with seeking health information online [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Develop mobile health applications that account for caregiver age and technology familiarity
