# Future research should measure both explicit stereotypes and implicit bias toward students with learning problems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the discussion, the authors recommend that future studies combine explicit stereotype measures with implicit bias measures, because explicit self-report may be distorted by socially desirable responding and implicit biases operate with limited awareness. They note teachers' implicit biases have mostly been studied for ethnicity rather than special educational needs. This strategy targets more holistic measurement of the label–stereotype connection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Measurement instruments capable of capturing automatic associations, since explicit ratings alone may reflect socially desirable answers
#### Constraints
- The article notes implicit beliefs can be challenging to measure and may not manifest in behavior

### Target Learners
- in-service general and special education teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate assessment of teachers' stereotype-based evaluations of students with learning problems

### Affordances
- Labeling Stereotype Expectation Framework
