# Inducing incremental theories through experimental priming and longer-term interventions to change self-regulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q4` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article reports that implicit theories, though often dispositional, can be changed: scholars have "primed implicit theories" and "temporarily changed them in both one-shot laboratory experiments (e.g., Spray, Wang, Biddle, Chatzisarantis, & Warburton, 2006) and longer-term interventions (e.g., Blackwell, Trzesniewski, & Dweck, 2007; [Burnette & Finkel, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0029531))." This supports designing interventions that lead learners to believe attributes are malleable, thereby shifting goal setting, operating, and monitoring toward mastery-oriented patterns.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must target beliefs about the malleability of the specific attribute in the relevant domain, since people can hold different theories in different domains.
#### Constraints
- The article notes implicit theories exhibit some degree of day-to-day and moment-to-moment fluctuation, and effects are stronger under ego threat.

### Target Learners
- Students and adults across achievement domains (ages 5-42 in the reviewed samples)

### Target Learning Goals
- Adaptive self-regulation: learning goals, mastery-oriented strategies, and optimistic expectations

## Claims

- [Effects of implicit theories on goal setting and goal operating are stronger in the presence (vs. absence) of ego threats such as failure feedback](../claims/ego-threat-moderates-mindset-selfregulation-links.md) [+M]

- [Incremental theories significantly predict goal monitoring: negatively with negative emotions (r = -.233) and positively with expectations (r = .157)](../claims/incremental-theories-predict-goal-monitoring.md) [+M]
- [Incremental theories significantly predict goal operating: negatively with helpless-oriented strategies (r = -.238) and positively with mastery-oriented strategies (r = .227)](../claims/incremental-theories-predict-goal-operating.md) [+M]
- [Incremental (vs. entity) theories significantly predict goal setting: negatively with performance goals (r = -.151) and positively with learning goals (r = .187)](../claims/incremental-theories-predict-goal-setting.md) [+M]
