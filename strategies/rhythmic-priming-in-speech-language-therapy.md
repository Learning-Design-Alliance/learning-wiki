# Use regular rhythmic priming during speech-language therapy sessions to boost syntactic processing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using short regular rhythmic stimulation immediately before syntactic tasks in clinical sessions: "using rhythmic priming during clinical sessions may help to enhance the learning of syntax and performance on syntactic tasks, which may then transfer outside of the session". Because sentence repetition is a central area of difficulty in DLD and regular primes improved repetition for both groups, the authors present regular rhythms as a promising tool to boost syntactic processing during therapy, citing prior long-term training work with hearing-loss populations. Digit span did not influence the effect, supporting use across cognitive profiles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rhythmic primes must be temporally regular (steady beat) rather than irregular
- Applied before or during grammar-focused tasks such as sentence repetition
#### Constraints
- The study was a within-subjects experiment, not a randomized clinical trial; efficacy for other difficulty areas such as morphological markers merits further research, as do individual differences in who benefits most

### Target Learners
- Children with developmental language disorder in speech-language therapy

### Target Learning Goals
- Complex syntax processing and sentence repetition

### Affordances
- Shared Rhythm Grammar Processing Mechanisms
