# Progress game difficulty systematically across repeated sessions (Easy, then Medium, then Difficult) over a multi-week intervention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the GEA evaluation, the games' difficulty was raised week by week: "In the first week of the study, all games were set to the ”Easy” difficulty level. The games were adjusted to a ”Medium” difficulty level during the second week. In the third week, the games were more challenging at the ”Difficult” level." An additional fourth week accommodated participants who were absent or started late. Sessions lasted approximately 20 minutes in quiet, minimally furnished rooms at the therapeutic centers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Games must support multiple difficulty levels (GEA's Find the Allergens used 4, 6, or 10 allergen icons for its three levels)
#### Constraints
- The article tested this progression only over a three-week period and notes a longer period might have yielded greater outcomes

### Target Learners
- individuals with neurodevelopmental disorders in therapeutic or daycare settings

### Target Learning Goals
- food and nutrition knowledge
