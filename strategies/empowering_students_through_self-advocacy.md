# Empowering Students Through Self-Advocacy

## Description
Empowering students to advocate for themselves involves teaching them about their rights and needs, and providing them with opportunities to communicate and act on that understanding. This approach promotes student agency, allowing them to become more engaged and effective in their education. Strategies include developing self-advocacy and self-determination skills, which have been shown to improve educational and life outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit instruction in self-advocacy and self-determination skills, opportunities for students to practice these skills in real-world settings, and a supportive environment where students feel comfortable communicating their needs.
#### Constraints
- Requires time and effort from both students and educators to cultivate self-awareness and effective communication skills. Students may initially resist taking responsibility for their learning or feel uncomfortable sharing their needs.

### Affordances
- Enhances students' ability to recognize their strengths and needs, encourages them to take responsibility for their learning, and prepares them for success in various environments by promoting independence and problem-solving skills.

### Personalization
- IEPs and transition meetings can become more student-centered, and students can be supported in participating in real-world learning opportunities where they can practice self-advocacy skills.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe students' willingness to participate in discussions, request help when needed, and articulate their learning preferences. Measure students' ability to set goals, solve problems, and advocate for themselves.

## Impact
- Research indicates that self-advocacy skills lead to improved academic outcomes and success in post-secondary education, the workforce, and civic life. Students who self-advocate are better equipped to navigate challenges and make informed decisions about their learning and lives.
