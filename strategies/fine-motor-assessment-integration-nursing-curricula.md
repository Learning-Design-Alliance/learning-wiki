# Integrate fine motor skill assessments into nursing curricula and provide targeted skills laboratory support for students with low dexterity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that nursing curricula incorporate fine motor skill assessments as a standard component of student evaluation, for example "during entrance examinations, in early laboratory‐based training, or as part of ongoing competency‐based assessments". Educators should apply hand skill tests starting from basic nursing courses, identify students with low fine motor skills, organise targeted skills laboratory sessions for them, and monitor their progress individually over time using reliable tools such as the Purdue Pegboard Test.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reliable measurement tools such as the Purdue Pegboard Test for objective tracking of student development
#### Constraints
- The article states hand function is only one of several factors influencing clinical competence, alongside cognitive, emotional, and instructional variables

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate nursing students, especially those identified with low fine motor skills

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of basic nursing psychomotor skills and clinical readiness
