# Map each step of a traditional food-making process to a physics concept and a curriculum outcome before designing the experiments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article first thematizes the traditional process via qualitative interviews and direct observations with two Gudeg producers, then builds a matrix (Table 1) linking each cooking step — peeling, chopping, mixing, boiling, preserving — to a collaborative project-based activity and its main physical concepts. It further maps the activities to learning outcomes mandated by the Indonesian national curriculum across Phase E (Grade 10) and Phase F (Grades 11-12) in Table 2. This mapping ensures the cultural context supports required content rather than replacing it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to local practitioners whose expertise connects the traditional process to the physics concepts
- Curriculum outcome statements to anchor each activity to mandated standards
#### Constraints
- The mapping was constructed for the Indonesian national physics curriculum using Gudeg; the authors state the approach "can be generally adopted beyond physics" but the specific matrix is Gudeg-specific

### Target Learners
- high school physics students (Grades 10-12)

### Target Learning Goals
- density, Archimedes' principle, Young's modulus, torque, viscosity, thermodynamics, and heat transfer
