# Establish Group Roles

## Description
Establishing group roles means assigning each member of a collaborative group a distinct, named responsibility — such as facilitator, recorder, timekeeper, or skeptic — that structures how the group works. Roles create positive interdependence: the group cannot succeed unless each member performs their function. Roles may be assigned by the instructor, negotiated by the group, or rotated across sessions so every learner practices each function.

## Design Implications

Role assignment converts loose cooperation into structured collaboration, which is what distinguishes effective cooperative learning from mere groupwork [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves achievement.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S]. Roles work because they make each member's contribution visible and non-substitutable, preventing the free-riding and status hierarchies that undermine small-group outcomes [Small-group learning improves STEM achievement.](../claims/small-group-learning-improves-stem-achievement.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Roles that are genuinely interdependent — each one necessary for task completion, not decorative
- Brief role descriptions learners can understand and enact (one or two sentences each)
- A task complex enough that multiple roles are meaningful ([Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md))
- Rotation or reassignment over time so roles do not fossilize into ability tracking

#### Constraints
- Roles assigned along perceived ability lines reproduce status hierarchies and depress participation from lower-status students [-M] — Cohen's work on groupwork shows unequal status participation reduces learning gains unless roles deliberately equalize interaction
- Roles that partition the task so members work in isolation (e.g., "you do questions 1–3") eliminate the discussion that drives collaborative learning gains [-S]
- Overly scripted roles can suppress emergent reasoning; if the recorder merely transcribes and the facilitator merely reads an agenda, cognitive engagement drops [~M]
- For simple or short tasks, role overhead exceeds its benefit [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Instructor-assigned** roles maximize structure for novice collaborators
- **Student-negotiated** roles build ownership and work better with experienced groups
- **Rotating** roles across sessions develops a fuller repertoire of collaborative skills
- **Complex-instruction roles** (Cohen's approach) assign open-ended, intellectually demanding roles to low-status students specifically to equalize participation

### Target Learners
- Novice collaborators who lack norms for productive group interaction [+M]
- Mixed-ability groups where status differences would otherwise silence some members [Small-group learning improves STEM achievement.](../claims/small-group-learning-improves-stem-achievement.md) [+S]
- Learners whose confidence is fragile — a defined role gives a legitimate, protected voice in the group, supporting participation [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Collaborative and communication skills: turn-taking, facilitation, constructive challenge
- Individual accountability within interdependent work
- Domain learning in tasks where discussion and elaboration drive understanding

### Instructions
1. Design a task that genuinely requires multiple contributions; roles bolted onto a solo-able task are theater.
2. Define 3–5 roles with one-sentence descriptions (e.g., facilitator, recorder, checker, skeptic, timekeeper).
3. Assign roles deliberately, countering perceived status hierarchies rather than following them (see [Assigned Positions](../elements/assigned-positions.md)).
4. Brief the group on each role's function before work begins, and post role descriptions visibly.
5. Monitor and intervene when roles are ignored or dominated; use a [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) mid-task to surface participation problems.
6. Rotate roles across sessions and debrief what each role contributed (see [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).

## Related Strategies
- [Jigsaw](../strategies/jigsaw.md) — an extreme form of role structure where each member owns unique material and must teach it
- [Think-Pair-Share](../patterns/think-pair-share.md) — a lightweight, implicit role structure for pairs
- [Establish Group Norms](../strategies/establish-group-norms.md) — roles define *who does what*; norms define *how members treat each other*

## Examples
- **Complex Instruction (Cohen & Lotan, Stanford)** — role structures (facilitator, recorder/reporter, materials manager, understanding checker) combined with status treatments to equalize participation in heterogeneous classrooms. See https://cgi.stanford.edu/group/pci/cgi-bin/site.cgi
- **PBL groups in medical education** — tutorial groups commonly assign a chair and a scribe per case session, rotating weekly, to structure discussion in problem-based learning curricula such as Maastricht University's.
- **Kagan Structures** — published cooperative-learning structures (e.g., "Numbered Heads Together") that embed role-like interdependence into routine classroom activities. See https://www.kaganonline.com
