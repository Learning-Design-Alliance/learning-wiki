# Structure at-home laparoscopic training as deliberate practice with objective metrics, feedback and proficiency benchmarks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a preclinical at-home training curriculum in which residents train basic laparoscopic tasks autonomously until expert-set proficiency levels are reached. It states that "Principles of deliberate practice were applied by making use of goal-oriented training, objective performance metrics, and structural feedback", with trainees instructed to train approximately 4–5 days per week with a minimum of 15 min per training. The authors conclude this curriculum should be part of regular surgical residency training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mobile box trainer with force, motion and time tracking; expert-set proficiency thresholds per task; online video instructions; an online database logging performance and video; trainee motivation to train autonomously at home.
#### Constraints
- Assessment scheduling depended on residents' and trainers' OR schedules, so training timespans varied; the pilot involved only six novices and a power analysis indicated 13 participants for significance in all comparisons.

### Target Learners
- First-year surgical residents with no or limited prior laparoscopic experience

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition of fundamental laparoscopic tissue manipulation and instrument handling skills to expert-set proficiency before operating-room practice
