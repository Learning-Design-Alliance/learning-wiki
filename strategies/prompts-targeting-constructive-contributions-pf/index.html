Design collaboration prompts that specifically target students' constructive contributions in PF for young learners¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that dyads with more constructive utterances generated more solution ideas, the authors recommend that future PF studies with young students go beyond the typical motivational prompts. They "suggest to design prompts that specifically target students' constructive contributions" — utterances such as posing questions, generating analogies, or drawing inferences that go beyond the information presented in the original problem. Such dialogs are expected to increase the solution ideas, and thus learning opportunities, students encounter during problem solving.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must already be able to engage in learning-centered dialog with peers; younger students may need additional practice before benefiting from collaborative PF

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is grounded in exploratory correlational process analyses, not an experimental test of the prompts

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students (fifth graders, aged 10–12)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • generating multiple solution ideas during problem solving prior to instruction
  • conceptual knowledge acquisition

Claims¶

  • In young students' collaborative PF, dyads that produced more constructive utterances generated more solution ideas [+M]
  • Students' relative frequencies of interactive, constructive, and active utterances during collaborative PF correlated positively with their conceptual knowledge posttest scores [+W]
  • Constructive behaviors are defined by generating outputs containing new ideas that go beyond the information given [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Combine scripting with awareness measures to tailor collaboration scripts to the dyad
