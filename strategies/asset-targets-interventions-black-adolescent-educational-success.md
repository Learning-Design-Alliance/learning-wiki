# Target self-esteem, school bonding, and educational expectations in programs fostering better educational experiences for Black adolescents

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "Mediation findings suggest further exploration of self-esteem, school bonding, and educational expectations as potential intervention targets for programs and services designed to foster better educational experiences among Black adolescents". It further proposes multi-tiered, multi-level approaches: staff training on structural racism and implicit bias, culturally responsive discipline (e.g., the Double Check coaching model), student involvement in school and classroom decision-making, and classroom interventions fostering student–teacher relationships.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multi-tiered and multi-level approaches, since discriminatory cultural ecologies can inhibit high expectations and positive connections to school
#### Constraints
- School-level bonding interventions should consider a multi-level approach because structural inequities and under-resourced schools may hinder bonding for youth of color

### Target Learners
- Black adolescents in secondary school settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved grades, reduced grade repetition and exclusionary discipline, stronger school bonding and educational expectations
