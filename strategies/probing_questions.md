# Probing Questions

## Description
Probing questions are used to encourage deeper reflection and critical thinking by challenging assumptions, exploring reasons and evidence, considering different viewpoints, examining implications, and questioning the basis of the inquiry itself. R.W. Paul's six types of Socratic questions include: Questions for clarification, Questions that probe assumptions, Questions that probe reasons and evidence, Questions about Viewpoints and Perspectives, Questions that probe implications and consequences, and Questions about the question. Probing questions help learners to explore material more deeply by making connections, summarizing, and assessing understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a facilitator to guide the discussion. May require learners to have some background knowledge on the topic.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging for learners who are not comfortable with open-ended questioning. Effectiveness depends on the facilitator's ability to guide discussion.
