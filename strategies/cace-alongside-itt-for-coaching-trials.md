# Use CACE analysis alongside ITT to estimate effects of teacher coaching accounting for implementation compliance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends supplementing intent-to-treat estimates with complier average causal effect estimation, because ITT estimates the effects of being assigned to treatment regardless of compliance and "may underestimate the effect of the intervention." CACE identifies compliers and noncompliers in both arms via a mixture modeling framework using compliance data and predictors of compliance. The authors note CACE "have not been applied to teacher coaching interventions on TSI" before this study, making the dual-analysis approach a methodological contribution for coaching trials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A compliance marker (here combining dosage and fidelity), predictors of compliance, and CACE assumptions including random assignment, monotonicity, no contamination, and the exclusion restriction
#### Constraints
- The exclusion restriction assumption (zero effect for noncompliers) is less likely in social–behavioral interventions; the authors ran sensitivity analyses (NACE) relaxing it

### Target Learners
- upper secondary school teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- unbiased estimation of coaching intervention effects for policy and practice decisions
