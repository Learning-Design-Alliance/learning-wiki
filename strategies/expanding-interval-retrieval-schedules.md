# Use expanding-interval retrieval schedules for long-term retention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The reminding view implies that because reminding both enhances memory and retards forgetting, retrieval schedules with expanding intervals should support better long-term retention than constant-interval schedules. The authors state this implication appears to be true, citing Landauer and Bjork (1978), while noting recent research has questioned its generality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schedules must balance the costs and benefits of forgetting: too much forgetting makes reminding unlikely, too little makes it impotent
#### Constraints
- Recent research ([Karpicke & Roediger, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.33.4.704); Logan & Balota, 2008) has called the generality of the expanding-interval advantage into question
