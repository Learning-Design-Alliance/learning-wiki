# Replace or supplement essay assessment with interactive oral assessments (viva voces) where writing is not the skill being assessed

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A strategy of moving away from asynchronous essays as proxies for other forms of learning, using oral assessments or professional discussions instead when the goal is to certify applied capability, since the viva is already used to investigate suspected ChatGPT use. The article argues that if we want to assess the ability of students to become biomedical scientists, we should focus more on their ability to do biomedical science rather than write about it, and notes vivas are often more authentic assessments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- If a viva is used, it must itself be a meaningful assessment of the learning being certified
#### Constraints
- New GenAI tools appear to be the final straw for the validity of asynchronous essays unless academic writing is the specific skill being assessed, e.g. dissertations and research papers, and even then challenges remain

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and postgraduate biomedical science students
- candidates for professional registration portfolios

### Target Learning Goals
- authentic assessment of applied knowledge, critical argument and laboratory competence
