# Confront science denialism by inviting the general public through institutional social media to collectively replicate a classic experiment, supported by a messaging group, a remote preparatory class and an electronic data form

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes replication of scientific experiments with non-specialist public participation as a strategy against science denialism. UFLA's social media invited the public to reproduce Eratosthenes' experiment; a messaging group delivered orientation, a remote class discussed the scientific reasoning, and an electronic form collected technical data and impressions of the experimental experience. The authors evaluate that "empreendimentos como este podem ser um recurso importante para o enfrentamento ao negacionismo da ciência".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional social media channels for the call, a messaging group for detailed orientation, and a preparatory remote class explaining the procedure, as the article describes
#### Constraints
- Completion skewed toward people involved in formal teaching despite broad outreach; weather forced extension of measurement days; the authors note some participants found the experiment alone insufficient to demonstrate curvature

### Target Learners
- general public, including teachers, students and families

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding how the scientific method observes reality through methodical procedures
- critical interpretation of false information such as flat-Earth claims
