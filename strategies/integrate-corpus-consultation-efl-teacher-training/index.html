Integrate corpus consultation into EFL teacher training and collocation teaching¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (5 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teacher trainers integrate corpus consultation into the teaching process and that vocabulary teaching materials be designed in line with DDL approaches. Instead of memorizing collocations, "students can see authentic examples by referencing the corpus". The authors also suggest compiling a learner corpus to detect students' collocation problems and studying the problematic ones via corpus tools, and using the paper's activity and material design processes in foreign language classrooms.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers should be confident about using technological tools and gain a thorough understanding of corpus analysis, since their own insecurity may negatively influence classroom application (Breyer, 2008)

Constraints¶

  • The study's corpus-based instruction is positioned as a supplementary method for teaching collocations, not a replacement for other approaches

Claims¶

  • The corpus-tool production advantage is bounded: findings are limited to verb-noun collocations in one Turkish ELT setting [~W]
  • Corpus-tool instruction significantly improves EFL student teachers' production of verb-noun collocations in writing, with a medium-to-large effect [+W]
  • Corpus-tool instruction produces no statistically significant advantage over traditional instruction on EFL student teachers' verb-noun collocation knowledge [-W]
  • EFL student teachers hold positive views of concordancing for collocation learning and most intend future use [+W]
  • Corpus-based collocation findings align with prior concordancing and data-driven learning studies [+W]
  • Corpus-based instruction using COCA concordance lines improved EFL students' verb-noun collocation recognition more than traditional instruction [+W]
  • The experimental group's collocation scores improved significantly from pretest to posttest after corpus-based instruction [+W]
