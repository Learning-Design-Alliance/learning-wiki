Engage students in active learning assignments using generative AI with structured reflection and critique of AI outputs¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The review recommends that educators craft assignments incorporating generative AI that require students to critique AI-generated text and reflect on what the AI did well or poorly, rather than banning the tools. Across practices, "a common thread is structured reflection": debrief questions or discussions where students articulate the AI's performance and how they arrived at their final answer. This treats AI as a partner to question and improve upon, illuminating its affordances and limitations and improving students' evaluative skills.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors must guide use and include debrief questions or discussions where students articulate what the AI did well or poorly

Constraints¶

  • The review warns that without guidance students may over-rely on AI, so instructional design should phase AI support or require students to attempt tasks themselves before consulting AI

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students across disciplines, including first-year writing and STEM students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • AI literacy
  • critical evaluation of AI outputs
  • feedback literacy

Related Strategies¶

  • Resistance Reading and Counter-Narratives
  • Require students to justify how they used AI output: what they accepted, rejected, revised, or verified, and why
  • Support learners to evaluate AI outputs by verifying sources, reporting errors, and overriding recommendations
  • Computing instructors should approach GenAI slide generation as programmers: work iteratively with coding assistants and text-based slide toolchains
  • Use shared metacognition as the routine practice for educators and learners to critically evaluate AI-generated outputs
  • Embed AI as a learning partner and redesign assessments for AI-rich contexts
