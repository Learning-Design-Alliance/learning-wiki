# Introduce a third-party observer when teaching the Einstein equivalence principle to prevent a global acceleration-gravity equivalence conclusion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
When covering the Einstein equivalence principle, the authors suggest comparing, via a third observer, a person stationary in a gravitational field with a person accelerating in free space: "The third observer would prevent students from concluding that there is a global equivalence between acceleration and gravity". The third party measures a difference, since the gravitational time-dilation rate is constant while γv increases with time for the accelerating person. This preserves the local nature of the EEP, which some textbooks violate by drawing the light source outside the accelerating container.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have been introduced to the EEP and to time dilation in both gravitational and accelerated settings
#### Constraints
- The comparison addresses the local EEP; acceleration is not globally equivalent to gravity

### Target Learners
- sophomore-level modern physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the local (not global) nature of the equivalence principle
