# Integrate online interactive simulation exercises into online, hybrid, and flipped-classroom curricular materials

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends leveraging the demonstrated advantages of interactive simulation by integrating such exercises into curricular materials, especially for online courses where direct instructor interaction is limited. It states that an online simulation model such as WILSIM-GC "lends itself naturally to the increasingly common practice of the ''ﬂipped classroom'' approach", in which lecturing is replaced with interactive in-class activities and learning happens online outside class. This follows from the simulation's favorable learning and attitudinal results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A freely accessible, Web-based simulation that students can run outside class on their own computers
#### Constraints
- The study's evidence base is a small, nonrandomized quasiexperiment in one 100-level course, which the authors call preliminary

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in online, hybrid, or flipped geoscience courses

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding long-term geological processes such as landform evolution
