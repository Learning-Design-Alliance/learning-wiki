# Structured three-day intensive suturing course progressing from daily single-knot practice to a final anastomosis task

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course design combined a daily one-and-a-half to two-hour theoretical session (ergonomics, instrumentation, suturing techniques, simulation methodologies) with a five-hour practical session each day. Training commenced with daily single surgical knot practice and progressed to executing an end-to-end anastomosis between Penrose drain segments on the final day, with intermediate tasks in thread manipulation, rubber band handling, cone placement and suturing under tension. The authors note complex exercises "can foster forward planning abilities, non-technical skills and movement economy among trainees".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Detailed procedural explanations and demonstration videos before exercises
- Continuous researcher supervision of exercise correctness
- Standardized module setups (identical starting needle positions, predefined thread lengths, standardized target locations) to mitigate variance across attempts
#### Constraints
- The authors state the simplified Penrose-drain model did not precisely replicate conditions of EA/TEF repair in newborns, and a more accurate model may transfer skills to the operating theatre more reliably
- The three-day course did not elucidate an individual's point on the learning curve or the time required to achieve proficiency

### Target Learners
- surgeons and residents across operative specialties with some MIS experience but no thoracoscopic esophageal anastomosis experience

### Target Learning Goals
- endoscopic suturing and sliding-knot technique
- performing an end-to-end anastomosis in a constrained space
