Use keyword-only subtitles instead of full-sentence subtitles when targeting vocabulary learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

For further research and materials design, the article recommends incorporating "only the keywords as subtitles" because whole subtitled sentences make it difficult for learners to follow the flow of speech, as they concentrate on either the speech or the subtitles. Including only target words as keywords is proposed as a way to bring measurement of vocabulary gains closer to the goal.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Subtitles should be limited to the target keywords rather than full sentences

Constraints¶

  • The article states this as an implication for further research, not a tested manipulation in this study

Target Learners¶

  • upper-intermediate foreign-language learners watching video materials

Target Learning Goals¶

  • incidental vocabulary learning from audiovisual input

Claims¶

  • Watching cartoons with English subtitles produces no statistically significant vocabulary-gain advantage over watching without subtitles among upper-intermediate ELT students [+W]
  • Vocabulary improvement occurred incidentally regardless of subtitles, attributed to target words being contextualized in the cartoons [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use pre-task subtitled videos of similar (not identical) tasks to build familiarity while preserving cognitive complexity
