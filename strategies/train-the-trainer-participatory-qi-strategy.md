# Use a train-the-trainer faculty development model with rapid-cycle quality improvement and participatory design to implement a longitudinal teamwork curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementation strategy pairs a train-the-trainer faculty development sequence with iterative, user-centered curriculum improvement. Faculty preparation included "viewing an instructor guide with a video demonstration", preparation sessions, instructing the activity, debriefing, and an instructor survey applied consistently to all instructors. Reaction data from learners and instructors are analyzed after each module and used for data-driven modifications such as simplifying guides, adjusting activity duration, and adding quarterly reports.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A project guidance team mentoring faculty, with team scientists serving as content experts
- Resource investments including a core group of medical educators and coordinators, dean-level support and oversight, and commitment of training resources such as time and money
#### Constraints
- The 1:2–8 instructor-to-learner ratio requires a considerable number of instructors, which may impact generalizability
- Differences in institutional culture, student demographics, and interprofessional expectations may also limit generalizability

### Target Learners
- medical students in pre-clerkship, clerkship, and post-clerkship phases

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational teamwork competencies including structured communication, closed-loop communication, clarifying questions, team mental models, psychological safety, and entrustability for EPA 8 patient handovers
