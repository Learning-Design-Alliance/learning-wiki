Design for influence, not simply participation, and scaffold without replacing partner leadership¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report's lessons for the field urge practitioners to go beyond representation: "Inviting people to meetings, gathering feedback, or including representatives on a team does not necessarily create shared leadership." Facilitators should provide structure, modeling, and coordination while "gradually expanding partners' ability to lead," since when facilitators carry too much of the process the work may continue without strengthening collective ownership. Programs should be designed as learning experiences allowing participants to assume increasing responsibility over time.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Practitioners should be explicit about which decisions participants can influence and create meaningful roles in defining problems, interpreting evidence, designing and implementing solutions, and assessing progress

Constraints¶

  • Outside facilitators may need to provide structure, modeling, coordination, and direct support particularly when partners face limited staffing, time, or prior experience

Target Learners¶

  • Students, families, educators, and community members engaged as co-leaders
  • Researchers, designers, and facilitators of participatory work

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Shared leadership and decision-making in co-research and co-design
  • Capacity to sustain participatory R&D without permanent dependence on outside facilitation

Affordances¶

  • Collaborative Innovation Core Tenets

Related Strategies¶

  • Supportive practices for inclusive youth co-design: reach beyond usual voices, respect time, ensure accessibility, and compensate fairly
  • Design participation deliberately: align entry points with partners' actual readiness rather than assuming a uniform baseline
  • Assess team readiness across leadership, facilitation, communication, shared understanding, and capacity before starting
  • Form a small cross-departmental team with defined facilitation roles before starting alignment work
