# Structure and incentivize equitable collaboration in lab groups: rotating roles, regular re-grouping, individual tinkering time, and scaffolded task division with TA buy-in

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors describe five approaches they have started to implement to address inequitable task division: regularly changing group composition, assigning and rotating roles such as 'experimenter' and 'recorder' with grade-incentivized checkpoints, avoiding isolated minorities in groups, building in individual tinkering time at the start of lab sessions, and explicitly dividing learning tasks between partners. For structured tasks, "Student A is assigned to develop the theoretical prediction while Student B carries out the measurement before they share, and switch roles for the next part of the experiment."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Professional development to establish TA buy-in, since the authors see little or no impact from these strategies when TAs do not believe in their necessity and benefit; grade incentives such as checkpoints to ensure role fulfilment
#### Constraints
- Rotating recorder and experimenter roles meant recorders had no opportunity to develop practical skills with the apparatus for that lab, and some recorders took over parts of the experimentation role when partners struggled; the approaches are described as promising but not yet systematically evaluated except the structured tasks

### Target Learners
- introductory physics lab students, including women and underrepresented minorities vulnerable to inequitable archetypes

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable participation in experimental work
- self-efficacy and hands-on apparatus skills for all group members
