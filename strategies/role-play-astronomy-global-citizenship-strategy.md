# Use role play in science teaching to develop empathy and problem-solving while conveying global citizenship

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating role play into science teaching, noting it is "still rarely included into science teaching" and calling this "an obvious missed opportunity as work by Francis (2006) has shown how powerful role play can be in developing problem solving and empathy skills". The stellar constellation activity operationalises this by having pupils inhabit two cultural roles, compare their reactions to a common situation, and experience how another culture reacts differently under the same conditions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An inspiring environment such as an astronomical observatory planetarium
- Role-themed background stories and artefacts to prime each group
#### Constraints
- The activity is only one possibility in incorporating the key concepts of citizenship within a lesson

### Target Learners
- school pupils across key stages
- PGCE primary science students

### Target Learning Goals
- empathy and problem-solving skills
- global citizenship concepts of values, perceptions and cultural understanding
