# Integrate immersive VR simulation as a complementary tool in continuing emergency training programs for low-frequency, high-complexity scenarios

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose progressively including immersive VR simulation in continuing emergency training, especially for situations where traditional simulation is complex, costly, or hard to replicate. They state the evidence "supports the progressive inclusion of VR as a complementary tool in continuing training programs on emergencies, especially in low-frequency and high-complexity clinical situations", and that integration must follow solid pedagogic criteria aligned with international recommendations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration "with solid pedagogic criteria, aligned with international recommendations"; assessment of impact on learning, patient safety, and institutional sustainability
#### Constraints
- Complementary role only; controlled studies comparing VR with traditional methods, retention, and clinical transfer are still needed

### Target Learners
- Health professionals in continuing emergency education programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical decision-making in low-frequency, high-complexity emergency scenarios such as hypothermia with aerial evacuation
