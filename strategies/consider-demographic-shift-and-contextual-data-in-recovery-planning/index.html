Educational leaders should weigh the demographic shift in tested students and other contextual data when planning student growth and recovery support¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The brief recommends that decisions about supporting students' academic growth and recovery after COVID-19 school shutdowns should not rely on fall 2020 test results alone. Because "Systematic demographic differences in available assessment data" mean the most vulnerable students may be underrepresented among those tested, leaders should "carefully consider the demographic shift in the students tested as well as other contextual data" when interpreting results and planning support.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to demographic information about which students were and were not assessed in fall 2020

Constraints¶

  • Grounded in grades 3-8 MAP Growth data from fall 2020; the brief does not quantify how large the demographic shift was

Target Learners¶

  • Students in grades 3-8 returning to school after COVID-19 closures

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Academic recovery of reading and math achievement and growth after pandemic-related school shutdowns

Related Strategies¶

  • Use federal ESSER and IDEA funding to provide early intervention, extended school year support in the summer, and other evidence-based supports for students with disabilities
  • When planning for the school year during COVID-19, schools and districts should weigh public health factors alongside educational factors such as potential learning loss
  • Use school-level recovery patterns to target support and long-term improvement investments where they are needed most
  • Use summer learning loss research to help educators, policy makers, and families plan for and address impacts of extended school closures
  • Use NAEP 2024 results, available January 2025, for better estimates of academic recovery
  • Use multiple evidence sources alongside benchmark scores when identifying students for intensive intervention
  • Educational leaders should examine demographic shifts in the tested student population before making growth and recovery support decisions
  • Include a district leader's perspective on supporting returning students in recovery planning
