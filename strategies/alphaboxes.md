# Alphaboxes

## Description
Alphaboxes is a strategy that encourages collaborative interaction with text by using a grid with boxes labeled with letters of the alphabet. Students generate words, questions, concepts, or ideas related to the text and write them under the corresponding letter in each box. It can be used as a pre-reading or post-reading activity to stimulate thinking and discussion about key ideas in the text. This strategy moves students beyond simple recall of factual information to higher-level cognitive processes such as generating questions, making connections, providing explanations, identifying unfamiliar words, and presenting different points of view. Students work in pairs or small groups, sharing cognitive responsibility and externalizing their thoughts as they work through the task.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to texts, Alphaboxes grid (paper or digital), and structured activity guidelines. Requires facilitation to model the process and guide initial interactions.
#### Constraints
- May be time-consuming. Requires explicit instruction and modeling. May not be suitable for students who struggle with collaboration or have limited vocabulary.

- Stimulate critical thinking, collaborative interaction with text, enhance recall, facilitate higher-level cognitive processes, and distribute cognitive responsibility among group members.

### Affordances
- Encourages active engagement with text, enhances comprehension through collaborative discussion, promotes higher-level cognitive processes, and distributes cognitive load among group members.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to various text types and content areas. Can be modified to require different numbers of boxes to be filled in. Can be used with different groupings (pairs, small groups, whole class).

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor the quality and depth of student responses in the Alphaboxes grid. Evaluate the level of cognitive processing demonstrated in the responses. Assess the effectiveness of group collaboration through observation and participation.

## Impact
- Studies indicate that generating questions, making connections, providing explanations, identifying and discussing unfamiliar vocabulary words, and presenting alternative perspectives are all higher-level cognitive processes that increase learning (Block & Johnson, 2002; Duke & Pearson, 2002; Pressley, 2006).
