# Sequence animations across graded source/observer motion situations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends developing animations that study particular situations related with the motion of the source, the observer, or both of them, progressing through qualitatively distinct cases: source moving slower than propagation with observers at rest; source at rest with observer moving more slowly than the propagation speed; observer traveling at the same speed as the generated waves (measured frequency zero); observer moving faster than the propagation wave (negative frequency); source at the wave-barrier speed; and source faster than the waves producing a shock wave.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each situation is tied to a figure showing two frames separated by a source oscillation period
#### Constraints
- Situations are limited to sinusoidal wave sources in a homogeneous medium

### Target Learners
- physics students studying wave motion

### Target Learning Goals
- relating observed frequency to source and observer motion, including zero and negative measured frequency and shock waves
