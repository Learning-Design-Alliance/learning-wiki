# Recruit volunteer trainers with dedicated ring-fenced time and embed micro-teaching in daily care routines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementation findings support selecting trainers who volunteer for the role and have time to train learners within their existing duties. Trainers were to offer learners "bite-size micro-teach sessions in daily practice", and an educator advised ensuring "someone with passion [for EoL care] and dedicated [ring-fenced] time". Where trainers held managerial posts they had to create time, which hindered uptake, whereas integrating teaching with everyday workflow supported engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainers who have completed the prerequisite ABC end of life care programme and volunteer for the role
- Educator/facilitator observation and evaluation of full teaching sessions
#### Constraints
- Staff shift patterns made gathering six learners for group work difficult, requiring flexibility and autonomy trainers' roles did not always permit

### Target Learners
- care home staff acting as peer trainers for end of life care

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining end of life care training capacity within care home organisations
