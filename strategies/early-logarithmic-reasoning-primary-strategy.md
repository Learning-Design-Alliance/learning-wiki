# Introduce powers of ten and logarithmic reasoning in primary school while children retain natural logarithmic thinking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends teaching powers of ten early, using child-friendly language that avoids conventional middle-school mathematical terminology, and leveraging young children's natural logarithmic-type number placement. It argues "it may be important to start learning powers of ten in primary schools, while students retain the natural ability to think logarithmically", since the shift to a linear mental number line occurs between first and fourth grade, and powers of ten are the only effective way to represent both small and big numbers on one line.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Child-friendly language avoiding conventional exponent and logarithm terminology
- Context-based learning connecting extreme numbers to real-life science
#### Constraints
- Estimation with single significant figures can produce errors of a whole order of magnitude through error propagation

### Target Learners
- primary school students aged 7-13

### Target Learning Goals
- logarithmic reasoning about extreme numbers
- reduced maths anxiety through relevant, approximation-tolerant mathematics
