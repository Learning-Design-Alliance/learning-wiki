# Deliver sessions using the SAAFE strategy set: supportive feedback, activity-maximizing structures, choice with rationale, fair mastery tasks and enjoyable variety

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy bundles the article's per-principle recommendations into an implementable session recipe. Teachers provide individual skill-specific feedback and praise for effort, optimize structure with small-sided games and minimal lines, avoid elimination activities, offer two to four choice opportunities with a meaningful rationale, modify activities so all students experience success, and design varied, enjoyable tasks. The article summarizes these as "Practical examples for how practitioners can implement the SAAFE principles" in Table 1.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers, coaches or instructors with control over session structure, activity selection, grouping and feedback practices
#### Constraints
- The authors caution against music if its distracting effects might undermine learning objectives, and warn that too many choice options can be burdensome and de-motivating

### Target Learners
- children and adolescents in PE, school sport and after-school programs

### Target Learning Goals
- increased MVPA during sessions
- autonomous motivation
- fundamental movement skill development
- enjoyment of physical activity
