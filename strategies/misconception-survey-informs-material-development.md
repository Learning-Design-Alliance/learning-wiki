# Identify common nuclear-issues misconceptions among students and the general population to develop educational material addressing them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes an iterative strategy: survey knowledge and views, identify the most common misconceptions, and use the results to build educational material. The author states that "identification of the most common misconceptions and faulty views of students and general population will contribute in the development of educational material that will improve the understanding of nuclear issues related to our everyday life", including revising the survey for larger samples and comparing student knowledge with administrative staff as a general-population proxy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A preliminary survey to document misconceptions, followed by revised survey instruments administered to greater numbers and to comparison groups.
#### Constraints
- Current results are preliminary, from a single institution with 131 respondents; the article states a revised survey and further administrations are planned.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate non-physics-major students
- secondary school students
- general population

### Target Learning Goals
- correcting misconceptions about nuclear energy, radioactivity and ionizing radiation applications
