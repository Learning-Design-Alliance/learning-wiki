# Apply various modes of learning to enhance the development of nursing students' competence.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This strategy responds to the finding that the mode of learning negatively predicted competence development, meaning that different learning methods such as face-to-face or online modes affect the development of nursing students' competency. The conclusion recommends that nursing training institutions apply various modes of learning to enhance development of nursing students' competence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- training institutions able to offer and vary learning modes, including face-to-face, online, and blended options
#### Constraints
- the mode of learning variable negatively predicted competence in this Namibian sample, and prior peer-learning work cited found face-to-face students reported higher competency than online students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical competence development
