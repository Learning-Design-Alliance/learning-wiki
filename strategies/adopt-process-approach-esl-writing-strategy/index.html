Strategies

Adopt the Process Approach in ESL writing classrooms and give writing practice adequate timetable time¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the study's outcomes, the article recommends that "English as second language teachers should adopt this approach for improved performances", that university lecturers train secondary teachers in its use, that publishers produce books employing the Process Approach, and that enough time be given to writing classroom practices. It also suggests school administrators may need to incorporate writing practice beyond the usual timetable allocation, and that the approach may assist teachers in over-bloated classes.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher knowledge updating on the Process Approach
  • Curriculum designers incorporating process activities and teaching auxiliaries

Constraints¶

  • The multi-draft nature of the approach may need planning to fit serialized educational systems

Target Learners¶

  • senior secondary school ESL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • essay-writing performance in English as a second language

Claims¶

  • Process Approach instruction produced significantly better overall essay-writing performance than the Product Approach in Nigerian ESL senior secondary students [+M]
  • The Experimental group significantly outperformed the Control group on each post-test variable, including Content, Organization, Expression and Mechanical Accuracy [+M]
  • Students taught with the Process Approach improved significantly from pre-test to post-test on all essay-writing variables [+M]
  • Earlier writing-instruction theories (frequency, grammatical, error correction, building blocks) failed to establish improvement in students' writing [+W]
  • The Control and Experimental groups were homogeneous in essay-writing performance at pre-test [+W]
