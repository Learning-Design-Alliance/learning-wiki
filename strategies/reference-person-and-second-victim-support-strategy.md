# Assign trainees a permanent reference person and provide institutional second-victim support programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Mentors proposed two structural supports: a permanent reference person for each trainee and institutional resources for second victims. In challenging clinical settings, "mentors mentioned the need for trainees to have a permanent reference person with whom they can establish a trusting bond from the moment they join the health care center." For second-victim support, "Most of the proposals were along the lines of the reference programs based on the Scott Three-Tiered Interventional Model of Second Victim Support," including peer supporters trained in emotional first aid, confidential group debriefings, Balint groups, and clear referral channels to psychological support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentors acting as reference persons need specialized training, resources, and supervision mechanisms
- Peer supporters need training in active listening, emotional validation, positive language, and empathy
#### Constraints
- Proposals are mentor recommendations; the article does not report evaluation of these measures in this study

### Target Learners
- health care trainees joining clinical centers
- health care professionals involved in adverse events

### Target Learning Goals
- establishing trusting mentoring relationships
- emotional support and recovery after adverse events
