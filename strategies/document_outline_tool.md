# Document Outline Tool

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A document outline tool supports learners in planning a written product before drafting: they organize the document into sections, headings, and bullet-level key points, establishing the logical skeleton first. The tool may be a structured template, a drag-and-drop outliner (e.g., Workflowy, Notion, Google Docs outline view), or an instructor-provided planning frame. The core move is separating *structural planning* from *prose generation*, so working memory is spent on organization rather than on composing sentences and arranging ideas simultaneously.

## Design Implications

Outlining reduces the simultaneous demands of ideation, organization, and transcription during writing, easing cognitive load [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Planning at the document level also shifts feedback earlier in the writing process: instructors can review and correct an outline before learners invest effort in full drafts, making feedback more efficient and less costly to act on [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. Outlines function as advance organizers that give learners a schema for the genre and its expected structure [Advance organizers improve learning of new material.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A genre-appropriate outline template or exemplar (e.g., argument essay, lab report, design doc) so learners know what sections are expected
- A low-friction tool or surface for drafting and rearranging the outline
- An instructor or peer checkpoint that reviews the outline *before* drafting begins
- Explicit instruction in what belongs at each level (section vs. point vs. evidence) — see [Clear Structure Presentation](../principles/clear-structure-presentation.md)

#### Constraints
- Outlining imposed as mandatory compliance without instruction in *why* produces formulaic, mechanical structures [-M] — learners fill slots without reasoning about the argument
- Overly detailed outlines can consume the planning effort that should go into content; rigid templates suppress voice and authentic organization [-W]
- For experienced writers, forced outlining can disrupt productive recursive drafting processes [~W]
- Weak writers may produce outlines that merely list topics without logical relations; without feedback on the outline itself, the tool adds little [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Instructor-provided skeleton** — sections pre-named; learners fill in points (strongest for novices)
- **Learner-generated outline with exemplar comparison** — learners draft their own structure, then compare against a model outline
- **Collaborative outlining** — groups negotiate structure before dividing writing tasks; supports [Collaborative Writing](../principles/collaborative-learning.md)
- **Reverse outlining** — learners extract an outline from an existing draft to diagnose structural problems, used at revision rather than planning stage

### Target Learners
- Novice writers and students new to a genre, who lack an internalized schema for document structure [Advance organizers improve learning of new material.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]
- Learners with working memory constraints or writing difficulties, for whom separating planning from transcription is especially valuable [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Less necessary for experienced writers with automatized genre knowledge [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Writing quality: coherent organization and logical flow of extended text
- Genre knowledge: understanding the conventional structure of reports, arguments, and explanations
- Self-regulated learning: planning and monitoring as explicit, teachable phases of composition

### Instructions
1. Provide or co-construct a genre-appropriate outline template with named sections ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md)).
2. Model outlining on a short example, thinking aloud about why each section exists and what belongs in it ([Think-Aloud Modeling](think-aloud_modeling.md)).
3. Have learners draft their outline — headings plus 2–4 bullet points per section — before any prose writing.
4. Review outlines (instructor, peer, or self against a rubric) and give structural feedback before drafting begins ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md)).
5. Learners draft from the outline, treating it as a revisable plan rather than a contract; revisit the outline during revision if the argument shifts.

## Examples
- **Google Docs outline view / Notion outliner** — learners build a collapsible heading hierarchy before drafting; headings double as a navigation aid during revision ([docs.google.com](https://docs.google.com)).
- **Purdue OWL essay outline guides** ([owl.purdue.edu](https://owl.purdue.edu)) — published templates for argumentative and expository outlines used as instructor-provided skeletons.
- **Workflowy** — drag-and-drop outliner used in composition courses for rearranging sections before drafting, making structural revision cheap.
