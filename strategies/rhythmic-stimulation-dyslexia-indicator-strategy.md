# Use learning-based neurophysiological indicators of speech-print mapping difficulty for early diagnosis and intervention of developmental dyslexia

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors propose that the present observation "may lead to learning-based neurophysiological indicators ofspeech-print mapping difﬁculty" that could benefit early diagnosis and intervention of developmental dyslexia, and note the findings "may inform educational practices for children with reading difficulties." The strategy is to use rhythmic sensory stimulation paradigms and the timing of phase-dependent associative learning as candidate indicators of mapping difficulty.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Future studies identifying the fine-grained temporal dynamics of alpha-theta interaction, which the authors recommend testing with transcranial stimulation combined with online EEG.
#### Constraints
- The evidence base is healthy Chinese undergraduates learning artificial speech-print pairs; no data on children with dyslexia were collected in this study.

### Target Learners
- children with reading difficulties
- children with developmental dyslexia

### Target Learning Goals
- early identification of speech-print mapping difficulty; supporting reading development
