# Develop clinician capacity, knowledge, and confidence in seizing teachable moments for smoking cessation in older patients, including those with chronic or terminal illness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (7 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's conclusion recommends targeted development of primary care clinicians' capabilities: "There is a need to develop clinician capacity, knowledge and awareness related to smoking cessation in older people specifically, and to build confidence in seizing teachable moments". The strategy addresses the survey's findings of knowledge gaps, the gap between asking about smoking and providing support, and lower confidence among non-medical staff. It applies even to patients with chronic or terminal illnesses, where clinicians most often hesitated.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clinicians require knowledge of smoking prevalence, impacts, and cessation benefits specific to older people, and confidence to raise the topic during routine contacts
#### Constraints
- The survey did not assess the content or nature of the smoking-related training clinicians had received, so the specific training design that would build capacity is not established by this study

### Target Learners
- General practitioners
- Practice and community nurses
- Healthcare assistants in primary care

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge of smoking prevalence and impacts in later life
- Confidence and skill in delivering smoking cessation advice and support to older patients
