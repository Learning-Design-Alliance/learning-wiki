# Tailor the emotional content of simulation sessions to individual learners' anxiety levels

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors using high-fidelity simulation identify students at higher risk of maladaptive stress responses and adjust session intensity accordingly. It states instructors "could consider modifying the emotional content of HFS sessions as a function of students' anxiety", giving more reassurance to vulnerable learners and harder cognitive stimulation to resilient ones. This is a forward-looking recommendation from the discussion, not a tested manipulation in this study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors able to identify students with a higher risk of maladaptive behavior before or during simulation sessions
#### Constraints
- Recommended in the discussion on the basis of the literature; not manipulated or tested in this study's design

### Target Learners
- medical students in senior years undergoing high-fidelity simulation

### Target Learning Goals
