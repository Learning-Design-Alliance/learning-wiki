# Instructors should make explicit the implicit physical meaning embedded in their mathematical reasoning and their model-rejection decisions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors encourage instructors to reflect on how and when physical ideas are incorporated, often implicitly, when doing math in physics contexts, noting that even choices like why summation appears in conservation laws have physical foundations. They suggest that "instructors, specifically when rejecting a model, may find it productive to consider ways to make their decision-making process explicit to the students in their physics courses", since instruction assuming mathematics is isolated from physical context may widen the gap between experts and students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must themselves recognize the physical foundations of their own mathematical reasoning during modeling
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a small interview study in classical physics graphing contexts; the authors emphasize further research is needed before strongly claiming an early-career effect

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting mathematical reasoning to physical meaning in physics
- building students' modeling connections

### Affordances
- Mathematical Riffing Construct
