Description¶

The review's recommendations for future research call for studies that evaluate how different variables affect medical students' satisfaction with e-learning, examine the advantages and disadvantages of various online teaching approaches, and investigate the limitations and obstacles of e-learning. The article states that "future research should evaluate the impact of different variables on medical students’ satisfaction with e-learning and examine the advantages and disadvantages of various online teaching approaches."

Claims¶

  • Convenience showed a significant positive relationship with medical students' satisfaction with e-learning during the COVID-19 pandemic [+W]
  • About half (51.79%) of medical science students were satisfied with e-learning during the COVID-19 pandemic across 24 cross-sectional studies [+W]
  • Age, clinical year, and stress perception showed negative and significant relationships with medical students' e-learning satisfaction during the COVID-19 pandemic [+W]
  • Gender, prior e-learning experience, level of study, adaptation, course content, interactivity, understanding of content, instructor participation in discussion, multimedia use, and adequate time showed significant relationships with medical students' e-learning satisfaction [+W]
