# Alternative Math Assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Alternative math assessments are formats for measuring number-fact fluency and mathematical understanding that do not rely on speeded, high-stakes recall tests. They include interview-based assessment, games, number talks, portfolio tasks, and untimed or low-stakes fluency checks. The goal is to measure what learners actually know — not how their knowledge holds up under time pressure and anxiety.

## Design Implications

Timed tests impose pressure that consumes working memory resources needed for retrieval, particularly for learners with math anxiety, and can produce scores that underestimate knowledge [Math anxiety interferes with working memory during computation.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.130.2.224) [+S]. Because anxiety about mathematics emerges early and correlates with avoidance, assessment format is not a neutral choice — it shapes learners' relationship with the subject [Math anxiety predicts lower achievement even in early elementary students.](https://doi.org/10.1080/15248372.2012.664593) [+M]. Alternative formats align with formative assessment principles: the purpose is to inform instruction, not merely to rank [Assessment functions formatively when it reveals learning gaps to teacher and learner.](https://doi.org/10.1080/0969595980050102) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear fluency criteria defined in terms of accuracy, flexibility, and efficiency — not speed alone
- Multiple assessment occasions, since single snapshots misrepresent developing fluency
- A way to record observations (interview notes, game-based data, work samples) so assessment informs [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) decisions
- Follow-up [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) tied to strategies, not just correctness [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]

#### Constraints
- Timed formats depress performance for anxious and working-memory-limited learners even when they know the facts [-S] — speed pressure converts a retrieval task into a stress task
- Games and open formats can mask whether fluency is automatic or scaffolded by counting; the assessor must probe strategy use, not just answers [~M]
- Alternative formats take more administration time than a one-page timed sheet; scaling requires routines (rotating interview schedules, self-recorded game data)
- Removing time pressure entirely can delay development of efficient retrieval if learners are never asked to consolidate strategies [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Interviews** ("How did you solve 8 + 7?") reveal strategy maturity and are the gold standard for diagnosing fluency quality
- **Games** (e.g., "How Close to 100," math card games) embed retrieval in purposeful contexts with repeated, spaced exposure [Spaced repetition improves retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S]
- **Number talks** make strategies public and let teachers assess the whole class's flexibility at once
- **Untimed fluency inventories** preserve the individual-recall format while removing the speed stressor
- **Portfolios and work samples** document growth over time rather than single-moment performance

### Target Learners
- Learners with math anxiety or test anxiety, whose timed-test scores systematically underestimate their knowledge [Math anxiety interferes with working memory during computation.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.130.2.224) [+S]
- Early-elementary learners, in whom timed testing can seed durable negative attitudes toward mathematics [Math anxiety predicts lower achievement even in early elementary students.](https://doi.org/10.1080/15248372.2012.664593) [+M]
- Learners building fact fluency through strategy-based instruction, where assessment should track strategy efficiency, not just speed
- Less necessary for learners with high fluency and low anxiety, for whom timed checks add little distortion [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Fact fluency: accuracy, flexibility, and efficiency with basic number combinations
- Strategy awareness: whether learners use derived facts and decomposition rather than counting
- Mathematical self-efficacy: assessment experiences that support rather than erode confidence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Define fluency criteria (accurate, flexible, efficient) and share them with learners so assessment targets are transparent.
2. Replace the weekly timed test with a rotating cycle: individual interviews, strategy-focused [Number Talks](../strategies/number_talks.md), and fluency games.
3. During interviews, ask learners to explain their strategy before confirming the answer, using [Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md) routines to record strategy level.
4. Give feedback on strategy choice and efficiency, not just correctness [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
5. Use results to plan targeted [Practice](../elements/practice.md) with spaced retrieval, rather than repeating whole-class timed drills [Spaced repetition improves retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S].

## Examples
- **"How Close to 100"** (youcubed, Stanford) — a dice-and-grid game in which learners repeatedly compute products in a purposeful context; the teacher observes fluency without any timed component. [https://www.youcubed.org](https://www.youcubed.org)
- **Number Talks** (Parrish, 2010, *Number Talks: Helping Children Build Mental Math and Computation Strategies*) — daily mental-math discussions revealing the strategies every learner uses.
- **Math Recovery / Add+Vantage MR** — one-on-one interview-based assessment instruments that profile a child's number strategies and inform targeted teaching. [https://www.mathrecovery.org](https://www.mathrecovery.org)
- **DreamBox Learning** — continuous, embedded game-based assessment that infers fluency and strategy from gameplay rather than discrete tests. [https://www.dreambox.com](https://www.dreambox.com)
