# Develop ontologies for ITS interoperability incrementally (mappings, benchmark tasks, folksonomies) rather than a top-down super-ontology

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "ontology development must play a key role for the future of ITS interoperability," covering learner data first and eventually learning tasks, pedagogical theories, and domains. It proposes several low-tech-to-automated routes: occasional hand-built mappings between ontologies, aligning ontologies by applying coding systems to shared benchmark tasks whose messages can be collected and compared, and Semantic Web matching research. It explicitly warns against one route: trying "to develop a super-ontology or new top-down standard for the types of information that learning systems communicate," since top-down ontologies have seldom achieved support in research or software communities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standards must cover learner data (events, assessments) before extending to tasks, pedagogical theories, and domains
#### Constraints
- Existing standards (PSLC DataShop, xAPI/LRS, IMS Global) focus on what happened (event streams) rather than how the learning task works; semantics must still be agreed between system designers

### Target Learners
- developers and researchers building interoperating AIED systems

### Target Learning Goals
- sharing learner data and pedagogical semantics across systems to enable adaptation and analysis
