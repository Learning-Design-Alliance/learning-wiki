# Use EEG-based real-time monitoring to inform personalized educational interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that EEG-derived markers of learning stage and cognitive state can drive personalized instruction. They state that "these results underscore the potential for real-time EEG-based personalized educational interventions" and that the classification results "provide theoretical support for the real-time monitoring of cognitive load and the development of personalized instructional strategies." The strategy is to monitor learners' EEG during authentic online course tasks and adapt instruction based on detected cognitive states.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Wearable multi-channel EEG acquisition during authentic course tasks
- Machine-learning models trained to discriminate learning stages from EEG features
#### Constraints
- Demonstrated with a small sample of six retained participants in one elective course; the article frames this as an initial exploration

### Target Learners
- undergraduate online learners

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing learners' cognitive states and learning stages to tailor instruction
