# Adopt the WHO personal drug selection method to improve medical students' knowledge and ability for appropriate medication use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends the personal drug selection method, suggested by the WHO, as one of the main measures to improve medical students' knowledge and ability for appropriate medication use. It notes the method "has successfully been applied in different countries such as Nepal and Japan", and situates it among undergraduate interventions that are inadequate despite students not yet having developed erroneous prescription habits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be directed at medical students before erroneous prescription habits develop, as the article states students have not yet developed them
#### Constraints
- The article notes a rigorous evaluation of interventions is required to determine if such efforts have indeed been effective, and that external validity of the present study was compromised because only three of six universities in the city were included

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- appropriate antibiotic prescribing
- prudent use of antimicrobials
