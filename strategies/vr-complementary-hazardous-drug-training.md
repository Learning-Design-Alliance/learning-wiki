# Use VR simulation as a complementary, not competing, component of hazardous-drug spill-management training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends treating VR as a complementary component within a broader instructional strategy for high-risk, infrequently encountered safety procedures such as chemotherapy spill management. VR "may serve a complementary rather than competing instructional role and provide a safe approach for rehearsing hazardous-drug spill-management procedures". Because most participants preferred combined instructor-led explanation with VR, the authors support further evaluation of blended instruction, and note VR could be placed at different points in a structured training program depending on curricular and logistical needs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Decisions about integrating VR should balance potential educational benefits against implementation feasibility and local resource constraints, including hardware and software costs, infrastructure and technical-support needs, and faculty time
#### Constraints
- The present findings do not establish objective educational effectiveness or readiness for routine implementation
- Asynchronous delivery was not evaluated; the VR intervention was delivered during scheduled sessions with standardized operational assistance
- Immersive environments may impose additional extraneous cognitive load on novice learners

### Target Learners
- Pharmacy interns and trainees in hazardous-drug handling

### Target Learning Goals
- Chemotherapy spill-management procedures
- Safe rehearsal of high-risk, low-frequency clinical procedures
