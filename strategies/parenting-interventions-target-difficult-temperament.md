# Target children with difficult temperament in parenting interventions if they are more susceptible to parenting effects on outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future research examine temperament within the context of parenting practices, and suggest parenting interventions could specifically target children with difficult temperament if such children are more susceptible to the impact of parenting on their cognitive and academic outcomes. This builds on their finding that temperament had small direct associations with outcomes after accounting for parenting, and on evidence that reactivity and persistence are modifiable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Evidence that temperamentally difficult children are more susceptible to the impact of parenting on their cognitive and academic outcomes, which the authors state is not yet established.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is conditional: the authors say interventions could target difficult temperament only "if they were more susceptible to the impact of parenting on their cognitive and academic outcomes."

### Target Learners
- young children (2-7 years) with difficult temperament

### Target Learning Goals
- improving cognitive and academic outcomes through parenting practices
