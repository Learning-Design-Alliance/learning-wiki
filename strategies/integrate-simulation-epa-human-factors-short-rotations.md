# Integrate simulation, entrustable professional activities and human factors education into short surgical rotations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend combining simulation-based learning, entrustable professional activities and human factors lectures and discussions within short subspecialty rotations, since subspecialty placements are often too brief to fit simulation. They report that this approach "enhances both technical and non-technical skills" and enriches the educational experience, and that entrustable professional activities can standardise the education experience for each student.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Key competencies identified and observed and evaluated by senior clinical staff
- Both positive and negative feedback provided
- Simulation equipment and facilities available within the rotation period
#### Constraints
- The study found the approach may not directly increase interest in surgical careers

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students on short clinical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- technical surgical skills
- non-technical skills including communication, teamwork and professionalism
- comfort in operating theatre environments
