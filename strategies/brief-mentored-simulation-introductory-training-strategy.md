# Integrate a brief one-on-one mentored simulation session as introductory training for novice cath-lab trainees

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding its simulation methodology as introductory training rather than for advanced trainees: "our described simulation training methodology should be integrated into a curriculum as part of introductory training." The recipe was one-on-one mentored training on the simulator for 10–40 min (median 20 min), with duration set by the learner's pace, in-person instruction from a trained operator using a standardized guide, and available conceptual checkpoints, supplementing didactic teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A high-fidelity endovascular simulator with 3D anatomy overlay
- A trained operator and standardized instruction guide
- Targeting trainees with little or no prior catheterization laboratory exposure
#### Constraints
- Effect was modest, and the fellow subgroup showed no apparent benefit
- More studies are needed to justify adoption and the cost of implementing simulation programs

### Target Learners
- early trainees: medical students and residents

### Target Learning Goals
- coronary angiographic projection interpretation competency
