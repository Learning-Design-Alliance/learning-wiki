# Target anti-bullying efforts at both high-risk children and high-risk schools, combining whole-school climate approaches with child-level risk identification

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that bullying interventions be targeted at both levels identified by its multilevel analysis: high-risk children (identified by gender, deprivation, SEN and their relation to school context) and high-risk schools (high deprivation, poor climate). It states that "promoting positive school climate through universal, whole-school approaches may be beneficial" and that identifying at-risk children should consider "not just the characteristics of the child (gender, deprivation, etc.) but also their relation to the wider school context". This supports integrating whole-school and targeted anti-bullying programs for more effective use of resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Data on both child-level characteristics and school-level deprivation and climate are needed to identify high-risk children and schools
#### Constraints
- The study is cross-sectional, so the directionality between school climate and bullying cannot be determined from these data

### Target Learners
- primary school children (Year 4 and Year 5)

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing bullying behaviour
- promoting positive school climate and peer relationships
