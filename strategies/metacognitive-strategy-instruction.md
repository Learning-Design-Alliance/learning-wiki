# Metacognitive Strategy Instruction

## Description
Metacognitive strategy instruction explicitly teaches learners *how* to approach tasks — planning, monitoring comprehension, selecting and adjusting strategies, and evaluating outcomes — rather than only teaching content. Effective programs combine declarative knowledge (what the strategy is), procedural knowledge (how to execute it), and conditional knowledge (when and why it works), typically through [Explicit Instruction](../principles/explicit-instruction.md), [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) modeling, and guided practice with [Fading](../elements/fading.md).

## Design Implications

Strategy instruction produces reliable but moderate gains, strongest when instruction covers not just the strategy itself but its conditional application — when, where, and why to use it [~S]. Teaching strategies as decontextualized procedures yields far weaker effects than embedding them in authentic subject-matter tasks [~S]. Sustained practice with feedback is essential: strategies must become self-initiated habits, not just performed on demand [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A named, explicitly described repertoire of strategies (e.g., self-questioning, summarizing, planning before writing)
- Expert modeling of strategy use in context, ideally via [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)
- Extended guided practice with [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) and gradual [Fading](../elements/fading.md)
- Prompts or routines that cue learners to apply strategies independently (e.g., planning checklists, [Self-Assessment](../elements/self-assessment.md) routines)

#### Constraints
- Instruction limited to strategy description without modeling and practice shows negligible effects [~S]
- Gains are often task-specific; far transfer to dissimilar tasks is rare without explicit bridging [~W]
- For novices, strategy instruction layered on top of high-element-interactivity content can add extraneous load and depress performance [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-M]
- Effects diminish when strategies are taught as compliance routines rather than with rationale — learners must understand *why* a strategy works to adapt it [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Embedded vs. stand-alone:** subject-matter-embedded instruction (e.g., strategy use during science reading) outperforms separate "learning skills" courses [~S]
- **Reciprocal formats:** teacher-led modeling alternating with student-led group application ([Reciprocal Teaching](../elements/reciprocal-teaching.md))
- **Prompt-based:** embedded prompts and checklists that cue self-questioning during tasks
- **Duration:** programs under ~4 weeks show markedly smaller effects than semester-long implementations [~S]

### Target Learners
- Struggling readers and learners with weak self-regulation, who show the largest gains [~S]
- Upper elementary through adult learners; effects are weaker but present in early grades, where working memory limits strategy coordination [~M]
- Less beneficial for already high-performing students who use effective strategies spontaneously [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension and writing quality in subject-matter domains
- Self-regulated learning: goal setting, monitoring, and revision habits
- Problem-solving heuristics and study-skill selection ([Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md))

### Instructions
1. **Select and name strategies** tied to a specific task domain; limit the initial set to avoid overload [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]
2. **Model** each strategy with a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md), making monitoring and decision points visible ("I don't understand this paragraph, so I'll reread the topic sentence…")
3. **Guide practice** with [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — collaborative application, then [Fading](../elements/fading.md) support as learners take over
4. **Teach conditional knowledge** — when the strategy applies and when it doesn't — using [Comparing Cases](../elements/comparing-cases.md) of effective vs. ineffective strategy use
5. **Prompt independent use** with routines and checklists, then progressively remove prompts
6. **Debrief** on outcomes so learners connect strategy use to results, building the evaluation component of metacognition

## Related Strategies
- [Reciprocal Teaching](../elements/reciprocal-teaching.md) — the canonical reading-comprehension implementation, alternating modeling and student-led practice
- [Think-Aloud Modeling](../strategies/think-aloud-modeling.md) — the primary modeling method for making strategies visible
- [Self-Assessment](../elements/self-assessment.md) — trains the monitoring and evaluating components
- [Spaced Practice](../principles/spaced-practice.md) — a study strategy whose value learners systematically misjudge, making it a prime target for instruction

## Examples
- **Reciprocal Teaching** (Palincsar & Brown, 1984) — small-group reading instruction cycling through predicting, questioning, clarifying, and summarizing, with teacher modeling fading to student leadership.
- **Self-Regulated Strategy Development (SRSD)** for writing — explicit instruction in planning and revising strategies (e.g., POW+TREE) with modeling, memorization of strategy steps, and self-monitoring; extensive evidence base in K–12 writing.
- **[Learning Scientists](https://www.learningscientists.org)** — classroom materials teaching evidence-based study strategies (spaced practice, retrieval practice, elaboration) with explicit when-and-why rationale.
