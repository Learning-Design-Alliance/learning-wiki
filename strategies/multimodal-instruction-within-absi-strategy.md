# Embed multi-staged multi-modal instruction within argument-based inquiry to boost achievement, writing, and argumentation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Within a uniform ABSI curriculum, treatment sections received "multi-staged multi-modal instructions in order to understand the roles and functions of modes, integration of modes into written communication, and evaluation of written materials with modal representations." The article concludes that "science instructors teaching science at different levels can adopt multi-modal representation instruction to enhance science learning, writing, and argumentation skills." This strategy adds explicit modal-representation awareness and integration practice on top of inquiry-based argumentation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A base argument-based inquiry curriculum with time-on-task held constant across groups
- Multi-staged instruction covering roles and functions of modes, integration into written communication, and evaluation of modal materials
#### Constraints
- Tested with college students in a laboratory applications course; generalization to other levels is the authors' recommendation, not a tested result

### Target Learners
- college science students
- science students at different educational levels

### Target Learning Goals
- science achievement
- writing skills
- argument quality
- multi-modal representation coherence
