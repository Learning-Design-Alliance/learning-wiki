# Integrate VR simulation design with prebriefing, intuitive interfaces, adaptive difficulty, scenario variability, ergonomic hardware, and structured debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future VR simulations "incorporate prebriefing, intuitive interfaces, adaptive difficulty levels, variability in scenarios, and ergonomic hardware to reduce extraneous load," while structured debriefing should remain a central component to reinforce reflection, motivation, and skill improvement. The goal is stated as not maximal immersion but optimal integration, where cognitive, affective, and technological dimensions align to create meaningful, sustainable learning experiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supporting autonomy through user control, competence through graduated difficulty and responsive feedback, and relatedness through debriefing and collaboration
#### Constraints
- The authors state effectiveness depends on balancing domains rather than maximizing any single aspect; findings come from a single-session qualitative study at one UK hospital and cannot be generalized to other professions

### Target Learners
- medical students
- undergraduate clinical learners

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning and decision-making
- clinical skill preparation
- reflection and consolidation of learning
