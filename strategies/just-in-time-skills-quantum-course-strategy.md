# Use just-in-time teaching to introduce new skills such as Python programming and matrices exactly when students need them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Just-in-time teaching defers new skills and concepts — here Python programming and matrices — until students need them for course projects, rather than teaching them up front. The pilot course used this as one of its three pedagogical tenets, and the authors note it "has also been shown to have positive outcomes on learning." For example, basic Python was not taught until students needed it for a quantum key distribution project mid-year, taught in Google Colab notebooks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course projects must be scheduled so that the moment a skill becomes necessary can be anticipated
- Access to free tools such as Google Colab for delivering the just-in-time instruction
#### Constraints
- The course limited polarization to linear polarization and deferred complex numbers, which were added only in the OnRamps version of the curriculum

### Target Learners
- High school students in a STEM academy pathway with only second-year algebra as prerequisite

### Target Learning Goals
- Programming skills for quantum protocols such as quantum key distribution
- Mathematical skills such as matrices as needed for quantum computing

## Claims

- [End-of-year focus groups revealed that students left the quantum computing course with a stronger self-reported understanding of matrix algebra and classical computing, beyond quantum topics](../claims/quantum-course-transfer-matrix-algebra-classical-computing.md) [+W]
