# Select books with detailed, varied, culturally relevant illustrations for dialogic reading with young children

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that dialogic reading programs choose books whose illustrations support discussion and retelling. They report that the curriculum-aligned books used in this study "were not the best examples of effective books for the dialogic reading method" because the illustrations were often very simplistic and repetitive, possibly limiting parent responses and interactions. Future programs should use books with detailed and varied illustrations including culturally relevant items children are familiar with.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Books should have detailed and varied illustrations and include culturally relevant items familiar to the children
#### Constraints
- The study's own curriculum-aligned books were simplistic and repetitive, which the authors say may have limited parent interactions

### Target Learners
- Preschool-aged English language learning children and their parents

### Target Learning Goals
- Expressive language and vocabulary growth through shared reading
