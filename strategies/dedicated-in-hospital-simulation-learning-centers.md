# Incorporate dedicated simulation learning centers within active hospital environments for accessible, distanced skills practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that "Dedicated learning centers could be incorporated within active hospital environments, allowing timely and easy access to learners interested in improving surgical psychomotor performance." A prototype center (Figure 1) arranges virtual reality stations with individualized feedback monitors and sufficient space for social distancing while limiting distraction. The strategy maintains technical training during pandemic-related restrictions on elective cases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulator stations with individualized feedback displays
- Sufficient space to maintain social distancing while limiting distraction
#### Constraints
- The authors recognize that the cost associated with such a training center would not be insignificant

### Target Learners

- medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- technical psychomotor surgical skills
- prevention of surgical errors
