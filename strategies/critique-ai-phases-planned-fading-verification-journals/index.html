Design AI-supported programming courses with critique-the-AI phases, planned fading, and verification journals¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's forward-looking design recommendations channel generative-AI use toward scaffolding rather than offloading. The authors state that "The model generates testable implications (e.g., critique-the-AI phases, planned fading, verification journals)" and invite multi-site tests of boundary conditions. These moves aim to preserve germane processing—testing, modifying, and explaining AI output—and strengthen evaluative skill and metacognitive calibration.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Intentional instructional design and classroom norms, since current tools are often always-on by default and blur the line between scaffolding and dependency

Constraints¶

  • The article frames these as testable implications awaiting multi-site evaluation, not as validated interventions

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate programming students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • evaluative skill, verification practice, metacognitive calibration

Affordances¶

  • Domain Tool Mastery Tension Model

Related Strategies¶

  • Integrate AI-driven scaffolding with teacher-led offloading and refine feedback granularity to prevent over-scaffolding
  • Use a mixed-methods assessment sequence: diagnostic inventory, structured verification tasks, and discipline-specific critique or workflow-design assignments
  • Discourage mental offloading to AI by balancing guidance depth and frequency with application opportunities
  • Design GenAI tools with modular offloading control and meta-cognition support for learning contexts
