# Use adaptive approaches and support systems, including virtual interprofessional experiences, to buffer disruptions to clinical education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that dental education adopt adaptive approaches and support systems when traditional clinical learning is disrupted, and specifically points to virtual interprofessional education as a promising curricular change. It reports that one cited study found students in "virtual interprofessional education (IPE) learning activities during the pandemic reported greater perceived gains in interprofessional skills" than peers in pre-COVID in-person visits, which "presents educators with an opportunity that can be utilized to make curricular changes".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning continuity during clinical disruption requires structured opportunities for in-person interaction, mentorship, and feedback, as the article states these are needed to cultivate competencies such as professionalism and communication skills.
#### Constraints
- The cited virtual IPE evidence is a comparative study of pharmacy and medical students, not dental students; the article's own interprofessional collaboration scores declined during the pandemic, so generalizing the strategy requires caution.

### Target Learners
- senior dental students on community-based rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical skills across patient care disciplines
- interprofessional collaboration
- professionalism
- time management
