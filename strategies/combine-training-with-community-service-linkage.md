# Combine brief practice-based physician training with community service engagement and systematic referral feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's central recommendation is that "combining a targeted physician practice-based educational intervention with community service linkage" improves dementia care competency and clinical-community linkages. Unlike more resource-intensive interventions that required case managers or practice redesign, this model required no major restructuring, and its feedback loops appeared to encourage continued referrals. The authors add that future dissemination will likely depend on flexible formats, including online options and tailored curricula to fit physicians' time constraints.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Direct engagement with local support service providers during training
- Consistent and systematic feedback to physicians about referred patients
- Inclusion of office staff in training sessions to handle dementia-related referrals
#### Constraints
- Low recruitment acceptance rates even with honoraria and CE credits; the authors speculate the weekend time commitment was off-putting and future trainings may need to be compacted

### Target Learners
- Primary care physicians
- Community medical practices without academic affiliations

### Target Learning Goals
- Dementia screening and assessment practice change
- Referral of families to community support services
