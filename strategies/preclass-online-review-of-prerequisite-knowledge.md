# Post pre-class online review materials for forgotten prerequisite knowledge (e.g., neuroanatomy) to free class time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because neuroanatomy is taught in the first year and forgotten by the fourth year, and class hours are too limited for in-class review, the article recommends putting prerequisite basic medical knowledge online before class "in diverse forms, such as videos and 3D animation for students to review before class." Students can repeatedly watch videos and post questions online, so teachers can see where problems exist and reserve class time for localization diagnosis and interaction. This strategy is the before-class stage of the SPOC+PBL model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An online learning platform hosting the review materials
- Teachers producing teaching videos and connecting different Internet platforms
#### Constraints
- The article notes producing online teaching content takes a lot of time and effort for teachers because no unified syllabus standardizes online neuroanatomy teaching content

### Target Learners
- undergraduate clinical medicine students who completed anatomy courses in earlier years

### Target Learning Goals
- retention and reactivation of prerequisite neuroanatomy knowledge
- preparation for in-class localization diagnosis learning
