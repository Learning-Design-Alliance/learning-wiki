# Make student reflection a mandatory component of experiential learning courses to integrate personal, professional, and academic learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding mandatory reflection into experiential learning courses. The authors state that "Reflection provides the opportunity for integrating personal, professional, and academic learning" and that "reflections are paramount for improving the quality of EL as a high‐impact practice." Both courses incorporated multiple student reflections into assessment, and reflective writing aligned with the four skill categories, with students writing in greater detail and depth than in surveys.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors ensure students reflect upon their curricular experience so students appreciate the value of EL in their learning and growth
#### Constraints
- Reflection quality is discussed in the context of senior undergraduate pharmacology electives with instructor support during discomfort from growth

### Target Learners
- undergraduate pharmacology and life science students in experiential learning electives

### Target Learning Goals
- integration of personal, professional, and academic learning
- metacognitive skills of self-reflection
- appreciation of the value of experiential learning
