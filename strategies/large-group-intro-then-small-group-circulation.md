# Begin instruction in a large group and split into smaller groups for the Circulation phase

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
For scaling the modified Peyton's Approach to the larger groups typical of skills-lab training, the authors recommend a staged group structure: "it could be helpful to begin the instruction in a large group and to subsequently split the group into smaller groups to continue 'Circulation' (Part V)". This keeps the single Demonstration and Deconstruction phase efficient while preserving the peer-instruction and feedback cycle in groups small enough for every trainee to perform.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors must monitor adherence to the defined sequence of parts A–F
#### Constraints
- The article states experiences with the approach still have to be gained in larger groups, since it was evaluated only with groups of three

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in skills-lab training groups of four to eight

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural clinical skills instruction in skills-lab settings
