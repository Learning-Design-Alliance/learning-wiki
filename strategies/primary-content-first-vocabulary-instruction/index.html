Sequence vocabulary curricula to present primary knowledge content first and set evolutionarily relevant contexts¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that primary knowledge words were better recalled and more motivating, the article suggests "a promising approach to utilize the motivational and cognitive advantages of primary knowledge to design language learning instruction for adults". Specifically, "it may be more motivating to learn vocabularies when the curricula first present primary knowledge content" and contexts related to these themes are set. This is a forward-looking design recommendation grounded in the study's own results.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learning content that can be ordered or framed by evolutionary relevance, such as vocabulary themes

Constraints¶

  • The supporting evidence is from a short-term lab-based vocabulary experiment with adults, not classroom curricula

Target Learners¶

  • adult language learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • vocabulary learning and retention

Affordances¶

  • Biologically Primary Secondary Knowledge Framework

Claims¶

  • Learning biologically primary words is perceived as less difficult and less effortful than learning biologically secondary words [+W]
  • Learning biologically primary words is rated as more enjoyable and more interesting than learning biologically secondary words [+W]
  • Adults recall more biologically primary knowledge words than biologically secondary knowledge words in a paired vocabulary learning task [+W]
