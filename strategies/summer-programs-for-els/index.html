Offer summer and out-of-school-time programs for current-ELs and dually-identified students¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because EL groups lost more learning during summers than never-ELs despite faster school-year growth, the author recommends that "schools and communities might consider offering programs during summers and other out-of-school time." The article cites evidence that targeted summer programs of extensive duration had positive academic effects for high school newcomer ELs and for elementary students from socioeconomically-disadvantaged families.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Programs must extend learning opportunities beyond the academic year for current-ELs and dually-identified students

Constraints¶

  • The article cites prior studies of extensive-duration programs; differential summer loss must be addressed or school-year gains will be partly undone

Target Learners¶

  • current-ELs
  • dually-identified EL/SPED students in elementary grades

Target Learning Goals¶

  • maintaining and developing academic skills gained during the school year
  • reducing summer learning loss in math and reading

Claims¶

  • Academic rebounding in reading and math was uneven across school years and summers, especially in reading [~W]
  • ELs lost more academic skill during summers than never-ELs in both math and reading [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide summer support to help English Learners maintain and develop academic skills
