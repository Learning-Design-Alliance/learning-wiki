# Present worked examples to novices and gradually fade them out as knowledge increases

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy sequences guidance by expertise: learners begin with fully worked examples and, as training progresses, shift toward independent problem solving. The article cites supporting evidence that "worked examples are best presented to novices but that they should be gradually faded out with increased knowledge." The experiments operationalized the contrast by giving one group examples followed by problems and the other group problems only, across successive training stages of increasing circuit complexity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A series of training sessions or tasks that progressively raise domain experience so the fade-out can track growing expertise
#### Constraints
- The article notes that at some point the effect of guidance may become negative rather than neutral or facilitative

### Target Learners
- inexperienced trainees in a new technical domain

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of solution schemas for problem solving in the trained domain
