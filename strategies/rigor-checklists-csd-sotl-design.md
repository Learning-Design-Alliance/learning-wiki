# Adopt rigor checklists and systematic reflection practices to elevate CSD SoTL quality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend that SoTL scholars look to translational clinical research practices to elevate rigor, adapting quality design checklists such as COREQ, a "32-item checklist" covering researcher, participant, and design information; TIDieR for intervention design and replication; and RoBiNT for small-N designs. Reflections should address implications for one's own teaching, reflect on patterns across cases, be relevant to current field issues, address the broader theoretical frame, and raise open questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Detailed descriptions of methods to increase replicability, bias checks such as member checking, and triangulation of multiple data sources
#### Constraints
- The authors note these checklists "may not be a perfect fit for SoTL" and RoBiNT is not a fit for all SoTL designs

### Target Learners
- CSD SoTL scholars, including doctoral students through senior faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- rigorous, replicable scholarship of teaching and learning in CSD
