# Blended 3-hour workshop combining a 1.5-h online module with a 1.5-h experiential face-to-face session for patient-centered communication training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's intervention is a blended workshop in which both groups "completed a 3-h workshop on patient-centered communication in older people care", comprising a 1.5-h online module with video-recorded lessons and knowledge tests plus a 1.5-h face-to-face experiential session based on either standardized patient simulation or role-play. The face-to-face session opens with a concept review based on the SAGE & THYME model, then observation of a modeled nurse-patient interaction, then practice and feedback or debriefing. The same facilitator delivered all workshops with a standardized structure to maintain fidelity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator with postgraduate clinical simulation training and prior role-play experience delivered all sessions
- A standardized structure with identical durations for theoretical review, observation, and interactive components
#### Constraints
- The short workshop duration limited repetition, which the authors identify as a key learning facilitator
- Scenarios did not match a real clinical or gerontology unit setting, which the authors say may have reduced learning outcomes

### Target Learners
- Generation Z nursing students in an Older adults Care module

### Target Learning Goals
- Patient-centered communication competence with older people (knowledge, skills, self-efficacy)
