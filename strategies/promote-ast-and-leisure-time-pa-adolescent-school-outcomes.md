# Promote both active school transport and cognitively engaging, enjoyable leisure-time physical activity to support adolescent classroom performance and school-related mental health

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that schools and communities should promote two complementary physical activity routes for adolescents: walking or cycling to school, which is "positively associated with perceived academic performance, competency in academic skills and school enjoyment", and leisure-time moderate-to-vigorous activity, which the authors say is likely to have more potential for classroom performance and school-related mental health. They also frame active transport as supporting public health and climate change mitigation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must have a feasible distance to school, since the study excluded pupils with over 10 km one-way distance where active transport prevalence reduced drastically
#### Constraints
- The cross-sectional design hinders causal conclusions
- AST may not lead to substantial educational or mental health benefits according to the authors

### Target Learners
- 15- to 16-year-old lower secondary school adolescents

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance and competency in concentration, writing, reading and mathematics
- school enjoyment and reduced school burnout
