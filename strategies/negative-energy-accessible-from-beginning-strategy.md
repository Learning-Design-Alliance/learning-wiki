# Use tasks and representations that make negative energy accessible from the beginning when chemical energy is central to the energy picture

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "when chemical energy is a central piece of the overall energy picture, the representational tools in use need to be set up so that negative energy is accessible from the beginning." Concretely, this means using representations such as energy bar charts (which can show negative bars) rather than pie charts (which break down for negative energy), and modeling chemical bonds via potential energy diagrams with a physically meaningful zero at non-interaction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Representational tools that can display negative energy values, such as bar charts
#### Constraints
- Needed specifically when chemical energy is central; less essential in introductory physics curricula that black-box chemical energy

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology students in introductory physics for the life sciences

### Target Learning Goals
- modeling chemical bond energy and energy conservation including negative energy
