# Use sequenced concept tests that address similar concepts in different contexts as peer instruction tools in quantum mechanics lectures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes designing "a sequence of multiple-choice questions to address similar concepts in diﬀerent contexts" for peer instruction on quantum measurement. For example, a sequence moves from expanding states in discrete energy eigenstates, to continuous position and momentum eigenstates, to a general continuous non-degenerate operator, so students generalize the projection method for calculating measurement probabilities. Concept tests are posed as multiple-choice questions, students discuss with peers, the class is polled, and the instructor gives explanations based on the responses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A classroom clicker system or equivalent polling mechanism; concept tests integrated periodically into lectures with peer discussion before polling.
#### Constraints
- Used with upper-level undergraduate quantum mechanics students; the article's illustration focuses on measurement-related concepts such as eigenstate expansions and projection probabilities.

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- expanding states in complete sets of eigenstates
- calculating probabilities of measurement outcomes via projection
