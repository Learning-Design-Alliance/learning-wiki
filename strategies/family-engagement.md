# Family Engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Family engagement is a sustained, collaborative practice in which families and school personnel partner to support children's learning and development. It spans behaviors at school (parent–teacher communication, shared expectations, participation in decision-making) and at home (supervision, high expectations, conversations about learning). Effective engagement is planned and reciprocal — schools actively build communication channels and model expectations rather than waiting for families to initiate contact.

## Design Implications

Family engagement functions as an out-of-school support system that amplifies in-school instruction: the strongest effects come not from attendance at school events but from home-based practices that connect to learning — expectations, conversations, and support for schoolwork [Parental expectations and home-based involvement show stronger associations with achievement than school-based participation.](../claims/joint-book-reading-predicts-literacy-success.md) [+M]. Engagement works best when it is relational and two-way: schools that build trust and communicate families' strengths see stronger participation and student outcomes than those that treat engagement as compliance or attendance at events [Henderson & Mapp's synthesis links relational engagement to achievement gains.](../claims/joint-book-reading-predicts-literacy-success.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Commitment from school personnel to initiate and sustain communication, not merely to host events
- Effective, two-way communication channels (translated materials, flexible meeting times, digital and in-person options)
- Active family participation supported by [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) on how to support learning at home
- [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) loops so families hear about progress and needs regularly, not only at crisis points
- Regular [Check-ins](../elements/check-in.md) that surface student needs shared between home and school

#### Constraints
- Engagement effects on achievement are modest and largely indirect — parental involvement correlates with achievement partly through expectations and self-efficacy rather than direct help with homework [Meta-analytic estimates show small indirect effects.](../claims/joint-book-reading-predicts-literacy-success.md) [~M]
- Homework help from parents can *reduce* achievement when parents lack current pedagogical knowledge or when help becomes intrusive [Parental assistance with homework shows weak or negative associations with achievement in some analyses.](../claims/joint-book-reading-predicts-literacy-success.md) [-M]
- One-size-fits-all engagement (evening events, English-only communication) systematically excludes working families, non-English-speaking families, and shift workers
- Engagement requires sustained time and resources; short-lived initiatives fade without institutional support
- Effects weaken as students age unless engagement shifts toward autonomy-supportive forms (discussing plans, expectations) rather than direct supervision [Achievement effects of involvement decline across grade levels.](../claims/joint-book-reading-predicts-literacy-success.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Home-based involvement** — conversations about school, high expectations, structured reading time; strongest evidence base [Dialogic reading at home improves children's expressive language.](../claims/dialogic-reading-improves-expressive-language.md) [+S]
- **School-based involvement** — attending conferences, volunteering, family literacy nights; builds relationships but weaker direct academic effects
- **Home–school communication** — regular two-way contact via apps (ClassDojo, TalkingPoints), conferences, home visits
- **Governance involvement** — families participating in school decision-making bodies; slow-moving but builds institutional trust

### Target Learners
- Students of all ages, with the strongest academic effects in elementary grades [Meta-analytic evidence shows larger effects for younger students.](../claims/joint-book-reading-predicts-literacy-success.md) [+M]
- Students from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds, when engagement is culturally responsive rather than assimilationist [Henderson & Mapp find engagement benefits are strongest for diverse and low-income students.](../claims/joint-book-reading-predicts-literacy-success.md) [+M]
- Early readers, where structured home reading practices predict literacy outcomes [Joint book reading predicts literacy success.](../claims/joint-book-reading-predicts-literacy-success.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement across subjects, mediated by expectations and engagement with learning
- Attendance and behavior improvement
- Social-emotional development and school belonging
- Long-term outcomes: graduation and postsecondary planning

### Instructions
1. **Build relational trust first.** Establish two-way communication before making academic asks; use [Check-ins](../elements/check-in.md) and [Active Listening](../strategies/active-listening.md) to learn families' goals, strengths, and constraints.
2. **Communicate learning, not just logistics.** Share what students are learning and how families can support it — model high expectations and specific home practices (e.g., reading routines) rather than generic "be involved" messages.
3. **Coach home-based support.** Provide [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and modeling for home practices such as dialogic reading [Dialogic reading improves expressive language.](../claims/dialogic-reading-improves-expressive-language.md) [+S], while avoiding direct homework takeover [Parental homework help can backfire.](../claims/joint-book-reading-predicts-literacy-success.md) [-M].
4. **Close the loop with feedback.** Use structured [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) so families see the effect of their support, and adjust practices based on family input.
5. **Fade school direction over time.** As families build confidence, shift from school-led scripting to family-led support, consistent with [Fading Support Promotes Transfer of Responsibility](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

## Examples
- **[Parent Teacher Home Visits](https://www.ptplus.org)** — National program in which educators visit families at home to build relational trust before academic partnership; associated with improved attendance and behavior in district evaluations.
- **[Flamboyan Foundation](https://flamboyanfoundation.org)** — Trains Washington, DC educators in relationship-building home visits and ongoing family engagement, with documented gains in student outcomes.
- **[ClassDojo](https://www.classdojo.com)** and **[TalkingPoints](https://talkingpts.org)** — Two-way home–school messaging platforms; TalkingPoints supports translated, multilingual communication to reduce access barriers.
- **[Raising a Reader](https://www.raisingareader.org)** — Rotating book-bag program that coaches families in shared book-reading routines, enacting home-based involvement.
