# Supplement or replace traditional lecture with recorded lectures, flipped classroom, and gamification, with caution about methodological diversity and risk of bias

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 6 studies (3 causal, 2 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 6 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that "Utilization of recorded lectures, flipped classroom, and gamification can supplement or replace the traditional lecture (Level 1a; Grade B)." Active learning is defined as "any instructional method that engages students in the learning process" and can include games, flipped classroom, audience response systems, case-based problems, and team-based activities. The flipped classroom combines independent pre-learning via video lectures or pre-reading with face-to-face activities; gamification uses games and competition to support learning of skills, ED throughput, decision-making, and medical knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Flipped classroom implementation requires learners to complete pre-session preparation; team-based learning additionally requires upfront faculty training in discussion facilitation and learner buy-in
#### Constraints
- Recent systematic reviews of the flipped classroom found high methodological diversity, inconsistent results, and risk of bias, so caution should be exercised

### Target Learners
- emergency medicine residents
- graduate medical education trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement and objective learning outcomes in didactic sessions
