# Integrate structured suicide prevention training with simulated practice of responding to suicidal communication into clergy formation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating structured suicide prevention training into clergy formation and continuing pastoral education, because relying solely on experiential exposure may lead to uneven preparedness. It states that "integrating structured suicide prevention training with pastoral experience is therefore important for ensuring consistent and effective responses to individuals at risk." For training design, it suggests that "simulated conversations, role plays, and structured opportunities to practice responding to suicidal communication may be more substantially beneficial than passive or observational learning." The recommendation rests on the finding that training moderated the exposure–competency association and that suicidal communication was the most central exposure node.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must be integrated into clergy formation and continuing pastoral education rather than left to on-the-job experience alone
#### Constraints
- The study was cross-sectional and assessed training with a single dichotomous item, so the recommendation is based on moderated associations rather than a training-effect experiment

### Target Learners
- Roman Catholic priests and other clergy in Nigeria and similar faith communities

### Target Learning Goals
- Consistent and effective responses to individuals at risk of suicide, including pastoral care, nonjudgmental attitudes, confidence in talking about suicide, and postvention
