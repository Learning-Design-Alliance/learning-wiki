# Embed MSF as a mandatory component within a program of assessment and actively promote it to faculty and staff to support rater recruitment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the lessons learned, the authors suggest that stakeholder and learner buy-in is crucial for implementing MSF as an elective, and that only one of four programs collecting data speaks to the feasibility limits of an elective approach. They write, 'We may need to embed MSF as a mandatory component within a program of assessment to fully evaluate its educational impact.' They also recommend that UGME programs actively promote MSF to faculty members and hospital staff so they can support the rater recruitment process students found burdensome.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stakeholder buy-in, particularly learner buy-in
- Active promotion to a broader audience including faculty and hospital staff
#### Constraints
- Only one of four participating programs collected data, so the educational impact of mandatory embedding remains unevaluated in this pilot

### Target Learners
- clerkship medical students in undergraduate medical education programs

### Target Learning Goals
- socializing students to receiving multisource feedback early in their careers
- identifying competency gaps earlier in training
