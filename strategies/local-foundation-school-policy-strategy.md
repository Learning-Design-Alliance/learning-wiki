# Postgraduate training policy should consider push-pull factors and promote the benefits of training at a local foundation school

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that postgraduate training policy take account of 'push-pull' factors in medical careers decision-making, since competition for popular regions such as London and the South of England reduces some applicants' chances of their top choices. It states that "policy initiatives could focus on the benefits of training at a local foundation school", framing this as an important stage of trainee doctors' working careers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Linked individual-level data on applicant characteristics, application scores and allocation outcomes to identify where push-pull factors operate
#### Constraints
- Findings are specific to the UK Foundation Programme and the 2013–2014 cohorts; the sample excluded international medical graduates and non-UKCAT applicants

### Target Learners
- UK medical graduates applying to Foundation Training

### Target Learning Goals
- Equitable access to preferred postgraduate training placements
