# Teach students systematic ways to find their own research paper topics rather than assigning topics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
For the course paper program, the authors did not offer topics to students; instead they show ways to find topics. Starting from the simple pendulum's list of idealizing conditions, they apply the idea to "remove or change any one or some condition(s) we will get a real problem," yielding hundreds of candidate topics from the combinatorial count they print. They also list other ways: reverse (inverse Compton Effect), change of dimensionality, combination, transplant, from experiments, from hobbies and games, and from topics already done. A recommended procedure runs from phenomenon to keywords, search, references, and repetition, with the rule that "research refuses replica" and topics must be confirmed by references.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Group mentoring to enumerate topic-finding ways and comment on excellent or trivial topics
- One-to-one tutorship through email or studio, with replies promised within 24 hours
#### Constraints
- Large classes bring teachers heavy email burdens during rush periods; supervisors need strong research background and wide knowledge coverage

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students writing course papers in introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- Independent topic selection, literature search skills, and research consciousness
