# Emphasizing clinical relevance during theoretical teaching to support motivation and perceived value of lecture attendance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (3 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes that connecting lecture content to future professional practice can strengthen student engagement. It reasons that "Students are more likely to engage when educational content is clearly connected to their future professional practice and clinical identity" and concludes that "emphasizing clinical relevance during theoretical teaching may support motivation and perceived value of lecture attendance." This recommendation is grounded in the finding that students most frequently reported attending lectures for examination preparation and easier understanding of the material.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educational content must be clearly connected to students' future professional practice and clinical identity
#### Constraints
- Grounded in self-reported attendance motivations from an exploratory cross-sectional survey; the article notes students and staff differed in their interpretation of the factors influencing attendance

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students

### Target Learning Goals
- lecture attendance
- motivation and engagement in theoretical learning
