# Adapt case-based learning delivery for resource-limited preclinical settings: simplify cases, phase complexity, standardize facilitation, add low-cost visual aids

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students and authors propose concrete, feasible adaptations rather than direct adoption of high-resource CBL models. Recommendations include that participants “Start with simple cases first, then move to more integrated ones later”, incorporate diagrams, flowcharts and basic animations, and implement uniform teaching strategies across faculty so sessions are consistent. These respond directly to the challenges students reported.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty orientation to session objectives and discussion flow prior to implementation
- Cases aligned with recently covered lecture content
#### Constraints
- Recommended by students based on perceived needs; the study did not test whether the adaptations improve outcomes
- Developed in a single public-sector medical university

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical (MBBS) students in preclinical years

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of physiology
- Early clinical reasoning
