# Design gender-tailored school-based PA health promotion interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that school-based health promotion interventions address multiple target groups, including children's families and the school environment, and tailor content by gender. It states that "interventions for girls could be effective when focusing on increasing the PA frequency and on decreasing sitting time when playing digital games, while interventions for boys are promising when encouraging their participation in sports clubs and engagement in vigorous PA." These directions follow from the study's gender-specific regression findings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should consider the mediating factors the article names: gender, grade level, developmental disability, family size, and parental encouragement for academic success.
#### Constraints
- The article recommends further high-quality intervention studies and systematic reviews to clarify the association between PA and academic performance, so the recommendations rest on cross-sectional evidence.

### Target Learners
- Primary school students in grades 4–6

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic competence (overall, mathematical, and reading) supported through physical activity and reduced sedentary screen time
