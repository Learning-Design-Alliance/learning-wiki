# Design online examinations with explicit strategies to tackle examiner frustration and enhance user experience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that assessment designers treat examiner frustration as a design target for online examinations. Its analysis found frustration was the component driving the workload difference between formats and was "strongly associated with the online examination format", which the authors link to "technical issues or examiner unfamiliarity with the technology". They conclude that "strategies to tackle frustration and enhance the user experience in online examinations should be considered when designing assessment".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online examination delivery platform and examiner-facing technology whose usability can be improved before deployment
#### Constraints
- The article identifies frustration as the target but does not test any specific frustration-reduction intervention; it calls for further interventional research on workload optimisation

### Target Learners
- postgraduate anaesthesiology and intensive care examiners and candidates

### Target Learning Goals
- standardised high-stakes assessment of clinical knowledge with acceptable examiner workload
