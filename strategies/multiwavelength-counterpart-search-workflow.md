# Three-step multiwavelength counterpart search workflow for unidentified gamma-ray sources

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates an implementable research workflow for students: first run an association-search tool (VOU-Blazars) that checks for potential counterparts in more than 70 different catalogs within the source uncertainty area and ranks associations by multiwavelength weights; second verify each candidate individually with an optical sky-map tool; third generate the spectral energy distribution with VOU-SED and estimate the synchrotron peak with the blast machine-learning code to classify the candidate. This workflow let high school students produce publishable candidate identifications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the Open Universe/Firmamento tools, Fermi-LAT source coordinates with uncertainty regions, and archival multiwavelength catalogs
#### Constraints
- Association ranking criteria are at the moment not tunable by the user; some candidates need additional observational data for validation

### Target Learners
- high school students
- citizen scientists

### Target Learning Goals
- multiwavelength astronomical source identification
- spectral energy distribution analysis
