# Extend conventional welfare-focused personal tutoring into Academic Tutoring that combines coaching-style one-to-one conversations, group tutorials and a study-skills lecture

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Academic Tutoring (AT) programme repositions tutors from welfare-only 'fixers' to facilitators who run learner-centred coaching conversations about study skills, professional development and wellbeing. It combines termly one-to-one tutorials using the GROW coaching model, structured group tutorials building a community of practice, and a first-week lecture on "The Science of Successful Learning". The article reports students valued the personalised one-to-one approach and that "the delivery of practically orientated content in group sessions, and an individualised approach in one-to-one conversations was highlighted as a key strength".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors trained in coaching skills for learner-centred conversations
- Support structures such as a Head of Academic Study Skills and a Senior Welfare Tutor
#### Constraints
- Student preferences for programme components were diverse and often conflicting, suggesting highly personalised views on Academic Tutoring
- Some students with existing effective strategies did not feel they needed the programme

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate medical students transitioning from secondary education

### Target Learning Goals
- effective self-directed learning strategies
- time management
- self-efficacy and growth mindset
- wellbeing and help-seeking
