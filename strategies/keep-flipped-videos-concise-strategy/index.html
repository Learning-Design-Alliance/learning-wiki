Keep flipped-classroom videos concise, ideally no longer than about 10 minutes, splitting longer videos into shorter segments¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The authors recommend that teachers new to flipped learning "make an extra effort to keep the information in the videos as concise as possible", citing Bergmann and Sams's recommendation that videos be no longer than 10 minutes and longer ones be broken into segments of around 5 minutes. In this study some videos ran long, with the longest close to 15 minutes, and at least one student complained there was too much information in the videos.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Videos must be produced with attention to students' limited concentration spans

Constraints¶

  • The video creator in this study did not fully meet the guideline; the longest video was close to 15 minutes

Target Learners¶

  • Japanese university EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English composition writing proficiency

Related Strategies¶

  • Keep online lecture videos under twenty minutes and segment by topic with speed control
  • Keep pre-class teaching videos short (10–20 minutes), goal-targeted, and followed by checking homework
  • Limit flipped classroom video duration to around 20 minutes and use opening quizzes
  • Design flipped-classroom videos with sound-image synchronization, short durations, full-body instructor visibility, and opportunities for immediate questions
  • Limit pre-class video length to 20 minutes to reduce student burden
