# Hybrid teaching sequence: simulation to introduce concepts, real experiment to manipulate, then simulation to model and generalize

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's proposed optimal combination integrates simulations and real experiments into one coherent sequence. "This hybrid approach consists of starting with a simulation to intro- duce concepts, visualize abstract phenomena, and men- tally prepare students." A real experiment follows, letting learners manipulate and confront the constraints of scientific practice, and the sequence returns to the simulation to model, analyze, compare results and generalize concepts. Teachers surveyed perceive this as the most effective strategy because it maximizes both conceptual understanding and experimental mastery.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need access to both digital simulation tools and laboratory equipment, and the ICT training the article says is needed to integrate these tools into practice.
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as teachers' proposed and perceived strategy, not as a tested intervention; its effectiveness claim is perceptual.

### Target Learners
- middle school students
- high school students in physics and chemistry

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of abstract scientific phenomena
- experimental skills and methodological rigor
- critical thinking
