# Apply focus-group consensus guidelines when writing pharmacology exam questions: make knowledge demands explicit, match verbs to year and format, and discourage low-reflection prompts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Implementable question-writing guidelines produced by the educator focus group: avoid open collocations — "the pharmacology of" was "open to interpretation and should be avoided when used alone", with demands such as pharmacodynamics, pharmacokinetics or clinical pharmacology made explicit instead; request "relevant examples"; and discourage "notes on" prompts. Verb choice should fit exam format and student prior knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A departmental consensus process on verb and collocation meanings, such as the survey-plus-focus-group sequence used in the study, before applying the guidelines.
#### Constraints
- The article states "The year of study and/or prior student knowledge may guide the choice of assessment action verbs" and that "outline" suits time-constrained SAQs while "explain" suits emphasis on clarity without in-depth or critical analysis.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate pharmacology students sitting summative constructed-response examinations

### Target Learning Goals
- Clear communication of cognitive processing demands in exam questions
- Fair assessment of knowledge rather than interpretation of ambiguous wording
