# Simulation-based safety training plus institutionalized safety case conferences for psychiatric nurses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on participants' expressed enhancement needs, the authors recommend that healthcare institutions and nursing management implement extended and more intensive pre-employment training programs that "emphasize simulation‐based exercises focused on risk assessment, de‐escalation strategies, and restraint procedures." They further recommend that "regular safety case conferences should be institutionalized, enabling teams to collaboratively analyze recent or near‐miss incidents and disseminate lessons learned." This responds directly to the finding that current training overemphasizes theory over practice and that nurses desire more experience sharing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional support from health administration departments and hospital managers to run training programs and case conferences
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a single-institution qualitative study whose participants may not represent the broader psychiatric nurse population

### Target Learners
- Psychiatric nurses in inpatient mental health settings, especially new and early-career nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- Practical patient safety skills: risk assessment, de-escalation, restraint procedures, and learning from adverse and near-miss events
