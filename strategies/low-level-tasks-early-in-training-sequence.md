# Place low-level perceptual tasks early in multi-skill training sequences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that tasks reliant on low-level sensory features were more prone to anterograde interference, the authors recommend sequencing: "placing low-level perceptual tasks early in the training sequence, particularly for clinical and expertise training requiring the acquisition of multiple proficient skills, could optimize learning outcomes." The strategy treats training order as a designable parameter in multi-skill curricula.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training programs must involve multiple tasks learned sequentially, with order under designer control
#### Constraints
- Some tasks, such as the shape search task, remained unaffected by sequence position, so ordering benefits are task-specific

### Target Learners
- learners in clinical and expertise training acquiring multiple proficient skills

### Target Learning Goals
- efficient acquisition of multiple perceptual skills
