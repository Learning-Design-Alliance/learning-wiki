# Hyperlinked Teaching

## Description
Hyperlinked teaching provides students with direct avenues for further inquiry and exploration. Students are not stifled in their learning tract but are able to choose what to learn more about within a topic, and what they already know and can move beyond.  Additionally, students are encouraged to reference their connections with evidence from multiple sources. Provide pockets of opportunities for your students to explore a topic in more detail, learn about the topic from a different perspective, or to create a solution using multiple sources and products.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a variety of learning resources (e.g., articles, videos, websites). Instructional time for exploration, discussion, and project development.
#### Constraints
- May require significant planning to curate relevant and high-quality resources. Some learners may struggle with the open-ended nature of inquiry and require more structure.

- Foster learner autonomy, promote deeper inquiry, encourage exploration of multiple perspectives, develop research skills

### Affordances
- Increases learner motivation and engagement by allowing choice and control. Supports the development of research skills and the ability to synthesize information from multiple sources. Encourages critical thinking and problem-solving.

### Personalization
- Adaptable by providing a range of topics, resources, and project options aligned to learner interests and skill levels. Teachers can scaffold inquiry with targeted questions and guidance.

## Impact
- Research suggests that inquiry-based learning can lead to deeper understanding, greater retention, and increased motivation. Learner choice and control are associated with positive academic outcomes.
