# Treat the STEM competition landscape as an interconnected ecosystem with multiple entry points rather than as isolated programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends analyzing and organizing STEM competitions as a coordinated system: "By linking entry-level, broad-participation competitions with selective, multi-round competitions across adjacent domains, such an ecosystem can reach a larger share of STEM-interested students and offer multiple entry points through which individual potential may be recognized and supported." The German landscape exemplifies this, with nationally advertised competitions held to shared quality criteria, formally recommended by the KMK, and with organizers exchanging experience across competition boundaries.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordination across competition boundaries, including shared quality criteria and organizer exchange, as described for the German landscape
#### Constraints
- Reach through the ecosystem is domain-specific: a student not entering one competition may still be reached by another, so non-participation in a specific competition does not indicate failure of the broader ecosystem

### Target Learners
- STEM-interested secondary school students
- Potentially successful non-participants of individual competitions

### Target Learning Goals
- Recognizing and supporting STEM potential through multiple competition pathways

### Affordances
- Stem Competition Format Taxonomy
