# Use computer-regulated psychometric conservation tests to measure relaxation time for testing critical slowing down

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
To operationalize the critical slowing down flag, the article recommends measuring relaxation time (the time to restore a perturbed equilibrium) with psychometric versions of Piagetian tasks. Because reaction time measures are unsuitable for classical Piagetian tasks due to the child-experimenter interaction and required verbal explanation, the authors note that "Computer-regulated conservation tests have been found to correlate high with traditional conservation tests, like the Goldschmid and Bentler test, and could be used for this objective." Training may be viewed as the perturbation whose recovery is timed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An effective perturbation of the system and a proper measure of relaxation time, such as reaction time in a computer-regulated test
#### Constraints
- Reaction time measures may not suit classical Piagetian tasks because of the complex interaction between child and experimenter and the required verbal explanation

### Target Learners
- children assessed for conservation (preoperational to concrete operational transition)

### Target Learning Goals
- detecting lengthened relaxation time near a stage transition (critical slowing down)
