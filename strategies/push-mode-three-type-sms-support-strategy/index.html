Push-mode SMS support with three message types sent on non-class days¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's implementable recipe: use the push mode, in which "the instructor sending educational SMS to the students", sending three messages per week on three different days over 12 weeks (36 messages total), on weekdays without class meetings between 4 pm and 7 pm. Messages contain reviews of concepts already discussed in class, hints for assignments and projects, and triggering questions for upcoming classes. This kept integration simple for first-time educational SMS users.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Collect student phone numbers with consent at the start of the semester
  • Time messages so students are not in other classes
  • Sequence message content based on analysis of the course content

Constraints¶

  • The article tested this in one introductory programming course with English-language messages; students suggested extending SMS to two-way and administrative communication

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in introductory programming courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • retention of programming concepts
  • solving assignments and projects
  • staying connected to the course between classes

Affordances¶

  • Sms Three Learning Paradigms Framework

Claims¶

  • Students receiving SMS learning support scored significantly higher on the programming posttest than control students [+W]
  • Both the SMS and control groups improved significantly from pretest to posttest in the programming course [+W]
  • Interviewed students perceived SMS learning support as having more advantages than disadvantages [+W]
  • Students reported SMS limitations including character limits, lack of formatting, and one-way communication [-W]
  • Chinese EFL students learning vocabulary via daily SMS messages outperformed a paper-based self-regulated group on the immediate posttest after a three-week intervention [+W]
