# Adapt multi-agent AI roles to learners' prior knowledge and regulate agent participation dynamically from interaction cues

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's design implications hold that "agent roles should be adaptive to learners' prior knowledge": low prior knowledge students should receive scaffolded step-by-step support, while high prior knowledge students benefit from reflective prompts eliciting reasoning and elaboration. Agent participation should also be dynamically regulated based on interaction patterns, minimizing teacher-agent interventions when students show confidence and fluency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The system must monitor interaction cues such as frequent help-seeking or surface-level responses to adjust agent involvement in real-time
#### Constraints
- Static, one-size-fits-all agent behaviors risk under-supporting novices and disengaging advanced learners

### Target Learners
- university students in online courses with varying prior knowledge

### Target Learning Goals
- learning gains in course content
- sustained motivation and engagement
