# Vary canonical SoV problems and have students derive the need for the infinite sum to discourage pattern matching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend directly targeting the separated form of the potential and the infinite sum, and varying canonical problems so pattern matching fails. They suggest "placing the non-zero boundary condition on either the y = 0 or x = 0 sides of a rectangular box", providing a function rather than a constant for the non-zero boundary condition, and for spherical SoV "asking for the potential between two nested spherical shells". Getting students to come up with the need for the infinite sum on their own seemed particularly productive for interview students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must design problem variants that complicate the usual simplification steps so that recall of a single canonical procedure is insufficient
#### Constraints
- The article notes solving SoV problems solely through pattern matching is often a highly effective strategy because there are a finite number of solvable SoV questions and they are all fairly similar; the study did not investigate the impacts of instructional strategies on difficulty prevalence

### Target Learners
- junior-level undergraduate physics students in electricity and magnetism

### Target Learning Goals
- justifying the separated form of the potential and the infinite sum; selecting efficient strategies for solving for unknown constants
