# Consult students, physics faculty, and non-physics faculty through interviews to identify learning goals in interdisciplinary course reform

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy adapts stakeholder-consultation models of course transformation to physics-for-non-majors settings: researchers conduct semi-structured interviews with students, course professors, and professors from other departments, transcribe them, and use inductive thematic analysis to identify how stakeholders view the course's interdisciplinary role. The authors state that "By seeking perspectives from a wide range of stakeholders, both students and faculty, across a variety of disciplines, we can discover how to improve physics for non-physics-major courses." The approach extends prior IPLS course-reform processes to a broader disciplinary range.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Semi-structured interviews with all three stakeholder populations (students, course faculty, non-physics faculty), audio recorded and transcribed
- An inductive thematic analysis with iterative category development, ideally with independent categorization checks between researchers
#### Constraints
- The article notes its project did not solicit feedback from geology or environmental analysis professors and did not produce a comprehensive list of relevant topics
- Member checking was used to validate quotes; nearly all participants responded

### Target Learners
- non-physics science students in introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying interdisciplinary learning goals and the role of physics in non-physics students' science education
