# Plan skill training using both local power-law progress tracking and global sequences of discrete strategy transitions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that training be planned using the two-process view of learning: monitoring progress "locally, within smooth PL trajectories, and globally, within sequences of discrete improvements". Concretely, if advanced strategies depend on mastery of simpler ones, learners could track progress on each power-law piece and transition at appropriate points, avoiding both superfluous practice on earlier strategies and premature attempts at later ones. The strategy rests on the article's PPL findings and is a forward-looking recommendation, not a tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners or systems must be able to track progress on each power-law piece of the learning curve
#### Constraints
- The article states future discoveries about transitions are needed to trigger transitions at specific times; the recommendation is not experimentally validated in this study

### Target Learners
- adult learners acquiring cognitive or perceptual-motor skills

### Target Learning Goals
- skill acquisition and strategy progression within a task
