# Future work should evaluate LLM performance on Part B, viva-style and clinical scenario assessments and study effects on trainee learning outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article sets a forward research agenda for AI in surgical education: performance on "Part B written papers, viva examinations and OSCE-style clinical scenarios" remains uncharacterised, as does the effect of prompting strategy such as chain-of-thought reasoning or retrieval-augmented generation on subspecialty accuracy. Integration into curricula also requires prospective study of impact on trainee learning outcomes, self-directed study behaviour and examination success.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal assessment across successive model versions to determine whether performance gains continue, plateau, or diverge over time
#### Constraints
- The present evaluation was limited to Part A written multiple-choice questions and point-in-time snapshots of rapidly evolving systems

### Target Learners
- urology trainees
- medical education researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- applied clinical reasoning
- operative decision-making
- examination success
