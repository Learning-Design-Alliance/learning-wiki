Teach voice choice through topic-based rules: active when the agent is the topic, passive when it is not¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The instructional treatment taught two rules for voice selection. The first links sentence topic to voice: "If the topic is the agent, the active voice is used. If, alternatively, the agent is not the topic... the passive voice is used." The second teaches that the passive is used when the speaker does not want to mention the agent, for reasons such as the agent adding no new information, being unimportant, or being unknown. Both groups received these rules with the same materials.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Feature-focused instruction concentrated on a specific grammatical structure with the same language data across conditions
  • Practice phases including input-based activities, controlled exercises, and semi-communicative production

Constraints¶

  • The standard treatment establishes a link between subjects and topics without providing semantic justification for it

Target Learners¶

  • EFL university students
  • learners receiving form-focused grammar instruction

Target Learning Goals¶

  • appropriate selection of active or passive voice according to topic and communicative intent

Claims¶

  • CG-based grammar instruction produces significant short- and long-term learning gains in use of the English active and passive voice [+W]
  • Focused audio-appended reading tasks produced significantly higher written accuracy than unfocused tasks for the rule-bound structure (passive voice) on the posttest [+W]
