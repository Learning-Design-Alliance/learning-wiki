# Use investigator-initiated (pushed) ICT delivery with theory-based design to enhance intervention efficacy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends designing ICT interventions on behavior change theory and delivering materials via an investigator-initiated strategy, in which an investigator delivers information to participants via ICT at fixed times rather than leaving access decisions to participants. The authors note the "investigator-initiation strategy uses a 'pushed' approach", sending automatic and specific materials directly to participants' email or SMS, which they argue makes interventions more compatible with existing routines. They conclude that "combining the use of behavior change theory and investigator-initiated strategies could be associated with enhanced intervention efficacy and larger effect sizes."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Intervention content should be grounded in a health behavior change theory (e.g., social cognitive theory)
- Materials are delivered automatically to participants' email or SMS at fixed times, venues, and conditions
#### Constraints
- The review could not confirm the effect of ICT initiation strategy on intervention exposure and adherence rate since these data were not available in most included studies; more studies are needed before solid conclusions

### Target Learners
- children aged 6-12
- adolescents aged 13-18

### Target Learning Goals
- physical activity behavior change
