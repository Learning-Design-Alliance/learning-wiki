# Use G-theory variance-component analysis as a quality-control procedure to decide the number of stations, test versions, and examiners needed

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends applying G-theory to summative and formative exams as a quality-control mechanism: it identifies which facets (stations, versions, sites, students) are the greatest sources of measurement error and lets decision makers "determine the number of stations, test versions, and examiners needed to obtain reliable measurements". D-studies built on the G-study predict how reliability changes when the number of stations or other facets is altered.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Variance-component estimates from a G-study
- Expertise in G-theory and suitable software (e.g., JMP, urGENOVA, SAS, G_String, SPSS)
#### Constraints
- In this study only one examiner per station was used, so interexaminer reliability could not be determined

### Target Learners
- medical students undertaking summative end-of-career assessment

### Target Learning Goals
- reliable summative assessment of clinical competence
- assessment quality improvement
