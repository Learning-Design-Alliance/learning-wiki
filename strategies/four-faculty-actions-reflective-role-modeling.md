# Four faculty actions to advance role modeling by targeting students: warn against uncritical imitation, share doubts and uncertainties, correct the hidden curriculum, and weigh role modeling in recruitment and promotion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that faculty enhance role modeling's value by targeting students as well as role models: "warn students against their tendency to uncritically imitate any of their preceptors; second, make students realize that their preceptors share their doubts and uncertainties; third, gain an insight into, and correct, undesirable features of the clinical setting’s hidden curriculum"; and finally develop recruitment and promotion policies that consider role modeling within an overall evaluation of strengths and weaknesses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty must gain insight into the undesirable messages of their clinical setting's hidden curriculum and institutional culture.
#### Constraints
- The author states these actions complement, and do not replace, targeting role models themselves and improving faculty performance.

### Target Learners
- medical students
- clinical faculty and preceptors

### Target Learning Goals
- critical assessment of preceptors' professional behaviors
- discerning which observed behaviors are worth imitating
