# Plan institutional interventions to reduce the out-of-course rate in first-cycle programmes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the analysis identifies significant benefits of graduating on time for the continuation decision at Master level, the authors recommend that institutions plan factual interventions targeting the out-of-course phenomenon. They propose "a more accurate revision of the course programmes and of the corresponding credits and/or a diﬀerent organization of the exam sessions during the a.y." as concrete measures. The strategy is grounded in the finding that late graduates have a higher probability of withdrawing after the Bachelor degree, and it aims to keep students on schedule so that more of them progress to second-cycle study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional capacity to revise course programmes and credit allocations and to reorganize exam sessions within the academic year
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one school (Economics) at a single Italian university; the authors note the MD regulation variable can be reinterpreted in other national contexts but the study itself is institution-specific

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate Bachelor students at risk of graduating beyond the legal course duration

### Target Learning Goals
- Timely completion of the first-cycle degree and progression to Master-level study
