# Adopt the formative exam approach and student-by-exam heat-map analysis in other courses to advise students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend transferring the formative approach and its student-by-exam heat-map analysis to other courses, using the heat maps to identify students whose performance patterns place them at risk of overestimating mastery and to advise them accordingly. They state: "Because this formative approach and the associated student-by-exam analysis (Figure 3) are independent of the course content, they can be easily adopted into other courses." Instructors can also show all students the possible learning patterns and explain the risks of flickering.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Weekly comprehensive exams with a grading scheme allowing early failures without penalty, immediate feedback, and heat-map tracking of individual performance across exams.
#### Constraints
- The qualitative interview portion of the study was limited and cannot be generalized to the whole class; the authors call for expanding it and for disaggregating the intervention's components.

### Target Learners
- first-year students in introductory science courses
- advanced students, based on prior success in an upper-division cell biology course

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained improvement on higher-order exam problems
- accurate self-assessment of mastery and metacognitive study planning
