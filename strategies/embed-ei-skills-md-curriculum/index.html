Embed EI skills explicitly in the MD curriculum or target them through professional development training¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that because EI is only weakly related to academic success, medical programs should deliberately develop EI-related skills rather than assume they follow from academic performance. They state EI learning outcomes "could be achieved either by infusing EI skills into the curriculum or through special training modules", citing a critical review supporting EI-based education for professionalism and communication skills.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • EI learning outcomes defined within the MD competency framework

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on correlational findings; the authors note their findings do not allow causal inferences

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate medical (MD) students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • empathy, communication, interpersonal sensitivity, emotion recognition and management

Claims¶

  • The EI–academic success effect size varies significantly by EI subscale, with perceiving emotions highest (r = .20) [+W]
  • The EI–academic success effect size varies significantly by EI test, with EQ-i showing the highest correlation (r = .14) [+W]
  • Emotional intelligence is significantly but weakly positively correlated with academic success in undergraduate MD programs (r = .13) [+W]
  • The authors argue academic grades are an unsuitable performance measure of EI in medical school because EI relates more to interpersonal and professional performance [+W]
