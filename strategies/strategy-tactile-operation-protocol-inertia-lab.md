# Run the inertia lab through a tactile operation protocol: disk selection, touch-positioning, rope winding, and button-pressed audio readout

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A step-by-step protocol for autonomous operation of the apparatus: "several acrylic disks of diﬀerent dimensions are presented" for tactile selection; the student identifies the cylinder axis and a small hole in the disk by touch to position it concentrically; double-contact tape couples disk and cylinder; the student winds the rope, confirms rotation via the beep-beep alarm, and presses B1 for the audio reading.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Disks must offer tactile positioning cues: the cylinder axis and a small hole in the acrylic disk
- Two small pieces of transparent double-contact tape prevent the cylinder slipping in rotation relative to the disc
#### Constraints
- The student rotates the disk with one hand while the free hand keeps the hanging mass from hitting the pulley and in a suitable free position

### Target Learners
- blind engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- operating laboratory equipment and acquiring experimental data autonomously
