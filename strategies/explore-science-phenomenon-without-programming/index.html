Design use-stage simulations so PSTs can explore the science phenomenon without programming¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's fourth design principle calls for simulations that allow exploration of the science phenomenon without programming, for example by including gliders in Scratch to vary parameters. The authors state this "facilitated exploring the scientific parameters of the program without programming", and that PSTs "focused more on the science behind the model when the simulation was based on physical laws such as Newton's laws".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Simulations with visual parameter controls such as Scratch gliders, and code based on natural-science models rather than geometrical representations

Constraints¶

  • The qualitative results suggesting strengthened TCK focus are tentative, as the authors note this "may strengthen the PSTs' focus on TCK"

Target Learners¶

  • Pre-service science teachers at the master's level

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Technological content knowledge (TCK) for simulation-based inquiry in science education

Related Strategies¶

  • Use coding cards to scaffold the modify-stage toward modeling-based inquiry
  • Challenge PSTs to develop both a simulation and an accompanying lesson plan
  • Use UMC rather than PRIMM to scaffold PSTs' learning with programmable simulations in science education
  • Keep initial TK-requirements low using block-based platforms and easily graspable simulation code
