# Diagnose scientific reasoning early in the semester and orient activities toward transitional and concrete reasoners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 associational, 1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Administer the Lawson Classroom Test of Scientific Reasoning in the first weeks of the semester, classify students as concrete, transitional or formal reasoners, and design classroom activities consistent with that profile. The article states: "In light of this diagnostic, obtained early in the semester (week 3), we orientated classroom activities to likely favor students with transitional or concrete reasoning skills." This tailoring is the mechanism the authors credit for the reduced academic risk of non-formal reasoners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Early-semester administration of the Lawson Classroom Test of Scientific Reasoning
- A literature-based activity selection process aligned with students' reasoning levels
#### Constraints
- The measured benefit applied to transitional reasoners; concrete and formal reasoners showed a null effect

### Target Learners
- first-year university students with heterogeneous reasoning profiles

### Target Learning Goals
- progressively improve reasoning skills
- reduce academic risk in introductory physics
