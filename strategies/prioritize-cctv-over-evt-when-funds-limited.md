# When funds are limited, provide a CCTV rather than eccentric viewing training for clients whose primary goal is reading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that low vision rehabilitation agencies allocate limited resources toward CCTV provision for clients whose primary rehabilitation goal is reading, because the trial showed greater early impact on reading speed from CCTV than from eccentric viewing training. As the conclusion states, "In most situations where funds are limited, it seems that providing CCTV would be more effective for people's primary goal than would eccentric viewing training." Both interventions are expensive, so agencies must choose.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Client's primary goal is reading
- Client has age-related macular degeneration with visual acuity in the range studied and has already received a low vision assessment and optical devices
#### Constraints
- Based on early indications from a trial with only 10 completers
- Eccentric viewing training may still have a place for improving near acuity with own glasses and low-acuity daily tasks

### Target Learners
- adults with moderate to advanced age-related macular degeneration whose primary rehabilitation goal is reading

### Target Learning Goals
- improved reading speed and performance of daily reading tasks
