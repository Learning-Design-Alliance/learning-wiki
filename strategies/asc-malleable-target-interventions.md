# Target academic self-concept through interventions and teaching practices, given its demonstrated malleability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because most ASC variation over time lies in the partly stable autoregressive component rather than the completely stable trait, the authors argue ASC is changeable during students' school careers. Their Educational Impact and Implications Statement recommends that "because academic self-concept changes over students' school careers, it is thus malleable and can be targeted by specific interventions and influenced by teaching practices." They further suggest that more fixed beliefs about mathematical ability could be challenged by teachers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of which ASC components are stable versus changing, to assess intervention potential
#### Constraints
- The STARTS model itself does not identify which specific factors drive change; the intervention implication is the authors' interpretation

### Target Learners
- primary and secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- fostering academic self-concept as an educational goal
