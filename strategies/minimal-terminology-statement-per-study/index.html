Attach a minimal terminology statement (term, operational definition, hypothesized pathway) to each study¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that each study include a minimal "terminology statement" combining a term, a brief operational definition, and a hypothesized pathway, so that translation to practice is explicit and confusion with superficially similar but mechanistically different practices is reduced. They also suggest journals require a Key Terms Definition table or boxed glossary for interdisciplinary submissions, listing how central terms are defined and operationalized, with notes on intended scope.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Brief operational definitions (what counts as embodiment here), hypothesized pathways (e.g., off-loading, attention, sensorimotor simulation), and stated boundary conditions (for whom, for which content domains, and under what classroom constraints)

Constraints¶

  • Such a requirement would not police theoretical positions; rather, it would make authors' usage explicit, help reviewers evaluate construct validity, and reduce unintentional scope drift across papers

Target Learners¶

  • education and health practitioners
  • curriculum developers
  • policymakers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • translating embodiment research into practice with mechanistic clarity

Claims¶

  • Loose use of movement-based learning language risks conflating evidence-based embodied learning with debunked neuromyths [+W]
  • Fragmented vocabularies bias AI-mediated literature retrieval toward the user's home discipline [+W]
  • Instruments show conceptual inconsistency: the same terms label different competence aspects, and disciplinary origin does not significantly correlate with definition or terminology choice [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Develop a community-maintained living glossary of embodiment terminology on an open-science platform
  • Broaden search terminology and bibliographic databases in future eWIL reviews
  • Future AI-SRL research should broaden search strategies, include educators and younger learners, address motivation, and apply longitudinal designs
  • Study curiosity broadly as information-seeking in ethological context rather than fixing a strict definition first
  • Establish standard definitions and mission-aligned competencies for admissions
