# Provide short, distributed booster sessions during the early clinical phase to consolidate instrumentation competence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because cleaning quality declined similarly under both schedules as students prioritised speed during first patient care, the article proposes "short, distributed booster sessions during the early clinical phase might help consolidate competence", explicitly as a hypothesis for a future controlled study. A pragmatic hybrid combining the compact block with such boosters is described as supported by current educational frameworks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Booster sessions must be scheduled during the clinical semesters following preclinical training, when the speed–accuracy trade-off emerges.
#### Constraints
- The article states this implication "should be regarded as a hypothesis for a future controlled study" — it has not been tested in this study.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students in their first periodontal patient courses

### Target Learning Goals
- consolidation of subgingival instrumentation skill across the preclinical-to-clinical transition
