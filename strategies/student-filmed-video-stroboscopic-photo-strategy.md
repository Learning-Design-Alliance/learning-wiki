# Use student-filmed videos of everyday phenomena to build stroboscopic photos for motion analysis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete recipe: students record the phenomenon with a smartphone or digital camera, load the video into Tracker, apply the Strobe filter with attenuation 0,00 and an appropriate step size, and export the superposed image for analysis. The article recommends extending the approach beyond the presented examples to "el movimiento circular", wave pulse propagation, and pendulum motion, and notes it adds another use of smartphones to interact with the physical world beyond known sensors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Videos obtained with certain care in filming; Tracker software; for circular motion, simply marking a point of the rotating system suffices
#### Constraints
- The presented examples are only some of the possible experiments; the article does not report classroom implementation data for the strategy

### Target Learners
- secondary school physics students
- pre-service physics teachers (project context on active methodologies with ICT and smartphones)

### Target Learning Goals
- analysis of different types of movement, including one- and two-dimensional kinematics and electromagnetic discharge phenomena
