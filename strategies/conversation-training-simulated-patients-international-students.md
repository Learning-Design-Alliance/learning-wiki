# Offer conversation training with simulated patients to prepare international medical students for patient encounters

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend conversation training with simulated patients as a targeted remedy for international students' weaker communicative OSCE performance, even after roughly four years of study in the host country. They argue such training is "a good opportunity to better prepare them for patient encounters" and "might help to counteract these deficits" in conversations with patients, which carry important implications for the doctor-patient relationship.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained standardized patients and a setting in which students can practise communicative abilities in realistic patient encounters
#### Constraints
- The recommendation follows a retrospective, exploratory study that could only support speculations about causal links; the authors call for further research on the reasons behind the performance differences

### Target Learners
- International medical students in the clinical phase of study

### Target Learning Goals
- Conversational and communicative competence in patient encounters
