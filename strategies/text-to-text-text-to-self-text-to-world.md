# Text-to-Text, Text-to-Self, Text-to-World

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Text-to-Text, Text-to-Self, Text-to-World (often abbreviated T-T, T-S, T-W) is a comprehension strategy popularized by Keene and Zimmermann's work on proficient reader thinking. Learners are prompted to connect a text to other texts they have read (comparing themes, structures, or characters), to their own lived experience (relating events and emotions to personal memory), and to broader world issues and current events. Connections can be prompted before, during, or after reading — to activate relevant knowledge, sustain engagement, or consolidate understanding.

## Design Implications

The strategy works by activating prior knowledge and prompting elaboration: articulating a connection requires learners to relate new information to existing schemas, which is a form of self-explanation [Eliciting self-explanations improves understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. However, the quality of the connection matters more than its presence — connections must be genuinely elaborative rather than superficial associations.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A text worth connecting to, with enough thematic or experiential surface for all three connection types
- Guiding questions or sentence stems for each connection type (e.g., "This reminds me of… because…")
- A recording structure — journal, two-column chart, or sticky notes — so connections are externalized and can be discussed ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md))
- Instructor modeling of what a *meaningful* connection looks like, contrasted with a trivial one

#### Constraints
- Superficial connections ("I have a dog too") do not improve comprehension and can consume reading time without benefit [~M] — without modeling, learners default to low-effort personal associations
- Text-to-self connections can pull attention away from the text itself; readers who over-personalize may substitute their own experience for the author's meaning, distorting comprehension [~W]
- For learners with little relevant experience (e.g., reading about unfamiliar cultures or historical periods), text-to-self prompts may frustrate rather than activate [~M]
- The strategy targets engagement and elaboration, not decoding or vocabulary; it will not remediate weak word recognition [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [-W] — learners still struggling at the word level need decoding support first

#### Implementation Variability
- **Timing:** pre-reading activation, during-reading annotation, or post-reading reflection and discussion
- **Format:** oral turn-and-talk, written journals, graphic organizers, or digital annotation tools
- **Weighting:** literature classes often emphasize text-to-text (intertextual themes); content-area classes emphasize text-to-world (application to current issues)
- **Choice-based:** learners select which connection type to pursue, supporting autonomy [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Upper elementary through secondary readers who have fluent decoding but need support constructing meaning
- Reluctant readers, for whom personal relevance increases engagement and persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Less useful for beginning readers still building [Automatic word recognition](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md), or for advanced readers who already make connections spontaneously [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension: integrating text with prior knowledge to build a situation model
- Critical literacy: text-to-world connections link texts to social and civic issues
- Intertextual reasoning: text-to-text connections build genre awareness and thematic comparison
- Reflective thinking: text-to-self connections support metacognitive and affective engagement

### Instructions
1. Select and assign the text ([Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md)); optionally preview it with an [Advance Organizer](../elements/advance-organizers.md) framing the three connection types.
2. Model one connection of each type aloud with a short mentor text, contrasting a deep connection with a superficial one.
3. During or after reading, have learners record connections using stems ("This reminds me of…", "This is like…", "This connects to what's happening in…"), optionally with [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) directly in the text.
4. Facilitate [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) or peer sharing in which learners explain *why* a connection matters to understanding the text — the explanation step is where elaboration happens [Eliciting self-explanations improves understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S].
5. Extend with an [Application](../elements/application.md) task, such as writing a text-to-world argument or comparing two texts on a shared theme.

## Examples
- **Reading workshop (grades 6–12):** Students keep a three-column connection log while reading a novel; columns feed small-group discussion and a reflective essay.
- **[Newsela](https://newsela.com)** — Leveled news articles paired with prompts that invite text-to-world connections to current events.
- **[Facing History and Ourselves](https://www.facinghistory.org)** — Humanities curriculum that systematically connects texts to historical and contemporary world issues (text-to-world as the organizing move).
