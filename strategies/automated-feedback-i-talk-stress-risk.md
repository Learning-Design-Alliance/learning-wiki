# Use automated feedback systems to identify student teachers at risk of intense stress and burnout based on their I-talk in written reflections.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that automated feedback systems could build on the finding that increased self-focus in written reflections is associated with greater physiological stress. Such systems could identify student teachers who repeatedly experience elevated stress and are thus at risk for depression and burnout, complementing existing feedback on the quality of their written reflections.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Written reflections from student teachers that can be analyzed for first-person singular pronoun frequency
#### Constraints
- The association between I-talk and stress was established in a VR context and its generalizability to non-virtual classroom environments has not yet been tested

### Target Learners
- student teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- stress identification
- burnout prevention

## Claims

- [Student teachers’ heartrate during VR teaching is positively associated with their subsequent use of first-person singular pronouns in written reflections.](../claims/i-talk-physiological-stress-vr-teaching.md) [+W]
- [Student teachers’ use of subjective first-person singular pronouns in written reflections predicts an increase in physiological stress in a subsequent VR lesson.](../claims/i-talk-predicts-increase-physiological-stress.md) [+W]
- [The use of objective first-person singular pronouns predicts self-reported stress in a subsequent VR lesson, but this association is explained by prior physiological stress.](../claims/objective-pronouns-predict-self-reported-stress.md) [+W]
