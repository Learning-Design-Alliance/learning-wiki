Implement lecture recording with joint staff-student agreement on how the recording object should be used, and give students guidance on its use¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that implementations of lecture recording 'should therefore be done with a view to creating a joint understanding of how the lecture recording object should be used by all parties in order to provide effective tools for learning'. It suggests staff and students could jointly review key recordings to revisit topics, particularly in revision sessions, reframing the lecture as a jointly-negotiated space. The author notes that guidance for students on using lecture recordings has up to now been limited.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Explicit communication between staff and students about what a lecture is for and how recordings should be used

Constraints¶

  • The author states implementation may be limited where technical issues become an insurmountable hurdle, and that staff may need a continued embrace of open education and intellectual property understanding

Target Learners¶

  • first-year undergraduate students
  • university lecturing staff

Target Learning Goals¶

  • effective use of recorded lectures as learning and revision tools

Claims¶

  • The literature review finds no consistent significant effect of lecture recording on student attendance or attainment [~W]
  • Staff worried that recorded lectures become canonised material that students cite verbatim and rely on too heavily [~W]
  • Staff framed lectures as performance and entertainment while first-year students framed lectures as a tool for study and revision [~W]
  • First-year students valued recorded lectures as a safety net for illness, caring responsibilities and accessibility barriers [+W]
  • Technical unpredictability of recordings severely frustrated students who valued them, while staff treated technical issues as abstract and eventually solved [~W]
