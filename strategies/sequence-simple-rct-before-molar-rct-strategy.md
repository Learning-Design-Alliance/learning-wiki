# Sequence undergraduate endodontic clinical training from simple anterior and premolar RCTs before molar RCT, with conservative molar management and post-graduation training for molar RCT

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that undergraduate endodontic clinical training emphasize simple RCTs on anterior and premolar teeth and conservative/emergency molar management (vital pulp therapy, access cavity and coronal pulp extirpation, incision and drainage), with molar RCT training after graduation. They argue "Inexperienced clinicians may benefit from a wider exposure to simple RCTs of anterior and premolar teeth before they can embark on the more technically demanding molar RCT." This follows their findings of insufficient molar RCT quality and low student confidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clinical training based on competencies rather than a minimum number of performed procedures, as the European Society of Endodontology guidelines state
#### Constraints
- The authors note this procedure may require further training, mentorship and experience that may not be ideally delivered during undergraduate training

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students

### Target Learning Goals
- performing root canal treatment safely and competently
- assessing tooth restorability and treatment complexity
- recognizing when referral to a specialist should be considered
