# Combine recurrence- and case-oriented research pragmatically (choosing paradigms that answer the question) or dialectically (creating tensions between assumptions for a more complex depiction)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes two ways researchers in PER take up both paradigms for the same phenomenon. Pragmatically, the choice of paradigm and method should "'work best' to meet the practical demands of a particular inquiry" and answer the research question. Dialectically, "researchers may also take up paradigms dialectically (Greene and Caracelli 1997; Rocco et al. 2003), so as to purposefully create tensions between assumptions and methods that allow for a more complex – and in some cases more realistic – depiction of a phenomenon." The authors argue such combination is not a third paradigm.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Researchers must be able to hold or move between commitments spanning both paradigms, whether committed primarily to assumptions or primarily to aims and methods
#### Constraints
- The authors state that taking up both paradigms does not constitute a third paradigm under Greene and Caracelli's definition

- physics students
- physics faculty
