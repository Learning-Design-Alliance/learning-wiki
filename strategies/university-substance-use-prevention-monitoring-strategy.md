# Universities should run substance-use prevention and intervention programmes and anonymously monitor student substance use over time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that universities address substance use among students through prevention and treatment programmes and ongoing anonymous monitoring. The authors state "Universities should consider prevention and intervention programmes for substance use" and that "Universities should anonymously monitor substance use among students over time to try to understand the extent of the phenomenon." They cite evidence that motivational interventions and some behavioural strategies, including digital interventions such as telemedicine, could reduce student substance use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Anonymous monitoring of student substance use over time; programmes tailored to motivational and behavioural approaches
#### Constraints
- The authors note one cited study found general prevention programmes were unlikely to be successful, with awareness rising alongside increased partying and drug use; effectiveness of these programmes in higher education remains to be tested

### Target Learners
- university students

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing substance use to protect academic achievement and student retention
