# Target factors related to parental education in interventions aimed at reducing child ADHD symptoms and academic problems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that, because associations between parental education and child ADHD and academic problems persisted after accounting for shared familial risk, factors related to parental education may be actionable intervention targets. They write: "If substantiated, these findings imply that interventions enhancing parental education could improve mental health and academic success in children at risk". They also suggest mediators such as living conditions, understanding of children's needs, and parental practices as additional targets, and note that identifying mediators common in low-education groups may have higher preventive potential than intervening directly on educational level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Substantiation of causal effects of parental education, which the authors state the quasi-experimental design cannot prove
#### Constraints
- Parental education explained only ≈1% of outcome variance
- In populations similar to the study, interventions aimed at parental education are likely to have no or only small effects on children's depressive symptoms
- If effects reflect relative rank rather than absolute educational level, raising one group's education could come at other groups' cost

### Target Learners
- school-aged children (8 years old) at risk of ADHD symptoms and academic problems

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing child ADHD symptoms and academic problems
