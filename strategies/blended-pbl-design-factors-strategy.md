# Consider ice-breakers, asynchronous content, netiquette and teaching presence when designing blended PBL

## Description
The article reports de Jong et al.'s recommendations for developing and implementing blended PBL in health education master-level programmes: "educators should carefully consider several factors in blended PBL education including ice‐breakers ('getting‐to‐know‐activities at the start of education'), asynchronous content ('lectures that are suitable for distance learning purposes'), netiquette ('guidelines for behaviour in online sessions') and teaching presence ('the importance of visibility and feedback during education')". The article's conclusion echoes this, advising educators to focus on engaging introductory activities, asynchronous content, and maintaining visibility and feedback.
