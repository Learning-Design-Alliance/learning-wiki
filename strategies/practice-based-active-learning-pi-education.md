# Prioritise practice-based, active learning over passive exposure to pressure injury material

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that education about pressure injuries should be built on practice-based, active learning approaches rather than passive exposure to reading or one-off training. Its stated rationale is that the survey found "nurses' previous education or literature review did not have a significant impact on pressure injury knowledge scores," which "indicates that passive prevention strategies may be inadequate." The article states this "emphasises the importance of incorporating practise‐based, active learning approaches" for this professional learning context.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training should embed learning in practice rather than rely on self-directed reading or passive educational material.
#### Constraints
- Derived from a cross-sectional, self-administered online survey; the authors note it cannot establish causality or evaluate interventions.

### Target Learners
- Practising nurses without wound care certification, including those in low-risk units

### Target Learning Goals
- Evidence-based pressure injury prevention knowledge and practice
