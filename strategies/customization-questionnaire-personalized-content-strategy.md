# Personalize e-learning content through an entry customization questionnaire and organize topics in graduated complexity levels

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's implementation strategy is to ask learners, at entry to the application, questions about the care recipient's dementia level and the caregiver's preferences, energy, and time availability, then adjust the information level shown accordingly. This item earned the highest pedagogical score (4.23 ± 0.99), and the authors argue personalization "allows the caregivers to tailor not only the content but also the level of information according to their individual needs and time allowance." Pairing this with the four-level complexity ordering (quick fixes to more details) is the article's concrete recipe for adaptive content delivery.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- an interactive questionnaire capturing dementia stage, preferences, energy, and time availability
- content database structured in graduated complexity levels
#### Constraints
- rated in a prototype with limited contents; tested only with experienced caregivers carrying a high burden

### Target Learners
- informal caregivers of people with dementia

### Target Learning Goals
- matching educational content to learner situation and capacity
- self-directed learning about dementia care
