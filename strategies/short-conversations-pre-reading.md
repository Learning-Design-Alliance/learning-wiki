# Short Conversations (Pre-Reading)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Learners are given one sentence drawn from a text they are about to read and engage in a short paired conversation about it. A rule constrains each turn to no more than three words, forcing learners to distill ideas, negotiate meaning, and build on each other's contributions. The activity serves as a pre-reading [Activation](../principles/activation.md) routine: it surfaces prior knowledge, generates curiosity about the full text, and primes relevant vocabulary before independent reading.

## Design Implications

The three-word constraint is the design's engine. By limiting each turn, it prevents any single learner from monologuing, distributes airtime evenly, and pushes learners toward compressed, high-value phrasing — a form of [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) applied to oral language. Pre-reading conversation activates relevant schemas, which improves comprehension of the subsequent text [Activating prior knowledge improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]. The constraint can also produce productive struggle: learners who cannot say what they mean in three words must re-analyze the idea, creating a knowledge gap the reading can then resolve [Cognitive disequilibrium motivates conceptual change.](../claims/cognitive-disequilibrium-motivates-conceptual-change.md) [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A pre-selected sentence from the reading that is generative — ambiguous, provocative, or central to the text's argument — not merely decorative
- Clear modeling of the three-word rule before learners begin ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) norms)
- A debrief or bridge into the reading so the conversation connects to the text rather than ending as an isolated game

#### Constraints
- The word limit frustrates learners with limited vocabulary in the topic domain, who may stall rather than elaborate [-W] — pair with [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md) supports for multilingual learners
- A poorly chosen sentence (trivial or too opaque) produces shallow or off-target talk; the activity inherits the quality of the selection
- The constraint suppresses extended reasoning; it is a primer, not a substitute for full [Discussion-Based Learning](../patterns/discussion-based-learning.md) afterward [-W]
- Conversations can drift to speculation unsupported by the text if no debrief anchors them back

#### Implementation Variability
- Adjust the limit (two words for higher challenge, five for lower proficiency) to tune difficulty
- Use a sequence of two or three sentences to build a mini-dialogue arc before reading
- Replace speech with written three-word exchanges on sticky notes or a shared doc for quieter groups
- Run it whole-class as a "three-word chain" where each learner adds three words to a collective response

### Target Learners
- Adolescents and adults reading informational or argumentative texts; the compression demand suits learners who can already form basic sentences
- Multilingual learners benefit from the low-stakes oral rehearsal, provided vocabulary support is available [~W]
- Less suitable for beginning readers, for whom the constraint adds load without sufficient language resources to compensate [-W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Activation of prior knowledge and prediction before reading [Activating prior knowledge improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Concise verbal expression and summarization
- Collaborative meaning-making and listening (turn-taking under constraint)

### Instructions
1. Select one high-leverage sentence from the upcoming reading and display it.
2. Model one three-word exchange with a volunteer to demonstrate the rule.
3. Pair learners; each partner takes turns offering three-word responses to the sentence for 2–3 minutes.
4. Debrief: collect two or three pairs' best three-word phrases on the board.
5. Transition into reading, asking learners to check their phrases against the text ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) can frame what to look for).

## Examples
- Before reading a news article on artificial intelligence, pairs discuss the sentence "AI is transformative," trading three-word turns ("changes every job," "hype or real?"), then read to test their claims.
- In an ELA class, the line "The road not taken" is discussed in three-word turns before reading Frost's poem, surfacing assumptions the poem then complicates.
