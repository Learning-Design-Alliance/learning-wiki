# Augment BLS training with more hands-on manikin time, CPR feedback devices, dyad training, and distributed monthly practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 6 studies (3 review, 2 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 6 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose concrete redesigns of BLS training to remedy observed compression-depth and ventilation weaknesses. They suggest "increasing the amount of hands-on time students have with the manikins during BLS training", cite evidence that "using CPR feedback/prompt devices or similar teacher-to-student CPR feedback during hands-on training improves performance", and propose dyad training in pairs with shared discussion plus distributed monthly practice with real-time feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to manikins for additional hands-on practice time
- CPR feedback/prompt devices or instructor feedback during hands-on training
- Pairing structures for dyad practice and scheduled regular practice sessions
#### Constraints
- Dyad training evidence cited is from ACLS training, and the authors state further studies are warranted to test it for BLS compression and ventilation parameters

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- Physician assistant students

### Target Learning Goals
- Deliver guideline-adherent chest compressions
- Deliver adequate ventilations during CPR
