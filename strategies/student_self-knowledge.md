# Student Self-Knowledge

## Description
Student self-knowledge involves helping learners understand their strengths, interests, and areas for growth as learners. It is a foundational element of self-advocacy, as individuals cannot effectively advocate for themselves if they do not understand their own needs and preferences. This practice helps to intentionally incorporate voice and choice into learning experiences, which is critical for making learning meaningful and relevant.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires the use of self-assessment tools, opportunities for reflection, and a supportive classroom environment that values learner voice.
#### Constraints
- Some learners may struggle with self-assessment or may be hesitant to share their thoughts and feelings. Educators need to create a safe and supportive environment to encourage honest reflection.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Learners in grades 3-8, but applicable to all ages, who need to take more responsibility for their learning.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Facilitate learners' understanding of their strengths, interests, and areas for growth, and support the development of self-awareness and self-advocacy skills.

### Affordances
- Increases learner motivation and engagement, empowers learners to make informed choices about their learning, and promotes a sense of ownership and agency.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted by using different types of self-assessment tools, providing a variety of learning options, and tailoring instruction to individual learner needs.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor learners' self-assessment skills, track their use of self-advocacy strategies, and gather feedback on their experiences with different learning options.

## Impact
- Research shows that self-awareness and self-advocacy are linked to improved academic outcomes, increased self-esteem, and greater success in post-secondary education and employment. (Edutopia article on Getting Students on the Road to Self-Advocacy)
