# Co-create contextualized AI literacy policies via multi-stakeholder task forces with phased roadmaps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The brief recommends that districts address AI literacy gaps through comprehensive institutional strategies prioritizing infrastructure and human capacity. AI task forces that bring together educators, administrators, learners, and families can "develop coherent and contextualized AI policies" covering data privacy, security, bias mitigation, and tool evaluation standards, building community buy-in. Task forces should also develop institutional roadmaps with a clear vision, strategies, and phased implementation plans, keeping humans in the loop as AI is designed, adopted, and used.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Effective AI literacy policies require input from those who will implement and be affected by them
#### Constraints
- The brief states successful implementations require adaptation to local contexts, resources, and community needs rather than a one-size-fits-all approach

### Target Learners
- District and school communities, including PK-12 learners, educators, leaders, and families

### Target Learning Goals
- Systems-level responsible AI integration and coherent AI literacy implementation across schools and districts
