# Use statistical-mode and induce-mode interactive questioning, especially for large classes, rather than passive or simple vote modes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article distinguishes five versions of interactive mode: passive mode (rhetorical "Is it so?" questions), vote mode (yes/no answers allowing guessing), statistical mode using clickers with instant statistical feedback, induce mode using question sets that lead students to accept new ideas, and student initiative mode welcoming unexpected student questions. After attending workshops demonstrating the modes, the authors conclude that "The latter two modes or similar seem to be quite efficient and are worth to be recommended," noting statistical mode "is especially effective for large classes" because it can accurately locate problems and control lecture progress.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors willing to create an atmosphere of free communication and accept that students are the center of learning
- Clicker equipment and designed question sets for statistical mode
#### Constraints
- Student-initiative interaction "is considerably valuable but is not easy to handle" and can make instructors uneasy; every instructor may choose a suitable mode

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in large introductory physics lectures

### Target Learning Goals
- Active participation, inquiry, and identification of unexpected problems in class
