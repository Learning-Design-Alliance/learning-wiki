# Run student research projects as realistic research environments with minimal teacher instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy places high school students in an environment as close as possible to a graduate student physics lab: students determine their own research subject, apply for research funds, write a paper and present at conferences, framed by the authors as an example of situated learning. The teacher deliberately avoids detailed instruction to keep the activity spontaneous, giving only minimum instruction on experimental technique and theory. The authors report that "providing an opportunity of realistic research is very eﬀective to promote their motivation for science" and to develop technical and social skills required for scientific research.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Giving enough freedom to students to choose their subject is important.
- The teacher must not give detailed instruction, so that the students' activity stays spontaneous; the teacher gives only minimum instructions for experimental technique and theoretical issues.
#### Constraints
- Finding enough time for research will be very difficult except for science club students, though inquiry-based activity within usual physics class or a summer-vacation project remain possible.
- Opportunities such as conference travel largely depended on SSH support for travel expenses, which are not available in ordinary schools.

### Target Learners
- high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- motivation for learning science
- technical and social skills required for scientific research
