# Recommendations for culturally responsive school belonging research: broader cross-cultural sampling, stronger methodologies, multilevel theorizing, and qualitative/mixed methods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The commentary closes with an agenda for moving culture to the center of school belonging research, "including broader cross-cultural sampling, stronger methodologies, explicit multilevel theorizing, and greater use of qualitative and mixed methods". It argues research should ask students how they understand belonging in their own contexts rather than assuming translated instruments capture the same construct everywhere, and should extend to materialist contexts shaped by poverty, conflict, and climate shocks where belonging may hinge on basic concerns such as safety and physical access.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements

#### Constraints
- The commentary notes the infrastructure required to produce such research might not be present in Global South societies

### Target Learners
- Students worldwide, especially in underrepresented Global South and Asian contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- Building a globally inclusive, culturally grounded science of school belonging
