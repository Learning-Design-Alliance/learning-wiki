# Use beat synchronization assessment and early music and rhythm training as early indicators and supports of reading readiness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that nonverbal rhythm assessment, possibly combined with the neural FFR metric, can identify children at risk of reading struggle before formal schooling. Based on differences in preliteracy skills and auditory processing in children as young as 3 years old, the authors "suggest that interventions, which may support these areas of development (e.g., music and rhythm training), be implemented early in childhood".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment requires a conga-drum drumming task administered by an experimenter who reliably produces synchronous rhythms to a pacing tone, with hits recorded and processed via circular statistics
#### Constraints
- Classification reliability is reduced for children who synchronize at only one rate, where attentional difficulties, fatigue, or tempo preference may confound performance; the relationships were established in typically developing children, not yet those with language disorders

### Target Learners
- preschool children aged 3–5 years

### Target Learning Goals
- preliteracy skills: phonological awareness, auditory short-term memory, rapid automatized naming
