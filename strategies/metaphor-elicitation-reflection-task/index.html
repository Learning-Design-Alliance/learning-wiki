Use metaphor-based reflection tasks to elicit learners' perceptions of educational technology, especially from introverted or culturally reticent students¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends asking students to capture and present their thoughts through carefully chosen visual images — metaphors — rather than relying only on questionnaires and interviews. It argues this is "more precise, concise, innovative or awareness-cultivating as the metaphor" and that metaphoric thoughts "can be used in academic training to address the necessity of apply -ing different approaches to di erent types of students". The method was administered in learners' native language (Mandarin) with an example metaphor discussed beforehand.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An example metaphor should be provided and discussed verbally before students complete the task; collection in the learners' native language is advised since metaphoric data could only be obtained effectively that way

Constraints¶

  • Metaphors are usually culturally based, and metaphoric analysis is not free of critics, per the authors' caution

Target Learners¶

  • introverted, reflective, or culturally reticent learners in higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • eliciting deep reflective perceptions of feedback and educational technology to inform teaching

Affordances¶

  • Delayed Uptake Online Feedback

Claims¶

  • Asking introverted Chinese learners to express perceptions of online feedback through metaphors elicited more profound reflection than direct questions [+W]
  • Learners' metaphoric perceptions of online feedback coded into six analogous characteristics describing how online feedback benefited them [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use metaphor-based reflective tasks as a recurring part of language teaching
