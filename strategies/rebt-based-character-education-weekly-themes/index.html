Strategies

Use structured character education activities based on Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy with weekly themes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The intervention used activities based on Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy (Vernon, 1989), organized into five weekly themes: self-acceptance, feelings, beliefs and behavior, problem solving/decision making, and interpersonal relationships. Each daily 30-minute session divided time between discussion and stimulus activity. The article reports that "The activities were run in an interactive manner; in that they were student-centered where students were encouraged to brainstorm and communicate with their friends whenever possible."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Daily 30-minute sessions with 15 minutes discussion and 15 minutes stimulus activity
  • Teacher training in character education delivery
  • Pilot testing of activities to examine pace, duration, and level

Constraints¶

  • The five-week implementation period may be too short to bring about significant changes in self-esteem levels of both ability groups.

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary students (grade 3)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Self-acceptance
  • Emotional regulation
  • Problem solving
  • Interpersonal relationships

Claims¶

  • Gender differences in self-esteem change under character education: boys increased while girls decreased [+W]
  • The main effect of character education program on self-esteem was non-significant compared to traditional instruction [~W]
  • The gap in self-esteem between intellectually able and less able students narrowed after character education [+W]
  • Character education activities improved self-esteem of intellectually less able students but decreased self-esteem of intellectually able students [~W]
  • The traditional instructional program was associated with a decrease in self-esteem for intellectually less able students [-W]
