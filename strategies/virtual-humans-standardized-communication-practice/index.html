Use virtual humans to standardize communication training scenarios and allow safe repetition of emotionally charged conversations¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends virtual human simulation as a means of providing "a safe environment in which to practice, reflect, and become adept" in communication skills. Virtual humans offer standardization — scenario context and virtual human behavior are precisely specified at any moment — and learners can repeat scenarios and explore different choices, confident the virtual human performance will remain consistent with pedagogical design requirements.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Virtual human agents capable of verbal and nonverbal interaction, system detection and recording of learner responses, and consistent scripted behavior across repetitions.

Constraints¶

  • The article notes research showing transfer depends on similarity between training and novel scenarios; greater dissimilarity yields poorer transfer.

Target Learners¶

  • Medical students
  • Healthcare professionals in communication training

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Advanced communication skills
  • Handling emotionally charged patient encounters
  • Nonverbal communication awareness

Claims¶

  • Students trained with MPathic-VR report significantly more positive attitudes toward their learning experience than computer-based learning students [+W]
  • MPathic-VR training produces a significant advantage on the OSCE nonverbal communication scale, suggesting particular value for acquiring nonverbal skills [+W]
  • MPathic-VR-trained students achieve significantly higher composite scores than computer-based learning students on a subsequent advanced communication OSCE, several days later [+W]
  • Qualitative reflections indicate MPathic-VR students valued immediate feedback, nonverbal skill teaching, and preparation for emotion-charged encounters, while CBL students reported lack of interactivity and information overload [+W]
  • Second-year medical students improve their intercultural and interprofessional communication performance between first and second run-throughs of MPathic-VR scenarios [+W]
