# Address digital well-being with empathy, root-cause understanding, asset framing, and tech-free spaces

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report offers recommendations for adults and educators addressing digital well-being with youth. Adults should reflect on their own digital habits to foster empathy, respond with genuine curiosity, and validate difficulties rather than immediately offering solutions; invest time in understanding root causes before jumping to solutions; and "Shift from deficit-focused, fear-based conversations about all screen time or social media use being "bad" toward more nuanced questions like "How might we use technology intentionally to support our goals and well-being?"" The report also recommends expanding access to low-tech and tech-free spaces with structured opportunities for youth to reflect on their digital experiences, and taking a community-based approach that acts on what is learned from youth expertise.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Create regular, structured opportunities within low-tech and tech-free environments for youth to process and reflect on their digital experiences and how technology may affect their well-being
