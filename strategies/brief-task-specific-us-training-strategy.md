# Use brief task-specific training with handheld ultrasound devices to enable rapid procedural skill acquisition in novices within limited rotations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a goal-directed, skill-focused curriculum using handheld US devices for novices with limited training time. The strategy uses "a short instructional video, live demonstration by a supervisor, and hands-on practice on a model" within a 25-minute session, followed by a practical assessment. The authors state handheld devices' portability and rapid availability facilitate both clinical application and medical education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supervisor (attending anesthesiologist and senior resident) provides live demonstration and real-time feedback during supervised practice.
#### Constraints
- Authors state "comprehensive US education and sustained practice remain essential" beyond rapid skill acquisition, and future training should incorporate multiple cannulation techniques such as the in-plane approach.

### Target Learners
- Medical students on short clinical rotations
- Novice ultrasound users

### Target Learning Goals
- US-guided peripheral venous cannulation
- Self-confidence in using handheld ultrasound
