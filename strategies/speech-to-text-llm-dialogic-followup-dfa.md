# Enhance DFA in-depth informativity by eliciting oral student talk via speech-to-text and dialogic follow-up questions generated with large language models

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes exploiting digital potentials to include two benefits of diagnostic interviews into group assessment: eliciting oral student talk through speech-to-text to reduce typing load, and exploring dialogic modes by asking refined follow-up questions to make students elaborate on explanations. Machine learning and large language models could automatically evaluate open-ended answers and generate item-specific follow-up prompts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The implementation must be developed carefully for each item, also to ensure the later acceptance of this feature by teachers
#### Constraints
- Students' individual explanations are often initially vague and not explicit, which hinders a valid diagnosis

### Target Learners
- Grade 5–7 students who struggle in mathematics

### Target Learning Goals
- Explaining meanings and justifying reasoning
