Rearrange existing practice problems into interleaved assignments rather than writing new materials¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that developers of mathematics instructional materials implement interleaved practice by reordering the problems they already have, rather than authoring new content. They state that "creators of mathematics texts and other instructional media can provide interleaved practice by simply rearranging practice problems," and that the intervention "could be implemented at all levels of mathematics instruction."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A bank of practice problems organized by lesson or problem kind, so problems from previous lessons can be redistributed into later assignments

Constraints¶

  • Tested in seventh grade mathematics with four focal problem kinds; the abstract reports no other levels of instruction

Target Learners¶

  • mathematics students at any level of instruction, per the authors' conclusion

Target Learning Goals¶

  • longer retention of mathematics skills through mixed practice

Claims¶

  • The experiment used a counterbalanced crossover design in which the same practice problems were rearranged into blocked and interleaved versions [+W]
  • Interleaved mathematics assignments nearly doubled final test performance compared with blocked assignments in seventh grade (72% vs. 38%, d = 1.05) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Teachers can create interleaved assignments from existing blocked textbook materials by pulling one problem from each of many assignments
  • Include a small block of problems of a new skill at the start of an assignment, then interleave the rest
