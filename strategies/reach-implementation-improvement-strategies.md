# Allow lead-in time for schools, provide ongoing TA support beyond initial training, and segment comprehension material into shorter, more varied pieces.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The report draws implementation recommendations from its process evaluation: schools need lead-in time before delivery, TAs need ongoing support after the five days of initial training, and the language comprehension material should be more varied and broken into shorter pieces. The report states "Schools in the second phase , which had more notice of the interventions’ introduction, were noticeably more prepared than those in the first phase." It also notes the 35-minute sessions were not well matched with standard one-hour school lessons.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- TAs receive the five days of initial training plus ongoing support from the project team
#### Constraints
- The 35-minute session length was reported as not well matched with standard one-hour school lessons

### Target Learners
- Year 7 and 8 pupils with reading difficulties in secondary schools

### Target Learning Goals
- reading accuracy
- reading comprehension
