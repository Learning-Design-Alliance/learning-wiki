# Strengthen final-year nursing preparation through competency-based, simulation-supported education focused on clinical skills, critical thinking, and ethics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends strengthening the integration of theoretical knowledge with clinical training: field-specific ethics and law courses, regular case discussions on ethical dilemmas, and fostering critical thinking through simulations, laboratory practices, and clinical case studies. It argues that "Collaboration with clinical nurses, timely feedback, and the integration of case-based learning and simulation exercises can strengthen students' decision-making abilities." It also recommends structured supervised hands-on training in patient monitoring and equipment safety, where students' error tendency was highest.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires collaboration between nursing schools and clinical institutions, including in-service training for supervising nurses and scientifically validated monitoring tools with individualized feedback
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a single-university descriptive-correlational self-report study; the authors note limited generalizability and possible social desirability and Dunning–Kruger biases

### Target Learners
- final-year undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- professional competence
- patient safety and medical error prevention
- critical thinking and reasoning
- ethical decision-making
