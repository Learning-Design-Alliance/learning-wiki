# Universities should develop emergency plans with psychological support interventions and resilient infrastructures to protect academic performance in future crises

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their finding that a quarter of students reported academic decline and over half felt the need for psychological support, the authors recommend that "universities should develop emergency plans aimed at ensuring a robust support system for academic needs". They further state that "emergency plans should certainly include psychological support interventions" and that creating resilient infrastructures and promoting strong online communities could mitigate negative effects of disruptions to in-person teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- University-level institutional capacity to mount emergency plans, psychological support services, and online community infrastructure before or during a crisis.
#### Constraints
- Recommendation derives from a cross-sectional survey at a single university; the design prevented causal conclusions, as the authors state.

### Target Learners
- University students

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining academic performance and mental well-being during institutional disruptions
