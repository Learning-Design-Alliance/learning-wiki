# Run the smartphone measurement challenge with a curated method subset, low-cost tinkering materials, and a collective comparison and debriefing session

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend implementing the challenge by choosing a subset of the 61 methods that fits pedagogical objectives, available time and material, giving students access to a wide variety of low-cost objects and letting them tinker their setups, and organising a collective comparison afterwards. They report that "Taking the time to organise a collective comparison and a debriefing session after the measurements is a key point", and that students clearly enjoyed getting out of the lab to do measurements with direct concrete meaning to daily life. Subsets can target uncertainties or themes such as free fall, pendulum or acoustics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A subset of methods chosen to fit pedagogical objectives, available time and material; a supply of low-cost objects (cords, tubes, tapes, foam, papers, straws, balls, balloons, playdough); time reserved for a collective comparison and debriefing
#### Constraints
- When the top of the building is not accessible, the height of an accessible window or indoor balcony can be measured; if going out of the classroom is not an option, a room height works albeit mundanely; the challenge can also run remotely with students experimenting at home

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students
- high-school physics teachers
- general public

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental design and protocol decisions
- assessing measurement precision
- peer discussion of what most precise means
