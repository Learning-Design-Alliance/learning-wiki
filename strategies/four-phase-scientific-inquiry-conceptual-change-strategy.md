# Implement four-phase scientific inquiry with conceptual change in physics lessons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Teachers can implement scientific inquiry learning model based on conceptual change by structuring physics lessons in four phases: posing an area of investigation to elicit and confirm current ideas, having students structure the problem to expose them to conflicting evidence, guiding students to construct new explanations, and having students apply and evaluate new explanations while reviewing changes in ideas. This strategy moves students away from their initial ideas toward explanations aligned with accepted scientific explanations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must structure lessons to include all four phases in sequence
- Students must be given opportunities to investigate genuine problems and propose evidence-based explanations
#### Constraints
- The strategy was tested in physics learning at senior high school level in Indonesia
- The study compared it against conventional learning, not against other inquiry-based or conceptual change approaches

### Target Learners
- Senior high school students (Class XI) studying physics

### Target Learning Goals
- Physics cognitive competence
- Science process skills
- Conceptual change toward scientifically accepted explanations
