# Open the teaching sequence with 'trigger' questions that problematize familiar observations, such as the Moon appearing in the daytime sky

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy is to implement "perguntas 'disparadoras' que convidem os alunos a lançar suas ideias sobre o tema", in order to surface prior knowledge and reveal students' explanations, including contradictory answers without apparent conflict for them. The authors highlight the appearance of the Moon in the daytime sky, contrasted with the '28 fases' images with night-sky backgrounds, as a valuable problem resource. Problematizing familiar observations obliges students to employ their knowledge and confront new perspectives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems must be relevant to and consistent with the notions to be taught, defining the didactic path
#### Constraints
- The authors note the Moon may be absent from the daytime sky on the day corresponding to the full Moon, and the '28 fases' image set excludes the new-Moon day

### Target Learners
- primary and secondary science students
- pre-service primary school teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- recognize that the Moon is observable during the day as well as at night
- articulate and revise prior explanations of lunar phases
