# Use combined training modules integrating extracted teeth, artificial/simulative teeth, and phantom bodies in endodontic preclinical curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that students "spend substantial time with training through a range of these educational models using a variety of learning resources from endodontic replicas, 3D-printed or injection-moulded, extracted teeth, and phantom bodies or heads". It proposes "the use of combined modules for endodontic training by integrating feedback from students, dentists, and educators", arguing that hybrid and simulative models bridge preclinical simulation and clinical application.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Availability of artificial/simulative models with root canal morphology, phantom bodies or heads, and a supply of extracted teeth
#### Constraints
- Cost of procuring artificial teeth was perceived as a disadvantage to applicability by 57.5% of respondents and would influence choice for 56%

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dentistry students
- postgraduate endodontic trainees and residents

### Target Learning Goals
- psychomotor skills for root canal treatment stages
- accurate perception of root canal treatment procedures
- self-efficacy before clinical training
