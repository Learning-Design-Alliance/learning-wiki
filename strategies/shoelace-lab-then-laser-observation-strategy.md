# Run the shoelace pathlength exercise as a hands-on lab in under an hour, followed by laser observation of the optical pattern

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors prefer expanding the demonstration into a hands-on lab exercise in which students move the paperclip holding the laces, match colors, and record y values and pathlengths where the waves align. The exercise "can easily be completed in less than an hour, leaving time for students to also observe the optical double-slit interference pattern using a laser", at which point the approximations' validity should be clear.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Shoelace apparatus with numbered wavelength repetitions
- A laser double-slit setup for the follow-up observation
#### Constraints
- If space is limited, place the slits toward one side of the table so the whole width is available for measurements to the other side of the center line, enabling measurements at higher θ

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting a macroscopic pathlength model to the optical double-slit pattern
- checking the validity of interference approximations

## Claims

- [Students commonly hold four misconceptions about double-slit interference, the most damaging being that constructive interference requires both pathlengths to be integer multiples of the wavelength](../claims/four-double-slit-interference-misconceptions.md) [+W]
