# Explicit Teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Explicit teaching is a structured approach in which the teacher clearly shows students what to do and how to do it. The teacher decides on the learning intentions and success criteria and makes them transparent to students, demonstrates them by modelling, checks for understanding throughout, and at the end of each lesson revisits what was covered and ties it together. It is carried out through short segments of instruction, guided practice with high rates of student response, and immediate corrective feedback.

## Design Implications

Explicit teaching works because it reduces unguided search and manages working memory load during initial skill acquisition [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]. Its effectiveness depends on sequencing: modelling must be followed by guided practice with feedback, then independent practice, since explanation alone produces shallow encoding [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S]. Meta-analytic evidence shows strong positive effects on achievement, particularly for foundational skills [Stockard et al. (2018) meta-analysis of Direct Instruction] [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clearly defined learning intentions and success criteria communicated to students ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md))
- A modelled demonstration with reasoning made visible ([Direct Instruction](../elements/direct-instruction.md))
- Frequent checks for understanding with immediate corrective feedback
- High rates of student response during guided practice, not passive listening

#### Constraints
- Pure exposition without student activity is ineffective; explicit teaching must incorporate active responding [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [-S] — lecture-only variants of explicit teaching underperform interactive ones
- Less effective for learners with strong prior knowledge, for whom extensive guidance becomes redundant [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]
- Can anchor learners to a single taught method, limiting transfer to novel problems if examples are not varied [~M]
- Overly long instructional segments overload working memory [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Direct Instruction (DI)** — scripted, tightly sequenced programs with choral responding (e.g., Engelmann's *Reading Mastery*)
- **Explicit Direct Instruction (EDI)** — teacher-authored lessons following a modelling–guided practice–independent practice cycle
- **Rosenshine's Principles** — a flexible set of teaching behaviours (small steps, questions, models, checks) rather than a script
- **Faded guidance** — moving from full modelling to partial prompts to independent work as competence grows

### Target Learners
- Novices and learners with limited prior knowledge, who benefit most from structured guidance [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]
- Struggling learners and students with learning difficulties, for whom unguided discovery is least effective [~S]
- Less beneficial for advanced learners, who may experience the expertise-reversal effect [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural skill acquisition: decoding, arithmetic operations, grammar, lab techniques
- Foundational conceptual knowledge that later supports transfer
- Strategy use: teaching cognitive and metacognitive strategies step by step

### Instructions
1. State the learning intention and success criteria in student-friendly language ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md)).
2. Activate relevant prior knowledge briefly before new content ([Activation](../principles/activation.md)).
3. Model the skill in small steps, narrating decisions aloud ([Direct Instruction](../elements/direct-instruction.md)).
4. Guide practice with frequent questions, prompts, and checks for understanding ([Provide Guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md)).
5. Release students to independent practice with feedback, fading support as accuracy grows.
6. Close by reviewing and connecting the lesson's content to prior learning.
