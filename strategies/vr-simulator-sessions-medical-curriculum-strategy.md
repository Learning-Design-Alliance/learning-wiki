# Integrate VR simulator sessions into the medical curriculum to build IR proficiency and spark career interest

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend raising students' exposure to interventional radiology through hands-on simulator practice, suggesting educators "elevate the exposure of IR during medical education by implementing a rotation in IR or hands-on practice to improve understanding of the field for recruitment." Repetitive simulator sessions with recorded objective metrics (virtual fluoroscopy time) let novices practice complex procedures safely and repeatedly, potentially improving skills, reducing radiation exposure, and fostering career interest in a specialty with workforce shortages.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An endovascular VR simulator with recorded performance metrics
- An identical standardized introduction to the simulator and procedure before the first exercise
- Guided sessions with mentor feedback
#### Constraints
- The authors position simulators as most suitable for students and beginners focusing on basic skills, noting experienced interventionalists do not benefit significantly in direct comparison; transfer to real patient interventions still requires validation

### Target Learners
- medical students at various stages (second to sixth year) without prior IR experience

### Target Learning Goals
- endovascular catheter-manipulation proficiency
- reduced fluoroscopy use
- awareness of and interest in interventional radiology as a career
