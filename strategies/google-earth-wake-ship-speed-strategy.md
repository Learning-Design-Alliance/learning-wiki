# Have students determine ship speeds from Google Earth wake images and check results against known boat speeds

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes a classroom activity in which students analyze Google Earth photographs of boat wakes to compute ship velocity from wake wavelength and angle. The authors recommend that, if possible, one should look for pictures of boats used for public transportation in the school area, and ask the students to compare their calculations with a typical speed of the boat they analyzed. The analysis can even be done with ruler and compass on a printout.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Google Earth images of boats with visible wakes, and a basic knowledge of water waves and kinematics
#### Constraints
- The authors note this limited search may not allow for the best choice of images

### Target Learners
- physics students in secondary or introductory university settings

### Target Learning Goals
- apply kinematics and wave velocity relations to real imagery
- measure quantities from photographs and validate calculations against independent data

### Affordances
- Kelvin Wake Speed Relation
