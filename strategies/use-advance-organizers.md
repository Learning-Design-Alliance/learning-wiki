# Use Advance Organizers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
An advance organizer is introductory material presented *before* the main learning content at a higher level of abstraction, generality, or inclusiveness than the content itself. Introduced by Ausubel, organizers come in two forms: **expository** organizers (new conceptual scaffolding for unfamiliar material) and **comparative** organizers (explicit links between new material and prior knowledge). They may be prose, outlines, concept maps, or analogies, and are carried out by framing the lesson's structure and anchoring concepts before details are introduced.

## Design Implications

Advance organizers work by providing ideational scaffolding: they give learners a subsuming framework into which new, otherwise arbitrary-seeming details can be integrated, reducing the load of encoding disconnected facts [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]. Meta-analytic evidence shows small but consistent positive effects on learning and retention, strongest when the organizer is well-matched to the material and learners lack relevant prior knowledge [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]. The organizer must be genuinely more general and more familiar than the content — a mere summary or list of objectives does not function as an organizer.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Analysis of what relevant prior knowledge learners already hold, so the organizer anchors to something real ([Activating Prior Knowledge](activating-prior-knowledge.md))
- An organizer that is *more abstract and inclusive* than the lesson content — a concept, model, or analogy, not a preview of the same level of detail
- Explicit connection during the lesson back to the organizer, so learners use it to subsume new material rather than forgetting it

#### Constraints
- Effects are near zero when learners already possess well-organized prior knowledge for the topic — the organizer is redundant and adds load [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [~M]
- A vague or overly abstract organizer that learners cannot relate to anything familiar provides no anchoring and can confuse [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [-W]
- Organizers that merely preview content at the same level of detail (objectives lists, summaries) do not produce the effect [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [-M]
- Benefits fade when the organizer is presented but never referenced again during instruction

#### Implementation Variability
- **Expository organizer**: a new anchoring concept for unfamiliar material (e.g., defining "homeostasis" before teaching specific physiological feedback loops)
- **Comparative organizer**: linking new material to known material (e.g., "photosynthesis works like the Calvin cycle you learned for respiration, but reversed")
- **Graphic organizer**: a concept map or skeleton diagram learners complete during the lesson
- **Analogical organizer**: a familiar-domain analogy that maps structure onto the new topic ([Analogies](analogies.md))

### Target Learners
- Novices or learners with weak or disorganized prior knowledge in the domain, who lack structures to subsume new material [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]
- Learners facing large volumes of unfamiliar, hierarchically organized content
- Less useful for advanced learners with well-structured domain knowledge, for whom the organizer is redundant [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Meaningful verbal learning: retention and comprehension of interrelated concepts
- Schema formation: building an inclusive structure before filling in details
- Transfer preparation: organizers that highlight structural relations support later analogical application [Analogical reasoning improves transfer.](../claims/analogical-reasoning-improves-transfer.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Identify the core anchoring concept or model the lesson depends on, and what learners already know that can serve as a foothold ([Activating Prior Knowledge](activating-prior-knowledge.md))
2. Draft an organizer that is more general, more familiar, and shorter than the lesson itself — a concept, analogy, or skeleton structure ([Analogies](analogies.md), [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md))
3. Present the organizer first, in a few minutes at most, before any detailed content ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md))
4. Explicitly revisit the organizer at key points in the lesson, placing each new chunk of content into the framework ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md))
5. Close by having learners use the organizer — complete the concept map, extend the analogy, or restate the framework in their own words ([Application of Knowledge](../elements/application-of-knowledge.md))

## Examples
- **Ausubel's original studies** — presenting a 500-word expository passage on the metallurgy of steel before a 2,500-word lesson on steel composition, significantly improving subsequent learning.
- **Biology instruction** — introducing "energy flows through ecosystems; matter cycles" as a one-sentence organizer before teaching food webs, nutrient cycles, and energy pyramids, then tagging each topic back to that framework.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** unit introductions and "what you'll learn" concept overviews that situate individual videos within a topic map.
- **History teaching** — using a comparative organizer ("revolutions in this unit follow the pattern: fiscal crisis → elite defection → mass mobilization") before studying the French and Russian revolutions.
