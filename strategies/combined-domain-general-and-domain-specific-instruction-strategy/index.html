Strategies

Combine direct teaching of mathematics- and reading-specific skills with instruction building domain-general skills for children with concurrent mathematics and reading difficulties¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that interventions for children with co-occurring mathematics and reading difficulties pair domain-general skill building (working memory, attentive behavior, processing speed) with direct teaching of foundational reading and mathematical knowledge. They state that "approaches aimed at building working memory, processing speed, calculation skill, and attentive behavior, in the context of direct teaching of mathematics- and reading-specific skills, may have greater impacts on academic development than those that do not address these additional domain-general processes." They also suggest NC tasks may serve as an early screener of longer-term academic difficulties beyond mathematics, such as reading.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interventions must address domain-general processes alongside domain-specific academic instruction, per the authors' recommendation.

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is based on longitudinal mediation findings in early-elementary children; the authors note findings are applicable only to early-elementary aged children and cannot be generalized to children with severe reading deficits.

Target Learners¶

  • Early-elementary children with concurrent mathematics and reading difficulties

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Foundational calculations, word-problem solving, and word-reading skills

Claims¶

  • Early working memory, processing speed, and attentive behavior predict later calculations, word-problem solving, and reading, but nonverbal reasoning is not significantly associated with any outcome [+M]
  • Numerical cognition assessed at end of first grade statistically mediates associations between early processing speed, working memory, calculation skill, word-problem solving, and attentive behavior and later calculations, word-problem solving, and reading [+M]
  • End-of-first-grade numerical cognition directly predicts later calculations, word-problem solving, and word reading, with the strongest relation to calculations [+M]
  • Crossover effects: early reading fluency predicts later mathematics outcomes more strongly than early mathematics skills predict later reading [+M]
