# Embed ICT-supported short narratives in the engagement, exploration, and evaluation phases of the 5E learning cycle

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable teaching recipe: compose short fables aligned to curriculum acquisitions, have them reviewed by language and science experts, convert them into animated cartoons, and play them within a 5E (Engagement, Exploration, Explanation, Extension, Evaluation) learning cycle. The article reports that "instructions conducted with the help of ICT-supported short stories in the phases (especially engagement, exploration and evaluation) of learning cycle", over six weeks at four hours per week alongside the course and activity books.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Narratives must cover the curriculum unit's acquisitions and be reviewed by language and science experts
- Conversion to animation requires computer expertise, software knowledge, and time
#### Constraints
- Preparing the stories "requires computer skills and abilities and software knowledge and time", which was a cost and limitation of the study

### Target Learners
- 6th-grade middle school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Force and Motion achievement
- attitudes towards science
- science self-efficacy
