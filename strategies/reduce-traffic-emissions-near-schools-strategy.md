# Prioritise interventions to reduce traffic and emissions near schools, with indoor air filtration as a complementary opportunity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends reducing children's exposure to traffic-related air pollution at school as a route to protecting both general health and cognitive outcomes. It states that interventions "to reduce traffic and emissions near schools should priority to improve the general health of school pupils as well as cognitive outcomes", and that where this is not feasible, "indoor air filtration systems may provide a valuable opportunity for interventions to improve air quality" and thereby cognitive outcomes. Schools are framed as a focussed intervention point influencing large numbers of children at once.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful study design and measurement of confounding variables are described as essential for assessing the impact of changes to the school environment.
#### Constraints
- Reducing traffic near schools may not always be feasible, per the review; other modifiable indoor factors such as ventilation and volatile organic compounds also affect children, and accurate measurement of air pollution within schools is challenging.

### Target Learners
- primary-school-aged children

### Target Learning Goals
- executive function (working memory, attention)
- academic achievement
