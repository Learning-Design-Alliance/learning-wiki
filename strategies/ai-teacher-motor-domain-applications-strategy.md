# Deploy AI teacher models for guided practice with real-time feedback in writing, art, music, and VR/AR motor training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's Applications in Education section proposes adapting the AI teacher model to domains involving gentle motion and precision. It suggests the model "can break down these techniques into manageable tasks, offering learners real-time feedback as they practice" in art, guide finger placement and bowing in music, and be embedded into VR/AR environments to teach effective motor task execution. Immediate feedback is claimed to "accelerate skill acquisition, boost confidence" and let educators focus on higher-order teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires adapting the model to the target motor domain, as the article states the model "can be adapted to teach other motor skills, particularly those involving gentle motion and precision"
#### Constraints
- The AI teacher was never compared side-by-side with seasoned human educators, and translation to physical robots may introduce hardware challenges

### Target Learners
- beginners in art or design courses
- music students
- VR/AR training users

### Target Learning Goals
- fine motor technique acquisition such as handwriting, brush strokes, instrument playing, and VR gesture skills
