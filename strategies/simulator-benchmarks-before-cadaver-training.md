# Sequence laparoscopic training from box trainers and simulators with preset proficiency benchmarks before cadaver training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends a staged progression in laparoscopic education: trainees first reach preset proficiency levels on box trainers and virtual reality simulators, then apply those skills to human cadaver training before live surgery. It states that "the transition to human cadaver training begins with achieving preset benchmarks for laparoscopic skills on both virtual and physical laparoscopic simulators". In the study, residents completed a mandatory simulation course with preset proficiency levels before the hands-on Fix4Life course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to box trainers and virtual or physical laparoscopic simulators with preset proficiency levels
- Supervised hands-on cadaver course as the subsequent stage
#### Constraints
- Box and virtual trainers focus on technical skills such as hand-eye coordination and do not train procedural skills to the same extent

### Target Learners
- junior surgical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- laparoscopic technical and procedural skills
- safe transition to live surgery
