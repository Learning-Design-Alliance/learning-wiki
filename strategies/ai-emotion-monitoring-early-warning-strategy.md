# Deploy AI-based emotion monitoring and early-warning systems plus adaptive academic support platforms to promote student mental health

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that, alongside conventional measures, schools "can build artificial intelligence-based emotion monitoring and warning systems to monitor students' emotional state changes in real time and issue timely warnings when abnormalities are detected". Such systems would integrate wearable physiological data (heart rate variability, sleep quality) and online behavioral data, use machine learning to predict mental health risks, and trigger interventions such as care messages, counseling referrals or notifications to the mental health center. A companion platform would dynamically adjust learning plans based on academic performance and mental health status.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration of wearable physiological data and online behavioral data with machine learning analysis to identify high-risk students
- Automatic intervention mechanisms such as counseling recommendations or mental health center notification
#### Constraints
- Presented as a forward-looking proposal illustrated by external examples (e.g., the Woebot chatbot), not implemented or tested in this study

### Target Learners
- College freshmen and students at risk of mental health problems

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining good psychological state while pursuing academic achievement
- Timely identification of and intervention in mental health risks
