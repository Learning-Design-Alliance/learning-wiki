Three-phase educational trip procedure: preliminary preparation, site visit with observation, follow-up evaluation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q1–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article details an implementable field-trip recipe: before the trip the teacher obtains factual background and skills and gives pupils an outline and purpose; during the trip teacher and pupils visit study sites such as illegal dump sites, an erosion site, and Old Oyo National Park, with each pupil writing down observations and asking questions of the teacher or community members; afterwards pupils present and discuss observations and the teacher evaluates by questioning.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The teacher obtains the factual background and technical skills required to understand the specific purpose of the trip before the visit

Constraints¶

  • Educational trips are rarely used in schools due to lack of adult supervisors, reluctance to assume risk, and lack of pre-service preparation in planning and evaluation

Target Learners¶

  • primary school pupils (primary-5)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • environmental knowledge through first-hand observation of real-life settings

Claims¶

  • Educational trips produced higher adjusted environmental knowledge scores than service learning, which in turn exceeded control [+W]
  • Treatment and school location interact significantly: educational trips favour urban pupils while service learning favours peri-urban pupils [+W]
  • Instructional treatment has a significant main effect on primary pupils' environmental knowledge [+W]
  • The packet's authors assert that pre-, on-site, and post-visit activities build on students' newly gained knowledge when performed in series [+W]
  • School location has no significant main effect on primary pupils' environmental knowledge [~W]
