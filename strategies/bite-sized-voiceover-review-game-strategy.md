# Provide short, bite-sized voiceovers as preassignment material followed by engaging in-class review games after a brief Socratic review

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends providing "short, 'bite-sized' voiceovers as preassignment material, followed by conducting engaging and informative in-class review games after a brief review of the voiceover" as a way to engage future generations of learners. The brief review is conducted through Socratic exchange using a document camera, and the review game uses platforms such as Kahoot and PointSolutions with team-based competition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty need access to training and information technology support to create voiceovers and run review games
#### Constraints
- Student enthusiasm for flipped classrooms is not universal; some students prefer learning from an expert instructor rather than a colleague
- Flipping classrooms does not need to be an all-or-nothing approach

### Target Learners
- first-year and second-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- immunology content knowledge
- student engagement and satisfaction
