# Use empathy interviews as a natural, informal conversation to uncover students' experiences, emotions, and unmet needs in mathematics learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Empathy interviews, drawn from the design thinking tradition, are conducted as natural, informal conversations whose aim is "to reveal the user's perspective of their experiences and feelings, and to identify their unmet needs within a given context." Unlike diagnostic or clinical interviews, they are not intended to fix, assess, or remediate the user's experience. In the professional development, teachers interviewed their own students with disabilities, family members, or colleagues, and facilitators coached design teams to attend to the emotions captured in the interviews.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interviewers take the role of listeners rather than assessors; interviews focus on emotions and narratives about experiences
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that empathy can serve as a mechanism for pity or further oppression without mindfulness of differently positioned power

### Target Learners
- Students with disabilities in mathematics classrooms; also teachers and families as interview users

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding students' experiences and emotions in mathematics learning; building relationships; informing instructional redesign
