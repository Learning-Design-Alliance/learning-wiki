# Teach idioms through interactive, contextualized activities such as role-play and movie clips rather than isolated definitions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that role-play, movies, and sentence-use outperformed definitions, the authors recommend that teachers create innovative idiom-based activities and embed idioms in authentic contexts. They suggest teachers 'resolve to create innovative idiom-based listening-speaking activities and to teach and explain more idioms in authentic contexts', and propose projects giving learners opportunities to comprehend and use idioms in real-life situations. They also suggest trying new technologies, which offer learners an authentic context to comprehend the meaning of expressions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need access to authentic materials (e.g., movie clips) and class time for interactive role-play activities; the article notes eight one-hour sessions were used.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on one quasi-experimental study with 47 Iranian upper-intermediate/advanced learners; the authors note their findings might be challenged by studies favouring decontextualized word-list learning for beginning and intermediate learners.

### Target Learners
- EFL learners in language institutes and classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- idiomatic competence in comprehension and production
