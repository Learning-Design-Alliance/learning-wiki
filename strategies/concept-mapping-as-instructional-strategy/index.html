Use concept mapping as an instructional strategy in place of traditional teaching to raise academic success¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article supports recommending concept mapping as an instructional strategy because its meta-analysis found academic success "higher in concept mapping instructional strategy than in traditional teaching". Experimental groups received instruction using concept mapping while control groups were taught by traditional methods without concept mapping strategies.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Experimental use requires studies or lessons contrasting concept mapping instruction with a traditional-teaching control group

Constraints¶

  • The article notes the studies were heterogeneous, so the data could not be generalized to the whole population

Target Learners¶

  • Turkish primary, secondary and higher education students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic success in subject-matter courses

Claims¶

  • Fixed effects model estimate of concept mapping effect on academic success was large (ES=1.047) but studies were heterogeneous [+M]
  • Concept mapping instruction has a very large positive effect on Turkish students' academic success compared to traditional teaching (ES=1.119) [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Future meta-analytic research should examine concept mapping effects on attitude, retention, anxiety, and computer-based concept mapping
