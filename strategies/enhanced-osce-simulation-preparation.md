# Enhanced OSCE-specific preparation with realistic simulation sessions to reduce anxiety and improve performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A preparation strategy in which institutions give students extended, OSCE-specific training including realistic simulation sessions and intensive practical exercises before the examination. The authors recommend it because the more exhaustive prepandemic training (15 h with real clinical cases and full simulations) was associated with lower baseline anxiety and better performance. They state that "training programs that include realistic simulation sessions and intensive practical exercises are recommended, enabling students to become familiar with the OSCE format and dynamics".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training time and staffing to run simulation sessions and practical exercises before the OSCE
#### Constraints
- The postpandemic cohort's reduced 10-hour training eliminated in-person real-case work, which the authors link to the observed declines

### Target Learners
- Health sciences undergraduate students facing OSCE or other high-stakes practical assessments

### Target Learning Goals
- Reduced examination anxiety
- Improved practical clinical competence demonstration
