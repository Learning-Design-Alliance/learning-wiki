Deliver motor skill instruction to young children with ASD using the eight Classroom Pivotal Response Teaching components as the instructional framework¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The intervention used the eight CPRT components — antecedent strategies (student attention, clear and appropriate language, easy and difficult tasks, shared control, multiple cues) and consequence strategies (direct reinforcement, contingent consequence, reinforcement of attempts) — as the framework for delivering 1:1 motor instruction in gym and outdoor settings. The authors state "central to CPRT is that child motivation is instrumental in facilitating learning acquisition", so choices in materials were facilitated throughout. Fidelity of research assistants was assessed biweekly with a minimum of 80% required.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructor training via informational sessions on the eight CPRT components; biweekly fidelity checks with a minimum of 80%

Constraints¶

  • CPRT was developed for classroom settings and adapted here to a gymnasium and outdoor environment

Target Learners¶

  • preschool-aged children with autism spectrum disorder (4–6 years)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • fundamental motor skills (locomotor and object control)

Claims¶

  • The intervention produces significantly greater overall gross motor quotient gains than no intervention in young children with ASD [+W]
  • An 8-week intensive motor skill intervention produces significantly greater locomotor skill gains than no intervention in young children with ASD [+W]
  • The intervention produces significantly greater object control skill gains than no intervention in young children with ASD [+W]
