# Provide formal training and accreditation for surgical trainers to support effective feedback delivery, and evaluate such interventions robustly

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors call for systemic change in how trainers are prepared for feedback roles: the study "calls for further training to support trainers to deliver effective feedback" and notes that any such training intervention "should be robustly evaluated". They note consultants are given the title of trainer without formal training, some with no desire to teach, and state the GMC's planned formalised trainer-approved system is supported by these findings. They also urge open discussion between trainers and trainees about feedback's effects on well-being.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainers currently receive the trainer role without formal training, so training provision must be established
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a small single-region qualitative study; the authors call for a larger UK-wide study

### Target Learners
- consultant surgical trainers supervising trainees in the operating theatre

### Target Learning Goals
- effective delivery of constructive verbal feedback to trainees
