# Use simulation-based and video-based training and assessment as adjuncts where local experts and case volume are lacking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because novel techniques are initially performed by few experts and pilgrimage training to expert centers is impractical and unsustainable, the review recommends simulation-based training as a valuable adjunct that lets learners repetitively perform intended skills and adjust training to target specific skills with no risk to patients, particularly attractive for third-space endoscopy. Indirect assessment via video-recorded procedures enables careful review and debriefing, and video-based live instruction may allow mentoring from geographically distant locations, a need heightened by COVID-19 travel restrictions. Focused courses remain adequate only for minor skills and complementary for complex techniques.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Additional studies on simulator-based assessment tools with predictive validity before simulators can be used to assess endoscopic skill
- Animal laboratories are costly and not widely available, motivating simulation alternatives
#### Constraints
- Focused training courses should be regarded as only complementary in the training of more complex techniques; data on simulators as assessment tools are scarce

### Target Learners
- endoscopists in practice seeking new advanced skills without local experts
- advanced endoscopy fellows

### Target Learning Goals
- acquiring technical and cognitive skills for complex novel procedures in a low-risk environment
