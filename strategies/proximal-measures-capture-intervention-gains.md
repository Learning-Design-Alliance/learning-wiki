# Use proximal measures alongside standardized assessments to capture intervention learning gains

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future studies include multiple assessments of proximal measures closely aligned with the intervention, because "Proximal measures are more likely to capture significant learning gains that may not be revealed on standardized assessments". They note such measures could isolate which language-based skills and general comprehension strategies improve at particular points in an intervention, informing instructional changes. They also caution that proximal measures may be seen as too close to the instructional tasks, essentially teaching to the test.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reliable and valid proximal measures; the article states the lack of reliable and valid proximal measures is a critical barrier to advancing understanding of text-processing skills
#### Constraints
- Proximal measures could be said to be too close to the instructional tasks, so that one is essentially teaching to the test

### Target Learners
- middle grade struggling readers

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring vocabulary, key word and main idea, inferencing, and knowledge acquisition gains during comprehension intervention
