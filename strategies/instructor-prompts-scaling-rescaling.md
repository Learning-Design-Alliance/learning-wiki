# Instructor-mediated questioning prompts that move students from time to energy, from energy to mass-normalized energy, and toward interpreting the fitted constant

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A set of representative instructor prompts scaffolds each analytical step of the activity without giving the result in advance. To move from time to energy, the instructor may ask, "If no electrical power is supplied, does the temperature increase simply because time passes?"; to motivate mass normalization, prompts ask what quantity would express the energy input per unit water mass; after model construction, prompts direct attention to what Γ characterizes. The article emphasizes the prompts "are therefore examples rather than a script; which prompt is useful depends on what a group has already noticed." The instructor circulates among groups, examines their plots, and adapts questions to each group's progress, data quality, and prior knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor responsiveness to students' current framing and progress, as the article notes the success of such efforts depends in part on this responsiveness
- Real-time student plots, constructed with spreadsheet software during data collection, that the instructor can examine and discuss with each group
#### Constraints
- The prompts are representative examples rather than a verbatim script; which prompt is useful depends on what a group has already noticed
- When time is limited, reduced procedures are used and the predictive test may be omitted

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in an introductory physics laboratory course working in groups of two or three

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguishing elapsed time from accumulated input energy as the physically relevant variable
- motivating mass normalization of input energy through proportional reasoning
- interpreting what the fitted empirical constant characterizes physically
