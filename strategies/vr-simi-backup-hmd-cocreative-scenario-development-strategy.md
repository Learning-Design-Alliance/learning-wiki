# Prepare backup head-mounted displays and on-site technical knowledge for VR simulation sessions, and develop scenarios through co-creative processes with end-users

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The study reports practical implementation strategies for VR-based simulation sessions. Participants valued "quick access to a backup HMD and as a person with technical knowledge at hand", which was considered valuable but not essential. For content, the authors advise that "it may be advisable a ensure greater involvement of end-users in the development of such scenarios through co-creative or participatory processes", to address unrealistic scenarios and the limited number available for training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A backup HMD and access to a person with technical knowledge during sessions
- Scenario development processes that involve end-users (trainees)
#### Constraints
- Scenarios should depict situations the trainees will actually encounter alone; the two-therapist format was criticized as unrealistic in residents' experience

### Target Learners
- Resident doctors in child and adolescent psychiatry
- Healthcare professionals in simulation-based training programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- Reliable delivery of non-technical-skills simulation training
- Scenario content matched to trainees' real clinical situations
