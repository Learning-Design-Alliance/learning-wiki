# When teaching Galileo and the right answer, also teach Riccioli and the wrong answer

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete teaching move: alongside the standard Galileo narrative, introduce the Jesuit astronomer Giovanni Battista Riccioli, who diligently and rationally weighed the evidence of his day and favored Tycho Brahe's geo-heliocentric model. The author's recommendation: "So next time you talk to students about Galileo and the right answer, also mention Riccioli and the wrong answer. Your students will understand science better for it."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Some treatment of the Copernican Revolution or the history of the Earth's motion in the course
#### Constraints
- The essay reports results from the author's own physics and astronomy courses only; effects on skeptical students are his impression, not measured

### Target Learners
- physics students
- introductory astronomy students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding how science works
- appreciating that scientific conclusions depend on the evidence available at the time
