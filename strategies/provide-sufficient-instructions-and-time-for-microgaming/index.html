Provide sufficient instructions and additional time for microgaming sessions in elementary schools to ensure sustainable completion of briefing, playing and debriefing activities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's most critical implication is to "provide sufficient instructions and additional time for microgaming sessions in elementary schools to ensure sustainable completion of the briefing, playing and debriefing activities." This includes careful planning to ensure step-by-step microgame-based learning, with the debriefing having a direct relation to learning grades so students are more likely to finish. Teachers should also select microgames according to learning objectives and contents, and explore different scenarios.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need to select available user-generated microgames according to learning objectives and contents
  • Careful planning including providing sufficient instructions and times
  • The debriefing should have a direct relation to learning grades

Constraints¶

  • Application on an open platform requires registered users so that rejoining does not count as a new joining attempt

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reinforcement of learning content through short serious gaming sessions

Claims¶

  • The completion rate for the debriefing activity was minimum, with no more than 30% of total join times completing the debriefing [+W]
  • Students enjoyed learning by playing user-generated microgames despite disadvantages such as missing learning instructions and inadequate interfaces [+W]
  • Teachers designed microgame-based learning with varied microgames and debriefing methods but shared similar scenarios, inserting microgame-based learning into the middle of the main session [~W]
