# Organize course vocabulary by normal use rather than contrived semantically-related sets, and present SR items at different times or in different contexts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that SU presentation produced significantly better delayed retention, the author recommends that course designers organize words according to their normal use rather than in contrived SR sets, and that teachers try presenting SR items at different times or in different contexts. Students are also encouraged to explore ways to avoid interference when learning new words.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Vocabulary items chosen should be compatible with students' English level
#### Constraints
- Recommendation rests on one small-sample study (38 students) with meaning-translation tests only

### Target Learners
- senior high school EFL students

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of new second-language vocabulary

### Affordances
- Interference Theory Semantic Sets

## Claims
