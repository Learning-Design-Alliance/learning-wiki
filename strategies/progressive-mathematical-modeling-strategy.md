# Implement progressive mathematical modeling in simulations: students propose an initial model, predict, observe, and compare fit with simulated data

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (4 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author transferred Tracker's video-modeling pedagogy into EjsS simulations: students propose an initial model (e.g. X = 2*cos(t) or g = -6.67*500.00/r^2), the simulation immediately draws the predicted plot, then runs for observation, and the mismatch or closeness of fit between model and simulated data facilitates explanation and discussion. A "show me" dropdown displays the generalized solution to support teachers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation must accept student-proposed model expressions, plot them as predictions alongside theoretical formulae, and offer a show-me dropdown with the generalized form
#### Constraints
- The article notes this was a new approach not implemented in other EjsS and non-EjsS simulations found on the internet

### Target Learners
- Grade 11 / A-level physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Mathematical modeling of motion and gravitational fields
- predict-observe-explain reasoning
