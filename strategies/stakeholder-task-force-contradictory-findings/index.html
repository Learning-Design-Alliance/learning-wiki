Convene a stakeholder task force to use contradictory findings in future program design and implementation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors tentatively recommend creating a task force comprised of members from key stakeholders upon completion of the study, as a way of exploring "how contradictory findings can be used in future program design and implementation." The task force should include MRSH developers, study participants, and researchers, with a three-person leadership team comprising a chair from each of the three populations. This strategy treats contradictory quantitative and qualitative findings as input for redesign rather than as grounds for abandonment.

Claims¶

  • Implementation fidelity to the SLMS model was mixed, with regular team meetings at only one of three treatment schools [+W]
  • Many treatment-school teachers interpreted the intervention as “one more thing,” reducing motivation except among a bought-in minority [+W]
  • Control schools saw greater science gains and a greater writing decline than treatment schools [+W]
  • Teachers' school-grade ratings diverged over time, with treatment-school teachers grading their schools less favorably [+W]
  • The authors tentatively conclude the SLMS intervention shows promise for boosting achievement despite unfavorable descriptive trends [+W]
  • Treatment schools showed greater decreases in reading and math proficiency than control schools in descriptive FLDOE data [+W]
