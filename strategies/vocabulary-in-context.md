# Vocabulary In Context

## Description
Vocabulary in context is a strategy in which learners acquire word meanings by encountering words embedded in meaningful sentences, passages, or discourse and inferring meaning from surrounding clues, rather than studying isolated definitions. It is carried out through wide reading of appropriately leveled texts, multiple varied encounters with target words, and instruction that models how to use contextual evidence to form and revise meaning hypotheses.

## Design Implications

Incidental word learning from context is real but low-yield per exposure: meta-analytic evidence shows readers learn only about 15% of unknown words encountered in a single text, so the strategy depends on volume and repetition rather than single encounters [Incidental word learning while reading occurs but at low rates per exposure.](../claims/incidental-word-learning-while-reading-occurs-but-at-low-rates-per-exposure.md) [+M]. Context quality is decisive — informative contexts support correct inference while misleading or neutral contexts actively teach wrong meanings [~M]. The strongest designs pair contextual exposure with explicit meaning instruction, since context alone yields gist-level knowledge insufficient for deep word learning [Pairing contextual encounters with explicit instruction produces stronger vocabulary outcomes than either alone.](../claims/pairing-contextual-encounters-with-explicit-instruction-produces-stronger-vocabulary-outcomes-than-either-alone.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Texts at roughly 95–98% known-word coverage, so unknown words are inferable rather than overwhelming
- Multiple encounters with each target word across varied contexts — meaning knowledge accrues gradually with repetition [Vocabulary knowledge grows incrementally with repeated encounters in varied contexts.](../claims/vocabulary-knowledge-grows-incrementally-with-repeated-encounters-in-varied-contexts.md) [+S]
- Contexts that genuinely constrain the target word's meaning (definitions, synonyms, contrasts, cause–effect signals), not merely mention it
- Learners with sufficient decoding fluency to spare cognitive resources for meaning inference [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Single-exposure learning is unreliable: most unknown words in one passage are *not* learned, and learners who need vocabulary most (weak readers) gain least from incidental exposure [~S] — the strategy alone cannot close vocabulary gaps
- Misleading contexts (where the word's meaning is not signaled or is contradicted by surrounding text) produce confident wrong inferences [~M]
- Context yields approximate, gist-level meanings; it rarely produces the precise, flexible knowledge needed for productive use of high-utility academic words [-M]
- For beginning readers and second-language learners with low text coverage, inferencing effort overloads working memory and suppresses both comprehension and word learning [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Wide reading / free reading** — high-volume self-selected reading; low instructional intensity, high cumulative exposure
- **Context-clue instruction** — explicitly teaching inference types (definition, example, contrast, tone) so learners treat unknown words as problems to solve rather than obstacles to skip
- **Contextual redefinition** — learners form a tentative meaning from context, then check it against a dictionary or teacher input, reconciling the two
- **Embedded pre-teaching** — brief meaning support for a handful of high-utility words before reading, followed by in-context encounters during reading
- **Audio-supported reading** — [Audiobooks](../elements/audiobooks.md) pair pronunciation with contextual meaning, useful for second-language learners

### Target Learners
- Intermediate and fluent readers who can decode automatically and devote attention to meaning inference [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M]
- Second-language learners reading texts within their comprehension threshold (≥95% coverage); below that threshold, contextual inference breaks down [~M]
- Least effective as a stand-alone approach for beginning readers and students with already-limited vocabularies, who need more explicit pathways [~S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Receptive vocabulary breadth: recognizing and approximating meanings of many new words
- Inferencing skill: using textual evidence to form, test, and revise hypotheses about meaning
- Reading comprehension: vocabulary knowledge and contextual inference jointly support passage understanding
- Incremental depth: moving from gist-level recognition toward precise, flexible word knowledge over repeated encounters

### Instructions
1. **Select or level the text.** Choose passages where target words are surrounded by informative context and overall coverage is at least 95% known words; adjust difficulty with leveled text sources (see Examples).
2. **Prepare the reader.** Briefly activate relevant background knowledge and preview 2–5 high-utility words so the context encounter is an *elaboration* rather than a first exposure ([Activation](../principles/activation.md); [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md)).
