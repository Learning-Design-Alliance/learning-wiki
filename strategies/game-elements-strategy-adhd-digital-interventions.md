# Incorporate game elements such as rewards, adaptive difficulty, immersive environments, and clear feedback into digital interventions for ADHD

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 against) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review identifies several game elements used across studies: "points, badges, rewards, customizable levels, and narrative-driven stories." The authors recommend "providing an optimal level of challenge tailored to individual performance" and "consistently providing clear and direct feedback as players progress" to maintain engagement and motivation. AI-driven real-time difficulty adjustment is highlighted as an emerging capability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Games must maintain engagement through tailored challenge levels and clear feedback
#### Constraints
- The authors note that lack of standardized tools across studies limits direct comparison of which elements are most effective

### Target Learners
- children and adolescents with ADHD

### Target Learning Goals
- attention
- working memory
- motivation
