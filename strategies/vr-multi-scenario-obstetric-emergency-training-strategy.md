# Develop multi-scenario VR programs for rare obstetric emergencies, enhanced with artificial intelligence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extending VR obstetric training beyond cesarean section to rare but disastrous emergencies. They state "there is a need for the development and clinical application of VR programs to prepare for rare but disastrous obstetric emergencies such as umbilical cord prolapse, shoulder dystocia, or post-partum haemorrhage", noting these situations require skilled expertise, have steep learning curves, and offer limited, uneven skill-development opportunities. The current application's architecture is described as flexible and adaptable for incorporating additional scenarios, and the authors aim to expand simulated scenarios in future versions, potentially enhanced by artificial intelligence technology.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A flexible, adaptable VR application architecture capable of incorporating additional scenarios
#### Constraints
- The scope of the current study did not include emergency scenarios such as cord prolapse, shoulder dystocia, or post-partum haemorrhage

### Target Learners
- healthcare professionals in obstetrics and gynaecology
- OBGY residents

### Target Learning Goals
- managing rare obstetric emergencies
- acquiring procedural expertise with limited hands-on opportunities
