# Prioritize rigorous RCTs with retention tests and transfer measures before expanding distance simulation programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the evidence gaps it identifies, the review recommends that future work use more rigorously designed randomized controlled trials to assess long-term skill retention and transferability to clinical practice, and suggests a focused systematic review restricted to high-quality comparative studies. It also recommends developing more simulators specifically tailored for distance simulation to address a wider range of subspecialty educational needs, and acknowledges that barriers such as variable Internet connectivity and setup costs must be addressed for sustainable, equitable programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies should include retention tests and clinical transfer (Kirkpatrick level 3) outcome measures rather than relying on learner satisfaction
#### Constraints
- The review notes its findings may not generalize to nurses, paramedics, or midwives, and that benefits may not be universally achievable in resource-limited settings

### Target Learners
- medical students
- residents
- doctors

### Target Learning Goals
- durable technical skill acquisition and transfer to clinical practice
