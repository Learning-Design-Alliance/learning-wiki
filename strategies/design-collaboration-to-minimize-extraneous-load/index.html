Design collaborative setups to minimize extraneous cognitive load¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that instructors attend to how collaborative activities are set up, since the initial setup rather than the amount of collaboration may drive extraneous load. The authors write that "Engaging in online collaboration is use-ful; however, care must be taken to minimize extraneous cognitive load", and call for future research on how group work can be set up and designed to reduce the extraneous processing of collaboration.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learning environments with embedded technology to promote student-to-student interaction, which the article says create greater degrees of interaction than ad hoc technology use

Constraints¶

  • The specific design features that reduce extraneous load were not tested in this study and are flagged as future research

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students in online or blended courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reducing confusion during collaborative learning activities

Claims¶

  • Within collaborative note-takers, amount of collaboration correlates significantly with germane load but not extraneous load [+W]
  • Collaborative note-takers also report higher extraneous cognitive load than controls [+W]
  • Collaborative note-takers report higher germane cognitive load than students given no collaborative note-taking instructions [+W]
  • Collaborative and non-collaborative note-takers show no significant difference in intrinsic cognitive load [+W]
  • Completeness of collaborative notes shows no significant correlation with germane or extraneous cognitive load [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Combine process-oriented analysis with cognitive load measures to determine which transaction costs are germane or extraneous for learning
