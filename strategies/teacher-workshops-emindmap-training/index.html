Train English teachers through workshops to design and apply electronic mind maps¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's principal recommendation is professional development: "Conducting workshops on how to prepare and apply the electronic mind mapping technique" and urging English teachers to attend so they can use it in teaching English language skills at different levels. In the study itself, the researcher trained the experimental group's teacher on IMindMap until proficient, and the teacher then ran a two-hour training session for students on using and designing the maps.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • teacher proficiency with the IMindMap software before classroom application
  • Ministry of Education and school support for workshop provision

Constraints¶

  • the recommendation is grounded in one quasi-experimental study with ninth-grade boys in a single directorate

Target Learners¶

  • English teachers of EFL students at different levels

Target Learning Goals¶

  • effective integration of electronic mind maps in teaching English language skills

Claims¶

  • Teaching ninth-grade English reading texts with electronic mind maps (IMindMap) produced significantly higher reading comprehension posttest scores than the traditional method [+W]
  • The effect of using electronic mind maps on ninth-graders' reading comprehension was medium in size [+W]
  • Fifth-grade female students taught an English unit with e-Mind Maps scored significantly higher on the post achievement test than students taught with the traditional teacher-guide method [+W]
  • The effect size of using e-Mind Maps on fifth-graders' English achievement was high (η² = 0.39) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Have pre-service teachers plan classroom activities with mind maps and model their use with students
