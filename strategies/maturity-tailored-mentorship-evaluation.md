# Tailor evaluation emphasis to program maturity: near-term domains first, downstream outcomes later

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that the focus of mentorship evaluation differ based on the relative maturity of the program and availability of resources. It states: "Newer initiatives, for example, may prioritize shorter term goals: high-quality mentor–mentee relationships, strong organizational capacity, and wellness among faculty and mentees." As programs become established, emphasis may transition to downstream results such as career advancement and academic productivity, given the time needed to measure long-term success of program graduates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Evaluations must serve the needs of the program, which are likely to evolve as programs grow
#### Constraints
- Downstream results require time to measure, so they cannot anchor evaluation of new programs

### Target Learners
- Mentorship program leaders in LMIC institutions with new or established programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Program improvement and resource allocation for mentorship
