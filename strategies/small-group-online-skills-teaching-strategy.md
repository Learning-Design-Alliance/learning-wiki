# Use small-group online breakout-room teaching with remote supervision on identical models as an appropriate method for early-years surgical skills training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that "Small group online teaching for early training years is an appropriate method to teach surgical skills, both at a basic and advanced level". The recipe: strict 1:5 demonstrator-to-delegate breakout rooms, demonstrators re-presenting skills and having students instruct them, then supervised practice on identical models with camera-visible technique correction, ending with structured assessment and detailed feedback. Trainees "can learn from their individual practice which can be effectively supervised online".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Equipment that is easy to use and readily available, with purchase guides emailed in advance
- Demonstrators with teaching and feedback training and a pre-course briefing
#### Constraints
- Validated for early training years; retention of skills needs future assessment

### Target Learners
- Junior surgical trainees
- Medical students in early years

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic and advanced surgical technical skills
