# Measure soccer ball kick speed by recording the sounds of kick and wall impact with a sound card and computing V = D/T

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A specific implementable activity: place the ball a known distance D from a wall, with the computer and microphone roughly equidistant from ball and wall. A student kicks the ball at the wall while the computer records; the two sound pulses (kick and wall hit) are located by zooming on the waveform, and the ball speed is computed as V = D/T from the time-of-flight. In the reported example "D = 2.5 m and T = 0.214 s, so that V = 42 km/h".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A known ball-to-wall distance D
- a microphone plugged into the sound card
- audio software to display and zoom the waveform, such as the free sound editor Audacity
#### Constraints
- The experiment is better executed in a gymnasium or open space; shots must hit a target area on the wall to keep distance uncertainty within acceptable limits

### Target Learners
- high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring speed, time intervals, and performing data analysis in sports physics
