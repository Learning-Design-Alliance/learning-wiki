# Substitute simulators for specialized single-photon equipment in high school quantum labs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because real single-photon experiments require apparatus unavailable to schools, the module uses free online simulators (e.g. a Mach-Zehnder interferometer simulator and the PhET Stern-Gerlach simulator) as lab substitutes. The text explains: "In practice, setting up a single photon source and detector requires specialized equipment, so we will instead run a simulator to see what happens." Students construct the interferometer in the simulator, fire single photons, and predict which detectors trigger.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- access to free browser-based simulators such as the St Andrews Mach-Zehnder simulator and the PhET Stern-Gerlach simulator
#### Constraints
- the authors take no responsibility for broken links or if the web interface of referenced material changes

### Target Learners
- high school students without access to specialized quantum optics equipment

### Target Learning Goals
- observing single-photon superposition and interference behavior that cannot be demonstrated classically
