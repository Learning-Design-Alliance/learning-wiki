# Design e-learning for information-giving skills to support transfer to clinical practice rather than knowledge enhancement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that an e-learning for improving oncological HCP information-giving skills be aimed at transferring skills to clinical practice rather than at enhancing knowledge. Suggested transfer-support functions include "brief notifications or alerts that raise awareness about specific skills shortly before a consultation, or an exercise such as writing down implementation intentions". The strategy follows directly from the finding that HCP know which skills matter but struggle to apply them in consultations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The e-learning must be neatly connected to clinical practice, e.g. centred around video-recordings of patient consultations
#### Constraints
- Virtual or standardized patients providing feedback can be perceived as not realistic, with low levels of emotional involvement reported

### Target Learners
- practicing oncological healthcare providers (medical specialists and clinical nurse specialists)

### Target Learning Goals
- effective information-provision to cancer patients
