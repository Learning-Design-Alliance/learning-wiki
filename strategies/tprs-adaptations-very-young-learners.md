# Adapt TPRS question techniques and prop use for very young learners' limited attention spans

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends concrete adaptations when applying TPRS with very young learners: divide circling question sets into sections of 1-2 questions asked at different story points, keep PQA sessions short with fast transitions, use far more visuals and real-life props than standard TPRS, maintain high activity variety (flashcard games, songs, videos, action games, crafts), and teach one concrete concept at a time. The authors observed that "asking similar questions in a row to very young learners may result in attention and interest loss" during implementation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher must vary question placement across the story and limit questions to 1-2 at a time
- Greater numbers of visuals and props than in standard TPRS design
#### Constraints
- Adaptations are grounded in the teacher-researcher's personal observations with four-year-olds, not in a controlled comparison

### Target Learners
- four-year-old kindergarten EFL learners

### Target Learning Goals
- L2 vocabulary acquisition and engagement during storytelling instruction
