# Future research should compare simple aerobic exercise with cognitively demanding physical activities and examine how intensity bouts are accumulated

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that research on physical activity and academic performance distinguish activity quality, not just intensity. It states that "Cognitively demanding physical activities seem to have a different effect on children's brains in comparison with simple aerobic exercises," and calls for experimental studies testing different bouts of intensity levels, since the present study did not examine bout accumulation. This is the authors' forward-looking recommendation, not a tested finding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experimental designs that manipulate intensity levels and bout accumulation patterns
#### Constraints
- The present cross-sectional design precludes causal inferences; heart rate measures cannot differentiate qualitative aspects of physical activity such as balance or coordinative tasks

### Target Learners
- school-aged children 9–13 years

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance
- executive function (cognitive flexibility, inhibition)
