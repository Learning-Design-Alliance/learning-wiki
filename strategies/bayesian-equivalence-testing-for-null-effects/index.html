Use Bayesian inference, equivalence testing, and multiple operationalizations to make null effects theoretically informative¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 6 studies (3 theoretical, 2 causal, 1 review), q2–q3 · 2 of 6 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends methodological moves for treating nulls as informative rather than inconclusive. "Bayesian inference provides one avenue for quantifying evidence in favor of the null hypothesis," allowing researchers to distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence, and equivalence testing enables formal evaluation of whether effects are practically negligible. Including multiple operationalizations of ostensibly similar constructs strengthens inference, as convergent null effects provide more robust constraints than any single test.
