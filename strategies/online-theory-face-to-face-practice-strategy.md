# Deliver theoretical content online and reserve face-to-face lessons for practice in blended communication courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the trial's satisfaction and feedback findings, the authors recommend a division of labor for blended medical communication courses: "theoretical content may be delivered via online learning platforms, allowing flexibility and self-paced learning, while face-to-face teaching should emphasize practical, interactive activities such as role-playing and simulations." Students' qualitative feedback indicated a preference for online formats for theory and face-to-face time for hands-on practice, and they wanted more theory content in such online formats with freely chosen learning times.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear task instructions and regular student feedback to ensure engagement and usability
#### Constraints
- The knowledge assessment used has not yet been validated, which the authors state may impact reliability and validity
- Effects on real-life daily practice were not assessed through OSCE or workplace-based assessment

### Target Learners
- Medical students in communication skills curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- Patient-centered communication techniques and satisfaction with course format
