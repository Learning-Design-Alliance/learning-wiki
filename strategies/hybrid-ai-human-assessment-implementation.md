# Implement AI in assessment as a hybrid support tool for low-stakes, formative stages while humans retain final grading authority

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's implications recommend that institutions "avoid positioning AI as a replacement for human grading and instead adopt hybrid models in which AI supports formative feedback, early drafting, and lower-stakes evaluation, while human instructors retain responsibility for final judgment". AI feedback should be "clear, structured, and easy to use for revision", focused on technical aspects such as grammar and organization, with transparency and human oversight kept in place to ensure trust. AI should be integrated into drafting and improvement stages rather than final grading decisions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- AI systems must be open and understandable, with human oversight maintained; implementation must complement, not displace, meaningful instructor feedback.
#### Constraints
- Students remain skeptical of AI for creative, subjective, or high-stakes evaluation, so the strategy is limited to low-stakes and formative contexts.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- effective use of AI-generated feedback to support revision and writing improvement

### Affordances
- Perceptions Preferences Distinction Ai Assessment
