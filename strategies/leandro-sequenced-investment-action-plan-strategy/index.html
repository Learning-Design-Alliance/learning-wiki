Sequenced multi-year investment plan across teacher and principal pipelines, early childhood, high-poverty schools, funding, and assessment and accountability to achieve Leandro compliance¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The action plan recommends that the state take immediate and six-year sequenced actions to transform its education system: ensuring an adequate supply of qualified teachers and principals, improving curriculum, instruction and assessment, expanding access to high-quality early childhood education, directing adequate and equitable funding to students with greater needs, strengthening supports for high-poverty schools and low-performing schools, and revising the state assessment and accountability systems to measure progress toward the Leandro tenets. Investments are phased in over an eight-year period across short-term and ongoing scenarios for K-12 district operations, early childhood programs, and state-level supports, with cost estimates provided for each critical need area.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sustained, significantly increased state commitment and investment at the school, district, regional, and state levels to build and maintain capacity

Constraints¶

  • The report states prior promising initiatives have neither been sustained nor brought to scale and are insufficient to adequately address the Leandro requirements

Target Learners¶

  • All North Carolina K-12 public school students
  • Economically disadvantaged students and students with disabilities
  • At-risk four-year-old children

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Equal opportunity to a sound basic education
  • College-, career-, and civic-life-readiness
  • Closing achievement gaps among student groups

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide professional learning and a bilingual workforce so preK and early elementary educators can sustain home language and oral language development
  • Use growth measures rather than achievement as the primary indicator of school effectiveness in ESSA accountability
  • Districts should map virtual-learning and equity actions onto the seven domains of a comprehensive, aligned principal pipeline
  • Grow-your-own and recruitment initiatives: alternative programs, Minority Serving Institutions, high-school pipeline programs, and district human-capital efforts to diversify the teacher workforce.
  • Short-term and longer-term state steps to scale community schools to all high-poverty schools
  • Use state licensing and program approval standards, infrastructure investment, equity targeting, and pipeline reforms to improve principal learning
  • Policymakers should fund and deliver authentic, job-embedded principal professional learning and remove access barriers
  • Using Title II professional development funds to strengthen teacher preparation, recruitment, induction, and support in high-need schools
  • Six-part strategy for assuring qualified teachers for all students
  • Phased policy agenda for building a statewide pathways system: immediate low-cost COVID-recovery steps followed by longer-term investments
