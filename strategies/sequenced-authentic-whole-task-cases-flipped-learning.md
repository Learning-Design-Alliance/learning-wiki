# Select simple, typical, important authentic cases and sequence them from simple to complex, starting text-based before immersive modalities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Drawing on complex learning theory and whole-task approaches, the article recommends using authentic, real-life clinical cases as learning tasks while adjusting their complexity for novices. Cases should be "sequenced from simple to complex, allowing learners to gradually build competence and confidence". Presentation should begin with text-based case formats, which offer psychological safety and help prevent cognitive overload, and transition to more immersive modalities such as virtual or standardized patients as learners gain fluency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cases must be authentic and resemble real clinical situations; case difficulty and presentation modality must be matched to learners' current experience
#### Constraints
- Whole-task cases carry inherent complexity often requiring simultaneous processing of multiple information sources, so unadjusted tasks risk overwhelming novice learners

### Target Learners
- Preclinical medical students (novices)

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning training on realistic diagnostic tasks
