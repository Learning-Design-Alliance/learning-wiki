# Use field-of-practice opinion data to fine-tune post-basic training programmes tailored to predominantly clinical, managerial, or academic occupational physician roles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that competency-priority differences between occupational physician groups be used to develop training that reflects practice. It states the findings "can help to fine tune, following basic OP training, more focussed and tailored training programmes with emphasis on the bespoke skills and competencies" such as advanced communication training for Physician and Manager/Physician groups and more comprehensive environmental impact training for Academic/Physician and Physician groups. The international perspective is said to facilitate common curriculum development at national, regional and international level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Basic OP training already in place, with tailoring applied following it
#### Constraints
- Based on self-reported job titles with high crossover between practice areas; relatively low response rate and stronger European response may limit representativeness

### Target Learners
- Occupational physicians in clinical, managerial, and academic roles

### Target Learning Goals
- Role-specific competency development, including communication skills and environmental issues related to work practice
