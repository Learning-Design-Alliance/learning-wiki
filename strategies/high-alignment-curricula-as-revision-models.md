# Use higher-aligned curricula as practical models for targeted AMS curriculum revision across professions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend targeted curriculum revision using the strongest programmes as exemplars: "Higher-aligned curricula should be used as practical examples for improving weaker areas and strengthening AMS preparedness in African OH pre-service training." They specify profession-specific priorities: medical curricula need stronger attention to guideline-based prescribing, resistant-infection risk factors, special populations, drug interactions, formulary development and antimicrobial product research and regulation; pharmacy curricula need emphasis on timely supply, documentation, dispensing accuracy and AMR drug-utilisation studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to documented curricula and international competency frameworks to identify which AMS components are weak or missing
#### Constraints
- The article notes curricula were obtained in 2021–2022 and some universities may have revised their programmes since then; only nine universities from five countries were included via convenience sampling

### Target Learners
- pre-service medicine, pharmacy and veterinary students in African universities

### Target Learning Goals
- day-one readiness for antimicrobial stewardship and AMR containment
