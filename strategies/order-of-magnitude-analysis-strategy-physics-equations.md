# Order of magnitude analysis: deliberately discarding precision to estimate and evaluate magnitudes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Order of magnitude analysis "deliberately discards precision (in the values it uses and in the physics it carries)" to determine the order of magnitude of a result. Established in science education as order-of-magnitude reasoning, Fermi problems, or back-of-the-envelope estimation, it is applied here to a given expression; the solar-intensity example shows one estimate describing, explaining, and predicting intensities at Earth and Mars.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Which effects can be set aside without changing the order of magnitude "has to be judged in advance", a judgment the article calls a central aspect of the strategy
#### Constraints
- "The estimate rests on a model that sets parts of the physics aside"; in the example, atmospheric absorption and orbital eccentricity were disregarded

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- estimating and evaluating physical magnitudes otherwise hard to obtain
