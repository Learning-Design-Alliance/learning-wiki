# Train question authors to craft high-quality, plausible distractors when implementing 3-option multiple-choice items

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because each remaining distractor must function effectively once options are reduced, the article recommends systematic author training. It states that "Training question authors in a systematic way, such as use of concept mapping and the crafting of realistic clinical scenarios, is essential for producing effective distractors" and notes distractors should represent common misconceptions or reasoning errors among lower-ability examinees. The authors add that such approaches could be particularly useful in formative assessment to clear misconceptions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each distractor in a 3-option item must function effectively, e.g. representing common misconceptions or errors in thinking and reasoning among lower-ability examinees.
#### Constraints
- The article notes reducing options may not necessarily save substantial testing time, since exam content such as longer clinical vignettes and images drives response time.

### Target Learners
- physicians in anesthesiology subspecialty fellowship training

### Target Learning Goals
- assessment of medical knowledge and clinical reasoning in subspecialty certification and in-training examinations
