Train students to visualise words in their mind's eye as a spelling strategy for L2 learners.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study implemented a visualisation strategy training programme drawing on Hickmott's Empowering Learning Professional Practitioner Training and Bell's Seeing Stars Program, which is "based on the premise that efficient spellers have the ability to see letters in their mind's eye". Students received 45-minute weekly training sessions before each spelling test, learning to picture words mentally rather than relying on phonetic decoding.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A trained instructor to deliver 45-minute weekly visualisation training sessions
  • Access to visualisation-based instructional materials such as the Seeing Stars Kit

Constraints¶

  • The study was conducted with female sixth-grade students in a Saudi Arabian private school context
  • Whole-class training caused some students to be distracted by others

Target Learners¶

  • L2 elementary school students
  • Sixth-grade students learning English spelling

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English spelling accuracy
  • Retention of correct spelling of high-frequency words

Claims¶

  • Students' perception that visual strategies are better than traditional methods strongly predicts their preference for using them. [+W]
  • Students hold highly positive attitudes toward visualisation strategies for spelling. [+W]
  • Integrating spelling games with visualisation strategies can re-motivate students when performance dips mid-intervention. [+W]
  • Visualisation strategies significantly improve L2 students' spelling skills compared to traditional memorisation methods. [+W]
  • Students trained in visualisation strategies show greater weekly improvement in words memorised than control students. [+W]
