# Use context-rich problems that place the student in a realistic scenario with missing or excess data to promote transfer

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Context-rich problems, developed by the University of Minnesota PERG, begin with "You..." and place the student in a specific realistic situation. Instructors deliberately decide whether to include a diagram, include excess data, exclude information that should be common knowledge, and specify a target variable. Through scaffolding, students solve increasingly less structured questions, learning to transfer knowledge to new situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems must be deliberately constructed with decisions about diagrams, excess data, omitted common-knowledge information, and target variables
#### Constraints
- Most context-rich problems are more complex than traditional problems because of these design decisions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate economics or physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- transferring concepts to real-world situations requiring discriminatory judgment
