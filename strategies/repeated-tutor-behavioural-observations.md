# Use standardised, repeated tutor behavioural observations throughout training to assess professional behaviours

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that medical schools make greater use of repeated, standardised behavioural observations by tutors as an outcome measure alongside formal examinations. In the study, PBL tutors who met their small group twice weekly rated each student's professional behaviours once in year 1 and twice in year 2, yielding high internal consistency (Cronbach alpha 0.87–0.91) and high retest reliabilities across different tutors. The authors ask whether "medical schools be making use of more standardised and repeated behavioural observations undertaken by tutors throughout the students’ training, in addition to current measures of clinical competence".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors who know their students well through regular small-group contact, such as PBL tutors acting as personal mentors.
- A defined set of observable behavioural items with rating scales, administered repeatedly across the course.
#### Constraints
- The authors note that improvement in predictive power depends also on "devising and using better measures of critical behaviours in medical school and professional practice".

### Target Learners
- medical students in problem-based learning cohorts

### Target Learning Goals
- professional behaviour
- teamwork and group functioning
- accepting feedback and acknowledging weaknesses
