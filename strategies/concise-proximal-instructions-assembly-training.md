# Keep instructional prompts concise and position instructions adjacent to the work to reduce Extraneous Load in assembly training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's key points recommend "keeping instructional prompts concise and early interpretable instructions during training". Low Extraneous Load instructions named the bar, color, and hole directly (e.g., "Take a medium red bar hole 3 and place it on the base bar hole 3."), whereas high Extraneous Load instructions were wordy and displayed on a panel offset and rotated 90 degrees from the work area. The HMD design itself follows the proximity compatibility principle by placing instructions in world space directly adjacent to the relevant component.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructions must remain interpretable; the article notes learners appeared to filter verbose instructions via selective attention, which was effective mainly under adaptive training
#### Constraints
- Extraneous Load effects were mixed: workload rose only under fixed (non-adaptive) training, and the retention-trial slowdown appeared in Experiment 1 but not Experiment 2

### Target Learners
- trainees using head-mounted or in-world instruction displays for equipment assembly

### Target Learning Goals
- reduce training time and perceived difficulty without sacrificing retention
