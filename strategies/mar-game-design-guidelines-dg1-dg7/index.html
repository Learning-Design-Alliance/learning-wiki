Design guidelines for competitive and collaborative MAR games for primary school children (DG1-DG7)¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q4 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

From the evaluation results the authors derive seven design lessons for competitive/collaborative MAR games: introduce time management as a challenge (DG1); provide scaffolding adapted to children's skills and age so tasks are neither too easy nor too difficult (DG2); mix competitive dynamics into collaborative games to intensify group communication (DG3); add dynamic 3D objects with more explicit visual emotional states (DG4); minimize distractions using challenge, fantasy and curiosity (DG5); require face-to-face play sessions so emotional and social skills are practiced and risks of addiction are controlled (DG6); and support active learning, since "the context should provide opportunities for exploration, problem solving and inquiry" that let students evaluate ideas, apply strategies, obtain feedback and consolidate learning (DG7).

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Games should allow students to select scaffolds according to their abilities to balance their emotions

Constraints¶

  • The guidelines derive from one short-duration evaluation in a limited physical space; the authors state the impact of more and longer game sessions remains to be studied

Target Learners¶

  • primary school children

Target Learning Goals¶

  • socialization and communication skills
  • engagement and intrinsic motivation in gamified learning activities

Claims¶

  • Collaborative MAR gameplay raises children's observable enjoyment and mood more than competitive gameplay [+W]
  • Collaborative MAR gameplay exceeds competitive gameplay on observed physical activity and interest in exploring the tool [+W]
  • In collaborative MAR play, children show positive collaboration quality on all measured dimensions except time management [+W]
  • Both competitive and collaborative MAR game modes are highly enjoyable and easy to use for primary school children, with no significant differences between modes [+W]
  • The game sessions showed high attendance, engagement, and enjoyment, with no differences among conditions [+W]
