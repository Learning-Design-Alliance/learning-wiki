# Include more open-ended items in classroom measurement and evaluation to reduce students' difficulty with that format

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 associational, 1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that because Turkish students find open-ended questions harder than multiple-choice ones, classroom "measurement and evaluation studies within the educational process should include more open -ended questions". Familiarity through repeated exposure is the proposed mechanism: encountering open-ended items more often "may help them to experience less difficulty". It also recommends teaching methods developing high-level mental processes and assessments including items targeting high-level cognitive skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment activities within the ongoing educational process, alongside instruction that develops higher-level mental processes
#### Constraints
- Grounded in TIMSS 2015 mathematics data for Turkey; the authors note a similar study could be extended to the science test or ABİDE, implying the evidence base is one subject and one country

### Target Learners
- Fourth- and eighth-grade mathematics students in Turkey

### Target Learning Goals
- Reduced difficulty with open-ended item formats
- Development of higher-level cognitive skills such as reasoning
