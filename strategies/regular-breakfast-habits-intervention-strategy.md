# Address regular breakfast consumption habits to shift adolescents from low-energy screen-based behaviors toward higher-intensity physical activity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (3 associational, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that intervention strategies target the development of regular breakfast consumption habits, based on the finding that breakfast skipping is the pivotal bridge node connecting lifestyle behaviors, physical conditions, and academic performance. It states: "Intervention strategies might consider addressing the development of regular breakfast consumption habits, thereby facilitating a shift from low-energy, screen-based behaviors to higher-intensity physical activities among children." The authors also call for an integrated multimodal lifestyle intervention strategy considering network structure and components.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular breakfast consumption habits as the intervention target, given breakfast skipping's highest bridge centrality among modifiable lifestyle factors
#### Constraints
- The article states the bridging role does not imply direct causality and that findings are observational associations requiring confirmatory efforts

### Target Learners
- Adolescents in secondary and vocational schools

### Target Learning Goals
