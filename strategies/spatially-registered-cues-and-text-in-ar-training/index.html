Use spatially-registered orientation cues and scene-embedded text to improve AR training interfaces¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on participant feedback and observed difficulties, the authors recommend two AR design improvements: adding orientation cues such as virtual arrows on both the slot and the component so students can align them, and using spatially-registered text within the scene instead of screen-aligned HUD text. Reading HUD text "required the participants to shift their focus from looking into the scene", and symmetrical components like the memory and processor made orientation hard to judge when virtual content lacked distinguishing features.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Orientation cues are needed when components are symmetrical and the virtual rendering lacks distinguishing features

Constraints¶

  • Screen-aligned HUD text draws attention away from the scene; the article proposes, but did not test, spatially-registered text as the remedy

Target Learners¶

  • novice users learning assembly tasks through AR

Target Learning Goals¶

  • correctly identifying component position and orientation during assembly

Claims¶

  • Participants rated both AR tutors positively, with no significant between-group differences in questionnaire ratings [+W]
  • Participants trained with the intelligent AR tutor completed the unassisted physical assembly task significantly faster, with no significant difference in errors [+W]
  • Intelligent AR tutoring produces significantly higher post-test performance than non-intelligent AR training for motherboard assembly [+W]
