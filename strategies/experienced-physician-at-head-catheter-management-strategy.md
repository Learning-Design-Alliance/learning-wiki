# Position the more experienced ENT physician at the patient's head managing the intubation catheter, with leakage-minimising precautions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A role-assignment and catheter-handling strategy distilled from the simulations: the more experienced ENT physician takes the endoscope position at the patient's head, freeing the leader to synchronise ventilation and guide the trainee performing the tracheostomy. The authors state: "The recommendation was to have the more experienced ENT physician at the patient's head (endoscope), as this position requires good experience of intubation and flexible endoscopy." Supporting precautions include a hand leaning on the patient's face during catheter movement and verbal call-out of catheter marks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The head-end operator needs good experience of intubation and flexible endoscopy; an Eschmann stylet and laryngoscope should be readily available for accidental extubation
#### Constraints
- Catheter movement should be slow and careful, with the holding hand leaning on the patient's face, to limit unintentional extubation

### Target Learners
- ENT physicians and ICU teams performing percutaneous tracheostomy in COVID-19 patients

### Target Learning Goals
- Secure optimal catheter positioning in the airway, minimise aerosolisation and ventilation circuit leakage, and manage accidental extubation
