# Use proactive reviewer-assignment strategies (competency- and social-network-based) as prevention rather than post-hoc grade correction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review argues that assignment distribution should be treated as prevention, in contrast to corrective techniques applied after grades are assigned. It states that "reviewerassignmentstrategiesleveragingcompetency and social networks can serve as proactive measures that reduce grading noise and enhance learning experiences without complex post-processing". Concretely, this means choosing reviewers by competency indicators or social context before reviewing begins, rather than relying on bias mitigation and noise reduction downstream.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Competency-based allocation needs reliable indicators of reviewer expertise such as prior performance, calibration accuracy, or past grading behavior
#### Constraints
- Social-network and bidding approaches have conceptual appeal but require rigorous testing to assess impacts on grading objectivity and student trust

### Target Learners
- higher education students
- MOOC learners

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate and fair peer grading with reduced noise before grades are assigned
