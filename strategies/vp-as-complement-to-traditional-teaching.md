# Use Virtual Patient cases as a complement to traditional teaching formats in medical education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that "The study supports the use of VP in medical education as complementing traditional teaching formats", since VP sessions achieved retention on par with lecturing while offering interactive contextualization, visualization, assessment practice and standardized SBAR communication training. The authors suggest VP frameworks could be developed for multiple trainers and inter-professional learning, and that VPs may be cost-effective by reducing lecturing time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Systematic validation and assessment of the tools must be paralleled with implementation to guarantee evidence-based learning
#### Constraints
- Evidence rests on one patient case; the authors call for studies on when and how VP formats are most effective, considering gender, learning styles and IT competence, and for retention testing beyond six months

### Target Learners
- medical students
- residents
- policy makers designing medical education strategies

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge retention in trauma assessment, diagnosis and treatment
- clinical communication skills
