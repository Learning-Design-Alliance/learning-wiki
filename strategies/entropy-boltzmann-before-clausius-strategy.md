# Introduce entropy with the microscopic Boltzmann definition before presenting the Clausius thermodynamic definition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy reverses the historical order followed by the majority of textbooks: entropy is introduced starting from the microscopic Boltzmann definition S = k log W, preceded and followed by examples and applications, and only later, in the next chapter, is the Clausius thermodynamic definition presented. The textbook also provides an argument for the equivalence of the two definitions for the ideal gas based on counting microscopic states.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A chapter sequence that separates the microscopic definition of entropy from the later Clausius definition, with examples and applications around the microscopic introduction.
#### Constraints
- The equivalence argument the article describes is given for the ideal gas.

### Target Learners
- high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding entropy and its microscopic meaning
- connecting microscopic and thermodynamic definitions of entropy
