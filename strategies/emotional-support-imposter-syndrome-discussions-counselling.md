# Emphasize emotional support alongside professional support by discussing imposter syndrome frequently and providing access to counselling

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that programs treat emotional support as a core component of research experiences, not an add-on. They advise "at least one formal discussion about imposter syndrome, preferably led by an invited specialist", plus multiple informal discussions among students and mentors, and one-on-one counselling access. During the virtual summer program, a mental health provider held weekly stress-reduction sessions and individual appointments, which the authors report as helpful and plan to continue.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a mental health provider or one-on-one counselling for students, where possible
- Formal and informal discussion opportunities addressing imposter syndrome early in students' careers
#### Constraints
- The authors note institutions should provide similar counselling access "if possible", acknowledging not all programs can

### Target Learners
- undergraduate researchers from marginalized groups in STEM

### Target Learning Goals
- coping with pandemic-related and academic stress
- normalizing discussion of imposter syndrome
- personal and academic wellbeing
