Use guided notes and response cards as low-cost active-responding techniques during lectures¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 5 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 2 review, 1 causal), q2–q4 · 4 of 5 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article presents two implementable classroom techniques recommended by behavior analysts. Guided notes are instructor-prepared notes that "cue students to respond to key information in a lecture or discussion" using blank spaces or notations prompting note-taking behaviors. Response cards are 3×5″ index cards or small laminated boards students use to hold up answers to teacher questions throughout a lecture, with color coding to help discriminate answers. Both increase active student responding at low cost.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructor preparation of the guided-note cues or response cards
  • Questions posed throughout the lecture to elicit student responses

Constraints¶

  • The article notes much work remains to determine the best method of incorporating guided notes into a course

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students
  • college students
  • students with learning disabilities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • retention of lecture content
  • class participation
  • quiz and test performance

Affordances¶

  • Stimulus Control Response Cards

Claims¶

  • Guided notes produce consistently higher performance on later quizzes than conditions without guided notes, including for learning-disabled students [+W]
  • Guided notes increase achievement and participation compared with lecture outlines, lecture only, or lecture plus slides [+W]
  • Response cards markedly increase student responding and quiz scores compared with hand raising [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Retrieval practice improves long-term retention [+M]
  • Generating information produces a memory advantage over passively reading it (the generation effect) [+W]
