# Adopt the stand-alone active-listening mode for interpreting learners with insufficient L2 proficiency, especially undergraduates seeking to boost listening skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
For programs that do not set strict L2 proficiency requirements (such as China's MTI programs), the authors recommend drawing on the MIIS experience by offering language-enhancement electives in the first semester, and borrowing from Lu (2009), Xu (2010) or the EG syllabus "to set up an interpreting listening course or at least make active listening training a stand-alone component before interpreting skills training is initiated". This targets beginners whose L2 listening is a stumbling block, since students may fail professional exams because of "insufficient command of their working languages, not because of insufficient technical or cognitive skills".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both L2 proficiency and learning objectives of interpreting learners should be fully taken into account when choosing a training mode
#### Constraints
- For admitted students who already demonstrate relatively high language proficiency and aim at professional careers, the built-in mode is adequate and the language-enhancement part could be excluded

### Target Learners
- interpreting student beginners with insufficient L2 proficiency
- undergraduate interpreting learners

### Target Learning Goals
- L2 listening competence
- consecutive interpreting readiness
