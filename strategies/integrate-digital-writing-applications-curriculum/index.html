Integrate digital writing applications into the curriculum and offer students choice of tools and tasks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that "The importance of digital writing applications, which are considered to be an alternative for students with writing difficulty or who are bored with writing, should be emphasised in the curriculum and be integrated into lessons." It further recommends storing student work in electronic portfolios, including wiki writing, blog writing or multimodal text preparation in lessons, and — drawing on its autonomy finding — giving students the choice of the tasks they do and the tools they use, since previous studies it cites report this increases motivation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • teachers familiar with the role of technology in students' and teachers' everyday lives

Constraints¶

  • students may lack previous knowledge or life experiences with digital tools, so using them has no meaning and they cannot be motivated

Target Learners¶

  • primary school students, including students with writing difficulty or who are bored with writing

Target Learning Goals¶

  • writing skill development
  • writing motivation
  • new literacy skills

Affordances¶

  • Self Determination Theory Digital Tool Motivation

Claims¶

  • Digital writing workshop participation improved the 6+1 analytic writing dimensions of ideas, organisation, word choice, sentence fluency and spelling [+M]
  • Students in the digital writing workshop significantly increased their use of story elements in their stories [+M]
  • Contrary to expectations, the digital writing workshop significantly decreased fourth-graders' writing motivation while control-group motivation did not change [-M]
  • A 14-week digital writing workshop produced significantly greater story-writing gains than regular curriculum lessons in fourth-grade students [+M]
  • The authors attribute the motivation decrease to researcher-selected applications limiting autonomy, low technological literacy undermining competence, and weak relatedness [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Offer high-interest text alternatives and choice alongside short stories to sustain engagement during strategy instruction
