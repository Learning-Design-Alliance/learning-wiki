# Embed the intervention in existing university infrastructures through a paid yearlong service-learning course that trains undergraduates as mentors and advocates.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
To sustain a long-term commitment to youth independent of grant funding, scholarly consultants suggested "embedding the intervention in existing university infrastructures to build sustainability at minimal cost". The resulting model has undergraduates pay to take a yearlong service-learning course in which they are trained to serve as mentors and advocates to their mentees, receiving course credit and supervision through the course sequence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Existing university infrastructure with a service-learning course structure and supervision capacity
#### Constraints
- Usability, feasibility, acceptability, and cost-effectiveness of the model outside the university and community in which it was developed had not yet been determined

### Target Learners
- low-income urban middle school youth
- university student mentors

### Target Learning Goals
- academic engagement
- academic achievement
- school completion
