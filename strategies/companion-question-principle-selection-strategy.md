# Add companion questions asking students to identify applicable physics principles to detect method errors hidden by MC equivalence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article notes that in its MC version of Question 2, using 1-D kinematics equations instead of conservation of mechanical energy cannot be detected because both methods yield the same numerical value. It proposes that "This deficiency may be remedied in the future by adding another question that asks students to select the physics principles they believe are applicable for solving the problem." A similar companion question for Question 1 would let the weight for choice (d) be assigned more precisely.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The companion question must be compared against students' answers to the original question (e.g., comparing frictional-force answers to normal-force answers)
#### Constraints
- Applies to the current MC questions in this study, where a single numerical option can mask a difference in solution method

### Target Learners
- calculus-based introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnosing students' problem-solving approaches and physics principle selection
