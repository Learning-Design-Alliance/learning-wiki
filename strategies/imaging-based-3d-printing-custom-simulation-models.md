# Build simulation models from patient imaging with 3D printing to accommodate anatomical variations at low cost

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using a 3D printing technique to create lifelike simulation models from patient imaging, stating it "would allow for custom, affordable, and realistic representations of models from patient imaging that are better able to accommodate variations in anatomy, patient, and surgical conditions". Segmentation of anonymized MRI scans in open-access software yields printable STL files of individual vertebrae.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to preoperative imaging, open-access DICOM viewing software (3D Slicer), file-editing software (Meshmixer), and a desktop 3D printer
#### Constraints
- Marketed models are not readily adaptable to anatomical variations in patient spines, as accommodated with imaging-based 3D printing

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees
- simulation educators

### Target Learning Goals
- technical surgical skills
- anatomy familiarity
