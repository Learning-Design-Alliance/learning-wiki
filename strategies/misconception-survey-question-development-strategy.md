# Three-stage development of concept questions from open-ended misconception surveys

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends building concept questions by first gathering open-ended written responses from students, then categorizing responses into misconception themes and measuring the strength of each misconception with 2-tier questions combining multiple choice with follow-up open-ended items that allow multiple answer choices, and finally tightening the questions with reviews from colleagues experienced at teaching the course. This procedure ensures distractors represent real student ideas rather than instructor guesses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to student open-ended responses before or during instruction
- Colleague review for scientific accuracy and content validity
#### Constraints
- Developing good concept questions is described as challenging

### Target Learners
- college students in large introductory science courses

### Target Learning Goals
- formative assessment of conceptual understanding
