# Involve surgical trainees in breast-conserving surgery for non-palpable lesions under standardized protocols and real-time supervision, favoring highly reproducible localization methods such as ROLL and MSL

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends entrusting surgical residents with breast-conserving surgery for non-palpable lesions when guided by "standardized protocols and real-time supervision", and suggests choosing "Localization techniques with high reproducibility, such as ROLL and MSL", which "may mitigate the influence of surgical inexperience". The recommendation rests on the study's findings that seniority did not significantly affect margins or complications and that residents achieved better CRR in ROLL procedures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adequate supervision and standardized protocols, as stated in the article's conclusions
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a retrospective multicenter design with possible selection and information bias; residents performed only a minority of cases and small resident CL numbers limited statistical power for that subgroup

### Target Learners
- surgical residents beyond their first year of training

### Target Learning Goals
- safe technical performance of breast-conserving surgery for non-palpable lesions
