# Embed a brief patient educator led MSK session within the general clinical skills course to develop basic joint examination approach before physician tutor teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends: "Course directors and curriculum planners could honour and augment the scarce physician tutor resource by employing physician tutors more efficiently once students have chance to develop an approach to the basic skills of joint exam through patient educator led teaching sessions." In the study, a two-hour MSK physical examination small group session replaced one of twelve usual general physical examination sessions in the first week of the block, taught by either patient educators or physician tutors. The strategy preserves scarce specialist time while giving students early authentic patient contact.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standardized patient educator program with trained educators
- A small group session slot within the existing clinical skills course
#### Constraints
- Findings apply to peripheral joint examination and a two-hour teaching dose; they may not extrapolate to spine, soft tissue, power or gait examinations

### Target Learners
- pre-clerkship medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- basic approach to musculoskeletal joint examination
- comfort with patient encounters
