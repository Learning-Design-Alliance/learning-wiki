Use a systematic design method with problem anticipation to reduce trial-and-error workload and frustration¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the case study findings, the authors recommend following a systematic, prescriptive design method in which problems are anticipated across phases. They state that "using a systematic method appears to be an appropriate way to reduce the workload during the design process", and that when the manufacturing process was well-defined, frustration decreased because designers considered the work an execution activity. They further argue that new design methods should be supported by expert knowledge systems providing appropriate information throughout the design process, with special attention to the initial phases.

Claims¶

  • Frustration during the design process was related to unexpected events, with more unexpected events yielding greater frustration and worse perceived performance [+W]
  • Module VI (conceptual design) showed the highest perceived workload, attributed to sketch-based conceptualization with extensive trial and error [+W]
  • NASA-TLX dimensions varied systematically by design phase: frustration and mental demand were highest in initial modules and mental demand low during manufacturing [+W]
  • Designer workload across an 11-module instructional design model for product development averaged normal-to-high (50–75%) [+W]
