# Evaluate simulation-based CME with Moore's outcome framework targeting at minimum learning and performance outcomes, and test feasibility across disciplines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends a research agenda for simulation-based CME: clarify effects on knowledge acquisition and application, measure clinically important outcomes using Moore's Outcome-based CME evaluation Model, which "proposes measuring outcomes on six levels: participation, satisfaction, learning, performance, patient health, and population health", and evaluate implementation feasibility across different medical disciplines. It advises that research efforts should at a minimum target learning and performance outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies must go beyond satisfaction and knowledge measures to target at minimum learning and performance outcomes, per the article's adoption of Moore's six-level model.
#### Constraints
- The article states that while effects on patient health and population health are the ultimate purposes of CME, research should at a minimum target learning and performance outcomes; feasibility findings in one specialty may not translate to another.

### Target Learners
- practicing physicians and residents attending continuing medical education activities

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge acquisition, application, and clinical performance in CME topics such as assessment and management of medical conditions in pregnancy
