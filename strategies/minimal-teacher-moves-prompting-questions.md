# Use minimal, targeted teacher moves: circulate, prompt with questions, and give feedback that prompts strategy change

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In effective collaborative environments the primer says "teacher intervention is minimal yet impactful, timely yet infrequent, and peripheral yet targeted." Recommended moves include circulating to observe and intervening only when absolutely needed, using "prompting questions rather than instructive statements," providing small amounts of direct instruction only when no one in the group can answer, and giving feedback only when it prompts students to alter strategies and engage all members fully.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need training, e.g., in Cognitive Apprenticeship and Reciprocal Teaching, to model, scaffold, observe, coach, articulate, and reflect
#### Constraints
- Direct instruction is reserved for cases where no one in the group can answer the question or a misconception continues despite questioning

### Target Learners
- K-12 students working in collaborative groups

### Target Learning Goals
- Collaborative knowledge construction
- socially shared regulation of learning
