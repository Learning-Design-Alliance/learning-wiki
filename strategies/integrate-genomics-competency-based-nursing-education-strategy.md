# Integrate genomics into competency-based nursing education at all training levels and provide continuing education with faculty support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend a multi-faceted educational response: embedding genomics in the AACN Essentials as programs transition to competency-based education, covering disease risk, diagnosis, therapeutic decision-making, and symptom management "at all levels of training", and providing sufficient training and support to faculty. Nurses already in practice require continuing education in genomics across all education and practice levels, supported by freely available resources from national institutes. The strategy targets the documented capacity deficits the survey measured.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Continuing nurse educators and academic faculty must have the capacity and resources to teach genomic content.
#### Constraints
- Currently genomics appears in the AACN Essentials only at the graduate level in one item (2.2i); the article notes demographic misalignment limits some 2010 comparisons and the cohort does not fully represent the national workforce.

### Target Learners
- Pre-licensure and graduate nursing students, practicing registered nurses, APRNs, and nursing faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- Genomic competency including family history assessment, pharmacogenomic recognition, and referral facilitation
