Use process driven math to break math problems into navigable pieces for students accessing math auditorily¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Process driven math, developed by researchers in the Logan Project, addresses cognitive load for BVI students who access math primarily through hearing. Instead of voicing a problem as one long chunk, the human reader/scribe breaks it down by mathematical vocabulary — e.g., reading a rational expression as "y equals rational" — and the student digs deeper by asking about the numerator, continuing until the problem is simplified. The review states that developing software operating similarly would eliminate the need for a human scribe and give BVIs more autonomy.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Substantial training for both the student and the reader/scribe

Constraints¶

  • As described, the method relies on a human reader/scribe; software automation is a stated future direction, not an existing tool

Target Learners¶

  • Students who are blind or have low vision accessing mathematics primarily through hearing

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reducing cognitive load when interpreting spoken mathematical expressions
