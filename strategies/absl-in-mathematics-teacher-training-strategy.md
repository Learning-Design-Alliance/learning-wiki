# Use the ABSL approach in mathematics teacher training to address pre-service teachers' knowledge gaps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extending the ABSL approach within mathematics teacher training: investigating its effect in different subjects and comparing it with other teaching methods, and using it in topics where it has a positive effect to help solve possible lack of pre-service teachers' knowledge in different subject areas. They further suggest that pre-service teachers' own use of the approach in their lessons would help increase their students' success.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Topics should be ones in which the ABSL approach has a positive effect, per the article's recommendation
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a single quasi-experimental study in probability; the article itself calls for further investigation in different subjects

### Target Learners
- pre-service elementary school mathematics teachers
- pre-service teachers across subject areas

### Target Learning Goals
- subject-matter achievement
- resolving pre-service teachers' knowledge deficiencies
