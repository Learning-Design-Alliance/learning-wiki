# Integrate DVA/child safeguarding training with existing identification and referral programmes rather than delivering it stand-alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation is that RESPONDS should not stand alone but be combined with existing DVA interventions into an integrated programme (IRIS+) covering women, men and children. They state: 'Ultimately we do not think that RESPONDS should be a stand-alone training intervention, but should be integrated into other DVA interventions for general practices.' They plan to test IRIS+ in a cluster randomised controlled trial powered to detect differences in identification and appropriate referral.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An existing programme such as IRIS to integrate with, plus a controlled evaluation design with adequate sample size and long-term follow up
#### Constraints
- As a stand-alone intervention, uncertainty remains about effectiveness in changing clinician behaviour and improving family outcomes; the pilot showed mixed results requiring refinement

### Target Learners
- general practice clinicians in practices serving patients affected by DVA

### Target Learning Goals
- identification and appropriate referral of women, men and children exposed to DVA
