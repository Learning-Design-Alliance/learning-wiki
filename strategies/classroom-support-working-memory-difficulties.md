# Support children with working memory difficulties in the classroom through approaches other than WM training, including teacher training about WM

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends finding classroom ways to support children with WM difficulties, since WM "underpins learning" in mathematics and reading, and WM deficits can disrupt learning opportunities and lead to disengagement. Because "meta-analyses have found no evidence of any clear transfer effects from WM training to improvements in educational achievement", the authors call for alternative supports and note a survey of nearly 1500 teaching professionals suggesting interventions "should include some element of teacher training", given most teachers overestimated WM capacity limits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should include some element of teacher training, per the survey of nearly 1500 teaching professionals the article cites
#### Constraints
- The article states further robust research on the best ways to support children with WM deficits is needed, as research to date is limited

### Target Learners
- primary school children (middle childhood)
- children from socioeconomically disadvantaged families

### Target Learning Goals
- educational achievement in mathematics, reading, and grammar/punctuation/spelling
