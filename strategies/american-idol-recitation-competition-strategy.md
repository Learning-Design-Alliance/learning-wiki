# American Idol style recitation competition in which student teams select and defend the best homework solution and a TA/UTA panel critiques finalist solutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In each PR recitation, students formed nine randomly generated teams of three to six students. Each team was given three minutes to identify the "best" solution for each of three timed homework problems, with TA/UTA facilitation if a team could not agree. The three finalists' solutions were projected and critiqued by a three-person panel that "gave their critique of each ﬁnalist's problem solving performance", citing strengths and improvements, after which students voted on the best solution with clickers. Bonus course credit for team members whose member advanced encouraged lively discussion of effective problem solving strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- TA and two UTAs per recitation to judge and facilitate
- computer-generated random team assignments projected on screen
- carefully timed 15-minute competition rounds to fit the fifty-minute recitation slot
#### Constraints
- No recitation quiz was given to the PR group, primarily due to time constraints; recitation scoring was based mostly on attendance plus bonus points

### Target Learners
- introductory algebra-based physics students in evening-section recitations of up to 40-plus students

### Target Learning Goals
- effective problem solving strategies such as drawing diagrams, identifying knowns and target variables, and justifying physics principles before writing equations
