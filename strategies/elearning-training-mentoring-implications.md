# University strategy of mentoring, training, and trial online exams to enrich the e-learning environment and foster engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implications section recommends an integrated institutional approach to e-learning: "Universities might use teaching assistants as a university mentor to help students better use the electronic academic course portfolios", alongside seminars, workshops, and ongoing training sessions to raise e-learning readiness and reduce fear and anxiety. It also states that "Conducting trial online exams will reduce student’s apprehension and promote their confidence" in the e-learning environment, and that instructors should receive training and continuous support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Universities must provide "the appropriate educational environment and infrastructure" to enrich students' academic experiences, with trained teaching assistants and faculty development.
#### Constraints
- Proposed following an unplanned pandemic shift to e-learning at a single UAE university; the article notes the abrupt transition lacked the strategic planning that individualises materials and strategies.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate university students
- university instructors and faculty members

### Target Learning Goals
- academic engagement
- e-learning readiness and positive attitudes toward e-learning
- academic achievement
