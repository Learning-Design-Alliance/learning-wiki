# Design nontechnical skills training with structured framework, real case discussion, and individualized feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention trained circulating nurses on the definition and importance of nontechnical skills, the CPLINTS domains and behaviors, and each domain specifically, using a face-to-face approach delivered by a human factors specialist in two-hour sessions held in operating rooms during rest times. The article reports that "Using a structured framework to train the circulating nurses, real case discussions, and giving necessary feedbacks regarding their behaviors were helpful in improvement of these skills." Each participant's first-phase observation data were used so the intervention provided feedback on their observed behaviors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A human factors specialist experienced in nontechnical skills education to deliver the training
- Participants' own observational data from a first phase to provide individualized feedback
#### Constraints
- Sessions were held during nurses' rest times in the operating rooms; participants had no prior nontechnical skills training

### Target Learners
- circulating nurses
- operating room staff

### Target Learning Goals
- improving nontechnical skills to prevent surgical errors and increase patient safety
