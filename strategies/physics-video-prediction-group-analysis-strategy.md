# Use videos of physics experiments for prediction-then-check activities, group data analysis, and demonstration of phenomena hard to show live

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article prescribes concrete classroom uses for its videos. For the viscosity video, "Before playing the first part, the student should predict the evolution of the two motions and then check his prediction by following the video". For the GRAC video, students work in groups where each group elaborates measurements from a different minute and results are compared with respect to experimental uncertainty; the teacher can also focus on a range of interest to show the statistical nature of the measurements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Videos with measurable data (screenshots with scale or counter indications) so students can estimate and compare quantities
#### Constraints
- Students favour this as a supplementary tool for pre-lab preparation, not as a substitute of the real experiment

### Target Learners
- first year undergraduates
- secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- prediction and verification of physical phenomena
- estimation of physical parameters from data
- understanding experimental uncertainty and the statistical nature of measurements
