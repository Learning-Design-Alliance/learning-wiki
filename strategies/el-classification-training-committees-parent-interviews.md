# Use trained staff, parent interviews, and EL classification committees to reduce initial EL misclassifications

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Participants attributed inconsistencies leading to misclassification to lack of guidance, lack of training, and lack of expertise. Recommended strategies include district-wide experts training school staff using scenarios and cases of potential EL students, informal parent interviews about students with complex linguistic histories before or after the classification assessment, a school or district EL classification committee of qualified trained educators reviewing potential misclassifications with parents involved, and EL experts consulting on ambiguous classification results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators with appropriate EL expertise; appropriately qualified and trained committee members; parent involvement in deliberations and decisions
#### Constraints
- Strategies are intended to be utilized within the provisional classification period

### Target Learners
- Potential and initially classified English learners with complex linguistic histories

### Target Learning Goals
- Reduced EL misclassification and sound classification decision-making
