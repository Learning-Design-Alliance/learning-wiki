# Include postural-alignment elements in addition to patient-handling techniques in LBP prevention programs for nurses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because nurses spend a large proportion of shift time on basic care tasks such as bed making, washing, and dressing—rather than on lifting—prevention programs focused only on manual patient handling may underestimate total postural load. The article concludes: "future investigations on prevention and reduction of LBP in nurses may profit by including elements on postural alignment in addition to patient-handling techniques." The strategy pairs sensitization to frequent bending with practical ergonomic work methods and workplace redesign.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Workplace interventions should sensitize employees to their postures during work, combining awareness training with changes in work practices and redesign of the work environment
#### Constraints
- The article notes systematic reviews found high evidence that interventions based solely on training in patient handling had no effect on LBP, while multidimensional strategies showed moderate evidence

### Target Learners
- nursing personnel in geriatric care

### Target Learning Goals
- reduction of stressful trunk postures during basic care tasks
- prevention of low back pain
