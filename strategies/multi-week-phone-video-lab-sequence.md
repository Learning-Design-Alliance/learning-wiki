# Sequence a multi-week video-analysis lab from free fall, to a ramp, to collision energy and momentum conservation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes spreading the phone-video experiment across multiple weekly lab reports, scaffolding from simple to complex. Students first digitize a ball in free fall to learn data taking and analysis, optionally use a ramp covered with different materials to produce rolling with and without slipping, then analyze the billiard collision over successive weeks. The article states "The second week could focus on applying Newton's laws to determine coeﬃcients of friction, forces and torques." Later weeks apply the work-energy theorem and study energy and momentum conservation, with open-ended extensions such as determining angular velocity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Data can be taken quickly in one session; students need access to a phone camera and the digitization software
#### Constraints
- An on-campus alternative uses pucks on an air table with video provided to students so rolling is avoided

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental design and controls
- fitting and error propagation
- Newton's laws and friction
- work-energy theorem
- energy and momentum conservation
