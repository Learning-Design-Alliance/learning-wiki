Clarify the role of targeted TA-led interventions within a broader teacher-led literacy strategy¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 mixed, 2 against) · 3 studies (3 causal), q2–q4 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The evaluation recommends that schools implementing targeted, TA-delivered interventions clarify how they fit within whole-school literacy teaching. The report found "some class teachers had limited awareness of Switch-on" and concluded that "Clarifying the role of Switch-on as part of a broader, teacher-led literacy strategy could improve implementation". It also recommends closer developer monitoring to ensure fidelity of prescribed session content, duration and format.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Class teachers and senior leaders aware of and involved in the targeted intervention
  • Communication between the TA and class teacher about pupils' progress

Constraints¶

  • Drawn from an implementation context of scaled-up delivery via a cascaded train-the-trainer model

Target Learners¶

  • Struggling readers in primary schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading and literacy skills

Claims¶

  • Many teaching assistants did not feel confident delivering the intervention after the initial five days of training without ongoing support, and the language comprehension component was hardest to deliver. [~W]
  • Implementation fell short of the intended dosage: very few learners received two 15-minute sessions weekly for 30 weeks, mainly due to timetabling and absence [~W]
  • Implementation fidelity of Switch-on was compromised in some schools, with modifications to prescribed content, duration and format of sessions [~W]
  • Switch-on Reading and Switch-on Reading and Writing produced no additional reading progress compared with business-as-usual control schools in a three-arm school-randomised effectiveness trial [-M]
  • Switch-on showed no effect on reading outcomes for pupils eligible for free school meals [-M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Allow lead-in time for schools, provide ongoing TA support beyond initial training, and segment comprehension material into shorter, more varied pieces.
  • Pilot multi-component interventions before trial and choose outcome tests that capture the targeted skill
  • Use trained TA-led small-group support sessions with worked and faded worked examples
