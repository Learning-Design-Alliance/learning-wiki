# Ask students to plot data on a number line to promote perturbation of an initial middle-value conception of median

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementable activity in which students plot data values on a number line to perturb an initial middle-value conception of median. The authors state they "sought to promote perturbation in students' initial conceptions of median" by asking students to begin plotting the data on a number-line, chosen for its potential for presenting the distribution of elements in a data set.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Number-line representations on which students plot each data value
- Tasks with both odd-numbered and even-numbered data sets
#### Constraints
- The article reports the supported scheme remained in activity: students continued sketching number lines in later episodes rather than interiorizing the conception

### Target Learners
- middle-grade students

### Target Learning Goals
- finding the median of data sets with odd and even numbers of elements

## Claims

- [Middle-grade students holding an initial middle-value conception identified the median of odd-numbered data sets but claimed there was no median for data sets with an even number of elements](../claims/even-data-set-no-median-conception.md) [+W]
- [After number-line modeling, both students computed 2.5 as the median, locating the middle between 2 and 3](../claims/number-line-center-value-scheme-construction.md) [+W]
- [Students continued to sketch number lines to find the median in later episodes, determining it in activity rather than from a fully interiorized conception](../claims/number-line-median-in-activity.md) [~W]
- [The authors conclude that middle-grade students' conceptions of median are more complex than previously recognized](../claims/median-conception-more-complex-than-recognized.md) [+W]
