# Text + Structured Activity

## Description
Students engage with text resources (physical or digital) accompanied by a structured activity designed to ensure active learning and engagement with the material. The text conveys information; the structured activity forces processing of that information — answering questions, applying concepts, annotating, or self-explaining — so that reading does not remain passive. This combination provides instruction in pre-class, in-class, or asynchronous settings, and serves as a low-bandwidth alternative to video-based delivery.

## Design Implications

Structured activities convert reading from passive reception into generative processing, which is what drives durable learning [Active learning improves exam performance relative to lecture-only formats.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. The activity must require learners to do something with the text — explain, apply, predict, or connect — rather than merely locate or restate information [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding when learners articulate how examples relate to underlying principles.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. Activities should be tightly aligned to specific sections of the text so learners know what to extract and when.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-selected text at an appropriate reading level, ideally with [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) signaling its structure
- A structured activity requiring generative processing: [Practice](../elements/practice.md) questions, [Application](../elements/application.md) tasks, or [Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md) prompts
- Clear instructions specifying what learners should do with the text, not just that they should read it
- A feedback or debrief mechanism — instructor review, answer keys, or class discussion — so errors are corrected

#### Constraints
- Unstructured reading assignments ("read chapter 4") produce little learning; without a task, most learners skim or skip [-M]
- Text-heavy delivery disadvantages learners with weak reading skills or limited language proficiency; comprehension support ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) of long texts, glossaries) is required [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Activities that ask only for information location (find-and-copy questions) do not produce conceptual learning [-M]
- Poorly timed activities — questions before the relevant text section without guidance — can increase extraneous load [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Guided reading questions** distributed through the text, answered as the learner reads
- **Reading + response**: text followed by short written application or reflection, submitted before class
- **Text + self-explanation prompts**: embedded prompts asking learners to explain why a claim holds or how an example illustrates a principle [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding when learners articulate how examples relate to underlying principles.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]
- **Text + problem set**: reading paired with problems that require applying what was read, in [example-problem pairs](../claims/example-problem-sequences-reduce-cognitive-load.md)
- **Annotated reading**: learners mark and comment on the text itself, using [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) tools such as Perusall or Hypothesis

### Target Learners
- Learners with limited bandwidth or technology access, since text is low-bandwidth and often offline-capable
- Self-regulated learners who can pace their own reading; weaker readers need embedded scaffolds and vocabulary support
- Learners who benefit from rereading at their own pace — text, unlike video, supports rapid review and skimming

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of declarative content (definitions, explanations, frameworks)
- Application: transferring concepts from the text to new problems or cases
- Preparation for higher-effort in-class work (flipped pre-class phase)
- Retention of factual and procedural information over time, especially when activities are [spaced](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. Select or write the text, chunked into coherent sections with clear headings ([Clear Structure & Presentation](../principles/clear-structure-presentation.md))
2. Add an advance organizer or guiding question framing what learners should extract ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
3. Design a structured activity requiring generative processing — application, prediction, or self-explanation — not information location ([Practice](../elements/practice.md), [Application](../elements/application.md))
4. Embed the activity at the point of use: after each section for long texts, or after the whole text for short ones
5. Provide a feedback loop: answer keys, instructor comments, or in-class debrief ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
6. Optionally schedule revisiting of the material at spaced intervals ([Spaced Repetition](../elements/spaced-repetition.md))

## Tools
- **Perusall** (https://www.perusall.com) — social annotation; the annotation prompts function as embedded structured activities
- **Hypothesis** (https://web.hypothes.is) — collaborative web annotation layered on assigned readings
- **Google Forms / LMS quizzes** — low-effort comprehension-check activities attached to readings
- **Anki** (https://apps.ankiweb.net) — spaced-repetition cards generated from text content

## Examples
- **Flipped pre-class reading with response**: an instructor assigns a textbook section plus three application questions due before class; class time opens by discussing the most-missed question. This is the text-based variant of the flipped model.
- **Perusall-annotated readings**: students annotate a journal article with questions and comments; the instructor's annotation prompts ("explain why this method was chosen here") structure the engagement.
- **OpenStax + embedded exercises** (https://openstax.org): free open textbooks with built-in checkpoint questions after each section, enacting the text + activity pairing at scale.
