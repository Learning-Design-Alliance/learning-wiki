# Use scenario-based case-based learning with small-group discussion to teach patient safety to ICU and emergency nurses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's CBL intervention presented nurses with realistic hospital safety-error scenarios; small groups discussed causes of errors, consulted related texts, then proposed error-management solutions in larger groups, with scenarios validated by specialist review. The authors conclude that "incorporating scenarios into education can be a valuable method for enhancing patient safety skills" and recommend CBL "along with other active methods" in nursing education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios adhering to realism, attractive storytelling, and focus on standard and vital patient safety challenges
- Content and form validity review by specialised employees, lecturers, and an expert panel
- Approximately 4 to 5 hours of training across weekly sessions
#### Constraints
- Advantage over lecture demonstrated for attitude and practice, not knowledge
- CBL did not significantly outperform reflection on practice in this study

### Target Learners
- ICU and emergency department nurses with a Bachelor's degree or higher in nursing

### Target Learning Goals
- Patient safety knowledge, attitude, and performance
- Error identification and management in clinical settings
