# Word-net exercises linking lexical knowledge to grammatical awareness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Word-net exercises have learners build networks of private psychological associations around antonym pairs such as plentiful and scarce, attaching central adjectives to generic superordinate nouns. At a secondary stage symbols are added to make overt relations of dependency, both structural-semantic (superordinate to hyponym) and grammatical (parts of speech), which then feed written sentence production. This makes overt the grammatical information Allwright observed learners acquiring covertly by asking questions about words.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Nets are structured by superordinate generic nouns; structural principles are made overt only at a secondary stage, after learners have added words
#### Constraints
- At early stages the aim is simply to encourage the learner to add words, not to focus on structure

### Target Learners
- second language learners building semantic competence in the L2

### Target Learning Goals
- sense relations such as synonymy and antonymy
- grammatical awareness via lexis
- written production
