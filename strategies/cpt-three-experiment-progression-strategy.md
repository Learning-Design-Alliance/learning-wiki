# Progression of three experiments — CPT observation, magnetometry, and atomic clock locking — on one shared apparatus

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article organizes student work as a sequence of three experiments on the same apparatus: first observing CPT transmission resonances by scanning modulation frequency, then exploiting the resonance's sensitivity to level shifts to measure small magnetic fields, and finally locking the rf oscillator to the resonance to build a prototype atomic clock. The authors state "Work with this apparatus will allow students to learn the basics of diode lasers, rf equipment, and atomic spectroscopy." Assembly and debugging is suggested as a senior research project.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The shared VCSEL-based apparatus with buffer-gas cell, magnetic shielding, and tunable microwave oscillator must first be assembled and aligned
#### Constraints
- The same apparatus can support slow and stored light experiments only if the microwave amplitude can be shaped into probe pulses

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students
- senior undergraduate research students

### Target Learning Goals
- coherent light-atom interactions
- atomic spectroscopy
- frequency standards and time-keeping
- magnetometry
