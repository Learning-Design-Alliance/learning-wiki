# Measure parental academic support with distinct companion-type and manager-type item sets when studying family support effects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
For researchers and survey designers, the article demonstrates operationalizing PAS as two four-item Likert scales distinguished by factor analysis: companion items such as "My parents teach me how to study" and manager items such as "My parents collect information about private tutoring or private institutes for me". The scales showed acceptable internal consistency (Cronbach's α 0.78 and 0.75), and the distinction revealed divergent longitudinal effects a single support measure would have masked.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough items per type to separate the factors; the authors state "more items are needed to discern the PAS type" and that future factor analyses should use more questions
#### Constraints
- Items were developed for the Korean KELS context; the authors call for "the use of samples from various cultures"

### Target Learners
- elementary and adolescent students reporting on parental support

### Target Learning Goals
- valid measurement of parental academic support as a predictor of motivation, self-regulation, and achievement

### Affordances
- Pas Companion Manager Two Types
