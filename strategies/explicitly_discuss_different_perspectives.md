# Explicitly Discuss Different Perspectives

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Learners engage in structured discussions that make diverse viewpoints explicit — examining how people with different backgrounds, disciplinary commitments, or stakeholder positions interpret the same phenomenon. The strategy goes beyond exposure: learners must articulate a perspective, compare it against alternatives, and reason about why the differences exist. It is typically carried out through facilitated whole-class discussion, structured controversy, or analysis of contrasting cases and primary sources.

## Design Implications
Structured perspective-taking discussion supports conceptual growth because learners must reconcile conflicting interpretations, which prompts elaboration and schema revision [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction of underlying principles.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+S]. Diversity-related discussion is associated with gains in cognitive and civic outcomes when it is *structured* rather than merely co-occurring with a diverse group [+M]. The facilitator's role is critical: unstructured discussion tends to reproduce existing opinions, while assigned positions and evidence requirements force genuine engagement with unfamiliar views.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A psychologically safe environment with established norms for respectful disagreement
- A facilitator skilled in guiding discussion and ensuring all voices are heard ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
- Authentic source material that genuinely embodies multiple perspectives (primary sources, [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md), stakeholder testimony)
- A structure that assigns positions or requires evidence, so learners argue views they may not hold

#### Constraints
- Unstructured or poorly facilitated discussion can entrench prior beliefs and produce polarization rather than perspective-taking [-M]
- Learners with low confidence or minority status may not voice their perspectives; cold-calling and small-group formats mitigate but do not eliminate this [-W]
- Perspective discussion consumes substantial instructional time and is inefficient for procedural or factual objectives where a single correct answer exists [-M]
- Superficial "tourist" treatment of perspectives can reinforce stereotypes rather than reduce them [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- [Structured controversy](../patterns/debate.md): learners are assigned positions, argue them with evidence, then switch sides and synthesize
- Contrasting-case analysis: small groups compare how different sources or stakeholders frame the same event
- Role-play and simulation: learners adopt stakeholder positions before whole-class [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)
- Written perspective-taking: learners draft an argument from an assigned viewpoint, then peer-review against an opposing draft

### Target Learners
- Intermediate learners who hold initial schemas firm enough to be productively challenged — complete novices lack the knowledge to evaluate competing views [~M]
- Learners in heterogeneous groups, where exposure to genuinely different experiences drives the effect [+M]
- Less effective for learners with very low prior knowledge, who cannot distinguish substantive from superficial disagreement

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: recognizing how framing and assumptions shape interpretation [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction of underlying principles.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+S]
- Argumentation and critical thinking: constructing, critiquing, and revising claims [Case-based learning improves exam performance when discussion requires reasoning through cases.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]
- Empathy and civic dispositions: understanding the experiences and reasoning of others [+M]

### Instructions
1. Select a topic with genuine, well-documented disagreement and gather materials representing at least three perspectives.
2. Establish discussion norms and activate relevant background knowledge ([Activation](../elements/activation.md)).
3. Assign learners to positions — ideally not their own — and have them prepare evidence-based arguments ([Argumentation](../elements/argumentation.md)); assigning positions prevents learners from defaulting to familiar views.
4. Facilitate structured exchange ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)), requiring learners to restate opposing views before critiquing them.
5. Introduce [Cognitive Conflict](../elements/cognitive-conflict.md) deliberately by highlighting genuine contradictions between perspectives.
6. Close with synthesis: learners articulate what the disagreement reveals about underlying assumptions and where they now stand ([Articulation](../elements/articulation.md)).

## Related Strategies
- [Case-based discussion](../patterns/case-based-learning.md) — cases are a natural vehicle for embedding multiple stakeholder perspectives in one artifact
- [Debate](../patterns/debate.md) — a formalized version of perspective discussion with assigned positions and evidence rules
- [Discussion-based learning](../patterns/discussion-based-learning.md) — the broader pattern this strategy instantiates with a multiperspectival focus

## Examples
- **Facing History and Ourselves** (https://www.facinghistory.org) — published curriculum in which students analyze primary sources from multiple stakeholder positions on historical events, followed by structured discussion and reflection.
- **Structured academic controversy in a psychology course** — students assigned to argue for and against a theory (e.g., nature vs. nurture in language acquisition), then switch sides before writing a synthesis paper.
- **Business school case discussion** — Harvard-style case teaching requires learners to analyze a decision from the perspectives of different executives, customers, and regulators before defending a recommendation.
