# Explicitly Discuss Different Perspectives

## Description
Engage learners in structured discussions that explore diverse viewpoints and consider the experiences of others. This includes examining cultural, social, and personal perspectives to foster understanding and empathy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a safe and respectful learning environment where learners feel comfortable sharing their thoughts and opinions. Requires instructors to be skilled facilitators who can guide discussions and ensure that all voices are heard.
#### Constraints
- Discussions can become heated or unproductive if not facilitated effectively. Some learners may be hesitant to share their perspectives in a group setting.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learner levels, particularly those who need to broaden their understanding of diverse perspectives, K-12, higher education, and adult learners in professional development

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance understanding of diverse viewpoints, foster empathy and tolerance, develop critical thinking skills, promote open dialogue

### Affordances
- Provides a structured environment for exploring different perspectives, encourages critical thinking, and promotes empathy and tolerance. Helps learners develop communication and collaboration skills.

### Personalization
- Can be tailored to specific topics or issues, allowing learners to explore perspectives relevant to their interests or concerns. Use case studies, role-playing exercises, and guest speakers to bring diverse perspectives into the classroom.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learner participation and contributions to discussions. Assess learner understanding of diverse perspectives through written assignments or presentations. Collect learner reflections on their learning and growth.

## Impact
- Research suggests that discussing different perspectives can increase empathy, reduce prejudice, and promote more inclusive attitudes and behaviors. Studies also suggest students who understand multiple perspectives are more well rounded.
