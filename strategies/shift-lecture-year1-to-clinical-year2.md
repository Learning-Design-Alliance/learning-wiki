# Purposefully shift lecture-based content from the first preclinical year to clinically based instruction in the second year

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that, when mapping teaching methods for basic science objectives, faculty retain lecture-based instruction in year 1 but "purposefully shifting lecture-based content in year 1 to a clinical-based instructional approach in year 2 with appropriate learning support." This follows their finding that second-year students rated and ranked lecture lower and patient presentation higher. Future investigations with gradual reduction of lecture for increased patient presentation and simulation are called for.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Appropriate learning support when shifting content to clinically based approaches in year 2
#### Constraints
- Results are specific to preclinical undergraduate medical education at one medical school and may lack application to clinical or graduate medical education

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in preclinical (years 1-2) training

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic and clinical science knowledge in an integrated preclinical medical curriculum
