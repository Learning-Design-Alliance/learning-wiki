# Profile new examiners' personality factors to inform examiner pairing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors suggest that assessment teams use personality factor profiling of new examiners when deciding pairings, since behaviour of new examiners is otherwise largely unknown. They write that "Personality factor profiling of new examiners may assist in pairing them with existing examiners", for example pairing an extremely stringent, very extrovert new examiner with a senior experienced examiner who is neither a hawk nor a dove. This remains a recommendation pending further research.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a validated personality instrument such as the NEO-FFI
- Data on existing examiners' degree of stringency from previous candidate scorings
#### Constraints
- The authors recommend further studies to test associations between personality factors and examiner stringency before drawing definitive conclusions

### Target Learners
- medical school assessment teams and examiner cohorts

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing examiner variability in clinical assessments
