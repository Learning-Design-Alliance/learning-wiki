# Offer e-learning oral health education courses to extend OHE to non-dental undergraduates and wider populations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends e-learning as a feasible delivery method for oral health education where face-to-face teaching by dentists is impractical. Because e-learning courses are "repeatable and convenient", the authors argue they may improve learning motivation and could be offered to teachers and school doctors so they can provide OHE instead of dentists, and may become the main teaching method for extended populations nationwide if made publicly available. The study's own comparison supports feasibility for non-dental undergraduates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online course content based on validated curriculum materials
- Availability of the course through the Internet and new media platforms
#### Constraints
- Interaction limitations partly compensated by simultaneous online communication
- Increased IT infrastructure costs for institutions
- Courses require routine updating as knowledge advances

### Target Learners
- Non-dental undergraduate students
- Teachers and school doctors in kindergartens and primary schools
- Adult populations in developing countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Periodontal health knowledge and prevention behaviours
- Oral health promotion at population scale
