# Be Present for Students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Being present means making oneself predictably and visibly available to students for individual, informal conversations — before and after class, in hallways, during office hours — particularly when course content is emotionally demanding. When students discuss sensitive topics such as race, they may experience anxiety or discomfort; the instructor's role is to signal that this discomfort is normal, expected, and workable, and that a trusted adult is available to help them process it. Presence is carried out through consistent availability, empathetic listening, and brief check-ins rather than formal counseling.

## Design Implications

Perceived teacher availability and warmth are among the strongest correlates of student engagement, motivation, and belonging across grade levels [Learner-centered teacher-student relationships are associated with higher engagement, motivation, and achievement.](../claims/teacher-student-relationships-improve-engagement.md) [+S]. Presence works by protecting students' sense of belonging at the moments it is most threatened — for example, when a discussion surfaces feelings of stereotype threat or social exclusion — and belonging is a causal lever on persistence and performance, not merely a byproduct of it [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes for at-risk students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+S]. The strategy is deliberately relational rather than instructional: it does not teach content, but it keeps students in a psychological state where learning from difficult content is possible.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Predictable, low-barrier availability: consistent times and places where students know they can talk (e.g., office hours, arrival/dismissal windows)
- Empathetic listening skills: the ability to receive emotional disclosure without immediately problem-solving, judging, or escalating
- Clear boundaries and referral knowledge: knowing when a conversation exceeds the teacher's role and requires a counselor or other professional
- Institutional support for the time this takes; presence that exists only when convenient is not perceived as presence

#### Constraints
- Effectiveness depends on relationship history built *before* the difficult moment; availability introduced only after a crisis reads as performative [~M]
- Teachers without training in responding to disclosures of trauma or discrimination can inadvertently minimize or amplify distress [-W]
- In large-enrollment or online settings, individual presence scales poorly; unmediated attempts (e.g., "come talk to me anytime" with no structure) produce low uptake [-M]
- If availability is perceived as surveillance or as conditional on "correct" emotional responses, it suppresses rather than opens communication [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Structured check-ins**: brief one-on-one or written check-ins ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md)) at the start of class, which normalize emotional processing for the whole group rather than singling anyone out
- **Written channels**: exit tickets, anonymous question forms, or email for students who will not initiate face-to-face conversation
- **Peer-mediated presence**: training peer facilitators so support does not depend solely on the teacher's availability
- **Online equivalents**: predictable virtual office hours, discussion-forum presence, and personalized feedback turnaround as proxies for physical availability

### Target Learners
- Students navigating emotionally charged content (race, identity, current events) who need a trusted adult to process reactions [Brief empathic discipline interventions improve teacher-student relationships and reduce suspensions for marginalized students.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S]
- Students at risk of disengagement or exclusion, for whom a single relationship with a teacher can be protective [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes for at-risk students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+S]
- Students who rarely seek help; presence must be actively offered to them, since they will not self-refer [-W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Affective and social-emotional goals: emotional regulation, trust, and classroom community
- Enabling goals: sustaining participation in difficult discussions ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)) by lowering the emotional cost of engagement
- Belonging and persistence: keeping students enrolled and engaged through moments of doubt

### Instructions
1. **Establish availability before it is needed.** Announce and keep consistent open times; presence must predate the difficult conversation to be credible.
2. **Normalize discomfort in the group setting.** Frame anxiety about sensitive topics as expected and workable, so individual conversations do not feel like exceptions ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md)).
3. **Listen before responding.** In individual conversations, lead with empathetic listening rather than correction or reassurance; students' perception of being understood drives the relationship effect [Learner-centered teacher-student relationships are associated with higher engagement, motivation, and achievement.](../claims/teacher-student-relationships-improve-engagement.md) [+S]
4. **Follow up.** A brief check-in a day or two later signals the concern was genuinely heard, not merely received.
5. **Refer when appropriate.** Recognize the boundary between emotional support and counseling, and have referral pathways ready.

## Examples
- A middle-school teacher holds open classroom time 15 minutes before school and stays after dismissal, and students processing feelings after a discussion of racial discrimination know they can drop in without an appointment.
- In a large lecture course on American history, the instructor pairs each difficult unit with a required short written reflection and responds individually to a sample of entries, creating written one-on-one contact at scale.
- [Facing History and Ourselves](https://www.facinghistory.org) curricula explicitly build teacher availability and reflection time into units on race and identity, pairing difficult content with structured opportunities for students to process reactions with the teacher.
