Implement blended BOPPPS teaching by combining online platform phases with the six BOPPPS steps¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends popularizing the online and offline blended BOPPPS teaching model in medical colleges and universities. Implementation combines students' pre-class preparation, in-class participation, and post-class follow-up on an online platform with the six BOPPPS steps: bridge-in with questions, classic cases and hot topics posted online; online pre-assessment tests with timely feedback; participatory learning through in-class or online discussions, group reporting, and role-playing; post-assessment self-testing; and two-layer summarization spanning in-class consolidation and post-class reflection.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An online teaching platform such as Xuexi Tong, Yunban Ke, Rain Classes, or MOOC
  • Teachers who design bridge-in questions and targeted teaching for key and challenging curriculum areas

Constraints¶

  • The authors note students may need time adapting to new learning methods, which may affect results, and only a small number of teachers received systematic teaching training

Target Learners¶

  • Medical undergraduate students in Chinese higher medical education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Theoretical knowledge mastery
  • Practical skills
  • Learning interest and initiative
  • Interaction between students and teachers

Affordances¶

  • Boppps Six Step Teaching Model

Claims¶

  • Blended BOPPPS teaching is associated with favorable subjective outcomes including autonomous learning, teamwork, and problem-solving ability [+W]
  • Blended BOPPPS teaching is associated with higher medical theory knowledge scores than LBL [+W]
  • Blended BOPPPS teaching is associated with higher practical skills scores than LBL [+W]
  • Blended BOPPPS teaching is associated with higher classroom satisfaction than LBL [+W]
  • Blended BOPPPS teaching is associated with higher total scores than lecture-based learning in Chinese medical undergraduates [+W]
  • Subgroup analyses show some blended BOPPPS effects are not significant for specific specialties and platforms [~W]
  • Differences among the six novel teaching strategies are minor, and in pairwise meta-analysis only BOPPPS and SBL significantly beat LBL on all three outcomes [+W]
