# Introduce BVI students to text-based compensatory tools—command line, LaTeX, programming languages, and AI image-description—early, ideally in high school

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy equips BVI students with tools that are "entirely textual and linear" and therefore compatible with screen readers and Braille displays. The article recommends introducing the command line early to demystify it, teaching LaTeX in high school so students take notes in an accessible structured format and bypass Braille transcription delays, using command-line Python or R in place of inaccessible scientific calculators, and adopting AI tools like ChatGPT and BeMyEyes for interactive descriptions of images and graphs. Mentorship and role models accompany technical training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- access to screen readers (such as NVDA or Orca), Braille displays, and free or open-source tools
- introduction early in the academic journey, ideally during high school
- mentorship and role models for guidance on assistive tools and collaboration
#### Constraints
- many BVI students are primarily trained on Windows, requiring Orca or Windows Subsystem for Linux to bridge to Linux environments

### Target Learners
- BVI high school students considering higher education in science

### Target Learning Goals
- independent engagement with scientific software, files and complex analyses
- accessible note-taking and mathematical writing
- dataset navigation and visualization
