Refer low-functioning at-risk students for in-depth assessment before or alongside Tier 2 writing intervention¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the latent-class finding that the most globally impaired at-risk students responded most positively to the PAL intervention, the authors recommend that "more in-depth assessment might be useful prior to certain students moving into a Tier 2 intervention in an effort to improve the outcomes from that intervention". They note low-functioning students may otherwise fall through the cracks of early intervention efforts. The strategy uses cognitive profiling to inform RtI placement decisions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Baseline cognitive assessment data capable of distinguishing latent subtypes such as Specific-Deficit and Low-g profiles

Constraints¶

  • The authors label the subtype-by-treatment findings preliminary and note such interactions have not been uncovered in the reading literature

Target Learners¶

  • at-risk elementary students with globally lower cognitive functioning

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improved response to Tier 2 written-language intervention

Affordances¶

  • Rti Three Tier Framework Writing

Claims¶

  • At-risk second graders with globally lower cognitive functioning (Low-g latent class) responded most positively to the PAL intervention, with a large effect by fall of third grade [+M]
  • PAL lesson plans delivered in a Tier 2 RtI model produced an accelerated rate of growth in writing skills for at-risk second graders [+W]
  • Language, attention/executive function, and reading skills did not significantly moderate response to the PAL writing intervention [~W]
