# Introduce formal mathematical systems through contexts sustained by everyday sense rather than teaching them disembedded first

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation for mathematics educators: do not treat mathematical systems as formal subjects from the outset. Instead, formal systems should be introduced "in contexts which allow them to be sustained by human daily sense". This follows from their finding that real-life and word problems may provide the 'daily human sense' that guides intuitive correct solutions, and their doubt about teaching operations disembedded before application. It is a pedagogical recommendation drawn from the study's results and discussion, not a separately tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators must identify everyday contexts meaningful to the learners that can carry the operations to be taught, as the market transactions carried multiplication and change calculations
#### Constraints
- The authors do not conclude that schools should simply let children develop their own computational routines: they note written school computation overcomes limitations of mental computation, such as the gross inefficiency of repeated chunked additions with large numbers

### Target Learners
- school children learning mathematical operations, particularly from lower socio-economic strata

### Target Learning Goals
- learning mathematical operations and their application to real-life and word problems
