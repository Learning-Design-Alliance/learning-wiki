# Use large-scale quantitative analysis of online reviews as a complement to smaller-scale qualitative audience feedback for assessing documentary impact

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that producers of scientific documentaries supplement labor-intensive qualitative audience studies (interviews, thematic analysis) with quantitative sentiment and impact classification of user-generated online reviews. The authors state their "large-scale quantitative analysis of the impact of these documentaries would be a useful complement to the smaller-scale audience-based feedback the AVL currently collects," and that their trained model "can be useful in future productions assessment by the AVL and other teams to assess the impact of their produced works." This provides scalable, reproducible feedback on which aspects of a presentation meet audience needs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trained and evaluated classifier (such as the fine-tuned GPT4o model) and a supply of user-generated reviews from a platform such as Amazon
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that LLM-based classifiers often make mistakes in predicting the best label, and that self-reported reviews collected only once cannot establish persistence of reported impacts

### Target Learners
- general public viewers of scientific documentaries

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing audience engagement, attitudes, and cognition for science communication
