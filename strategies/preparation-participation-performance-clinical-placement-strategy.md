# Structure clinical placement learning as a progression of preparation, participation and performance in medication-related work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends promoting students' preparation for, participation in and performance of medication-related work during clinical placements. Preparation means introducing students to the electronic prescribing system and decision supports; participation means discussing pharmacotherapeutic reasoning with supervising physicians; performance means students carrying out the entire prescribing process themselves, as outlined in the WHO practical manual to good prescribing. The authors state clinical placements "deserve particular emphasis from an educational perspective".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supervised clinical placements with medication-related work tasks
- Access to electronic prescribing systems and decision support during placements
#### Constraints
- The current electronic medical record system does not permit students to prescribe under supervision, which the authors identify as a gap requiring better integration between medical education and healthcare

### Target Learners
- medical students on clinical placements

### Target Learning Goals
- translating theoretical pharmacotherapy knowledge into prescribing practice
