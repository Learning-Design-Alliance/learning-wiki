# Measure students' NFC to identify who needs support in developing academic interest, and foster NFC through optimal challenge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that NFC predicts later interest but not the reverse, the authors propose measuring students' NFC to identify learners needing interest support, and promoting NFC development through optimally challenging learning contexts supported by teacher enthusiasm, task-value feedback, task structuring, and individualized instruction. They argue that "interventions that promote academic interest may not be effective in promoting the development of NFC, but fostering NFC may be effective in promoting the development of interest—and therefore engagement—in school."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Self-report NFC measures are available for students from elementary to secondary school; interventions require evidence from multivariate studies with frequent measurement points, which the authors say is still needed
#### Constraints
- The authors state their findings cannot be the basis for causal interpretations and call the practical implications tentative

### Target Learners
- secondary school students, especially students with lower NFC

### Target Learning Goals
- academic interest development and engagement in school subjects
