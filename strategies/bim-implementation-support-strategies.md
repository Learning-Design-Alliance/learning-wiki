# Implementation supports for BIM: faculty training, hierarchical small groups with contribution tracking, and shared case resources

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends concrete measures for sustaining BIM implementation. Faculty challenges "may be addressed through specialized training, the use of online resources, and increased peer collaboration"; workload and uneven participation can be managed via "small-group hierarchical teaching and individual contribution tracking", using collaborative platforms to monitor literature-review activity and discussion frequency; resources can be sustained through open-source materials, free platforms, inter-institutional sharing, industry partnerships, or grants; and complexity can be reduced with course design templates, educational design experts, and a modular approach.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaborative platforms to monitor student engagement and ensure equitable contributions
#### Constraints
- Students with weaker learning abilities or lower autonomy may experience increased workload, potentially leading to disengagement, reduced efficiency, and uneven group participation

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate public health students

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained implementation of student-centered instruction across multiple course sessions
