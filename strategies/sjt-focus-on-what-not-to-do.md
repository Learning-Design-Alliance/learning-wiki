# Focus an SJT on assessing the ability to correctly identify what one should not do

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that selection SJTs assess applicants' ability to recognise inappropriate response options, because the CD-based (what not to do) score showed stronger convergent validity than the HH-based (what to do) score. The authors conclude "It may be promising to focus SJTs on the ability to correctly identify inappropriate response options (i.e. what one should not do)", and suggest this could support screening out unsuitable applicants, since SJTs appear more informative at the lower end of the distribution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Response options must include deductively established undesirable (CD-based) options whose inappropriateness commands broad consensus
#### Constraints
- The application of an SJT as a screen-out test must account for the high base rate of suitable applicants and the low prevalence of unprofessional behaviour; the stronger convergent validity of the CD-based score has not yet been linked to future performance or tested for other constructs such as empathy

### Target Learners
- medical school applicants being screened in selection procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying unsuitable applicants by detecting poor judgement of inappropriate behaviour
