# Integrate financial education as a requirement in regular high school rather than leaving it as optional summer camp enrollment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend embedding financial literacy instruction in the regular school curriculum because voluntary summer enrollment selects against the very families who most need it. Their finding that "parents with present bias are less likely to enroll their students" implies optional camps miss these students, and time and transportation constraints further limit access. They suggest policymakers and educators make financial education a high school requirement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Availability of instructional time within the regular school day and a financial literacy curriculum such as the FFFL themes used at the camp
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a small, single-site program; the authors state the small scale of the program limits generalization of the results

### Target Learners
- low-income, predominantly Hispanic middle and high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- financial knowledge and financial capability
