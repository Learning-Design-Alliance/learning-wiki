# Uncluttered Environment

## Description
A learning environment with minimal visual distractions is created by storing supplies and materials off of tables and desks and limiting decorations and other things hanging on the walls. When students are the primary users of the space, the majority of materials should be for students. A de-cluttered learning environment allows the focus to be on learning. This practice can be used for any learning environment, including classrooms, workspaces, and homes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated time for decluttering and organizing the learning space. Storage solutions such as shelves, cabinets, and bins. A commitment to maintaining a clutter-free environment.
#### Constraints
- May require significant effort and resources to implement initially. Some learners may find a completely minimalist environment uninspiring or sterile. Regular maintenance is required to prevent clutter from accumulating.

- Reduces cognitive load and distractions, enhances focus and attention, improves visual processing, promotes a sense of calm and order

### Personalization
- Adjust the level of decluttering based on the individual needs and preferences of learners. Provide personalized storage solutions or visual barriers for students who need additional support.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learners' ability to focus and engage in learning activities. Collect feedback from learners on the impact of the uncluttered environment on their learning experience. Compare academic performance and behavior before and after implementing the strategy.

## Impact
- Studies show that reducing visual clutter can improve attention, reduce stress, and enhance creativity. Research suggests that well-organized and visually appealing learning environments can positively impact student achievement and well-being.
