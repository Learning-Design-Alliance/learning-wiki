# Run the rotating saddle demonstration as an interactive sequence with student prediction, incremental frequency increases, and repeated dispensation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article prescribes a classroom procedure: present the stationary saddle, dispense LN2 so students observe it sliding down the negative curvature, prompt students "to predict factors that might enable the LN2 bead to remain trapped near the saddle's center once rotation begins", demonstrate instability below threshold, then increase frequency in increments of approximately 50 millihertz and repeat dispensation to reveal the sharp transition. This approach "aligns with research-based best practices for interactive demonstrations, which emphasize prediction, discussion, and conceptual reasoning".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An overhead camera with a frame rate of at least 60 frames per second and additional directional lighting for visibility of the few-millimeter droplets
#### Constraints
- The stated frequencies (start at or below approximately 1.15 rps, threshold near 1.2 rps) refer to the asymmetric saddle with β = 2.67

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large lecture settings

### Target Learning Goals
- dynamic stability concepts
- prediction and conceptual reasoning about stability thresholds
