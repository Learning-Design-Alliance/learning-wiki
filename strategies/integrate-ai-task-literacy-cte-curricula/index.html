Integrate AI task literacy into CTE curricula now, ahead of employer adoption¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The brief recommends that CTE practitioners integrate AI task literacy into curricula while adoption in Heartland-heavy occupations is still low, so students shape adoption rather than react to it. It specifies teaching "the augmentation/automation/displacement distinction" explicitly so students understand how AI enters diagnostics, recordkeeping, and planning before touching a trade's physical or judgment-based core. Instructional time should focus on occupations showing early augmentation, where AI expands worker scope, rather than those trending toward displacement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Curriculum decisions made while adoption in Heartland-heavy occupations is still low, before employers standardize on any one approach

Constraints¶

  • Focus instructional time on early-augmentation occupations rather than displacement-trending ones

Target Learners¶

  • CTE students in Heartland-region programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • AI task literacy; understanding how AI enters diagnostics, recordkeeping, and planning

Affordances¶

  • Augmentation Automation Displacement Distinction

Claims¶

  • The gap between technical AI exposure and actual employer adoption is a limited opportunity window for CTE programs to shape adoption [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Integrate AI literacy into health sciences curricula and tailor career counselling to programme type
  • Combine accurate AI labor-market information, employability-building activities, and growth-oriented career support for students facing AI employment threat
  • Retrain workers for occupations complementary to, rather than competing with, automation
