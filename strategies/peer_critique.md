# Peer Critique

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Peer critique is a structured strategy in which learners review one another's work against explicit criteria and deliver actionable feedback, then revise their own work in response. It is carried out through protocols (e.g., gallery walks, "two stars and a wish," critical friends protocols) that specify what reviewers look for, how comments are phrased, and how authors respond.

## Design Implications

Peer critique works because evaluating someone else's work forces learners to articulate quality criteria, which strengthens their own performance monitoring; structured peer feedback produces learning gains comparable to instructor feedback in many contexts [Topping's review of peer assessment](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543068003249) [+M]. The critique must be anchored in criteria or exemplars — unstructured comments ("looks good") do not improve work. Feedback is most useful when it targets the task and the process rather than the person [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit criteria, a rubric, or annotated exemplars so reviewers share a definition of quality
- Training and modeling of feedback quality — often by critiquing a sample work as a whole class first
- A revision opportunity: critique without revision becomes a dead-end exercise
- Psychological safety norms (critique the work, not the author)

#### Constraints
- Untrained reviewers default to vague praise or personal comments, which produce no measurable improvement [-M]
- Peer grades are unreliable for high-stakes evaluation; peer critique is a learning activity, not an assessment instrument, unless calibrated [~M]
- Strong status differences or friendship dynamics distort feedback honesty [-W]
- Novices may lack the domain knowledge to detect substantive flaws, limiting critique depth to surface features [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Warm/cool feedback protocols** (e.g., EL Education's critical friends): separate praise from improvement suggestions
- **Anonymous or blind review** to reduce social bias, common in platforms like [Peergrade](https://peergrade.io) and [SWoRD/Peerceptiv](https://peerceptiv.com)
- **Calibrated review**: reviewers first rate exemplars with known quality and get accuracy feedback before reviewing peers
- **Gallery walk / silent critique** with sticky notes for fast, low-stakes cycles

### Target Learners
- Most effective in middle grades through higher education; requires enough metacognitive maturity to evaluate against criteria [~W]
- Particularly valuable for learners who overestimate their own work quality — reviewing others builds the evaluative judgment they lack for their own drafts [+M]
- Reviewers with moderate (not minimal) expertise give the most useful feedback [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Evaluative judgment: internalizing what quality looks like in a domain
- Revision and drafting processes in writing, design, and project work
- Communication and professional feedback practices

### Instructions
1. Establish criteria: share a rubric or co-construct one; show annotated exemplars of strong and weak work (contrast supports abstraction [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M])
2. Model critique: as a class, critique a sample work using the protocol, with the instructor thinking aloud about phrasing and specificity
3. Exchange work in pairs or small groups; reviewers identify strengths and specific, actionable improvements tied to criteria
4. Authors paraphrase the feedback back and state what they will change (ensures uptake)
5. Revise; optionally run a second critique round on the revision
6. Debrief: discuss feedback patterns and what they reveal about the quality criteria

## Examples
- **EL Education (Expeditionary Learning)** — crew-level critique cycles and "kind, specific, helpful" norms are embedded in project-based curricula ([eleducation.org](https://eleducation.org)); students revise expedition products through multiple critique rounds
- **Peerceptiv (formerly SWoRD)** — calibrated peer review for writing at scale in university courses ([peerceptiv.com](https://peerceptiv.com))
- **Studio critique in art and design education** — the canonical professional form: public, criteria-anchored critique followed by revision of the piece
