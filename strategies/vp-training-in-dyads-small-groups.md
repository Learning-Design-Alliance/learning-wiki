# Conduct virtual patient clinical reasoning training in dyads or small groups of two to three students rather than individually

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that clinical reasoning practice with virtual patients be organised in pairs or small groups of two to three students, because group discussion during case work increases interaction and supports reasoning. Students reported that "participating in a small group of 2–3 students facilitated better learning through discussion and collaboration," and the authors conclude such training "should be recommended to be conducted in dyads or small groups of students rather than individually."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to virtual patient platforms that can be used by two or three students simultaneously around one case
#### Constraints
- Benefit documented for dyads in this rheumatology undergraduate setting; the authors note future studies could extend collaboration aspects to students from different professions

### Target Learners
- third-year medical students on clinical placements

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- communication and collaboration skills
