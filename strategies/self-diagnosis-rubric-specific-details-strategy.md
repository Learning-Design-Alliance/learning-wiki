# Provide a self-diagnosis rubric requiring students to explain specific missing details when reviewing their solutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
When asking students to self-diagnose their problem solutions, provide a rubric structured around problem-solving steps that explicitly requires explaining what is missing in problem description (sketch, knowns, unknowns, target variable), solution construction, and answer checking. The article found this maximal-guidance condition produced the best diagnosis of both physics and presentation mistakes, and suggests that clear instruction requiring specific details is needed for students to diagnose better.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor provides an outlined correct solution and a rubric listing problem-solving steps; students fill in the rubric during a dedicated class time slot
#### Constraints
- Coaching was minimal given TA time constraints in a large college classroom; one session of modeling with almost no feedback may be insufficient

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-diagnosis of problem-solving mistakes in physics principles and solution presentation
