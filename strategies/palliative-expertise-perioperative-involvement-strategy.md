# Involve physicians with palliative medicine expertise in perioperative care and determine treatment limitations beforehand through structured advance care planning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that perioperative care of palliative patients involve "physicians who have palliative medicine expertise, ideally anesthesiologists with additional training in palliative medicine", ideally as a multi-professional team. It further recommends including "the perioperative handling of an advance directive and limitations of treatment in the process of advanced care planning", so the exact handling is determined in advance and only applied perioperatively.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Anesthesiologists without additional training cannot, per se, be regarded as palliative medicine experts, since palliative medicine requires more knowledge than one specialty can cover alone
#### Constraints
- The authors state the anesthesiological preoperative interview cannot provide the right framework for the complete care of a patient under palliative care, especially for an end-of-life discussion

### Target Learners
- anesthesiologists without specialty training in palliative medicine (specialists and trainees)

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing and adequately addressing perioperative challenges of patients under palliative care
- conducting advance care planning and limitation-of-treatment discussions
