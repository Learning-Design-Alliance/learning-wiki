# Provide teletherapy-specific skills training that targets the therapeutic skills impacted by remote delivery and allocates resources by demonstrated performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends moving teletherapy training beyond ethics and guidelines toward actual skill enhancement. It states that few programs "prioritise the enhancement of therapeutic skills" and argues for "teletherapy‐specific skills training that targets the attributes impacted in remote settings (e.g., alliance rupture repair alliance, verbal fluency) and incorporate practice opportunities", citing simulation-based deliberate practice such as Lin et al. (2025). Training should be tailored to each therapist's strengths and weaknesses, with resources prioritised by actual performance rather than formal qualifications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular assessment of each therapist's teletherapy skills to identify major areas of growth and allocate training resources by demonstrated needs
#### Constraints
- The article notes most existing teletherapy training programs focus on ethics, guidelines and policies rather than therapeutic skills, so such training may be difficult to access

### Target Learners
- licensed therapists
- psychotherapy trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- therapeutic interpersonal skills in teletherapy, including alliance rupture repair responsiveness and verbal fluency
