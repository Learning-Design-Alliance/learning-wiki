# Combine patient-derived slides with curated slide libraries to guarantee breadth of morphology exposure in PBS curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The consensus recommendations specify that PBS teaching materials should draw on two sources: slides from patients under the trainee's own care and formal slide libraries of high-yield morphologies. The article states "Procurement of PBS slides should encompass both patients under the care of the trainee and slide 'libraries' to ensure all trainees see similar morphology regardless of personal experience on ward and consult rotations." Slide libraries are of specific importance for rare diagnoses or diagnoses missed because of referral bias, such as sickle cell disease or endemic parasitic infections.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to collated slide libraries of high-yield morphologies in addition to patient-derived slides
#### Constraints
- Trainees at centers with low sample volume may not encounter certain morphologies without libraries, per the article's referral-bias discussion

### Target Learners
- hematology fellows

### Target Learning Goals
- broad exposure to normal and pathologic PBS morphologies required for graduation competency
