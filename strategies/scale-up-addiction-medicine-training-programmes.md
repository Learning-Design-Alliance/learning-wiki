# Establish and scale up addiction medicine training programmes with continued funding, combined didactic and interactive learning, and integration into graduate medical education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that programmes like the Vancouver fellowships be established, funded and expanded: "funding current programmes is not enough; new programmes should be established". It endorses "a combined didactic and interactive learning strategy for SUD education" and argues integration should address the two major barriers to practising addiction medicine: insufficient knowledge, training and experience with patients with SUDs, and a lack of specialist support. Promoting SUD education among generalist physicians can heighten chances of screening, early diagnosis and treatment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Continued funding for programmes and refinement of activities
- Addressing insufficient knowledge, training and experience working with patients with SUDs and a lack of specialist support
#### Constraints
- The article states training alone will not solve the SUD problem, though it is a conditio sine qua non for successful treatment

### Target Learners
- medical students
- residents
- physicians
- fellows in addiction medicine

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnosis and treatment of substance use disorders
- clinician-scientist research skills in substance use
