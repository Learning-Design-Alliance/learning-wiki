Treat peer defending as a partial support for victims rather than a stand-alone fix for bullying¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's implication statement holds that "being defended only partly relieves victims' plight by increasing their feelings of belongingness to the peer group" and that being defended did not reduce victimization or depressive feelings nor promote self-esteem. Antibullying efforts that promote peer defending should therefore be paired with additional supports for victims rather than expected to stop bullying or lift adjustment by themselves. This follows directly from the longitudinal null findings on victimization, self-esteem, and depression.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Defending promotion should not be relied on as the sole mechanism for reducing victimization or improving victims' psychosocial adjustment.

Constraints¶

  • The article states "the beneﬁcial effects of being defended may be more limited than currently assumed" and that more research is needed on whether and how the effectiveness of peer defending can be increased.

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students who are victims of bullying

Target Learning Goals¶

  • safe education free of bullying
  • victims' psychosocial functioning and belonging

Claims¶

  • Victims with at least one defender at the start of the school year report higher feelings of belonging at year's end than nondefended victims, but lower belonging than nonvictims [+W]
  • Defended and nondefended victims did not differ significantly in severity of victimization, self-esteem, or depressive symptoms at the end of the school year [+W]
  • Being defended related more positively to victims' adjustment in intervention schools than in control schools [+W]
  • The number of defenders was positively associated with victims' feelings of belonging but unrelated to self-esteem, depressive symptoms, and victimization [+W]
