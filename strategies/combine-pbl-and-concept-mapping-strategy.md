# Incorporate PBL and concept mapping into the curriculum to enhance students' knowledge structures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' concluding recommendation is that "educators aiming to enhance their students' knowledge structures should incorporate PBL and concept mapping in the curriculum." Concept mapping is positioned as a feasible tool for evaluating and monitoring students' learning in PBL, with maps collected after each problem case to track how knowledge changes as discussion proceeds. In the study, students received two hours of concept-mapping instruction and were given two sets of 10 terms plus freedom to add their own.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two hours of concept-mapping instruction before students construct maps
- Term sets related to the domain from which students build maps
#### Constraints
- The authors state the study "did not explore the long term effect of concept mapping coupled with PBL" and lacked explanations for learning-material amount relative to cognitive loading

### Target Learners
- undergraduate health-professions students, here occupational therapy undergraduates

### Target Learning Goals
- developing an integrated knowledge structure
- clinical reasoning
