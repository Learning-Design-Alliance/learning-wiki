Standardize recycle materials used for laboratory practicum to make science practicum safer¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In the Conclusions and Suggestions section the authors recommend that recycle materials used for lab practicum be standardized: "the need to standardize the recycle materials to be used for lab practicum. Thus the science practicum would be safer." This is an implementable follow-up action for institutions adopting recycle-materials practicum, addressing safety once the materials are in regular use.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutions adopting recycle-materials practicum should define standards for the materials used

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • Open University distance-education students in science practicum courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Safe and effective conduct of science practicum activities

Claims¶

  • Using recycle materials for science practicum has a statistically significant effect on Open University students' basic science process skills [+M]
  • Students responded positively to using recycle materials in science practicum (questionnaire mean 2.82 of 3) [+M]
  • The article attributes the recycle-materials advantage to problem-solving challenge, motivation, and creativity, and notes control-class students without worksheets scored lower [+W]
  • Experimental-class students scored higher on average (78 vs 70) on the science process skills test [+M]
  • Score distributions differed: the experimental class majority scored in the 80–90 range (49%) while the control class majority scored in the 70–80 range (64%) [+M]
