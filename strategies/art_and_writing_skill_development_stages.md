# Art and Writing Skill Development Stages

## Description
Drawing and writing skills in young children develop through distinct stages, progressing from random scribbling to letter and word practice. These stages include: 1) Random Scribbling (15 months to 2½ years), where children discover the lines and scribbles they create through movement; 2) Controlled Scribbling (2 years to 3 years), where scribbles become more controlled with repeated marks; 3) Lines and Patterns (2½ years to 3½ years), where children understand that writing is made up of lines, curves, and patterns; 4) Pictures of Objects or People (3 years to 5 years), where children begin to draw recognizable images with increasing detail; and 5) Letter and Word Practice (3 to 5 years), where children begin to experiment with letters, particularly those in their own names, and understand that text and print have meaning. Encouraging art and writing skills involves providing a variety of art materials, noticing the process rather than just the product, helping children express strong feelings through art, and displaying their work to show its value.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Variety of art materials (crayons, markers, paint, play-dough, etc.), paper, and a supportive environment.
#### Constraints
- Requires parents/educators to focus on the process rather than the product, which may be challenging for some. Timetables are approximate, and children may master skills faster or slower than indicated.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the child's drawings and writing to identify the stage of development. Assess the child's creativity, self-expression, and problem-solving skills. Track the child's understanding of the power and meaning of words.

## Impact
- Creative activities help children learn how to solve problems, come up with their own answers, discover the cause-and-effect of their actions, and feel confident about the choices they make. Art experiences help children develop independence within limits, and gives them the opportunity to represent their ideas on paper or in other formats. Most important, creative expression lets children tap into the magic of their own imaginations.
