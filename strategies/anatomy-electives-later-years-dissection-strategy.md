# Offer anatomy electives during clinical years, shift complex anatomy teaching to later course years, and incorporate dissection as a teaching method

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes concrete curriculum actions: medical schools "could offer electives in anatomy for interested students during the clinical years" and "shift the teaching of complex anatomy to the later years" so students learn clinically relevant anatomy in context. It also recommends that "Anatomy education should also incorporate dissection as a teaching method" and that prior anatomy education be encouraged before graduate-entry courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to dissection facilities or elective anatomy units within the medical program
#### Constraints
- Only one of twelve Australian graduate-entry programs currently requires prior anatomy units, limiting prerequisite-based approaches

### Target Learners
- medical students, particularly those in clinical years and graduate-entry students without prior anatomy

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of clinically relevant anatomy knowledge and confidence for internship
