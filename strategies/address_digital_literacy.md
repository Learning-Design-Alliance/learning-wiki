# Address Digital Literacy

## Description
Addressing digital literacy means deliberately teaching the digital skills, vocabulary, and tool conventions a course or program depends on, instead of assuming learners arrive with them. It includes diagnosing actual skill levels, providing direct instruction and practice in the required tools and concepts, and making support available as tasks increase in complexity. The strategy treats digital competence as learnable content, not a prerequisite.

## Design Implications

Digital skill varies widely within any cohort, and self-reported confidence is a poor proxy for actual competence [Hargittai's work on the "second-level digital divide" shows skill gaps persist even when access gaps close] [+M]. The "digital native" premise — that younger learners acquire digital competence incidentally — has been empirically discredited [Kirschner & De Bruyckere review evidence that age predicts neither skill nor multitasking ability] [X]. Explicit instruction in tool use and terminology reduces extraneous cognitive load during content learning, freeing working memory for the actual subject matter [Teaching tool mechanics separately prevents tool navigation from competing with content processing.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A diagnostic activity early in the course that surfaces actual skill levels (not just self-report)
- Explicit instruction in required tools, terminology, and conventions before or alongside their first assessed use
- Low-stakes practice opportunities with the tools before high-stakes tasks ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) used formatively)
- Ongoing support channels — help documentation, peer support, or instructor check-ins ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md))

#### Constraints
- Assumes instructor time and institutional infrastructure; under-resourced settings may not sustain tool-specific support
- Over-scaffolding digital skills for already-proficient learners wastes time and can reduce engagement [Guidance that is redundant for experts imposes extra load rather than reducing it.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M] — differentiate or make support optional
- Tool-specific training transfers poorly; teaching generic concepts (file management, search evaluation, privacy) generalizes better than click-path memorization
- One-time orientation sessions decay quickly; skills need spaced practice within authentic tasks or they are forgotten

#### Implementation Variability
- **Just-in-time micro-instruction:** short tutorials embedded at the point of need rather than front-loaded training
- **Differentiated pathways:** diagnostic results route learners to either foundational modules or advanced extensions ([Adaptive Learning](../principles/adaptive-learning.md))
- **Peer-expert models:** confident learners demonstrate workflows, pairing skill-building with [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md)
- **Embedded glossaries and advance organizers** that pre-teach terminology before technical readings ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))

### Target Learners
- Adult learners and career-changers returning to education after long gaps, who often face the largest skill gaps despite high motivation
- Learners from lower-income backgrounds, who show systematically lower web-use skills even with equal access [Hargittai & Hinnant find socioeconomic status predicts skill independent of access] [+M]
- First-generation and international students navigating unfamiliar platform conventions
- Less beneficial as a whole-class requirement for demonstrably proficient learners [Expertise reversal: redundant instruction for skilled learners can depress performance.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency with the specific tools a course requires
- Conceptual understanding of digital systems (how files, accounts, permissions, and search work)
- Critical evaluation skills: assessing source credibility, privacy implications, and algorithmic bias
- Self-efficacy with technology, which predicts persistence in online coursework

### Instructions
1. **Diagnose.** Open with a short, ungraded skills inventory and a hands-on task (not a self-rating survey) to establish a baseline.
2. **Pre-teach vocabulary.** Introduce required terminology with plain-language definitions and visuals, following [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md); avoid jargon in instructions until it has been taught.
3. **Model explicitly.** Demonstrate core workflows with narration, following the [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) modeling phase — show the screen, verbalize decisions, and anticipate common errors.
4. **Practice at low stakes.** Provide a sandbox task that uses the same tools as graded work but carries no penalty, with [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) feedback at the task level [Feedback is most effective when it addresses the task and process rather than the learner.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
5. **Embed and space.** Require tool use in subsequent authentic tasks so skills are retrieved and consolidated rather than demonstrated once.
6. **Differentiate.** Offer optional extension support for proficient learners and structured support for novices, adjusting as the course progresses.

## Examples
- **Community college online-course onboarding:** Many institutions (e.g., the California Community Colleges' [Online Education Initiative](https://cccconline.org)) provide pre-course readiness modules covering LMS navigation, file management, and netiquette before credit coursework begins.
- **Northstar Digital Literacy Standards** ([literacymn.org](https://www.literacymn.org/digital-literacy)) — a widely adopted curriculum and assessment framework used in adult basic education to teach and certify essential computer skills.
- **First-week LMS sandbox assignment:** An instructor requires students to submit a practice document, post to a discussion forum, and take an ungraded quiz in the course LMS during week one, with narrated screencast demonstrations of each workflow.
