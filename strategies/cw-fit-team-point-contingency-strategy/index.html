Implement CW-FIT's team point contingency with timer, daily goals, and immediate team rewards¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes a concrete classroom routine: teachers state daily point goals set at approximately 75-85% of the possible intervals, sound a timer at approximately 3-min intervals, scan teams of two to six students, praise and mark points for teams whose every member is on task, and give teams meeting the goal an immediate reward. Points earned are never taken away for misbehavior. This routine establishes the interdependent group contingency at the heart of CW-FIT tier 1.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers divide their classes into teams and display a point chart; the reward must be "short and simple, feasible for teachers, and reinforcing to students"

Constraints¶

  • Timer intervals are adjusted based on behavioral needs, e.g., younger students needed shorter intervals

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students, including students at risk for emotional and behavioral disorders

Target Learning Goals¶

  • demonstrating appropriate social skills and on-task behavior during academic instruction

Affordances¶

  • Cw Fit Program

Claims¶

  • Higher fidelity of CW-FIT implementation is associated with better outcomes on social skill, problem behavior, and academic measures in the treatment group [+W]
  • Teachers and students report high social validity for CW-FIT [+W]
  • CW-FIT improves teacher-rated academic competence on SSBS-2 and CPS-E but not on the SSIS Academic Competence scale [+W]
  • CW-FIT improves teacher-rated social skills of elementary students at risk for EBD, with small effect sizes [+W]
  • CW-FIT shows no significant effect on teacher ratings of problem behaviors at post-test [-W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Implement CW-FIT as 30- to 60-minute sessions three to four times per week, fading monitoring and rewards as behavior improves
  • Use CW-FIT group contingency game format with teams, points, and rewards for appropriate behaviors
