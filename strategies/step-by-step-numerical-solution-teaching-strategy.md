# Teach computational physics through detailed step-by-step construction of a numerical solution to a challenging problem

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's pedagogical strategy is to give undergraduate students explicit, detailed instructions for constructing a numerical solution — the finite-difference method, initial conditions, boundary conditions and seismic source — because the authors found "the available information about the construction of a numerical solution of these equations is not as explicit as it should be". Students work a challenging, realistic problem (wave propagation across a limestone/wet-sand interface) using transversal tools such as numerical methods and data analysis, moving "back an forth between supplying some evidence, and given more premises that allows to reach a logical conclusion". Layer parameters are left as adjustable student decisions to simulate more realistic environments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a working numerical code and its flowchart of subroutines
- A challenging physics problem whose equations cannot be solved analytically
#### Constraints
- The paper deliberately omits mathematical background described elsewhere in the literature and focuses on the numerical solution

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students having their first contact with wave propagation and numerical simulations

### Target Learning Goals
- computational skills
- understanding of seismic wave propagation and stress-strain concepts
