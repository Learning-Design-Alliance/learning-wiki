Use sketch-based problem solving to train imaginative, realistic and logical thinking in physics¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends the sketch knowledge strategy as a way to train thinking skills in physics teaching. It states the sketch "is not only a picture but it is also filled with the quantities, concepts and principles of physics", and lists three benefits of sketching: exercising complex thinking, exercising connections between facts, concepts and principles, and exercising thinking about abstract things. It further claims SKS trains imaginative thinking realized in reality and logical thinking based on reality.

Claims¶

  • Sketch knowledge strategy produces significantly higher metacognitive knowledge test scores than target variable strategy in physics teaching [+M]
  • SKS and TVS show no significant difference at the easiest problem level (level-1), with the advantage appearing at levels 2 and 3 [~M]
  • Most new physics education students and a majority of in-service physics teachers failed a metacognitive knowledge ability test [+W]
