# Concomitant use of VR and physical simulators, matched to procedural versus task-based training needs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends combining simulator types rather than choosing one: "concomitant use of VR and physical simulators is beneficial given the skill transferability [24] and additive effect they have [11]". It further advises matching modality to training goal, with VR and cadavers generally better for full procedural simulation and dry-lab bench models more cost-effective for task-based simulation. Bench-top simulators with real instruments and lasers can overcome VR drawbacks such as lack of safe laser protocol practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to both a VR platform and a physical (bench, cadaveric or 3D-printed) simulator within the training curriculum
#### Constraints
- Validated VR simulators are lacking for open, emergency and reconstructive procedures, where physical models remain the default

### Target Learners
- urology trainees at curriculum and programme design level

### Target Learning Goals
- technical skills acquisition, haemostasis practice, and full procedural simulation
