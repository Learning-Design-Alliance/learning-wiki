# Early identification of undernutrition, especially stunting, followed by timely prevention strategies and community-based feeding and agricultural skills interventions at schools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends identifying undernutrition early, especially stunting, and implementing timely prevention strategies during early childhood. It further urges empowering mothers, daycare mothers, and teachers in lower SES environments with nutrition knowledge, and pursuing "learning agricultural skills... at rural schools in cooperation with community-based feeding interventions" with follow-up strategies to evaluate these interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Government commitment from various departments to address developmental challenges in young children
- Doctors cooperating with the Department of Basic Education to educate schools and parents about optimal nutrition for cognitive outcomes
#### Constraints
- The article states limited resources make it difficult to address undernutrition in South Africa with intervention programs similar to those in developed countries

### Target Learners
- Primary school girls aged 6 to 13 in low SES environments
- Preschool and adolescent girls recommended for future study

### Target Learning Goals
- Protecting academic performance in language and mathematics
- Supporting cognitive development through optimal nutrition
