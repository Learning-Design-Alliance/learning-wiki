# Prepare medical students before interdisciplinary design modules by outlining design stages and promoting interprofessional benefits

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because survey data showed medical students are less positively inclined toward interdisciplinary learning than engineering students, the authors recommend preparatory work before interdisciplinary modules. As they state: "This could include outlining the relevance of each of the stages of the design process and promoting the benefits of interprofessional learning in general." This targets motivational factors and priorities specific to each discipline rather than treating both groups identically. Engineering students, conversely, benefit from exposure to clinical professionals and authentic clinical scenarios.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examination of the relative motivational factors and priorities of medical and engineering students before participation
#### Constraints
- Generalisability of the underlying survey findings was not formally evaluated; response rates limited sample size

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- undergraduate engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- willingness to participate in interdisciplinary teams
- understanding of the design process stages
