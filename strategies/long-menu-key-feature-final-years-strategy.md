# Use long-menu questions in the final years of the curriculum, within key-feature problems, to assess clinical reasoning and patient management skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends deploying long-menu questions selectively rather than universally: because they take more time to develop than Type A items, they should be reserved for the final years of the curriculum and embedded in "more realistic key-feature problems, which focus on assessing clinical reasoning and patient management skills". Key-feature problems sequence questions as in real patient management, from history-taking to diagnosis and treatment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Computer-based exam infrastructure that supports free-text entry with a narrowing answer list, and time to develop long-menu item lists
#### Constraints
- The authors recommend use in the final years of the curriculum, within key-feature problems, taking into account the increased item-development time compared with Type A items

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in the clinical years of the curriculum

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning and patient management skills
