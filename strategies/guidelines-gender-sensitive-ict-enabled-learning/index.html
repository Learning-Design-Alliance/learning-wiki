Four guidelines for designing and implementing inclusive, gender-sensitive ICT-enabled learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review proposes four guidelines mapped to the GAF dimensions: implement ICT-enabled learning "affordably and cost-effectively to ensure girls' access"; design it to be "inclusive and gender-sensitive to improve girls' practice and participation"; use "inclusive and gender-sensitive pedagogies" to facilitate changes in belief and perception, including gender-sensitive teacher education; and embed inclusiveness and gender sensitivity in educational policies. The guidelines direct educators, designers, parents and policymakers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Needs and situation analysis before designing ICT-enabled learning, with local partners and teachers invited to co-design, and women included in all aspects and processes of ICT-enabled learning.

Constraints¶

  • The article states the guidelines are suggested based on reviewing studies conducted in Asia, and putting them into practice requires educators and policymakers to consider the local contexts.

Target Learners¶

  • primary and secondary schoolgirls in Asia

Target Learning Goals¶

  • girls' access and participation in learning activities
  • changes in belief and perception
  • education inclusiveness and equity

Claims¶

  • Digital game-based learning aligns most with practice/participation and belief/perception dimensions, while mobile-enabled learning leads access to assets [+W]
  • Four ICT-enabled learning approaches are used in empirical studies of schoolgirls' learning in Asia, with computer-assisted learning the most common [+W]
  • ICT-enabled learning may change beliefs and perceptions about girls' capacity and ICT's value [+W]
  • ICT-enabled learning improves schoolgirls' access to resources, knowledge, and skills [+W]
  • ICT-enabled learning improves schoolgirls' practice and participation in learning, with girls sometimes scoring better than boys [+W]
  • Fewer reviewed studies align with the policy dimension, and policies ignoring gender differences may deter girls' participation [+W]
