# Confront pupils with a physically incorrect statement to create cognitive conflict and trigger verification and reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
One worksheet introduction type presents an everyday situation containing a physically incorrect statement (e.g. that oil dissolves glass) that pupils must verify experimentally. The article states: "This type of introduction purposefully creates cognitive conflict and activates the need for verification and reasoning", promoting evaluating statements, drawing conclusions from evidence, and metacognitive reflection on one's own ideas. The authors cite research that confronting error stimulates metacognitive processes and promotes long-term cognitive change.
