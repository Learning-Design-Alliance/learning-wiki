Integrate podcasts into EFL classes as a supplementary listening tool, both in and out of the classroom¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that EFL teachers introduce podcasts as listening materials to raise motivation and listening ability, using them "both directly in the classroom and out of the classroom as a supplementary tool". In this study, podcasts from www.china232.com were normalized with learning tasks, played about 15 minutes per session in class, and re-listened to at home as homework. Curriculum developers are advised to attend to students' openness to new technology.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Careful planning and adequate training of teachers so podcasting becomes an effective teaching and learning tool

Constraints¶

  • Podcasts should not be used as the sole tool of instruction or the focus of attention in L2 courses (citing O'Bryan and Hegelheimer, 2007)

Target Learners¶

  • Intermediate EFL learners in language institutes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Listening comprehension
  • Motivation for listening

Claims¶

  • Interviewed learners unanimously perceived improvement in their listening comprehension and valued podcasts as a supplementary tool [+W]
  • The podcast group significantly outperformed the control group on the listening comprehension post-test [+M]
  • Using podcasts as a motivational teaching strategy significantly increased Iranian EFL learners' motivation for listening (ELCMS) [+M]
  • Participants held predominantly positive perceptions of podcasts as learning tools [+M]
  • The two groups did not differ significantly in listening ability at pretest, supporting group homogeneity [+M]
  • Listening to podcasts improved EFL students' self-regulation more than listening to radio programs [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use podcasts on mobile phones for extensive out-of-class listening practice
