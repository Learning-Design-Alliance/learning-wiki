# Use conceptual blending analysis with gesture analysis as analytic tools for modeling learners' cognitive dynamics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 4 studies (3 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends conceptual blending, combined with gesture analysis, as machinery for understanding how novices and experts blend multiple metaphors when reasoning about science concepts. The authors write, "We believe that cognitive blending offers one such tool" for seeing how knowledge resources interact and generate new knowledge structures. Concretely, researchers can code verbal predicates for ontological markers (substance vs. location) and layer frame-by-frame gesture analysis onto speech to infer blended mental spaces via coordination of meaning, temporal proximity, and speech-gesture mismatch.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video data capturing both speech and co-occurring gesture; predicate categories adapted to the concept under study
#### Constraints
- Gesture analysis is interpretive rather than following a systematic coding scheme; the analysis does not claim blending is prevalent or typical

### Target Learners
- learners and experts in science contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- modeling how students build new knowledge structures about abstract science concepts
