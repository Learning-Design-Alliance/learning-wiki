# Deliver a short post-exploration instructional video to complement science simulations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a brief instructional video delivered after students explore a science simulation, teaching an effective inquiry strategy while explicitly warning students about the allure of their default strategy. The videos had a teen-friendly wacky host in the style of a children's science show. The authors list among their contributions that they "offered an easily implemented and scalable instructional design that can complement the influx of science simulations," and found the post-simulation video worked equally well whether administered at home or at school.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students first explore the simulation so the video's example is relevant and a pretest of strategy use is available
#### Constraints
- Conducted during COVID with online administration; the authors caution generality to normal schooling or other regions is untested

### Target Learners
- middle-school and early secondary science students
- online and homework learners

### Target Learning Goals
- inquiry strategies for self-directed learning with simulations
