# In classes with many pre-existing student connections, instructors should adapt to and leverage existing interconnectivity rather than assume students start isolated

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Standard advice for interactive-engagement classes assumes most students enter isolated and directs faculty to separate existing friends so new connections form. This article argues that in classes with dense pre-existing networks this may be impossible, and instead "this interconnectivity may be leveraged if instructors can adapt to work with it productively", since some of the work of connecting students has already been done.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need information about pre-existing collaborative structure, e.g. from early-semester sociometric surveys
#### Constraints
- The article notes students outside the existing community, especially those living off-campus and working many hours, may experience difficulty breaking in

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large introductory courses, particularly commuter and nontraditional populations

### Target Learning Goals
- building connections among students and fostering classroom community
