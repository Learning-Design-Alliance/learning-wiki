# Collect periodic surgeon self-reported experience data and adjust for it statistically when a trial's experimental technique is still being learned

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates a two-part approach for surgical RCTs comparing a new technique against an established one: a surgeon inclusion criterion mandating minimum experience, plus periodic collection of experience data. Surgeons reported "the number of previous laparoscopic operations and the number of previous robotic operations that they had performed prior to randomising their first patient" and again at approximately 3-monthly intervals, allowing experience at each operation to be derived and entered into a learning effects regression model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participating surgeons must report previous operation counts at baseline and at regular intervals throughout recruitment so experience at each operation can be derived
#### Constraints
- The mandated minimum experience level trades off against recruitment feasibility; the article states a pragmatic balance had to be found

### Target Learners
- surgeons learning a new operative technique

### Target Learning Goals
- achieving expert proficiency in a new surgical technique
