Strategies

Design future summer school trials and programmes to avoid recruitment, attendance and testing attrition¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 3 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The report draws implementable lessons from the trial's failures: future summer schools "may wish to consider a shorter intervention and greater advance notice for parents", since a four-week format and late recruitment clashed with family holiday plans. For testing, researchers should "explore the use of whole -class testing or using routinely collected data for outcome assessment", and both primary schools supplying baseline data and secondary schools administering outcome testing should be engaged from the outset. Full involvement of independent evaluators in recruitment is also recommended.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sufficient engagement with schools so their role in administering tests is fully understood
  • Greater advance notice for parents considering participation
  • Involvement of secondary schools undertaking outcome assessment from the outset

Constraints¶

  • Day et al. (2013) suggest summer schools over two weeks in length may not be feasible due to clashes with family holidays, yet sufficient duration is needed to improve literacy skills

Target Learners¶

  • pupils at the primary-secondary transition, particularly those eligible for free school meals

Target Learning Goals¶

  • literacy catch-up in reading and writing at the primary-secondary transition

Claims¶

  • The Discover Summer School showed no statistically significant effect on spelling and grammar attainment [-W]
  • The Discover Summer School showed no statistically significant effect on extended writing attainment in an underpowered randomised trial [-W]
  • Observations suggested pupils were engaged by and enjoyed the summer school, with the interactive poetry approach appearing to motivate writing [+W]
  • The Discover Summer School was relatively expensive compared to other literacy catch-up approaches, at an estimated cost of about £1,790 per pupil [-W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Pilot multi-component interventions before trial and choose outcome tests that capture the targeted skill
