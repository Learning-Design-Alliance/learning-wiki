# Educator experiments: pre-lecture fundamentals quizzes, mental math, and prerequisite mathematics assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes formal or informal experiments by which instructors can test science-informed methodologies, with modifications gradual, tested, adapted, and tweaked. Suggestions include supplying 12-20 new essential relationships a day or two before a topic with a 5-minute first-day quiz; activating prior learning by opening mid-topic lectures with a problem from a previous class; contriving estimate-then-calculate and no-calculator tests using whole-number data solvable by mental math; assigning reading on how students can help themselves learn; and using posted editable quizzes on mathematics automaticity to identify topics needing refreshing of memory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- To motivate on-time preparation, a quiz must count to some extent; measurement of progress requires comparison to a widely-accepted standard such as an examination with national norms
#### Constraints
- Every class has unique students, goals, and resources

### Target Learners
- students in courses prior to and including college chemistry and physics

### Target Learning Goals
- preparation for lecture, computational automaticity, and retention of fundamentals
