# Use intelligent tutoring systems with persistent student models to implement expertise-reversal-aligned adaptivity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), `q4` · 2 of 2 report an effect size

## Description
The article identifies intelligent tutoring systems as "a promising option to design instruction in line with the expertise reversal effect": they maintain "a regularly updated estimation of the learners' current domain knowledge and can readily adapt instruction to changes in this knowledge". Because expertise changes during learning, assistance should be gradually faded as expertise grows; the article notes such fading is already prevalent in instruction supporting self-regulation, where learners risk over-relying on regulatory assistance. ITS can also be combined with approaches letting students self-individualize by choosing assistance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A persistent, regularly updated student model of current domain knowledge; repeated assessment of domain knowledge over time
#### Constraints
- The article notes such adaptations and repeated assessment are no trivial task, especially in regular classroom settings

### Target Learners
- individual learners whose domain expertise changes during extended learning processes

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining optimal assistance levels as expertise develops; preventing over-reliance on assistance
