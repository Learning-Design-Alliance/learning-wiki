# Position AI writing-support systems as adjuncts to supervision and prompts for reflective dialogue rather than authoritative sources of truth

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that systems like GAMER PAT serve as a complementary layer of guidance, helping students articulate questions, adopt a meta-perspective, and critically review drafts before meeting supervisors, thereby reducing faculty cognitive load while leaving higher-level judgment to human mentors. They caution that over-reliance may diminish students' sense of authorship and that not all LLM feedback is pedagogically sound, so such systems should be positioned as adjuncts to supervision and prompts for reflective dialogue rather than authoritative sources of truth.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students retain access to standard research supervision; the tool is introduced as supportive, not a replacement for academic mentoring
#### Constraints
- Over-reliance on AI support may diminish students' sense of authorship; LLM feedback is not guaranteed to be pedagogically sound or unbiased

### Target Learners
- graduate students
- novice researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- research writing
- reflective learning
- agency and authorship
