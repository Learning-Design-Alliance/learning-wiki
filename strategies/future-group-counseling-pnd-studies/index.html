Investigate group-based psychological counseling interventions, such as group therapy and group guidance, for reducing families' postnatal depression¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In the conclusion the authors recommend extending this line of research to group formats: "In future studies, the effects of various psychological counseling interventions, such as gro up therapy and group guidance, on the postnatal dep ression levels of families could be investigated." This follows their discussion of group therapy's advantages, including helping people realize they are not alone and seeing problems from a different perspective.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Future studies should compare intervention formats against the family-level outcome measured with the EPDS

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on one small quasi-experimental study with 26 participants and non-random convenience sampling

Target Learners¶

  • prospective mothers and fathers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • preventing postnatal depression in families

Claims¶

  • The authors interpret the weaker individual-level effects as possibly due to the informative-only format, short duration, and highly educated sample [~W]
  • Prospective fathers' postnatal depression scores decreased after the orientation interviews but not statistically significantly [~W]
  • Prospective mothers' postnatal depression scores decreased after the orientation interviews but not statistically significantly [~W]
  • Families (couples analyzed as a whole) who received antenatal orientation interviews showed a statistically significant decrease in postnatal depression scores, while control families did not [+W]
