# Interdisciplinary task-design guidelines: respect disciplinary differences in language, identify cross-disciplinary reasoning similarities, and engage students in seeking coherence across apparently contradictory disciplinary ideas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's research-and-design cycle includes explicit guidelines for constructing interdisciplinary tasks. Teams should "Attend to differences in how disciplines represent and talk about big ideas (such as energy, entropy, work, etc.), but avoid collapsing into a singular language", identify similarities between reasoning strategies across disciplines, engage students in seeking coherence among things they know that appear to contradict each other, engage students in re-evaluating their own and others' ideas, and create space and time for students to coordinate representations from different disciplines.
