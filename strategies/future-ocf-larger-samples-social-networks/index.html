Future studies should use larger samples and test online social networking sites like Facebook or blogs for academic writing feedback¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors' forward-looking recommendation for research on online corrective feedback: replicate with more participants, possibly at different educational levels, and extend the delivery channel beyond email. The Discussion states it is "recommended that in future studies a larger sample size is applied to the study perhaps in different levels of education" and that "online social networking sites like facebook or blogs be tested for use in the academic writing classroom".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Future studies compare feedback modes with larger samples than the two-group comparison used here

Constraints¶

  • The present study's conclusion is preliminary due to its small sample size

Target Learners¶

  • tertiary academic writing students, potentially at different levels of education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving academic writing through online corrective feedback channels

Claims¶

  • Online corrective feedback produced larger reductions in sentence structure, grammar and vocabulary errors than conventional corrective feedback, which showed no grammar improvement [+W]
  • Tertiary L1 Malay learners given online corrective feedback via email outperformed the conventional corrective feedback control group on the final draft [+W]
  • Most students preferred receiving corrective feedback online via email, with ease of submission and time saving the leading reasons [+W]
  • Students were more motivated to correct errors when feedback was delivered electronically, a factor the authors attribute to the appeal of electronic devices [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Future SNA e-learning studies should examine two-mode networks and additional communication channels such as chat rooms, wikis, blogs and microblogs
  • Direct future wiki and blog research toward native-language and writing teaching, and conduct separate meta-analyses per environment
  • Future research recommendations for Facebook-based writing education studies
  • Integrate online corrective feedback into the teaching and learning of academic writing
