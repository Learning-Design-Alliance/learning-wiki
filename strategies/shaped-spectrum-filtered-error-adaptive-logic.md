# Use shaped-spectrum filtered error for rapid adaptive logic response

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
To separate trainee performance from disturbance effects in adaptive logic, the article describes passing the disturbance through a high pass filter and the error through a low pass filter, each with a 5 sec. time constant. This shaped-spectrum, filtered-error scheme has the advantage of fairly rapid response with less influence by the disturbance than instantaneous error, enabling difficulty level to be obtained as a function of filtered error.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Filters must cut off sharply enough that difficulty level is not affected directly by the disturbance
#### Constraints
- The additional lag made the task easier but negated to some extent the attempt to reduce the direct influence of the forcing function on the difficulty level

### Target Learners
- trainees in adaptive manual-control training systems

### Target Learning Goals
- manual control skill
