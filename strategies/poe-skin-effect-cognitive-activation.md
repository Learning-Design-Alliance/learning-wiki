# Deploy the demonstration as a Predict–Observe–Explain sequence in which students commit to predictions before confronting the counterintuitive impedance surge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating the demonstration into lecture via a Predict–Observe–Explain (POE) sequence, since passive observation is often ineffective. Before the measurement, the instructor prompts students to predict the relative AC impedance of both materials compared to their DC baseline, "requiring them to commit to a choice via live polling or immediate written notes". The subsequent measurement confronts them with a counterintuitive result that induces a cognitive conflict, which is then resolved by introducing skin depth as a permeability-dependent length scale.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must commit to a prediction before the measurement, via live polling or immediate written notes
- The discrepancy must be resolved by introducing the skin depth as a permeability-dependent length scale
#### Constraints
- The article notes that simply performing a physics demonstration does not guarantee learning; learning gains depend critically on how the demonstration is integrated into the lecture

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory electrodynamics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- challenging the misconception that the skin effect is only a high-frequency phenomenon
- re-evaluating assumptions about electric transport and material properties
