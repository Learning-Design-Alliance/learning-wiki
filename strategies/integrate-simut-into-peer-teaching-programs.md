# Integrate simulation-based ultrasound training into peer-teaching programs rather than treating them as rivals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend combining simulator-based and peer-assisted ultrasound training: "peer-teaching-based training approaches and SIM-UT should not be seen as two competing rival methods—simulation-based training can be integrated into peer-teaching training programs", which they speculate might lead to synergistic effects and improve overall learning outcome. They also note the simulator's teaching module can embed teaching videos and supplemental didactic material, making autonomous learning feasible even for difficult specialized skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a fetal echo simulator whose teaching module supports embedded didactic material, alongside an existing peer-teaching structure.
#### Constraints
- The integration hypothesis is the authors' own speculation, not a tested result in this study.
- Relatively high acquisition costs of simulators are named as an obstacle to curricular implementation.

### Target Learners
- medical students
- residents and Ob/Gyn physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- fetal echocardiography and obstetric ultrasound standard-plane skills
