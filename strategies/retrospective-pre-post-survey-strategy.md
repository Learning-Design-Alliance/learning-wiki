# Use retrospective pre/post surveys on the final day to measure science-identity and engagement change in short informal workshops

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends retrospective pre- and post-surveys, in which participants rate how they felt before and after the workshop on the final day, as the measurement approach for short informal science workshops. It states that "retrospective pre- and post-surveys were selected for the virtual workshops to decrease this response shift bias", because participants can more accurately describe what they did not know once introduced to topics in an active learning environment. The four Likert-scale statements used assess talking about science, excitement about science classes, belief in doing well in science, and seeing oneself as a scientist.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Consultation with science-education assessment expertise; the article's items were developed in consultation with the Science Education Department at the Harvard-Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics, based on the Common Instrument Suite and PRISE instruments
#### Constraints
- This approach depends on a participant's memory recall and carries the potential for self enhancement bias
- The difference in survey administration between in-person and virtual workshops may lead to biases in analysis and conclusions

### Target Learners
- middle-school girls in informal science workshops

### Target Learning Goals
- science identity
- engagement with science
