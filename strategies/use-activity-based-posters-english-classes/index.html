Use activity-based posters in English classes to support achievement, retention and positive attitudes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teachers adopt activity-based posters as a classroom activity in English lessons, based on its finding that poster teaching outperformed guidebook-based teaching on achievement and 21-day retention. It states teachers "can use the activity-based posters which enhance the academic achievement of the students" and that the posters help students remember subject matter longer and contribute to positive attitudes. Implementation follows the study's cycle of group preparation and class presentation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Time for brief subject introduction, group poster preparation and class presentations within the lesson schedule
  • Availability of poster-making materials and visual sources

Constraints¶

  • Evidence base is a single quasi-experiment in one school; the recommendation is stated by the authors for English classes at all stages

Target Learners¶

  • Secondary school English language learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Academic achievement in the English unit content
  • Longer-term retention of learned material
  • Positive attitudes toward the English course

Claims¶

  • Ninth-grade English students taught with activity-based posters scored significantly higher on the unit post-test than students taught with guidebook, course book and workbook [+W]
  • Students taught with activity-based posters scored significantly higher on a retention test 21 days after the post-test than the control group [+W]
