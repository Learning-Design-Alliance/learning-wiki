Engage teachers with predictive analytics dashboards to trigger timely interventions with students at risk¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), q1 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's central recommendation is that teachers be engaged with predictive learning analytics data so they can identify and assist students at risk. Teachers used OUA to systematise existing monitoring practices, become more proactive in contacting students, and reach "ghost" students invisible in forums. The study found "teachers can positively affect students' performance when engaged with PLA," with interventions such as emailing, texting, or phoning flagged students taking place outside the dashboard.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need access to a dashboard presenting per-student risk predictions and VLE activity
  • Contact mechanisms (email, text, phone) and recording of outreach must exist outside the analytics tool

Constraints¶

  • Infrequent dashboard access may limit intervention effectiveness
  • Voluntary uptake was low: only 5–6% attended briefing sessions and seven teachers never logged in

Target Learners¶

  • distance learning students at risk of not submitting assessments or failing courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • assignment submission
  • course pass and completion rates

Affordances¶

  • Tam Arm Lenses Pla Adoption

Claims¶

  • Teachers accessed the OUA dashboard infrequently and irregularly, mostly around assignment submission weeks [+W]
  • Teachers' weekly OUA dashboard usage is associated with higher odds of students completing their course [+M]
  • Teachers' weekly OUA dashboard usage is associated with higher odds of students passing their course [+M]
  • Teachers primarily used OUA's colour-coded risk flags and VLE activity indicators, ignoring other features [+W]
  • Teachers perceived OUA as useful and easy to use, with positive cognitive evaluations suggesting low academic resistance [+W]
  • Predictive learning analytics can generate early warnings of dropout risk and trigger targeted interventions in adult education [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use discussion analytics to alert instructors about students who may need more assistance
  • Deploy ML-based early-warning prediction to shift retention support from reactive to proactive intervention
  • Pedagogical nudges triggered by WBA analytics to encourage engagement
  • Use early cheating-risk predictions only for low-stakes support with human review, never for disciplinary decisions
