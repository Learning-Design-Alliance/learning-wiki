# Class-sourcing: students curate and share resources, reviewed by faculty and peers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes its approach to resource collection — "resources identified and selected by students, and reviewed by both faculty and students" — as a form of crowd-sourcing, or more accurately class-sourcing. Students post resources to group curation pages, staff select particularly useful content and re-scoop it to whole-year topic pages, and faculty gain awareness of the resources students actually use and can appraise websites they would not otherwise see.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Staff time to review student posts; with a year of 300+ students, more training, guidance and a clear governance structure would be needed for a sustainable implementation
#### Constraints
- Reviewing posts from a year of 300+ students is a considerable task; the public nature of the platform may deter some students, e.g. those with dyslexia unwilling to post a public comment

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students working in small case-based learning groups

### Target Learning Goals
- finding and sharing high-quality learning resources
- critical appraisal of online sources
- linking learning to current affairs and wider health issues
