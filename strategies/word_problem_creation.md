# Word Problem Creation

## Description
Learners select math operations and create multistep word problems for peers to solve, integrating math and literacy skills. This promotes careful reading, understanding of problem-solving steps, and application of math concepts in creative contexts. The strategy can be used across multiple strands in math (e.g., operations, geometry, statistics).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to math resources, tools for writing and solving problems (e.g., paper, pencils, calculators), and structured time for creating, sharing, and solving problems.
#### Constraints
- May require significant instructional support to ensure learners create solvable and relevant problems. Some learners may struggle with the creative aspect of the task or with accurately solving problems created by peers.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Upper elementary, middle school, and high school learners, particularly those studying math and related subjects.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Apply math operations in real-world contexts, enhance problem-solving skills, integrate math and literacy skills, promote creative thinking, and develop peer assessment abilities.

### Affordances
- Fosters creativity and critical thinking, promotes deeper understanding of mathematical concepts, and enhances communication skills. It also allows for personalized learning experiences as learners create problems tailored to their interests and abilities.

### Personalization
- The complexity and type of word problems can be tailored to different skill levels. Learners can choose the math operations to use, the context of the problem, and the degree of complexity.

## Assessment Evidence
- Evaluate the quality, complexity, and accuracy of the word problems created by learners. Assess the completeness and correctness of solutions provided by learners. Observe learner engagement and participation in problem-solving activities.

## Impact
- Word problems are seen as a concrete and fun way to link math and literacy skills together. They require reading carefully, understanding what is being solved for, and underlining the most relevant information.

## Examples
- Following a Flocabulary math video, students create word problems that use math operations they've learned, then solve problems created by their classmates. Variations include giving students the answer and asking them to write the word problem that leads to it, or creating stories with a word problem at the center.
