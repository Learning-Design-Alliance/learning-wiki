# Use the Three Step Question to link case conceptualization to a concrete intervention goal before rehearsal

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article introduces the Three Step Question of therapy—1. Where is the client currently at? 2. Where do we want the client to be? 3. How do we get him there?—as a decision-making routine practiced first artificially in supervision until it becomes automatic and intuitive. Asking where the client should be sets a clearly defined intervention goal, and staying close to specific real-life therapy moments allows a quick transition from conceptualization to operationalization, making it easier to assess whether a chosen intervention worked.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a specific, real therapy moment (e.g., from a session recording) to anchor the three questions
#### Constraints
- Tying clinical work into theory can often distract from concrete practice and actual in-session interventions, so the routine is introduced deliberately within practice

### Target Learners
- therapists learning to connect theoretical conceptualization with in-session intervention choices

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical decision-making and goal-oriented treatment planning

### Affordances
- Deliberate Practice Supervision Model
