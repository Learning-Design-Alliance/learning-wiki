# Professional development for emerging DBER researchers should account for DBER's close ties with disciplinary science and make norms explicit

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "professional development opportunities need to take into account DBER's close ties with disciplinary science to create a smoother and more inclusive transition for scholars in diverse institutional contexts and career stages." It further proposes that the DBER community advocate for the value of education research within STEM departments, articulate norms of what it takes to do DBER by providing resources and training, and hold explicit discussions about newcomers' dilemmas during professional development activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit discussions of newcomers' dilemmas during professional development activities; resources and training articulating DBER norms.
#### Constraints
- Support must address the perception of legitimacy of DBER within STEM departments and gatekeeping by the broader DBER enterprise.

### Target Learners
- emerging STEM education researchers in STEM departments

### Target Learning Goals
- persistence in education research careers
- managing tensions between DBER and home research fields
