Use a pre-test, post-test, and delayed retention test with the same achievement test to evaluate both achievement and permanence of technology-supported teaching¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 6 studies (6 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 6 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's design applies one validated achievement test three times: as a pre-test to check group equivalence, as a post-test after the application, and a third time "four weeks later" as a retention (permanence) test. This lets a researcher measure not only immediate achievement gains but also how much learned information students recall after a delay, comparing dependent- and independent-samples t-tests across the three administrations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A validated achievement test covering the unit's critical behaviors, with reliability established (here KR-20 = 0.797)

Constraints¶

  • The authors note the four-week, four-hours-per-week implementation period was short and recommend longer-range studies with larger and more various groups

Target Learners¶

  • Secondary school students in intact classes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Measuring academic achievement and retention of instructed subject-matter knowledge

Claims¶

  • The experimental group showed no significant score decline between post-test and four-week retention test, while the control group declined significantly [+W]
  • Students taught with smart board support retained more information four weeks after instruction than students taught with the 2005 curriculum [+W]
  • The formative-assessment-embedded group retained higher NOS and SK scores than the control group on a retention test four weeks after the intervention [+W]
  • Knowledge gained through performing-arts teaching persisted: permanence-test scores four weeks later showed no significant drop from posttest and remained higher than the control group [+W]
  • Technology-supported teaching produced higher learning retention four months later, while control-group retention fell below its pre-test level [+W]
  • Learning from interactive whiteboard lessons supported by visual materials persisted, with only a small significant decline four weeks after the post-test [+W]
  • Achievement gains from educational games-based iPad applications were retained on a delayed test four to six weeks after the post-test, with significant delayed-test differences favoring the experimental group [+W]
