# Use group interaction, peer evaluation and shared productions in task-based grammar teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on the sociocultural aspects of language development, the article recommends instructional designs focusing on optimal learner interaction, converting learner roles from participants to interlocutory participants. It specifies "sharing post-task productions within the group and evaluating one another's written productions," and advises valuing content organization and cohesion alongside accuracy in assessment criteria for task-based productions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-class applications such as sharing post-task productions within the group and evaluating one another's written productions.
#### Constraints
- Some participants (26.9%) found individual work not effective and wanted individual productions shared with others, indicating sharing is expected.

### Target Learners
- Tertiary-level EFL students

### Target Learning Goals
- Grammatical accuracy, cohesion, and communicative language use
