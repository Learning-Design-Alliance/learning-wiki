# Use cognitive feedback showing cue–outcome relationships to improve nurses' risk judgement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends cognitive feedback — feedback describing the probabilistic relations between symptoms/signs and outcomes — as a way to improve nurses' judgement performance in risk assessment. It reports that providing cognitive feedback with task information "has proved to be useful for improving judgement performance in doctors", and proposes testing its potential for nurses in future research, alongside constructing predictive models of common judgement situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback must convey the relations between clinical cues and ecological criteria, capturing task probabilism and environmental uncertainty
#### Constraints
- The article states the potential of cognitive feedback for nurses' judgements has not yet been tested and should be examined in future research

### Target Learners
- student nurses
- experienced registered nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate recognition of patients at risk of acute deterioration
- understanding probabilistic cue–outcome relationships
