# Integrate AR into classrooms by training teachers, providing ready-made content, and equipping schools with devices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend enhancing AR use in classrooms by improving content, facilitating teacher training, and giving schools appropriate technical personnel. They argue the adaptation effort "does not seem to be within the reach of teachers", because it requires ICT training and specific classroom endowments of software, hardware and contents, and note that AR content in textbooks and reasonably priced AR/VR programs would ease teachers' task.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher training in ICT and AR content creation
- Classroom endowments of software, hardware and contents
- Technical support personnel in schools
#### Constraints
- School rules in the authors' context did not allow student smartphone use, and most classrooms lacked the technology; the teacher saw AR as unsuited to some subjects such as language, maths or physical education

### Target Learners
- Primary education students
- Pre-service and in-service teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Integration of AR as a teaching and learning resource across curriculum topics
