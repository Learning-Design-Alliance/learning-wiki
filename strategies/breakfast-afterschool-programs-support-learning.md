# Support breakfast consumption and after-school physical activity programs to support student health and learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (7 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends school-based supports for healthy behaviours as potential levers for academic outcomes. It reports that "increased participation in PA after school was significantly associated with better academic performance in Mathematics" and highlights the negative association between breakfast skipping and Mathematics performance, suggesting breakfast programs may reduce food insufficiency and support nutritional status.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School capacity to deliver breakfast and after-school programming, since health promotion initiatives require staff time beyond designated responsibilities
#### Constraints
- Evidence is cross-sectional; the article states further research is needed on the dose-response of PA and school performance

### Target Learners
- Elementary school students in grades 4–6

### Target Learning Goals
- Mathematics and English Language Arts academic performance
