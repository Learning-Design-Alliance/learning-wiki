# Implement role-play and role reversal in emergency simulation teams to improve doctor-nurse interpersonal understanding and patient safety

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a concrete implementation: teams of four residents, two playing nurse roles and two physician roles, with roles randomly chosen and role-specific tasks enforced, so physicians experience nurse perspectives. The authors report using role-play and role reversal because doctor-nurse relationships in acute care present conflicts, and because simulation-based role-play training "may improve these interpersonal issues, as well as aspects related to patient safety".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator who orients participants beforehand and states that task-management decisions belong to the team without facilitator interference
- Role-specific procedure assignments for each played role
#### Constraints
- Whether interprofessional learning transfers from simulation to real-life settings remains, per the authors, a question for future research

### Target Learners
- internal medicine residents
- residents building mental models for multidisciplinary team work

### Target Learning Goals
- doctor-nurse collaboration and communication
- confidence and attitudes in multidisciplinary teams
- patient safety culture

### Affordances
- Role Play Psychological Fidelity Simulation
