# Tailor reward timing to learners' existing interest: post-learning unexpected rewards for high-interest material, promised performance-linked rewards for low-interest material

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 7 studies (3 review, 2 causal, 2 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q4` · 2 of 7 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article derives a practical approach from its findings: "when students already possess a high level of interest in a course, pre-learning extrinsic rewards may be unnecessary. Instead, unexpected rewards given after learning could help further improve their performance." Conversely, for students lacking intrinsic motivation, promising performance-linked rewards before learning could boost engagement. The strategy exploits the temporal dynamics of reward and interest rather than adding rewards indiscriminately.

## Design Implications

### Context
