Combine game log data with eye-tracking data to inform adaptive AI feedback and scaffolding in educational software¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 design), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend complementing behavioral log data with eye-tracking measurements, since each alone is limited: a fixation cannot distinguish blank staring from careful processing, and a logged correct response cannot distinguish chance from informed choices. They conclude that "the findings are important in showing the potential gains of combining log data with eye-tracking data for developing and refining AI algorithms for adaptive individual feedback and scaffolding". Such data can support diagnosis, game evaluation, user modeling, and real-time adaptation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Eye-tracking equipment that can record valid data from young children in an ecologically valid setting, and intelligent combination of the two data sources to disambiguate interpretations

Constraints¶

  • Gaze fixation measures cannot by themselves differentiate processing from blank staring, and log data cannot by itself separate chance from well-informed choices

Target Learners¶

  • preschool children aged 4–6 using educational games

Target Learning Goals¶

  • real-time identification of learners' processing and understanding to individualize feedback and scaffolding

Claims¶

  • Time and gaze-shift evidence are complementary predictors of self-explanation: time alone yields higher sensitivity, gaze shifts alone higher specificity, and the combination the best accuracy [+M]
  • Longer eye fixation time is ambiguous between processing difficulty and extra processing effort, so eye movement data should be validated with outcome measures [+W]
  • Certain multimodal data combinations improve predictive model performance, with audio plus eye-tracking data most effective in one K–8 study [+M]
