# Use instructional technology to manage situational interest by matching learning styles, leveraging difficulty, segmenting goals, and arousing curiosity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article synthesizes the literature into four factors by which instructional materials motivate interest: matching learning styles, leveraging difficulty levels, providing feasible missions or tasks, and arousing curiosity. It argues instructional technology lets teachers manage student situational interest by incorporating these factors into course learning. IGLU operationalized them through interactive visualization, interconnected sub-missions, and a non-threatening fun environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Technology use has to appropriately map the content knowledge with a right amount of usage
#### Constraints
- Web-based or technology-based learning could be a double-edged sword for students' motivation because the result is dependent on whether the technologies are used effectively

### Target Learners
- college and university students
- digital-generation engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- learning interest and motivation
- engagement with abstract technical concepts
