Phased intervention framework supporting at-risk first-year students before and during the exam period¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 review, 1 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the exam-pattern analysis and prior research, FME implemented phased interventions that "identify at-risk students based on the achieved ECTS credits". Steps include an Open Day information campaign, a White book of study information, an optional Vice-Dean lecture three weeks before the winter exam period on progression rules, statistics and best exam-taking strategies (around 70% of first-year students attend), offers of planning help in the first exam week to students with low credit scores, individual anonymous help two weeks before the period's end for students with less than 10 ECTS credits, weekly vice-dean consultations, and post-semester course surveys.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • ECTS credit data to identify at-risk students
  • Staff availability for lectures, consultations and individual support

Constraints¶

  • Only about 10% of invited students respond to the first-week help offer, and only a few respond to the later call
  • A detailed analysis of the intervention framework efficiency has not been carried out so far

Target Learners¶

  • first-year engineering students at risk of failing

Target Learning Goals¶

  • exam planning, time management, and timely help-seeking

Claims¶

  • Ninth-grade early warning systems around course grades and attendance led to substantial improvements in grades, graduation rates, and test-measured learning gains [+W]
  • During the intervention, linear algebra exam performance rose distinctly above overall first-year performance, a pattern the authors interpret as intervention impact beyond cohort differences [+W]
  • Failing students are predominantly inactive in the first two weeks of the exam period [+W]
