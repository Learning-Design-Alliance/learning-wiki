Two-stage song-work technique: pre-listening word work, then work during and after listening¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

For song material, the article allocates two stages: "1. use of the words without music, prior listening to the song. 2. work with the song during and after listening." Stage one develops reading/listening strategies and language skills via text reconstruction, rhyme completion, and tense/pronoun changes, plus dictations and verse writing. Stage two targets speech skills, linguistic memory, expressive reading, rhythm and intonation, creative writing, philological connections, and text interpretation through tasks such as singing along, retelling, essays, and discursive song analysis.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Song work follows the text-work system, since the song is both an example of foreign-language speech sound and a carrier of cultural information

Constraints¶

  • The poetic-analysis tasks (rhyme schemes, alliteration, metaphor) "were suggested for advanced level learners"

Target Learners¶

  • university students learning English, including advanced-level learners for analysis tasks

Target Learning Goals¶

  • lexical, phonetic and grammatical skills; linguistic memory; expressive reading; rhythm and intonation; creative writing; text interpretation

Claims¶

  • Using music and musical information in English classes somewhat improved formation of communicative skills relative to traditional methods [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Video-clip techniques: silent viewing, pause-based prediction, and rewind
  • Introduce authentic listening materials to lower-level learners by simplifying the task, grading the text, and staging the listening
  • Listening and reading for specific information
