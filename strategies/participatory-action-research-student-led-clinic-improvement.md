# Use participatory action research with student-identified challenges and student-developed action plans to let students lead transformation of clinical practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy has students identify clinic challenges with structured prioritization criteria, theorize them against scientific literature individually, then collaboratively build action plans specifying "required resources, timelines, and responsibilities" and implement them over four weeks. The article reports this let participants lead their teaching-learning process and transform the work process, with strategies ranging from a daily patient-check list to clinic-hours changes. The final focus group evaluates both the strategies and the research process itself.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional receptiveness to student-proposed changes and channels to coordination and hospital services
- Structured prioritization criteria (modified MAPP) and semi-structured guiding questions
#### Constraints
- Strategies outside students' governance scope, such as creating a scheduling tool and distributing dental supplies, were not implemented
- Infrastructure solutions require continuous financial resources and institutional support

### Target Learners
- undergraduate health-professions students in clinical placements

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying and addressing real-world clinical challenges
- leading the teaching-learning process
