# Use senior medical students as near-peer teachers for earlier-year medical students in simulation workshops

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A strategy in which senior medical students, with a junior resident, deliver simulation-based workshops to earlier-year medical students. The article reports that "the stations led by medical students in this workshop resulted in similar changes in participant confidence and knowledge as those led by a junior resident," supporting medical students as near-peer teachers despite minimal differences in formal training relative to participants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Near-peer teachers trained by staff otolaryngologists and more senior learners
#### Constraints
- Student teachers are limited in experience and depth of knowledge; teaching skills may differ across medical students

### Target Learners
- preclinical and earlier-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural confidence and knowledge in otolaryngology emergencies
