# Administer a short no-feedback interim assessment at the end of a small group work session shortly before the formal examination

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article implements an interim assessment as seven validated multiple-choice questions on the current topic, taking 10 minutes, administered at the end of an obligatory small group work session 3 days before the formal examination, with no explicit feedback and no stakes. The authors conclude that "An interim assessment during a small group work session is found to stimulate students to learn better and to increase their score of the formal examination."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participation is voluntary and the result does not count toward the formal examination score; questions are validated for content and educational level and drawn from the same bank as examination questions
#### Constraints
- The demonstrated benefit was measured in a single 4-week Bachelor course; the authors state the generalizability of the findings is currently limited and more similar studies are needed

### Target Learners
- second-year medical and biomedical science Bachelor students

### Target Learning Goals
- biomedical subject knowledge (general pathology, tumour pathology)
