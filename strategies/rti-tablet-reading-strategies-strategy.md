# Use tablet computers in Response to Intervention for self-recorded reading, electronic graphic organizers, and electronic text notation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors encourage teachers to explore how tablet computers can support struggling readers while awaiting formal research. They recommend that RtI teachers "consider experimenting with tablet computers to allow students to gain insight into their reading by hearing themselves read," to improve comprehension with electronic graphic organizers, and to support interaction with passages through electronic notation in the text. Teachers differentiating instruction may also let students work in stronger learning modalities while developing weaker ones.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- eBooks with self-recording capability becoming available
- teacher willingness to experiment with tablet devices
#### Constraints
- Recommended while awaiting published research identifying which factors actually produce gains for struggling readers

### Target Learners
- Struggling readers in Response to Intervention programs
- Students with ADHD

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension
- Self-monitoring of reading
- Engagement with text
