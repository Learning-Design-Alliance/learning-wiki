# Use heterogeneity simulations or real peer feedback in university teacher education to promote heterogeneity-responsive teaching beliefs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implications section recommends that university lecturers use heterogeneity simulations in courses on student learning to promote beliefs about heterogeneity-responsive teaching, then build on those beliefs with materials on adapting lessons. As an alternative enabling real self-experience, lecturers "could collect feedback from pre-service teachers about the difficulty of a task" and make it transparent, e.g. with audience response systems, then reflect with students on their heterogeneity and relate it to school students' heterogeneity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A course context in which pre-service teachers complete a task and their difficulty feedback can be collected and made transparent; lecturers should be aware that students who found the task harder may experience negative emotions such as shame and lower task-related motivation
#### Constraints
- The article states further research is needed to determine under what conditions heterogeneity simulations have substantial and significant effects, and that no follow-up measures were used to test sustainability

### Target Learners
- pre-service teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- beliefs about the importance of heterogeneity-responsive teaching
