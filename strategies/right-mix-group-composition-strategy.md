# Compose heterogeneous PBL groups by the 'right mix' of academic ability and match facilitator expertise to group needs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Facilitators recommended that grouping, currently based on race and gender, should instead be based on "academic strength i.e., appropriate portions of strong and weak students" to enhance cognitive interactions, elaborations and social cohesion. The 'right mix' also extends to selecting the appropriate facilitator for a group, e.g., using "the stronger facilitator where the group has students with less knowledge." This was the third recommendation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Information on students' academic strength to compose groups
- Facilitator expertise and experience data for matching
#### Constraints
- The article states this suggestion "needs further investigation" and that facilitator matching is "a new concept that needs to be explored in more detail"

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in PBL tutorials

### Target Learning Goals
- effective collaborative learning
- balanced group participation
