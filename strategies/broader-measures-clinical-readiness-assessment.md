# Assess clinical readiness using a broader range of measures including communication and professionalism

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend moving beyond reliance on pre‐clinical summative assessment scores to determine readiness for patient clinics. They state that "a new approach is required to understand and define clinical readiness, looking at a broader range of measures including communication and professionalism", and that the Knowing/Being definition indicates a constellation of qualitative and quantitative metrics is required. The recommendation rests on the study's finding that pre‐clinical marks were weak or non‐significant predictors of clinical performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to longitudinal summative assessment data across pre‐clinical and clinical years, as collated in this retrospective cohort study
#### Constraints
- The article notes it is unlikely that pre‐clinical summative assessment performance alone can assess clinical readiness; further research is required to better define and quantify clinical readiness

### Target Learners
- dental students in pre‐clinical and clinical years of a graduate dental programme

### Target Learning Goals
