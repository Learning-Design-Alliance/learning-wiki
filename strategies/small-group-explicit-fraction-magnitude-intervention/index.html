Deliver at-risk fourth graders' fraction intervention in small groups using systematic, explicit instruction centered on fractions magnitude understanding¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article's concluding lesson is that at-risk fourth graders can succeed with challenging fraction content when intervention occurs in small groups and relies on "systematic, explicit instructional principles as well as state-of-the-art thinking about the content area (i.e., fractions magnitude understanding, in the case of fractions)." The five RCTs used 12 weeks of small-group sessions (2-4 students, three times weekly, 30-35 min) outside the classroom, with tutor fidelity above 95%. The strategy pairs the core Fraction Face-Off! program with component modules selected by the component analyses.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Intervention delivered in small groups of 2-4 students outside the classroom, three times per week for 30-35 min over 12 weeks
  • Instruction follows systematic, explicit principles with responsive tutor feedback
  • Content emphasis on fractions magnitude understanding rather than the part-whole interpretation

Constraints¶

  • The intervention only addresses fourth-grade fraction standards; research is needed on multiplying and dividing fractions and other complex targets
  • Students beginning below the 13th percentile on the WRAT screener failed to achieve normalized NAEP performance and may need continuing intervention

Target Learners¶

  • Fourth-grade students at risk for difficulty learning fractions (below the 35th percentile in whole-number computation)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Fractions magnitude understanding
  • Fraction addition and subtraction
  • Fraction word problems
  • Generalized fraction concepts

Claims¶

  • Fraction magnitude intervention significantly outperforms control on fraction calculation each year (mean ES 1.72) despite control allocating more time to computation [+W]
  • Fraction magnitude intervention significantly outperforms business-as-usual control on fraction number line estimation each year, with a mean ES of 0.93 across five RCTs [+W]
  • Improvement in fractions magnitude understanding, but not part-whole understanding, mediates the intervention's effects on fraction outcomes [+W]
