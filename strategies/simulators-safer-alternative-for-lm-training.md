# Use simulators or alternative emergency approaches such as intra-osseous lines instead of exposing patients during landmark training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that because the landmark learning phase carries significant complication and failure rates for patients, programs should consider safer alternatives. They suggest that "the use of simulators, which have already been shown to improve physician training in cannulation techniques, could also be a safer alternative to teach the LM technique," and that alternative emergency approaches such as intra-osseous lines could be considered when ultrasound is unavailable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to cannulation training simulators, or institutional adoption of alternative emergency vascular-access approaches
#### Constraints
- The authors state future investigations should address these questions; the alternatives are raised as options, not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- novice medical residents in intensive care settings

### Target Learning Goals
- landmark central venous catheterization skills without patient risk
