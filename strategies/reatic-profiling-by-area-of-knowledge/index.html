Use REATIC-based profiling to characterize university students' ICT use and consideration by area of knowledge¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends characterizing university students' ICT use and consideration through survey instruments analyzed by area of knowledge. It states that "it is necessary to appreciate the particular ways of learning in each area that students have and their experiences in the use of ICT", supporting area-differentiated profiling of ICT integration in higher education.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A validated questionnaire covering ICT use and consideration
  • Sampling stratified by branch of knowledge

Constraints¶

  • The transversal design does not allow establishing causal relationships

Target Learners¶

  • university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • ICT use for learning

Claims¶

  • In all areas of knowledge, students' consideration of ICT exceeds their use of ICT [+W]
  • Four well-differentiated clusters of students emerge from the factor-analysis-based cluster analysis by ICT consideration and use [+W]
  • Use and consideration of ICT differ significantly across the five areas of knowledge, with Health Sciences students showing the highest preference level in use [+W]
  • University of Salamanca students most frequently use basic and personal interrelation programs, search engines, online video portals and virtual libraries [+W]
  • Students value ICT positively as a help and importance in their learning process [+W]
  • Factor analysis identifies three components of ICT consideration: help/importance, tools for an end, and negative valuation [+W]
