# Tailor attendance interventions to pupils' specific absence trajectory patterns rather than uniform attendance-threshold policies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools and policymakers move beyond minimum attendance thresholds and penalties toward identifying individual pupils' absence patterns and intervening accordingly. It states that "Tailored interventions are required depending on specific patterns of absences": moderate levels may need encouragement and support, while increasing patterns require addressing root causes such as underlying health conditions. Schools should build capacity to analyse attendance patterns and detect issues early so intervention precedes achievement damage.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools need the capacity to analyse and identify attendance patterns, including distinguishing authorised from unauthorised absences over multiple years.
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a pre-pandemic English cohort; the authors note post-pandemic trajectory patterns and effects may differ.

### Target Learners
- School-aged pupils at risk of escalating absenteeism

### Target Learning Goals
- Preventing cumulative achievement losses from escalating absence trajectories

### Affordances
- Joint Authorised Unauthorised Absence Trajectory Typology
