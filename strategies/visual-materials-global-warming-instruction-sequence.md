# Use documentary films, photographs, cartoons and student-made PowerPoint presentations in workshop groups to teach global warming

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's experimental treatment combined an approximately 80-minute documentary film with evaluation, a PowerPoint presentation on environmentally friendly behaviors, workshop groups analyzing "a minimum of 5 visual materials displaying the effects of global warming", and student-prepared presentations and posters voted on by the class. This multi-week sequence replaced lecturing with visual, hands-on activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to documentary film, photographs, animations, cartoons and presentation tools
- Time for workshop group work and student presentations across several weeks
#### Constraints
- The study lasted 3 weeks (7 class hours) plus tests, and the authors note short-term studies may not produce permanent attitudes

### Target Learners
- preservice elementary teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the reasons and results of global warming
- adopting environmentally friendly behaviors
- developing positive attitudes towards global warming

## Claims

- [When pretest scores were controlled, the posttest attitude difference between the visual-materials and traditional groups was not statistically significant, though the experimental group's adjusted mean was higher](../claims/ancova-no-significant-group-attitude-difference.md) [+W]
- [All six interviewed experimental-group preservice teachers said visual materials changed their outlook on global warming and made them follow related news more attentively](../claims/interviews-visual-materials-changed-outlook.md) [+W]
- [Visual-materials instruction produced a statistically significant within-group increase in global warming attitudes (d = 0.93), while traditional instruction did not (d = 0.33)](../claims/visual-materials-attitude-gain-significant.md) [+W]
- [Environmentally friendly behaviors increased far more in the visual-materials group (about 65%) than in the control group (about 4%)](../claims/visual-materials-increase-friendly-behaviors.md) [+W]
- [Instruction with visual materials rectified more global warming misconceptions than traditional instruction (about 40% versus about 12% success)](../claims/visual-materials-rectify-global-warming-misconceptions.md) [+W]
- [Both experimental and control preservice teachers held knowledge gaps and misconceptions about global warming before instruction, most commonly that global warming happens due to ozone layer depletion](../claims/preservice-teachers-ozone-global-warming-misconception.md) [~W]
