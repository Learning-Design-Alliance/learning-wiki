# Space word-learning exposures over time so children can forget irrelevant details between exemplars

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy arranges multiple exemplars of a new word category across minutes, hours, or days rather than labeling them closely together in time. The article states that "spacing learning apart in time provides children the opportunity to forget irrelevant details of one label-object pairing before learning a novel exem plar, thereby enhancing generalization." When exemplars are massed and children cannot forget irrelevant details between them, the article suggests staying awake rather than sleeping immediately after learning may be important to promote forgetting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple exemplars of the same word category must be presented, with exposures separated in time
#### Constraints
- The article notes it is rare in real-world word learning that multiple exemplars are labeled so closely in time, so the detrimental-sleep finding applies mainly to massed learning conditions

### Target Learners
- 2.5-year-old children learning new nouns

### Target Learning Goals
- Generalizing new word labels to novel exemplars encountered in multiple contexts
