# Use group roles, rotating roles, shared rubrics, and product-and-process debriefs to foster belonging in group work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The primer offers concrete activities mapped to characteristics of collaborative learning. Group roles "help students work more smoothly, encourage all to participate, and prevent one or a small number of students from dominating the conversation." Rotating roles (facilitator, recorder, time keeper, presenter) build shared responsibility. Shared rubrics involve "collaborating with students to co-create the criteria for success," and debriefs should emphasize both product and process with questions about group dynamics, such as whether everyone felt able to contribute.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Preparation of students to actively participate in group activities before monitoring and assessing collaboration
#### Constraints
- There is no perfect group; group membership should be regularly changed so students learn from different perspectives

### Target Learners
- K-12 students working in collaborative groups

### Target Learning Goals
- Shared responsibility and belonging
- Effective teamwork and communication
- Equitable participation
