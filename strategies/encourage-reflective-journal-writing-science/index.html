Encourage reflective journal writing in science teaching and build it into science textbooks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study recommends that "reflective journal -writing should be encouraged by sci ence teachers and in science textbooks", and that textbooks could use the Figure 1 model to encourage writing and reflection. The authors also suggest smartphone note-taking applications such as Endnote and OneNote to make journal writing more interactive, allowing photos, videos, sketches, and instant teacher comments.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Journals structured around self-reflection components rather than unstructured writing

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on one small quasi-experiment in a single female school in Oman; the authors call for replication in other cultures and contexts and larger samples

Target Learners¶

  • tenth-grade science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-regulated learning strategies; reflection on science lessons

Claims¶

  • Student journal reflections surfaced comprehension difficulties that the teacher then addressed in instruction [+W]
  • Reflective journal writing significantly improved the learning strategies subscale of self-regulation [+W]
  • Reflective journal writing significantly improved the motivation subscale of self-regulation strategies [+W]
  • Tenth-grade students who wrote structured reflective science journals significantly outperformed control students on overall self-regulation strategies [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Teachers read student journals and give regular individual feedback on them
