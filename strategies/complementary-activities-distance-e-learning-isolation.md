# Use complementary online group activities, responsive instructor support, and paper-based activities to counter isolation and screen-related stress in distance e-learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 6 studies (3 qualitative, 1 associational, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 6 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators minimize negative consequences of distance e-learning by adding diverse complementary and alternative class activities. It states "It will be necessary for educators to provide opportunities to students for online-based group projects during which they are able to communicate and cooperate with each other", to respond immediately and sensitively to students' needs, and to provide emotional support. Because higher screen exposure time has been shown to be associated with stress and burnout, the article adds that "some paper-based class activities should be added to decrease students’ levels of stress and burnout".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor capacity to respond immediately and sensitively to students' needs and queries and provide emotional support
#### Constraints
- Recommended in the context of pandemic-era distance e-learning where feelings of isolation, loneliness and lack of peer support are known negative aspects

### Target Learners
- nursing college students in distance e-learning

### Target Learning Goals
- reduced stress and burnout; sustained engagement and learning outcomes
