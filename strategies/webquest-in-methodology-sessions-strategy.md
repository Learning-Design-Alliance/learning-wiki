# Incorporate WebQuest procedures into teacher-preparation methodology sessions and adapt them per sport

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's practical implications recommend incorporating WebQuests into physical education teacher preparation programs. "The procedures followed in this study can be successfully incorporated into the methodology sessions addressing different teaching skills and sub-skills." The authors add that the WebQuest can be modified according to the nature of each sport, presenting models of how to plan for a PE class, manage the class, and evaluate the outcomes of the students, and call for additional research evaluating WebQuests in various subject areas with learners of differing age groups and academic abilities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A properly designed, implemented and monitored WebQuest, per the authors' concluding statement
#### Constraints
- The authors state the study is the first attempt to apply the WebQuest technique to PE teacher candidates in the Egyptian environment, so generalization beyond that setting is untested

### Target Learners
- preservice physical education teacher candidates in teacher preparation programs

### Target Learning Goals
- teaching skills and sub-skills addressed in methodology sessions, including class planning, management and evaluation
