# Introduce student perception surveys by first making teacher feedback (surveys of administrators) salient

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because a brief pre-question about teacher perception surveys of administrators raised teachers' subsequent support for student perception surveys, the authors recommend that principals and districts attend to how they introduce student feedback. They suggest that "If everyone in a school is receiving feedback from one another, evaluation may become a cultural norm and teachers are likely to be more open to student perception surveys." The ask itself is the lever.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A brief survey-based priming step in which teachers first give their views on evaluating administrators via teacher input, before being asked about student perception surveys.
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a low-stakes online survey with a snowball convenience sample; effects under real high-stakes implementation are untested.

### Target Learners
- K-12 teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Receptiveness to student feedback and openness to student perception surveys in evaluation

### Affordances
- Cognitive Dissonance Teacher Belief Shift
