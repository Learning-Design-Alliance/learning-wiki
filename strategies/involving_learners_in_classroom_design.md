# Involving Learners in Classroom Design

## Description
Involving learners in classroom design means actively soliciting their input and ideas when creating or modifying the physical learning environment. This can include asking learners about their preferred seating arrangements, color schemes, and use of space. By incorporating learner feedback, educators can create a classroom that is more student-centered, engaging, and conducive to learning. Involving learners in the design process can also foster a sense of ownership and responsibility for the learning environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a willingness to solicit and incorporate learner feedback. May require adjustments to classroom management strategies to accommodate different design preferences. Instructors must be willing to relinquish some control over the physical learning environment and trust learners to make responsible choices.
#### Constraints
- May be time-consuming to solicit and incorporate learner feedback. Can be challenging to balance diverse design preferences. May not be feasible to implement all learner suggestions due to budget constraints or other limitations.

### Affordances
- Increases learner motivation by giving them a voice in the design of their learning environment. Supports collaboration and communication by creating spaces that are conducive to group work. Can improve focus and productivity by accommodating different learning styles and preferences. Fosters a sense of ownership and responsibility for the learning environment.

### Personalization
- Learners can contribute their individual ideas and preferences to the design process. Instructors can offer a variety of design options and allow learners to choose what works best for them. Involving learners in classroom design can be combined with other personalization strategies, such as flexible pacing and flexible grouping.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learner engagement and participation in class activities. Collect learner feedback on the impact of the classroom design on their learning. Measure learner achievement and compare outcomes in classrooms designed with learner input versus those designed without learner input.

## Impact
- Improved engagement and motivation: Research suggests that involving learners in classroom design can increase their engagement and motivation by giving them a sense of ownership and control. Enhanced collaboration: Learner-designed classrooms can promote collaboration by creating spaces that are conducive to group work and discussion. Increased achievement: Some studies have shown that learner-designed classrooms can lead to improved learner achievement, although more research is needed to confirm these findings.

## Examples
- A teacher surveys learners about their preferred seating arrangements and incorporates their feedback into the classroom layout. A principal invites learners to participate in a design charrette to brainstorm ideas for renovating the school library. A professor asks learners to create a vision board for their ideal learning environment and uses these boards to inform the design of the course.
