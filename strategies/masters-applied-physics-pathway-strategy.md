# Establish a Master's degree in Applied Physics as a pathway to technological careers and a springboard toward PhDs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 qualitative, 1 review, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a Master's Degree in Applied Physics providing "an advanced degree beyond bachelors level that could open meaningful career paths for students into technological careers in laboratories or the private sector". It is framed as widening participation for underrepresented groups and for students from less favored communities who may exit with a Masters rather than continue to a PhD. The authors pair the proposal with a set of assessment recommendations rather than reporting an implemented program.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Conduct a needs analysis to assess the value and chances of success of such a program
- Identify the curriculum for such degree(s) and whether they could be made available nationwide through shared curriculum
- Assess university, PUI and CC buy in and participation in such a program
#### Constraints
- The proposal is untested in this article; the authors recommend assessing private sector buy in and comparing the program with current models of Masters Degrees in engineering and MBA degrees

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students seeking technological careers
- students from underrepresented groups and less favored communities

### Target Learning Goals
- preparation for technological careers in laboratories, accelerator facilities and the private sector
- springboard toward PhD study
