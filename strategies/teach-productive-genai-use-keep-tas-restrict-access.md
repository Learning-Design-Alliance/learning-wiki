# Teach productive genAI use, keep teaching staff available, and restrict genAI access for foundational skill development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that instructors lead students toward using genAI in ways that ensure they understand and verify results along the way, and urge students to plan before they code. They argue "teachers and teaching assistants remain vital to the learning environment, and should continue to be present and available", especially for conceptual physics questions and verifying knowledge. They also argue open-ended assignments with genAI access are unlikely to build foundational programming and modeling skills, so other instruction and assessment restricting genAI access is necessary for core modeling skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors and teaching assistants must remain present and available to verify students' physics and programming knowledge
#### Constraints
- The authors note students given little guidance while struggling with content and time-pressure may end up with poor learning outcomes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students learning programming and computational modeling

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational programming and modeling skills
- productive and critical use of generative AI
