# Targeted achalasia education using case-based discussions, guidelines, and CME, with structured curricula across specialties

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends targeted educational initiatives and structured interventions to address gaps in achalasia recognition, noting that "Preferred educational strategies included case-based discussions (68.0%), evidence-based guidelines (60.9%), CME/webinars (54.7%), and patient-education materials (51.0%)". It further calls for integrating structured esophageal motility curricula into both surgical and internal medicine training and fostering interdisciplinary collaboration, alongside improved diagnostic infrastructure and HREM access.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Addresses documented knowledge and practice gaps among non-gastroenterologists and primary-care physicians
#### Constraints
- The cross-sectional, self-reported design limits causal inference about which educational changes would improve outcomes

### Target Learners
- Physicians across primary, secondary, and tertiary care in Türkiye
- Medical trainees in internal medicine and surgical sciences

### Target Learning Goals
- Recognition and timely diagnosis of achalasia
- Familiarity with HREM as the gold-standard diagnostic test
- Knowledge of achalasia treatment options
