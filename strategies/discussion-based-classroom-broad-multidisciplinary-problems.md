# Restructure the adaptive-course classroom as a discussion-based setting with broad multidisciplinary problems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because students on individualized paths gain competencies at different paces, traditional lecturing is not viable in an ALICE course. The article argues that "With ALICE, the classroom becomes a discussion-based setting to encourage collaborative thinking and problem solving skills between disciplines." Domain-specific exercises are not useful in this setting; instead, broad multi-disciplinary problems or case-studies let students explore solutions as a group from many angles. The instructor's role changes from lecturing to answering questions, encouraging progression, providing feedback, and facilitating discussions, monitoring student progression before class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors should monitor where students are in lexia progression prior to class time, and no centralized lecture should be given at the start of class in heterogeneous groups
#### Constraints
- In more homogeneous classes, the article suggests a different structure allowing a short centralized lecture at the start of class

### Target Learners
- graduate and senior undergraduate students in adaptive interdisciplinary courses

### Target Learning Goals
- collaborative thinking and interdisciplinary problem-solving skills
