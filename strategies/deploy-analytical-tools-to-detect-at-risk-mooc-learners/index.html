Strategies

Deploy analytical tools on learning platforms to detect learners exhibiting behaviours that may lead to failure or dropout and propose actions to recover them.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors propose deploying analytical tools, such as the process mining method they present, on learning platforms like edX. This would allow instructors of online courses to improve the tracking of learners and "easily detect learners who exhibit behaviours that may lead them to fail or drop out of the course and, in such situations, propose actions to recover those learners." This strategy is grounded in the study's finding that procrastination and behaviour during study sessions directly influence users' final performance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to learning platform event logs
  • Process mining analytical tools capable of detecting procrastination patterns

Constraints¶

  • The study was limited to one specific MOOC on the edX platform, though the authors believe the methodology can be extrapolated to other MOOCs using the same platform.

Target Learners¶

  • MOOC users, particularly those in language learning courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improving course completion rates and final performance in MOOCs

Claims¶

  • Process mining of MOOC event logs reveals that users who fail or drop out exhibit distinct patterns of intra-session and extra-session procrastination compared to users who pass. [+W]
  • Users who stay and finish the MOOC utilize the course material better than those who do not, suggesting interest in learning from the material is a contributing factor for completion. [+W]
  • Users who fail a MOOC tend to combine evaluation tasks with consultation of support materials, suggesting they attempt to answer activities without having absorbed the essential knowledge. [+W]
  • There is a considerable drop in course participation during the second week of the language-learning MOOC, indicating content diversity alone is insufficient to ensure user engagement. [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Deploy ML-based early-warning prediction to shift retention support from reactive to proactive intervention
  • Devise more engaging and compelling content structures in language-learning MOOCs to capture user interest and commitment throughout the course.
