Pair print texts with app-based digital activities and text-message nudges to parents to support at-home reading¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends pairing print and digital resources so that supports reach families even when app statistics undercount reading. Its results "indicate that the text-message supports encouraged parents to engage in other, non–app-based reading activities with their children, underscoring the importance of pairing print and digital content as a strategy to support at-home reading activities." Text messages removed access barriers with direct links, highlighted app features, shared reading tips, and presented aggregate usage statistics to correct parental beliefs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Parents must have access to a smartphone with the app installed; text messages require valid parent phone numbers

Constraints¶

  • Only 26% of the sample accessed the app, highlighting the need for better implementation procedures to increase take-up

Target Learners¶

  • kindergarten to Grade 2 children and their parents in low-income households

Target Learning Goals¶

  • summer reading comprehension and home literacy engagement

Claims¶

  • Assigning K–2 nonresponders to gamification plus parent text messages, rather than gamification alone, raised one fall reading-comprehension subscore; the overall MAP gain was marginal [+W]
  • Only 26% of participating families accessed the MORE@Home app, revealing low take-up of app-based summer activities [+W]
  • Parents reported reading about 7 of the 10 provided books with children, and guided questions were the most frequently used app feature [+W]
