# Provide both digital 3D and physical learning modalities to accommodate diverse student preferences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends offering complementary digital and physical resources in pre-clinical dental training rather than a one-size-fits-all approach, because a minority of students preferred the tactile, hands-on experience of physical models while others benefited from the 3D IMs. As the authors state, "providing both digital and physical learning modalities is critical to accommodating varying student preferences and abilities." They also recommend expanding access to 3D IMs across the curriculum and earlier years.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Availability of both physical reference models and accessible digital 3D IMs within the same course
#### Constraints
- The 2021 experience shows that early-stage rollout with technical access issues can limit student engagement with digital resources

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate dental students with diverse learning preferences

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning indirect tooth preparation designs in pre-clinical fixed prosthodontics
