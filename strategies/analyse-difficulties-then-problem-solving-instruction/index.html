Chemistry educators should analyse student difficulties and implement problem-solving instruction in stoichiometry¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's recommendations direct chemistry educators to first analyse and understand the difficulties students encounter, so as to help learners become confident and efficient problem solvers, and then to "implement the problem-solving pedagogical technique as a means of addressing the difficulties students have in stoichiometry problem-solving". The study's quasi-experimental results support this sequence: diagnosing six difficulties, then remediating them with problem-solving instruction based on Ashmore, Frazer & Casey (1979) and Selvaratnam-Frazer (1982).

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers trained on the use of the intervention (one week of training in this study)

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from a quasi-experimental design without randomisation, using intact groups in real classroom settings

Target Learners¶

  • High school chemistry students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Stoichiometry problem-solving
  • Conceptual understanding of the mole concept, limiting reagent and related concepts

Claims¶

  • ANCOVA confirmed a significant effect of the problem-solving instructional strategies on stoichiometry problem-solving performance [+W]
  • Problem-solving instruction reduced the percentage of students showing each stoichiometric difficulty far more than conventional lecture instruction [+W]
  • Post-test achievement was significantly higher for students taught with problem-solving instruction than with conventional lecture [+W]
  • High school chemistry students show six recurring difficulties when solving stoichiometric problems, with identifying the limiting reagent the most common (88%) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Chemistry teachers should employ varied effective pedagogies, especially inquiry-based and problem-based approaches, supplemented with visualization and real-world materials
