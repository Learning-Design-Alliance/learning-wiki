# Universities with large-enrollment classes should adopt interactive e-learning and provide on-campus computer and internet facilities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings that an online course with interactivities overcame drawbacks of large enrollment classes, the authors recommend that "universities with large enrollment classes adopt e-learning and provide facilities for its implementation". They specify this "can be achieved by providing computer halls with internet connections on campus and at university dorms", responding to the access barriers non-participants reported. Teachers are positioned as facilitators of learning and assessors of competency rather than distributors of content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interactive course content (visuals, animations) and interaction structures between students and instructors and among students
- On-campus infrastructure: computer halls with internet connections on campus and at university dorms
#### Constraints
- Non-participation data showed lack of internet/computer access and time/deadline pressures were the main barriers, so access support is a stated precondition

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in large-enrollment required courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Course knowledge acquisition and student-student and student-teacher interaction in large classes
