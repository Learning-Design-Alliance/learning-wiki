Researchers should refrain from interpreting Classical Twin Design shared-environment estimates for educational attainment as between-family differences in educational opportunity¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that quantitative claims about inequality of educational opportunity between families should not rest on CTD C estimates, because unmodelled assortative mating inflates C and twin-specific shared environments make siblings in one family differ. As the authors put it: "These results suggest that researchers should refrain from drawing strong conclusions about the differences in educational opportunity between families based on CTD estimates for EA." They recommend designs incorporating siblings, parents, and spousal data instead.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Use of family designs that model assortative mating and separate twin-specific from sibling-shared environments before making between-family opportunity claims

Constraints¶

  • The bias direction and size vary across traits depending on which CTD assumptions are violated
  • A mixed homogamy scenario may place true values between PA and SH estimates

Target Learners¶

  • researchers and policymakers interpreting behavioural-genetic estimates of educational attainment

Target Learning Goals¶

  • accurate interpretation of shared-environment estimates as measures of unequal educational opportunity

Affordances¶

  • Ntfd Framework Ea Decomposition

Claims¶

  • Twin-specific shared environments account for a substantial portion of CTD shared-environment estimates for educational attainment and cannot be interpreted as between-family environmental differences [+W]
  • Dizygotic twin correlations for educational attainment are consistently higher than non-twin sibling correlations across countries, suggesting twin-specific shared environments are a general phenomenon [+W]
  • The best-fitting NTFD model estimates heritability of educational attainment at 51% and total shared environmental influence at 26%, disaggregated into twin-specific (16%) and sibling-shared (10%) components [+W]
  • Under phenotypic assortment NTFD models, parental education shows no environmental effect on offspring educational attainment once genetic influences are accounted for [+W]
  • A Classical Twin Design model of educational attainment in the TwinLife sample estimates shared environmental influence at 43% and additive genetic influence at 34% [-W]
