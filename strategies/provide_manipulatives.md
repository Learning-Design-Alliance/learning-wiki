# Provide Manipulatives

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 5 studies (2 review, 2 associational, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 5 report an effect size

## Description
Learners are given tangible objects — counters, base-ten blocks, fraction tiles, balance scales, or their virtual equivalents — that they physically arrange, combine, and transform to model abstract concepts. The manipulative serves as an external, manipulable representation of an idea that would otherwise exist only symbolically, allowing learners to act on the concept before representing it symbolically.

## Design Implications

Manipulatives work by grounding abstract symbols in perceptual, enactive experience, consistent with the progression from enactive to iconic to symbolic representation [Bruner, 1964]. Meta-analytic evidence shows moderate positive effects on achievement when manipulatives are used across a full instructional program, but effects shrink or reverse when the objects are used briefly or without connection to the target symbol system [~M]. The critical design decision is linkage: learners must be explicitly supported in mapping actions on objects to the symbolic notation they are meant to learn, otherwise the concrete representation becomes a parallel, disconnected activity [Uttal et al., 1997] [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Manipulatives whose structure maps transparently onto the target concept (e.g., place-value structure in base-ten blocks), not merely attractive or novel objects
- Explicit instruction connecting object actions to symbols and language — the teacher must narrate and label the mapping ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md))
- A plan for fading: moving learners from concrete objects to drawings to symbolic representation ([Fading](../elements/fading.md))
- Clear behavioral routines for distribution, use, and collection, since unstructured access invites off-task play
- Explicit linkage between the manipulative, the concept, and the formal notation — the teacher must name the mapping ("this rod is one ten; the numeral 1 in this column means one ten")
- A structured task with a goal, not free exploration alone ([Practice](../elements/practice.md) with the representation)
- For virtual manipulatives, interaction that is meaningful rather than decorative clicking

#### Constraints
- Highly perceptually rich objects can distract learners from the intended mathematical structure; learners may remember the objects but not the concept [~M]
- Concrete knowledge is often bound to the specific object used and does not transfer to symbols or other representations unless bridging instruction is provided [-M]
- Brief, isolated use ("manipulative Tuesday") shows little to no effect; benefits accrue only with sustained, integrated use [~M]
- Learners with sufficient symbolic fluency may find manipulatives redundant and slower than working directly with notation [~W]
- Highly perceptually rich objects can distract from the target structure; "seductive details" in the object compete with the concept for attention [Carbonneau et al. found plain, non-realistic manipulatives often outperform richly realistic ones.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031080) [~M]
- Manipulatives alone, without explicit mapping to symbols, produce little transfer to symbolic tasks [Uttal, Scudder, and DeLoache showed transfer fails when the object–symbol connection is left implicit.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.89.1.30) [-M]
- Learners can develop dependence on the concrete support and fail to internalize the abstraction if the concrete phase is never faded
- Handling objects adds motor and procedural load that can overwhelm novices if the task is complex; simplify the surrounding task ([Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md))
- Benefits shrink or reverse for learners who already grasp the abstraction [Carbonneau et al. found effects diminish with learner age and prior knowledge.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031080) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Physical vs. virtual:** virtual manipulatives (e.g., Brainingcamp, Didax virtual manipulatives, [PhET simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu)) offer unlimited supply, reset, and annotation; physical objects offer richer tactile input. Meta-analytic comparisons find roughly comparable effects when both are well designed [~M]
- **Structured vs. exploratory:** teacher-guided use with specific tasks outperforms free exploration for novices; open exploration suits learners with more prior knowledge
- **Duration:** single lessons vs. year-long programs (e.g., Cognitively Guided Instruction classrooms) — longer integration produces larger effects
- **Physical manipulatives**: Cuisenaire rods, base-ten blocks, algebra tiles, fraction strips; best for tactile grounding and social negotiation
- **Virtual manipulatives**: [Brainingcamp](https://www.brainingcamp.com), [The Math Learning Center apps](https://www.mathlearningcenter.org/apps), [PhET simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu); scalable, low-cost, and can constrain actions to relevant ones
- **Everyday objects vs. mathematical models**: realistic contexts boost engagement but can blur the mathematical structure; simplified models make structure salient
- **Duration**: brief concrete experiences to introduce a concept vs. extended Concrete-Representational-Abstract instructional sequences for struggling learners

### Target Learners
- Novices and young learners who lack the symbolic fluency to reason about abstract notation directly [~M]
- Learners with working memory constraints, for whom the object holds intermediate states externally [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Less beneficial for learners who already operate fluently with the symbolic system, for whom the concrete step adds load without adding meaning [~W]
- Young children and novices who lack the prior knowledge to interpret abstract notation directly [Physical experience enhances science learning.](../claims/physical-experience-enhances-science-learning.md) [+M]
- Learners with learning disabilities in mathematics, for whom CRA sequences have consistent evidence [Carbonneau et al. found stronger effects for learners with disabilities.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031080) [+M]
- Less beneficial for advanced learners, for whom concrete supports can be redundant and slow [Carbonneau et al. found effects diminish with age and expertise.](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031080) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual foundations: place value, fractions, arithmetic operations, conservation, part–whole relations
- Bridging informal to formal reasoning: connecting counting and grouping actions to standard algorithms
- Early spatial and quantitative reasoning: structured block and pattern play is associated with later mathematics achievement [Block play predicts math achievement.](../claims/block-play-predicts-math-achievement.md) [+W]
- Conceptual understanding of mathematical and scientific structures (place value, fractions, conservation, equilibrium)
- Bridging informal intuition to formal symbolism
- Spatial and proportional reasoning

### Instructions
1. Select a manipulative whose internal structure matches the target concept, and verify the mapping yourself before teaching.
2. Model the intended use with think-aloud narration, showing how an action on the objects corresponds to a symbolic step ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)).
3. Have learners act on the objects to solve specific tasks ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)), recording each action in symbols or words as they go.
4. Explicitly ask learners to explain the mapping — "what does this regrouping action mean in the written problem?" ([Articulation](../elements/articulation.md)).
5. Fade toward drawings and then symbols ([Fading](../elements/fading.md)), keeping the manipulative available but no longer required.

## Examples
- **Cognitively Guided Instruction** ([CGI for Math](../patterns/cognitively-guided-instruction-cgi-for-math.md)) — children model word problems with counters and drawings; teachers use the children's own strategies, not a prescribed procedure, to connect actions to notation.
- **[PhET Interactive Simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu)** — virtual manipulatives (e.g., fraction matcher, balancing chemical equations) with immediate, linked feedback between object state and symbolic representation.
- **Montessori materials** (golden beads, stamp game) — a fully developed system in which each material's structure encodes the concept and a fixed sequence fades from material to abstraction.
- **[PhET Interactive Simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu)** — virtual manipulatives (e.g., *Balancing Chemical Equations*, *Circuit Construction Kit*) that let learners vary parameters and observe consequences
- **Concrete-Representational-Abstract (CRA) intervention** — a scripted sequence used widely in special education mathematics, moving from base-ten blocks to drawings to numerals
