# Target informal health care providers with skill-based educational interventions, including simulation and peer-to-peer training, to improve neonatal care in rural settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building on the knowledge some IHCPs already possess through targeted educational interventions, arguing this is a route to reaching rural populations served by the informal sector. It cites the Helping Babies Survive program, which was "developed to reduce preventable new born deaths through skill-based learning using simulation, learning exercises, and peer-to-peer training of healthcare providers". The authors note a systematic review found short-term knowledge and skill gains that were not sustained over time, yet fresh stillbirths dropped and first-day mortality decreased, attributed to increased bag-and-mask ventilation within the golden minute.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Education opportunities must be provided to IHCPs, for example through the Government of India's skill development program offering short-term training or courses
#### Constraints
- A systematic review cited in the article found significant short-term improvements in knowledge and skills scores were not sustained over time
- The study did not record the number of neonates seen by IHCPs, which could have helped identify targets for future intervention

### Target Learners
- Informal health care providers with no formal medical education in rural India

### Target Learning Goals
- Evidence-based neonatal care practices including resuscitation, cord care, breastfeeding and immunization follow-up
