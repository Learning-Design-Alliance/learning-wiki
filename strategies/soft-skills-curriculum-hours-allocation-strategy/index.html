Allocate 10–15% of curriculum hours to structured soft skills training with mandatory modules and OSCE/portfolio assessment¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on its findings and institutional case studies, the review proposes operationalizing soft skills integration by allocating "approximately 10–15% of curriculum hours to structured soft skills training, progressively distributed throughout pre-clinical and clinical phases to ensure longitudinal reinforcement". Curriculum committees should introduce mandatory modules on communication, empathy, teamwork, and ethical decision-making early in medical education, with continued emphasis during clinical rotations. Formative and summative assessments such as OSCE stations, reflective portfolios, and multi-source feedback monitor progress, with accreditation-defined minimum competency standards phased in over three-to-five years.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Stakeholder coordination: universities champion integration, accreditation agencies set standards, curriculum developers design evidence-based strategies and scalable evaluation tools
  • Faculty development initiatives to equip educators with evidence-based teaching and assessment tools

Constraints¶

  • The review states there is an urgent need for standardized assessment tools and that institutional and andragogical barriers must be addressed for training to become a core curricular component

Target Learners¶

  • medical students

Claims¶

  • Communication skills training programs improve clinician patient-centeredness, but training approaches are variable and long-term retention is untested [+W]
  • Innovative andragogical strategies — simulation, narrative medicine, reflective writing — enhance empathy, compassion, and communication skills in medical students [+W]
  • Barriers to soft skills integration: time constraints, systemic obstacles, learner emotional strain, and adapting to new modalities [~W]
  • Empathy and communication interventions show short-term gains but evidence on persistence over time is inconsistent [~W]
