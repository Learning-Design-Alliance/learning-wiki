# Online-only flipped-classroom surgical curriculum combining video preparation, pre-tutorial exam, and supervised online tutorial

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable recipe: students prepare a week in advance with either case-based interactive videos or textbook excerpts (5–8 pages), take a ten-question multiple-choice online exam one day before the tutorial, then join a 60-minute online tutorial where three surgeons discuss the exam in depth and watch the video with both groups. The design applies blended learning and flipped-classroom concepts with no face-to-face teaching, encouraging "students' cooperation and engagement" and enabling assessment of knowledge before the tutorial.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recorded surgical procedures processed into an interactive video format
- Written exam followed by a live online tutorial to encourage engagement
- Access to all videos and materials granted to all students at study end
#### Constraints
- Whether an online-only curriculum can meet demands of high-quality surgical training remains unexplored
- Long-term effects of missing OR experience cannot yet be assessed

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- preparation for surgical tutorials
- theoretical surgical knowledge
- engagement with case-based surgical content
