# Gather undergraduate women's own suggestions, separated into personal advice for peers and suggestions for physics instructors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's participants generated improvement suggestions that the authors organize into two categories. As the abstract states, "Their suggestions are separated as personal advice for peers and suggestions for physics instructors." This strategy treats undergraduate women as experts in their own experiences whose recommendations can inform both peer support practices and instructor actions to create safer, more equitable and inclusive physics learning environments. It emerged inductively during coding: the authors did not anticipate the women would provide such specific, separable suggestions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Semi-structured, empathetic interviews that ask women directly what would improve their experiences
#### Constraints
- Suggestions come from seven women in one medium-size physics department at a small liberal arts college

### Target Learners
- undergraduate women in physics and astronomy

### Target Learning Goals
- improving experiences and support for current and future undergraduate women in physics
