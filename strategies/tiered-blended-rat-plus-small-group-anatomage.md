# Pair large-group RAT with small-group Anatomage laboratory sessions to create a tiered blended learning model

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that "RAT could be paired with small-group Anatomage laboratory sessions to create a tiered blended learning model, aligning with best-practice frameworks for technology-enhanced anatomy education." This strategy leverages the complementary preferences identified in the qualitative data: the Anatomage for three-dimensional structural visualization and PowerPoint-based teaching for clinical case discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to both a lecture theatre with smart digital podium and a dissection laboratory with an Anatomage table
#### Constraints
- The authors note a theoretical risk that enthusiasm for large-group technology-enhanced teaching could reduce student motivation for hands-on dissection, which was not assessed in the present study

### Target Learners
- First-year medical (MBBS) undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- Gross anatomy and neuroanatomy competency within a blended curriculum
