# Select LRRd or LRRi for ratio-scale outcomes and report the more homogeneous measure; use Tau when ratio-scale assumptions fail

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For single-case meta-analyses with ratio-scale dependent variables, the article recommends parametric within-case log response ratios: "If outcomes are measured using a ratio scale, LRRd and/or LRRi may be the most useful magnitude estimators." Authors should compute both LRRd and LRRi, compare their homogeneity, and report the more homogeneous measure (LRRi in this review). When outcomes are not on a ratio scale, parametric indices cannot be calculated and Tau or NAP is the recommended alternative. LRRi should be used cautiously until further studies examine it with other interventions, dependent variables, and designs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dependent variables must be measured on a ratio scale with a true zero, and the baseline mean must be greater than zero, for LRRd and LRRi to be calculable.
- Selection should follow guidelines considering design type, dependent variable, and observational measurement system (Pustejovsky & Ferron, 2017).
#### Constraints
- Due to the limited applications of LRRi, it should be used cautiously until further studies examine it in other intervention literatures, dependent variables, and SCD designs.
- Standard errors for LRRd and LRRi assume mutually independent outcomes and under-state true standard errors under positive auto-correlation.

### Target Learners
- Researchers conducting single-case design meta-analyses
