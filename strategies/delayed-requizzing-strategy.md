# Reopen graded quizzes as ungraded, delayed practice near exam time and encourage all students — especially lower prior performers — to use them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy is to take weekly low-stakes quizzes that already count for credit and reopen them before each exam for "unlimited, untimed, ungraded practice", announced by email. Re-quizzing predicted higher cumulative posttest and final exam scores even after controlling for preparation, at a cost of roughly one hour per semester per student. Because the benefit on the final exam was greatest for students who had struggled in a previous STEM course, the authors write that "instructors might want to encourage delayed re-quizzing."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Weekly autograded quizzes delivered through a learning management system, with attempt logs available to count retakes
- Quiz questions aligned with course learning objectives and largely drawn from pools so retakes present slightly different versions
#### Constraints
- None of the outcome assessment questions were identical to quiz questions, and effects were small (βstd = 0.1581 posttest; βstd = 0.1149 final)
- The students who benefited most were significantly less likely to participate, so participation may need active encouragement

### Target Learners
- First-year students in large-enrollment introductory STEM courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Cumulative retention of course content measured by novel cumulative assessment items
