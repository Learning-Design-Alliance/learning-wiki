# Examine the causal relationship between arts involvement and academic performance with experimental designs controlling for IQ and motivation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend moving beyond correlational longitudinal surveys to designs that can identify causality in arts-transfer research. They state: "Future research should examine the causal relationship between art involvement and academic performance by controlling for other factors (e.g., IQ, motivation, etc.)". They further call for experimental designs with balanced participant numbers across comparison groups, noting this study's free-will club choice means effects may involve the choice to participate as well as participation itself.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experimental or quasi-experimental designs that control for confounds such as IQ and motivation, and balanced numbers of participants across club-type and non-participation comparisons
#### Constraints
- The article notes impacts may differ between race, regional and cultural backgrounds, requiring meta-surveys of these differences
- The article states correlational designs cannot specify causal relationships

### Target Learners
- school-aged children in junior high school

### Target Learning Goals
- establishing causal effects of arts education on general academic performance
