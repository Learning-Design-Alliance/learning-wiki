# Use a structured one-day simulation program combining pre-briefing, theoretical training, practical skills practice, scenario-based simulation, and video-based debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (7 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a specific instructional sequence for caregiver emergency-response training: a structured pre-briefing covering objectives and psychological safety, theoretical training on common emergencies, individual practical skills practice under instructor supervision, a five-minute scenario-based simulation using medium-fidelity pediatric mannequins, and a structured video-based debriefing following the PEARLS framework. This sequence was designed to develop both theoretical understanding and practical psychomotor skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained facilitators to conduct pre-briefing, skills supervision, and PEARLS-framework debriefing
- Pediatric mannequins and CPR feedback devices
- Video recording capability with participant consent
#### Constraints
- Only PBLS psychomotor performance was formally evaluated; seizure first aid and choking management components were not objectively assessed

### Target Learners
- Lay caregivers of children with medical vulnerability
- Primary caregivers of children with Rett syndrome

### Target Learning Goals
- Recognition of and response to life-threatening pediatric emergencies including apnea, seizure-related respiratory compromise, and cardiopulmonary arrest
- Acquisition of PBLS psychomotor skills
