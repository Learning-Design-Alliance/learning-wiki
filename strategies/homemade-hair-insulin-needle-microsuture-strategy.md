# Make homemade microsurgical sutures from human hair and insulin pen needles for low-cost home practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes a do-it-yourself microsuture made by threading human hair through an insulin pen needle, adapted from Luangjarmekorn et al. Trainee feedback suggested hair sutures were a "good-excellent" standard for microsurgical practice, equal to that of standard sutures. The authors add practical preferences: "a hair of dark colour, mid length, coarse texture and wavy consistency works best". A step-by-step recipe is printed in Table 1.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Insulin pen needle (e.g. BD Ultra-Fine Pen Needles 4 mm × 32 G), human hair, microforceps, fine smooth pliers, loupe magnification and scissors, plus a light source
#### Constraints
- Cost and precarious supply chains of commercial microsutures motivated the approach; the reported standard is based on trainee feedback from a cited study, not tested by this article

### Target Learners
- plastic surgery trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- microsurgical suturing and fine motor skills
