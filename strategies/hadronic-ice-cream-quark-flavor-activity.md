# Hadronic ice-cream: free ice cream whose scoops represent quark flavors, with guests ordering mesons or baryons instead of single scoops

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The hadronic ice-cream stand is an outdoor-event highlight the authors call one of their favorites, with 'the amazing feature that the general public approaches us to learn more about particle physics'. Each scoop represents a different quark flavor and sprinkles turn a quark into its anti-quark; because quarks occur only as confined hadrons, 'instead of ordering a scoop of ice-cream, our guests need to order mesons or baryons!' The menu lists hadrons built from the up, down, charm, strange, and bottom flavors, since the top quark's lifetime is too short to form flavored bound states.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Free ice cream and a menu mapping scoops to quark flavors and sprinkle-decorated scoops to anti-quarks
#### Constraints
- Only up, down, charm, strange, and bottom flavors are on the menu because the top quark's lifetime is too short to form flavored bound states

### Target Learners
- the general public at outdoor events such as university open days

### Target Learning Goals
- curiosity about particle physics, quark confinement, and hadron composition
