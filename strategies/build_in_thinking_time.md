# Build in Thinking Time

## Description
Providing dedicated, silent thinking time before pair talk allows students to generate their own ideas and rehearse explanations before being influenced by their partner. This ensures that all students engage in generative thinking processes and are prepared to contribute to the discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires allocating dedicated time for silent thinking and explicitly instructing students to use this time productively.
#### Constraints
- Some students may struggle to stay focused during silent thinking time. Requires creating a classroom culture that values and respects silence.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All grade levels, particularly important for students who are less confident or need more time to process information.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Ensure that all students engage in generative thinking, allow students to formulate their own ideas before being influenced by others, promote deeper processing of information, and encourage active participation in discussions.

### Affordances
- Gives students time to explore their schema, rehearse explanations, and generate ideas. Prevents them from being overwhelmed by their partner's ideas. Models thoughtful reflection.

### Personalization
- The amount of thinking time can be adjusted based on the complexity of the topic and students' needs. Students can be given prompts to guide their thinking.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe whether students are using the thinking time productively. Listen to the quality of their contributions during pair talk. Ask students to reflect on how the thinking time helped them prepare for the discussion.

## Impact
- Dedicated thinking time can improve the quality and depth of student contributions during pair talk, leading to better understanding and retention of information.
