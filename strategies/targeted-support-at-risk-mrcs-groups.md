# Provide targeted support and resources (mentorship programmes, greater flexibility, grants and bursaries) to candidate groups at increased risk of failing MRCS

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that training providers use the identified at-risk groups to direct additional support, stating such support "may reasonably include mentorship programmes, greater flexibility in training programmes and the provision of grants and bursaries to make access to training opportunities and revision resources equitable". It argues this mitigates accumulated disadvantage, since the current model of equal policy-level support and remediation only after multiple failures has disproportionately benefited some groups and perpetuated differential attainment. Interventions should be sustainable, have clearly defined outcome measures and be audited regularly for effectiveness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be sustainable, have clearly defined outcome measures that aim to reduce differential attainment, and be audited regularly for their effectiveness, as the article states there is a paucity of studies on the effectiveness of such actions.
#### Constraints
- The article notes it may be argued that targeted support programmes stigmatise the recipients, and states the current equal-support model has so far disproportionately benefited some groups.

### Target Learners
- UK postgraduate surgical trainees preparing for the MRCS examination, particularly groups the study identifies as at increased risk of failing

### Target Learning Goals
- Success in the MRCS Part A and Part B examinations
- Equitable access to training opportunities and revision resources
