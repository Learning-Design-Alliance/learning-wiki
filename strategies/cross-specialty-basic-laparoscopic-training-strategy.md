# Cross-specialty shared training and testing in basic laparoscopic skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends training novice laparoscopic surgeons across specialties on a common basic-skills test before specialty-specific training. It argues that because "the techniques practised are used widely", experienced surgeons from one specialty can supervise trainees from another, trainees from different specialties can practise together and learn from one another, and departments and simulation centres can pool resources for more frequent courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A test whose tasks reflect techniques used widely across specialties
- A portable trainer to increase the flexibility of training
#### Constraints
- After mastering basic skills, trainees move on to specialty-specific training such as operation modules on a virtual reality simulator

### Target Learners
- Novice laparoscopic trainees in general surgery, gynaecology and urology

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic laparoscopic competencies prior to supervised surgery on patients
