# Promote evidence-based education and integrative interdisciplinary training to bridge medical education and cognitive neuroscience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Among its conclusions the article recommends strengthening the bridge by promoting evidence-based education, which 'tries to bridge the gap between the practice of learning on the one hand, and the development of learning and educational research on the other hand'. Its strongest recommendation is that 'Integrative training to professionals with interdisciplinary competences in the domain of mind, brain and education, who share the same language and theoretical frameworks, is strongly advocated'. It also endorses establishing explicit learning and teaching outcomes, implementing best evidence medical education, and evaluating teaching and its impact.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers trained in basic neuroscience and neuroscientists trained in basic educational theory and methodology, as the article reports Coch and Ansari propose
#### Constraints
- Premature or over-simplified introduction of neuroscience findings into the classroom has produced neuromyths with adverse effects on the interaction

### Target Learners
- medical teaching faculty
- education researchers
- cognitive neuroscientists

### Target Learning Goals
- building interdisciplinary competence for mind, brain and education research
