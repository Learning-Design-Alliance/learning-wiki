# Derive simulation scenarios from real clinical events and pitch them to the learners' background at the edge of their comfort zone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building scenarios with relevant, problem-centred problems per Knowles's adult learning principles: "Scenarios can be derived from real events to obtain well-staged, realistic scenarios with clinical relevance and optimal authenticity." Scenarios should be pitched to the learners' background so the group feels it is operating at the edge of its comfort zone, generating the dissonance that motivates reflection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of participants' background and expertise to pitch difficulty
- Access to real clinical events as scenario sources
#### Constraints
- Simulation-based education can complement, but should not replace, education involving real patients in genuine settings

### Target Learners
- Health professionals in simulated team training programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- Non-technical teamwork skills during critical events
