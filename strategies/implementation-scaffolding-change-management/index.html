Strengthen implementation scaffolding for new edtech rollouts with routines, guided practice, and change management grounded in observed workflows¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's recommendation for district leaders rolling out new tools: "pair new feature rollouts with concrete routines, guided practice, and modest incentives when possible, such as preparing teacher champions to support school-wide adoption and/or offering stipends to early adopters." It further advises keeping change management front and center in rollout plans grounded in real, observed workflows, and leveraging usage data to guide priorities while avoiding overstating conclusions from usage of periodic-meeting tools.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Observation of existing workflows to support a clear change management process
  • Training and follow-through, especially for AI tools given varied staff comfort levels

Constraints¶

  • Usage data must be interpreted cautiously: a tool meant for a periodic meeting will by design show lower usage than one for individual teachers

Target Learners¶

  • District leaders implementing new edtech tools
  • School-based student support teams

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Integration of new tools into daily educator workflows
  • Sustained adoption of data-driven meeting practices

Related Strategies¶

  • Embed product rollout into existing routines with structured cadences, teacher champions, and stipends to accelerate adoption
  • Embed new solutions into existing operations and procedures to sustain and scale them
  • Plan mutual accountability for implementation, including rollout plans, educator supports, and early indicators for pivoting
  • Build trust early through quick wins and visible responsiveness to feedback, using a tiered rollout to grow champions
  • Mobilize & Implement: provide professional learning throughout the pilot and use real-time data dashboards to keep usage aligned with dosage requirements
