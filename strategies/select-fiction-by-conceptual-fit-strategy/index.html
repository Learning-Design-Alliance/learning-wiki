Strategies

Select fiction by conceptual fit rather than popularity, and use it as discrepant events and small-group critique prompts¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

From pilot and follow-up observations, the authors recommend teachers "pay little attention to the popularity of the film, the quality of special effects, the overall cinematic value of the film, quality of acting, box office budgets, or popularity of the actors." The most impactful examples addressed the concepts in question directly, presented evidence to refute or confirm claims inferred from the script, or led to divergent conversations. The most impactful strategies were fiction prompts for small group discussions, fiction as discrepant events, and video vignettes during whole-class discussion with intentional opportunities for student questioning.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers intending to insert vignettes from film should pay close attention to copyright restrictions on media they access, utilize, or assign.

Constraints¶

  • Derived from one teacher's action research in chemistry classrooms; the authors note these recommendations pertain to fiction selection and the nature of instruction in that context.

Target Learners¶

  • high school chemistry students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical evaluation of media representations of science
  • core chemistry concepts

Claims¶

  • Fiction excerpts increased engagement and interest particularly among students who typically underachieved in science [+W]
  • Inserting fiction excerpts into chemistry instruction shifted classroom discourse toward more student questions, peer challenge, and evidence-based argumentation [+W]
  • Intervention students reported fiction videos made instruction more interesting and helped them understand difficult concepts, but rated hands-on labs as most helpful [+W]
