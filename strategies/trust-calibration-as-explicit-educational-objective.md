# Treat trust calibration as an explicit educational objective of ML education, pursued through ICE-T's three facets

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that school curricula promote transparency, data literacy, and an understanding of key algorithmic principles, and proposes that "trust calibration be treated as an explicit educational objective of ML education, alongside traditional objectives such as conceptual understanding and procedural skill". ICE-T supplies the concrete mechanism: intermodal transfer builds representational richness, the UMC progression enacts graduated process control, and PETSP-ML builds the capacity to contextualize errors, so trust calibration becomes an organic consequence of a well-designed, multi-faceted learning experience rather than an isolated add-on.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning units must integrate all three ICE-T facets rather than treating them as independent strategies applied in isolation
#### Constraints
- The predicted gradient of the UMC progression is a testable hypothesis derived from the aversion literature rather than an established result; ICE-T has not been evaluated in classroom studies

### Target Learners
- K-12 students
- students with little or no prior exposure to programming

### Target Learning Goals
- calibrated trust in AI systems
- informed skepticism toward AI outputs
- appropriate reliance on ML systems
