# Integrating physics and mathematics into biology courses requires cross-disciplinary collaboration and attention to student epistemologies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that efforts to integrate physics and mathematics into biology courses be supported by collaboration among biologists, physicists, and other scientists, drawing on the physics education community's "disciplinary perspective as well as the tools and analytic methods." It further recommends attending to the epistemological ideas students hold for each discipline, and warns that reforming physics courses to include more biology may encounter similar epistemological issues.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Significant contributions from the physics education community for disciplinary perspective and analytic methods.
#### Constraints
- Based on preliminary observations of one reformed course and student interviews; the authors describe their results as by no means conclusive.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory biology students
- undergraduate physics students in biology-infused reforms

### Target Learning Goals
- integrating quantitative and physical reasoning into biological understanding

## Claims

- [In a reformed introductory biology course, instructors used equations as referents for reasoning about biological systems rather than for problem-solving with precise answers, unlike typical introductory physics use](../claims/biology-equations-as-referents-not-problem-solving.md) [+M]
- [A second interviewed student responded positively to equations, viewing them as tools for sense-making and communication of biological concepts](../claims/student-equations-sense-making-communication.md) [+W]
- [One interviewed student responded negatively to equations in biology, viewing biology as tangible and perceivable and treating equations as material to memorize for exams](../claims/student-negative-epistemology-biology-tangible.md) [+W]
- [A typical IPLS exam problem can be physically authentic yet not biologically authentic, because it does not require using physics tools for biological ends](../claims/physically-authentic-not-biologically-authentic.md) [+M]
- [Students' epistemologies about math-physics-biology connections can shift through biologically authentic instruction, though many introductory biology students initially see no connection](../claims/student-epistemology-shifts-interdisciplinary-biology.md) [+M]
- [In the studied organismal biology course, instructors' uses of mathematics and physics fell into four categories: recalling, synthesizing data, calculating, and making sense of biological phenomena](../claims/four-categories-math-physics-use-biology-course.md) [+M]
- [About half of the analyzed questions used mathematics and physics for biologically authentic sense-making of organisms, a use differing from typical physics-course uses of the same tools](../claims/biologically-authentic-sense-making-prevalent.md) [+M]
