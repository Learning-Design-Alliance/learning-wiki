# Adopt onsite LDHF/m-mentoring for health worker capacity building where feasible, keeping staff at service delivery points

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' recommendation is that, in their words, "capacity building among healthcare workers, to the extent possible, should utilize the onsite LDHF/m-mentoring learning approach". The strategy trains entire facility teams at their workplace in spaced, simulation-based sessions with mobile mentoring, so health workers remain at their place of work during training events and service delivery is less disrupted. The article says health care managers should have the option to select the approach depending on their country's priorities or context.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Anatomical models available onsite for continuous simulation practice
- Mentors reachable by telephone and SMS for mobile mentoring
- Facility management support such as arranging duty rosters so trainees can attend practice sessions and assessments
#### Constraints
- Different work schedules and workplace distraction limited peer practice sessions
- Lack of medical equipment and supplies and health workers' strikes were reported barriers
- Further evidence is needed on the value of m-mentoring for learning outcomes and the cost-effectiveness of both approaches, which were not explored

### Target Learners
- Maternal and newborn care providers (doctors, nurses, community health extension workers) in LMIC health facilities

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition and retention of BEmONC day-of-birth care competencies while minimising service disruption from offsite training absenteeism
