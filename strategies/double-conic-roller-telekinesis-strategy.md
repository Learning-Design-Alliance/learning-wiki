# Present the double conical roller as a telekinesis magic trick to expose the misconception that high and low are absolute properties of a support surface

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The instructor lets a pen roll down an inclined wedge to establish which side is higher, then claims mental power will drag the double cone up the incline — and the cone rolls 'upward'. The demonstration targets the belief that "what constitutes 'high' and 'low' in determining the direction in which an object will fall is absolute", showing instead that fall direction follows the path of the center of mass. Students are then asked to debunk the trick through structured inquiry.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The pen must first be rolled down the incline so students form a clear expectation; without clear expectation, a strong cognitive conflict cannot arise
#### Constraints
- The trick should be repeated once or twice so students can focus on different aspects, and the cone should be picked up quickly at the top so the trick is not spoiled

### Target Learners
- high school and college physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding center of mass dynamics and how support geometry affects apparent direction of motion
