# Develop a range of linguistic comprehension skills (word-level and sentence-level language) for ELs with reading comprehension difficulties in the upper-elementary grades

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that linguistic comprehension effects exceeded word reading effects for struggling ELs, the article recommends that "developing a range of linguistic comprehension skills (e.g., word-level and sentence-level language skills) may be important for ELs with reading comprehension difficulties in the upper-elementary grades." This targets the vocabulary and listening comprehension weaknesses that the study identified as more prominent sources of reading comprehension failure for ELs than for non-ELs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction should address multiple levels of language, including word-level vocabulary and sentence- or discourse-level oral language skills
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is specific to ELs with reading comprehension difficulties; for non-ELs in the study, word reading was the larger source of difficulty

### Target Learners
- English learners in grades 3–5 with reading comprehension difficulties

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension
- Vocabulary
- Listening comprehension
