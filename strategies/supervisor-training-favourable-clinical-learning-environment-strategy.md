# Train supervisors as coaches and mentors and provide a favourable clinical learning environment to support trainee doctors' talent development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that healthcare professionals run training programmes teaching supervisors to serve as coaches and mentors, and that administrators supply a favourable clinical learning environment with opportunities for mixed practice, continuous feedback, case presentations and exposure to varied clinical problems. It argues these conditions facilitate trainee doctors' talent development, since the study found supervisory style effects contingent on the learning environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supervisors trained in coaching behaviours such as selecting suitable tasks, critical evaluation, advice and structured ways to address weaknesses
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a cross-sectional self-report survey; the authors call for longitudinal data to establish causal relationships

### Target Learners
- trainee doctors undergoing housemanship in public hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- professional and medical competencies (clinical, communication, personal and professional competence)
