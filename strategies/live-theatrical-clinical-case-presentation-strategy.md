# Present a clinical case in class as a live theatrical performance with actors as an alternative to video projection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advocates staging a simulated clinical scene with actors in the classroom, followed by the same interactive debriefing used with video cases. It reports the live format was rated highly useful (9.04 ± 1.16) and produced slightly better scores, concluding it "appears to be an attractive alternative to the traditional format and as effective as a video projection". The authors recommend further similar experiences to evaluate the practice more widely.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Actors must rehearse and be prepared for the unexpected that can go wrong when done live; both live and pre-recorded formats required a lot more preparation
#### Constraints
- Repeating the scene live is more complicated and requires actors to return to the classroom; video allows replay, pause and rewind; cost, feasibility and teacher preference should factor into the choice of method

### Target Learners
- medicine and physiotherapy students in classroom-based seminars

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding and managing a clinical syndrome through case-based discussion

### Affordances
- [Vicarious Learning Observing Simulation](../theories/social-learning-theory.md)
