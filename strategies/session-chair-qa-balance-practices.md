# Session-chair practices for balanced, inclusive Q&A sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Appendix A compiles best practices assigning the biggest responsibility for Q&A balance to session chairs, who shape the discussion. Chairs should encourage junior scientists at the session start, keep talks to time so question sessions are not shortened, ask specifically for questions rather than comments, wait before selecting the first hand and prioritize women, junior scientists, and underrepresented individuals, and interrupt questioners who turn questions into speeches. The document recommends chairs be trained in person or supplied a list of guidelines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Session chairs should ideally be trained in person on the guidelines; if that is not possible, a list of guidelines should be supplied.
#### Constraints
- The article states that due to the lack of correlations between the fraction of questions asked by women and any other examined variable, it has no recommendations based solely on the Cool Stars data; these practices draw on Davenport et al. (2014) and Pritchard et al. (2014) and other sources.

### Target Learners
- conference session chairs in STEM

### Target Learning Goals
