# Actionable suggestions for elementary out-of-school-time science education emphasize creativity, questioning, explicit NOS instruction, and experience over knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article closes with a list of "actionable suggestions for elementary OST science education" drawn from its findings. These include "Incorporate student creativity into science activities", "Encourage students to think of questions about what they have learned", and framing "lessons or science demonstrations as experiments with observations and inferences". The list also advises that where the goal is interest or science identity, educators should "focus on students having science experiences rather than learning science knowledge", and that teaching nature of science requires "explicit instruction on these topics".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must be able to structure activities that give students ownership, such as creative contributions and question-asking opportunities
#### Constraints
- The suggestions arise from a self-selected sample of students who chose to join the astronomy OST programs, which the authors note may bias perceptions toward favorable views of science

### Target Learners
- Elementary students in grades 3-6 in out-of-school time science programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Science interest
- Science identity
- Nature of science understanding
