# Use the quality of students' wrong answers to diagnose natural number bias and guide students forward from partially correct answers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the natural number bias is visible in students' answers during classroom discussions or formative tests, teachers can identify students who persistently rely on natural number knowledge and give conceptual change support. The article recommends guiding students forward "building on their partially correct answers, while confronting their inconsistencies", treating intermediary concepts as necessary developmental steps rather than mere errors. Refutation-style approaches that make the limits of prior concepts visible are suggested, adapted to the numeric domain.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be able to gather qualitative information about the nature of students' wrong answers, e.g. through classroom discussion or formative tests
#### Constraints
- The article notes a text-based refutation format faces obvious limitations in the numeric world of fractions and decimals, requiring cross-refutation across fraction and decimal representations

### Target Learners
- primary school students learning fractions and decimals

### Target Learning Goals
- overcoming natural number bias in rational number reasoning
