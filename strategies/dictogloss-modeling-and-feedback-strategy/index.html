Model dictogloss procedures and provide feedback during collaborative reconstruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article advises that 'familiarity with the task procedures is a key point in the accurate accomplishment of the task. So, teacher modeling and role-playing of the activity is useful at the first sessions of instruction.' Teachers should remain available during collaborative activities, attend to the accuracy of the final product, and give feedback because students tend to remember their incorrect solutions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher models the steps of the dictogloss process before students co-construct texts on their own

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • pre-intermediate and intermediate EFL learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • accurate collocation use
  • task procedure familiarity

Claims¶

  • The dictogloss group earned the highest posttest mean (32.20), with discussion (29.15) and control (27.65) groups not significantly different from each other [+W]
  • Form-focused dictogloss tasks improve collocation knowledge more than meaning-focused discussion tasks or conventional instruction among intermediate EFL learners [+W]
  • The authors attribute the dictogloss advantage to depth of processing, discovery learning, pushed output, noticing, awareness raising, negotiation, and collaboration [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use dictogloss tasks in listening lessons to raise motivation and integrate all four language skills
