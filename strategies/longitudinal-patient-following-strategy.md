# Assign clerkship students to follow a single patient longitudinally from outpatient visit through surgery and postoperative review

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends guiding students "to follow selected patients throughout their entire clinical course" to foster deep understanding of disease evolution, diagnostic reasoning, and therapeutic decision-making. Concretely, students participate in initial reception, history collection, physical examination, admission processes, inpatient management, preoperative planning, observation of the operation, and postoperative review. The authors report this continuous engagement bridges theory and practice and cultivates clinical proficiency and humanistic qualities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An experienced physician at associate chief physician level or above must monitor and guide the entire process; attending physicians must screen eligible and willing surgical patients
#### Constraints
- Feasibility depends on the availability of suitable surgical patients who can be immediately admitted; substantial physician time is needed to familiarize student groups with the case, develop the surgical plan, and conduct postoperative review

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in specialty clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- Holistic clinical reasoning
- Continuity of learning and professional responsibility
- Practical competence and problem-solving
- Empathy and patient-centered communication
