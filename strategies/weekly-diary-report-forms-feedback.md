# Use weekly diary-report forms with feedback to develop pre-service teachers' self-regulation strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study implemented diary keeping within regular classroom instruction over fourteen weeks. Pre-service science teachers filled a 10-question Diary-Report Form after studying, and the researcher collected the diaries weekly and provided feedback and suggestions about the self-regulation strategies used. This combination of structured self-reporting and weekly feedback supported increases in metacognition, time management, intrinsic motivation, task value, and academic achievement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 4-hours training about the purposes of the application, when and how to fill the Diary Report Forms, and how to take advantage of feedback
- Weekly distribution, collection, and evaluation of diary forms with individual feedback and suggestions
#### Constraints
- No additional training program was applied regarding self-regulation strategies, so participants used only the strategies they already knew
- Fourteen weeks of diary keeping was not enough to enhance motivation strategies, which declined in both groups

### Target Learners
- pre-service science teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- self-regulation strategies
- metacognition
- time management
- academic achievement in instructional planning and evaluation
