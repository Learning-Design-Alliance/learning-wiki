# Use repeated observational competence assessments to target top-up training and give immediate structured feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that future feasibility studies use tools like ENACT-E in two ways: "to identify weaker areas across the cohort of CBR workers" and "to provide immediate structured feedback to CBR workers". Low month-five ratings triggered targeted top-up training on problem solving, goal setting and advice-giving, and prompted amendments to home visit forms adding compulsory substance-use and physical-health sections.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Non-specialist raters trained to score the scale; supervisors able to give immediate feedback
#### Constraints
- Role-play assessments were not repeated during the pilot, so training and pilot ratings were not fully comparable

### Target Learners
- lay health workers in training and early practice

### Target Learning Goals
- competence in structured assessment, goal setting and collaborative problem solving
