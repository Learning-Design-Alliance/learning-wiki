Planned future curricular changes: more ultrasound integration, in-situ simulations with interprofessional recruits, and a peer-to-peer medical education component¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

From the end-of-curriculum survey and focus group, the authors identified three planned changes: increase ultrasound training by reviewing the curriculum with an ultrasound expert; increase realism by moving some months to an in-situ setting and recruiting pediatric emergency department nurses, technicians, faculty, and other learners; and create an adjunct curriculum focusing on adult learning, basic SBL skills, and SBL-related instructional strategies enabling peer-to-peer SBL education.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Collaboration with a content expert (ultrasound) to integrate more training
  • Access to an in-situ setting and interprofessional participants
  • Development of an adjunct curriculum on adult learning and SBL skills

Constraints¶

  • These are planned changes responding to learner feedback, not yet implemented at the time of reporting

Target Learners¶

  • pediatric emergency medicine fellows

Target Learning Goals¶

  • ultrasound procedural skills
  • realism and interprofessional teamwork in simulated resuscitation
  • peer-to-peer simulation education skills

Claims¶

  • Mixed interprofessional group training promoted mutual respect, understanding of roles, and appreciation of teamwork among ED participants [+W]
  • In a theory-guided in-situ simulation programme, an interprofessional primary care team progressed through the expansive learning cycle from questioning current practice to consolidating new practices [+W]
  • P2P-teacher trainings integrate medical subject content with teaching skills, sometimes explicitly and sometimes implicitly [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Condense curriculum development into three phases — needs assessment, instructional strategies, evaluation — using multiple needs assessments to select strategies
