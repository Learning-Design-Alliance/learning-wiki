# Train medical students on simulators in tracheal intubation with the Macintosh blade, and consider the Bonfils as an alternative novice-training technique

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (4 for, 5 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that airway management, including tracheal intubation, be a mandatory part of medical curricula, with simulator practice before graduation. It states that "medical students must be trained on simulators before graduating from medical school" and that the Bonfils "is a promising intubation technique and might be easier to learn than the MB, at least in a manikin". In the study, training followed Peyton's four-step approach with alternating demonstration, explanation, and supervised practice on a Laerdal Airway Management Trainer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulator-based practice opportunities within the medical curriculum, with instructor-led four-step technique instruction before independent trials
#### Constraints
- Manikin results may not translate to clinical reality: the study environment was stress-free and the simulator airway lacks mucus, coughing, and realistic tongue stiffness

### Target Learners
- Final-year medical students with little or no intubation experience

### Target Learning Goals
- Tracheal intubation skill with Macintosh blade and alternative devices such as the Bonfils fibrescope
