# Making Connections

## Description
Making connections involves linking new information to existing knowledge and experiences to enhance understanding and retention. This can be done by relating the text to personal experiences (text-to-self), comparing it to other texts (text-to-text), or relating it to real-world events and knowledge (text-to-world). Proficient readers actively relate what they are reading or listening to with something in their schema to construct meaning. Modeling the process, especially text-to-self connections initially, is crucial, using strategy-related language to verbalize the connections and providing opportunities for students to share their connections.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to texts, modeling by an instructor or facilitator, chart paper or digital tools for recording connections, and structured activities for practice.
#### Constraints
- Superficial connections may not enhance understanding. Requires explicit instruction and modeling. Some students may struggle to make meaningful connections if they lack relevant background knowledge or experience.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- K-12 students, adult learners, and anyone who wants to improve their reading comprehension and ability to connect with texts on a deeper level.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance comprehension, build connections between new information and prior knowledge, improve retention, and develop critical thinking skills by relating text to personal experiences, other texts, and the world.

### Affordances
- Improves comprehension by linking new information to existing knowledge, increases engagement by making the material personally relevant, and fosters deeper understanding and retention of information.

### Personalization
- Scaffolded prompts and sentence starters can be used for beginners. Learners can choose the type of connection (text-to-self, text-to-text, text-to-world) that resonates most with them. Can be adapted to various text types, including narrative, informational, and multimedia.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor the depth and relevance of student connections through discussions, written reflections, or graphic organizers. Evaluate how well students can articulate the link between the text and their own experiences, other texts, or the world. Track improvements in comprehension scores and engagement levels.

## Impact
- Research supports that making connections enhances comprehension and engagement with text. Studies have shown that thought-provoking questions and peer discussions can force students to link newly acquired information to material outside of the current lesson. (King et al., 1997; Morrison, 2005)
