# Making Connections

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Making connections is a strategy in which learners deliberately relate new material to what they already know — to their own experiences (text-to-self), to other texts or domains (text-to-text), or to real-world events and situations (text-to-world). It is carried out through prompts, discussion, or structured activities that require learners to generate the link themselves rather than having it supplied, converting new information from isolated facts into a networked knowledge structure.

## Design Implications

Connection-making works because new information is encoded more durably and retrieved more flexibly when it is integrated into existing schemas; the act of generating a link is itself a form of elaborative processing [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. The strategy depends on the learner actually possessing relevant prior knowledge to connect to — instruction should first activate that knowledge, then prompt connections to it [Activation](../principles/activation.md). Connections generated by the learner outperform connections merely presented to them, because generation forces retrieval and elaboration rather than passive recognition.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activated and accessible prior knowledge relevant to the new material ([Activating Prior Knowledge](activating-prior-knowledge.md))
- Prompts that specify the *kind* of connection sought (self, text, world) rather than asking vaguely to "relate this to something"
- Time and structure for learners to articulate connections aloud, in writing, or through [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md)
- Instructor follow-up that evaluates the accuracy of connections, since a plausible-but-wrong link can entrench misconceptions

#### Constraints
- Learners with impoverished or inaccurate prior knowledge cannot make productive connections and may fabricate superficial or erroneous links [~M]
- Connections to personal experience can anchor understanding in idiosyncratic, non-generalizable cases if not bridged to the disciplinary concept
- Overly open prompts ("make a connection") yield low-quality responses from novices; specificity of the prompt drives quality
- When working memory is already loaded, generating connections competes with comprehension of the material itself [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Elaborative interrogation**: learners answer "why is this true?" questions about statements, forcing links to prior knowledge
- **Analogy mapping**: instructor supplies a familiar analog and learners map its structure onto the new concept ([Analogies](../elements/analogies.md))
- **Text-connection protocols**: during reading, learners mark passages as text-to-self, text-to-text, or text-to-world and justify each mark
- **Cross-domain linking**: learners identify structural parallels between the current topic and a previously studied topic or discipline

### Target Learners
- Learners with moderate prior knowledge benefit most — enough knowledge to connect to, but not so integrated that connections are automatic [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]
- Novices need instructor-supplied analogs or advance organizers first, since unguided connection-making exceeds their available knowledge structure
- Very knowledgeable learners gain less, as they spontaneously make connections without prompting [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: integrating new ideas into existing knowledge networks
- Transfer: building multiple retrieval routes so knowledge can be applied in novel contexts
- Reading comprehension: constructing a coherent situation model of a text
- Retention: elaborative encoding that supports long-term recall

### Instructions
1. Activate relevant prior knowledge before introducing new material ([Activating Prior Knowledge](activating-prior-knowledge.md), [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
2. Present the new concept or text in a chunked, comprehensible form ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md))
3. Prompt learners to generate connections of a specified type — to their experience, to other texts or topics, or to world events — and to explain *why* the connection holds ([Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md) if available; otherwise structured questioning)
4. Have learners record connections visibly through annotation or a connection log ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md))
5. Discuss and evaluate connections as a group, correcting misconceptions and highlighting the most structurally informative links
6. Revisit and refine connections as understanding deepens, replacing idiosyncratic links with disciplinary ones

## Related Strategies
- [Activating Prior Knowledge](activating-prior-knowledge.md) — the prerequisite step; connections can only be made to knowledge that is accessible
- [Analogical Reasoning](../principles/analogical-reasoning.md) — the principled form of text-to-text connection, mapping structure from a familiar to an unfamiliar domain

## Examples
- **Reciprocal Teaching (Palincsar & Brown)** — small-group reading routine in which students take turns questioning, summarizing, clarifying, and predicting, with explicit "connect this to…" prompts during discussion
- **Elaborative interrogation in science** — students study a physics principle by answering "why does this make sense given what I know about X?" for each statement, linking new laws to previously mastered ones
- **Text-connection journals in literature courses** — students annotate novels with text-to-self and text-to-world entries, then revise them after seminar discussion to distinguish personal resonance from textual evidence
