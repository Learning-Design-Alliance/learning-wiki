# Combine targeted EBM courses with integration of EBM terminology and concepts into other courses, with course placement after the second year

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends two complementary moves: targeted EBM training, which its data associate with better self-rated skills, knowledge and retrieval habits, and embedding EBM terminology into other courses, since knowledge rose with study years alongside rising EBM-course exposure. It states that "Significantly higher EBM-related knowledge and skills among EBM trained students underline the importance of targeted EBM education" and highlights "the importance of integrating EBM terminology and concepts also into the thematic of other courses." Students most often wanted the course in the third or fourth year (50.93% of medical students).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- EBM-related terms appear in non-EBM courses, which requires coordination of course content across a faculty's basic, preclinical and clinical modules
#### Constraints
- The evidence is cross-sectional and self-reported; the article did not collect details on the hours or content of the EBM courses attended, so what made training effective cannot be specified

### Target Learners
- medical students
- health sciences students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of EBM-related terms
- skills in searching and critically appraising medical literature
- use of evidence sources for healthcare decisions
