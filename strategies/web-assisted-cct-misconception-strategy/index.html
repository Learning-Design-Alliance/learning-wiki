Use web-assisted conceptual change texts with visualizations to prevent and remediate science misconceptions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 8 claims (7 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend combining conceptual change texts with web devices: "Through the use of web devices such as pictures, video, animation and simulation of especially concrete and difficult to reach concepts can decrease the formation of errors." They also recommend three-stage tests for simultaneous, objective diagnosis of conceptual errors and suggest visualizing science textbook texts.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Diagnostic pre-study to identify target conceptual errors in upper grades
  • Web-assisted texts prepared from identified errors

Constraints¶

  • Authors note conceptual change texts work "even if they don't have a success of 100%"

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary and secondary science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Replacing misconceptions with scientifically correct concepts

Claims¶

  • Conceptual error reduction was more durable in the web-assisted group (61%) than the control group (3.8%) three months after instruction [+W]
  • Web-assisted conceptual change texts reduced 8th graders' conceptual errors in water chemistry more than classic conceptual change texts (65% vs. 14% error reduction) [+W]
  • Web-assisted conceptual change texts produced significantly higher three-month permanency test scores than classic texts (U = 67, p = 0.011) [+W]
  • Web-assisted conceptual change texts produced significantly higher three-stage post-test scores than classic conceptual change texts (U = 103, p = 0.038) [+W]
  • Students reported that videos and 3D animations facilitated conceptual understanding via concretization, pausing, slowing down, replaying, and enlarging features [+W]
  • The quantity of videos and 3D animations was related to success in remedying misconceptions: where materials were quite limited, incorrect or partly true answers remained high [~W]
  • Students who used videos and 3D animations in class or as homework gave fewer incorrect answers about basic computer units after instruction (descriptive pre-post counts, no untreated control) [+W]
  • 8th and 9th graders held multiple persistent conceptual errors about water chemistry despite prior instruction [+W]
