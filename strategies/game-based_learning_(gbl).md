# Game-Based Learning (GBL)

## Description
Game-based learning involves designing learning activities so that game characteristics and game principles inhere within the learning activities themselves. It utilizes games to reach specific learning objectives, whether they belong to knowledge, skills, or attitudes. This includes board games, real-life games, and digital games.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful design of game activities to align with learning objectives. Selection or creation of games that support specific skills and knowledge. Facilitation to guide students through the game and debrief afterward.
#### Constraints
- Can be time-consuming to design and implement effectively. Requires careful alignment of game mechanics with learning objectives. May not be suitable for all learners or subject areas.

### Affordances
- Engaging and motivating due to game-like structure. Allows for experimentation and application of knowledge in a safe environment. Fosters critical thinking and problem-solving skills.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to different learning styles and needs through varied game types (board, real-life, digital). Activities can be customized to align with specific learning objectives.

## Assessment Evidence
- Assess student performance within the game environment. Observe collaboration and problem-solving skills during gameplay. Gather student feedback on engagement and learning.

## Impact
- Research indicates that game-based learning can enhance motivation, engagement, complex problem-solving, and social-emotional skills such as self-regulation. Studies have found that students in game-based courses exhibit more positive working relationships and entrepreneurial skills.
