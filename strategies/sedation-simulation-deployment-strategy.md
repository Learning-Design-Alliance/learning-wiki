# Use sedation complication simulations for new trainees at the start of the academic year and as review where a sedative is less commonly used, adding deeper consent discussion in future iterations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend deploying these cases for new trainees at the beginning of fellowship or residency and as review for more experienced providers: "These simulations can be used for new trainees at the beginning of the academic year for institutions where ketamine and propofol are commonly used and as a review for trainees at institutions where propofol is not as commonly utilized." They also state a concrete revision: "Future iterations of these simulations will feature a more in-depth discussion of obtaining consent during the debrief session and inclusion of this information on the educational PowerPoint." They further suggest the scenarios can be used "throughout fellowship/residency as a means to review this material."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor access to all appendices, including the debriefing guide and educational PowerPoint
- Simulation instructors familiar with the cases to standardize delivery
#### Constraints
- Learner feedback indicated a need for further discussion on obtaining proper sedation consent and assent, and further education around propofol

### Target Learners
- first-year fellows and residents
- trainees at institutions where propofol is not commonly utilized
- PEM providers generally

### Target Learning Goals
- comfort with pediatric procedural sedation
- consent and assent discussion
- management of propofol and ketamine side effects
