# Use Fermi problems grounded in local natural events to train pre-service physics teachers in estimation and logical reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy poses an everyday, locally salient problem — here estimating the power of a nearby volcanic eruption — and has students build reasonable working hypotheses from "personal insights, educated guesses and general knowledge" before computing order-of-magnitude answers with high-school-level physics. The article reports using it in the first year of the Physics Teacher Program at UNRN, with satellite imagery and simple measurements as inputs, and contextualising results against everyday quantities like truckloads or national electric power.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A first step of building a set of reasonable working hypotheses before any calculation, as the article prescribes for every Fermi problem
- Willingness to accept approximate answers: the goal is a reasonable approximation within about half an order of magnitude, not an accurate result
#### Constraints
- The article states the assumed covered area was smaller than the actual fallout, so energy and power may be underestimated
- Some inputs, such as the output speed, had no direct measurements available

### Target Learners
- first-year university students in physics and chemistry teacher programs

### Target Learning Goals
- order-of-magnitude estimation
- logical reasoning
- observation skills
- imagination in framing physical problems
