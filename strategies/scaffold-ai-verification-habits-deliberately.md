# Deliberately scaffold AI-output verification habits rather than assume they develop alongside rising AI use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because increased AI use in this engineering mechanics cohort did not uniformly translate into increased checking of AI outputs, the authors recommend that instructors treat verification as a habit to be explicitly taught. As the article puts it, "That checking did not keep pace with use suggests verification is a habit instructors will need to scaffold deliberately rather than assume". The strategy applies in courses where students routinely use LLMs for problem solving and can be implemented through recurring instructor modeling of verification practices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured opportunities for students to practice and observe verification (e.g., re-solving, external cross-referencing) rather than relying on students to develop checking spontaneously
#### Constraints
- Derived from descriptive self-report data with no causal claims; the authors caution findings from a single semester may not remain directly applicable as tool capabilities change

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students using LLMs in coursework

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate evaluation of AI-generated solutions
- independent verification and error-checking skills
