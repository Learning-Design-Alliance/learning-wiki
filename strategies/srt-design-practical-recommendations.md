# Practical recommendations for designing and running SRTs in scaled digital learning environments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's implications-for-practice section advises researchers to choose a SRT only when the scientific questions concern sequencing, timing, and personalization, and to ensure "All intervention sequences in a SRT should be useful, feasible, and scalable", avoiding sequences that could never be used in practice or are knowingly deleterious. It recommends basing sample size and power calculations on the most important research questions, noting that "sample sizes usually need to be larger for SRTs (compared to A/B tests)", and that randomization probabilities may be changed to favor interventions of interest to increase power.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sample size and power calculations based on the trial's most important research questions, considering which subset of users will be randomized to the sequences of interest
#### Constraints
- Platform feasibility limits designs: in PERCS, emails were sent only once per week because manual daily sending would not be feasible for instructors on the platform

- learners in scaled digital learning environments

### Target Learning Goals
