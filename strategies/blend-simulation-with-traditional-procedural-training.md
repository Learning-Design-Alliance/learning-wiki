# Blend simulation with traditional training rather than separating them into discrete components

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
For this study, simulation-based training and traditional training were deliberately separated into two discrete components with no overlap to control for confounding. The authors state that "in reality a more blended approach would be potentially more beneficial", because interleaving simulation with hands-on training "would likely lessen the negative impact of deficiencies of, for example, an inadequate training tool/simulator".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to both a simulator and traditional training components (didactic, cadaveric, volunteer scanning, phantom needling) within the same programme
#### Constraints
- Stated only as a recommendation from this pilot; the blended approach was not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- novice anesthesiologists training in ultrasound-guided regional anesthesia

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition and transfer of ultrasound-guided procedural skills to clinical performance
