# Assess inquiry-based laboratory curricula across institutions using published, validated instruments, and report effect sizes or complete summary statistics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that systematic assessment of inquiry-based laboratory curricula use published, validated instruments and span multiple institutions, because "The use of published, validated instruments will improve our ability to compare the effectiveness of inquiry-based learning in the laboratory across courses and institutions." It also asks authors to report effect sizes or complete summary statistics so cross-study comparison is possible. The strategy addresses the review's finding that few studies were controlled, instrument-based, or multi-institutional.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Use published, validated assessment instruments rather than course-specific surveys
- Report effect sizes or complete summary statistics (means, SDs, sample sizes) so effect sizes can be estimated
- Collect data across multiple courses or institutions to test generalizability
#### Constraints
- Withholding a believed-beneficial innovation from a control group may be unethical; the article suggests before/after semester comparisons as an alternative
- Faculty at smaller institutions teaching a single section may be unable to run concurrent controls

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate biology students

### Target Learning Goals
- Disciplinary content knowledge, scientific reasoning, and attitudes in inquiry-based laboratory courses
