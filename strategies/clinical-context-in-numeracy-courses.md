# Implement clinical context in all numeracy- and statistics-related courses in medical education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that numeracy and statistics teaching be situated in clinical rather than abstract contexts, because learners who encounter numbers in a relevant context appear more likely to improve both objective and subjective numeracy. The authors state: "These findings emphasize the importance of implementation of clinical context in all numeracy- and statistics-related courses and programs in medical education." They also suggest more frequent biostatistics courses with medical-context numerical concepts throughout the curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Numerical content must be embedded in medical or clinical contexts rather than abstract definitions
#### Constraints
- The article reports the intervention made very little practical difference in objective numeracy for students already high at baseline

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- health numeracy
- interpretation of numerical information in clinical practice
