# Classroom Routines And Procedures

## Description
Classroom routines and procedures are consistent, explicitly taught patterns for recurring activities — entering the room, distributing materials, transitioning between tasks, seeking help, submitting work. They are carried out by naming the procedure, modeling it, rehearsing it until fluent, and maintaining it with brief reinforcement, so that management becomes automatic rather than negotiated.

## Design Implications

Routines convert recurring logistical decisions into automatic behavior, reducing the working-memory and attentional demands of classroom life for both teacher and students [Chunking reduces the load on working memory.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Research on classroom management consistently finds that teachers who establish and enforce clear procedures at the start of the year have fewer disruptions and gain more time for instruction than those who rely on reactive discipline [~S]. The mechanism is [Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md)-consistent (predictable cues and consistent consequences) but also cognitive: predictability lowers uncertainty, which otherwise consumes attention that could go to content.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of the recurring activity types worth routinizing (transitions, materials, start/end of class, help-seeking)
- Explicit teaching of each procedure: name it, model it ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)), rehearse it, and give feedback on the rehearsal
- Consistency of execution — a routine applied intermittently loses its cue value
- A small set of routines (typically 5–10 core procedures), not an exhaustive rulebook

#### Constraints
- Routines taught but never rehearsed to fluency collapse under pressure; naming a rule without practice does not produce automatic behavior [-M]
- Over-routinization can suppress productive [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) — if every minute is scripted, students have no room for inquiry, discussion, or productive struggle [~M]
- Routines imposed without rationale can undermine [Autonomy](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~M]; explaining the purpose of a procedure and involving students in designing it increases buy-in
- Routines that are culturally inflexible (e.g., silence norms, turn-taking conventions) can disadvantage students whose home discourse patterns differ [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Entry/exit routines** ("do-now" tasks, material check-out) — highest leverage for instructional time recovery
- **Transition routines** — timed, cued transitions (countdowns, signals) reduce the dead time where most disruption occurs
- **Help-seeking routines** — e.g., "ask three before me" or help queues, which protect [Practice](../elements/practice.md) time from interruption
- **Co-constructed routines** — students help design procedures, trading some consistency for ownership and [Belonging](../elements/belonging.md)

### Target Learners
- All learners benefit from predictability, but effects are strongest for younger students and those with attentional or executive-function challenges, for whom unstructured transitions are most costly [~M]
- Novice teachers benefit indirectly: routines reduce the classroom-management load that otherwise consumes their cognitive resources
- Students from environments with different schooling norms need routines taught explicitly rather than assumed

### Target Learning Goals
- Executive and self-regulation: internalizing procedures builds transferable habits of [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md)
- Automaticity of logistics: freeing attention for content rather than management
- Social norms: learning the discourse and collaboration conventions of a learning community

### Instructions
1. **Inventory recurring activities** — list the events that happen daily or weekly (arrival, materials, group work, dismissal).
2. **Design a procedure for each** — a short sequence of steps with a clear cue to start it.
3. **Model and rehearse** — demonstrate the procedure ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)), then have students practice it as a rehearsal, correcting gently and re-practicing.
4. **Reinforce briefly and consistently** — acknowledge correct execution early; avoid long lectures about rules.
5. **Maintain and fade** — after fluency develops, reduce explicit reinforcement; revisit and re-teach after breaks or when a routine degrades.

## Examples
- **Responsive Classroom** (https://www.responsiveclassroom.org) — the "Interactive Modeling" routine teaches procedures through a seven-step model-practice-feedback cycle used across thousands of elementary schools.
- **KIPP schools' "SLANT" routine** — a cued procedure (Sit up, Listen, Ask and answer questions, Nod, Track the speaker) for attention during instruction.
- **Rosenshine's "daily review"** — beginning each lesson with a short review of prior material, a routine with strong process-product research support.
