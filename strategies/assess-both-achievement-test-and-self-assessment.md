# Assess blended e-learning outcomes with both achievement tests and self-assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the study found blended e-learning had no significant impact on achievement test scores but significantly affected self-assessment scores, the authors suggest that "teachers who engage in blended e- learning should not only assess students’ learning performance by achievement tests, but also by self-assessment." Assessing both ways lets teachers capture objective test performance alongside students' subjective perceptions of cognition, skill, and attitude. The article also recommends that the Employment e-Training Platform add more course content and materials with animated simulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A self-assessment instrument covering cognition, skill, and attitude alongside the achievement test
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a five-week study in which achievement test differences were not significant; the authors note the effect on achievement should be examined in the long run

### Target Learners
- vocational high school students in blended e-learning courses

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing electrical machinery learning performance both objectively and subjectively
