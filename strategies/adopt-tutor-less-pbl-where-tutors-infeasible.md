# Adopt tutor-less PBL in large classrooms where funding or time constraints prohibit tutor-led small-group PBL

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing tutor-less PBL in large undergraduate classrooms as a lower-cost alternative when small tutor-led groups are not feasible. Citing Kaliyadan et al. (2012), it notes tutor-less and tutor-led PBL showed no significant difference in effects on student learning, indicating tutor-less PBL "could be implemented in instances in which funding and time constraints had previously prohibited use of small PBL groups requiring use of additional tutors." The authors' conclusion emphasizes that avoiding tutors avoids additional expenses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stand-alone PBL activities embedded in an existing lecture course
- Ill-structured, decision-making case problems
#### Constraints
- The article's own evidence cannot isolate PBL from other BIOC course activities; the authors were still seeking a way to conclusively establish the PBL component as the cause

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- generic problem-solving skills
- student engagement and satisfaction
