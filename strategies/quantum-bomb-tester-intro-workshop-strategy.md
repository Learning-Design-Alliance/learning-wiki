# Run a 1-hour introductory quantum computing workshop built around the quantum bomb tester story, accessible to students as young as 11

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article outlines an eight-step workshop agenda: set the stage with the bomb-tester story, explain the Mach-Zehnder interferometer, the effect of a bomb, map to qubits and gates, run the circuit on a simulator, play a Quantum Minesweeper game, discuss philosophy and applications, and extend to the 100% effective bomb tester. The author reports that at the Oxford Physics Marie Curious event 2020, 11-to-14 year old girls "successfully engaged with and completed Qiskit coding tasks" after a high-level overview.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A Jupyter Notebook with adjustable amounts of pre-filled code, run on a cloud service such as IBM Quantum Lab or Google Colab to avoid local installation; basic quantum circuit knowledge must be introduced separately for new learners.
#### Constraints
- The article notes hardware runs are possible only "if access is available during the workshop", and difficulty must be adjusted to participants' age and experience.

### Target Learners
- school students as young as 11
- beginners in quantum computing
- advanced participants via the extension

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding interaction-free measurement and interference
- coding quantum circuits with Hadamard and CNOT gates
