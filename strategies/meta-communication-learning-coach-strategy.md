# Meta-communicate about pedagogy to enlist students as active collaborators

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The bulletin's advice section recommends meta-communication—high-level communication about the nature and purpose of a course's normal communication—as the chief tool for enlisting students in student-active pedagogy. It states that 'the most powerful tool for changing students’ attitudes about learning and enlisting them as active collaborators in their own education is meta-communication,' covering course objectives, the rationale for techniques and assignments, and individual learning habits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The instructor must accept a learning-coach role and consistently direct student attention toward learning rather than grades, and toward reasoning rather than answers
#### Constraints
- This strategy is particularly important with students who display frustration or hostility toward CCS use and associated pedagogy

### Target Learners
- college students adjusting to student-active, CCS-based courses

### Target Learning Goals
- learning how to learn
- understanding the purpose of course activities and instructional techniques
