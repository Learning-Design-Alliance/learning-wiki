# Have students fill a data table of descent times and path lengths across reshaped tracks to discover the minimum-time curve themselves

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The core student activity is empirical discovery: students reshape a grooved track into successive paths between two fixed points, time each descent electronically, and measure each pathlength with string and ruler. The authors state that "Main task the students are supposed to do is to fill the data in Table 1", and that it "will not be difficult for students to explore that cycloid is truly the minimum time curve of fall under constant gravitational field". After each timing, students check that the minimum-time curve satisfies S4 = 4a and is tangent at O/A.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A track of at least 2 meters with a fixed release point O and electronic timing at O and A
#### Constraints
- Mathematical details of the cycloid, described as more apt for sophomore classes, can be suppressed for freshmen

### Target Learners
- freshman physics lab students
- sophomore physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- experimentally identifying the brachistochrone curve
- comparing measured pathlengths with theoretical predictions
- practicing timing and length measurement with error analysis
