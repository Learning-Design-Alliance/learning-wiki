# Physical Activity Breaks

## Description
Physical activity breaks are brief (2–10 minute) episodes of movement — stretching, walking, calisthenics, or breathing exercises — inserted into lessons, meetings, or study sessions. They counteract the attentional and physiological costs of prolonged sitting by increasing arousal and blood flow, returning learners to tasks with restored focus.

## Design Implications

Sustained seated attention degrades over time, particularly for children; short movement breaks counteract this decline and improve on-task behavior afterward [Classroom physical activity improves attention and on-task behavior.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+S]. Timing matters: brief moderate activity can enhance subsequent memory encoding, but very vigorous or poorly timed exertion immediately before high-load tasks can temporarily divert resources [Acute exercise timing moderates effects on memory.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~M]. Breaks function as attention management, not instruction — they prepare the learner to learn rather than teach content themselves, though they can be designed to reinforce content (e.g., movement-based recall games).

### Context
#### Requirements
- A predictable slot in the routine (e.g., every 25–30 minutes of seated work) so breaks become habitual rather than disruptive
- Minimal or no equipment; movements adaptable to the physical space (seated stretches for small rooms, walks for larger settings)
- Clear start and end signals so the break does not bleed into instructional time
- Options accommodating varied fitness levels and physical limitations

#### Constraints
- Poorly bounded breaks can cost more time than they return; without clear transitions, re-engagement is slow and off-task behavior increases [-M]
- Very vigorous activity immediately before tasks demanding fine motor control or new skill acquisition can temporarily impair performance [Acute exercise timing moderates effects on memory.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~M]
- Breaks imposed without learner choice or perceived purpose can feel like disruption and reduce buy-in [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [-M]
- Requires adaptation for learners with mobility limitations; a one-size-fits-all routine excludes some learners

#### Implementation Variability
- **Energizing breaks** (jumping jacks, power walks) for arousal restoration during mid-morning or post-lunch dips
- **Calming breaks** (breathing, stretching, brief relaxation) when the goal is reducing anxiety before assessments
- **Content-integrated breaks** (acting out vocabulary, movement-based math facts) that combine movement with [Practice](../elements/practice.md)
- **Standing breaks** — simply alternating sitting and standing during lectures or meetings

### Target Learners
- Children and adolescents, whose attention spans for seated work are shorter and who show the largest on-task gains after movement [Classroom physical activity improves attention and on-task behavior.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+S]
- Learners with ADHD or high activity needs, for whom movement breaks are a common [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md) strategy [+M]
- Adult learners in long meetings or training sessions, where fatigue effects are comparable though less studied

### Target Learning Goals
- Attention and self-regulation: restoring capacity for sustained focus on subsequent tasks
- Well-being and climate: signaling that physical needs matter, supporting [Belonging](../elements/belonging.md) and classroom community
- Indirect support for any content goal by improving the conditions under which [Attention](../elements/attention.md) is allocated

### Instructions
1. **Set a rhythm.** Schedule breaks at fixed intervals (e.g., after 25–30 minutes of seated work) or at natural segment boundaries in the lesson plan.
2. **Signal clearly.** Use a consistent cue to start and end the break so transitions stay short.
3. **Offer choice.** Provide 2–3 movement options (stretch, walk, breathing) so learners can match the break to their state [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
4. **Match intensity to the next task.** Use moderate movement before routine work; use calming breaks before tasks requiring fine focus or new [Procedural Information](../elements/procedural-information.md).
5. **Optionally integrate content.** Use movement-based recall or vocabulary games so the break doubles as retrieval [Practice](../elements/practice.md).
6. **Re-enter with purpose.** Open the post-break segment with a brief [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) or framing statement to re-anchor attention.

## Examples
- **Instant Recess®** ([https://www.instantrecess.com](https://www.instantrecess.com)) — a 10-minute structured movement routine developed by Dr. Toni Yancey, designed for classrooms and workplaces.
- **GoNoodle** ([https://www.gonoodle.com](https://www.gonoodle.com)) — short movement and mindfulness videos widely used in elementary classrooms as transition breaks.
- **Classroom-based physical activity curricula** such as TAKE 10!® ([https://www.take10.net](https://www.take10.net)) — integrate 10-minute activity bursts tied to academic content.
- **Walking meetings** in professional and higher-education settings — replacing seated meetings with walks to sustain energy and divergent thinking.
