Integrate limited media lessons and multimedia projects into existing courses across the curriculum¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A practical alternative to dedicated media literacy courses: embed a small series of media lessons, such as video design instruction, into existing classes in any discipline and have students create multimedia projects related to course material. The article reports that incorporating limited media lessons into a variety of existing classes "can be a practical and effective alternative" for exposing non-media students to media literacy. Implementation models include trained media instructors traveling between classes or professional development for discipline instructors.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Trained media instructors deliver standardized lessons, or instructors across disciplines receive professional development to develop basic media skills they can teach in their classes

Constraints¶

  • Limited instruction may not be adequate when more sophisticated outcomes are the goal, and would likely not be sufficient for students majoring in a communication- or media-related field

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in non-media and communication majors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • foundational media literacy competencies including media access, analysis, and mediated message communication

Claims¶

  • Narrow media production lessons increase students' reported thinking about media literacy concepts in daily life [+W]
  • The study's central hypothesis that narrow mediated-message-communication instruction leads to broader media literacy competencies was supported, with stated limits [+W]
  • Narrow media production lessons improve overall demonstrated media literacy knowledge, but gains concentrate in messages-and-meanings concepts [+W]
  • Narrowly focused media production lessons increase university students' perceived media literacy competencies across all four dimensions [+W]
