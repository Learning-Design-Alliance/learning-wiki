# Use subvocal imagery and ideomotoric training, with novices rehearsing systematically and experts focusing on difficult steps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Subvocal training recalls "visual images ... by an internal self-talk"; ideomotoric training involves movement patterns "imagined several times, visualized and verbalized by a highly trained expert," with the subject visualizing from an inner perspective. The article advises novices to attend to every detail systematically (e.g., patient positioning, trocar insertion) while "more experienced surgeons can focus more on a specific difficult step," and both should include sensory, motivational, and complication-management elements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The trainee should include sensory aspects such as theatre atmosphere, tactile properties of instruments and tissues, and potential intraoperative complications and their management
#### Constraints
- Ideomotoric training depends on a highly trained expert who verbalizes and demonstrates the imagined movements

### Target Learners
- novice and experienced orthopedic surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining procedural readiness and technical fluency during non-performance periods

### Affordances
- Mental Practice Mechanism Theories
