# Spinning-top analogy to teach Earth's axial precession

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The teacher uses spinning tops to show that a rotating body's axis both spins and wobbles. The article explains that the top's angular momentum keeps the axis from simply falling, so 'el eje describe un movimiento circular de oscilación o bamboleo', and that this motion is called precession. The Earth's case is presented as analogous, with the Moon's differential gravitational pull on the equatorial bulge playing the role the top's weight plays, and students compare the two situations on the blackboard and with tops on their desks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Spinning tops for students to spin on desks or the floor
- Blackboard drawings comparing the top and the Earth (figure 1)
#### Constraints
- The analogy differs in direction of precession: the top precesses in the same sense it rotates, while Earth precesses in the opposite sense to its rotation, because the torques act in opposite directions

### Target Learners
- upper secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding precession of a rotating body's axis
- understanding the lunar torque on Earth's equatorial bulge
