Prioritize meaningfulness of reflection over accuracy of language use when assessing learner reflection¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

When reflection bears on assessment in language classrooms where students are developing proficiency, the authors recommend that "teachers may want to prioritize the meaningfulness of the reflection, instead of the accuracy of language use". This creates an environment where communicative abilities take precedence over correct language use in isolation. Assessment of reflection can also position it as a crucial teaching and learning activity rather than a peripheral task.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers place value on reflection throughout the period of its use
  • Students see reflection as directing them toward achieving course goals

Constraints¶

  • Assessing reflection is challenging when students are still developing proficiency

Target Learners¶

  • English language learners developing proficiency

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Communicative abilities
  • Academic growth through reflection

Claims¶

  • Reflection in language learning contexts is hindered by learners' difficulty expressing thoughts in the target language [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Define a specific scope for reflection in language classrooms rather than using open reflection
  • Provide scaffolding and a safe space for reflection to ensure depth despite large class sizes
