# Graphic Organizers (Math)

## Description
Graphic organizers break down math problems into steps, which helps students figure out what’s being asked in a word problem or which operation to use. Can also be used to learn new math concepts. There are many types of math-specific graphic organizers that work for students of all ages. Types include: the Frayer model, Step-by-step, Line it up, Graph it out, Break it down, Blank Pictographs, Blank Bar Graph, Version 2, Horizontal Bar Graph, Vertical Bar Graph, Double Venn Diagram, Version 1, Triple Venn Diagram, Tens and Ones Mat, Hundreds, Tens, and Ones Mat, 2-Group Sorting Mat, 3-Group Sorting Mat, Number Lines, Blank 100 Grid, 10 x 10 Grids, Tenths Grids, Hundredths Grids, 2 x 10 Graphing Grid, Coordinate Grids: Blank Printable Template, Graphing Grid (4 rows), 4-Digit Place-Value Charts, 9-Digit Place-Value Charts, 12-Digit Place-Value Charts, 26-Digit Place-Value Charts

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Graphic organizer templates, writing utensils or digital tools
#### Constraints
- May not be effective for all students or all types of math problems. Requires instructor guidance and support.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Students of all ages who need support with math, including students with learning disabilities

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Visualize and solve math problems, understand number relationships, identify similarities and differences, learn new concepts

### Affordances
- Provides a visual framework for organizing information, simplifies complex problems, supports working memory, can be adapted to different learner needs

### Personalization
- Can be adjusted depending on individual learner’s needs and the specific math problem or topic. Students can fill them in by hand or digitally. If a student struggles with writing, the instructor can write in the problem and let the student do the rest.
