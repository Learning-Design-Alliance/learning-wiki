# Have students practise on the osteotomy model before their first clinical treatment and before assisting oral surgeons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend that students operate on the 3D-printed model before their first real patient treatment, so they can discover and build surgical skills and confidence in a low-stakes setting. They argue, citing Kulasegaram et al., that allowing learners to experiment before instructor interaction can improve learning, and conclude: "we clearly recommend practicing on the model before the first clinical treatment." In future exercises students should be able to choose teeth, design the periodontal gap expression, or try different tooth positions according to skill level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the 3D-printed model with a real wisdom tooth, established in the dental school curriculum
#### Constraints
- Phantom exercise improves skills and confidence but cannot replace clinical experience

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students before first clinical surgery and before assisting oral surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical skill acquisition and self-confidence in wisdom-tooth osteotomy
