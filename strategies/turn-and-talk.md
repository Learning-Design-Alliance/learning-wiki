# Turn-and-Talk

## Description
Turn-and-talk asks learners to pair up for a brief, focused exchange — typically 30 seconds to three minutes — in which each partner articulates their understanding of a prompt, compares notes, and identifies gaps together. It converts passive listening time into [active-learning](../principles/active-learning.md) by requiring every learner to speak, not just a few volunteers.

## Design Implications

Turn-and-talk works because verbalizing forces retrieval and elaboration: learners must reconstruct ideas from memory and express them in their own words, which strengthens encoding [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. Peer discussion also exposes learners to alternative framings and misconceptions, providing immediate feedback without instructor mediation. The strategy is most effective when prompts are specific and when both partners are accountable for speaking — otherwise one partner dominates and the other rehearses silence.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A focused, specific prompt tied to what was just presented — open-ended prompts drift into unstructured chat
- Clear timing and turn-taking norms (who speaks first, how long, how pairs report out)
- Instructor circulation to listen in, surface misconceptions, and select pairs to share
- A follow-up mechanism (whole-class share, quick write, poll) that makes pair talk consequential

#### Constraints
- Effectiveness depends on learners' ability to articulate their understanding; novices may simply swap half-formed ideas and reinforce errors [-M]
- Unequal participation is the default failure mode — without assigned roles or accountability, one partner talks and the other nods [-M]
- Too-frequent or too-long use erodes attention and cuts into instructor explanation time [~W]
- In large or noisy rooms, pairs can be hard to monitor, and off-task talk goes uncorrected [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Think-Pair-Share**: silent thinking time before talk, improving the quality of first utterances
- **Predict-then-confirm**: pairs predict an outcome before the instructor reveals it, adding a commitment device
- **Elbow-partner annotation**: pairs talk over a shared text or problem, combining talk with [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md)
- **Structured roles**: one partner summarizes, the other checks against notes, then they swap

### Target Learners
- All levels; particularly effective for adult learners, who benefit from connecting new content to experience [~M]
- Reluctant speakers who will not address a whole class but will talk to one peer
- Less effective for learners with very low prior knowledge, who lack material to retrieve and may need [Explicit Teaching](../patterns/explicit-teaching.md) first [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Consolidation and [retrieval](../principles/activation.md) of recently presented content
- Disciplinary literacy: practicing the vocabulary and discourse patterns of the field
- Metacognitive monitoring — discovering what one cannot yet explain [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Pose a specific prompt immediately after a chunk of instruction ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) keeps the talk anchored to a manageable amount of content).
2. Give 15–30 seconds of silent think time.
3. Direct pairs to take turns speaking, with each partner accountable for a full response ([Articulation](../elements/articulation.md)).
4. Circulate and listen; note strong responses and misconceptions ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md)).
5. Debrief by having selected pairs report out, then correct or extend as needed ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).

## Examples
- **K-12 literacy blocks** (e.g., Lucy Calkins' Units of Study) — students turn to a partner to rehearse a reading strategy before independent practice.
- **Peer Instruction (Eric Mazur, Harvard Physics)** — students discuss answers to concept questions with neighbors after individual voting; discussion dramatically improves re-vote accuracy ([Peer Instruction](https://www.per-central.org/)) [~S].
- **Adult professional development** — workshop participants turn to a colleague to apply a framework to their own context, strengthening transfer through personalization.
