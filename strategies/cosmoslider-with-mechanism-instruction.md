# Pair CosmoSlider exploration with explicit instruction on the physical mechanisms underlying CMB anisotropies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that interactive slider exploration be combined with teaching of the physics behind the spectra, arguing that "Simply memorizing the qualitative behavior of the spectra is, however, insufficient for a solid fundamental understanding of the anisotropies." Students should vary parameters hands-on while the instructor explains the mechanisms that give rise to the observed changes, since a combination of physical-process understanding and parameter-varying study is described as often the most effective way to build intuition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the tool plus concurrent instruction on recombination-era physical processes
#### Constraints
- Memorizing qualitative spectral behavior alone is judged insufficient by the authors

### Target Learners
- undergraduate astrophysics students

### Target Learning Goals
- building intuition for CMB anisotropies and parameter dependence of power spectra
