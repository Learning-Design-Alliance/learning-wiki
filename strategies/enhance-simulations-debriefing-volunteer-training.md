# Enhance poverty simulations with structured debriefings, trained volunteers, and psychological safety guidelines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article reports implementation enhancements drawn from the literature and its own practice. It notes "Researchers at the University of Alabama at Birmingham recommended implementing several best practices in simulation, including training volunteers, pre‐briefing students in small groups and including psychological safety guidelines, and structured debriefings with trained volunteers." The authors also added a half-hour small-group discussion period facilitated by faculty or staff members and an on-site mental health counselor.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Volunteers should be trained before facilitating the simulation.
- Debriefings should be structured and led by trained facilitators.
- Psychological safety guidelines should be included in pre-briefing.
#### Constraints
- The article notes some volunteers and students who have experienced poverty find the simulation upsetting or triggering, and the authors lacked budget to compensate low-income volunteer staffers.

### Target Learners
- predoctoral dental students
- health sciences students

### Target Learning Goals
- empathy with patients facing socioeconomic barriers to care
- understanding of systemic challenges facing families in poverty
