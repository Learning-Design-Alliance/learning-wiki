# Organize students into collaborative clusters where peers copy better-performing partners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A strategy derived from the article's cluster simulations: organize students into collaborative clusters in which peers adopt the practices of better-performing partners, mirroring ABM1's imitation of the cluster's best performer and ABM2's copying of a peer who did better in the shared aspect. The simulations predict that "final knowledge is always greater when individuals form clusters" than when students work individually, with low-achieving students gaining most.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Copying must transmit performance information; in the simulations, fully random copying produced no cluster advantage.
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is simulation-based (agent-based models) rather than a controlled experiment.
- The article reports improvement percentages differ across achievement groups and change with course size and group proportions.

### Target Learners
- university students in large theoretical-practical courses
- low-achieving students

### Target Learning Goals
- improving final achievement in physics courses through structured peer interaction
