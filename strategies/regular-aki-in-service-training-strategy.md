# Incorporate regular AKI lectures and seminars into continuing medical education for all cadres of doctors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educational programs such as lectures and seminars on AKI be incorporated regularly into continuing medical education for all doctors, because knowledge deficiencies spanned specialty and experience levels. It states "Educational programs such as lectures and seminars on AKI should be incorporated regularly into the continuing medical education for all cadres of doctors", and cites reports that focused programs improved AKI management and patient outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should emphasise the identified gap areas: mode of presentation, risk factors, potentially nephrotoxic medications, AKI definition and staging criteria, newer biomarkers and indications for renal replacement therapy
#### Constraints
- The authors note the study's small sample size means findings cannot be generalised

### Target Learners
- non-nephrology doctors (medical and surgical specialties) in government hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- prevention, early diagnosis, staging and management of acute kidney injury, including nephrotoxic medication safety and renal replacement therapy referral
