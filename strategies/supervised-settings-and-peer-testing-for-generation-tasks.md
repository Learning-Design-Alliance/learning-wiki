# Integrate generative-AI-vulnerable take-home tasks into supervised or in-class settings and use student-generated MCQs in peer-testing or formative assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because generative AI can produce notes and MCQs outside class, the authors propose moving these generation tasks into supervised contexts and repurposing student-generated MCQs for accountability and pedagogical value. They suggest: "use student-generated MCQs not only as a learning task but also as part of peer-testing or formative assessments, ensuring accountability, authenticity and pedagogical value." They also flag critical evaluation of AI outputs as an emerging core competency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators must critically appraise how generative AI may alter engagement with take-home learning tasks
#### Constraints
- AI-detection software is imperfect and should not replace thoughtful task design; validity of AI-generated MCQ content remains under-researched and prompt-dependent

### Target Learners
- health-professions students completing post-class learning assignments

### Target Learning Goals
- authentic engagement with generative learning tasks such as note-taking and MCQ creation
