# Employ varied gonioscopy teaching formats in residency, including small-group practice-guided meetings with dry and wet labs, case presentations, hands-on clinic time, and live-video tutoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
To remedy perceived residency shortcomings, the authors recommend employing multiple teaching formats: "small-group practice-guided meetings with dry and wet labs, case presentations, more time for hands-on training in the glaucoma clinic, and tutoring with live video at the clinic or operating theaters". They also call for emphasizing correct patient screening in continuing medical education and for conference organizers to offer gonioscopy workshops, citing the fully booked 2023 World Glaucoma Conference workshop as an example.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires curriculum revision with focus on enhancing basic knowledge and clinical skills, and dedication of sufficient examination time in clinic
#### Constraints
- The article notes some methods were suggested before and that it will be interesting to analyze the benefit of each incorporated format; the benefit of individual formats has not been established

### Target Learners
- ophthalmology residents
- ophthalmologists in training
- practicing ophthalmologists

### Target Learning Goals
- gonioscopy technique and interpretation of anterior chamber angle findings
- correct sequencing of the ocular examination for glaucoma
- glaucoma diagnosis and angle-closure risk screening
