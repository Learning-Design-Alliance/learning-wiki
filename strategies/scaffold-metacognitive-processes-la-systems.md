# Learning analytics systems should capture and scaffold metacognitive processes in secondary students' online writing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that secondary students' essay-writing strategies showed limited planning, monitoring, and evaluation, the authors recommend that analytics tools move beyond activity counts. They write that "the results highlight the need for LA systems to better capture and scaffold metacognitive processes—such as planning, monitoring, and evaluation—that were found to be limited in secondary school students' strategies". Such systems would provide strategy-level insights to support students and teachers, and the conclusion adds that scaffolds should strengthen metacognitive regulation rather than simply supporting task completion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trace-data capture of learning actions mappable to SRL processes, so strategy-level rather than activity-count insights can be provided
#### Constraints
- The authors frame this as a need arising from one short two-session study; they call for future research on how analytics-informed scaffolds support productive self-regulated learning

### Target Learners
- secondary school students writing from multiple sources online

### Target Learning Goals
- strengthening planning, monitoring, and evaluation during writing tasks
