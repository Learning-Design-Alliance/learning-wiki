# Have players compare expected with software-computed outcomes to reflect on and retrace quantum-operation misunderstandings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends using the gap between prediction and evaluation as a learning mechanism: "Comparing the expected outcome to the mathematically correct outcome, provided by the software after a card is played, allows one to reflect on misunderstandings and retrace the effects of each operation." Instructors can use this comparison during classroom play so that students revisit their reasoning about gate sequences, superposition probabilities, and interference whenever their predicted winning state differs from the software's result.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The evaluation software must be available so the mathematically correct outcome is shown after each played card
#### Constraints
- The strategy presumes players commit to making explicit predictions before evaluation; no formal study of this specific mechanism is reported

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students in quantum information and computation courses
- outreach audiences

### Target Learning Goals
- retracing the effects of quantum operations
- correcting misconceptions about superposition, interference and entanglement
