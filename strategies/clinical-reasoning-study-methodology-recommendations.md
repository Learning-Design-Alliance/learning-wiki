# Methodological recommendations for future clinical reasoning education studies: report effect sizes with confidence intervals, control confounders, use ITT analysis, and determine MCID

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that future researchers "include confidence intervals for effect sizes to clarify the precision of their findings alongside p-values", rigorously identify and control confounders (e.g., via multivariable regression or stratification), report adherence and compliance, employ per-protocol and intention-to-treat analyses, use CONSORT standards, perform a priori sample size calculations, and determine the minimal clinically/practically important difference (MCID) as a basis for significance levels and sample size calculation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Future studies should prospectively estimate sample size and power targeting the smallest educationally important difference
#### Constraints
- The review notes RCTs and meta-analysis have notable limitations in medical education, and a washout period is impossible for learning interventions in crossover designs

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning skill
- diagnostic accuracy
