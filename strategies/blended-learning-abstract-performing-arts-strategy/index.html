Adopt blended learning to teach abstract performing arts such as Oriental Music in secondary education where lesson time and one-to-one coaching are constrained¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend blended learning as the instructional method for teaching abstract, performance-based subjects at secondary level. They argue music "requires one to one coaching" and that improvisation, extempore performance and music creation are competencies students cannot achieve individually, while lesson time is limited; the blended course with Moodle discussions, supplementary audio-video materials and group activities addresses these constraints. The experimental results are offered as the evidence base for the recommendation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An LMS with discussion forums for real-time clarification, teacher-prepared supplementary audio and video materials, and face-to-face sessions retaining demonstration and group activities

Constraints¶

  • Generalization is limited: the study focused on Oriental Music at secondary level, and the digital divide and urban-rural disparities of technological infrastructure are stated limitations requiring validation

Target Learners¶

  • Senior secondary students in technology-equipped government schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Development of practical and theoretical competencies in abstract performing arts subjects

Affordances¶

  • Mixed Objectivist Constructivist Blended Design Model

Claims¶

  • Both groups improved significantly from pre-test to post-test, but the blended learning group's average gain (16.11) was roughly twice the control group's gain (9.62) [+W]
  • Sri Lankan senior secondary students taught Oriental Music under a blended learning approach scored significantly higher on Ordinary Level competency achievement tests than students taught by traditional methods [+W]
  • Secondary students taught in an e-classroom with ICT resources significantly outperform students taught by conventional methods on post-test achievement [+M]
