# Handle incorrect completions by giving full credit for correct or as-good-as-correct answers and part credit for answers that give sense but are not strictly correct

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article suggests two methods of handling accuracy in scoring: give no credit for what is not correct, or give full credit for correct or as-good-as-correct answers and "part credit for what gives sense but is not strictly correct." The authors believe the second is fairer, especially where papers contain many errors, since part-credit proportion can be assigned by individual judgment of accuracy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Credit in neither case is given for elisions left unfilled or filled so as to have no connection with the rest of the thought.
#### Constraints
- For the study's own papers there were so few errors that the arbitrary-deduction method was adopted; the authors note that with some papers results would be very different and the second method should then be selected.

### Target Learners
- students taking completion tests

### Target Learning Goals
- fair scoring of partially correct completion-test responses
