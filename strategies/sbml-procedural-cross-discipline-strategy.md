# Implement SBML as a teaching modality for procedural-based training and cross-discipline education initiatives

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation is that "other institutions" leverage simulation-based mastery learning "as a teaching modality for procedural-based training" and advocate cross-discipline education initiatives such as pairing emergency medicine with ophthalmology. In this study the interdisciplinary pairing leveraged geographic proximity and consultant relationships between two hospitals 0.2 miles apart, letting content and education experts from both institutions build and review the curriculum. The authors also suggest dedicated teaching days for larger participant recruitment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to expert instructors from the partner specialty for demonstration and feedback
- Internal or grant support for financial incentives and standardized patient volunteers
#### Constraints
- Replication may be difficult at community sites without a close relationship with ophthalmology; scheduling in-person evaluations was one of the most significant challenges

### Target Learners
- Attending emergency physicians and other practicing clinicians learning specialty-focused procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural competency in a device-assisted examination and downstream teaching of junior learners
