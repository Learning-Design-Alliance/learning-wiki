# Pinwheel Discussion

## Description
Learners are divided into 4 groups. Three of these groups are assigned to represent specific points of view. Members of the fourth group are designated as “provocateurs,” tasked with making sure the discussion keeps going and stays challenging. One person from each group (the “speaker”) sits in a desk facing speakers from the other groups, so they form a square in the center of the room. Behind each speaker, the remaining group members are seated: two right behind the speaker, then three behind them, and so on, forming a kind of triangle. From above, this would look like a pinwheel. The four speakers introduce and discuss questions they prepared ahead of time (this preparation is done with their groups). After some time passes, new learners rotate from the seats behind the speaker into the center seats and continue the conversation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a physical space that can be arranged in a pinwheel formation, preparation of discussion questions, and clear instructions for learners.  Pre-reading materials may be necessary for discussions based on texts.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging in small or crowded classrooms. Requires careful facilitation to ensure all learners are engaged and on task. Can be time-consuming to prepare and implement.
