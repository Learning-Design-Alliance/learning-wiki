# Design assessment and manage workload so that deep learning is explicitly rewarded in PBL environments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the review finds that assessment perceived as not rewarding deep learning pushes students toward surface approaches, it recommends taking the role of assessment into account in studies and design of learning environments. Assessment formats perceived as measuring higher levels of cognitive processing, such as assignment essays, are associated with deeper approaches than multiple-choice formats. Workload should also be managed, since high perceived workload is more likely to result in surface approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment procedures must explicitly reward understanding, and perceived workload must be kept manageable, particularly for beginning students.
#### Constraints
- The review notes that in some situations a surface approach, or a combination of deep and surface approaches, may best support effective learning.

### Target Learners
- higher education students in PBL curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- deep approach to learning
- reduction of surface studying strategies
