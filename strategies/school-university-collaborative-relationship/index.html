Foster a school-university collaborative relationship to support identification, assessment access, and communication¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors attribute the impact of the pilot reading intervention to their "school-university collaborative relationship, which proved essential in terms of identifying students in need of reading assistance, access to pertinent assessment data, providing an environment conducive to tutoring sessions, and enabling excellent communication among tutors, parents, and the first grade teachers". This collaboration was central to building the school's capacity to support the literacy development of at-risk readers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A partnership between a university teacher-education program and an elementary school, including access to assessment data and communication channels among tutors, parents, and teachers

Constraints¶

  • The collaboration was developed in the context of a specific Fine Arts magnet school with a large Independent School District

Target Learners¶

  • At-risk first grade readers in a public elementary school setting

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Building school capacity to support literacy development of at-risk readers

Claims¶

  • An achievement gap remained between tutored at-risk students and typically developing peers despite significant tutoring gains [+W]
  • At-risk tutored students gained nearly 20 NCE points while non-tutored at-risk peers declined from fall to spring [+W]
  • At-risk first graders who received 47 hours of one-on-one tutoring read significantly more proficiently than matched at-risk non-tutored peers [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide a sufficient dose of 44-80 hours of intensive instruction as a general target for at-risk reading interventions
  • Three-step tutor training model: initial training, daily debriefing, and individual consultation
  • Employ a comprehensive local assessment model instead of relying solely on standardized test scores
