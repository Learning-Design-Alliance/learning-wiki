# Use week-to-week written reflection rubrics and major-tailored context-rich problems to refine attitudinal categorization

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
As future refinement, the article proposes more thorough qualitative analysis of students' metacognitive rubric responses, analysis of week-to-week rubric data throughout the semester (currently underway), examination of lab group working dynamics suggested by a common student focus on the group-collaboration process, and testing whether certain majors fare better attitudinally with individual context-rich problems more pertinent to their respective majors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Week-to-week written artifacts, namely the metacognitive reflection rubrics used by students
- qualitative analysis of rubric responses
#### Constraints
- Presented as preliminary and currently underway rather than completed

### Target Learners
- introductory algebra-based physics students in lab group settings

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding and improving student attitudes toward problem solving
