Do not assume teaching methods validated with one SES group will transfer to a different SES group¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes that accumulating findings of qualitative SES differences in psychological processing carry a direct implication for education policy: "we should not assume that teaching methods that have been validated with one SES group will necessarily be effective for a different group." Where academic domains show SES-related neural differences in how material is processed, instructional validation should be checked per population rather than generalized. The recommendation rests on the arithmetic-processing moderation finding and is framed as a future implication of such findings accumulating.

  • Evidence of SES-related qualitative differences in processing within the academic domain of interest

Target Learners¶

  • children from lower and higher socioeconomic status backgrounds

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic skills such as mathematics

Claims¶

  • ERPs reveal SES disparities in children's auditory attention filtering even when behavioral task performance shows no significant SES difference [+W]
  • Family income and parental education show regionally specific positive relationships with children's cortical surface area, strongest at the lowest SES levels [+W]
  • Lower SES adolescents show greater stress-related increase in serotonin transporter gene methylation, associated with greater amygdala reactivity and depression symptoms [+W]
  • SES moderates the brain-behavior relationship in children's arithmetic, with higher and lower SES children engaging different neurocognitive systems [+W]
  • Parenting quality and life stress fully mediate the relationship between SES and children's hippocampal volume [+W]
