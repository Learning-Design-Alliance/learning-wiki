# Set students up for affectively positive experiences with conceptual reasoning rather than only confronting problematic epistemological beliefs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (3 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, for students like Judy, fostering epistemological change may depend more on engineering positive affective experiences with conceptual reasoning than on directly targeting beliefs. The authors write that bringing about epistemological change "might have more to do with setting her up to have affectively positive experiences, and less to do with eliciting, confronting, and replacing her problematic epistemological 'beliefs.'" They do not reject directly addressing the real/ideal gulf by showing how idealizations approximate the real world, but judge it insufficient on its own for such students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must create opportunities for learners to succeed at and feel good about conceptual reasoning, so that positive affect can destabilize the stance sustained by annoyance.
- Instructional designs must create opportunities for learners to succeed at and feel good about the kind of conceptual reasoning they find annoying
#### Constraints
- The authors state the analysis is ultimately limited to one student in one interview, so the strategy's reach beyond students like Judy is not established.
- The authors state this is limited to one student in one interview and that the direct real/ideal-gulf strategy is not wrong, just likely not enough

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students with negative affect toward conceptual problems

### Target Learning Goals
- willingness to engage in qualitative, conceptual reasoning about circuits
- epistemological stance that conceptual reasoning is useful for real circuits
