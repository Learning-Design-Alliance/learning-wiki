# Integrate prompt engineering into medical curricula through hands-on workshops, simulation-based learning, and AI courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends teaching prompt engineering as a foundational AI competency for medical students, because effective prompting was required to make ChatGPT perform adequately as a standardized patient. It suggests incorporation "through, for example, hands-on workshops, simulation-based learning, and courses on AI in health care," and argues such competencies should be embedded in existing core courses rather than electives to ensure equitable learning opportunities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty capacity to develop and refine prompts iteratively
- Curricular space for AI competencies including ethical considerations and algorithmic fairness
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is supported in this study by a single-institution qualitative study of 9 students; the article notes resource allocation and faculty training add complexity to curricula integration

### Target Learners
- medical students
- health professions trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- prompt engineering skill
- effective use of LLMs for clinical skills practice
