Strategies

Develop writing rating scales that score cohesion and coherence together, or via the textual metafunction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends developing "More modern Writing scales", in which the cohesion and coherence are considered side by side, under the title of 'Discourse Analysis' (DA). It argues that since discourse is created when both cohesion and coherence work together, it is necessary to develop rating scales considering both together, and that because the textual metafunction is directly related to creating cohesion and coherence, a scale could consider the textual metafunction instead. It also states analytical writing scales let teachers provide much better feedback.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Raters need an analytic scale covering all aspects of writing, such as the Hungarian scale's four components scored 0-7

Constraints¶

  • The article notes there are fewer rating scales in which both cohesion and coherence have been considered together

Target Learners¶

  • adult intermediate EFL learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • assessment of coherence and cohesion in writing

Claims¶

  • The Hungarian analytic scoring scale showed high reliability (Cronbach's Alpha 0.83) in this study [+W]
  • Teaching the textual metafunction significantly improved Iranian EFL learners' essay coherence and cohesion ratings compared with essay-writing instruction alone [+W]
  • EFL students trained to use a rubric as an instructional tool outperformed control students on compare-and-contrast essay writing [+W]
