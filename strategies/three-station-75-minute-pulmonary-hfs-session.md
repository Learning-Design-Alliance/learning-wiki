# A 75-minute three-station high-fidelity simulation session progressing through phases of patient care

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The session placed students in three sequential 25-minute stations simulating phases of patient care: a hallway trauma scene, an emergency room trauma bay, and an intensive care unit, each using a full-body manikin. As the authors describe it, they "created a novel 75 min session with multiple case scenario simulations that included three separate 25 min stations each simulating different phases of patient care which highlighted basic pulmonary concepts and physiology." Students progressed from basic pulmonary examination to interpreting hemodynamic monitoring and treating the simulated patient.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Three faculty instructors, one per station, plus an instructor facilitator driving case progression and a simulator operator behind two-way glass
- High-fidelity manikins preprogrammed with the target physiological scenarios
#### Constraints
- Each session was limited to eight medical students, so scaling to a full class requires multiple sessions

### Target Learners
- First-year preclinical medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic pulmonary physiology concepts including oxygenation, the alveolar gas equation, V/Q mismatch, lung volumes, ventilation, dead space, and West's zones of the lung
