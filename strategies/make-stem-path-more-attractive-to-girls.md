# Make the STEM pathway more attractive to girls to increase recruitment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because girls graduating school are as likely as boys to have earned grades high enough to pursue STEM, the article recommends that the STEM path itself be changed rather than ability: "To increase recruitment of girls into STEM, this path should be made more attractive for them." The strategy targets the perceived competitiveness and gendered threats of STEM pathways, since the article identifies stereotype threat and backlash effects, not achievement gaps, as deterrents.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Address the internal and external threats (stereotype threat, backlash) girls perceive in STEM paths
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a recommendation following its achievement findings; it does not test an intervention

### Target Learners
- school students, particularly girls approaching career decisions

### Target Learning Goals
- increasing girls' recruitment into STEM careers and reducing occupational segregation
