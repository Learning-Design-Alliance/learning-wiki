Pair demand-side nudge messaging with supply-side teacher professional development and attend to heterogeneous effects¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the trial results, the authors recommend that low-cost, message-based nudge programs be treated as complements rather than stand-alone solutions. They report that "without additional support to parents and teachers, their effects are likely to be limited", that SMS "messages may be a good complement to programs focused more on content and in-person support", and that "demand-side programs such as this one likely need to be complemented by supply-side programs (i.e., teacher professional development)". They also caution that "attention must be paid to heterogenous effects for different groups of vulnerable children" and that future program iterations must consider parents' views on child labor.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • an operational SMS delivery pipeline and school/parent enrollment, as in this trial

Constraints¶

  • effects likely limited without additional support; the study was underpowered and limited to two regions of Cote d'Ivoire

Target Learners¶

  • children in rural, low-resource primary schools in sub-Saharan Africa, reached through their parents and teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving children's literacy and numeracy learning while monitoring equity across vulnerable subgroups

Claims¶

  • Parents-only SMS/audio nudges produced statistically non-significant learning gains of d = 0.081 in rural Cote d'Ivoire [+W]
  • The nudge intervention had no statistically significant effects on parenting mechanisms of motivation and engagement [+W]
  • Parents-only nudges marginally significantly increased child-reported child labor (d = 0.113), contrary to intervention goals [+W]
  • Parents-only nudge effects on learning were larger and positive for children with below-median baseline skills [+W]
  • Teacher-only and combined parent-teacher nudges showed no impacts on learning, with small negative point estimates [+W]
  • Teacher-targeted nudges showed significant negative interactions with child sex, with negative learning impacts for girls [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Pair personalized messages with an effort to call attention to them
