Promote self-regulation competence through high-quality teaching and whole-school approaches¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2–q4 · 3 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that promoting self-regulation competence become a guiding principle of education systems, orchestrated through high-quality teaching and whole-school approaches. High-quality teaching is characterized by "effective classroom manage - ment, cognitive activation, and student support", and these elements are combined with school-level coordination rather than isolated strategy training. The strategy spans behavioral, cognitive, and affective processes and synthesizes insights from multiple research traditions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A systematic, multicomponent approach spanning behavioral, cognitive, and affective processes
  • Coordination across classrooms via whole-school approaches rather than isolated interventions

Constraints¶

  • The authors acknowledge that implementing promotion of self-regulation competence sustainably in real-world school systems faces non-trivial obstacles and that parts of the argument are speculative
