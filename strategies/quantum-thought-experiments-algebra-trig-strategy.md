# Teach quantum concepts through thought experiments on Stern-Gerlach analyzer loops and two-slit experiments using only algebra and trigonometry

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes using thought experiments—the Stern-Gerlach analyzer loop, the two-slit experiment, and the Mach-Zehnder interferometer—to build conceptual foundations without advanced mathematics. "Calculus is not needed to analyze these systems; algebra and trigonometry suffice." Feynman's arrow method for the two-slit experiment and Styer's high-school-level algebra treatment are named as accessible analyses, with Dirac notation optional for training teachers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have analyzed a Mach-Zehnder interferometer before discussing the beam splitter separation-fallacy example
#### Constraints
- The concepts are counterintuitive and hard and take time to sink in; the program cannot be done in a few-day bootcamp

### Target Learners
- Teachers and nonscientists with an algebra and trigonometry background

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding superposition, indeterminacy, complementarity, tagging, and measurement through concrete experiments
