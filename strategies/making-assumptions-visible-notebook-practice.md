# Making assumptions visible by reserving a dedicated notebook area during investigations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because students' prior courses give assumptions pre-made or implicit, the course makes assumption-making a central practice. The article states: 'at the beginning and during every investigation, the students are invited to divide their notes in two different areas, one of which is reserved for the list of assumptions they are making through their investigation'. Students discuss assumptions' plausibility, test them at the first possible point, and learn that research results are contingent on assumptions. Two assumption types are distinguished: underlying assumptions covering known ignorance, and simplifying assumptions verified a posteriori.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Notebook practice with a dedicated assumptions area, facilitation inviting plausibility discussion, and trial-and-error verification of assumptions during activities
#### Constraints
- Trial and error strategies are time consuming, though the article calls them the most effective for learning to make assumptions

### Target Learners
- University MSc physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Making and testing assumptions
- understanding the contingent nature of scientific results
