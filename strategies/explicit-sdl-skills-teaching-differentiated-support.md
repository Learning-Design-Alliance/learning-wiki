# Teach SDL skills explicitly and differentiate support by student group in bioscience courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends treating SDL as "a set of learnable competencies rather than an innate trait" and teaching SDL skills explicitly early in the nursing programme, using strategies such as spaced repetition, retrieval practice, and concept mapping contextualized to bioscience learning. Support should be differentiated: orientation and academic-literacies workshops for mature students, and structured transition support with scaffolded early assignments for younger students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators and institutions must recognize and address intrinsic factors when designing learning environments and support strategies
#### Constraints
- Recommendations arise from an exploratory single-site qualitative study with unequal age groups and descriptive counts only

### Target Learners
- First-year nursing students, both younger (19-25) and mature (26+)

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective self-directed learning in anatomy, physiology, and biochemistry
