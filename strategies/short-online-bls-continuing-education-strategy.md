# Use short online BLS courses for continuing education of health professionals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend short online basic life support courses as a resource for training students and for the continuing education of professionals, because resuscitation guidelines suggest online courses and indicate that "short periods of study and regularity seem to positively influence learning with no significant correlation with training time". The article's own 20-hour online course produced significant theoretical gains and accurate simulated BLS performance, supporting the strategy's feasibility. The authors also note that frequent use of short videos increases learning and self-efficacy during cardiac arrest care.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An online course requires careful planning and organization with defined educational objectives, relevant content, and distribution of the workload
- Access to a virtual learning platform and, ideally, feedback devices or simulators for practical skill verification
#### Constraints
- The authors call for future research with larger samples to assess prospects beyond immediate learning
- Skill retention over time tends to decrease even after satisfactory theoretical learning, per the article's discussion

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students
- Health professionals in continuing education

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic life support knowledge and skills
- CPR performance quality and self-efficacy in cardiac arrest care
