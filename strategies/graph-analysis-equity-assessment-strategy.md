# Use graph theory talk-turn analysis to inform equitable classrooms and give students feedback on group contribution

## Description
The article recommends several applications of its methodology: observing how demographic combinations affect participation to inform how instructors create more equitable classrooms; examining effects of class structure such as seating and instructor talk; tracking participation across a term to see how groups coalesce; and, for assessment, providing "a means for instructors to quantify contribution by individual members in a group and provide feedback to students."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Talk-turn data collected at the relevant time points in the course
#### Constraints
- The study had a limited number of groups, so the authors were not able to make generalizable conclusions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students working in small groups with peer facilitators

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable participation in group discussions; learning to collaborate
