# Map a curriculum onto evidence from the science of learning to justify and adapt pedagogy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article models and recommends a mapping exercise: systematically aligning a curriculum's design features with established cognitive principles so that practitioners gain "a stronger position to justify what and how they teach, and to make decisions about how to adapt their teaching". The authors argue understanding which components of an effective approach are effective facilitates wider adoption and adaptation, and they hope to inspire others to map other curricula similarly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of the target curriculum's design and sequence, plus an evidence base in the science of learning to map onto
#### Constraints
- The article notes that adapting the Montessori maths curriculum to non-Montessori settings does not give as good results as high-fidelity classic implementation, though better understanding of critical components might enable greater success

### Target Learners
- Educators, curriculum designers, and researchers across early-years and other domains

### Target Learning Goals
- Designing and justifying curricula that support abstraction and durable learning
