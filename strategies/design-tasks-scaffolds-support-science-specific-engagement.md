# Design tasks and scaffolds that support science-specific engagement to improve the validity of task-based diagnoses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend using insights from the TSMS model to inform the design of tasks or scaffolds that increase the likelihood that students engage in science-specific reasoning when encountering a task. They state that 'Such insights may also inform the design of tasks or scaffolds that support science-specific engagement' and that this 'may help improve the validity of task-based diagnoses by increasing the likelihood of science-specific engagement with these tasks'. The strategy targets the predecisional phase, before students commit to an approach.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Understanding of how contextual cues, initial task typification, and metacognitive experiences interact in early task processing
#### Constraints
- The article notes this would certainly not guarantee successful task solving

### Target Learners
- Science students, particularly in physics

### Target Learning Goals
- Disciplinary analytic reasoning
- Valid competence diagnosis via tasks
