# Talking to Young Children About Race and Racism

## Description
This strategy involves parents and caregivers initiating conversations with young children about race, racism, and anti-racism. It emphasizes the importance of being honest, specific, and trustworthy in these discussions to raise the next generation to confront racial injustice. It utilizes resources such as watch and play guides, discussion guides, and commitment activities to facilitate meaningful conversations. It includes using media, stories, and books to talk about race and racism.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Watch and play guides, discussion guides, commitment activities, media resources, books, and a safe and supportive environment.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging for parents and caregivers who are uncomfortable or lack experience in discussing race and racism. Requires ongoing commitment to anti-racist education and advocacy.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Young children (3 months to 8 years), parents, caregivers, and educators

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Introduce children to the many cultures and people of our world, address racial identity and sense of belonging, develop positive feelings about themselves, effectively manage and heal when treated unfairly, and promote anti-racist behavior.

### Affordances
- Addresses race and racism in an age-appropriate way, promotes understanding and empathy, encourages positive racial identity, fosters a sense of belonging, and equips children with the tools to stand up for themselves and others.

### Personalization
- Resources can be adapted based on the child's age, understanding, and cultural background. Parents and caregivers can select resources that align with their values and beliefs.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the level of engagement in conversations, analyze children's questions and comments, track changes in attitudes and behaviors, and gather feedback from parents and caregivers.

## Impact
- Early conversations about race and racism can reduce prejudice, increase empathy, and promote social justice. Children who are taught to understand and confront racial injustice are more likely to become anti-racist advocates and allies.
