# Teach normal forces by starting from everyday experience and guiding students toward two realizations: deformability and the action-reaction principle

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
An instructional sequence for introductory physics that is "intentionally informal and conversational in tone to teach students how to build an intuition to complement mathematical formalism." It begins with common, everyday experience (objects on floors), poses apparent puzzles (the floor that 'knows' what force to apply), then guides students toward two realizations: that real objects are deformable even when deformations are not easily visible, and that the friction–normal relationship follows from the action-reaction principle. Only after this intuition is built are the traditional static and kinetic formulae analyzed.
