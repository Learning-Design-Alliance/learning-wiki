# Advocate against employing physics GRE cut-off scores in admissions because they additionally exclude applicants from smaller, less resourced institutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation, grounded in their modelling of five universities' admissions records: because institutional effects on cut-off attainment are stable across many cut-off scores, employing a cut-off limits access not only along racial and ethnic lines but also by institutional background. They write that "the results are stable across many cut-oﬀ scores" and on that basis "we advocate against the practice of employing physics GRE cut-oﬀ scores in admissions."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Admissions decisions currently relying on a minimum P-GRE score, formal or informal, as the filtering mechanism the article analyses.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on modelling of 2017/2018 admissions records from four public Big Ten universities and one private Midwestern university, a data set the authors describe as suffering from a selection bias effect favoring applicants with higher grades and GRE scores.

### Target Learners
- Prospective physics PhD program applicants

### Target Learning Goals
- Equitable graduate admissions practices in physics
