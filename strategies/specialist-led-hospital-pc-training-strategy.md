# Deliver short specialist-led basic palliative care training to hospital ward staff, based on Kirkpatrick's and Moore's evaluation models

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable recipe: a 4-hour training programme, run in two editions to facilitate participation, "taught by II-level PC specialists to HPs of all departments (I-level)". Content focused on the WHO vision of palliative care, the purpose of a palliative care unit in the hospital and sharing of PC needs across wards. Evaluation followed the first three steps of Kirkpatrick's and Moore's models with pre/post questionnaires and focus groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Availability of II-level specialist palliative care staff as trainers
- Pre- and post-training data collection (questionnaires and focus groups) to evaluate impact
#### Constraints
- The study measured impact only on professional competencies (Moore's third order of learning), not effectiveness on patients and health outcomes

### Target Learners
- Hospital healthcare professionals of all professional categories in non-specialist wards

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge of palliative care goals and characteristics, the hospital PCU's organization, and when and how to activate specialist palliative care

### Affordances
- Two Level Palliative Care Competency Model
