# Explicitly normalize the discomfort of not understanding, and coach participants to ask questions early and often

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A concrete program practice for supporting novice learners doing authentic research: facilitators explicitly and repeatedly discuss that it is legitimate to feel stupid and legitimate to not like it, share Schwarz (2008) on the importance of stupidity in scientific research, and remind participants they will not understand everything and it is ok to ask questions again and again. The number one piece of advice returning participants give each new class every year is to ASK QUESTIONS, early and often. Telecons are run as a "safe space" for questions feared to be dumb.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular group telecons at least every week or two, which the article says are essential to keep teams functional
#### Constraints
- Some educators still find the experience overwhelming; the article reports fewer, not zero, teachers disengage since the discussion was implemented

### Target Learners
- teachers without prior research experience working on authentic science projects

### Target Learning Goals
- persisting through productive discomfort
- developing question-asking skills
