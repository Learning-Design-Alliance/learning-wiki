# Integrate open-source AI-supported, self-paced tracing tools with immediate feedback into undergraduate orthodontic curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating AI-supported teaching tools like MCAT into orthodontic curricula: students train independently on their own computers at their own pace, trace landmarks on digital cephalograms, and receive both visual and quantitative feedback comparing their points to a model's predictions. The authors state that "integrating tools like MCAT may help to ensure that dental students are well-prepared for modern clinical practice", and 94.2% of participants favored long-term implementation. The strategy targets digital-competency requirements under Germany's new dental licensing regulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to teaching media on students' own computers with compatibility across common operating systems, which the authors call a precondition for successful digital learning.
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness on mean accuracy was largely not statistically distinguishable from conventional training; the study was single-institution, and time spent training with MCAT was not assessed.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate dental students learning cephalometric diagnosis

### Target Learning Goals
- Consistent, precise cephalometric landmark identification and preparation for end-of-course manual tracing assessments
