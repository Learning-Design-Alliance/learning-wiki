# Balance GenAI integration by permitting documented use while steering students toward deliberate engagement in user research and feedback analysis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adjusting HCI classes in response to GenAI's impact, "emphasizing the need to strengthen its empowering aspects while mitigating potential drawbacks." The course in this study permitted but did not encourage GenAI use, requiring students to honestly document usage as they would document instructions from YouTube or GitHub. A student concluded that "straight up banning it is just impossible" and that coursework "has to be reviewed and possibly reimagined" to refocus student time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students honestly document their GenAI usage, as the course required documentation of tool use
#### Constraints
- Relying on GenAI for evaluations like feedback synthesis could lead to decisions misaligned with user needs, missing nuanced understanding from direct human engagement

### Target Learners
- graduate students in HCI/design education

### Target Learning Goals
- deep engagement in user research
- reflective practice
- critical design thinking
