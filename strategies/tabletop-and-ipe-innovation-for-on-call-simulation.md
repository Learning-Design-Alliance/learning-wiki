# Adopt tabletop and virtual formats, and interprofessional participation, to address resource burden and the absence of IPE in on-call simulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review proposes two innovations to existing models. Tabletop methods, used in disaster-management training for prioritisation, are scalable and low-resource and translatable to on-call simulation, though they may compromise psychological realism and are likely suited as an adjunct. No reviewed on-call simulation incorporated interprofessional education; adapting an interprofessional training ward model, nursing roles could replace facilitators so nurses and students practise profession-specific competencies such as escalating deteriorating patients while retaining interprofessional communication benefits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Significant logistical undertaking to ensure tasks remain relevant and useful to both professions in the IPE adaptation
#### Constraints
- Tabletop simulation may compromise psychological realism, a factor highlighted as crucial for good learning outcomes; there is a paucity of published accessible tabletop on-call simulations

### Target Learners
- medical students
- newly qualified doctors
- nursing students and staff

### Target Learning Goals
- prioritisation and task management
- interprofessional communication
- escalation of deteriorating patients
