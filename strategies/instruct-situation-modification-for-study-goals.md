# Teach students to remove temptations from their surroundings rather than exhorting willpower

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A brief classroom activity in which students set a study goal for the coming week and then modify their environment to minimize temptations — for example, "setting reminders or alarms, installing online apps to block online temptations such as Facebook." In Studies 2 and 3, students assigned to this strategy reported better goal attainment one week later than students instructed to exert willpower or given no strategy. The authors suggest "teachers and professors might instead explain that removing temptations from their surroundings makes staying on task easier."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students set a personally relevant and feasible study goal for the coming week
- Students make environmental modifications they think would be useful, repeating temporary changes as necessary
#### Constraints
- Effects were measured via self-reported goal attainment one week later; durability beyond that time point was not measured

### Target Learners
- high school students
- college students

### Target Learning Goals
- academic self-control
- attaining self-set study goals
