# Design controlled studies varying the frequency and duration of the same active-learning strategy to determine how time distribution affects learning and affect

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors propose a research strategy: have the same instructor implement the same active-learning strategy at different frequencies and durations — for example, 15 minutes per class period three times a week compared with 45 minutes in one class period — and compare students' learning gains, attitudes, metacognition, and motivation. They also recommend student interviews and think-aloud procedures to explore how affective changes occur.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assignment of strategies or frequency/duration conditions must be controlled (random assignment of instructors or within-instructor comparison across classes)
#### Constraints
- The present study was quasi-experimental and could not dictate strategy choice or frequency, so it could not establish causal effects of time distribution

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large-lecture introductory STEM courses

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding
- attitudes and motivation in science
