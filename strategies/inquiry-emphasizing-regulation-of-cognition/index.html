Develop inquiry-based learning that explicitly emphasizes regulation of cognition, especially information management, monitoring, evaluating, and debugging¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend building on the inquiry learning model by making regulation of cognition an explicit, maximized component. They state it is "necessary to develop a learning model by making the inquiry learning model as a specific basic model emphasizing on students to carry out maximally the process of regulation of cognition, especially towards the aspects of IMS, M, E, and D", to be achieved "through integration of possible strategies, such as cognitive conflict, non -routine concept presentation, or implementing portfolio -based performance". This follows from their finding that inconsistent impacts traced to differences in these regulation abilities.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Integration of supporting strategies such as cognitive conflict, non-routine concept presentation, or portfolio-based performance

Constraints¶

  • Grounded in a weak-experimental study without a control class; the recommendation addresses the inconsistency the authors observed across their three groups

Target Learners¶

  • prospective physics teacher-students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • metacognitive regulation: information management, monitoring, evaluation, debugging

Affordances¶

  • Metacognition Knowledge Awareness Distinction

Claims¶

  • Metacognition gains after inquiry learning differed across three classes: knowledge gains differed among all three, while awareness gains differed only between Group A and Groups B and C, not between B and C [~W]
  • Prospective teachers’ overall metacognition awareness (MAI) rose significantly from pre-test to post-test after inquiry learning in three classes with no control group, at moderate overall n-gain in each class [+W]
  • Prospective teachers’ metacognition knowledge rose significantly from pre-test to post-test after inquiry learning about fluid in three classes with no control group, with high n-gain in Group A and moderate n-gain in Groups B and C [+W]
  • Students could not generalize MAI questionnaire statements to their actual metacognition awareness, motivating culturally grounded awareness instruments [~W]
