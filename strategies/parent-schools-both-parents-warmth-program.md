# Parent awareness-raising programs and parent schools should emphasize both parents’ warmth and replace excessive control with communication and inductive discipline

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "awareness-raising programs or parent schools should emphasize the importance of the role of both parents", especially displays of affection and warmth, since maternal and paternal affection both contribute to adolescents’ adjustment. It further recommends increasing warmth, avoiding excessive control, and replacing it with "close and frank communication between parents and children" and inductive discipline with age-appropriate supervision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should address fathers as well as mothers, not only the mother as most frequently highlighted in the scientific literature.
#### Constraints
- The recommendations rest on a cross-sectional study of Basque secondary students and are framed as practical implications rather than tested program effects.

### Target Learners
- Parents of adolescents
- Practitioners running parent schools or awareness-raising programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Adolescents’ psychosocial adjustment
- Adolescents’ academic performance

## Claims

- [In adolescent males, only the father’s control and warmth were inversely related to school maladjustment, which mediated academic performance, and the model explained 15% of performance variance](../claims/fathers-control-warmth-school-maladjustment-males.md) [+W]
- [In adolescent females, school maladjustment inversely mediated the relationship between the mother’s warmth and academic performance, and both parents’ warmth was negatively associated with emotional symptoms](../claims/maternal-warmth-school-maladjustment-females.md) [+W]
- [Parental warmth and control relate to adolescents’ academic performance only indirectly, through psychological and school maladjustment, with no direct relationship with performance](../claims/parental-socialization-indirect-effect-academic-performance.md) [+W]
