# Base referee training and performance analysis on expertise and cognitive processing rather than gender-based assumptions or gaze metrics alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that referee development and performance analysis emphasize individual expertise and cognitive interpretation of game situations rather than gender-based assumptions or gaze-behavior differences. It states these findings "underscore the importance of focusing on individual expertise and cognitive processing rather than gender-based assumptions in the analysis of refereeing performance", and that understanding officiating gaze behavior is crucial for enhancing training programs, particularly for novice referees.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training programs should target interpretation and categorization of game situations, e.g. through video-based decision scenarios with accuracy feedback
#### Constraints
- The article notes laboratory findings may not generalize to field conditions, and future research should examine natural game settings with time pressure, noise, and physical exertion

### Target Learners
- novice and developing handball referees
- referee educators in sports federations

### Target Learning Goals
- decision-making accuracy in officiating situations

### Affordances
- Cognitive Interpretation Referee Expertise Account
