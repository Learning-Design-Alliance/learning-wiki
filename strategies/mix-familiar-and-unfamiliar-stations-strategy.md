# Include common high-familiarity stations alongside unfamiliar advanced-skill stations when designing bootcamps for new registrars

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because trainees show high baselines for frequently practised tasks yet still improve, the article recommends "the inclusion of the stations in future ENT ST3 focused bootcamps would be beneficial in the training of new ST3s to enhance their capability in the clinical environment even further". Unfamiliar advanced stations such as rigid bronchoscopy address the largest preparedness gaps, since novices were least comfortable with such techniques. Combining both station types targets the full range of incoming capability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stations matched to the job-role demands set by the training curriculum, such as the 2021 ISCP requirement to perform and change a tracheostomy
#### Constraints
- Mannequin artifacts may teach poor technique; the article cites reports of improper mask ventilation seals and stiffer-than-patient airways

### Target Learners
- ENT ST3 registrars transitioning from core surgical training

### Target Learning Goals
- Confidence and preparedness across both routine tasks (ward rounds, tonsillectomy, tracheostomy) and unfamiliar emergency/elective procedures (rigid bronchoscopy, SPA ligation)
