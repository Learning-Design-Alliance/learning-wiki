# Test individual MI workshop components in larger multi-arm studies rather than assuming single exercises drive gains

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend investigating which specific components of MI training workshops contribute to empathy gains, responding to a call to "seek to better understand the effective training ingredients." They propose a much larger study, ideally with multiple arms testing additional learning conditions, to assess whether workshop components act synergistically in increments rather than at a single decisive point.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A larger sample size than the 53-student analytic pilot, and multiple study arms, to detect component-level effects.
#### Constraints
- Pilot effect data suggest a medium or large effect from a single training modification of this type is unlikely; power analyses should not rely solely on pilot effect sizes.

### Target Learners
- MI training participants across healthcare disciplines

### Target Learning Goals
- optimizing within-workshop outcomes such as therapeutic empathy
