# Provide two consecutive half days of suturing practice plus refresher sessions to build and maintain laparoscopic suturing proficiency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a specific training dosage: "one half day to practice suturing skills is not sufficient and recommend two consecutive half days for optimal skill acquisition". Because performance decrements appear after 6 months without use, it further advises that "a retention interval and refresher session should be provided for the junior trainees who do not have adequate exposure in the operating room". Portable and virtual reality simulators are named as valid tools for this maintenance purpose.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutored hands-on sessions on physical models with expert tutors in laparoscopic suturing
- Structured ongoing training or refresher sessions when operative-room exposure is inadequate
#### Constraints
- Trainees without clinical application opportunities may deskill quickly, so maintenance practice is required
- Current VR simulators are described as a long way off providing effective simulation for laparoscopic suturing

### Target Learners
- junior surgical trainees learning intracorporeal suturing

### Target Learning Goals
- skill acquisition and proficiency maintenance in laparoscopic suturing and knot tying
