# Introduce a two-step peer assessment process so students can act on feedback between assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends moving from a single post-summative peer assessment to a two-step process: students evaluate group member performance after the formatively assessed scoping presentation and again at the summative assessment of proposed interventions. The authors state this "would provide students with an opportunity to change their behavior," and judged that this opportunity for poor performers to change outweighed the potential for conflict.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Peer assessment criteria produced by the cohort at the start of term
- An online system allowing confidential peer scoring
#### Constraints
- Peer evaluation was brought in after the summative assessment mark had been awarded, so students had no opportunity to get feedback and respond

### Target Learners
- Final-year undergraduate students working in groups of four to six

### Target Learning Goals
- Fair assessment of individual contribution to group work
- Reflective and evaluative skills
