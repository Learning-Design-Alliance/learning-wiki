Integrate AI feedback as a supportive resource within process-oriented drafting cycles, with explicit guidance on critical evaluation¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's procedure: students receive explicit genre instruction, draft independently, submit to GROK for AI feedback, critically evaluate suggestions, and revise into a second draft, with the instructor providing supplementary feedback when necessary. Students were told AI feedback "should be used as a supportive learning resource rather than a replacement for their own critical judgment during the revision process." This gave all participants sustained hands-on experience before reflecting on perceptions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Free and equal access to the AI tool for all students
  • Instructor available for supplementary feedback to clarify misconceptions

Constraints¶

  • AI was the primary formative feedback source; the instructor's role was supplementary, which students later found insufficient for individualized guidance

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate EFL students in academic writing courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic paragraph and essay writing, revision, linguistic accuracy and coherence

Affordances¶

  • Sct Algorithmic Feedback Lens

Related Strategies¶

  • Implement AI-peer integrated feedback as a standardized two-phase revision cycle with rubric-aligned prompting and instructor monitoring
  • Collaborative Human-AI Revision Design embedding prompt engineering across drafting, feedback, and rewriting phases
  • Classroom exercise cycle: select a task, formulate a baseline prompt, submit it to an LLM, then critically analyse the output before revising instructions
