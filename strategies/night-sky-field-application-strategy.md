# Night-sky field test where students apply their circumpolar maps and stellar compass under real conditions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A supplemental wrap-up activity in which students, at home or as a class event, take their circumpolar-range maps and stellar compasses outside on a clear evening to identify stars, find North or South, and check the maps against the sky. The article motivates it thus: "Nothing is more instructive than actually applying to real conditions what has been learned and exercised in theory." Dimmed or red torches are used to preserve dark adaptation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear evening, a site with a good view to the horizon, dimmed (preferably red) torches, and the maps and compasses from activities 1 and 2
#### Constraints
- In the southern hemisphere a magnetic compass might be needed because there is no bright pole star

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students aged 14–19, working individually at home or as a class group

### Target Learning Goals
- transferring classroom navigation skills to real sky observation
- identifying constellations and circumpolar ranges in the actual night sky
