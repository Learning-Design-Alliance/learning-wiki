# Two-lecture preparation module teaching linear algebra, linear systems, and radiometry before the color science tutorials

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Before the tutorials, the authors "spend two lectures at the beginning of the course introducing the necessary math and physics for learning color science." The first lecture builds on linear algebra to teach geometric transformations and linear systems, from which the course builds the axiom that human color perception is a linear system. The second lecture builds on basic calculus to introduce radiometric and photometric properties of light, most importantly the spectral power distribution, introduced as the recipe of constructing a light so students can reason constructively about abstract concepts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students enter with background in linear algebra and calculus; the lectures are scheduled at the beginning of the course before the tutorial series.
#### Constraints
- The preparation is designed for a second-year EECS student audience with no required prior exposure to vision, image processing, or graphics.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate EECS students in a visual computing course

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding color space transformation and chromaticity
- constructing lights by specifying SPDs to understand abstract color concepts

### Affordances
- Colorvis Tutorial Series
