# Maintaining an Inclusive Curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A culturally responsive curriculum ensures that all students are included within all aspects of schooling while acknowledging the unique cultural backgrounds and differences they bring. It requires teachers to understand and recognize each student's non-school cultural life and to incorporate that knowledge into curriculum design, so that content, examples, texts, and tasks connect to learners' identities rather than assuming a single default cultural frame [Ladson-Billings' culturally relevant pedagogy framework.](https://doi.org/10.1080/0142569950180107) [+M].

## Design Implications

Inclusive curriculum works through relevance and belonging: when learners see their own experiences reflected in content, task value and engagement rise, which supports persistence [Curriculum perceived as relevant increases task value and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]. It is not a one-off unit on diversity but a sustained design discipline — auditing examples, authors, names, imagery, and framing across the whole course for whose experience is centered and whose is absent.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A commitment to diversity and inclusion, willingness to learn about students' cultures, and creativity in curriculum design
- Systematic audit of existing materials: whose perspectives, names, geographies, and knowledge traditions appear in readings, examples, and assessments
- Mechanisms for learning about students' backgrounds ([Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md) routines, community and family input, student voice surveys)
- Willingness to revise canonical content, not just append token examples

#### Constraints
- Requires additional time and effort to develop culturally responsive materials; without structural support, the burden often falls disproportionately on instructors of color and on individual goodwill [-W]
- Surface-level "heroes and holidays" approaches — adding isolated cultural examples without changing underlying framing — can reinforce stereotypes rather than reduce them [~M]
- Some students (and colleagues) may resist changes to the traditional curriculum; resistance is reduced when revisions are framed as expanding rigor and perspective rather than replacing content [-W]
- In highly standardized or externally examined programs, alignment requirements can constrain how much content can be revised [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Content-level:** diversify readings, cases, and examples (e.g., [Case Study](../elements/case-study.md) selections drawn from multiple regions and traditions)
- **Pedagogy-level:** incorporate students' funds of knowledge as legitimate course content through [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) and [Activating Background Knowledge](../strategies/activating_background_knowledge.md) routines
- **Assessment-level:** offer choice of topics or contexts so learners can connect tasks to their own communities
- **Language-level:** audit terminology and symbols for bias ([Address Biases in the Use of Language and Symbols](../strategies/address_biases_in_the_use_of_language_and_symbols.md))

### Target Learners
- Students from culturally and linguistically minoritized groups, who otherwise encounter curricula that treat their experience as peripheral [Culturally relevant pedagogy improves engagement and achievement for minoritized students.](https://doi.org/10.1080/0142569950180107) [+M]
- All students benefit from exposure to multiple perspectives; relevance supports attention and encoding [Relevant emphasis directs attention and improves processing.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [+M]
- Learners whose sense of belonging is fragile — inclusive curriculum supports the self-efficacy that predicts persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Belonging and identity safety: students feel valued and connected to the curriculum
- Transferable perspective-taking: analyzing issues from multiple cultural standpoints ([Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md))
- Critical literacy: recognizing whose knowledge is represented and how
- Standard content goals, pursued through culturally connected examples rather than instead of them

### Instructions
1. Audit existing curriculum for representation: catalog authors, examples, names, and cases across the course; identify whose experience is centered.
2. Revise content to include multiple perspectives — select [Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md) and [Case Study](../elements/case-study.md) materials from diverse traditions, avoiding tokenism by integrating them as core rather than supplementary.
3. Elicit and incorporate students' backgrounds through [Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md) routines and structured [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), treating home knowledge as an asset.
4. Review language, examples, and imagery for bias ([Address Biases in the Use of Language and Symbols](../strategies/address_biases_in_the_use_of_language_and_symbols.md)) and for [Accessible Vocabulary and Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md) for multilingual learners.
5. Offer assessment choices that let students connect tasks to their own communities and interests.
6. Iterate: gather student feedback each cycle and revise, treating inclusion as maintenance rather than a one-time redesign.

## Patterns That Use This Strategy
- [Case-Based Learning](../patterns/case-based-learning.md) — case selection determines whose contexts and problems are treated as canonical
- [Discussion-Based Learning](../patterns/discussion-based-learning.md) — discussion pedagogies depend on an inclusive climate where all students' contributions are legitimate

## Examples
- **Zinn Education Project** (https://www.zinnedproject.org) — published K-12 curriculum materials centering marginalized perspectives in history teaching, with downloadable lessons organized by theme and grade level.
- **Learning for Justice (formerly Teaching Tolerance) Critical Practices framework** (https://www.learningforjustice.org) — a published anti-bias curriculum audit and implementation guide for K-12 schools.
- **Culturally responsive mathematics teaching (Aguirre & del Rosario Zavala)** — the "Culturally Responsive Mathematics Teaching Tool" supports teachers in auditing math lessons for cognitive demand, mathematical agency, and cultural/community connections.
