# Design sex-specific intervention programs to raise GPA in low-performing university students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that intervention programs for students with low academic performance be addressed by sex, because academic achievement was regulated by different variables in each sex. For women it suggests programs on "emotion perception and positive relations with others, as well as healthy lifestyle habits", and for men programs on self-motivation and emotion regulation, in order to increase GPA.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should target the variables found associated with GPA in each sex, per the article's sex-stratified correlation and regression results.
#### Constraints
- The article states longitudinal studies are needed to corroborate these possible causal associations before interventions can be assumed effective.

### Target Learners
- health sciences university students with low academic performance (failed the same subject twice)

### Target Learning Goals
- increase grade point average (academic achievement)
