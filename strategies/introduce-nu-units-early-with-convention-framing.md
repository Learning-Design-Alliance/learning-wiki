# Introduce the nu-unit convention from the beginning of the course with explicit framing of unit conventions versus physical identities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's qualitative and limitation sections point to two implementation moves for instructors adopting the convention. A student "suggested that the convention would be most eﬀective if introduced from the beginning of the course rather than midway through", and another student's question about why two constants with different SI values can be set equal is described as "a productive confusion that points to the need for clearer pedagogical framing of the distinction between a physical identity and a unit convention". Students also recommended more step-by-step comparisons between SI and the nu-system during derivations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit framing distinguishing a unit convention from a physical identity
- Step-by-step SI-versus-nu comparisons during derivations
#### Constraints
- Mid-course introduction after students have internalized SI may reduce effectiveness
- Transitional cost for students who later work in SI-only settings such as exchange programmes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students encountering electromagnetism

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the status of unit conventions
- reducing confusion when two vacuum constants are set equal

### Affordances
- Nu Unit Convention Epsilon Mu Over C
