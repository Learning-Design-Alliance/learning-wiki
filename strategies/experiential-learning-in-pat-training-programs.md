# Combine experiential learning with specialized psychedelic-assisted therapy training programs to strengthen competency and optimize safety and outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that PAT training programs incorporate formal experiential learning, in which therapists have supported personal experiences with the medicine in a safe, controlled setting. It reports that all eight interviewed KAT therapists desired such training and viewed it as "a key element missing from their training". The authors conclude that "experiential learning might be combined with specialized PAT training programs to strengthen professional competency and optimize safety and outcomes for patients and therapists".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must occur in a supportive, controlled setting with a container of safety, as participants requested
#### Constraints
- The article notes experiential training may not need to be an outright requirement, and current legal restrictions and regulatory hurdles limit access to formal experiential training options

### Target Learners
- Licensed therapists and healthcare providers training to deliver ketamine-assisted and other psychedelic-assisted therapies

### Target Learning Goals
- Therapist competency in delivering KAT, including empathy, therapeutic alliance, confidence, and safe navigation of client psychedelic experiences
