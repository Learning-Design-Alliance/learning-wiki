# Use the proposed decision tree in teacher education to systematize YouTube video selection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that teacher education courses use the decision tree as a tool for preparing teaching, and that future research examine whether it facilitates efficient identification of high-quality videos, how it can be adapted to different subject areas and teaching contexts, and how it can accommodate evolving platform features. The strategy rests on the finding that teachers' unaided selection is unsystematic and overlooks available empirical evidence on explaining quality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Future studies must first evaluate whether the decision tree facilitates efficient and successful identification of high-quality videos
#### Constraints
- The decision tree is a hypothesis, not a validated tool; its steps may need supplementation or specification; platform features and metrics evolve over time

### Target Learners
- pre-service science teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- systematic selection of high-quality instructional videos for lesson preparation
