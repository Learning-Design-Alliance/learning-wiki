# Segment security-training content by learner age group to maximize personal relevance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Rather than a one-size-fits-all approach, this strategy tailors scam scenarios to each demographic's everyday digital life: simple game-based interactions for children, dating-app and AI-generated-media scenarios for teens and college students, and coupon, shopping-deal, and lottery scenarios for learners over 50. The article concludes that "the feedback strongly supports a shift toward a segmented model, where age-relevant scenarios... ensure that the training maximizes personal relevance and real-world applicability," including a proposed Enter Age feature at login.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A demographic intake mechanism such as the proposed Enter Age feature at login to filter and deliver age-appropriate content
- A library of scenario content matched to each age group's actual exposure
#### Constraints
- The strategy derives from participant brainstorming feedback in the qualitative analysis, not from a tested comparison of segmented versus unsegmented training

### Target Learners
- children ages 4-10
- teens and college students
- adults over 50

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing scams relevant to one's own daily digital life
- verification habits such as checking senders and URLs
