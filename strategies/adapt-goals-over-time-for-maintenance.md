# Adapt self-monitoring goals over time based on data to program for prolonged behavioral change

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends systematically adapting behavioral goals upward (or downward to ensure success) as student response data accumulate, so that improvements sustain after intervention is withdrawn. It states that "even if teachers are using a paper–pencil self-monitoring form, they can still set goals, graph progress, and adapt goals over time to foster prolonged student success." MoBeGo operationalizes this with an automated, data-based algorithm that notifies teachers and students when a goal should be increased or decreased, and teachers may accept or keep the current goal.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Goal adaptations should be based on student response data collected during the intervention
- Teachers or the app must review progress data regularly to determine when goals should change
#### Constraints
- The article notes further research is needed to replicate these findings before adapting goals can be broadly recommended beyond MoBeGo
- In a prior single-case study, adaptations produced only further minimal increases after initial gains, though positive behaviors sustained

### Target Learners
- third- to eighth-grade students with challenging classroom behavior

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained reductions in disruptive behavior after intervention withdrawal
- prolonged behavioral change in self-monitoring interventions
