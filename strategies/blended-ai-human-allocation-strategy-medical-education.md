# Design blended learning environments that allocate AI tools to foundational knowledge and human expertise to conceptually challenging areas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that educators "strategically leverage this knowledge to design blended learning environments that utilize AI tools for foundational knowledge building while preserving human expertise for conceptually challenging areas". Challenging topics such as muscle tissue and the lymphoid system become natural learning objectives for direct faculty instruction. The strategy rests on the finding that performance varies by content type rather than platform choice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prior empirical evaluation of LLM accuracy and reliability for the specific subject before deployment
#### Constraints
- Applies to text-based factual content; image interpretation and microscopic analysis remain largely untested and require human expertise

### Target Learners
- Medical students in basic science curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- Foundational factual knowledge building and formative practice in basic medical sciences
