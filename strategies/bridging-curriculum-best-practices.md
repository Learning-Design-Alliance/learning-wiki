# Five best practices for bridging curricula: explicit conceptual bridging, strategic group formation, resource management education, integrated ethics, peer teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Through multiple course iterations the authors identified five insights for other educators. "Explicit Conceptual Bridging proves essential", using comparison matrices and mapping exercises; "Strategic Group Formation yields better outcomes than random team assignment" via a skills assessment survey forming balanced teams; resource management education teaches quantization and batching; "Integrated Ethics Education works more effectively than standalone ethics modules"; and "Peer Teaching Opportunities significantly enhance learning outcomes."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- a skills assessment survey covering programming experience, mathematical background, and communication preferences for group formation
#### Constraints
- insights drawn from the authors' own course iterations rather than controlled comparison

### Target Learners
- students in AI and data science courses

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual connections between ML and LLM paradigms
- collaborative dynamics
- ethical frameworks across AI technologies
