# Pair increased academic demand with increased teacher support when raising instructional rigor

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article argues that raising academic demand alone can backfire: students with a history of low achievement "may become disengaged and stop trying when academic rigor increases without simultaneous eﬀorts to help them meet the new demands," producing frustration, disorder, and worse outcomes. It recommends that efforts to increase challenge be accompanied by "increased scaﬀolding, assistance, encouragement, and other forms of support," noting that ideal learning environments combine demand and support. The CPS CCSS-M transition illustrates this pairing: extensive-PD elementary schools raised demand while also showing significantly higher teacher support, and course performance improved rather than deteriorating.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers able to provide monitoring, feedback, assistance, and socioemotional support alongside more challenging coursework
#### Constraints
- Previous CPS demand-raising reform (College Preparatory Curriculum for All) increased course failures and lowered grades when support was not simultaneously emphasized

### Target Learners
- K-12 students, particularly those considered academically at risk

### Target Learning Goals
- Engaging successfully with more challenging, standards-aligned coursework without disengagement or course failure
