# Structure a remote AR simulation session as orientation, preparatory video, simulation, structured debriefing, and post-intervention questionnaires

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable session recipe: an orientation introducing the study and familiarizing learners with the AR headset and holographic elements; a five-minute video reviewing the NRP algorithm, the case context, and team-leader responsibilities; an approximately 10–12 minute simulation with continuous NTS observation; a structured five-minute debriefing focused on "guided reflection regarding their clinical performance and decision-making processes"; then questionnaires. Total commitment is about one hour per participant. This sequence allowed NTS ratings to be based strictly on pre-debriefing simulation performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A research assistant trained in NTS rating observing continuously during the simulation
- Independent, blinded scoring by two trained raters when the session doubles as assessment
- Scripted scenario and pre-specified instructor prompts to standardize delivery
#### Constraints
- The article prints no evidence that this sequence improves learning; it reports feasibility and assessment viability only

### Target Learners
- Pediatric and anesthesiology residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Assessing and supporting non-technical skills during neonatal resuscitation
