# Use dynamic software so students visualize, explore and discuss data instead of only computing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that technology be used not merely to compute numbers but as a tool for exploring concepts: teachers are encouraged "to view technology use not just as a way to compute numbers, but also as a way to explore concepts and ideas and enhance student learning". In practice, students use software such as Fathom to visualize multiple representations of the same data, perform basic computations quickly, and then explain, communicate and defend their conclusions in discussion with peers and the teacher.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must encourage students to explain, communicate and reason their answers so they can make arguments statistically; actual data are used to prompt conclusions.
#### Constraints
- The article states traditional teacher-centred, procedure-focused teaching does not develop deeper understanding; the strategy was tested over eight weeks in one school.

### Target Learners
- Form Four secondary students learning statistics

### Target Learning Goals
- Statistical reasoning, interpreting graphs and data, drawing conclusions and making statistical arguments
