# Support low-efficacy and socially anxious students with low-stakes mastery-focused assignments and by mitigating anxiety-exacerbating classroom features

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes classroom actions to increase low-efficacy students' engagement with active learning and support performance. It recommends "the use of low-stakes, mastery-focused assignments with clear and constructive feedback", and, to reduce social anxiety, mitigating classroom features that may exacerbate anxiety, such as decreasing overly competitive classroom climates and increasing instructor transparency in the rationale behind instructional practices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must be willing to adjust classroom climate and explain the rationale behind instructional practices.
#### Constraints
- The article states its design does not allow causal inferences about the relationships of these psychological variables with student performance; these are proposed, not tested, interventions.

### Target Learners
- community college students in STEM-related courses
- socially anxious students
- students low in academic self-efficacy

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance in active-learning courses
- engagement with evidence-based instructional practices
