# Family Book Club

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The educator selects a book that reflects students' identities, interests, and — where possible — their home languages, then records a short video of themselves reading a portion aloud each week until the book is finished. Alongside each video, the educator posts discussion questions that families can talk through with their children at home, with questions provided in families' home languages when feasible. The strategy converts family time into a shared literacy experience anchored by the teacher's read-aloud modeling.

## Design Implications

Family Book Club leverages the home literacy environment, which is a reliable predictor of children's language and reading growth: parent–child shared reading and book exposure are associated with stronger vocabulary and emergent literacy outcomes [Sénéchal & LeFevre's home literacy model](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-8624.00418) [+S]. The weekly read-aloud video functions as a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)-adjacent model of fluent, expressive reading, while the discussion questions prompt dialogic interaction rather than passive listening — conversational turns around books predict language gains more strongly than reading alone [~M]. Providing questions in home languages signals that families' linguistic resources are assets, supporting [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md) and identity-safe classrooms, and increases the likelihood that families with limited English proficiency participate [+W].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A book representative of students' backgrounds and interests; home-language editions or translations where possible
- Video recording equipment and a platform families can access (e.g., a class website, LMS announcement, or messaging app)
- Discussion questions per installment, translated into families' home languages when feasible
- A predictable weekly cadence so families can build a routine around it

#### Constraints
- Effectiveness depends on family participation; without it, the strategy reaches only the students whose homes already support literacy, potentially widening gaps [-M]
- Recording and translating weekly content is time-intensive for the educator; unsustainable pacing leads to abandonment mid-book
- One-way video delivery offers no mechanism to hear families' discussions; without a feedback loop (e.g., children bringing a comment back to class), the educator cannot adjust or sustain engagement [~W]
- Home-language availability of the chosen book is often limited; translation of questions only partially compensates

#### Implementation Variability
- Live virtual read-aloud sessions instead of recorded videos, allowing real-time family questions
- Student-led installments: older students record a week's reading themselves, deepening engagement through [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md)
- Multilingual family co-hosts reading in their home language, positioning families as co-educators rather than recipients
- Physical book kits sent home alongside videos for families without reliable internet

### Target Learners
- Early and emergent readers, whose literacy development is most sensitive to home shared-reading frequency [+S]
- Multilingual learners, when home-language materials and questions validate and build first-language literacy, which transfers to second-language reading [+W]
- Families who want to engage but lack confidence in how to discuss books with children; the posted questions scaffold the interaction
- Less effective for students whose families cannot or do not participate; a classroom-only fallback discussion should exist [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Literacy development: vocabulary growth, listening comprehension, and positive reading dispositions
- Family engagement: building home–school connection and shared purpose around learning
- Community building: a common text that all students, regardless of home resources, can discuss at school
- Motivation: connecting reading to family and identity raises task value [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Select a book that mirrors students' identities and interests; check for home-language editions or audio versions ([Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md)).
2. Record a short weekly read-aloud video, modeling expressive reading and briefly voicing your own reactions or confusions to model engagement ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)).
3. Post 2–4 open discussion questions per installment, translated into families' home languages where possible; favor questions that invite personal connection over recall.
4. Provide a way for children to bring family conversations back to class — a comment card, a class discussion circle, or a shared wall ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).
5. Gather family feedback mid-book and adjust pacing, language support, or book choice accordingly.

## Examples
- **ReadWorks and Unite for Literacy** ([https://www.uniteforliteracy.com](https://www.uniteforliteracy.com)) — free digital libraries with narrated books in multiple languages that educators can use as home-language book club texts.
- **Raising A Reader** ([https://raisingareader.org](https://raisingareader.org)) — a national program that rotates book bags through homes and trains families in dialogic shared reading; Family Book Club is a teacher-driven, virtual adaptation of the same logic.
- A dual-language kindergarten teacher records weekly Spanish and English read-alouds of the same book and posts questions in both languages, then opens Monday circle with children's family comments.
