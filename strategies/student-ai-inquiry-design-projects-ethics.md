# Students learn AI ethics through inquiry or design projects examining societal impact and bias

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that 'Students should likewise engage in ethical reflection through AI inquiry or design projects, critically examining societal impact, justice, privacy, and bias, supported by evidence-based argumentation'. Such practices, it argues, enable learners to interrogate existing data-driven systems and envision alternatives, extending AI literacy beyond technical skills toward critical and political forms of agency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Support for evidence-based argumentation within the projects
#### Constraints
- The article links this to transformative AI education addressing a widening divide between those who can critically engage with AI and those who cannot

### Target Learners
- K-12 students

### Target Learning Goals
- critical examination of AI systems' societal impact
- envisioning alternatives to existing data-driven systems
