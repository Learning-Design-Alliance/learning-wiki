# Focus on the Question They Answered Correctly

## Description
Instead of focusing on a student’s mistake, instructors identify the question a student answered correctly, even if it’s different than the one asked. This approach assumes all student ideas are interesting and aims to value the student’s thinking. By identifying what the student understood, instructors can then connect that correct understanding to the question the student didn’t answer correctly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Skilled teachers who can ask effective questions and facilitate meaningful discussions.
#### Constraints
- Requires teachers to be skilled in questioning techniques. May be time-consuming, especially in large classes. This strategy is not useful for students with no prior knowledge of the topic.

### Personalization
- Requires instructors to be aware of their student's individual needs, and to be flexible and willing to ask open ended questions rather than simply looking for the right answer
