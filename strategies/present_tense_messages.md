# Present Tense Messages

## Description
This strategy involves shifting the focus of internal dialogue from future anxieties to actionable steps in the present to manage overwhelming feelings. It encourages individuals to concentrate on what they can do right now rather than getting caught up in endless streams of worry about the future. By changing internal talk to focus on the manageable present, individuals can take necessary steps to build a better tomorrow, focusing thoughts and inner dialogue on the present moment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires self-awareness and a willingness to shift focus from future worries to present actions. May benefit from external support such as therapy or coaching to address underlying anxiety. Access to resources such as to-do lists, goal-setting templates, or mindfulness apps can be helpful.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging to implement for individuals with severe anxiety or panic disorders without professional support. Requires consistent effort to maintain focus on the present moment and avoid getting caught up in future worries. May not be effective if not accompanied by a willingness to take action in the present.

### Target Learners

- All learner levels, particularly useful for individuals prone to anxiety, procrastination, or feeling overwhelmed by future challenges.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Reduce anxiety about the future, shift focus to actionable steps in the present, promote a sense of control, manage overwhelming feelings, encourage proactive behavior.

### Affordances
- Reduces anxiety by shifting focus from future worries to present actions. Increases motivation by breaking down overwhelming tasks into manageable steps. Fosters a sense of control by empowering learners to take action in the present. Enhances self-esteem by promoting a sense of accomplishment and progress.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted with customized questions tailored to individual anxieties and goals. Flexible practice allows for integration into daily routines through journaling, mindfulness exercises, or goal-setting activities. Scaffolding can be provided for beginners, such as lists of present-focused actions or guided prompts.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track changes in self-reported anxiety levels using questionnaires or journals. Observe improvements in productivity, task completion, and proactive behavior. Analyze ability to identify and shift focus from future worries to present actions.

## Impact
- Research suggests that focusing on the present moment can reduce anxiety and improve well-being (e.g., American Psychological Association). Studies show that mindfulness practices can enhance attention and promote emotional regulation. Regularly engaging in present-focused activities can foster a sense of control and reduce stress.

## Examples
- An individual feeling anxious about an upcoming deadline breaks down the task into smaller, present-focused steps, such as 'I will spend the next hour outlining the report.' A student feeling overwhelmed by a challenging course focuses on completing one assignment at a time, rather than worrying about the entire course.
