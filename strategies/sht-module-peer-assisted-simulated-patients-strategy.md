# Offer students an SHT module, peer-assisted learning, and simulated-patient practice for intimate-area skills, with positive teacher support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers provide positive support and adopt novel teaching approaches for sensitive clinical skills. Students themselves most often selected peer-assisted learning (77.6%) and simulated patients (77.2%) for IAE, and 'Nearly, 81% of the students thought that the SHT module would be an effective method of clinical skills training.'

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires teaching approaches that do not compromise the Kingdom's religious, sociocultural, and ethical values, as the article states.
#### Constraints
- Recommendations rest on student opinion in a 50.5%-response-rate survey at two colleges, not on outcome testing; several other universities declined participation.

### Target Learners
- final-year undergraduate medical students in Saudi medical colleges

### Target Learning Goals
- intimate-area examination skills
- sexual history taking
