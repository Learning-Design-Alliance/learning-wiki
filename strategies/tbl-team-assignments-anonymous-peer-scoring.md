# Team assignments graded by teachers plus anonymous peer scoring within and across teams

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A grading-and-assignment recipe for team-based learning: classes were “divided into four groups” and completed a health education WeChat push and a student-chosen role-playing scenario based on clinical pediatric cases. Weighting was printed as: “Teacher scoring comprised 60% of the final grade, anonymous scoring by teammates in the same team 20%, anonymous scoring by teammates to other groups after discussion 20%.”

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires the platform's anonymous rating system, since the article notes the non-anonymous format of mutual ratings may not be objective.
#### Constraints
- Team-based learning alone “suffers from an uncontrollable division of labor among team members”, which the anonymous scoring is intended to reduce.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- teamwork
- health education skills
- communication skills
