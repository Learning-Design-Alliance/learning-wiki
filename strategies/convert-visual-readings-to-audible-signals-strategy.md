# Convert visual instrument readings into acoustic signals so blind students measure alongside sighted classmates

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's core intervention strategy is to keep the blind student inside the regular laboratory session by changing only the measurement instrument rather than the activity. As the introduction puts it, "A natural alternative to visual readings is to include sounds that relate to the value of the physical variable that we intend to measure." Each slit crossing of the segmented disk produces one beep, so the student counts beeps to obtain dilation, with synthetic-voice vocalization as a backup when external noise interferes. This let the student do all measurements for the aluminum tube himself during the normal group session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An open-source electronic prototyping platform (Arduino) with suitable sensors to translate the physical variable into sound, and calibration of counts to physical units
#### Constraints
- Vocalization was used only as verification because external noises can impede hearing the beeps; the reading resolution is limited to the disk's slit spacing

### Target Learners
- blind and visually impaired students in undergraduate laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- hands-on experimental measurement skills, here measuring thermal expansion
