# Craft diagnostic pre-assessments and path-diverse exams carefully for adaptive courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Adaptive courses require beginning-of-course assessments to determine each student's strengths and weaknesses so students can navigate unique paths. The article warns that "Multiple choice for diagnostic purposes are diﬃcult items to craft, as students may be able to guess correctly otherwise and therefore exempt lexia competencies from being activated in their learning path." Students should be strongly encouraged not to guess and reassured assessment will not count against them. Exams should test application and deeper problem-solving per Bloom's Taxonomy rather than surface lexia knowledge, and can assess each student on only a subset of questions drawn from lessons that student traversed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each diagnostic question must be crafted in a meaningful way to assess knowledge levels accurately; discussion of why wrong choices were selected informs planning of lexia use
#### Constraints
- Exam timing requires students to have mastered a certain amount of content by a deadline; practice exams are suggested to test question crafting

### Target Learners
- students entering adaptive interdisciplinary courses with diverse prior knowledge

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate placement of students' individualized learning paths and assessment of higher-order thinking
