# Offer participation incentives sized to the data burden, consulting the advisory board or IRB to avoid undue influence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests offering incentives such as extra credit or monetary compensation because incentives have been shown to increase participation rates, and cash incentives are more effective than non-monetary or gift card incentives. However, it cautions that "excessive monetary incentives may act as 'undue influence,' potentially pressuring individuals to participate and raising ethical concerns," disproportionately affecting financially vulnerable populations, and that increasing incentives may have diminishing returns.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Incentive value should be commensurate with the type(s) of data being collected, such as time required to participate
#### Constraints
- Undue influence may disproportionately persuade financially vulnerable populations, such as lecturers, adjunct professors, and low-income students

### Target Learners
- Instructors and students at participating institutions

### Target Learning Goals
- Achieving adequate response rates in multi-institutional data collection
