# Emphasise life-threatening adverse events in take-away points and add a case-based example to improve retention of safety-critical content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because retention was poorest for identifying potential adverse events of high flow use, including life-threatening complications such as pneumothorax, the authors recommend redesigning the training module's take-away points around these complications. They propose emphasising these serious adverse events in the take-away points of the training module and/or including a case-based example of a child with a pneumothorax to raise awareness and improve retention over time. General high flow knowledge, they note, could instead be delivered via bedside handouts or modules.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training module must include take-away points highlighting serious adverse events; optionally a case-based example of a child with a pneumothorax
#### Constraints
- Recommended after observing poor retention; not itself tested in this study

### Target Learners
- PICU clinical staff using high flow in resource-limited settings

### Target Learning Goals
- prompt recognition of life-threatening high flow complications such as pneumothorax
