# Manufacturers of future VLBW infant simulators should take physiological aspects into account and use identified weaknesses to improve anatomic accuracy and fidelity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (7 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that all tested simulators' airway physiology deviated significantly from preterm reference values, the authors recommend that "In developing future VLBW infant simulators, manufacturers should take physiological aspects more into account and use the identified weaknesses to improve further the simulators' anatomic accuracy, physiological properties, and physical and functional fidelity". They also state that natural resistance and compliance during ventilation should be emphasized in future preterm simulators, and that crucial airway structures' angles and distances should be optimized to facilitate realistic airway training and effective skill transfer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ventilator testing of resistance, compliance, and tidal volume against published preterm reference values during simulator development
- CT or comparable measurement of airway angles and distances against human references
#### Constraints
- The article notes manufacturers do not currently claim that the installed lungs of the simulators have natural properties

### Target Learners
- neonatology trainees
- neonatal healthcare professionals learning VLBW airway management

### Target Learning Goals
- realistic bag-mask ventilation technique
- oral and nasal intubation
- less-invasive surfactant administration
