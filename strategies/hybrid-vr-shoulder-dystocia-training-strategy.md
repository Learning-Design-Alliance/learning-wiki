# Hybrid shoulder dystocia training combining theoretical instruction, simulation and 360°-VR

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend combining modalities rather than replacing high-fidelity training: "we suggest hybrid shoulder dystocia training, which is also supported by the literature". The strategy sequences a brief theoretical lesson, manikin-based simulation, and immersive 360°-VR exposure, exploiting VR's availability on demand without specific infrastructure, high-fidelity equipment, or trainers. They call for additional research with larger samples to assess hybrid training's advantages.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants need a theoretical understanding of the maneuvers needed to solve a shoulder dystocia scenario, stated as an eligibility prerequisite
#### Constraints
- The trial could not rule out information exchange between participants of both groups during one-to-one high-fidelity training, and the VR training lacked a haptic modality, which the authors say could have influenced outcomes

### Target Learners
- resident and attending physicians
- midwives
- final-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- adherence to the modified ALSO (HELP-RER) algorithm for shoulder dystocia
- reducing diagnosis-to-delivery time
- managing subjective workload in obstetric emergencies
