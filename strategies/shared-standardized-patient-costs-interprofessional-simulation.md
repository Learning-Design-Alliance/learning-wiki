# Share standardized patient costs across specialties to run interprofessional simulation cost-effectively

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend interprofessional simulation as a relatively cost-effective education tool because individual program costs drop when standardized patient costs are shared among students from multiple specialties. In this study the simulation cost $61 per student, covering the simulation laboratory and standardized patients hired from an outside company, with no additional faculty staffing cost because the activity was integrated into existing clinical coursework. The authors also suggest using alumni and community members as standardized patients to reduce costs further.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration into existing clinical coursework to avoid additional faculty staffing costs
- Access to a simulation laboratory and standardized patient services
#### Constraints
- Costs reported for a single private university in a large urban area; the authors note future cost reduction ideas are only considered, not tested

### Target Learners
- Advanced practice nursing students across multiple specialties

### Target Learning Goals
- Interprofessional communication and collaboration in managing postpartum depression
