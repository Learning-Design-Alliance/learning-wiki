# Incorporate CIA and pCIA content and scalp cooling training into residency and continuing medical education for dermatologists and oncologists

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes specialty-specific education to close the knowledge gaps its survey documented. For dermatologists, the authors recommend "incorporating CIA and pCIA content into residency and continuing medical education (CME) programs"; for oncologists, adopting a broader view of hair loss, recognizing pCIA risk, and integrating scalp cooling as a standard preventive tool, supported by "Incorporating SC training into residency programs and CME". The strategy targets practicing physicians and residents in both specialties.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curricula must cover CIA and pCIA separately, since the article treats them as distinct clinical entities with different features and course
- Scalp cooling training must be added to residency programs and CME for integration into oncology protocols
#### Constraints
- The study is exploratory and hypothesis-generating, based on a feasibility-sized sample from tertiary centers, so the knowledge-gap estimates motivating this strategy may not generalize to all clinicians

### Target Learners
- dermatology residents and attending physicians
- oncology residents and attending physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of CIA causes, prevalence, prevention, and treatment
- recognition of pCIA risk and scalp cooling as a preventive intervention
