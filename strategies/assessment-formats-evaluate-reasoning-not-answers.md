# Assess reasoning, design, and judgment through design critiques, audit reports, POP documentation, and stakeholder communication exercises rather than correct final answers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a shift away from assessments that reward correct final answers, which GAI can now produce for most standard problems, toward formats that evaluate reasoning, design, and judgment: design critiques, audit reports, POP cycle documentation, context instructions for configuring an AI agent, and stakeholder communication exercises. Each format targets specific human core competencies; for example, "audit reports target ethics and accountability; POP documentation targets statistical reasoning and computational thinking; communication exercises target sensemaking". All share one structure: they require students to show their reasoning, not just their results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formats must "require students to show their reasoning, not just their results", distinguishing students who merely identify a concept from those who trace its mechanisms and consequences.
#### Constraints
- Correct final answers for most standard problems can now be produced by GAI, so answer-rewarding assessment no longer discriminates genuine understanding.

### Target Learners
- data science and statistics students in programs adopting GAI tools

### Target Learning Goals
- causal identification
- ethics and accountability
- statistical reasoning
- sensemaking

### Affordances
- Human Core Competencies Data Science Gai
