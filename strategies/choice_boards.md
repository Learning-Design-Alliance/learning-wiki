# Choice Boards

## Description
Choice boards offer learners options for how they demonstrate their learning, increasing engagement and ownership. They present an alternative to standard assessments, empowering learners to choose how they show mastery of a topic and providing instructors with a variety of ways to check for understanding. Choice boards can be structured as a grid or table with various tasks or activities aligned to specific learning objectives. Learners select which tasks to complete, often with a requirement to complete a certain number or type of tasks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires careful planning and alignment of tasks to learning objectives. Clear instructions and expectations must be provided. Access to necessary resources and materials for completing tasks.
#### Constraints
- Some learners may feel overwhelmed by too many choices. May require more instructor time to prepare and assess. Requires clear communication to ensure learners understand expectations.

### Affordances
- Increases motivation by giving learners a sense of control over their learning. Accommodates different learning styles and preferences. Provides instructors with diverse assessment data. Encourages self-directed learning and responsibility.

### Personalization
- Activities can be tailored to individual learner needs and interests. Difficulty levels can be adjusted. Different modalities (visual, auditory, kinesthetic) can be incorporated into the tasks.

## Examples
- In a middle school English class, students can choose to develop a movie trailer, draft diary entries, or create podcast episodes to demonstrate their analysis of characters in a novel. In a third-grade class, a homework choice board might include options such as finding examples of syllable types in books, reading high-frequency words to a family member, or practicing words on an online app.
