# Offer different learning routes and evaluations to increase course satisfaction for male and female students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that those responsible for e-learning "introduce different learning routes and evaluations with the aim of increasing course satisfaction", because male and female students value different aspects of e-learning teaching. They suggest establishing two alternative assessment routes, one of which "could prioritise students’ ongoing participation throughout the course, through forums and chats, and also collaborative action in problem solving". This follows directly from their findings that planning, participation, tutor contribution and practical cases matter differently for each gender.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course design must be able to offer alternative learning routes and assessment options to students
- Awareness of which e-learning aspects matter to male and female students in the specific course context
#### Constraints
- The recommendation stems from a study of 27 courses at a single university with a non-random sample, and the authors note the questionnaire was too long and contained double-barrelled questions

### Target Learners
- Male and female university students in e-learning courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Increasing student satisfaction with e-learning courses
