# Integrate the simulator into the formal oculoplastics curriculum with dedicated scheduled practice time early in residency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding simulator practice into residency training rather than leaving it optional. Half of participants recommended inclusion in the formal oculoplastics curriculum, and the ambivalent respondents said they "would have had a favorable response with curriculum integration if time was specifically allocated for these practice sessions". The authors suggest building dedicated simulation time into the resident schedule at the beginning of residency, since busy PGY2 clinical demands prevented anyone from completing more than three of the suggested five sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated time for practice sessions must be allocated within the resident schedule, ideally at the start of residency
- A 1-h didactic session on eyelid anatomy and a demonstration should precede the first simulation session
#### Constraints
- None of the participating residents completed more than three sessions in the pilot, which the authors attribute to busy clinical demands of the PGY2 year

### Target Learners
- Beginning ophthalmology residents (PGY2) before substantial live laceration repair experience

### Target Learning Goals
- Development of surgical skill, instrument handling, and confidence in margin-involving eyelid laceration repair before live surgery
