Three solutions for data costs in mobile learning: avoid data, batch data, and transfer data locally¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article identifies three main literature-derived solutions to data costs, which primarily impact mobile learning: don't rely on data, use data in batches, and use data locally. Standalone one-time installs (Cognitive Tutor) suit synchronized institutional settings; the 'well model' (EDUCA) lets learners download tutoring modules when data is free or cheap over fixed-price WiFi; and Bluetooth OBEX transfer (Learning Pills) lets an instructor's machine transmit materials directly to students' phones, eliminating significant remote downloading.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Standalone installs require a one-time transfer mechanism such as a CD installer, Internet download, or USB stick; the well model requires a dedicated mobile application rather than just a web-browser portal

Constraints¶

  • The article notes data costs were not a significant barrier for classroom use but could significantly impact informal and home use

Target Learners¶

  • mobile learners in developing regions with costly or limited data

Target Learning Goals¶

  • access to tutoring content without persistent, costly network connectivity
