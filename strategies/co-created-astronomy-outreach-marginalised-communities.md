# Co-creating astronomy outreach with marginalised community leaders and cultural agents

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents a strategy of designing astronomy outreach not for but with marginalised communities, working through local leaders, educators and artists. It states that the projects 'tackle the importance of ethno-astronomy, and the collaboration with leaders and cultural agents of marginalised communities'. In Under Other Skies, indigenous professors and chapes (leaders) of the Village Aldeia Verde were full team members; in OruMbya, the hosts were the cultural centre 'Remanescentes da Tia Ciata'; in Closer to the Sky, astronomers collaborate with artists and educators living in the favela of Cantagalo Pavão e Pavãozinho.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration must be established with recognised leaders and cultural agents of the target community, as the article does with indigenous professors, an afro-Brazilian cultural centre, and favela-based educators and artists.
#### Constraints
- The article notes that measuring the impact of astronomy for development projects 'poses several challenges in terms of time, cost, accurate data and cooperation', especially with marginalised communities in fragile contexts.

### Target Learners
- Children, teenagers and young adults in marginalised and indigenous communities in Brazil

### Target Learning Goals
- Scientific literacy in astronomy
- Intercultural understanding and appreciation of indigenous and afro-Brazilian knowledge
- Educational continuation and access to higher education
