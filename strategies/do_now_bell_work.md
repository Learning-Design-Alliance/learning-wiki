# Do Now / Bell Work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A Do Now (also called bell work, bell ringer, or warm-up) is a brief task — typically 3–7 minutes — posted or distributed so students start working without teacher prompting as class begins. Common formats include retrieval questions on prior material, a problem to solve, a quick write responding to a prompt, or a prediction about the day's topic. The task is deliberately short, doable independently, and connected to the lesson that follows.

## Design Implications

Do Nows serve two functions at once: a classroom-management routine that establishes an academic tone from the first minute, and a learning event — most powerfully as retrieval practice on previously taught content [Retrieval practice produces durable learning beyond restudying.](../claims/retrieval-practice-improves-long-term-retention.md) [+S]. Because the task occupies the opening minutes, its content choice determines whether the time is spent consolidating memory, activating relevant prior knowledge [Activation improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M], or merely filling time.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task visible or distributed before students enter, so no transition time is lost
- Content students can complete independently — no new instruction required
- A consistent routine: same location, same expectations, low-stakes accountability (e.g., checked for completion, not graded for accuracy)
- A brief follow-through: reviewing answers or connecting the task to the day's objective, so the work is not left dangling

#### Constraints
- Novel-content Do Nows defeat the purpose: tasks requiring new instruction leave students stuck and generate off-task behavior [-M]
- If never reviewed or referenced, Do Nows become busywork; students calibrate effort to whether the task "counts" [-M]
- Daily grading creates a heavy teacher workload and shifts student attention from thinking to compliance [-W]
- Repeating the same format daily reduces engagement; novelty and format variation sustain attention [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Retrieval Do Now**: 2–4 questions on material from a previous lesson, days, or weeks — spaced retrieval [Retrieval practice produces durable learning beyond restudying.](../claims/retrieval-practice-improves-long-term-retention.md) [+S]
- **Activation Do Now**: a prompt or question that surfaces relevant prior knowledge or misconceptions before new content ([Activation](../elements/activation.md))
- **Quick write**: a 3-minute written response to a provocative question, priming discussion ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
- **Prediction Do Now**: students predict an outcome or generate a hypothesis, creating curiosity for the lesson
- **Spiral review**: rotating coverage across the term's topics to distribute practice over time

### Target Learners
- All ages; the routine is especially valuable for students who struggle with transitions or self-regulation at lesson start
- Struggling learners benefit from the low-stakes retrieval format, which surfaces gaps without test pressure
- Advanced students may disengage from overly easy Do Nows; difficulty should sit at a "can do with effort" level [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Long-term retention of previously taught content through spaced retrieval
- Activation of prior knowledge in preparation for new learning
- Fluency building: rapid, accurate execution of foundational skills (facts, conventions, procedures)

### Instructions
1. Choose the function first — retrieval, activation, or priming discussion — and write 1–3 items students can complete in under 5 minutes.
2. Post the task in a fixed location before class begins; students know to start without direction.
3. Keep stakes low: completion-based credit or none at all; accuracy feedback comes during review.
4. Circulate and scan responses while students work, using the task as a formative check ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md), [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md)).
5. Debrief in 2–3 minutes: confirm answers, address surfaced misconceptions, and explicitly connect the task to the day's objective.
6. Vary format across the week (retrieval, quick write, prediction) to sustain engagement.

## Examples
- **Retrieval Do Now (secondary math)**: Two problems from last week and one from three weeks ago on the board; students solve in notebooks while the teacher takes attendance, then pairs compare solutions.
- **Quick write (ELA)**: "Is Shylock a villain or a victim? One paragraph." Responses are read aloud to launch discussion of *The Merchant of Venice*.
- **[Teach Like a Champion](https://teachlikeachampion.com) "Do Now" technique** — Doug Lemov's codification: posted before entry, 3–5 minutes, no instructions needed, in the same place daily.
- **Elementary morning work**: spiral-review math sheets (e.g., [Every Day Counts](https://www.rigbypub.com) calendar math routines) completed during arrival, reviewed as a whole class.
