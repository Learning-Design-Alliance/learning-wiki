# Counteract instructor implementation variation by writing learning opportunities explicitly into the curriculum or providing more explicit, personalized first-quarter TA training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
For curricula whose interactive elements are enacted by multiple instructors, the article recommends either building the intended interactive learning opportunities explicitly into the curriculum materials or giving new TAs more explicit, personalized training in their first quarter. The authors conclude: 'we either need to write these opportunities more explicitly into the curriculum, or TAs need more explicit and personalized TA training in their ﬁrst quarter of teaching.'

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Applies to curricula that depend on multiple individuals, such as graduate students, for implementation, where the enactors are not the same people who created the course.
#### Constraints
- The authors make no claim about which instructor behaviors are ideal, noting 'at this point, we are unable to identify which behaviors, if any, are ideal.'

### Target Learners
- graduate student teaching assistants in large-enrollment reformed courses

### Target Learning Goals
- similar learning opportunities for students across all sections of the same course
