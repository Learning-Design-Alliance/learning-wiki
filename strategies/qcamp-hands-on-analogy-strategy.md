# Use hands-on analogies and physical props to ground abstract quantum concepts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
QCaMP lessons repeatedly ground abstract quantum ideas in physical experience and analogy: the Bloch sphere was introduced by comparing a classical bit to "a position on a stick with two ends" and a quantum state to "a ship navigating on a globe", with students given small globes for hands-on coordinate exercises; the sit/stand game made combinatorial optimization tangible; coin boxes mimicked the BB84 protocol; and a levitating superconductor lab demonstrated the Meissner effect. This strategy makes phenomena without prerequisites accessible and, per the authors, analogies such as the measurement decision analogy "proved particularly effective, sparking thoughtful discussions among students".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor-developed physical props or kits (globes, coin boxes, superconducting disk and liquid nitrogen)
#### Constraints
- Some activities are demonstrations or analogies rather than the quantum process itself (e.g., coin boxes mimic BB84)

### Target Learners
- High school students without prior quantum physics coursework

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding quantum states, measurement, superposition, and quantum algorithms
