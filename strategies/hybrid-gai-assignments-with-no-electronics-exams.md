# Pair GAI-allowed and GAI-discouraged assignment portions with no-electronics proctored exams to incentivize independent mastery

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A course-design strategy in which each module's assignments contain both AI-allowed and AI-not-advised portions, while midterm and final exams are closed-book, proctored, and electronics-free. Students are told they will see problems like the AI-not-advised subset on the exams, which incentivizes unassisted work "without imposing a compliance-monitoring burden on the instructional staff". The course grade was heavily weighted toward the no-GAI assessments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear advance communication of which assignment portions allow GAI
- Substantial exam weighting on no-GAI assessments so students have an incentive to attain independent competence
#### Constraints
- The article reports this seemed successful overall but provides no controlled evidence of learning benefit

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in courses where GAI proficiency and domain mastery are both goals

### Target Learning Goals
- independent mastery of foundational domain knowledge
- GAI proficiency
