# Break Tasks Into Pieces

## Description
Breaking tasks into pieces means decomposing a complex task into smaller, sequenced sub-tasks, each with a clear goal, completion criterion, and checkpoint. Learners complete one piece at a time — often with a short break or review between pieces — rather than attempting the whole task in a single sitting. The strategy reduces perceived workload and overwhelm, and is especially useful for learners with attention or executive-function difficulties.

## Design Implications

Chunking reduces the working-memory and attentional demands of a task by letting learners process one segment at a time [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]. For novices, practicing isolated sub-skills lowers cognitive load during early skill acquisition [Part-task practice reduces load for novices.](../claims/part-task-practice-reduces-load-for-novices.md) [+M]. However, the pieces must eventually be re-integrated: learners also need whole-task experience to develop the coordination and transfer that isolated parts alone do not produce [Whole-task performance improves transfer.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [+M]. Small, achievable sub-goals also build a sense of progress that sustains self-efficacy and persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task analysis identifying natural sub-tasks and a sensible sequence
- Clear completion criteria and checkpoints so learners know when a piece is "done" ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) or self-check prompts)
- A visible plan or tracker showing the pieces and progress ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) or a checklist)
- Scheduled breaks or transition points between pieces

#### Constraints
- Chunks that are too small fragment the task and disrupt workflow, forcing learners to repeatedly reconstruct context [-M]
- Part-task practice alone can produce learners who can perform each piece but cannot coordinate them into fluent whole-task performance [Whole-task performance improves transfer.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [-M] — re-integration must be planned, not assumed
- Over-fragmentation can undermine autonomy and motivation for learners who prefer self-directed pacing [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~M] — offer choice in chunk size or order where possible
- Requires structured task management; learners with weak executive function may need the breakdown done *for* them initially, then [Fading](../patterns/cognitive-load-reduction-clt-scaffolding-approach.md) toward self-generated breakdowns [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Chunk size:** from single-step prompts for young or attention-challenged learners to multi-session project milestones for older students
- **Who does the breaking:** instructor-provided breakdowns, co-constructed plans, or learner-generated sub-goals (the last best supports transfer of planning skill)
- **Timing:** pieces completed in one session with micro-breaks, or distributed across sessions with spaced review [Spaced repetition improves retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S]

### Target Learners
- Learners with attention difficulties or executive-function challenges, who cannot sustain engagement across long unstructured tasks [+W]
- Novices facing complex multi-step tasks, for whom part-task sequencing reduces early cognitive load [Part-task practice reduces load for novices.](../claims/part-task-practice-reduces-load-for-novices.md) [+M]
- Learners low in self-efficacy, for whom early small wins build momentum [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Less necessary for experts, who naturally chunk tasks themselves and may find imposed breakdowns constraining [Expertise reversal effect.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Task completion and persistence on long or complex assignments
- Procedural fluency: mastering component skills before integration
- Self-regulation: learning to plan, monitor, and segment one's own work [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Process goals for novices, who benefit more from "complete this step" framing than outcome goals [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Analyze the task** — identify its component sub-tasks and their dependencies; sequence them from prerequisite to integrative ([Cognitive Load Management](../elements/cognitive-load-management.md)).
2. **Define each piece** — give each chunk a concrete goal, an estimated time, and a visible completion criterion ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
3. **Set checkpoints** — insert brief self-checks, [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) touchpoints, or peer review between pieces so errors are caught before they compound.
4. **Schedule breaks** — place short breaks at natural boundaries, not mid-chunk, to protect focus within each piece.
5. **Re-integrate** — after pieces are practiced, assign a whole-task performance so learners coordinate the parts [Whole-task performance improves transfer.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [+M].
6. **Fade the breakdown** — progressively shift responsibility for segmenting to the learner, moving from provided checklists to learner-generated plans [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

## Examples
- **Writing instruction:** a research essay broken into topic selection, source gathering, outline, draft, and revision, each with its own due date and rubric line — standard practice in process-writing curricula such as [Writer's Workshop](https://www.heinemann.com/writersworkshop/) models.
- **Khan Academy (https://www.khanacademy.org)** — long skill domains are segmented into short lessons and mastery checkpoints, so learners complete one piece before the unit-level challenge integrates them.
- **Duolingo (https://www.duolingo.com)** — language learning divided into short daily lessons with unit checkpoints; the segmented structure is central to its completion rates.
- **Special education IEP goal-setting** — annual goals routinely broken into short-term benchmarks, with progress monitored at each checkpoint.
