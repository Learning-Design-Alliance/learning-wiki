# Combine online and offline teaching (blended learning) to maximize benefits in undergraduate medical education

## Description
The review recommends blending online and offline methods to avoid the potential limitations of purely online delivery in undergraduate medical education. It states it "might be worthwhile to combine the advantages of online and offline teaching methods, called blended learning", and that a combination "might be the most effective" despite uncertainties about online learning. This is a forward-looking recommendation grounded in the review's mixed findings, not a tested manipulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Effectiveness of the online component should be supported by design principles of digital learning materials, learning goals and students' preferences and characteristics being rigorously evaluated, as the conclusion states.
#### Constraints
- The article states online learning might not work better than offline learning for topics not yet studied, and its effectiveness varies across students and contexts.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- medical knowledge and skills
