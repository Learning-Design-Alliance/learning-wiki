# CAMIL-informed design guidelines for deploying immersive VR in instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article distills five actionable guidelines: "instructors should prioritize IVR for retention-oriented goals when dealing with 3D manipulations"; blend IVR with theoretical introductions such as a short video lecture; choose natural avatar morphologies and low-overhead locomotion (head-gaze and teleportation); leverage IVR's early arousal window with clear opening goals before content carries learning; and plan multiple short exposures with pre-use tutorials to temper novelty and build control fluency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Brief pre-training or theoretical lectures plus digital, interactive hands-on sessions
- Short, repeated sessions paired with pre-use tutorials and consistent mappings across modules
#### Constraints
- Hand tracking removes controller haptics and may reduce grasp confirmations, a trade-off designers should weigh against task needs

### Target Learners
- upper-secondary and early-tertiary learners
- high-school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge retention
- factual knowledge acquisition
- engagement and motivation

### Affordances
- Camil Framework
