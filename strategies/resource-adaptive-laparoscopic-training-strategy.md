# Adapt the progressive laparoscopic program to resource-limited settings by grouping trainees per animal, expanding lower-cost ex vivo practice, and retaining core box trainer and VR modules

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend resource-adaptive adjustments to keep the program's multi-modular structure under budget constraints. They state that "High-cost modules, such as live animal surgery, are optimized by grouping trainees from different specialties to perform representative procedures in rotation, maximizing trainee exposure per animal," while lower-cost alternatives including ex vivo organ practice and high-fidelity simulated models are expanded. Core modules like box training and VR must be retained, although the frequency of animal use may be reduced.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Retain the core multi-modular structure comprising knowledge delivery, box training, VR simulation, and tissue handling/teamwork
#### Constraints
- Intended for contexts with uneven distribution of medical training resources; animal-use frequency may be reduced under constraint

### Target Learners
- CPMDRs and surgical residents in resource-limited training centers in China

### Target Learning Goals
- Laparoscopic skill acquisition and clinical translation within a limited training period
