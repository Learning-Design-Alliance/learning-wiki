Provide training and clear instructions on LinkedIn use to enhance student confidence and engagement¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study recommends that educators support students with training on the functionality of LinkedIn, which may enhance students' confidence and engagement levels. Providing clear instructions on the use of LinkedIn can mitigate challenges such as privacy concerns and lack of competencies that discourage students from engaging with social media educational communities.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors need to assess and understand student characteristics that influence engagement with social media educational communities

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • Postgraduate students
  • International students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Student engagement
  • Digital literacy

Claims¶

  • International students with prior LinkedIn experience show higher levels of engagement within the experimental LinkedIn group [+W]
  • Qualitative findings: ease of use supports engagement and confidence, but challenge must be tailored to ability and content must be culturally relatable [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use a LinkedIn group community to complement classroom learning rather than replace it
  • Build students' competence in AI-enriched environments by choosing appropriate tools, giving clear guidance, establishing role models, and offering frequent encouragement
  • Layered educator support: coaching, curated resources, low-lift entry points, and community engagement
