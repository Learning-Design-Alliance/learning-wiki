# Use the Carr-Ellis multiverse discussion as classroom material for undergraduate philosophy-of-science courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The chapter recommends the recent multiverse controversy as teaching material because it does not rely on advanced theories but is "essentially of a qualitative and philosophical nature". It names a discussion between George Ellis and Bernard Carr in Astronomy & Geophysics as a recommendable source, reporting that it "has for some years been used in courses in philosophy of science for undergraduate science students at Aarhus University, and with considerable success" and provokes much good discussion among students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A copy of the Carr & Ellis (2008) discussion in Astronomy & Geophysics
#### Constraints
- Suited to introductory cosmology teaching precisely because it avoids advanced theories; addresses a controversy whose scientific status is itself contested

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate science students in philosophy-of-science courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Discussing whether cosmological questions such as the multiverse belong to physics or metaphysics
- Critical and rational thinking about borderline scientific questions
