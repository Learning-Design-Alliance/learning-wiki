Use AI-based analysis of student log files to scaffold inquiry competencies and guide teachers in real time¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends building on log-file analytics to design guidance for inquiry learning: AI algorithms can assess a wide range of inquiry competencies and support students' learning in real time via a digital agent, and AI-driven dashboards can give teachers detailed information to guide inquiry instruction. Specific activities needing guidance include applying the Control of Variables Strategy in multivariable situations, coordinating theory and evidence, and constructing scientific explanations.

Claims¶

  • Meta-analysis of 164 studies found assisted inquiry more effective than explicit instruction [+W]
  • Well-controlled comparison studies show clear advantages of inquiry-based instruction over direct instruction for science conceptual learning [+W]
  • Generative instructional conditions (inquiry and explanation) outperformed receptive conditions on far transfer, with inquiry best on far transfer problems [+W]
  • Guided play outperformed direct instruction for executive functioning and numeracy in early childhood, but not for literacy or socioemotional outcomes [~W]
  • Large randomized evaluations of project-based learning programs show advantages over traditional science teaching of about 0.2 standard deviations [+W]
  • Successful low-prior-knowledge inquiry learners managed cognitive load by exploring simple circuits first and pausing during learning [~W]
