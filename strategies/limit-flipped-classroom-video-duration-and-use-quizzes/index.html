Limit flipped classroom video duration to around 20 minutes and use opening quizzes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article cites Hew's recommendation that "limiting the total duration of video clips in flipped classrooms to around 20 minutes is appropriate, and that using quizzes at the beginning of each class will make the flipped classroom more effective." This strategy addresses the risk that students perceive pre-class videos as a time burden, which can hinder effective participation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Flipped classroom format with pre-class video materials

Constraints¶

  • Recommendation is attributed to a cited meta-analysis rather than directly tested in this study

Target Learners¶

  • Medical students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Pre-class preparation and engagement in flipped classroom settings

Claims¶

  • Guo et al. found engagement depends on video production style: short videos, talking heads on slides, Khan-style drawing more engaging than slides or coding, classroom recording weak online [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Limit pre-class video length to 20 minutes to reduce student burden
  • Keep online lecture videos under twenty minutes and segment by topic with speed control
  • Keep pre-class teaching videos short (10–20 minutes), goal-targeted, and followed by checking homework
  • Use start-of-class quizzes on pre-class video content as a regular part of flipped in-class sessions
  • Keep video materials used for learning under 30 minutes to sustain learner attention
  • Design flipped-classroom videos with sound-image synchronization, short durations, full-body instructor visibility, and opportunities for immediate questions
