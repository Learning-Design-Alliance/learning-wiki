# Embed curricular support for co- and socially shared regulation: awareness discussions in debriefing and regulation-focused feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that elements supporting, stimulating, and facilitating regulation of collaborative learning permeate healthcare professions curricula, since most currently pay more attention to SRL than to CRL and SSRL. A crucial starting point is increasing team members' awareness of each other's knowledge, activities, emotions, motivation, and views of the group's functioning; the authors suggest stimulating discussions of own and others' regulatory processes 'during debriefing sessions of simulation‐based team training sessions'. Curricula must also include learning tasks requiring collaborative learning and its regulation, with feedback explicitly aimed at specific self-, co-, and shared regulatory processes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning tasks that require collaborative learning as well as regulation of that learning
#### Constraints
- Whether CRL is transitional towards other modes of regulation depends on team dynamics, team leadership, and task requirements

### Target Learners
- health professions students
- healthcare teams in simulation training

### Target Learning Goals
- collective regulatory competence
- awareness of own and others' regulatory processes
