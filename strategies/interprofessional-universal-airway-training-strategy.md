# Use interprofessional, simulation-inclusive training toward a universal airway management approach, blending simulation, clinical volume, and effective learning strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that training move beyond site- and specialty-specific paradigms. It states that instruction "should be focused on a blended experience of simulation, clinical volume, and effective learning strategies rather than specific sites of instruction or who is providing the training," and urges interprofessional training with staff from all acute care areas to create a shared mental model of difficult airway approaches, since teams with shared mental models are more adaptable with improved processes and outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration between specialties and regular communication between training anesthesiologists and the sending department
#### Constraints
- The review notes education within specialties typically occurs in isolation, producing different terminology, attitudes, and approaches

### Target Learners
- Nonanesthesiologist trainees in emergency medicine, critical care, and other acute care specialties

### Target Learning Goals
- Airway management technical and nontechnical skills, teamwork, and crisis management
