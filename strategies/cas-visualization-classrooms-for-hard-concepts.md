# Equip classrooms with visualization software such as CAS and DGS so students construct their own knowledge of hard concepts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend using technology to concretize mathematical concepts that are hard to learn, so that learning becomes permanent. They write that classrooms should be satisfied with computers and visualization tools such as CAS and DGS software, and that researchers can develop teaching environments that do not allow misconceptions to form in hard subjects like derivative and integration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classrooms equipped with computers and visualization software
- Software and experienced staff available in schools
#### Constraints
- The article notes that even some schools with computer facilities lack the necessary software and experienced staff

### Target Learners
- 12th-grade secondary school mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding the derivative concept and resolving derivative misconceptions
