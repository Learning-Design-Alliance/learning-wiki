# Limit support during self-diagnosis (final answer only, with notes and textbooks) before providing the detailed solution, to promote productive struggle

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 4 studies (3 causal, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
For challenging problems, the article's group S condition gave students only the final answer and let them use notes and textbooks to diagnose their errors, delaying the detailed solution until after the self-diagnosis activity. The article reports that "students were only given the final answer to the problem, and they were allowed to use their notes and textbooks for self-diagnosis, but otherwise had to struggle on their own to determine their errors". This struggle-first sequencing produced the strongest transfer benefits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A detailed solution must be provided to all students after the self-diagnosis activity is over
#### Constraints
- Instructors face the challenge of finding the right balance between limiting support to allow deep-level engagement and providing support to connect learning with prior knowledge

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students working on challenging multi-part problems

### Target Learning Goals
- transfer of problem-solving principles to similar problems
- cognitive engagement with solutions
