# Integrate the basics of handling a port catheter into anesthesiological training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on the simulated interviews, in which most anesthesiologists considered port catheter use possible but the majority rejected primary use over concerns about damaging or clogging it, the authors conclude "It seems worthwhile to integrate the basics of handling a port catheter into anesthesiological training". The recommendation rests on the point that a port can be used for anesthesia induction provided its handling is known.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- knowledge of the handling process is necessary to prevent complications
#### Constraints
- a port catheter is not suitable for administering higher volumes

### Target Learners
- anesthesiologists in training

### Target Learning Goals
- safe use of an implanted port catheter for anesthesia induction
