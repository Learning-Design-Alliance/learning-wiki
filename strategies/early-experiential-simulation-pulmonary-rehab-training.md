# Introduce structured, practical pulmonary rehabilitation content early in the physiotherapy curriculum, prioritising experiential and simulation-based teaching in airway-clearance techniques

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend curriculum change based on their finding that early-year students report the lowest self-assessed competence, especially in specialised airway-clearance techniques. They state the need "to introduce structured, practical content in this area early in the curriculum" and that "Curriculum developers and clinical educators should prioritize experiential and simulation-based teaching in pulmonary rehabilitation". The recommendation targets the competence gaps documented in their survey of 110 Polish respondents.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured practical pulmonary rehabilitation content introduced early in the physiotherapy curriculum
- Experiential and simulation-based teaching opportunities for specialised techniques such as postural drainage and manual chest techniques
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is self-reported perceived competence from a non-random, convenience-based cross-sectional survey, which the authors state does not allow causal interpretations and did not include objective assessments of practical skills

### Target Learners
- Physiotherapy students, especially early-year students
- Early-career physiotherapists

### Target Learning Goals
- Practical competence in pulmonary rehabilitation techniques for post-COVID-19 patients, particularly airway-clearance techniques
