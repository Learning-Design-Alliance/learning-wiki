# Integrate graffiti walls both before and after drop-in activities, with quantitative and thematic analysis, to demonstrate short-term impact

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy embeds a common drop-in evaluation tool, graffiti walls, at both the start and end of the activity, prompting participants to write or draw responses to a broad question. The paired datasets then support change-detection analyses such as Zipf's-law word-diversity analysis and grounded-theory thematic analysis. The authors state that "commonly used evaluation tools suitable for drop-in activities, such as grafﬁti walls, should be integrated both before and after the main activity in general, rather than only using them afterwards as is typically the case."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Graffiti walls must be integrated into the activity itself so participants respond before and after; responses must be captured and analysed with rigorous statistical or qualitative coding techniques to demonstrate change.
#### Constraints
- Surveys before and after are neither appropriate for nor commensurate with drop-in activities; post-only usage of such tools limits the ability to demonstrate change and thus impact.

### Target Learners
- young families and general public attending drop-in science engagement activities

### Target Learning Goals
- demonstrating short-term impact of drop-in public engagement on participants' language, conceptions, and perceptions of science topics
