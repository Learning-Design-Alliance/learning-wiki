# Supplement VR lessons with pre-training and follow-up reflection activities, and keep VR sessions short to limit fatigue and cybersickness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementation guidance holds that "the literature is quite clear about using VR for experiential learning and supplementing the VR experience with pre-training", and about "including reflection activities following a VR-lesson". The authors combined VR with other classroom activities such as educational games, kept sessions to 25–45 minutes with breaks, and note that longer VR sessions can lead to cognitive load and cybersickness symptoms such as eyestrain and nausea.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Short VR sessions (25–45 min) with breaks; pre-training before VR experiences; reflection activities afterwards
#### Constraints
- Full immersive VR lessons can cause cybersickness symptoms and longer sessions can increase cognitive load

### Target Learners
- Rural school children participating in VR-based social competence education

### Target Learning Goals
- Social skills practice and social knowledge acquisition through experiential VR learning
