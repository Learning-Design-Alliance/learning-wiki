# Establishing Classroom Norms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Establishing classroom norms means making expectations for behavior, participation, and academic work explicit, teachable, and shared — rather than leaving them implicit or imposed. It is typically carried out in the first days of a course through discussion, co-construction with learners, and deliberate modeling and rehearsal of the norms, followed by consistent, low-stakes reinforcement.

## Design Implications

Norms function as procedural scaffolding: they reduce the cognitive and social uncertainty of "what am I supposed to do here," freeing working memory and attention for content [Cognitive load management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md). Classroom management practices that establish and teach expectations are among the most consistent predictors of achievement and time-on-task [Hattie, 2009] [+M], and well-structured environments show fewer disruptions and more instructional time [Emmer & Stough, 2001] [+M]. Norms also shape the social fabric: a sense of belonging and psychological safety is a precondition for risk-taking behaviors such as asking questions and attempting difficult problems [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A small set (typically 3–7) of positively framed, specific norms ("build on others' ideas" rather than "be respectful")
- Explicit teaching: modeling, rehearsal, and examples/non-examples of each norm in action
- Consistent, calm reinforcement — brief redirection rather than escalating consequences
- Co-construction or at least discussion with learners, which increases buy-in and perceived autonomy [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]

#### Constraints
- Norms announced once and never revisited decay rapidly; effectiveness depends on sustained reinforcement [-M]
- Top-down rule lists without learner input or rationale reduce intrinsic motivation and produce compliance rather than internalization [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [-M]
- Norms that conflict with learners' prior classroom cultures (e.g., cold-calling in a group where silence is the norm) require explicit framing of *why*, or they generate resistance [~M]
- Overly numerous or vague norms ("respect everyone") are unteachable and unenforceable [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Co-constructed norms**: learners propose and refine norms; instructor ensures completeness and commits to upholding them
- **Instructor-set norms with rationale**: appropriate when safety, timing, or institutional policy constrains negotiation
- **Discussion norms vs. work norms**: separate protocols for [class discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) (e.g., talk moves, wait time) and for collaborative work (e.g., role assignment, accountability structures [Cooperative learning group rewards and individual accountability.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S])
- **Re-norming at transitions**: revisiting norms when group composition or task type changes (new project phase, new discussion format)

### Target Learners
- All learners, but especially novices and learners new to an institution or format (first-year, online, adult returners) who lack the tacit knowledge of "how this classroom works" [+M]
- Learners from backgrounds whose prior schooling norms differ from the instructor's assumptions; explicit norms reduce the advantage of cultural insider knowledge [~M]
- Groups where participation is uneven — norms that distribute airtime support equitable [collaborative learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural: smooth execution of routines (transitions, group work, peer review)
- Social-emotional: psychological safety, belonging, and productive discourse norms
- Self-regulation: internalized standards learners eventually apply without external enforcement

### Instructions
1. **Identify the 3–7 behaviors** your activities actually require (e.g., for [class discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md): respond to ideas, not people; make reasoning visible).
2. **Introduce norms with rationale** on day one, connecting each to a learning goal rather than to obedience.
3. **Co-construct or discuss**: invite learners to add, question, or rephrase; record the final list visibly (syllabus, poster, course homepage).
4. **Model and rehearse**: run a low-stakes activity (a [check-in](../elements/check-in.md), a quick pair-share) and name the norms in action; use non-examples to sharpen boundaries.
5. **Reinforce consistently and briefly**: redirect with a gesture or a restatement of the norm, not a lecture; apply norms to yourself visibly.
6. **Revisit and revise** at natural transitions, retiring norms that no longer serve the work.

## Examples
- **Intensive Summer institutes (e.g., HILT/Harvard Bok Center teaching consultations)** recommend posting 3–5 discussion norms and rehearsing them with a first-day pair-share before the first graded discussion.
- **Responsive Classroom** (https://www.responsiveclassroom.org) — K–8 program in which teachers and students co-create "classroom agreements" in the first six weeks, with interactive modeling of each routine.
- **Project Zero thinking routines** (https://pz.harvard.edu) — norms such as "make thinking visible" embedded in routines like *Claim–Support–Question*, giving discussion norms a concrete enactment.
