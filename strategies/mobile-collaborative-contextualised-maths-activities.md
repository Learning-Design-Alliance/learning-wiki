# Use low-cost tablets with camera, sensors and connectivity for collaborative, contextualised maths activities indoors and outdoors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's intervention used 7-8 inch Android tablets costing less than £100 each, chosen for low cost and portability, in weekly hour-long sessions over three months. Activities spanned symmetry, area and perimeter, information handling and angles, using the camera, built-in sensors and network connectivity to connect mathematics concepts to the students' environment, with work saved to a shared workspace for group discussion. The authors state the rationale was "to allow students to connect mathematics concepts to their environment, taking advantage of the different features of mobile devices".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Network connectivity stable enough for shared workspaces
- Teacher preparation to integrate activities into existing curriculum
#### Constraints
- Technical issues with connectivity and app stability disrupted some sessions
- Lesson plans lacked differentiation and had to be adapted by the teacher

### Target Learners
- primary school students aged 9-11

### Target Learning Goals
- geometry
- information handling
- area and perimeter
- angles
