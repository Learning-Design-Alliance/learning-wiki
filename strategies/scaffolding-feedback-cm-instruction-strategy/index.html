Provide scaffolding (fill in the map) plus explicit instruction and feedback when teaching concept mapping for reading comprehension¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends teaching reading comprehension strategies under scaffolding conditions with systematic feedback. It argues that "novice learners learn more from studying worked examples" and that the fill in the map group, "whose designed work was based on this strategy, performed better than the other groups". It concludes that "providing scaffolding learning condition and provision of feedback are the central components of every educational context particularly in the reading comprehension domain". In practice, students received handouts, explicit step-by-step instruction, five 20-minute practice sessions, and oral feedback each session.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Handouts defining the strategy and its applications
  • Explicit instruction following a step-by-step procedure
  • Per-session submission of assignments with oral feedback

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from one study of intermediate female pre-university students in intact groups

Target Learners¶

  • intermediate EFL pre-university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading comprehension strategy use

Affordances¶

  • Concept Map Technique Assimilation Theory

Claims¶

  • All three strategies (learner-constructed CM, fill in the map CM, and summarizing) produced significant pre-to-post gains in reading comprehension [+W]
  • ANOVA showed a significant difference in reading comprehension posttest performance among the three strategy groups [+W]
  • Students and teachers in the concept map groups reported increased motivation to use the strategy, especially in the fill in the map group [+W]
  • The fill in the map concept map group performed significantly better than the summarizing strategy group on reading comprehension posttest [+W]
  • Differences between the learner-constructed CM group and the other two groups were not statistically significant [+W]
  • Review evidence cited in the article indicates filling in a story map after reading a text is more effective than providing it before reading [+W]
