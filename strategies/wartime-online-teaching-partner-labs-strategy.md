# Sustain laboratory practice under wartime online teaching by routing students to operating labs at partner institutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
When safety requirements forced fully online instruction at Kharkiv University after the 2022 invasion, the school kept hands-on physics training alive by sending students to laboratories still operating at KIPT and at partner institutions abroad. The authors state that "it is obviously unsatisfactory for students-physicists to study without practical exercises on real physical installations" and that "This issue is resolved by attending them laboratories in KIPT and partner institutions abroad." Lecturers were also provided digital learning tools and trained in new online methods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to operating physical laboratories at partner institutions (KIPT or abroad) and digital learning tools and training for lecturers and tutors
#### Constraints
- Online-only instruction is imposed by safety requirements during the war and is described by the authors as unsatisfactory compared with in-person classroom teaching

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students studying during wartime disruptions

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental physics skills, measuring physical observables on real installations
