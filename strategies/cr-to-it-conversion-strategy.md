# Convert constructed-response problems into concept-equivalent integrated testlets by deconstructing solution elements and selecting key steps as MC items

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's appendix outlines a conversion procedure: "we started with a set of CR problems taken from past exams, deconstructed the concepts and procedures needed to solve the problem, weighted the importance and diﬃculty of each part much as one would when constructing a scoring rubric, and created four multiple-choice items that addressed one or two speciﬁc conceptual or numerical steps in the solution". The final testlet item is chosen as the intended destination of the problem, and intermediate items act as intra-question scaffolding. Distractor design should align with the assessment objectives, including traps for identifiable misconceptions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Use past-exam CR questions to assure construct and content validity; test-drive exams with teaching assistants before administration, since the IF-AT answer key is immutable
#### Constraints
- The number of items per testlet and which solution steps to include are based on time constraints and a targeted difficulty level; distractors that trap trivial procedures may be non-discriminating and should be avoided

### Target Learners
- introductory university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- assessment of concept integration and multi-step problem solving in physics
