# Introduce standardised multifaceted training programmes with competency checklists, hands-on refresher courses, and departmental protocols prioritising regional analgesia for hip fractures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends addressing the knowledge-practice gap through standardised training and system-level changes. It states that "the introduction of standardised multifaceted programmes with competency checklists could promote proficiency in RA for hip fracture pain management", supplemented by regular hands-on refresher courses for skill retention, and departmental protocols prioritising RA above parenteral analgesia to provide structure, accountability and reduced analgesia delays.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must build procedural competence; the article cites evidence that competence is achieved via repeated hands-on simulation combined with competency-based training.
#### Constraints
- The feasibility and impact of such interventions in this setting had not yet been tested and the authors propose this as the focus of future studies.

### Target Learners
- Emergency department doctors at varying training levels, particularly junior doctors

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural competence in regional analgesia (FNB/FICB) for hip fracture pain management
