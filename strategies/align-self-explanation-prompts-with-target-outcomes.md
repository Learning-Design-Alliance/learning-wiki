# Design self-explanation prompts aligned to target outcomes, favoring explanations of correct content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators prompt self-explanation of content known to be correct, and additionally of why incorrect information is incorrect, rather than of learners' own potentially incorrect ideas. It further advises that "explanation prompts must be carefully designed to align with target learn-ing outcomes," since prompts direct attention to particular information and can draw effort away from other important information.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The domain should be consistently guided by general principles or heuristics with few exceptions
- Prompt type should match the target learning outcome: why-prompts for conceptual knowledge, how-prompts for procedural transfer
#### Constraints
- Self-explanation of learners' own ideas is inadvisable unless problems are usually solved correctly or accuracy feedback is provided first

### Target Learners
- students from preschool through adulthood learning principle-guided material

### Target Learning Goals
- comprehension
- transfer of principles and procedures

- Four Constraints Self Explanation Effectiveness Framework
