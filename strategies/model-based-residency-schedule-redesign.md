# Collaborative model-based schedule analysis and redesign for residency programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that residency program directors use mathematical modeling tools to pre-screen proposed schedules quantitatively before implementation, reducing the number of alternatives to consider. In a dialog with the Boston Combined Residency Program, "the BCRP selected schedules that were assessed to be best by our methods," and the collaboration also provided education in sleep and circadian biology to residents. The authors state that "Mathematical modeling provides a quantitative comparison approach with potential to aid residency programs in schedule analysis and redesign."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A dialog with residency program officials to define schedule constraints and generate realistic scheduling alternatives, plus access to simulation software
#### Constraints
- The article notes the rationale for generating different scheduling alternatives is not incorporated in the methods, and simulation approaches cannot replace experimental approaches

### Target Learners
- Residency program directors and resident physicians in tertiary care and community hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- Designing safe, evidence-based work schedules that adhere to work-hour limits and sleep and circadian science

### Affordances
- Linked Circadian Performance Alertness Models
