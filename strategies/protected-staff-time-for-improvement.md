# Allocate protected staff time for improvement work, as highly effective industrial organisations do

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (3 qualitative, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article identifies release of frontline staff for training and project work as a fundamental barrier to improvement in NHS hospitals, since activity is kept near theoretical maximum at all times. It recommends that, like "highly effective industrial organisations and HROs" which "allocate specific staff time for improvement on a regular basis", the NHS may need to do the same to allow major improvement to occur, and argues senior management incentives need re-thinking to permit this.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Senior management policy and incentive structures that permit releasing staff time for improvement activities
#### Constraints
- Current NHS focus on keeping activity near the theoretical maximum militates against allocating such time

### Target Learners
- frontline healthcare staff in operating theatre teams

### Target Learning Goals
- participation in quality improvement and safety improvement work
