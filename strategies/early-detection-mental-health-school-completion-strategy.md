# Detect internalizing and externalizing problems from preschool age onward and adjust educational practices with adequate treatment to support school transition and completion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that child mental health problems be detected early and continuously because selection effects run from mental health to academic performance across all studied periods. It states these problems "need to be noticed at preschool age and educational practices adjusted and adequate treatment given to promote transition to the school environment and completion of compulsory school". It adds that knowledge of internalizing problems' effects may be especially important since these problems tend to be less explicit to others than externalizing ones.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal findings indicate problems should be noticed from preschool age and continuously throughout the school years
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a Swedish cohort context; no support for social causation was found in this setting

### Target Learners
- children from preschool age through compulsory school

### Target Learning Goals
- fair opportunities to education
- school transition and completion of compulsory school
