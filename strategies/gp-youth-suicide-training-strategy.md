# Deliver specialist GP education on youth suicide risk as small face-to-face group training supplemented by online resources, with young people involved in delivery

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
GPs proposed small, face-to-face group training in practice during clinical meetings, with online resources as a practical and cost-effective supplement, adapted for different learning styles and years of experience. They proposed "the involvement of young people in the delivery of such training to obtain their views and expectations from a GP consultation", and wanted training on youth-specific predictors, question lists, family involvement and brief psychological interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trainer with a good understanding of the role of GPs and experience conducting risk assessments; GPs agreed a GP with youth mental health expertise supported by a mental health provider representative would be most suitable
#### Constraints
- The study recommends this education but has not tested its effectiveness; the linked systematic review by the authors highlighted a lack of evidence-based educational interventions for GPs targeting suicide risk in youth aged 14–25

### Target Learners
- General practitioners caring for young people aged 14–25

### Target Learning Goals
- Assessing and managing suicide risk in young people
- Communicating openly with young people about suicide
- Conducting holistic psychosocial risk assessment
