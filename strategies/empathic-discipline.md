# Empathic Discipline

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Empathic discipline is a teacher-facing intervention that reframes student misbehavior as a developmental, context-driven response rather than a character flaw, and positions discipline as an opportunity to maintain the teacher–student relationship. In its canonical form it is a brief (~45-minute) online module combining non-judgmental framing of misbehavior, stories from students describing how punitive discipline damaged their sense of belonging, and short writing exercises in which teachers articulate their own empathic approach to discipline [A brief intervention reframing discipline empathically reduces suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S].

## Design Implications

The intervention targets teachers' implicit theories of misbehavior: when teachers interpret defiance as fixed and personal, they respond punitively, which erodes student trust and triggers escalating cycles of misbehavior and sanction [A brief intervention reframing discipline empathically reduces suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S]. By making students' perspectives salient — through first-person narratives and perspective-taking writing — the intervention shifts teachers toward responses that preserve [Belonging](../elements/belonging.md) while still addressing behavior. It is a mindset intervention, not a behavior-management script: it changes the *orientation* from which teachers improvise, not the specific consequences they apply.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured activity that presents misbehavior as malleable and context-driven (non-judgmental framing)
- Student-voice materials (quotes, narratives, or interviews) conveying how punitive responses affect trust and belonging
- A self-persuasion component — teachers write their own empathic philosophy or plan, generating arguments in their own words
- Administrative conditions that allow relationship-preserving responses rather than mandating exclusionary consequences

#### Constraints
- Effects depend on school context: in schools with high suspension rates or rigid zero-tolerance policies, individual teacher mindset change is buffered by institutional practice [A brief intervention reframing discipline empathically cuts suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [~M]
- A one-time brief intervention is not a substitute for ongoing training; mindset effects can decay without reinforcement through coaching, [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md), and consistent policy
- If implemented as a compliance exercise (teachers complete the module without genuine self-persuasion), the writing component loses its mechanism
- Does not address structural drivers of disproportionality (racism, resource inequity); empathic framing without systemic change risks placing the burden of reform on individual teachers

#### Implementation Variability
- Delivered online as a self-paced module (original format) or in-person as a workshop with discussion
- Extended through ongoing practice: [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md) activities, restorative-practice training, or perspective-taking prompts embedded in staff meetings
- Adapted for other relationship-intensive roles: residential staff, online instructors, teaching assistants

### Target Learners
- Teachers and instructional staff who respond to student misbehavior, especially in middle and high school where disciplinary escalation is most common [A brief intervention reframing discipline empathically reduces suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S]
- Teachers of students from historically marginalized groups, who disproportionately experience exclusionary discipline — the original trial showed the largest suspension reductions among Black and Latino students [A brief intervention reframing discipline empathically reduces suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S]
- New teachers still forming their disciplinary habits, for whom mindset framing can shape default responses before punitive patterns consolidate

### Target Learning Goals
- Professional mindset change: adopting a malleable, contextual view of student misbehavior
- Relationship-maintenance skill: responding to misbehavior in ways that preserve trust and student [Belonging](../elements/belonging.md)
- Equity-oriented practice: reducing racial disparities in exclusionary discipline

### Instructions
1. Present the non-judgmental framing: misbehavior is developmental, context-driven, and malleable — not evidence of a "bad kid."
2. Share student-voice narratives describing how punitive discipline (suspension, public reprimand) damaged students' sense of belonging and trust.
3. Have participants write, in their own words, how they will incorporate empathic responses into their discipline practice (self-persuasion; see [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md)).
4. Reinforce over time: revisit the framing in coaching conversations, [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) with students, and staff debriefs of disciplinary incidents.
5. Align school policy so empathic responses are feasible — exclusionary consequences mandated from above undermine the intervention.

## Examples
- **Okonofua, Paunesku, & Walton (2016) field trial** — A 45-minute online module for 39 middle-school math teachers cut suspensions roughly in half (from ~9.6% to ~4.8% of students) across five middle schools, with the largest gains for Black and Latino students ([PDF](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1523698113)).
- **PERTS Mindset Kit** — Free online teacher training modules from the Project for Education Research That Scales include the empathic discipline session alongside growth-mindset teaching practices ([perts.net](https://www.perts.net)).
- **Restorative justice implementations** — Districts such as Oakland Unified School District pair empathic framing with formal restorative circles, using student-voice activities similar to the original intervention's narratives.
