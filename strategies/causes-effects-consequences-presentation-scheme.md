# Present light pollution through a causes-effects-consequences scheme so the problem is not relegated to astronomers and amateur observers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors detected that light pollution is strongly associated with artificial night-sky brightness, so its consequences "suelen reducirse a un segmento de ciudadanos compuesto por astrónomos y observadores aficionados". As a divulgation strategy they propose presenting the topic through a three-part scheme treated in extenso: causes (careless, inadequate, inefficient, badly placed lighting), effects (light intrusion, glare, sky glow, energy waste), and consequences divided into cultural, economic and environmental (including biodiversity changes, circadian-rhythm alteration and melatonin suppression by blue-wavelength night light). They report that presenting the problem this way exposes its real dimension and rapidly produces awareness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each part of the causes-effects-consequences scheme must be treated in extenso when presenting the topic
#### Constraints
- The article notes the topic is strongly associated with sky glow, which risks reducing its perceived audience to astronomers and amateur observers

### Target Learners
- professionals of diverse technological areas
- university teachers
- general citizens

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing light pollution's societal and environmental impact
- awareness that mitigation is feasible and economically beneficial
