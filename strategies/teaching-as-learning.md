Constraints (must use [-] or [~]):
- If teaching is done from notes/script (knowledge-telling/parroting), benefits collapse [Roscoe & Chi 2007].
- Expecting to teach without actually teaching: still benefits, but smaller than actual teaching? Actually Nestojko found expecting to teach produced recall benefit even without teaching. Fiorella & Mayer found actual teaching added benefits beyond expecting (regarding deeper understanding). So the constraint: if the "teaching" is only preparation and the learner never actually explains, gains are limited to organization/retrieval of what was prepared — weaker on transfer.
- Teaching works poorly when tutee's questions are absent and tutor just performs rehearsed script.
- Cognitive load: teaching can overload novices; tutor errors propagate misconceptions to both tutor and tutee.
- Effective only when the tutor has sufficient baseline knowledge — teaching a topic about which the learner knows nothing produces fabrications.
- Timidity: learners with low self-efficacy may experience anxiety; peer tutor readiness.
- Group size: tutoring one-on-one vs. presenting to class.
- If assessment rewards recall, tutors revert to knowledge-telling.

Requirements:
- Learner must prepare/re-study the material with expectation of teaching.
- An authentic audience — a peer, younger student, tutee, teachable agent, or even a simulated audience — and a live explanation, not reading from script.
- Feedback or checks: opportunity to answer questions, discover gaps.
- Prompt for elaboration and monitoring: tutee questions, checklists.
- Post-teaching reflection/re-revision.

Implementation variability:
- Peer tutoring dyads, cross-age tutoring, jigsaw classroom (expert groups), teach-back protocols, teachable agents (Betty's Brain), LLM/tutoring scenarios, student-created video lessons (e.g., Khan Academy-style student screencasts; Flip (formerly Flipgrid)), "explain to a stuffed animal / rubber duck," jigsaw method, reciprocal teaching (teaching role rotation — Palincsar & Brown 1984).
- Preparing to teach vs actually teaching; teaching with vs. without feedback; synchronous vs. video recording.

Target learners:
- Learners with foundational but incomplete knowledge — the effect is strongest when tutor has some familiarity with material.
- Peer tutor and tutee both benefit; tutees often benefit as much or more in one-to-one peer tutoring (Topping 1996) [~M].
- Weaker for complete novices (risk of fabrication) and possibly for experts (no new learning).

Target learning goals:
- Deep understanding, conceptual integration, explanation-based learning
- Retrieval strengthening / long-term retention
- Metacognition: identifying gaps (self-monitoring)
- Communication skills, empathy/perspective-taking (audience awareness)
