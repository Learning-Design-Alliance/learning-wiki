# Deliver a brief confidence-building educational programme that maps patient beliefs, explains pathoanatomy positively, and uses metaphors to reduce pain focus

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy operationalises the non-injury model as a concrete clinical teaching activity. The clinician first maps the patient's perception of the back problem, then delivers information emphasising positive aspects of the MRI scan and the temporary nature of pain from high-load movements, using "several simple metaphors ... to help reinforce this message". The goal is a new insight that reduces focus on the pain condition and instils confidence in managing it. In the trial it was delivered in one to three 30-60 minute sessions plus a group session and a home-study CD.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clinician trained in the method (a medical professor delivered sessions in the trial) and time for one to three 30-60 minute individual sessions plus an optional one-hour group session.
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that adding even a little caution at the end of such a session may reduce the patient's likelihood of acquiring better coping strategies, so the message must stay consistent with the non-injury model.

### Target Learners
- adults with chronic or recurrent low back pain referred to a back centre

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing fear-avoidance beliefs
- reducing undue focus on pain
- promoting natural spinal movement
