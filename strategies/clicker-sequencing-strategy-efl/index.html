Sequence clicker use in EFL courses: a limited accustoming period, quality controls, and role swapping to sustain participation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article closes with three implementable suggestions for question–answer sessions in EFL courses. First, "instructors should allow them to use clickers for a limited period" to accustom students to responding via clickers. Second, instructors should discourage irrelevant clicker use, perhaps by controlling answer quality during a period. Third, role swapping might continue increased participation even after students discontinue clicker use.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Technological infrastructure must be set up in the classroom, including Wi-Fi, clicker software, and loaded questions, as done before this study's implementation.

Constraints¶

  • The instructor could not entirely control irrelevant clicker use despite warnings; the sample of twenty students is too small for generalization, and longer clicker-use periods are suggested to reduce the novelty effect.

Target Learners¶

  • 5th-grade secondary-school EFL students (ages 11–12)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Increasing classroom participation and response quality in EFL question–response sessions

Claims¶

  • Positive effects on participation continued after students stopped using clickers, but the quality of their answers declined [+M]
  • Both groups increased their participation rates while using clickers, whichever period of the course they used them in [+M]
  • Mann-Whitney tests found no significant difference in the number of answers between clicker and non-clicker groups in either experimental period [-M]
  • Reluctant students' response rates increased more than those of active students when each group began using clickers [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Implement clickers grade-free, with a classroom set and limited frequency, to support positive student perceptions
