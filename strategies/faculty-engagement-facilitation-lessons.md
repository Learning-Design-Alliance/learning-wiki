# Facilitation lessons for sustaining faculty engagement in course transformation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article distills facilitation lessons for keeping faculty engaged in transformation: explain the process early and often, listen to faculty, allow faculty to modify the process but not its central elements, provide examples, introduce new language slowly, and support change from the outside. A broad theme is that "the input and perspectives of the faculty are important and should be respected." These tactics mitigated challenges that could have disengaged faculty.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator who keeps discussion productive within the confines of the process
- Institutional supports such as grants, administrative support, and outside speakers
#### Constraints
- Formalizing the process into subcommittees limits which faculty can engage, though it legitimizes the process in a department of about 65 faculty

### Target Learners
- Physics faculty participating in course transformation

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained faculty engagement in developing shared vision for course change
