# Use introductory physics courses as a venue for improving calculus fluency and transfer skills, with attention to self-efficacy effects on women

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends leveraging introductory physics courses, whose curricula emphasize quantitative problem solving, to improve fluency with applied mathematics. It states "Introductory physics courses are the ideal venue for improv ing fluency with applied mathematics" and that "Providing opportunities to improve calculus fluency and transfer skills will likely have the largest benefit for students with weaker secondary mathematics preparatio n". The study's own course coordinated calculus and physics scheduling so students used calculus continually, and the authors caution that the calculus emphasis did not help men's calculus self-efficacy and negatively impacted women's.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordinated scheduling of calculus and introductory physics courses so students use calculus continually in physics
#### Constraints
- The calculus emphasis does not appear to impact the calculus self-efficacy of men and negatively impacts the calculus self-efficacy of women

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in calculus-based introductory physics, particularly students from traditionally underserved populations

### Target Learning Goals
- applied calculus proficiency and transfer of calculus skills to physics
