Condense curriculum development into three phases — needs assessment, instructional strategies, evaluation — using multiple needs assessments to select strategies¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The authors condensed Thomas et al's six-step approach, combined with the ADDIE approach, into three phases: "1. Needs assessment (combining problem identification, needs assessments, and goals and objectives) 2. IS and 3. Evaluation (combining implementation and evaluation)". Four needs assessments (literature search, milestone/EPA review, learner survey, in-service exam review) identified core deficits that determined the instructional strategies chosen. Providing detail about the process is offered as making the curriculum more generalizable and easier to reproduce or adapt.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Conducting needs assessments (literature, accreditation milestones/EPA review, learner survey, exam score review) before selecting instructional strategies
  • Linking each competency-based curricular goal with an evaluation strategy

Constraints¶

  • The article notes there is little evidence guiding choice of instructional strategies within SBL

Target Learners¶

  • pediatric emergency medicine fellows
  • graduate medical education trainees in simulation-based programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • alignment of curricular goals with instructional strategies and evaluation
  • filling identified training gaps such as procedural competence and crisis resource management

Related Strategies¶

  • Planned future curricular changes: more ultrasound integration, in-situ simulations with interprofessional recruits, and a peer-to-peer medical education component
