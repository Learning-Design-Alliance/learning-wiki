# Design XRCL applications by building on fundamental CSCL pedagogy research, viewed through the lens of TICOL

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that designers of XRCL applications ground their pedagogical techniques in the established CSCL literature on scripts, predefined roles, and other techniques shown to enhance social interaction, interpreting that literature through TICOL's factors. The authors state: "We therefore suggest that the design of XRCL applications build on the fundamental research that has been conducted in CSCL, viewed through the lens of TICOL". They also note XR enables novel pedagogical techniques impossible in less immersive media, such as recording, pausing, and re-entering a recorded avatar interaction for reflective generative learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pedagogical techniques such as scripts, role assignment, peer feedback, and representational tools adapted to what the technological features of the XRCL system allow
#### Constraints
- The authors expect CSCL findings to generalize to XRCL but acknowledge this is an expectation; XRCL-specific techniques remain largely untested

### Target Learners
- distributed learners collaborating through head-mounted displays

### Target Learning Goals
- productive cognitive and socio-emotional social interaction during collaborative learning

### Affordances
- Ticol Theory Immersive Collaborative Learning
