# Integrate experiential critical care training into pharmacy curricula in LMICs using small groups and graduated hands-on roles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend embedding a structured, experiential critical care course in pharmacy curricula in low-to-middle-income countries. The course pairs daily lectures with ward rounds, case-based scenarios, and supervised task performance, "starting with merely observing and then performing small tasks under their supervision", in small groups of six or seven trainees. They further recommend replication in multi-centers and designing a standardized critical care experiential learning program.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Professional clinical pharmacy preceptors and an academic supervisor
- A supportive hospital administration providing critical care unit access
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single-site pre-post study with 72 participants and only 10 days of follow-up; the authors call for larger, longer, multi-center studies

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and postgraduate pharmacy students in LMICs

### Target Learning Goals
- critical care knowledge
- self-efficacy and confidence for critical care practice
