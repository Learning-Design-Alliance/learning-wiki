Teach the synthesis strategy within music theory curricula to simplify learning polyrhythms on piano and other instruments¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on their results, the researchers recommend that "the strategy of rhythmic organization through synthesis be taught in the context of music theory curricula and be used to simplify playing and learning the piano and other instruments." The strategy restructures difficult polyrhythmic notation into organized partitions so unequal structures can be divided equally, and the study's experimental group learned it in a two-hour session followed by a week of metronome practice.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Direct instruction covering the strategy's eight operational phases, mental pre-rhythmic organization and metric modulation, plus independent practice with a metronome

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from a single small experiment (N = 18) with second-year music education undergraduates on five polyrhythmic structures

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate music education students
  • piano and instrument learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • performing difficult polyrhythmic structures

Affordances¶

  • Organizing Rhythmic Structures Through Synthesis

Claims¶

  • The experimental group improved significantly from pre-test to post-test after learning the synthesis strategy [+M]
  • Item-level analysis attributes the experimental group's gains specifically to the target polyrhythmic structures (3:2, 4:3, 8:3, 5:4, 3:5) [+M]
  • Teaching the strategy of organizing rhythmic structures through synthesis produces significantly higher polyrhythmic tapping post-test scores than traditional training [+M]
