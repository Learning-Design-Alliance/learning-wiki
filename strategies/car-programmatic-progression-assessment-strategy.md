# Apply the CAR at the programmatic level to capture learner progression in conclusion-making across a curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the CAR can identify positive shifts in students' abilities to make conclusions as they gain more experience in process-of-science courses, the article recommends applying it at the programmatic level. Departments could systematically choose key components that highlight specific course learning objectives as students progress across a series of courses, and assess students' abilities to develop a comprehensive conclusion using all key components in a culminating capstone course. One reviewer noted it "would be very effective at measuring progress between introductory and upper level biology courses."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course-level learning objectives should guide which key components are assessed at each stage
- A sliding scale may be used to modify expectations for 'excellent' in a first-year course compared with a capstone course
#### Constraints
- Key components #1 and #2 were found to be more difficult to assess and might be good candidates for generating a sliding scale

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology students progressing through a multi-semester curriculum

### Target Learning Goals
- conclusion-making progression across a curriculum
- programmatic assessment of process-of-science skills
