# Institutions should mine existing LMS login data to map social jet lag and guide schedule choices that minimize its negative impact on learning outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes using pre-existing learning management system login records as a low-cost, large-scale data source for mapping individual- and population-level social jet lag. The authors state that "online records can be used to map individual- and population-level SJL, allow deep mining for patterns across demographics, and could guide schedule choices in an effort to minimize SJL's negative impact on learning outcomes". Because LMS data are generated in-house, they avoid the privacy and security issues of outside data sources and could support within-campus chrono-counseling.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to LMS login timestamp data combined with student demographic and academic records from institutional systems
- Sufficient login frequency to generate reliable individual activity profiles
#### Constraints
- LMS login data do not contain high enough login frequencies to reliably generate actograms capturing an individual's phase every single day
- Logins are proxies for circadian information, not direct biological measures like sleep or melatonin levels

### Target Learners
- University students
- Late chronotype students (owls)

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance (GPA)
- Schedule optimization to reduce social jet lag
