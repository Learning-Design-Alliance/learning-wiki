Six strategies for training teams, including cross-training and guided self-correction¶

Description¶

The article lists six strategies used to train teams, attributed to Landon et al. (2016): Event-Based/Scenario-Based Training, Self-Correction/Guided Self-Correction Training, Cross-Training, Stress Training, Team Adaptation and Coordination Training, and Team Building. Cross-training trains "teams on the tasks of different team members in order to foster a shared understanding of how each contributes to the team objectives". Guided self-correction has teams review past performance, self-diagnose, and create improvement plans.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Delivery should be fitted to training objectives; the article notes some capabilities are better trained in simulation and others in field exercises

Constraints¶

  • When teams are geographically dispersed, the article states there is a limit on the level of training that can be achieved in terms of team performance

Target Learners¶

  • military and organizational teams, including newly assembled pick-up teams

Target Learning Goals¶

  • team coordination and adaptation
  • shared understanding of member roles
  • stress recognition and self-correction skills

Claims¶

  • Training team leaders to present pre-briefs and interactive post-briefs significantly improved both leader capabilities and team performance [+W]
  • Teams with shared mental models synchronize more easily, while teams with inconsistent models suffer ineffective team processes [+W]
  • Team training delivered in the anticipated operational context with realistic scenario-based experiences is more likely to transfer to operational performance [+W]
