# Explain in-situ simulation clearly in lay language during consent or introduction to increase patient acceptance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends transparent communication about what in-situ simulation is. It notes "Most patients are unfamiliar with simulation but become more accepting once it is clearly explained" and that "Transparent communication helps minimise dissatisfaction related to extended waits." In the study, in-situ training was explained during consent in lay terms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-situ simulation must be explained in lay language so patients understand it is staff training
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that despite lay language, in-situ simulation training may have been misunderstood by survey participants

### Target Learners
- emergency department patients
- waiting-room patients at surveyed EDs

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding what in-situ simulation is
- acceptance of training-related delays
