# Policy strategy: promote adequate sleep duration and screen for sleep duration to safeguard adolescent students' physical health alongside academic scores

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that government and education departments prioritize students' physical health alongside academic scores, stating that "screening for sleep duration may be beneficial in promoting overall wellness among adolescent students." It grounds this in its finding that students obtained less than 8.5 hours of sleep per night, below the recommended minimum of 9 hours, and proposes a comprehensive reform of the educational system shifting focus from grades to well-being.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Education departments able to formulate policy interventions aimed at promoting adequate sleep duration for students and alleviating their academic burden
#### Constraints
- The article notes the seemingly positive academic effect of shorter sleep comes at the cost of compromised physical health, including increased risk for overweight and obesity

### Target Learners
- Chinese adolescent students aged 12–14 in rural and urban schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance in Chinese, mathematics, and English
- Physical health and sleep adequacy
