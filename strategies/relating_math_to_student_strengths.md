# Relating Math to Student Strengths

## Description
This strategy leverages learners’ existing strengths and interests to make math more relatable and engaging. By connecting math concepts to learners’ passions, instructors can increase confidence and motivation. For example, if some students love playing basketball. Teachers can relate percentages to the basketball statistics students often look at, which allows students to feel more confident with the content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need to be aware of learners’ interests and be able to make meaningful math connections. May require additional time and effort to identify and create relevant examples.
#### Constraints
- Some learners may not have clearly defined interests or strengths. Connections may seem forced if not carefully aligned with content and learner needs.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learners, especially those who struggle with math or have negative attitudes toward the subject.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance learner motivation, engagement, and confidence in math. Demonstrate the relevance of math to learners’ lives and interests.

### Affordances
- Increases learner engagement by tapping into intrinsic motivation. Builds confidence by leveraging learners’ expertise in other areas. Provides opportunities for learners to see themselves as capable math learners.

### Personalization
- Tailored to individual learner interests and strengths. Instructors should gather information about learners’ passions and find relevant math connections.
