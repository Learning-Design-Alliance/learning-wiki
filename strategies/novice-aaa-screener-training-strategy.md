# Use maximal coronal-plane measurement plus referral safeguards and continuing quality improvement when training novices as AAA screeners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that screening programmes employing novice trainees take a maximum measurement from a view encompassing a continuous section of the aorta rather than segmental transverse diameters, and pair novice screening with safeguards. It states that "a continuing quality improvement programme should be required which monitors performance as well as the inclusion of safe guards in the screening protocol to reduce near misses", including referral of difficult scans for re-evaluation and monitoring perceived difficulty scores and referral rates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A referral protocol sending scans rated difficult for second assessment by an experienced sonographer
- A continuing quality improvement programme monitoring individual screener performance
#### Constraints
- The article notes the study's one-on-one evaluation design over 200 scans "might not be pragmatic for a training programme" and that proposed quality measures are time and resource intensive

### Target Learners
- novices without health professional qualifications or imaging experience being trained as AAA screeners

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate ultrasound measurement of the abdominal aortic diameter for aneurysm screening
