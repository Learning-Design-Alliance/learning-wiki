# Train students and secure IT resources before deploying mathematical e-assessments, and design tasks that combine utility with enjoyment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' design recommendations for effective online mathematical assessment: give students adequate training before they use the assessment engines, check the hardware and software circumstances under which assessments have problems, steer students to sufficient IT resources, and design tasks students find enjoyable as well as useful. They also recommend studying which tasks students find enjoyable or dislike, and note feedback needs to be good but proved less important than expected.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adequate student training before using the assessment engines
- Sufficient IT resources that deal with the assessments well
- Well-designed assessment tasks
#### Constraints
- Developing online assessments for material beyond the first year of the English university curriculum remains a largely unsolved problem
- Feedback quality, while needed, is less important to acceptance than the authors initially expected

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduate STEM students

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective use of online mathematical assessments
- Positive attitude and usage intention toward e-assessment
