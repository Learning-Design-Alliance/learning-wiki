# Specific Technique Identification

## Description
Providing specific guidance on what to look for in a text, naming sentences or words whenever possible, helps students learn from the text. The teacher should point out specific examples and explain their significance, rather than leaving students to learn on their own. Questions with quotes or line numbers can be more accessible.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Appropriate reading material. Preparation of specific examples and explanations. Time for analysis and discussion in class.
#### Constraints
- May be overly directive. Students may become too reliant on teacher guidance. Requires careful planning and implementation to avoid spoon-feeding.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All grade levels, particularly beneficial for struggling learners and those new to literary analysis

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve understanding of writing techniques, enhance ability to analyze and interpret text, develop close reading skills, reduce cognitive load for struggling learners

### Affordances
- Provides clear and direct guidance. Reduces cognitive load by focusing attention on specific elements of the text. Increases confidence in analyzing complex texts.

### Personalization
- Specific techniques can be tailored to student needs and interests. Examples can be chosen from a variety of texts and genres. Scaffolding can be provided for struggling learners.
