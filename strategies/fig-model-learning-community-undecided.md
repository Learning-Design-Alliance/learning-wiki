# Use a Freshman Interest Group (FIG) model learning community as a resource-parsimonious retention intervention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes implementing the learning community intervention in its most minimal form using a Freshman Interest Group (FIG) model, in which cohorts of 20 or fewer undecided FTIC students co-enroll in the FYS plus two required courses. Following Habley, Bloom, and Robbins (2012), the authors note this LC approach is "most parsimonious with regard to resources and organizational overhead," and it produced the numerically highest retention rates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An on-line registration system that bundles the course sections together so students must register for the LC course sequence first.
- Academic advising staff to counsel students into an LC during summer orientation.
#### Constraints
- Curricular integration featuring coordinated assignments and collaborative pedagogy were not part of the intervention, which the authors suggest might explain the lower GPA and good-standing outcomes relative to the FYS alone.
- Learning communities require more time and resources to implement, even in their simplest form.

### Target Learners
- Undecided first-time-in-college (FTIC) students

### Target Learning Goals
- Increased retention to subsequent semesters
- Peer support through cohort course enrollment
