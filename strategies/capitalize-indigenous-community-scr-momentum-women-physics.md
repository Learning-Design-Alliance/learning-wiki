# Capitalize on rising physics continuation in communities with larger Indigenous populations when designing interventions for women in physics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A forward-looking recommendation from the discussion: because larger Indigenous community shares predict both higher physics continuation and a smaller gender gap, interventions aimed at women in physics should build on this existing momentum rather than treat all communities uniformly. The article states: "Future interventions to promote women in physics should capitalize on the momentum of increased SCR for larger Indigenous populations, while also being mindful of the gendered differences in continuation." It rests on the study's correlational school-level findings, not on a tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School-community demographic data (Indigenous population share) to identify communities where the momentum exists
#### Constraints
- The supporting findings are descriptive correlations without established causality; the gendered differences in continuation must be monitored alongside any intervention

### Target Learners
- Female high school students in Ontario communities with larger Indigenous populations

### Target Learning Goals
- Increasing continuation into senior high school physics and fostering gender equity in physics education
