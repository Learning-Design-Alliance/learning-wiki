Keep mathematics teaching and evaluation gender bias-free and give boys and girls the same opportunities and challenges¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (2 causal, 2 associational, 1 qualitative), q2–q3 · 1 of 5 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that "Mathematics teaching and evaluation strategies should be gender bias -free" and that there is a need to give boys and girls exactly the same opportunities and challenges in the mathematics class. It further urges teacher professional development programs to advise teachers on approaches that avoid disadvantaging particular groups of girls or boys, and joint work by male and female teachers toward a socially just, inclusive approach creating equal opportunities for all students.

Context¶

Target Learners¶

  • male and female secondary school mathematics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • equitable mathematics achievement
  • active participation of female students in mathematics learning

Claims¶

  • Male and female students taught algebra with problem-based learning show no statistically significant difference in achievement scores [+M]
  • Male and female students taught algebra with problem-based learning show no statistically significant difference in retention scores [+M]
  • Boys rate their mathematics ability under challenge significantly higher than girls at every level of observed mathematics ability, including the highest (p < 0.001) [+M]
  • Under authentic learning, girls outperformed boys at posttest although both genders improved [~M]
  • Tenth grade perceived mathematics ability under challenge is the most influential predictor of advanced high school science course completion, and girls have 24.0% lower odds than boys of completing the most advanced science courses [+M]
  • Girls report higher initial mathematics enjoyment and boys higher initial boredom, with no gender differences in arithmetic skill development or mathematics performance [+M]
  • Two gendered classroom cultures coexisted: rule-following vocal girls and disruptive boys receiving disproportionate time and space [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use problem-based learning as an additional mathematics teaching strategy to overcome the male image of mathematics
  • Introduce computer science to girls in elementary school, before gender-interest stereotypes take root
