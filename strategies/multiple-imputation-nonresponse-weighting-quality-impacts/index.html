Use multiple imputation and non-response probability weighting to estimate unbiased impacts on classroom quality¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

As a methodological next step, the authors propose using multiple imputation and non-response probability weighting to estimate the impacts of random assignment to Head Start on quality, and comparing those results with the multinomial logit and Tobit estimates reported here. The goal is to find the most appropriate method given the high amount of missing data while utilizing the continuous quality data for greater precision.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Methods must handle the high amount of missing data on the quality measure while utilizing the continuous quality data, which allows for greater precision of final impacts.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • 3- and 4-year-old children in early childhood programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accurate estimation of program impacts on classroom quality and child school readiness skills

Claims¶

  • The original Head Start Impact Study threshold-variable method conflates low quality with missing data and discards continuous variance [+W]
