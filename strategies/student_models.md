# Student Models

## Description
Student models are examples of writing produced by other students, typically curated by instructors and made available for learners to reference. They provide concrete illustrations of effective writing strategies, structures, and techniques within a specific context, such as a particular genre or assignment. Student models help learners understand expectations, develop critical analysis skills, and improve their own writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collection of well-written student models, structured analysis activities, instructor guidance to ensure learners focus on relevant aspects of the models.
#### Constraints
- Learners may blindly imitate models without critical thinking. Models might not align with all writing contexts or learner needs. Over-reliance on models can stifle creativity and originality.

### Affordances
- Provides clear and relatable examples of effective writing. Supports understanding of assignment expectations. Enhances analytical skills through comparison and critique. Promotes self-improvement through observation and imitation.

### Personalization
- Models can be tailored to different genres, skill levels, and individual learner needs. Learners can select models based on their specific writing challenges or interests. Flexible analysis methods accommodate varied learning styles.

## Assessment Evidence
- Improvements in learner writing quality as evidenced by better structure, clarity, and use of techniques observed in the models. Increased self-awareness and critical analysis skills demonstrated through reflective writing and peer feedback.

## Impact
- Providing student models helps learners understand expectations and see what effective writing looks like in practice. Observing and analyzing models helps learners internalize effective writing practices, leading to improved writing outcomes.
