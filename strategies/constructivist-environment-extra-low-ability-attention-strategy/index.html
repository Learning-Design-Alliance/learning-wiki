Create a constructivist learning environment in basic science while giving extra attention and tutorials to low ability students¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Based on its findings, the article recommends that basic science teachers create the constructivist learning environment because both ability groups achieved more in it, while deliberately supporting low ability students. The article states teachers "should consider the low ability students and ensure they are given extra tutorials to reduce the gap existing between the high and low ability". It further calls for research on ways to reduce this gap within constructivist classes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers trained to create the constructivist environment and follow structured lesson guides

Constraints¶

  • The high-low achievement gap was not closed by either learning environment in this study

Target Learners¶

  • junior secondary basic science students of high and low ability

Target Learning Goals¶

  • basic science achievement

Claims¶

  • Students taught basic science in a constructivist learning environment achieved significantly more at posttest than students taught in a transmissive learning environment [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use alternative labels such as new learning environments instead of constructivist learning environments to avoid false expectations
  • Adopt constructivist/inquiry strategies (7E and CBL) in chemistry teaching with adequate teacher preparation
