# Design future DCM evaluations with longer follow‐up, more proximal outcome measures and more stressed staff samples

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their null findings, the authors recommend changes to how person‐centred care interventions such as DCM are evaluated in intellectual disability care. They state that "a longer follow‐up period may be useful, as a transition to more person‐centred care may require more time than provided by the follow‐up of our study". They further recommend in‐depth interviews or direct observation, including more stressed staff (for example via lower staff/resident ratios), examining resident outcomes such as quality of life as more proximal measures, and using outcome measures more closely related to the intervention such as quality of care and quality of staff–client interactions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Evaluation designs should allow sufficient time for a transition to person‐centred care, which may exceed the 14‐month follow‐up used here
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a study whose null result the authors themselves say deserves further study and may reflect ceiling effects

### Target Learners
- care staff in intellectual disability group homes
- researchers evaluating person‐centred care interventions

### Target Learning Goals
- person‐centred caring skills
- quality of staff–client interactions
