# Graduated prompts training hierarchy: general metacognitive prompts, then explicit cognitive prompts, then direct scaffolding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The dynamic training procedure provides standardized prompts in a "gradual, hierarchic fashion when independent problem-solving does not lead to an accurate solution". It starts "with general, metacognitive prompts", then provides "a more explicit, cognitive prompt that emphasized the specific transformations in the series", and finally gives "direct guidance by scaffolding". Correct answers received positive feedback and a request to explain the choice. Prompt provision is determined by the child's needs, which the authors say yields more information about the problem-solving process than conventional testing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained examiners or a computerized system capable of delivering the predetermined prompt sequence in order
#### Constraints
- Training in this study consisted of two short sessions of six items each, ordered difficult to easy; the authors note session length was limited by school hours

### Target Learners
- second-grade children in primary education

### Target Learning Goals
- inductive reasoning (series completion) accuracy and strategy use
