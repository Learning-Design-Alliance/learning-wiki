# Recommendations for oral and maxillofacial surgery residency training: simulation-based skill modules, mandatory trainee-status disclosure with patient education, and stronger undergraduate basic clinical skills training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendations for closing the gap between residents' learning needs and patient expectations: develop standardized training modules focused on enhancing practical skills through "simulation-based training, virtual reality tools, and structured feedback mechanisms"; promote transparency by requiring residents to "disclose their training status" and educating patients about trainee involvement; strengthen undergraduate basic clinical skills education; and explore hybrid learning models combining theoretical instruction with hands-on practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional policies requiring residents to "disclose their training status" and patient education about the benefits of trainee involvement
- Simulation-based and virtual-reality training resources with structured feedback mechanisms
#### Constraints
- The supporting findings come from a single-center study whose "single-center design and relatively small sample size may limit the generalizability of the findings"
- Reliance on self-reported resident data and patient dissatisfaction feedback may introduce bias

### Target Learners
- Dental residents in standardized residency training in oral and maxillofacial surgery
- Medical and dental undergraduates preparing for residency

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving practical diagnostic and treatment skills in outpatient oral and maxillofacial surgery
- Building patient trust and doctor-patient communication
