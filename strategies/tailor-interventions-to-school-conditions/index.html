Tailor school interventions to school conditions and prior performance rather than applying uniform mandated interventions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because the study found treatment-effect heterogeneity by implementation year and treatment propensity, the authors call for moving beyond a "one size fits all" approach to mandated interventions. The article states the quantitative results "call for qualitative analysis that addresses why and how 'one size fits all' approach to mandated interventions did not work at the school level," and that the findings "have implications for both theory of action and program implementation." Intervention design should account for schools' pre-existing conditions and capacity.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Attention to school-level contextual factors — prior performance status, resources, and demographic risk variables — that the study found differentiated successful (Exit) from unsuccessful (Watch, Fail) treatment schools

Constraints¶

  • State education agencies face limitations in their own fiscal, administrative, and technical capacities for building school improvement capacity, per the article's review

Target Learners¶

  • Students in schools identified as in need of improvement under accountability systems

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improving school-level achievement in reading/ELA and mathematics

Affordances¶

  • Nclb Test Driven Accountability Theory Of Action

Claims¶

  • Differential analysis of the five NCLB intervention stages found null or negative effects, including significantly lower gains for SES schools in Grade 4 ELA and for restructuring schools in Grade 8 ELA [+W]
  • Treatment effects varied by propensity of treatment assignment: schools with better pre-treatment conditions (lower propensity) gained more, with a significant negative intervention-by-propensity interaction in Grade 8 math [+W]
  • Intervention effects varied by year of implementation: positive intervention-by-year interactions suggest schools treated in later NCLB years produced better achievement gains [+W]
  • After propensity-score matching, NCLB accountability interventions showed negative or null effects on New York schools' Performance Index gains, with only Grade 8 math showing a small positive effect [+W]
