Interventions targeting educational expectations in low SES families and schools may help bridge the achievement gap¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review suggests that exploring the effectiveness of interventions targeting educational expectations in low SES families and schools in disadvantaged neighborhoods, such as workshops or counseling programs to raise the educational expectations of parents, children, and teachers without imposing undue academic pressure on youth, may offer a promising direction for future research in bridging the achievement gap.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interventions should avoid imposing undue academic pressure on youth.

Constraints¶

  • Further research is needed to confirm these findings across cultural contexts.

Target Learners¶

  • Children from low SES families
  • Students in disadvantaged neighborhoods

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Academic achievement

Claims¶

  • Educational expectations, classroom and school environment, and teacher–student relationships mediate the association of SES with academic achievement [+M]
  • Preschool attendance, home learning activities, and parental support buffer the association between low SES and lower cognitive and language outcomes [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Interventions targeting cognitive stimulation may help reduce SES-related cognitive and educational disparities
  • Offer Services and Events to Bring Parents into the School
  • Prefer subsidizing targeted summer programs or low-income families' program tuition over modified year-round school calendars
  • Promoting teacher–student relationships for children from low SES backgrounds may be a promising intervention avenue
