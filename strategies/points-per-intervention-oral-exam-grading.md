# Points-per-intervention grading scheme to standardize oral exam scoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
To reduce subjectivity, each question is worth 10 points and each category 30. "students start with a perfect 10/10 points, but each time I intervene, they lose a point." Interventions are hints, corrections, or prompts; more major errors are punished more because they require more interventions. Self-corrections, clarifying questions, and errors unrelated to the question are not penalized, and grading is completed in real time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standardized scoring system must be defined before the assessment begins
- Instructor must take care not to provide some students with more information than others, since hints are difficult to plan ahead of time
#### Constraints
- Questions must have clearly defined answers, since "evaluation based on open-ended questions and the depth of the answer invites more opportunities for bias than a single correct answer regardless of depth"

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics and astronomy majors

### Target Learning Goals
- standardized assessment of conceptual mastery
