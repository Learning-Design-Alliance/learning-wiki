# Academic counseling that builds adolescent girls' academic confidence and parental involvement in educational activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 6 studies (4 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 3 of 6 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that being female and coming from lower socio-educational contexts negatively predicted achievement, the authors recommend "strategies of academic counseling in which the adolescent girls are empowered to generate confidence in their academic skills", since academic confidence predicts achievement. They further recommend involving parents in educational activities and offering them upbringing tools to support, encourage and direct education and emotional regulation of their children, because home is a strong predictor of achievement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Counseling programs that empower adolescent girls' confidence in academic skills
- Parental participation and upbringing tools offered to families
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a single public school sample in Bogotá; the authors call for broader demographic inclusion of more public and private schools

### Target Learners
- adolescent high-school students, especially girls

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement
- academic confidence
- emotional regulation supported at home
