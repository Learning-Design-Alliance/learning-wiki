# Address generative AI in higher education through assessment redesign and proportional reflective practices rather than surveillance or punitive controls

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that institutions govern generative AI use by redesigning assessment so that meaningful reflection is credibly valued, rather than relying on detection or punishment. Reflective assessment requires students to articulate, justify, and critically evaluate how AI tools contributed to their work, and the reward for reflection must be proportionate to the effort it demands. The article states this provides "a formal, quantitative foundation for recent calls within higher education to address generative AI through assessment redesign and proportional reflective practices, rather than through increased surveillance or punitive controls".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reflective tasks must be credibly aligned with learning goals and their assessment value must be proportionate to the cognitive and time demands they impose
#### Constraints
- The article notes reflective incentives fail when reflection effort is high relative to its reward, and that the model's predictions are not yet empirically validated

### Target Learners
- higher education students

### Target Learning Goals
- responsible, transparent, and pedagogically aligned engagement with generative AI
