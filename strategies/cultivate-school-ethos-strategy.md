# Strengthen school ethos through value-based policies and practices to support achievement and, indirectly, wellbeing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advocates that schools, including sociodemographically disadvantaged ones, systematically implement and enforce policies that strengthen shared values and normative practices. It states that "by cultivating positive common beliefs, values and norms at a school, it may be possible to promote positive academic outcomes, regardless of the students' family background and the school's sociodemographic composition". The wellbeing benefit is indirect, operating through higher average achievement and lower school-level distress.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School leadership that establishes values and principles guiding policy and practice at a higher level in the school structure
#### Constraints
- Cross-sectional design limits causal interpretation; the direct ethos-distress path was not significant, so wellbeing benefits run through achievement

### Target Learners
- Ninth-grade students in lower secondary schools, including those in sociodemographically disadvantaged schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement
- Reduction of psychological distress

### Affordances
- School Ethos Teacher Level Construct
