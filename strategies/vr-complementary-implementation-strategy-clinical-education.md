# Implement VR in clinical education as a complement with robust infrastructure, faculty training, and short adaptive sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends positioning VR "not as an alternative but as a complementary training method," and advises institutions to invest in robust technical infrastructure, provide ongoing training and support for students and faculty, and continuously update VR content. For VR sickness, it advises starting "with short sessions, adjust headset settings for comfort, take breaks, avoid intense content."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Robust technical infrastructure, ongoing training and support for students and faculty, and continuous evaluation and updating of VR content
#### Constraints
- High cost of acquiring and maintaining VR hardware and software remains a major constraint for institutions with limited budgets; limited haptic feedback constrains substituting physical training

### Target Learners
- clinical students and lecturers in health care professionals' education

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical skills training and assessment integration
