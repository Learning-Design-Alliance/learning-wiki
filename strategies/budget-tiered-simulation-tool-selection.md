# Prioritize layered models for budget-limited institutions and reserve VR simulators for advanced individualized training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the trial's cost-effectiveness discussion, the authors recommend a tiered adoption strategy for preclinical simulation. They conclude that "institutions with limited budgets may prioritize layered models as a more accessible yet pedagogically valid alternative, while VR could be selectively used for advanced individualized training." This follows their observation that layered models are more cost-effective and logistically feasible for repeated group use, whereas VR requires substantial initial investment and technical maintenance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness that VR simulators require substantial initial investment and technical maintenance, and that effectiveness depends on integration into existing curricula and simulation quality
#### Constraints
- The article warns that inadequate faculty training in simulator use can hinder effective adoption, and that high costs can be a barrier for some institutions

### Target Learners
- Preclinical dental students in institutions with constrained budgets

### Target Learning Goals
- Manual dexterity and cavity depth control in restorative dentistry
