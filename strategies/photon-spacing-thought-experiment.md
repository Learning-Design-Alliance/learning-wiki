# Use a starlight photon-spacing thought experiment to reveal how far apart photons are

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Students first calculate spacing between successive Nerf bullets using distance = speed × time, then extend the same logic to real photons from a 6th magnitude star. The article reports that "Thus when a photon from a 6th magnitude star enters our eye, the next one is 1000 km away!" This thought experiment emphasises the weirdness of quantum behaviour and prepares students to see that photons cannot themselves be adding and subtracting like waves in interference.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students can apply the formula distance = speed × time and know the speed of light and approximate photon fluxes for stellar magnitudes
#### Constraints
- The discussion assumes photons are really particle-like, which the article notes is not necessarily true for real photons

### Target Learners
- school students in early years of science education

### Target Learning Goals
- calculating spacing between photons and appreciating the discreteness of light
