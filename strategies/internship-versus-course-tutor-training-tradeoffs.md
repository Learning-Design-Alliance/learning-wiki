# Weigh the implementation trade-offs of internship-based versus course-based ultrasound tutor training when designing a peer-tutor program

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's practice points lay out the trade-offs between the two basic formats: internship training has "a low effort to implement" and much scanning practice but low capacity and limited content control, while course training offers high capacity and full content control but high organizational effort and little scanning time. Programs should match these trade-offs to their context.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A course format requires faculty-delivered lectures, hands-on phases, and an accompanying script; an internship format requires access to ultrasound laboratories and a script to guide self-directed coverage.
#### Constraints
- An internship limits the number of trainees to laboratory capacity and offers limited control over which pathologies trainees encounter; a course limits time spent practicing actual scanning.

### Target Learners
- medical students in years 3–5 training as peer ultrasound tutors

### Target Learning Goals
- practical ultrasound scanning skills
- theoretical ultrasound knowledge
