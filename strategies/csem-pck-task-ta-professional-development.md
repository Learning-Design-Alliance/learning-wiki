# Use a predict-the-most-common-incorrect-answer task with a standardized concept survey in TA professional development to engender discussion of student difficulties

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 10 claims (10 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

## Description
In this strategy, TAs are given a standardized conceptual multiple-choice test such as the CSEM and, for each item, asked to identify the most common incorrect answer students would select after traditional instruction. Quantitative student data are then used to show how prevalent each alternate conception actually is, followed by class discussion. The article reports that "this type of activity in a TA professional development course engenders a rich discussion about student difficulties" and suggests such tasks can convince TAs by having them reflect on quantitative performance data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to standardized conceptual multiple-choice tests developed by physics education researchers and quantitative student response data from those tests
#### Constraints
- The task targets alternate conceptions that persist after traditional instruction rather than pre-instruction conceptions, since prior research suggests TAs and instructors are often reluctant to contemplate students' pre-instruction conceptions

### Target Learners
- first-year graduate teaching assistants teaching introductory physics recitations and labs

### Target Learning Goals
- awareness of common student alternate conceptions
- appreciation of the importance of knowledge of student difficulties for effective teaching

### Affordances
- [Pck Knowledge Of Student Difficulties Shulman](../theories/tpack.md)
