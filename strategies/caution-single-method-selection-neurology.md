# Exercise caution when selecting a single teaching method; consider disciplinary characteristics and student demographics, and weigh combined approaches

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that course designers not assume one method is universally optimal: it states "instructors and medical education institutions should exercise caution in designing courses and selecting a single teaching method". This follows from its finding that combined methods may outperform single methods and that rankings differ slightly between theoretical and practical outcomes. The strategy applies to selecting or constructing teaching models for clinical neurology training programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Decisions should take into account disciplinary characteristics, student demographics, and other factors, as the article states.
#### Constraints
- The article restricts its evidence base to interns and residents within the Chinese educational context, limiting generalizability.

### Target Learners

- practical clinical skills in neurology
