# Co Creating Classroom Norms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 5 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q4` · 1 of 5 report an effect size

## Description
Co creating classroom norms is a strategy in which the teacher facilitates a structured process — typically early in a course or year — through which students propose, discuss, and agree on shared expectations for behavior, participation, and interaction. The resulting norms are owned jointly rather than imposed, and are revisited and enforced with reference to student authorship.

## Design Implications

Participation in setting norms increases students' sense of ownership and buy-in, which supports engagement and reduces rule-negotiation friction [Autonomy-supportive teaching contexts raise intrinsic motivation and engagement.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]. The process also signals that students are legitimate members of a community whose voice matters, contributing to belonging [Interventions that strengthen students' sense of belonging improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. The teacher's role shifts from rule-giver to facilitator: they must constrain the space of acceptable norms (safety, respect, equity are non-negotiable) while genuinely delegating the rest — token consultation followed by a teacher-authored list undermines the effect.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured elicitation process (brainstorming, small-group proposals, synthesis) rather than an open-ended "any rules?" prompt
- Teacher clarity about which norms are non-negotiable and which are genuinely open to student decision
- A visible artifact (poster, syllabus section, shared document) that norms can be referred back to
- A routine for revisiting norms when they are violated, framed as "the norm we agreed on" rather than teacher preference
- A structured process (brainstorm → cluster → refine → ratify) so the conversation produces a short, usable set of norms rather than a wish list
- Teacher facilitation that treats student proposals seriously while steering toward norms that protect learning and safety
- Public, visible documentation (poster, shared document) that norms can be referenced against
- A routine for revisiting norms — especially after violations or at natural breakpoints (new term, new project)

#### Constraints
- Consultation without real authority backfires: when students perceive the co-creation as performative, autonomy and trust are reduced [Autonomy-supportive teaching contexts raise intrinsic motivation and engagement.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [-M]
- Student-generated norms can entrench existing group hierarchies if dominant voices set the terms; structured turn-taking and anonymous proposal collection mitigate this
- Norms alone do not change behavior — without consistent teacher and peer referencing, co-created norms decay into wall decoration [-M]
- Time-intensive relative to simply posting rules; the investment pays off mainly in long-duration, relationship-dependent learning contexts
- Co-creation without genuine teacher influence produces norms students perceive as fake, undermining trust [-M] — if the outcome is predetermined, the ritual reads as theater
- Norms generated purely by students may omit protections for marginalized students or fail to address academic behaviors; the teacher must add and justify missing items [~M]
- One-off creation at the start of the year without revisiting leads to rapid decay; norms must be reactivated through [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) and referenced during conflict
- Time-consuming; in short courses, a lighter-weight ratification of a proposed draft may be more efficient [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Restorative / circle approaches**: norms generated through talking circles and revisited communally when harm occurs
- **Syllabus co-design (higher education)**: students negotiate participation, technology, and late-work policies in the first session
- **Contract models**: individual or team "learning agreements" in project-based and online courses
- **Responsive Classroom morning meetings**: norms refreshed through daily structured meetings in elementary settings
- **Full co-creation:** students generate norms from scratch (works well in advisory, seminar, and project-based settings)
- **Draft-and-ratify:** teacher proposes a small set; students amend, question, and commit (efficient for shorter courses)
- **Restorative variant:** norms are framed as community agreements with explicit repair processes, not rules with penalties
- **Online variant:** norms for discussion forums and video sessions (camera norms, response etiquette) co-created in the first synchronous session

### Target Learners
- K–12 students, where norm co-creation doubles as instruction in self-regulation and civic participation [+M]
- Adolescents, for whom autonomy needs are heightened and imposed rules provoke reactance [Autonomy-supportive teaching contexts raise intrinsic motivation and engagement.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Adult and online learners, where negotiated participation norms reduce ambiguity in discussion and group work
- Less suited to very short-duration or highly procedural settings (single workshops, safety-critical labs) where norms must be fixed and explicit
- K–12 students, where the process doubles as instruction in self-regulation and community membership [~M]
- Adult and higher-education learners, where co-created agreements support candid discussion of contested material [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Social-emotional learning: self-management, relationship skills, responsible decision-making
- Classroom community and belonging [Interventions that strengthen students' sense of belonging improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Metacognitive and self-regulatory habits: students internalizing standards they helped articulate
- Civic and democratic dispositions: practicing collective rule-making
- Social-emotional: perspective-taking, shared responsibility, conflict resolution
- Metacognitive/self-regulated learning: internalizing standards for one's own conduct and work quality
- Community building: establishing the relational foundation required for [Collaborative Learning](../patterns/collaborative-learning.md) and discussion-based formats

### Instructions
1. **Frame the purpose** — explain that the community will agree on how members treat each other and do work together, and name the non-negotiables (safety, dignity, equity) up front.
2. **Elicit individually or in small groups** — use a [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) or structured prompt ("What does a class look like where you can do your best work?") to generate candidate norms before whole-group discussion.
3. **Synthesize and consolidate** — cluster proposals, phrase them positively and behaviorally ("we build on each other's ideas" rather than "don't interrupt"), and test each against the non-negotiables.
4. **Ratify publicly** — have every student signal agreement (signature, thumbs, voice vote) to create a genuine commitment, and post the artifact visibly.
5. **Reference and maintain** — when norms are violated, redirect to the agreed norm rather than issuing a personal ruling; revisit and revise the list at natural intervals (term change, new project, after conflict).

### Instructions
1. **Open with purpose.** Ask students what they need from classmates to learn well; ground the conversation in shared goals rather than rules ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) or quick-write).
2. **Brainstorm and cluster.** Collect proposals, group them into themes (respect, participation, honesty, effort).
3. **Refine to a short list.** Push for 4–6 positively framed, observable norms ("We build on each other's ideas") rather than prohibitions.
4. **Add what's missing.** Teacher names any protections or academic expectations students omitted and explains why.
5. **Ratify publicly.** Every member (including the teacher) explicitly commits; post the norms visibly.
6. **Revisit and repair.** Reference norms during [Discussion Groups](../patterns/discussion-based-learning.md) and conflict; hold periodic norm check-ins to revise as the community matures.
