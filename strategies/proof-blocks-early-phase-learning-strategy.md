# Use Proof Blocks activities instead of or before written proofs for students in the early phases of learning a new proof technique

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's conclusion recommends that "Future work should continue to investigate the merits of Proof Blocks as a learning tool in various contexts and how it can be extended to further scaffold and improve learning." Within the studied scope, students in early phases of learning proof by induction learned as much from Proof Blocks or hybrid activities as from writing proofs, in far less time, making Proof Blocks a time-efficient substitute or precursor to proof writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students read the same lecture notes on the target proof technique before the activity
- Problems matched to the target proof technique (here, proof by induction)
#### Constraints
- Limited ecological validity: the study was a controlled lab study, not part of a course
- Learning may have come entirely from the lecture notes rather than the activities
- Learning saturation point (how many problems before no further learning) is unknown

### Target Learners
- first-year computer science and computer engineering students in discrete mathematics

### Target Learning Goals
- writing mathematical proofs, specifically proof by induction

## Claims

- [Students completing the Proof Blocks activity finished their learning activity about four times faster than students writing proofs from scratch, with no loss in learning](../claims/proof-blocks-four-times-faster.md) [+M]
- [The equal-learning and time results held in robustness and conditional analyses: across familiarity subgroups, across all rubric sections, and across both test questions](../claims/proof-blocks-results-robust-across-subgroups-and-rubric-sections.md) [+M]
- [The relationship between time on task and posttest score differs by condition: essentially uncorrelated for Proof Blocks but moderately correlated for hybrid and written-proof activities](../claims/time-on-task-posttest-correlation-by-condition.md) [+W]
