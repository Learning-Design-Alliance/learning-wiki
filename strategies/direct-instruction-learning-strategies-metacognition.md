# Directly instruct learning strategies in course context: model strategies with think-alouds, explain benefits, and train with practice and feedback over time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
For students who cannot execute the strategies they select, the article recommends direct instruction in learning strategies. It states: "we recommend that instructors create class activities that introduce students to learning strategies and help students understand how to execute those strategies (procedural knowledge)." Strategies should be explored in course context, modeled (including by peers) with think-alouds, their benefits made explicit, and practiced over time with instructor feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Strategies are explored in the context of course material so students recognize when and why to use them; instructors model the strategy and provide practice opportunities and feedback over time.
#### Constraints
- The article targets this instruction at students in the struggling and emerging categories; prompts alone were found insufficient for these students.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory biology students, especially those in the struggling and emerging metacognitive-regulation categories

### Target Learning Goals
- selecting and executing appropriate learning strategies; developing procedural and conditional metacognitive knowledge
