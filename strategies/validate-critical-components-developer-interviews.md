# Validate critical components and intended activity flows through developer and expert interviews

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that future research validate the sets of critical components, their COPUS code mappings, the fractions of class time instructors should spend on these codes, and the intended flow of or transitions between activities, for example through developer and/or expert interviews. It also recommends future studies conduct multiple full-length observations of high-fidelity implementations and measure fidelity with a more representative sample of college physics instructors, including those outside science education research.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to method developers or experts with extensive research experience on the methods, and full-length classroom observations of high-fidelity implementations
#### Constraints
- The current study's sample was skewed toward instructors who conduct or consume physics education research, which the authors state may limit generalizability of the high-fidelity pattern

### Target Learners
- college physics instructors implementing named active learning methods

### Target Learning Goals
- faithful implementation of research-based active learning methods
