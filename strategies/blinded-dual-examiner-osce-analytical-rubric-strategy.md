# Assess clinical examination skills with blinded, dual-examiner OSCE scoring using analytical rubrics with perform/did-not-perform items

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The study assessed OHNS clinical examination skills through five 10-minute OSCE stations using standardized assessment forms with an analytical scoring rubric developed by the authors. "Using this method minimizes examiner bias because students either performed the action, or did not perform the action." Two blinded examiners scored each student independently and the final station score was an average, with examiners barred from assessing students they personally knew.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple examiners receiving prior instructions to minimize inter-rater differences; standardized forms and a defined pass mark (65%)
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge a degree of measurement bias may remain given six examiners, despite prior instructions to ensure marking consistency

### Target Learners
- Final-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Objective assessment of clinical examination skills and knowledge application
