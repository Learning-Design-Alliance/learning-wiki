# Cite evidence for the Big Bang at Key Stage 4 level: expansion redshift and the cosmic microwave background

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
To confront the conceptual side of the ex-nihilo obstacle, the article advises acknowledging that scientific understanding is still developing, and that "Teachers are therefore advised to make appropriate citations to evidence for the Big Bang". Starting simply, the expansion of the Universe explains redshifted line spectra from distant galaxies; at a more sophisticated level, the cosmic microwave background radiation provides "more evidence for the Big Bang", interpreted as highly redshifted radiation from a primeval fireball.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both the redshifted expansion and the CMBR must be pitched so they can be explained at Key Stage 4 level, as the article states they can be.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the Big Bang model has had problems in the past and will no doubt have problems in the future too, so scientists must also be sceptical.

### Target Learners
- Key Stage 4 science and physics pupils in England

### Target Learning Goals
- Evidence-based understanding of the Big Bang model, including expansion and the cosmic microwave background radiation
