# Introduce and promote PBL pedagogy in undergraduate pharmacology education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that PBL, a student-centered method in which students "learn and discuss in small groups" and construct knowledge by solving problems, should be introduced and promoted among undergraduate pharmacology students. The recommendation rests on the meta-analytic evidence of higher examination scores and satisfaction with PBL versus LBL. The authors state it is "imperative that pharmacology pedagogues further introduce and promote PBL pedagogy among undergraduate students."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must address appropriate scientific problems to help students understand pharmacology
- Teachers lacking experience in leading self-studies require more training
- Additional human and material resources are needed for the implementation of PBL
#### Constraints
- Some included studies do not provide evidence that PBL benefits factual knowledge, and one control group showed an opposite tendency to perform better on factual knowledge
- Students may spend more time and energy and are more likely to miss some key knowledge
- Some lecturers may be uncomfortable with this method and therefore cannot apply it properly

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical, pharmacy, and nursing students in pharmacology courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Pharmacology examination performance
- Learning interest, comprehension of knowledge, thinking ability, self-study ability, and communication skills
