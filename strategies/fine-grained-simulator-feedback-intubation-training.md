# Provide fine-grained simulator feedback on intubation performance as a complement to traditional training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend designing and implementing a tool that emphasizes practice and efficiency for junior physicians while giving senior physicians "instant feedback on their performance". Because the simulator "analyzes different aspects of every attempt at intubation, and thus can characterize each user's shortcomings and capabilities", identified insufficiencies can be refined. Such "fine-grained feedback could serve as a complement to traditional training and provide a sustainable learning model for medical education."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A high-fidelity simulator capable of measuring multiple performance variables (time, force, glottic view) on every attempt.
#### Constraints
- The authors note the study is only a small-scale investigation and that the training course design was not intended as an intervention.

### Target Learners
- Junior physicians
- Senior physicians
- Medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Intubation speed and safety
- Reducing applied force on incisors and tongue
- Updating senior physicians' knowledge and competence
