# Support international implementation by adapting the model locally, relying on local leaders, and providing long-term job-embedded professional development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
From their cross-country experience the authors draw implementable lessons: "models have to be adaptable," "some principles and practices are indeed universal," and "leadership is vital." In every successful implementation, one or more local leaders went through the stages of implementation, and implementers from different countries learned from each other. They also warn against "entertrainment" — appealing presentations with few practical strategies and no follow-up — because implementation "can only happen if implementers commit to serious, long-term, job-embedded professional development."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Local leaders who explore options, decide on the model, shepherd initial implementation, and plan for full implementation
- Ongoing monitoring and support of professionals after intensive training
#### Constraints
- Rigid implementation-fidelity approaches can be expected to yield little international replication; sustaining an innovation is harder than implementing it

### Target Learners
- Early intervention professionals and agencies implementing practices across countries and cultures

### Target Learning Goals
- Fidelity of family-centered early intervention practices across cultural contexts
