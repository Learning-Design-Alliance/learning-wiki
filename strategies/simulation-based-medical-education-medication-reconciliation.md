# Use simulation-based medical education as an integral part of the medication reconciliation curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review concludes that the use of simulation based medical education should be an integral part of the curriculum by boosting trainees' performance without compromising patient care. Experiential teaching methods including standardized patient encounters and medication reconciliation simulation were used in both undergraduate and postgraduate interventions in the included studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation activities such as standardized patient encounters or medication reconciliation simulation exercises
#### Constraints
- In resident studies, experiential methods alone showed little effect, with only 37.2% of residents rated high performers after a standardized patient encounter

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- Medical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Medication history-taking
- Discharge counseling
- Medication reconciliation performance
