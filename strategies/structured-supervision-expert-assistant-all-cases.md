# Introduce a standardized complex laparoscopic procedure via structured supervision with an expert assistant in every case

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementation recipe pairs a procedure-naïve surgeon with a single experienced assistant in all cases: an assistant with experience in more than 100 laparoscopic pancreaticoduodenectomies as primary operator and an active role in surgical education. The assistant "functioned as an integral component of the anastomotic system by maintaining suture traction and adjusting the jejunal positioning", shifting the focus from individual dexterity to coordinated team performance. This aligns with the educational framework the article cites from Nakamura et al., emphasizing standardization with intraoperative coaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An experienced assistant available in every case, plus a standardized technique whose steps and roles are explicitly defined
#### Constraints
- Demonstrated only for a surgeon with more than 50 open pancreaticoduodenectomies as primary operator; not evaluated for novice surgeons

### Target Learners
- procedure-naïve but experienced pancreatic surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- safely adopting laparoscopic hepaticojejunostomy during the early learning phase
