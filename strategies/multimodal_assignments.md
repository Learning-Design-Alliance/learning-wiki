# Multimodal Assignments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Multimodal assignments involve a variety of assessment methods that challenge learners to create video or audio projects, write personal essays, produce visualizations, or complete quizzes. The pedagogical rationale is twofold: learners encode content through multiple representational channels, and they must actively transform material into a new form — a generative process that supports retention and transfer.

## Design Implications

Multimodal output works because combining verbal and visual representations engages both processing channels [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [+M], and because producing an artifact is a form of [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) rather than passive reception [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. However, the benefit comes from the *generative transformation* of content, not from the medium itself — a poorly designed video assignment is no better than a poorly designed essay.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to tools for creating video and audio projects; an LMS or platform for uploading and sharing assignments
- Clear instructions and assessment criteria, ideally a rubric that evaluates understanding rather than production polish
- [Supportive Information](../elements/supportive-information.md) describing the concepts to be represented and [Procedural Information](../elements/procedural-information.md) for the tools and formats

#### Constraints
- Production demands can impose extraneous [Cognitive Load](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md): when tool manipulation is unfamiliar, working memory is consumed by the medium rather than the content [~M] — teach the tool separately or allow low-fidelity formats
- Media richness does not guarantee learning; redundant or decorative media can depress performance [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [~M]
- Potential for inequitable access to devices, bandwidth, and quiet recording spaces
- Grading multimodal work is more time-consuming and less standardized than grading essays or quizzes

#### Implementation Variability
- Learner choice of format (video, podcast, essay, infographic) against a common rubric — supports autonomy and [Task Value](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Fixed format for all learners when the medium itself is a learning goal (e.g., scientific communication via video)
- Low-stakes multimodal micro-tasks (annotated screenshots, 2-minute audio reflections) as alternatives to full projects

### Target Learners
- Diverse learners with varied expressive strengths; offering format choice lets learners play to strengths, which can build confidence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Learners for whom a single writing-heavy assessment underrepresents understanding
- Less suitable when learners lack the technical fluency to operate the medium — the tool becomes the task

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding: explaining ideas in one's own words and representations [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]
- Communication and disciplinary representation skills
- Reflection and metacognition through [Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md) and [Peer Discussion](../elements/peer-discussion.md)

### Instructions
1. Define the learning goal and the criteria for understanding, independent of format ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
2. Provide [Supportive Information](../elements/supportive-information.md) on the concepts and exemplars of strong multimodal work
3. Provide [Procedural Information](../elements/procedural-information.md) and a low-stakes trial run with the chosen tool
4. Have learners produce the artifact with structured checkpoints ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
5. Deliver [Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) focused on the quality of understanding, not production polish [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
6. [Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md) with a format-neutral rubric, then close the loop with [Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md), [Peer Discussion](../elements/peer-discussion.md), or [Whole-Class Sharing](../elements/whole-class-sharing.md)

## Related Strategies
- [Authentic Audiences, Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) — multimodal artifacts gain power when addressed to a real audience beyond the grader
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — format choice and rubrics make the assignment a learning event, not just a measurement

## Examples
- A biology course where students produce a 3-minute explainer video of a cellular process instead of a lab report, uploaded to the LMS and peer-reviewed
- **VoiceThread** (https://voicethread.com) — learners post audio/video commentary on shared media and respond to peers asynchronously
- **Padlet** (https://padlet.com) — mixed-media boards combining text, images, and audio for collaborative multimodal submissions
- A writing course offering essay, podcast, or photo-essay options against a single argumentation rubric
