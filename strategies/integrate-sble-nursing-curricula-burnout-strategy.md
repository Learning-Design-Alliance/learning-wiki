# Integrate SBLE into nursing curricula to strengthen emotional resilience and prepare students for clinical psychological demands

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating simulation-based learning experiences into nursing education as a strategy against burnout. They state that "Implementing SBLE in curricula could contribute to the development of more resilient and competent healthcare professionals", and that integrating SBLE "may serve as an effective strategy to strengthen emotional resilience and better prepare students for the psychological demands of clinical practice". This follows their experimental finding that SBLE reduced exhaustion and cynicism while enhancing perceived efficacy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation designed around emotionally challenging topics students report as stressful
- Structured pre-briefing, briefing, and debriefing sessions
- Trained faculty facilitators and adequate student emotional intelligence
#### Constraints
- Evidence is from a single academic setting with convenience sampling of second-year nursing students
- Assessments were limited to the medium term, precluding conclusions about long-term sustainability

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- emotional resilience
- coping with the psychological demands of clinical practice
- reducing burnout symptoms
