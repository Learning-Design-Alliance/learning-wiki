# Building Cultural Competency Skills for School Staff

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Building cultural competency develops staff knowledge, skills, and dispositions for working effectively across cultural differences — shifting from deficit thinking (viewing families' backgrounds as obstacles) to strengths-based thinking (viewing families as holders of expertise and resources). It is carried out through sustained professional learning that combines conceptual reframing, structured reflection on one's own cultural assumptions, and applied practice in real family and community interactions, supported by school-wide policies that make welcoming practices routine rather than individual heroics.

## Design Implications

Cultural competency training works when it moves beyond awareness-raising toward skill practice and organizational change; one-shot diversity workshops produce awareness but rarely change behavior [~M]. Effective programs pair conceptual learning with [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) drawn from the school's own community, structured [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md), and reflection that surfaces implicit assumptions — leveraging [Cognitive Conflict](../elements/cognitive-conflict.md) to destabilize deficit narratives and [Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md) to reframe family behavior as adaptive rather than deficient. Because attitudes are embedded in practice, training must be situated in the actual work context rather than delivered abstractly [Situated learning ties learning to the practices and norms of the community where it is used.](../theories/situated-learning.md) [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained time allocation across a school year, not a single professional development day
- Facilitators skilled in managing emotionally charged discussion of bias and identity
- Leadership commitment to changing policies (communication practices, event scheduling, interpretation services), not just individual attitudes
- Authentic engagement with families and community members as co-educators, not merely subjects of study

#### Constraints
- One-off awareness workshops show little durable change in staff behavior; effects decay without follow-up practice and coaching [~M]
- Mandatory training perceived as compliance exercise can trigger defensiveness and reactance, reducing openness to change [~W]
- Training that essentializes cultures ("families from X culture value Y") can increase stereotyping rather than reduce it [~W]
- Individual staff change stalls when school structures — scheduling, communication channels, discipline policy — remain unchanged [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Case-based formats using anonymized dilemmas from the school's own community allow safe examination of missteps
- Community walk-throughs and home-visit programs ground learning in direct relationship-building
- Professional learning communities sustain the work through ongoing [Peer Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) and [Collaboration](../elements/collaboration.md)
- Depth can vary by role: front-office staff, teachers, and administrators need different scenario sets

### Target Learners
- All school staff, including front-office and support staff who are often families' first contact point
- Teachers serving communities whose cultural backgrounds differ substantially from their own [~M]
- Administrators, whose policy decisions determine whether individual staff learning translates into school-wide practice [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Attitude change: recognizing and interrupting deficit framing of students and families
- Interpersonal skill: conducting culturally responsive conferences, communication, and classroom management [Weinstein et al. frame culturally responsive management as a learnable skill set, not a disposition.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022487103259812) [~M]
- Organizational knowledge: mapping community resources and building two-way family-school partnerships

### Instructions
1. Assess current practice — survey staff and families to identify specific partnership gaps rather than generic "cultural awareness" needs
2. Surface assumptions — use structured [Individual Reflection](../elements/articulation.md) and identity-mapping activities to make staff members' own cultural lenses visible before examining others'
3. Examine contrasting cases — analyze [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) pairing deficit-framed and strengths-framed interpretations of the same family situation; contrasting cases support abstraction of the underlying principle [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M]
4. Practice skills — rehearse family conferences, difficult conversations, and welcoming routines through role-play with feedback from peers and community members
5. Apply in context — assign concrete relationship-building tasks (positive phone calls home, attendance at community events) with follow-up debriefs
6. Sustain through community — embed the work in ongoing professional learning communities and coaching cycles [Communities of practice sustain learning through shared norms and mutual engagement.](../principles/communities-of-practice.md) [~M]
7. Change structures — revise policies (communication timing, translation, event design) so that culturally responsive behavior is the default, and monitor progress with family feedback

## Examples
- **Bridging Cultures Project** (WestEd) — a research-based professional development program in which teachers examined conflicting frameworks (individualistic vs. collectivistic) and applied them to immigrant Latino family engagement; documented changes in teachers' interpretations of family behavior and in school practices (https://www.wested.org)
- **Dual Capacity Building Framework** (U.S. Department of Education, Mapp & Bergman, 2019) — widely used framework linking staff capacity-building to family-school partnership outcomes (https://www.dualcapacity.org)
- **Home visit programs** such as the Parent Teacher Home Visits model (https://www.pthvp.org) — staff training paired with structured, relationship-first home visits, associated with improved attendance and family trust in district implementations
