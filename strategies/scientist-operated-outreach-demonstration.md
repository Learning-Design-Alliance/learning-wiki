# Keep the outreach experiment scientist-operated rather than automated so visitors can ask questions and meet a living experimenter

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends having a human operate the demonstration during public events. Automatization, attempted by the French space museum, failed because "automatization results in a sharp increase in health and safety regulations" and the experiment becomes static. An operated experiment keeps the flexible configurations possible, lets visitors ask questions that posters cannot anticipate, and appears strikingly modern to audiences used to screen-based interactive science. This is described as the main lesson learned from the project.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A professional scientist or engineer present to operate the experiment and answer questions.
#### Constraints
- Automatization only suits a static Terrella, not the multi-configuration Planeterrella; automated museum versions trigger stricter safety regulations.

### Target Learners
- general public
- school classes
- politicians and agency leaders

### Target Learning Goals
- introduction to space science
- answering audience questions about space sciences
