# Iterate simulation scenarios through expert Delphi rounds plus a student pilot, adding clarity, printed results, and structured assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates a concrete refinement recipe: build the scenario from explicit structuring elements, submit it to expert judging with a Content Validity Index (minimum 80% set), revise and re-evaluate in rounds, then pilot with students and incorporate their suggestions. Experts modified secondary objectives, replaced the physical-examination-derived clinical case, introduced "Printed complementary tests introduced (X-ray and full blood count), given to the student when requested", and added OSAT evaluation; students requested clearer cases and more objective task instructions. The authors state scenarios "should be constructed with clear language and incorporate significant data into their execution".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Expert judges selected by explicit criteria (here Fehring criteria with a minimum score of five of 14)
- A CVI and agreement threshold (minimum 80%) set before evaluation
- A student pilot with structured feedback instruments
#### Constraints
- The article notes the Delphi method recommends a minimum of 12 participants, and this study used only three experts, which may limit generalizability

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in simulation-based procedural training

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical case management and procedural technique execution in simulated scenarios
