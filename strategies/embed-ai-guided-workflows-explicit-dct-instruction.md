# Embed AI in guided accept/reject/justify workflows, teach digital critical thinking explicitly, and integrate institutional authorship standards

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's three practical implications for AI-supported writing instruction. First, "AI should be embedded within guided workflows requiring students to frame problems, draft iteratively, and log accept/reject/justify decisions—not used as unstructured open access". Second, digital critical thinking and AI literacy should be explicitly taught and assessed through dedicated instruments rather than assumed as incidental outcomes of technology exposure. Third, institutional standards on authorship, attribution, and academically appropriate AI use must be integrated into instruction so students exercise technical judgment and ethical discernment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Guided workflows with accept/reject/justify logging
- Dedicated instruments for teaching and assessing digital critical thinking and AI literacy
- Institutional standards on authorship, attribution, and appropriate AI use integrated into instruction
#### Constraints
- Low scaffolding is a critical threshold: without structured workflows and accountability mechanisms, over-reliance and superficial learning risks rise substantially
- Students with weak digital critical thinking or limited metacognitive regulation may lack the evaluative capacities needed to interrogate outputs and detect hallucinations
- Unclear AI-use policies and insufficient instructor training may undermine the ethical self-regulation behaviors central to beneficial outcomes

### Target Learners
- EFL higher education students
- Saudi university students

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic writing quality
- Digital critical thinking and AI literacy
- Ethical, responsible AI use
