# Design practice problems emphasizing identifying small parameters and mapping expansion templates across varied contexts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that students work problems with a deeper emphasis on identifying small parameters and mapping known expansion templates onto mathematical models in a variety of contexts, "which would likely beneﬁt many students." This targets the Construction and Execution difficulties documented in their Taylor approximation interviews: students struggle to identify relative scales of variables and almost never use optimized template-mapping methods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems must present contexts in which a natural comparative scale exists so students can practice identifying the small expansion parameter rather than applying a fixed criterion.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation follows from interviews with eight self-selected, relatively motivated students at one institution; the authors describe it as what "would likely beneﬁt many students," not a tested intervention.

### Target Learners
- upper-division physics majors

### Target Learning Goals
- selecting appropriate approximation methods and identifying small parameters when constructing mathematical models of physical systems
