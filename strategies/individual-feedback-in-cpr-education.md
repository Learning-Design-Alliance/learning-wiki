# Include time for systematic individual feedback in CPR education, since feedback is a powerful influence on performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend building individual feedback into CPR training, citing Hattie's synthesis showing feedback is among the most powerful influences on achievement and noting feedback is one of Kolb's four stages needed for effective learning. They state "Including individual feedback in CPR education can be a way to increase learning, regardless of native language", while observing that time for feedback was limited during whole-class film-based training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires time for systematic and individual feedback, which may mean pausing the film repeatedly or teaching in smaller groups rather than whole class
#### Constraints
- During the film-based training session the time available for individual feedback was limited; the recommendation was not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- Seventh-grade school students learning basic life support

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved quality of practical CPR skills (e.g. compression depth, ventilation volume, hands-off time)
