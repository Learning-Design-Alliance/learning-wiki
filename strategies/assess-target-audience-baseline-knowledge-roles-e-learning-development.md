# Systematically assess the target audience's baseline knowledge and professional roles when developing e-learning interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The review recommends that developers assess the target audience during intervention development to enhance relevance and perceived usefulness. It states these findings "point to the importance of systematically assessing the target audience during intervention development, including baseline knowledge and professional roles", because content pitched too elementary or drawn from tertiary-care practice was perceived as less useful by experienced primary care practitioners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Understanding of participants' existing competencies and professional roles before content development
#### Constraints
- Design elements like modular structures and self-paced formats cannot fully compensate for content that does not reflect learners' clinical context or expertise

### Target Learners
- experienced primary care healthcare professionals, including nurse and pharmacist prescribers and GPs

### Target Learning Goals
- perceived relevance and uptake of e-learning for infection management
