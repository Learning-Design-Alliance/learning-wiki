# Maintaining High Expectations for Student Performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Maintaining high expectations means holding every student accountable to demanding, individually calibrated achievement standards rather than lowering demands for students perceived as struggling. Expectations are set relative to each student's current performance and growth trajectory: students receive praise in proportion to genuine accomplishment, not lavished on trivial tasks. When a student stalls, the teacher engages positively and guides the student through the initial steps needed to complete the task, rather than reducing the task itself.

## Design Implications

High expectations work through the combination of demanding goals and strong support — the "warm demander" stance. Challenging, specific goals produce higher performance than easy or vague goals [Specific difficult goals lead to higher performance.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md) [+S], and teacher expectations shape student self-efficacy, which in turn predicts persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]. The critical design condition is that expectations are paired with actionable feedback and scaffolding; demandingness without support produces disengagement rather than achievement.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear learning goals against which "high" can be defined and communicated
- Ongoing formative assessment to calibrate expectations to each student's current level ([Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md))
- Feedback that is task- and process-focused rather than person-focused [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- A positive relationship base that lets students read high demands as belief in their capability, not rejection

#### Constraints
- Praise for low-level accomplishments signals lowered expectations and can depress achievement; undifferentiated praise undermines the credibility of later praise [-M]
- Expectations not matched to current performance (too far beyond a student's zone) produce failure cycles and learned helplessness [~S]
- Students who have historically experienced low expectations may initially resist increased demands; withdrawal of support at the first resistance collapses the strategy
- Requires substantial time for differentiation and individual conferencing; under-resourced classrooms often default to uniform low demands

#### Implementation Variability
- Individual goal-setting conferences in which students co-construct targets ([Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M])
- Tiered tasks with a common high ceiling, so all students work toward the same demanding endpoint via different routes
- Revision-required assessment policies: work below standard is not accepted as final, but returned with process-level feedback
- Publicly tracking growth against personal baselines rather than normative comparisons

### Target Learners
- Students in culturally diverse classrooms, where teacher expectation biases disproportionately depress opportunities for marginalized students [+M]
- Students with low academic self-efficacy, for whom credible teacher belief and structured success experiences build persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Advanced students who are otherwise under-challenged by uniform, low-floor expectations
- Less effective when applied as uniform demands without calibration — novices need process goals and heavier scaffolding [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Achievement growth across all content domains — the strategy is domain-general
- Self-regulation and persistence: students internalize demanding standards over time
- Growth mindset: framing ability as developable through effortful engagement with challenge

### Instructions
1. Establish clear, demanding learning goals and communicate them as attainable for every student.
2. Use formative assessment to locate each student's current performance ([Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md)).
3. Set individual targets just beyond current performance, framed as process goals for novices [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M].
4. Provide frequent, task- and process-level feedback tied to the goal ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)) [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
5. Require multiple practice attempts and revisions rather than accepting first-draft performance ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
6. When students stall, guide them through the initial steps of the task rather than lowering the target; praise only genuine accomplishment.

## Examples
- **[Zearn](https://www.zearn.org)** — Digital math curriculum that requires students to reach lesson proficiency before advancing, with built-in remediation supporting the demand rather than replacing it.
- **Expeditionary Learning (EL Education)** — School network built on "warm demander" pedagogy; students defend revised, high-quality work in public exhibitions, with revision cycles required until standards are met.
- **Writing conferences in workshop models** — Teacher holds every student to grade-level writing standards but tailors the conference focus to each writer's next step, praising specific growth rather than general effort.
