Use causal diagramming (strategy maps) as a systems thinking tool to improve mental models and decision making¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that managers should take advantage of systems thinking tools like causal diagrams to model and review their understanding of the system. By using causal diagramming, participants strongly improve the capacity of learning as they become system modellers rather than passive knowledge recipients. The study suggests that to improve mental models, managers should deal with very simple systems thinking approaches like causal diagrams to model and review their understanding of the system.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Participants must be able to review and modify the causal diagram by cutting or inserting links between indicators and defining cause-and-effect relationships

Constraints¶

  • The participants' mental model of the simulated business context is only marginally improved when causal diagramming is not applied

Target Learners¶

  • Managers in strategic decision-making roles

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improving mental models of business system structure and dynamics to support better strategic decisions

Claims¶

  • Level of Feedback Information does not significantly influence Mental Model Structure Similarity in regression analysis [~W]
  • Group C with high feedback information and high systems thinking facilitation showed the best mental model structure similarity, behaviour similarity, and performance [+W]
  • Mental Model Behaviour Similarity positively influences Performance (financial value creation) [+W]
  • Mental Model Structure Similarity positively influences Mental Model Behaviour Similarity [+W]
  • Level of Systems Thinking Facilitation positively influences Mental Model Structure Similarity in a simulation-based management learning experiment [+W]
  • Visualizing support—provided diagrams or learner-generated drawings—facilitates higher-quality explanations and better comprehension than explaining text alone [+W]
