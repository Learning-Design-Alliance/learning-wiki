# Include a psychologist in audit and feedback procedures and train audit teams in communication

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding communication training and psychological supervision into audit and feedback workflows. The authors state that "the inclusion of a psychologist in the audit procedures constitutes a pioneering aspect of this study", and that feedback sessions were video-recorded and supervised by the same psychologists who delivered the training, with post-session interviews summarized and given back to the team as feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Psychologists specialized in communication to deliver training and supervise feedback sessions
- Video-recording of feedback sessions and brief post-session interviews
- Periodic feedback of summarized interview and recording data to team members
#### Constraints
- Preliminary data from one team; the authors call for evaluation on larger samples of different teams and for long-term follow-up of whether improvements enter work practice

### Target Learners
- healthcare audit teams
- healthcare professionals receiving audit feedback

### Target Learning Goals
- effective communication during feedback delivery
- management of interpersonal and group dynamics in teams
