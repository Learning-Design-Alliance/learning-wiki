# Allow learners at least 3 days of self-paced study time so computer-based skill training can transfer to everyday behavior

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 4 studies (3 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends giving participants enough time (at least 3 days) to complete all sessions of a self-paced computer-based training program. In the trial, 40% of intervention participants finished the program in 1 or 2 days, and the authors suggest "they did not have time to incorporate their newly acquired knowledge and skills into their everyday life". When these rushed participants were excluded, significant improvements in coping skills appeared that were absent in the full-sample analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Self-paced program structure allowing multi-day access
- Deadline scheduling that does not compress first access to the final days
#### Constraints
- Even with a short nominal learning time (about 30 minutes per topic), one or two days was not enough during working hours
- The authors suggest the practice phase may benefit from more intensive follow-up sessions for applying skills at work

### Target Learners
- full-time employees undertaking workplace e-learning during working hours

### Target Learning Goals
- transfer of newly learned coping skills into everyday work behavior
