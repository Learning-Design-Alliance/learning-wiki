# Design introductory educational technology courses around the four sources of self-efficacy, prioritizing mastery experiences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educational technology instructors design courses to build preservice teachers' self-efficacy by structuring hands-on successively more difficult assignments, instructor modeling and peer observation, realistic positive feedback, and support that diminishes negative emotional arousal. The article states instructors "should design course work to avoid immediate failure and provide successively more difficult assignments to sustain the likelihood of success", and that "the main attention should be given to" mastery experiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assignments must be difficult enough that success is attributed to ability rather than ease; feedback statements must be seen as realistic
#### Constraints
- Experiences do not directly influence technology integration behavior; they operate through self-efficacy and outcome expectancy beliefs

### Target Learners
- preservice teachers in introductory educational technology courses

### Target Learning Goals
- self-efficacy for technology integration
- intentions to integrate technology
