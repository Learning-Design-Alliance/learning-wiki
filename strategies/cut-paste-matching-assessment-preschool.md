# Adapt assessment formats to preschoolers' incomplete psychomotor skills: cut-and-paste, matching, and play-dough tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because children "could not do the drawings as desired", the researcher prepared an alternative cut-and-paste test in which organs were pre-cut and children pasted them where they fit, plus a matching test pairing objects with senses (alarm clock–hearing, book–seeing, ice cream–tasting, flower–smelling, hand–touching) and a play-dough organ-design activity. Children who could not use scissors were helped, so learning was tested without penalizing undeveloped motor skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Organs pre-cut by the researcher and placed mixed in front of the students
- Adult help available for children who cannot hold or use scissors
#### Constraints
- Developed for children whose psychomotor skills were not fully developed; drawing-based items were graded leniently (full grades for disproportionate drawings)

### Target Learners
- preschool children aged four to five

### Target Learning Goals
- demonstrating knowledge of sense organs and their corresponding senses
