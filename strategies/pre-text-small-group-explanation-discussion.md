# Present a problem for small-group explanation discussion before assigning the relevant expository text

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
Confront small groups with a description of natural phenomena and ask them to propose explanations in terms of underlying processes, principles, or mechanisms; only after the discussion is a text presented that provides information relevant to the solution. The article reports this activation of prior knowledge through small-group discussion facilitates comprehension of the subsequent expository text, particularly its explanatory content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A problem describing natural phenomena whose explanation requires the concepts of the target text; small groups with an experimenter or teacher acting as chairperson who summarizes points of view without providing information about underlying mechanisms
#### Constraints
- The facilitative effect was demonstrated for explanatory propositions in free recall; descriptive facts were not better remembered as a consequence of the analysis of the blood cell problem

### Target Learners
- secondary-school and college science students, both novices and experts

### Target Learning Goals
- comprehension and recall of causal, explanatory concepts from expository science text
