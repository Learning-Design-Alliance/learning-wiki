# Categorize quantum games in a maintained repository with evaluation ratings and best practices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building evaluation infrastructure for quantum games: first, "categorize quantum games in terms of their basic information such as on which platform they run on and their licenses, their types, and the concepts that they are drawing upon"; second, develop a quantum games version of Physport, a repository of research-based recommendations where games receive ratings based on whether they have been evaluated; and third, collect best practices for evaluation alongside the games, denoting whether each game is actively developed, usable, or deprecated.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Researchers should develop best practices for evaluating quantum games, focusing on the common goals of research, education, and fun
#### Constraints
- The article notes no common design methodology or universal evaluation tool currently exists, and games not actively developed quickly become obsolete or fill with game-breaking bugs

### Target Learners
- quantum outreach and education practitioners
- game developers
- researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- quantum literacy
- effective selection and use of quantum games in pedagogic materials
