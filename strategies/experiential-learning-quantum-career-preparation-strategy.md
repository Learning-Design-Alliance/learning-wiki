# Support quantum career preparation by connecting coursework to experiential learning opportunities and professional networks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators view coursework as part of a broader preparation ecosystem: courses "can be more impactful when connected to opportunities for application of that course content", such as research experiences, interdisciplinary projects, and professional development opportunities relevant to quantum technologies. Students are advised to build a portfolio of experiences—undergraduate research, internships, quantum-focused programs, and student organizations—making their skills "applicable, visible, and recognizable across multiple contexts". Industry professionals are encouraged to expand internship programs and university partnerships so students can access networks and opportunities offered by industry.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coursework must be connected to opportunities for application, such as research experiences, interdisciplinary projects, and extracurricular or professional development opportunities relevant to quantum technologies
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that "may limit connections between employers and qualified and ambitious candidates graduating from less-resourced institutions" when hiring relies on personal networks, and calls for structured, transparent, and broadly accessible entry points

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students preparing for quantum careers
- quantum industry professionals
- educators designing quantum curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- career preparation and entry into the quantum information science and engineering workforce
