# Formally include a structured patient safety course early in postgraduate clinical training in LMICs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that a structured patient safety and quality course be formally included for early career clinical staff in low- and middle-income countries, where preventable harms remain a major patient safety concern. Their evidence shows the course "significantly increases knowledge, systems thinking and knowledge efficacy regardless of previous patient safety education exposure", and participants themselves suggested extending the course duration and expanding it to multidisciplinary healthcare teams.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured module combining interactive didactic sessions with applied learning activities such as case-based discussions and communication labs
- Pre- and post-module assessment to evaluate knowledge acquisition
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a one-arm quasi-experimental study at a single private tertiary care institution without a concurrent control group

### Target Learners
- Early career clinical staff, including medical interns, in LMIC settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Patient safety competencies
- Systems-oriented thinking about adverse events
- Confidence and communication for error disclosure and reporting
