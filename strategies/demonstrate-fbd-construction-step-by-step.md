# Demonstrate the step-by-step process of constructing free-body diagrams rather than presenting already completed diagrams

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that physics instruction avoid presenting already completed diagrams and instead demonstrate the process of creating the diagram, for example on the blackboard. Step-by-step construction means "only a low number of chunks of knowledge is processed at one time" and ensures sequential integration of chunks over time. Teachers should also systematically require students to construct and use free-body diagrams and provide more opportunities for self-regulated learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher guidance accompanying diagram use; when teacher-prepared representations are used, extraneous load should be kept low by explicitly emphasizing the teacher's design intentions
#### Constraints
- The impeding effect of provided FBDs was observed only for students with a good mechanics background already accustomed to drawing FBDs; for students not well versed in diagrams, provision of pre-fabricated FBDs would not reasonably be expected to impede solving

### Target Learners
- first-year university physics students
- introductory mechanics students at lower educational levels

### Target Learning Goals
- mechanics problem solving
- constructing and using free-body diagrams

### Affordances
- Internal External Representation Coherence
