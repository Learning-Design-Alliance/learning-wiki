# Use an open on-line drill system as a supplement to in-class teaching, for remote learning, and as remedial material for students lacking prerequisites

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the tutor-web is freely accessible without regard to physical location or school registration, it can serve multiple deployment modes. The article demonstrates this with a graduate remedial calculus/programming course for statistical modellers, covering introductory calculus, linear algebra, multivariate calculus and programming, developed for graduate students who "frequently lack the basis for taking advanced statistics courses". The course was subsequently used as an entry criterion for applicants to a PhD programme, taken remotely. The strategy suits institutions needing to bring students lacking prerequisite background knowledge up to entry level and to supplement classroom teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Freely accessible content with drills; a grading scheme that reflects expertise so remote completion is meaningful
#### Constraints
- The system is mainly a support tool used to supplement education in the classroom; no formal record is maintained of users' whereabouts except when an instructor uses it with a class

### Target Learners
- graduate students lacking prerequisite mathematics background
- undergraduates in large calculus and statistics courses
- secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- remedial mathematics and programming preparation for advanced statistics study
