# Question-answer directions that let children answer as part of the administration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
An implementable administration technique for young groups: the examiner gives directions through simple questions whose answers children supply aloud, so the answers constitute the real directions. The article reports "But, by using questions, the answers to which are very simple, the directions are kept practically standard." The method keeps examiner and class in touch and automatically regulates the pace of directions to the time the class needs to understand them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions must have very simple answers so every child can volunteer
- The examiner asks the same questions of every group to keep directions standard
#### Constraints
- The author notes other methods of controlling interest should certainly be tried; she is certain only that a special problem of directions exists with very young children

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten through fourth-grade children

### Target Learning Goals
- Standardized yet interest-sustaining administration of group assessments
