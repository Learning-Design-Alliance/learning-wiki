# Parental Involvement in Physical Activity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Engage parents by providing details about the benefits of physical activity and encouraging their participation. Parents can promote physical activity at home through family walks, active play, and shared recreation; at school, they can volunteer to supervise before- or after-school programs or lead classroom exercise breaks. Active parental support increases the likelihood of program success, because parents are among the strongest correlates of children's activity levels [Rhodes et al. review of parental correlates.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2016.12.004) [+M].

## Design Implications

Family- and parent-focused components reliably strengthen school-based physical activity interventions, though effects on children's activity levels are typically small-to-moderate and depend on sustained engagement rather than one-off information sessions [Systematic review of child/adolescent activity interventions.](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.a834) [~M]. Information alone rarely changes behavior; programs that pair communication with concrete opportunities to participate (events, take-home activity tasks, volunteering roles) outperform awareness campaigns [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two-way communication channels with parents (newsletters, apps, family events), not just one-way notices
- Concrete, low-barrier participation options: family activity challenges, take-home task cards, volunteer roles for supervision or leading [exercise breaks](../elements/exercise-breaks.md)
- Resources that model *how* to be active together (e.g., suggested family walks, active games), since parental modeling and logistical support (transport, equipment, encouragement) are distinct mechanisms [Gustafson & Rhodes parental correlates review.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ypmed.2005.11.010) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Requires active parental participation; engagement is unevenly distributed — working parents, shift workers, and lower-income families face time and access barriers, so programs relying on in-person volunteering systematically under-include these families [-M]
- Information-only interventions (flyers about benefits) show negligible effects on children's activity [-M]
- Effects attenuate when school programs end without a home-side maintenance plan [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Home-based: family activity challenges, active commuting (walking to school), shared screen-time agreements
- School-based: parent-led [active recess](../strategies/active-recess.md) supervision, classroom movement-break volunteers, family fitness nights
- Indirect: parents as advocates — supporting safe routes to school, funding for equipment, and policy for daily activity time

### Target Learners
- Parents and caregivers of K–12 students, with children benefiting indirectly
- Strongest evidence in elementary-age children, where parental logistical support and modeling have the largest influence [+M]; adolescents respond more to autonomy-supportive parenting than to directed family activity [~W] (see [Self-Determination Theory](../theories/self-determination-theory.md))

### Target Learning Goals
- Increase daily physical activity and reduce sedentary time
- Build durable habits and positive attitudes toward movement
- Improve attention and readiness to learn, since classroom physical activity improves attention [Classroom physical activity improves attention.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Communicate the *why* and the *how*: share benefits information alongside concrete, ready-to-use family activity ideas ([Supportive Information](../elements/supportive-information.md)).
2. Offer a menu of participation roles with varying time commitments so all families can contribute something ([Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md)).
3. Launch a family activity challenge with take-home task cards and a simple tracking method ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md)).
4. Recruit parent volunteers for supervision of before/after-school activity and classroom movement breaks ([Provide Guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md)).
5. Gather feedback from parents and students each term and adjust offerings to reduce barriers.

## Examples
- **[SPARK](https://sparkpe.org)** (Sports, Play, and Active Recreation for Kids) — an evidence-based elementary PE program that explicitly includes family take-home activities and parent promotion as part of its design (Sallis et al., 1997).
- **[Action Schools! BC](https://actionschoolsbc.ca)** (Canada) — a whole-school model where family and community engagement is one of the action planning components.
- **[The Daily Mile](https://thedailymile.org)** — schools often extend participation to parents via family run days and at-home versions of the daily 15-minute run/walk.
