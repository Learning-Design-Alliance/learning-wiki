# Match students' gender with their learning companions in adaptive learning programs to promote engagement and prevent disengagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review recommends assigning learning-companion agents whose gender matches the student's. It reports that female learning companions influenced female students' agentic engagement more, and that girls engaged in more frequent quick guessing when a male character was assigned. As the review puts it, "matching the students’ gender with their learning companions in adaptive learning programs could also be a viable strategy to promote student engagement and to prevent disengagement." The strategy addresses conditions under which social-interaction affordances interact with learner characteristics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adaptive platform must include affective learning companions or pedagogical agents whose gender can be assigned or selected
#### Constraints
- The review notes this outlines conditions when social interaction affordances interact with learner characteristics and preferences, so effects depend on learner characteristics

### Target Learners
- Students using adaptive learning platforms with pedagogical agents, including primary-school children

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustaining behavioral, agentic and affective engagement and preventing disengagement such as quick guessing
