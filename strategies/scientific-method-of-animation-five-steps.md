# Scientific Method of Animation: a five-step intentional process for animation as inquiry in a scientific research setting

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A process developed by intern Declan McKenna during a NASA GSFC astrobiology internship, in which the animator poses a question the animation will answer, sets an objective, experiments with creative and research processes, draws conclusions from feedback, and adjusts to a finished piece. The intern reports that "I developed the Scientific Method of Animation. Similar to the scientific method, this process incorporates five steps". It was applied to a NASA Web Feature on the Asuka 12236 Chondrite, producing two published hand-drawn abstract gifs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to scientists, science writers, or collaborators who can pose questions and give feedback during the process
#### Constraints
- Remote operation complicated the work because in-person meetings allow drawing, discussing, and restructuring material alongside scientists, a process difficult to translate online

### Target Learners
- undergraduate animation interns working within scientific institutions

### Target Learning Goals
- produce animations purposefully engaged with scientific topics
- increase accessibility of scientific research to audiences
