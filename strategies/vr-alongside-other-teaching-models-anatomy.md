# Use VR simulation alongside other teaching models to raise motivation and satisfaction in anatomy education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend adding VR software to the existing mix of teaching models in anatomy education rather than replacing them. They conclude that "the use of this software along with other teaching models in anatomy education can increase the motivation and satisfaction of learners", and note VR is especially valuable where cultural or religious beliefs limit touching patients or where institutions lack real patients, and that the desktop version avoids the costliest headset hardware.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- VR software verified for anatomic correctness; same content and teacher across modalities when combining models
#### Constraints
- VR is just a technology used to deliver a learning technique; some headset-based studies reported headaches, dizziness, and blurred vision

### Target Learners
- medical students in anatomy and clinical skills courses

### Target Learning Goals
- surface anatomy knowledge and procedural skill performance, learner motivation and satisfaction
