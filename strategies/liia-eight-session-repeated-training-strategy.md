# Eight-session repeated simulation training program on the 3D-printed LIIA model to shorten the learning curve

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's training program has surgeons repeatedly perform the standardized, modular LIIA procedure on the 3D-printed model, with three sessions each per week implied by scheduling not stated; three intermediates and three novices "received LIIA training eight times". Progress was tracked with operation scores, times, and CUSUM learning curves, and gains were verified on pig ex vivo organs. The authors report this reduced the learning curve and improved operative performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the 3D-printed model and laparoscopic platform, a skilled specialist on hand during initial simulations, and a prerecorded instructional video viewed before performing
#### Constraints
- Operation times of trained intermediates still did not match experts after eight sessions; whether skills transfer to actual operating-room settings remains to be verified

### Target Learners
- surgical novices and intermediates training in LIIA

### Target Learning Goals
- LIIA operative performance, operation time, and readiness for patient-oriented surgery
