Integrate cost-effective simulation with structured, proficiency-based curricula into pediatric MIS training¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends integrating cost-effective simulation into pediatric MIS training through structured, proficiency-based curricula rather than short exposure courses. It reports that "short exposure courses ... lack the structured deliberate practice necessary for expert performance, emphasizing the need for a mastery learning framework". The review concludes that "no universal MIS training model exists for neonate/infant patients", making validated, standardized models and curricula a priority for future research.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Validated simulation models with established validity evidence
  • Structured, proficiency-based curricula rather than short exposure courses

Constraints¶

  • No universal MIS training model currently exists for neonate/infant patients

Target Learners¶

  • pediatric surgery residents
  • pediatric surgeons

Target Learning Goals¶

  • minimally invasive surgical skills in neonates and infants

Claims¶

  • Systematic review of 72 studies finds no universal MIS training model for neonates and infants, supporting structured proficiency-based curricula [+W]
  • Patient-specific 3D-printed cloaca model rated high-quality and feasible for preoperative cysto-vaginoscopy training, with 85.4% recommending its use [+W]
  • Avian (chicken) tissue model validated for neonatal MIS training with high perceived fidelity and overall satisfaction of 4.64 ± 0.56 [+W]
  • PLS simulator is the most commonly used pediatric MIS training model, with construct validity established for its pattern-cutting task [+W]
  • Rabbit model outperformed porcine model for pediatric MIS training, with higher surgical performance scores (8.1 versus 6.0) [+W]
  • Weisser et al. 3D-printed pediatric MIS curriculum: expert surgeons outperformed residents on all tasks with high interrater reliability [+W]
  • Prior basic SuSiPed training significantly increases performance on the more advanced pediatric MIS simulator (SuSiPed 2.0) [+W]
