# Simplify novel-technique controls and support teachers when deploying role-play and immersive tools in lectures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's design implications hold that novel lecturing techniques raise teachers' extraneous cognitive load, so systems should make them effortless to operate. After the role-play enter/exit action was simplified with a shortcut key, the teacher's proportion of close-ended questions returned to normal. The authors conclude that "the ease of use and necessary training are important factors" and that "the system needs to support teachers (i.e., using AI assistants to help classroom management and learning process management) and allow them to control the pace of role-play easily".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Easy-to-use controls such as shortcut keys for entering and exiting role-play, plus teacher training and system-side support such as AI assistants for classroom management
#### Constraints
- The observation that close-ended questions increased during role-play came from the authors' observation during design, and the fix was confirmed by the experiment teacher's self-report

### Target Learners
- Teachers deploying immersive lecturing techniques; their primary and secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining teaching presence and higher-quality questioning while using novel interactive techniques in lectures
