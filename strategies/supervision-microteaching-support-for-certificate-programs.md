# Support short intensive gifted-education certificate programs with supervision or micro-teaching practices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that “Such trainings may be supported by supervisory processes or micro-teaching practices,” because teachers need one-to-one implementation experience with gifted students that a 40-hour format cannot provide. It also suggests providing “an environment that allows the participants to spend at least two days with gifted children or to observe them for at least two days” before training, and designing hybrid formats to extend time while maintaining attendance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- supervisors with experience working with gifted children
- access to gifted children for observation or practice
#### Constraints
- supervision requires longer and more costly training arrangements

### Target Learners
- in-service teachers attending certificate programs

### Target Learning Goals
- applying gifted-education methods in classroom practice
