# Pair LLM-assisted learning with VR/AR or simulation-based training for procedural skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that because text-based LLM support has limited influence on hands-on procedural competence, programs should integrate LLM support with simulation-based training such as VR/AR platforms offering virtual practice with haptic feedback, or more focused bedside teaching. It also proposes future AI features such as real-time adaptive questioning tailored to group discussions or gamified elements to strengthen interactive participation beyond what current LLM prompts achieve. The recommendation extends to combining AI with immersive technologies to personalize training based on individual performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration with simulation-based training (VR/AR with haptic feedback) or focused bedside teaching for procedural skills
- AI features redesigned for group settings, such as adaptive questioning or gamified elements, to amplify participation
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a null finding from a single non-randomized trial at one institution; the article notes further studies are needed
- The article does not report data on VR/AR integration itself, only recommends it

### Target Learners
- Medical residents in procedural specialties

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural and clinical skills acquisition
- Active participation in group case analysis
