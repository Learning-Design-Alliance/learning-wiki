Strategies

Use Toulmin-based argumentation activities (developing arguments, competing theories, predict-observe-explain, expression tables, concept maps) with small-group techniques to teach science units.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study employed seven scientific argumentation-based activity types — developing arguments, competing theories-cartoons, predict-observe-explain, competing theories-ideas and evidence, expressions table, concept maps and competing theories-stories — all based on the Toulmin model of argumentation. Activities were implemented through study sheets reviewed by science education specialists and paired with small-group techniques such as pair talk, listening triads, pairs and quadruples, ambassadors and discussions. The intervention lasted 24 class hours.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Study sheets developed for each activity and reviewed by science education specialists and science and technology teachers in terms of scope validity

Constraints¶

  • Implemented in eighth-grade science and technology course over 24 class hours

Target Learners¶

  • eighth-grade students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • cell division and inheritance content knowledge
  • nature of scientific knowledge
  • willingness to participate in discussions
  • attitudes toward science course

Claims¶

  • Scientific argumentation based learning improves student attitudes toward the science and technology course on the positive attitude and interest dimensions, but not on negative attitude or importance. [+W]
  • Scientific argumentation based learning significantly improves eighth-grade students' academic achievement in the unit of cell division and inheritance compared to traditional teaching. [+W]
  • Scientific argumentation based learning improves students' understanding of the nature of scientific knowledge on five of six dimensions. [+W]
  • Scientific argumentation based learning increases students' willingness to participate in discussions. [+W]
  • Students most liked the competing theories, news, and concept cartoon activities because these made creating and defending arguments easier [+W]
