# Use short, gamified low-cost simulation tasks as an introductory pre-clinical training method before real laparoscopic exposure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that students unfamiliar with laparoscopy first train on simple, playful, gamified tasks in a controlled environment before any real surgical situation. The gamified approach "makes the learners the protagonists of their own learning", with goals to accomplish each time, which the authors say motivated students and avoided pressure, stress, or anxiety. All 51 participants agreed the simulator should be used before the real situation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two instructors, each responsible for one simulator, to present equipment handling and task instructions
- A short introductory lecture on laparoscopic concepts and instrument manipulation before simulation
- Checklists with a completion deadline to structure each five-minute practice
#### Constraints
- The intention is not to train robust or complex surgical procedures but to direct primary learning to an unfamiliar audience
- Simulated training does not replace practice on real patients; the authors call for studies with residents and specialist physicians to confirm resemblance to the real situation

### Target Learners
- medical students with no previous laparoscopy experience

### Target Learning Goals
- basic laparoscopic instrument handling
- two-dimensional depth-perception adaptation
- motor coordination
- motivation to study laparoscopy
