# Provide adaptive online support and targeted guidance for students with weaker self-study motivation in blended flipped courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 2 of 2 report an effect size

## Description
A forward-looking implementation strategy the authors derive from their mixed survey response: while 59.32% benefited from online pre-learning, 11.86% remained neutral, indicating varied self-study abilities. They recommend that "Future reforms should offer adaptive online resources, such as implementing AI-based diagnostic tools to identify students' gaps in gene expression regulation (a common difficulty in biochemistry) and deliver targeted learning resources", and that teachers give more assistance to less motivated or less confident students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Diagnostic tools capable of locating individual knowledge gaps (e.g., in gene expression regulation)
- Teacher time to provide additional guidance to low-motivation students
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a single-institution cohort; the authors call for multicenter trials to validate the model across diverse student populations

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students with heterogeneous self-study abilities

### Target Learning Goals
- independent pre-class preparation
- mastery of difficult biochemistry topics such as gene expression regulation
