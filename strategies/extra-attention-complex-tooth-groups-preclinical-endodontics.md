# Give complex, multi-canal tooth groups additional attention during preclinical endodontic training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that preclinical endodontic instruction allocate additional attention to tooth groups with complex canal anatomy, because filling adequacy was lowest in multi-canal groups, particularly mandibular molars. The authors state these findings "suggest that complex tooth groups may require additional attention during preclinical endodontic training". They also suggest future studies evaluate students' perceived difficulty of each tooth group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training should use a standardized preclinical protocol with instructor supervision and anatomical training before each session, as in the evaluated course
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from a single-institution retrospective study of third-year students using hand instrumentation on extracted teeth, and should not be directly generalized to clinical performance or patient outcomes

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate dental students in preclinical endodontics

### Target Learning Goals
- performing technically adequate root canal fillings (length, taper, homogeneity)
