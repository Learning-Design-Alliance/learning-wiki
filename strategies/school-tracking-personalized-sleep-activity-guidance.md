# Schools offer programs that frequently track students' physical activity and sleep and provide personalized guidance for optimizing academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A practical application strategy the authors propose for schools: programs that monitor both movement behaviors and translate the combined optimal ranges into individualized advice. The authors write that "schools could consider offering programs that involve frequent tracking of students' physical activity and sleep behavior for the purpose of offering personalized guidance on how they may change their behaviors to optimize their academic performance." Self-report is described as cost-effective, with consumer-grade wearables as a more precise option.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Frequent monitoring of both physical activity and sleep behavior, since the interaction findings indicate both behaviors must be considered jointly.
#### Constraints
- The study is cross-sectional and self-reported, so the guidance rests on associations rather than causal evidence; pandemic-era declines in activity and sleep may have shifted the relevant behavior distributions since the 2019 data were collected.

### Target Learners
- Adolescents in grades 9–12 in US secondary schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement (self-reported school grades)
