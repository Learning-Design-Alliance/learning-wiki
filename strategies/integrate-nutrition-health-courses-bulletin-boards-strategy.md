# Integrate nutrition knowledge into existing health courses and use school environment messaging such as bulletin boards

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Teachers endorsed low-cost, environment-based practices for delivering nutrition education: posting knowledge on bulletin boards in classrooms and canteens, integrating nutrition into health courses, and using social media such as WeChat or Weibo. The article reports "more teachers were willing to integrate nutrition education into health courses rather than offer an independent course." The authors note integration reaches learners but may secure fewer teaching hours than an independent course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to school communication channels such as bulletin boards, health courses, or social media platforms
#### Constraints
- The authors note integrated delivery may result in fewer teaching hours than an independent course, which matters given insufficient teaching hours

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in Shanghai schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Nutrition knowledge about diets and health, dietary guidelines, and healthy food choices
