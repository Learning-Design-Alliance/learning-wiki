# Integrate simulation as a complementary learning tool across oncology curricula, continuing education, and recertification programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educators use simulation as a complementary learning tool in oncology courses, given its ability to teach skill acquisition and decision-making through real-life situational experiences without compromising patient safety. They advise that "Educators can find creative ways to integrate simulation into oncology curricula, continuing education, and recertification programs to create engaging and immersive learning environments". The recommendation rests on the bibliometric evidence that SBME publications span surgical, medical, radiation oncology, nursing and pharmacy education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation settings and activities must be guided by the learning objectives and incorporated into the educational curriculum
- Instructors must be adept at both the technical aspects of simulation and the skills needed to facilitate debriefing sessions and offer constructive feedback
#### Constraints
- The article notes SBME requires more time and financial resources, with substantial expense for acquiring and maintaining simulation equipment, specialized facilities and trained staff

### Target Learners
- Oncology trainees including medical students, residents, fellows, nurses and pharmacy students

### Target Learning Goals
- Technical and procedural clinical skills
- Communication and interpersonal skills
- Decision-making in oncology clinical scenarios
