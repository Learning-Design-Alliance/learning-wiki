# Longitudinal faculty development using the EFeCT tool to improve narrative feedback quality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend leveraging faculty development to enhance the specificity and actionability of narrative feedback in EPA observations. They suggest "Longitudinal approaches that allow for practice, reflection and calibration are more likely to produce sustained change than one‐off sessions", with the EFeCT tool serving as a framework for structured workshops analysing sample narratives, peer-to-peer evaluation of feedback, and integration into electronic EPA platforms to provide real-time prompts. Addressing contextual factors such as clinical workload and faculty time pressures is also recommended.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty development sessions require opportunities for practice, reflection and calibration across faculty.
#### Constraints
- The optimal format for faculty development is uncertain; the effect of such initiatives on feedback quality is a question for future research.

### Target Learners
- clinical faculty supervising medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- providing specific, actionable narrative feedback

## Claims

- [Workplace observation by faculty is the dominant EPA implementation and assessment method, and logistical difficulties and faculty development issues are leading implementation barriers](../claims/epa-implementation-workplace-observation-logistical-barriers.md) [+W]
- [Narrative feedback in undergraduate EPA observations at one Canadian institution was moderate to high quality, with a mean EFeCT score of 4.3 and 59% of observations meeting all criteria](../claims/epa-narrative-feedback-moderate-to-high-quality-efect.md) [+W]
- [The most commonly omitted element of high-quality feedback in EPA narratives was actionable 'where to next' feedback, with 273 (36.5%) of observations losing this point](../claims/where-to-next-feedback-most-commonly-omitted-epa.md) [+W]
