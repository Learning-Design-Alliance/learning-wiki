# Meaningfully engage end-users and experts as partners throughout e-learning co-development and evaluation using the IKT Guiding Principles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy engages a diverse group of end-users and experts as partners across the whole life cycle of an e-learning resource, from needs analysis through pilot testing to evaluation, following the Integrated Knowledge Translation approach. The article reports it "used the new IKT Guiding Principles to guide the meaningful engagement of a diverse group of end-users and experts as partners throughout this project," gathering survey data from 130 end-users, interviews with 8 clients with SCI, and multiple rounds of pilot and think-aloud testing. The authors suggest their reporting of shared principles and strategies may provide a model for others developing e-learning resources for professionals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Engagement of potential end-users early and throughout the development process
- An established panel with relevant expertise (researchers, counsellors, therapists, people with SCI) to guide co-development
#### Constraints
- Navigating different opinions and perspectives of end-users and experts added additional complexity to the project and required flexibility in the approach

### Target Learners
- Health, fitness and lifestyle professionals in health and community settings, including rural and remote communities

### Target Learning Goals
- Co-development of e-learning resources aligned with end-users' needs and preferences
