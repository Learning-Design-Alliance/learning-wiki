# Incorporate human decision-making points and reflection into AI scaffolding to maintain attention and increase satisfaction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adding human decision-making points to AI scaffolding design so learners actively participate in problem-solving processes. Learners could choose among AI-suggested approaches, prove inaccuracies in AI-generated content, and compare their methods to AI approaches. This addresses the finding that lack of active involvement decreased satisfaction and that learners did not scrutinize AI-generated information, helping maintain attention and increase intrinsic motivation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scaffolding design should include human decision-making points and reflection/criticism to increase learners' intrinsic motivation.
#### Constraints
- Expected outcomes and their measurement criteria should not be based on whether learners attained the correct answers.

### Target Learners
- College engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- Critical thinking and problem-solving skills in STEM
