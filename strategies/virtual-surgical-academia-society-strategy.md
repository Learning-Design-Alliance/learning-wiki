# Adopt free, expert-curated video-lecture formats as a virtual surgical academia under professional societies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that national and international surgical societies such as the European Surgical Association adopt the Zurich format to create a "virtual surgical academia": select field experts who guarantee high-quality content, set up a steering committee, apply strict quality control, optimize content for mobile use, and keep access completely free of charge to reach surgeons in countries with smaller financial scope. They argue free and unrestricted access via social media fills a global education gap and that external funding, perhaps through industry sponsors or a foundation, must cover costs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A steering committee to clarify the concept's basic principles and coordinate contributing experts
- Strict quality control of published content, described as pivotal for user experience and low churn
- External financing, since free access is a prerequisite for attracting a global audience
#### Constraints
- Substantial cost and organizational overhead to set up, administer, and update online content, estimated at roughly 1200 euros per video clip

### Target Learners
- surgical residents worldwide
- surgeons in developing countries and low-resource settings

### Target Learning Goals
- global access to expert-curated surgical education
- integration of cutting-edge expertise into official surgical curricula
