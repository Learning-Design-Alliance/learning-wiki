Build computational thinking skills in the younger grades through plugged and unplugged activities¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends developing CT skills early: "In younger grades (K–3), students will likely be engaging primarily in computational skills in application to relevant problems in the classroom." It describes both unplugged activities not reliant on digital devices and age-appropriate programmable devices such as Beebots or Codeapillar. It holds that while both plugged and unplugged activities are valuable, leveraging technological tools where appropriate supports learners to connect and apply skills to computational practices in older grades, where students combine skills into practices such as automation, modeling, and data practices.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Activities should apply computational skills to relevant problems in the classroom, with tools matched to age and cognitive capacities

Constraints¶

  • The report scopes skill-focused engagement primarily to grades K-3, with practice-focused engagement in grades 4-12

Target Learners¶

  • students in grades K-3, extending to grades 4-12 for practices

Target Learning Goals¶

  • foundational computational thinking skills such as decomposition, pattern recognition, and algorithmic thinking

Affordances¶

  • Ct Integration Framework Three Circles

Related Strategies¶

  • Design early learning (K-3) CT opportunities both plugged and unplugged, with devices leveraged where appropriate
  • Integrate computational thinking into existing disciplinary teaching across grade bands rather than adding it on
