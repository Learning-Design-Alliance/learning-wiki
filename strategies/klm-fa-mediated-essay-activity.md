# Tool-mediated essay activity in which students evaluate web forms with KLM-FA and also conduct pen-and-paper KLM modeling

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A graded learning activity in which students receive the KLM-FA tool, a short video tutorial and an essay description, and apply HCI predictive models to real web forms. Students were asked to 'use KLM-FA to evaluate signup forms of diverse complexity' with various tool parameters and Fitts' Law, and to conduct a pen-and-paper KLM modeling of a complex non-working prototype form.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the KLM-FA tool and its demonstration video
- A course LMS through which the essay description, questionnaires and submissions are managed
#### Constraints
- Evaluated in a single elective course with 22 completing students; the authors state that an experiment with a control group using paper and pencil and a treatment group using the KLM-FA tool is needed to examine the learning phenomena in more detail

### Target Learners
- Final-year undergraduate computer engineering students
- Students in HCI, software design or software quality courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Calculating task completion times with the KLM
- Modeling pointing movements with Fitts' Law
- Comparing alternative interaction strategies and form designs
