# Use multivariate genetic modeling in future studies to test whether entry into university and achievement at university are predicted by different heritable traits

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends a specific next research step: testing the differential heritability hypothesis. The authors observe that standardized tests used for college admittance, such as the SATs, are poor predictors of graduation rates after admittance, and state that "Future studies using multivariate genetic modeling can test this differential heritability hypothesis". The strategy is to apply multivariate genetic modeling to distinguish the heritable traits behind university entry from those behind university achievement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal genetically informative samples with measures of both university entry and university achievement.
#### Constraints
- The article notes current DNA methods capture only common SNPs, so GPS will underestimate genetic influence where non-additive effects or rare variants contribute.

### Target Learners
- young adults in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding individual differences in university entry and achievement
