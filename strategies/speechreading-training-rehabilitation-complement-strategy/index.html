Offer computer-based self-guided speechreading training as a low-cost complement to hearing aids in aural rehabilitation for middle-aged and older adults¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (1 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends computer-based speechreading training as a complement within aural rehabilitation: "computer-based speechreading trainings offer a valuable complement that is cheap, easily applicable and incorporable into daily life." It is intended for cases where hearing aids do not fully mitigate communication problems, and it trains listeners to use visual speech cues to complement the degraded acoustic signal.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Self-guided use requires a training schedule (about 20 minutes, five days per week, three weeks in this study) and motivation to adhere, which the authors cite as a reason for non-randomized group assignment

Constraints¶

  • The article reports the benefit was shown for the audiovisual condition only, in normal to moderately hearing-impaired adults, using a between-group design with self-selected groups rather than randomization

Target Learners¶

  • middle-aged and older adults with normal to moderately impaired hearing

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving speech comprehension in noisy everyday communication

Claims¶

  • Three weeks of computer-based speechreading training improves audiovisual speech-in-noise comprehension in middle-aged and older adults, with the effect limited to the audiovisual condition [+W]
  • Speechreading training does not significantly improve subjectively perceived everyday listening effort or hearing abilities [-W]
  • Visual enhancement is not significantly associated with hearing level, age, visual working memory or processing speed, and learning rate in speechreading training is not significantly associated with those or with hearing aid use or training intensity [~W]
  • Lexical overlap between speechreading training material and test sentences did not significantly account for the observed training effect [~W]
  • Participants who improved on audiovisual speech-in-noise comprehension had significantly lower pre-training scores than non-learners, reflecting the inherent correlation between baseline and change scores [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Design auditory-cognitive trainings with natural, relatable speech material and multisensory presentation to target far transfer to real-world listening
