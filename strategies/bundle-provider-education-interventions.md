# Bundle multiple provider education modalities (workshops, lectures, outreach visits, distributed materials) rather than a single session

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors propose that changing provider screening behavior requires combining several education formats rather than one lecture or one emailed slide set. They write that "Provider education may require a variety of interventions to be effective including education workshops, meetings, lectures, education outreach visits and distribution of educational material," and suggest that "Bundling education interventions, for example, between lectures and workshops could have proven more effective." This strategy responds directly to their own null intervention result.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained follow-up of roughly 6-12 months, which prior guideline-implementation studies used and this study lacked
#### Constraints
- In this study a single in-person session or emailed slide set produced no significant change in documentation; the bundled approach itself was not tested

### Target Learners
- Internal medicine primary care physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- Increase knowledge of preeclampsia as a CVD risk factor and increase documentation of obstetric history during risk screening
