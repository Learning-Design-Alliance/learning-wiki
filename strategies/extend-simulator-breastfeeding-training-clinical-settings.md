# Extend simulator-assisted breastfeeding training to hospitals, NICUs, and antenatal classes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that hospitals and antenatal services adopt simulator-assisted breastfeeding training alongside standard verbal training, because in their trial the applied, realistic simulation outperformed video training. They write: "breastfeeding simulator training can be extended to expectant mothers in hospitals, neonatal intensive care units and antenatal classes, and to mothers whose babies are admitted to neonatal intensive care immediately after birth" and that "The use of a breastfeeding simulator in clinical settings would be a good technique to encourage mothers to breastfeed and continue breastfeeding their newborn babies."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Availability of a wearable breastfeeding simulator; single-session individual or small-group training with time for questions
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single trial in one province in eastern Turkey; the authors state the results cannot be generalised for the whole of Turkey

### Target Learners
- Pregnant women, mothers who have given birth, and mothers whose babies are in neonatal intensive care

### Target Learning Goals
- Starting and continuing breastfeeding; increasing breastfeeding self-efficacy and maternal attachment
