Design GenAI governance and cross-role contact around integrity disputes, the principal site of sustained faculty-student contact¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's discussion draws implications for governance, pedagogical design, and cross-role contact design from its finding that adversarial integrity themes dominate faculty-student contact. Because "Mixed threads contain 2-3×more records and last 2-4×longer than same-role threads", integrity disputes are where faculty and students most sustainedly engage, so governance and contact design should address detection, enforcement, and false-accusation dynamics rather than treating stakeholder communities separately.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional awareness of which themes host cross-role contact (AI Detection and Misconduct Enforcement host one third of it)

Constraints¶

  • Findings are drawn from Reddit discourse in Anglosphere communities and may not generalise to offline institutional settings

Target Learners¶

  • faculty and instructors
  • undergraduate and graduate students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic integrity governance
  • constructive integration of generative AI into teaching and assessment

Claims¶

  • 17% of GenAI education threads are cross-role; one third of cross-role contact occurs in adversarial integrity themes [+W]
  • Academic integrity was the panel's most frequently cited GenAI policy concern, requiring clear definitions of misconduct and enforceable consequences [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Institutions should respond to GenAI with clear governance frameworks and structured professional training programs for staff.
  • Structure GenAI integration in higher education around feedback-literacy curricula and reflective course design
