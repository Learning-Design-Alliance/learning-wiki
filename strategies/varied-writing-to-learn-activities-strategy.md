# Teachers should use varied writing-to-learn activities in science classrooms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on this study and related work, the authors recommend that teachers employ different writing-to-learn activities in the classroom. They state that "the use of different writing -to-learn activities by teachers in classroom environment will benefit them in many aspects" and that such activities "will make positive contribution to learning of students, and develop their skills of remembering, commenting, eliminating misconceptions, reinforcing the subject, and making reasonable explanation". Their recommendations call for testing further activities (story, concept map, poster), other grade levels, and other units and subjects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Guidelines and verbal instruction on how to perform the writing activity, as given to the experimental groups in this study
#### Constraints
- The poetry benefit was shown only for the Force and Motion unit with fifth-grade students; the journal activity showed no significant effect in this study

### Target Learners
- fifth-grade secondary school science students
- students across grades 6-8 and high school (recommended for future study)

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement in science
- remembering
- eliminating misconceptions
- reinforcing the subject
