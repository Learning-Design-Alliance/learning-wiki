# Use school-based markers — poor attendance, frequent sickness, low mood and slow learning — to identify and link unreached adolescents living with HIV to care

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools, which ALHIV access at similar rates to peers, serve as low-cost, low-stigma sites for identifying adolescents needing HIV testing and linkage to care. Markers are observable without knowing HIV status: "low attendance, frequent sickness, low mood and slow learning". This avoids unwanted disclosure while targeting testing, counselling and educational support services.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools need linkages to HIV testing, counselling and care services and educational support capacity to act on the markers
#### Constraints
- Cross-sectional data limit causal claims; about 7% of ALHIV are not in school, so community-based outreach remains essential for the most vulnerable

### Target Learners
- Adolescents aged 10–19 in high-HIV-prevalence, resource-limited settings

### Target Learning Goals
- School engagement and attendance
- Identification of adolescents needing health and educational support
