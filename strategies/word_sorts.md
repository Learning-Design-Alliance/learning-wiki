# Word Sorts

## Description
Word sorts are a hands-on activity where learners categorize words based on shared features to build phonological processing, morphological knowledge, vocabulary, and spelling skills. The sorts can be 'closed' (instructor-provided categories) or 'open' (learner-determined categories). Cutting apart the words adds a multisensory component. Sorts can be done independently or collaboratively to promote social skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- List of words, sorting categories (optional), scissors (optional)
#### Constraints
- May require preparation time for instructors to create word lists and categories. Effectiveness depends on the relevance and appropriateness of word choices.

- Elementary to middle school learners; adaptable for older learners needing foundational literacy support

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->

### Affordances
- Supports multisensory learning; allows for differentiation based on learner needs; promotes collaborative learning and discussion

### Personalization
- Instructors can provide words and categories (closed sorts) or allow learners to generate their own (open sorts). Complexity of words can be adjusted by the instructor. Can be done independently or with partners.

- Observe learners' ability to accurately categorize words and explain their reasoning. Analyze learners' ability to identify patterns and make connections between words.

- Word sorts have been shown to improve phonological awareness, vocabulary acquisition, and spelling skills. Multisensory approaches enhance memory and retention.
