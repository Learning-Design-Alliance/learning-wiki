# Introduce affective strategies to reduce anxiety before pushing oral practice, and build relaxed, supportive classrooms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's pedagogical implication is that instead of forcing EFL learners to practice conversation and oral skill, teachers should first introduce affective strategies to reduce anxiety, which then improves oral proficiency. Teachers should "create a relaxed environment, and encourage learners to achieve their linguistic goals", and materials developers should consider strategy use in materials development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need time to devote to the development of affective strategies and familiarity with general affective and motivational concepts
#### Constraints
- The article notes its own limitations: the duration of the study and the number of participants; more longitudinal studies are needed on how learners internalize the strategies

### Target Learners
- EFL learners, particularly pre-intermediate secondary students

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing language anxiety
- improving oral proficiency
- learner independence and strategy awareness
