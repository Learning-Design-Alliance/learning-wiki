Prepare GSP activities beyond geometry and train teachers in dynamic geometry pedagogy¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's recommendations for mathematics teachers, teacher-training institutions, and researchers: prepare GSP activities and sketches for other areas of mathematics, teach dynamic geometry software use in pre-service and in-service courses, increase computer lessons in teacher education, set up well-equipped computer laboratories, and allow group and teacher-guided class discussions so students engage in social interaction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers should use the computer well; the authors state this lack of teachers' information should be eliminated first
  • Fully equipped computer laboratories with sufficient computers

Constraints¶

  • Activities took time because students reached generalizations by struggling, exploring, and building their own knowledge

Target Learners¶

  • Primary and secondary school mathematics students
  • Pre-service and in-service mathematics teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Geometry learning through exploration and generalization
  • Creative production in transformation and fractal geometry

Claims¶

  • Seventh graders learning geometry with GSP dynamic geometry software scored significantly higher on a geometry achievement posttest than peers taught by traditional methods [+W]
  • GSP-taught students retained significantly more geometry learning one month after instruction than traditionally taught students [+W]
  • Form Six students taught graph functions with Geometer's Sketchpad scored significantly higher on the post-test achievement measure than students taught by chalk and talk [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use dynamic mathematics software with guided discovery for math topics students find difficult
  • Sustained, rigorous, relevant technology-centered PD with school-district support, collaborative facilitation, and lesson-plan sharing to promote faithful implementation
  • Integrate mathematics content with the context of teaching in methods courses, sequencing content before pedagogy
  • Equip classrooms with visualization software such as CAS and DGS so students construct their own knowledge of hard concepts
