# Use indirect means—athletics, social affairs, literature, religion, and a lifework—to redirect the youth's instinctive energies alongside direct sex instruction.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Beyond instruction in a narrow sense, the essay recommends filling the adolescent's life with absorbing activities and purposes. It lists "Interest and participation in athletics and social affairs, in literature, poetry, language, religion, and so on, the filling of the youth's mind with a lifework" as means that help, in the terms of Hall, Jones and Cabot, to long-circuit instinctive tendencies, sublimate desires, and transform vital energy. These indirect means supplement, not replace, direct instruction.

## Design Implications
