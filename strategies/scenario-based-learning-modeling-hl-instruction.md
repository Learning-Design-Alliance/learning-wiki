# Use realistic scenario-based learning, digital access, and modeling of clear communication in health literacy instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on health literacy intervention research from clinical settings, the article identifies transferable design strategies for university-based HL instruction. The authors state that "The commonality between studies indicates the significance of realistic and explicit scenario-based learning [42,44,45], which can be achieved through internet access to develop digital HL skills." They also highlight modeling within programs, including modeling clear communication and teach-back techniques, as useful to replicate in classroom settings, and cite recommendations for interactive pedagogy and critical consciousness strategies structured around knowledge, compass, skills, and action.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios must be realistic and explicit, supported by internet access to develop digital health literacy skills
#### Constraints
- The cited clinical-setting studies were not specific to initial teacher education, and the recommended four-part structure has not yet been pretested and posttested

### Target Learners
- preservice teachers in university initial teacher education programs

### Target Learning Goals
- health literacy and electronic health literacy
- critical health literacy for social action
