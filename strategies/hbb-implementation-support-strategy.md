# Improve HBB implementation through better work routines, equipment maintenance, psychological safety, and training that explains neonatal physiology

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a bundle of implementation supports for HBB in low-resource settings: better routines for organising work, cleaning and maintaining equipment, improved staffing and reduced staff rotations, recognition and incentives to raise motivation, and psychological safety. Training should go beyond how and when to resuscitate and address "the physiological reason behind it", including the role of mucus and secretions, so that suction does not delay uninterrupted ventilation. The authors state their findings "can guide the development of neonatal resuscitation training in low-resource settings".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hospital management must enable equipment maintenance routines, adequate supplies and staffing for the strategy to function as described
#### Constraints
- Findings are context-specific to one referral hospital's high-risk labour ward in 2018 and transferability should be approached with caution; further studies are needed to identify effective long-term strategies

### Target Learners
- clinically active midwives
- doctors and other birth attendants in low- and middle-income countries

### Target Learning Goals
- neonatal resuscitation skill
- fidelity to the HBB protocol
- understanding of neonatal physiology and transition to breathing
