# Restructure LTT courses: add brief and debrief, restrict attendance to sufficiently experienced learners, and reserve LTT for objectives no alternative simulator can meet

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's Table 5 sets out phase-by-phase recommendations (preparing, briefing, simulating, debriefing/reflecting, evaluating) combining simulation best practices, learning theory, and the study's own empirical themes. The three most significant are introducing a formal brief and debrief to the schedule, clarifying and restricting attendance to learners with sufficient technical experience, and using LTT only for complex procedures with no suitable alternative simulator, focusing on the animal as a patient and maximising contextual and clinical realism.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course directors must reconceptualise LTT as a simulated learning event rather than a clinical learning experience
- Faculty able to facilitate debriefing with psychological safety
#### Constraints
- The authors argue LTT remains ethically contentious and should follow the 3Rs; alternatives should be used wherever a procedure can be practiced on another simulator model

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate surgical trainees at an appropriate stage of training
- Trauma surgery course directors and faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- Integrated technical and non-technical trauma surgical skills
- Reflection and transfer of learning to clinical practice
