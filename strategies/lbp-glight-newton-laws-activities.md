# Use game constructions (frictionless tracks, bomb forces, piston-sled) to explore Newton's laws

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Little Big Planet and Guardian of light support activities for Newton's laws: objects on frictionless 'glass' tracks show uniform motion; bombs placed on one versus opposite sides of a sphere demonstrate the vector nature of force; and a piston pushing against a wall on a sled with a pendulum accelerometer illustrates the third law. The authors observe that "forces of the same magnitude can hav e diﬀerent eﬀects depending on their direction."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Games with material properties (e.g., a frictionless material), placeable force sources, and constructible machines such as pistons and pendulums
#### Constraints
- The frictionless-track activity is suggested as a prelude to a real lecture demonstration based on airtracks

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Newton's first, second, and third laws; vector nature of force
