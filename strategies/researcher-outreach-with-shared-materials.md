# Researchers should run school outreach in their specialty and share adaptable lesson materials

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors advocate that networks researchers (and scientists generally) engage in school outreach, arguing it lets students interact with specialists, see scientists as regular human beings, and experience discovery firsthand. They explicitly invite reuse: "we encourage you to use, steal, adapt, and improve any material in this article or in the SOM", and ask for contributions toward a large repository of networks-related school activities. They also encourage school teachers to include network science in lessons and math/science clubs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Willing volunteers from the research community, including professors, postdocs, doctoral students, and undergraduates
- Adaptable materials, since activities and modules need to change from one school to another
#### Constraints
- Outreach takes time away from the existing school curriculum, though the authors judge it time well spent
- Working with weaker students is more difficult, but the authors call this struggle a necessary one

### Target Learners
- School students aged 13–16, especially those who have not yet chosen subject specializations

### Target Learning Goals
- Motivating students to pursue mathematics and science
- Building appreciation of what scientists actually do
