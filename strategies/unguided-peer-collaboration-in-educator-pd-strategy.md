# Incorporate unguided peer collaboration into professional development programs for early educators, supported by communities of practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy has early career educators (TAs, teachers-in-training, pre-college educators) work together on content problems without instructor guidance, so they experience collaborative learning first-hand while deepening content knowledge. The article recommends that "Incorporating structured peer collaboration activities in such programs, even in unguided formats, can be an effective strategy for improving their teaching outcomes both due to improved content and pedagogical knowledge." Because it requires no leader time investment, the article proposes embedding it in communities of practice where educators connect in person or virtually (e.g., via Zoom).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supportive community of practice of like-minded educators with shared goals and a sense of belonging
- Content relevant to what participants are likely to teach
#### Constraints
- The article hypothesizes outcomes may have been positively influenced by participants being a close-knit cohort, and notes value of investigating collaboration when group members lack shared belonging

### Target Learners
- first-year physics graduate TAs
- early career and pre-college STEM educators

### Target Learning Goals
- content knowledge in topics they teach
- pedagogical knowledge of collaborative learning
- confidence facilitating peer collaboration
