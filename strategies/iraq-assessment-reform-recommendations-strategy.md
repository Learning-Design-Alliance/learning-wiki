# Reform Iraqi student assessment through a national framework with standardized rubrics, mandatory faculty training, peer-reviewed grading, structured remediation pathways, and external accreditation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's reform agenda combines system-level and faculty-level actions: a nationwide framework with "standardized rubrics and competency-based grading criteria," priority faculty training in competency-based assessment and remediation, peer-reviewed grading of high-stakes exams, clear faculty-supervised remediation pathways, and accreditation aligned with international bodies such as WFME and ECFMG. The authors propose implementing these stepwise, starting with transparent grading criteria and training workshops.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty training programs should be made available online or in a hybrid format and linked to academic or professional advancement to encourage participation
#### Constraints
- Immediate implementation may be hampered by institutional resistance, cultural obstacles, and resource limitations; the authors recommend a stepwise approach and a trial phase in selected universities

### Target Learners
- medical and health sciences educators in Iraq
- medical, dental, nursing, pharmacy, and laboratory science students

### Target Learning Goals
- competency-based student assessment
- academic integrity in grading
- effective remediation of underperforming students
