# Ground physician counseling curricula in theory with central experiential learning, start early, and strengthen nutrition and exercise instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on a 2020 systematic review of weight-related communication trainings and their own findings, the authors recommend that physician trainings be grounded in a theoretical framework and emphasize patient-centered communication, with experiential learning and skill development as central components. They state that "Future WMC curricula must improve instruction in nutrition and exercise, along with behavioral change counseling skills" and that experiential education should start early in medical school and be boosted throughout the four years of training. They also recommend real-time assessments of the specific WMC curriculum in comparison schools in future studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curricula combining didactics, interactive counseling practice (role-play or standardized patients), and observed clinical experiences
#### Constraints
- Medical schools are restrained in adding curricula; students struggled to provide specific nutrition and physical activity guidance even after general behavioral counseling training

### Target Learners
- medical students
- physician trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- weight management counseling
- nutrition and exercise guidance
- behavioral change counseling skills
- confidence in delivering counseling
