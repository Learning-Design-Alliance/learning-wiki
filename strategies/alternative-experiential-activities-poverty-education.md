# Explore alternative experiential learning activities for students who do not respond positively to poverty simulations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends alternatives for students who do not benefit from the Community Action Poverty Simulation. It suggests that "Alternatives to poverty simulations might include service learning or panel presentations featuring patients in poverty" and that "an experiential learning activity like trying to find a dentist who accepts Medicaid may make an impact." It also cites comparative evidence that a table-top simulation had a more profound effect than CAPS on nursing students' willingness to help and their empathy toward those in poverty.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Alternative activities should be assessed for their impact on attitudes and intentions to treat patients in poverty.
#### Constraints
- The article notes research has not yet identified an instructional method that reliably increases intentions to treat.

### Target Learners
- predoctoral dental students
- health professions students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of poverty and barriers to care
- intention to treat low-income and Medicaid patients
