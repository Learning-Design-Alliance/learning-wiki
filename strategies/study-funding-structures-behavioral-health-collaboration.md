# Continue studying alternative funding structures for behavioral health improvement efforts to identify which best promote cross-sector collaboration and student benefit

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The brief's conclusion recommends that California's differing funding mechanisms for behavioral health improvement be evaluated. SBHIP funneled funding through managed care plans to incentivize MCP-COE collaboration, while more recent TA investments for the new fee schedule provide funding directly to COEs, which then direct most funding to LEAs. The authors state these "different structures for funding behavioral health improvement efforts should continue to be studied to understand which models most effectively promote collaboration between health care and education" and which provide the greatest benefit to students through expanded access to programs and services.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ongoing study comparing funding routed through managed care plans versus funding directed to COEs and then LEAs
#### Constraints
- California currently faces a substantial budget deficit, making it more important to maximize existing TA investments toward long-term system improvements

### Target Learners
- State policymakers, local educational agencies, and health care partners

### Target Learning Goals
- Promoting collaboration between health care and education and expanding student access to behavioral health programs and services
