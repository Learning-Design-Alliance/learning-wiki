# Extend de-escalation training with early-agitation recognition, an advanced fourth-year SP workshop, and a postcurriculum OSCE

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their evaluation, the authors recommend a practical extension of the curriculum: "include training to identify the signs and symptoms of early agitation so as to prevent escalation of the situation." They also plan "an advanced SP workshop on verbal de-escalation in the fourth year to allow more opportunities to practice in more complex situations, such as encounters with family members," and suggest an assessment component with a postcurriculum OSCE to assess skill acquisition, arguing a verbal de-escalation curriculum across all medical schools would bridge the training gap.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An existing SP program and clerkship infrastructure comparable to the University of Pittsburgh's longitudinal standardized patient curriculum
#### Constraints
- The authors frame these as future extensions; none were implemented or evaluated in this article

### Target Learners
- third- and fourth-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- recognize early signs of agitation to prevent escalation
- practice de-escalation in more complex situations such as encounters with family members
