# Design formal professional development to strengthen teachers' involvement in informal activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because informal professional development predicted self‐efficacy while formal professional development did not, the authors recommend that "formal training could be designed to strengthen teachers' involvement in informal activities". They suggest this could be done "by enhancing more collegial exchange (Van Waes et al., 2015) and stimulating teachers' future efforts to read educational materials (Bednall & Sanders, 2017)".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formal training should be designed to enhance collegial exchange and stimulate future reading of educational materials
#### Constraints
- Formal professional development was not strongly associated with self‐efficacy beliefs in this study

### Target Learners
- University teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Professional development of teaching skills and self‐efficacy
