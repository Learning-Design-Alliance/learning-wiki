# Add a four-stage experiential learning session two weeks after volunteer training to sustain motivation and boost activity involvement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy schedules a three-to-three-and-a-half-hour experiential learning session roughly two weeks after an initial lecture-based training, so learners progress through concrete experience, reflection, conceptualization, and active experimentation with a facilitator. The authors conclude that "The experiential learning program for dementia supporters can be used to improve other volunteer and professional programs," and that Kolb's theory can support individuals with dementia in the community.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An initial training session completed beforehand, so participants hold basic knowledge and perceive the activity's importance
- A facilitator able to create an affable atmosphere, encourage self-expression, give positive feedback, and help participants face new challenges
- Real interaction opportunities with the target population
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single daycare center with one facilitator; the authors call for replication across centers before the findings' robustness is established

### Target Learners
- Adult community volunteers trained as dementia supporters
- Trainers of volunteers and professionals in comparable programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained motivation for community support activity
- Translating training knowledge into actual support activity
