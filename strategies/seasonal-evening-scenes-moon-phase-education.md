# Use Sinterklaas, Santa Claus, Halloween and Sint Maarten evening scenes as moon phase education opportunities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (2 design, 1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that the happy end-of-the-year seasonal events be exploited by educators to teach the physical origin of moon phases. The author states that "The Halloween, Sint Maarten, Sinterklaas, and Santa Claus settings provide wonderful opportunities for moon phase education, through simple naked eye observations." The strategy works because evening scenes should show waxing, first quarter or full moons, giving learners a familiar, culturally salient setting in which the required insight — that full moon rises at sunset and first quarter sets around midnight — can be checked by direct observation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need "A tiny bit of insight that full moon, being opposite to the sun, rises when the sun sets and sets when the sun rises, that first quarter moon, being at ~90o, sets around midnight, and that last quarter moon, at ~270o, rises around that time"
#### Constraints
- The claims concern illustrations for evening scenes; in a Santa scene late in the night the moon "can have all phases"

### Target Learners
- children and the general public encountering seasonal illustrations

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the physical origin of the moon phases and the timing of evening moon visibility
