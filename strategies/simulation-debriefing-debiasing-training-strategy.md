# Focused simulation-based training with debriefing and de-biasing strategies to help doctors recognize and manage cognitive errors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extending the simulation program's debriefing focus beyond teamwork and leadership to cognitive errors, proposing that "focused simulation based training could help doctors to recognize and manage situations where they are at risk for cognitive errors". They note that metacognition and de-biasing strategies common to simulation-based learning have been demonstrated in other fields to be effective in cognitive error prevention and recovery, and that simulation allows errors to evolve safely as a learning catalyst.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- High-fidelity simulation facilities, trained facilitators using advocacy-inquiry debriefing, and scenarios engineered to elicit targeted cognitive errors
#### Constraints
- Simulation based teaching is resource intensive; in this project setup and recurring costs of approximately $200,000 were distributed across several teaching programs

### Target Learners
- post graduate doctors
- medical students
- nurses and allied staff

### Target Learning Goals
- recognition, avoidance and recovery from cognitive errors in clinical reasoning
