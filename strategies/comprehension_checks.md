# Comprehension Checks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Comprehension checks are brief, targeted questions or tasks inserted at intervals during reading, listening, or instruction to verify that learners are constructing the intended meaning. They interrupt passive reception, surface misconceptions while they can still be corrected, and give the instructor real-time evidence for adjusting pacing and support.

## Design Implications

Comprehension checks convert reading from passive input into retrieval practice, which strengthens retention far more than rereading [active-learning-improves-exam-performance](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. Their formative value depends on what happens next: checks must be followed by feedback or instructional adjustment, not merely administered [Black & Wiliam's synthesis of formative assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md) [+S]. Questions should target the *why* of the text — causal links, inference, and main ideas — rather than surface detail, since process-level questioning produces deeper understanding than fact-level probing [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear model of what understanding should look like at each point in the text, so checks target the right content
- Questions pitched to require construction (inference, prediction, summarizing), not yes/no recognition
- A mechanism for responding to answers — reteaching, [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md), or [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) — so the check actually changes instruction

#### Constraints
- Checks that ask only for literal recall can fragment the text and disrupt the construction of a coherent situation model [~M] — over-frequent low-level questioning reduces comprehension of connected prose
- If answers are not acted upon, checks become assessment theater; the formative benefit collapses without instructional response [-S]
- Public questioning can suppress honest "I don't know" responses from low-confidence learners; anonymous formats (response cards, clickers, quick writes) mitigate this [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Embedded questions**: questions inserted between text segments during reading
- **Stop-and-jot**: learners write a one-sentence summary or prediction at a pause point
- **Peer explanation**: learners explain a segment to a partner, making understanding audible [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]
- **Digital polling**: tools like [Kahoot!](https://kahoot.com) or [Poll Everywhere](https://www.polleverywhere.com) aggregate responses instantly and anonymize errors
- **Reciprocal teaching**: learners rotate the role of questioner, internalizing the checking behavior

### Target Learners
- Developing readers and multilingual learners, who may mask comprehension gaps by decoding fluently [~M]
- Novices in any domain, who lack the background knowledge to detect their own misunderstandings [~M]
- Less beneficial for expert readers monitoring their own comprehension, who already self-check [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Literal and inferential comprehension of connected text
- Monitoring and repair of one's own understanding (comprehension monitoring)
- Retention of key content through retrieval [active-learning-improves-exam-performance](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. Segment the text or lesson at natural meaning boundaries — [Chunking](../principles/chunking) reduces the working-memory load of each segment [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
2. Before reading, set a purpose with an [Advance Organizer](../elements/advance-organizers.md) so learners know what the checks will target
3. At each pause point, ask one targeted question requiring inference or summarizing, not literal recall
4. Collect responses (quick write, pair-share, polling) and scan for misconception patterns
5. Respond immediately: reteach, model with a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md), or release into [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)
6. Fade the external checks over time, teaching learners to generate their own questions — consistent with [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md)

## Examples
- **Reciprocal Teaching** (Palincsar & Brown) — small groups rotate predicting, questioning, clarifying, and summarizing roles during reading; the questioning role is a learner-run comprehension check
- **[Newsela](https://newsela.com)** — leveled articles with embedded quiz questions at each reading level, providing checks calibrated to text difficulty
- **KWL charts** — the "What do you know? / What did you learn?" columns frame checks before and after reading
