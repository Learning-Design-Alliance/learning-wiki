# Target UK overseas graduates for additional transition support when returning to practise in the UK

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that policy highlight UK overseas graduates as a group that could be targeted for additional support with transitioning to UK postgraduate training, because they are culturally and linguistically unlikely to stand out from home graduates and so may not be identified as needing help. It reports that "UK OGs were more likely to receive a ‘less than satisfactory’ outcome at ARCP" and that NHS experience predicted better outcomes, implying additional training opportunities could be effective.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of UK overseas graduates within training cohorts, since culturally and linguistically they would not be expected to stand out from home medical graduates
#### Constraints
- The article notes it is unclear what proportion of non-UK graduates use the Alternative Certificate of Foundation Competence route or whether they are disadvantaged
- The recommendation rests on observational findings, which the authors state do not give rise to causal explanations

### Target Learners
- UK-national doctors who obtained their primary medical qualification abroad and are entering UK postgraduate specialty training

### Target Learning Goals
- Successful progression through postgraduate specialty training (satisfactory ARCP outcomes) and transition to UK clinical practice
