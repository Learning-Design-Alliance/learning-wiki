# Form lab groups of three or more students avoiding groups with exactly one woman

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A group-formation strategy for physics labs intended to reduce women's isolation, either from exclusion in self-chosen groups or from group-mates not valuing their contributions. The review states that "one solution to reducing isolation is by ensuring that groups of 3 or more students are formed in such a way as to include either 0 or 2 or more women; in other words, avoiding forming groups with exactly 1 woman". The suggestion originates from Heller et al. and was incorporated into curricular projects such as SCALE-UP.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor control over group composition in courses with groups of three or more students
#### Constraints
- The review notes some students may find the strategy disconcerting, and a grouping strategy effective in introductory college labs may not transfer to high school classrooms

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics lab students

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable participation in collaborative lab work
- inclusive learning environment
