# Integrate layered student support services across the transitions from middle and high school, into college, and through college

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Guidebook's Step 5 organizes support services by transition: supporting students in middle and high school, during the transition to college, and during college. Its example assumptions enumerate dual enrollment and near-peer mentoring on high school campuses, family presentations on affordability, campus field trips, FAFSA and Dream Act completion workshops, summer orientation and courses, "personalized text messaging as well as follow-up phone calls and face-to-face meetings," an early alert system, required counseling support twice a year, and tutoring with an optional cohort study area. A Student Support Services Worksheet supports planning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordinate support services with internal programs such as EOPS, DSPS, MESA, FYE, TRIO, and peer mentoring
#### Constraints
- Some components especially target first-generation and low-income students, who are assumed less likely to receive external family and community support

### Target Learners
- prospective and enrolled community college students, especially first-generation and low-income students

### Target Learning Goals
- college readiness, transition to college, academic progress, and completion
