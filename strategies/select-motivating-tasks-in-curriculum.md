# Select motivating tasks and include more motivating practices in course books and curriculum programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that content designers and teachers choose effective teaching instruments and build motivating practices into materials, because the study's two task types differed mainly in the motivation they offered learners. As the authors put it, "the main difference between the tasks is the amount of motivation which they offer to learn a foreign language". The recommendation follows their finding that the CALL-based task group outperformed the written-question group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks should be selected that offer learners motivation to learn the foreign language, as the article attributes the group difference to motivation
#### Constraints
- The article states it only indicated the superiority of CALL-based tasks over the control group and said nothing clear about advantages and disadvantages of specific technology and non-technology instruments

### Target Learners
- EFL learners in junior high school

### Target Learning Goals
- English grammar learning
