# Process Writing (Journals)

## Description
Process writing uses journals to encourage writing skills from the beginning levels of English proficiency. Learners write frequently, focusing on communication rather than perfect grammar. Mistakes are addressed gradually, based on material covered in class. Journal entries are written on one side of the page, with teacher feedback on the back. Topics can include hobbies, work, or observations.  It eases learners’ fears about producing subpar written work, encouraging them to improve their writing abilities. Only mistakes made in grammar and vocabulary that has been covered up to the current point in time should be corrected.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Notebooks, regular writing prompts, teacher feedback, class time for writing and review
#### Constraints
- Requires consistent teacher feedback, may not suit learners who need immediate error correction

## Impact
- Encourages writing from early stages, promotes gradual skill development, aligns instruction with learner progress. Addresses the reality that adults are often hesitant to produce written or spoken work that is not up to the same level as their native language skills.
