# Teach evidence-based general rules (One Minute Preceptor) to address knowledge gaps about disparities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For learners diagnosed with a knowledge gap, the paper recommends time-effective strategies, chief among them the One Minute Preceptor (five-step microskills) model: "The hallmark of this model is the teaching of general rules to address gaps in knowledge." A general rule is brief, applicable to the case at hand, addresses the learner's need, and highlights principles transferable to other patients. Teaching evidence-based rules about disparities also signals that the topic "are amenable to discussion and inquiry based on evidence."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A diagnosed knowledge gap in the learner and a brief, case-applicable rule grounded in evidence
#### Constraints
- The paper recommends deferring skill teaching when clinic pace does not allow it, as in the knee replacement scenario

### Target Learners
- Residents
- Medical students on clinical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge of documented disparities (e.g., joint replacement rates, asthma severity underestimation) and general principles for equitable care
