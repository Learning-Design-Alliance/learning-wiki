Dedicate specific course time and themes (such as 'Me and others') to developing balanced self-monitoring from a young age¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In its conclusion, the article recommends that curricula devote explicit space to self-monitoring development. It states that "Conducting the courses or themes such as „Me and others‟ taught in some countries in our country will help us to raise a happy, well -adjusted and productive individual", so that more and specific time can be devoted to raising individuals who monitor themselves in a balanced way. It further argues that developing self-monitoring at a young age may contribute to later professional competencies, and calls for continued research given that even 14-18 week studies show partial effects.

Claims¶

  • Self-monitoring strategies used during or at the end of Social Studies lessons moved fifth-graders' self-monitoring scores significantly closer to the scale's ideal value than formal curriculum teaching [+M]
  • Self-monitoring strategies used during or at the end of Social Studies lessons significantly improved fifth-graders' academic achievement compared to formal curriculum teaching [+M]
  • Teachers and students reported that self-monitoring strategies supported academic self-monitoring but produced little behavioral change and were not used for self-regulation [-W]
