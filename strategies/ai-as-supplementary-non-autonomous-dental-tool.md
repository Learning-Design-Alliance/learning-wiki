# Use AI chatbots as supportive, non-autonomous supplements in dental education and practice, with expert oversight

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating AI models into dental education and clinical work only in supportive roles rather than as autonomous decision-makers. It states that "AI should only be used in supportive, non-autonomous capacities in dentistry practice, ideally as a component of a larger clinical decision support system that is supervised by qualified experts", and that limits must be recognised so AI supplements rather than replaces human expertise. In teaching, the models are positioned as supplements offering instant feedback and reinforcing fundamental concepts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recognition of the systems' limits and supervision by qualified experts
#### Constraints
- Relying solely on AI-generated responses without careful review may result in misunderstandings or improper treatment planning

### Target Learners
- dental students
- dental educators
- clinicians

### Target Learning Goals
- formative assessment
- self-directed learning
- reinforcement of fundamental dental concepts
