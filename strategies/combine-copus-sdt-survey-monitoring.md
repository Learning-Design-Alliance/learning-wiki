# Combine COPUS observation with an SDT-based survey to monitor progress toward active learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors and departments "interested in flexible methods to monitor progress toward active learning practices in biology and other STEM disciplines by combining the COPUS and self-determination survey results" use the two instruments together. COPUS profiles document the level of active teaching, while the SDT survey captures students' affective response, including competence, autonomy support, peer-relatedness, and effort. Observation notes add detail about how practices were implemented beyond what the codes capture.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An expert observer administering COPUS with supplemental descriptive notes
- A validated SDT-based survey administered during the course
#### Constraints
- Observation protocols alone may not capture the differential impact of how activities are perceived by students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate STEM students
- instructors and departments in biology and other STEM disciplines

### Target Learning Goals
- monitoring adoption of active learning practices
- student motivation
