# Assign students to write MCQ on specific course topics shortly before exams as a low-cost learning activity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes an implementable activity: students are invited in a first lecture to write four MCQ with four potential choices on a randomly assigned topic, with a small bonus (up to 0.25 of 10 points) for quality, and all students get access to the questions. The authors state "This intervention is not time-consuming. Thus, it is easy to apply in large groups." Questions are evaluated on importance, adequate writing, unambiguity, middle difficulty and originality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions are evaluated against criteria including "importance of the topic, adequately written, unambiguous question (only one valid answer), middle difficulty, and originality"
- A small incentive (up to 0.25 points out of 10) is used to stimulate participation
#### Constraints
- The beneficial learning effect may be evident only if questions are good enough; fewer than 50% of second-year and fewer than 20% of third-year students wrote at least two good questions
- The benefit was not significant in all comparisons

### Target Learners
- medical students in second and third year

### Target Learning Goals
- General Pathology (Immunopathology, electrolyte and acid-base status)
- Pathophysiology (Blood, Respiratory system)
