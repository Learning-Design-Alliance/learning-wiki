# Systematically diagnose barriers to CDSS receipt and use, then design implementation strategies that directly address them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because high print rates and satisfaction coexisted with low self-reported use and very low engagement with supplemental tools, the authors recommend that "Future efforts should focus on systematically understanding the barriers to CDSS use and designing implementation strategies that directly address these barriers." They frame this within implementation science frameworks developed in partnership with clinicians, care teams, leaders, patients, and care partners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Partnership with clinicians, care teams, clinic leaders, patients and care partners; use of implementation science frameworks to identify barriers before designing supports
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single integrated health system in the upper Midwest with an existing non-CI CDSS; results may vary in other settings

### Target Learners
- Primary care clinicians and clinical implementation teams

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained adoption of clinical decision support in routine care
