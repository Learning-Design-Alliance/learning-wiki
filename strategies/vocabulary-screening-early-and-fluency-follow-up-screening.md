# Use vocabulary screening in early childhood and fluency or vocabulary screening after comprehension tests to catch foundational deficits

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends screening practices at two points. In early grades, "The increased use of vocabulary screening in early childhood may assist in early identification of children who may be at risk for reading comprehension difficulties due to vocabulary and oral language delays," helping detect late-emerging reading disabilities. For older students, it cites the suggestion of using a fluency assessment following a comprehension test, and notes group-administered fluency or vocabulary tests can be efficient screening methods that also reveal systemic instructional issues across a school or district.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Screening must extend beyond state accountability test results, which may not indicate whether low scores stem from poor text reading or vocabulary skills
#### Constraints
- Prior state test performance may be the strongest indicator of future state test performance, but does not diagnose component skill deficits

### Target Learners
- early childhood students
- adolescents at risk for reading failure

### Target Learning Goals
- early identification of reading comprehension risk
- alignment of intervention with foundational skill needs
