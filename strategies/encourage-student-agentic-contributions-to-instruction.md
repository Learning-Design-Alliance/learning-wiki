# Encourage students to express preferences, ask questions, and communicate needs and interests to their teacher

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article characterizes agentic engagement through concrete classroom acts students can perform: students can "express their preferences, ask questions, and let the teacher know what they need, want, and are interested in." The five retained AES items operationalize these acts, such as "During class, I ask questions to help me learn" and "I let my teacher know what I am interested in." Teachers and instructional designers can treat these student-initiated contributions as a lever for creating more supportive learning environments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A classroom climate in which students can safely communicate preferences, questions, and needs to the teacher
#### Constraints
- The strategy is student-initiated rather than teacher-initiated; the article distinguishes it from teacher-initiated formats such as formative assessments and clickers

### Target Learners
- middle-school students
- college students

### Target Learning Goals
- greater achievement
- greater perceived autonomy support
