# Creating a Digital Library Section for SEL Topics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A digital library includes a dedicated section of books focused on social and emotional learning (SEL) topics — empathy, managing emotions, relationships, problem-solving, perseverance, and perspective-taking. Titles are organized by topic, skill, or grade level so educators can integrate SEL into the curriculum and students can locate resources matched to their needs. The curated section signals that SEL is a legitimate curricular priority and lowers the search cost of finding relevant literature.

## Design Implications

Curated access matters because literature can serve as a safe, indirect route into emotionally charged topics: narrative fiction in particular has been linked to improved perspective-taking and social cognition [Reading literary fiction improves social cognition.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [~M] — though effect sizes are contested and depend on text quality and reader engagement. A library section alone does not teach SEL skills; outcomes depend on how the texts are used in [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md), or structured reflection.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A digital library platform with search and tagging functionality
- A curated collection of SEL-relevant titles, vetted for developmental appropriateness and diverse perspectives
- A categorization scheme (by skill, topic, and grade level) that teachers and students can navigate
- Staff orientation to the collection so curation translates into classroom use

#### Constraints
- A browsable collection without instructional integration produces little measurable SEL growth [-M] — access to books about empathy does not itself build empathy without discussion, role-play, or application
- Requires device access and digital literacy; inequitable access shifts benefits toward already-advantaged students [-M]
- Collections age quickly; unmaintained sections surface outdated or developmentally mismatched titles [-W]
- Text-based approaches under-serve students with weak reading fluency, for whom comprehension demands crowd out the social-emotional content [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Organize by SEL competency (CASEL's five: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making) rather than by genre
- Teacher-facing curated lists (read-alouds by grade band) versus student-facing browsing with topic tags
- Pair each title with a discussion guide or [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) prompt to convert reading into dialogue
- Audiobook versions extend access to striving readers ([Audiobooks](../principles/audiobooks.md))

### Target Learners
- K–12 students, with the strongest evidence for narrative-based social learning in elementary and middle grades [~M]
- Students who respond better to indirect, story-mediated discussion of emotional topics than to direct instruction [-W] — some students need explicit teaching instead
- Teachers seeking curriculum-integrated SEL materials rather than stand-alone SEL lessons

### Target Learning Goals
- Social awareness and perspective-taking through character identification [Reading literary fiction improves social cognition.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [~M]
- Emotional vocabulary and self-awareness through [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md)-vetted texts
- Belonging and identity: seeing one's own experience reflected in the collection [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Audit needs: survey teachers and students for SEL topics and gaps relevant to the school community.
2. Curate and tag titles by SEL competency, topic, and grade level; prioritize diverse voices and authentic representation.
3. Build the section in the digital library platform with student-facing topic browsing and teacher-facing curated lists ([Assigned Readings](../elements/assigned-readings.md)).
4. Attach a discussion prompt, [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), or role-play task ([Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md)) to each title so reading leads to application ([Application](../elements/application.md)).
5. Model use: demonstrate how to search the section and how to talk about characters' emotions ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)).
6. Close the loop with [Whole-Class Sharing](../elements/whole-class-sharing.md) or small-group discussion ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).
7. Review usage data and the collection each term; retire and replace titles.

- [Epic!](https://www.getepic.com) — school digital library with curated SEL collections for elementary readers
- [Sora (OverDrive)](https://soraapp.com) — school ebook platform supporting curated collections and usage analytics

## Examples
- **Booksource** maintains a dedicated SEL section allowing teachers and students to search titles by specific SEL skills (empathy, perseverance, managing emotions) and grade band.
- **Epic!** offers educator-curated SEL collections organized by theme, with read-to-me audio support for emergent readers.
- A middle school organizes its Sora collection by CASEL competency and pairs each title with an advisory-period discussion prompt.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track section usage (checkouts, searches by topic tag) and survey teachers on integration frequency.
- Analyze student discussion contributions and written responses for growth in emotional vocabulary and perspective-taking.
- Audit the collection annually for diversity, currency, and developmental fit.

## Impact
- SEL programming broadly improves academic performance, attitudes, and behavior [SEL programs improve academic achievement and social behavior.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+S], and a curated digital library lowers the cost of integrating literature into that programming. The library itself is an enabling condition, not an intervention; measured impact comes from the instructional uses built on top of it.
