# Integrate nutrition-related competencies horizontally and vertically and teach them in practical teaching formats from the start of studies through specialist training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend embedding nutrition-related competencies across the whole medical curriculum rather than in isolated units. Building on the Charité Berlin communication curriculum example and problem-oriented learning, they "recommend to integrate nutrition-related competencies both horizontally and vertically and to teach them in practical teaching formats from the start of studies through to specialist medical training". This responds to the finding that conventional lecture-based nutrition teaching transfers poorly to practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A competency-based core curriculum such as the NKLM that names the nutrition-related learning objectives to be integrated
#### Constraints
- The article notes it seems difficult to implement mandatory culinary medicine courses at this point in time given the great amount of content in medical studies

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- paediatrics specialists in training

### Target Learning Goals
- preventive nutrition counselling competencies
- communication competencies
- nutritional knowledge
