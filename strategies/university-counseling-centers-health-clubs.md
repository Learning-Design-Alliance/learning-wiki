# Establish university counseling centers and campus health clubs promoting regular exercise, weight control and healthy lifestyle behaviors.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's closing recommendation: universities should take an active institutional role in student wellbeing. The authors state that universities "should take an active role to establish counseling centers, and health clubs" to promote students' wellbeing and healthy lifestyle behaviors, such as regular exercise and weight control, based on the observed associations with academic performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- University institutional commitment and campus infrastructure for counseling and health-club facilities, as recommended by the authors.
#### Constraints
- The supporting associations come from a cross-sectional study; the authors state future research is required to examine whether they represent causal relationships.

### Target Learners
- female undergraduate health-college students at King Khalid University, Saudi Arabia
- university students generally, as addressed by the recommendation

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance (cumulative GPA)
- adoption of regular exercise and healthy lifestyle behaviors

## Claims

- [Physically active students (38.8%) showed a significantly higher overall mean of academic performance than physically inactive students (61.2%) (F = 3.106, p = 0.027).](../claims/active-students-higher-gpa.md) [+W]

- [Students without chronic disease had significantly higher academic performance (p = 0.000) and lower BMI (p = 0.026) than students with chronic illness.](../claims/chronic-disease-lower-gpa-higher-bmi.md) [+W]
