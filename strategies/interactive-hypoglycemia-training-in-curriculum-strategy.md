# Incorporate interactive, lecture-based hypoglycemia training with hands-on glucagon practice into the medical school curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend incorporating interactive teaching on hypoglycemia into medical education, noting that "incorporating interactive, lecture-based teaching methods into the medical school curriculum is recommended" because clinical recognition of hypoglycemia is typically unfamiliar to students. The strategy pairs a brief interactive lecture with practical tools: the 15-15 Rule, a hypoglycemia treatment kit, and hands-on glucagon kit practice, framed by patient-perspective content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interactive lecturing techniques rather than standard didactic presentation
- Access to glucagon emergency kits for demonstration and group practice
#### Constraints
- The study lacked a control group; a randomized comparison against standard teaching content is still needed

### Target Learners
- medical students
- healthcare providers in training

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing and managing hypoglycemia risk
- glucagon administration
- patient-centered diabetes care
