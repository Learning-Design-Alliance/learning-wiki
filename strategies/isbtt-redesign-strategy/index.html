Redesign ISBTT programs with explicit interprofessional learning goals, power-focused debriefing, matched facilitators, and longitudinal formats¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's conclusions recommend that programs add "learning with, from and about each other" as an explicit goal, use debriefing to create space for discussing how power dynamics, hierarchies, and conflicts influence collaboration, involve facilitators from the same professions as participants, favor longitudinal experiences, and target participants at similar training levels to reduce power discrepancies.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Skillful facilitation is essential; the review states facilitator training to ensure they possess the relevant skills is essential

Constraints¶

  • The review cautions that some of these elements have been incompletely studied, including whether matching facilitator profession is always best and whether facilitator skill may matter more than background

Target Learners¶

  • Interprofessional teams of health professionals and students in simulation-based training

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Interprofessional collaborative practice across all four IPEC domains

Affordances¶

  • Power Intergroup Relations Lens For Isbtt

Claims¶

  • Most ISBTT studies report improvement in collaborative behaviors immediately after training, but longer-term and clinical-setting outcomes are rarely measured [+W]
  • Most ISBTT programs are one-time events involving two or three professions, with debriefing nearly universal and psychological safety rarely addressed [+W]
  • Assessed collaborative behaviors in ISBTT rarely cover all four IPEC domains, with values and ethics almost never assessed [+W]
  • Most published ISBTT programs are grounded in crisis resource management and related frameworks rather than interprofessional education frameworks [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Blend the PEARLS debrief with CHAT-guided facilitation to extend debriefing from the individual to the team and system
  • Reword learning outcomes with broadly applicable terms ('healthcare seeker', 'health professional') to support interprofessional application
