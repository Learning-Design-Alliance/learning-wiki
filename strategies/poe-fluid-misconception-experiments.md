# Use challenging, low-cost Predict–Observe–Explain experiments that put students' alternative conceptions about fluid pressure into play

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend experimental activities that confront students' ideas: "resulta conveniente presentar a los estudiantes actividades experimentales desafiantes y que pongan en juego sus concepciones alternativas". They specifically suggest the coin-blowing experiment adapted by Dutra et al. (2020), with the coin in a slot slightly larger than itself, as simple and low-cost, replicable in any classroom in a POE modality, and usable in science museums.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experiments must be chosen so that extrapolating higher speed–lower pressure leads to contradictions with the observed outcome.
#### Constraints
- The article recommends this activity on the basis of its analysis and prior literature; it did not test the activity's effectiveness in this study.

### Target Learners
- university students
- school students and museum visitors

### Target Learning Goals
- confronting and revising alternative conceptions about the velocity–pressure relation in fluids
