# Use Objective Structured Teaching Evaluation with a simulated learner to assess intraoperative teaching skills, since the UK Observation of Teaching assessment excludes teaching in the presence of a patient

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article identifies a gap: no formal teaching training or assessment for intraoperative teaching exists in UK surgical training, and the existing Observation of Teaching work-based assessment explicitly excludes teaching in the presence of a patient. The authors propose an alternative: "an alternative could be Objective Structured Teaching Evaluation (OSTE), which uses a simulated learner to assess teaching skills, and has been tested in Neurosurgical trainees teaching lumbar puncture and ventriculostomy." They also suggest unscrubbed Consultant observation and feedback, and a workshop on intra-operative teaching, which increased Fellows' interest and confidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulated learner and structured assessment format; Consultant time for observation and feedback on teaching
#### Constraints
- The article reports OSTE has been tested in neurosurgical trainees for lumbar puncture and ventriculostomy, not in paediatric appendicectomy; evidence for the workshop intervention is a single pilot study

### Target Learners
- Junior and Senior Registrars developing intraoperative teaching skills

### Target Learning Goals
- Intraoperative teaching skill
- Assessment of teaching performance in the operating theatre
