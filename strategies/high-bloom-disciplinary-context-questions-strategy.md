# Use high-Bloom-level questions rich in disciplinary or real-world context to drive team discussion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' first recommendation for practice is that instructors "use questions that are themselves at a high Bloom’s level/conceptual level and, further, that are rich in the context of the discipline", so that questions "can only be answered via a process of disciplinary thinking". They note many course questions were framed in real-world, usually medical, contexts that students may find inherently motivating, echoing the TBL criterion of significance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor time to write application questions in meaningful disciplinary or clinical contexts
#### Constraints
- For some concepts or learning goals, a true real-world or clinical application is difficult to construct; a similar effect may come from asking about a theoretical change to a system

### Target Learners
- Upper-level undergraduate biology majors in team-based courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Disciplinary conceptual reasoning rather than calculation or recall

## Claims

- [Co-construction of answers was common in team discourse, particularly in response to higher-level questions](../claims/co-construction-common-with-higher-level-questions.md) [+W]
