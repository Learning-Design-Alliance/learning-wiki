# Combine low-fidelity theoretical training with high-fidelity real-world application to prepare teams for complex tasks.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article states that "Theoretical knowledge in the low-fidelity setting seems to connect with real-world application in the high-fidelity setting" and that "the combination of settings as in this study prepare the C2 team to handle complex tasks". This suggests a sequential design in which low-fidelity simulation provides an advanced starting point and conceptual grounding before high-fidelity simulation. The low-fidelity setting supports training in communication, organizational matters, and the chain of command.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires both a low-fidelity setting (e.g., tabletop exercise) and a high-fidelity setting (e.g., a purpose-built training facility) using the same escalating scenario.
#### Constraints
- Low-fidelity simulations may pose challenges for novices due to their lower realism.

### Target Learners
- Naval command and control team members
- Experienced crew members

### Target Learning Goals
- Handling complex tasks
- Communication and organizational matters
- Understanding methodology and the chain of command
