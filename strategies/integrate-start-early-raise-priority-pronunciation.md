# Three-pronged approach to the time obstacle: integrate pronunciation, start early, and increase its priority via assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
To work around the time obstacle, the article recommends a three-pronged approach: "Integrate, start early, and increase its priority (e.g., via assessment)." Teachers integrate pronunciation into every lesson (e.g., microlessons on intonation within a grammar unit on wh-questions), begin instruction early even at beginner levels rather than reserving it for advanced electives, and push for explicit pronunciation-related learning outcomes and regular assessment so that teachers and learners perceive pronunciation as valued. The third part requires collaboration among teachers, learners, and programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration among teachers, learners, and programs for the priority/assessment component
- Institutional adoption of explicit pronunciation-related student learning outcomes and assessment
#### Constraints
- The article notes the priority-raising solution requires institutions to adjust in response to demand from teachers and learners

### Target Learners
- L2 learners of English at all proficiency levels, including beginners

### Target Learning Goals
- Regular, integrated pronunciation development across skill areas
