# Combine direct vocabulary teaching with multiple story encounters, especially for less-able learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that an ideal vocabulary instruction for EFL children should "incorporate both the direct teaching of words" and "vocabulary learning through multiple opportunities to encounter novel words in authentic and interesting texts". It highlights that at-risk children's word learning improves when read-aloud sessions include explicit vocabulary instruction and follow-up activities using the book-related words.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Read-aloud sessions should incorporate explicit vocabulary instruction and follow-up curricular activities engaging children in using the book-related words
#### Constraints
- Less-able children, in particular, are not likely to learn new words from just listening to stories

### Target Learners
- beginning EFL learners in elementary school

### Target Learning Goals
- vocabulary learning from story read-alouds
