# Organize laboratory meetings as short introduction followed by inquiry in which students explore qualitatively, guess, and immediately test hypotheses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Each meeting combined a short introductory discussion with an experimental session in which students could "explore waves behavior in qualitative way, guessing what could happen and suddenly testing their hypothesis." Realization by inquiry was used whenever possible, even when quantitative measurements were requested. The authors adopted this structure because previous PLS activities had shown real effectiveness in students' active and cooperative behavior in laboratory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Joint planning by teachers and university; laboratory conceived as a method, not a place, with students as the main characters of learning
#### Constraints
- Implemented as optional extracurricular deepening laboratories for motivated and talented students, meeting about 3 hours almost every month

### Target Learners
- Motivated and talented upper-secondary science students

### Target Learning Goals
- Qualitative and quantitative understanding of wave behavior, resonance and energy transfer
