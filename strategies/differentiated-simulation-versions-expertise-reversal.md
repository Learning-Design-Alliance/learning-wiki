# Differentiate simulation versions by prior knowledge using the expertise reversal effect

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For future work the authors recommend using EJS to create different versions of the same simulation catering to different student needs, guided by the expertise reversal effect: features effective for low-knowledge learners 'can lose their effectiveness and even have negative consequences for more proficient learners' (Kalyuga, 2007). Concretely, they suggest hiding the graph for weaker students who prefer tables of values, and providing more interactivity for stronger students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An authorable toolkit allowing multiple versions of the same simulation; knowledge of students' prior knowledge levels
#### Constraints
- Based on the pilot's focus-group finding that weaker students found the moving graph difficult to understand; the differentiation itself was not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- elementary school students of mixed ability

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding energy conversion relationships
