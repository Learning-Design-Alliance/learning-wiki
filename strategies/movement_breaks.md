# Movement Breaks

## Description
Movement breaks are brief, structured activities incorporated into classroom settings to energize learners, improve focus, and enhance learning. These breaks typically last from 1–5 minutes and involve physical activity, such as stretching, dancing, or simple exercises. Movement breaks can be used after periods of sedentary activity, such as after lunch or before a test, to increase blood flow to the brain and stimulate cognitive function. They also provide an opportunity for learners to manage stress and improve self-regulation. Empowering students to suggest and lead movement breaks fosters inclusivity, motivation, and engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires 1–5 minutes of class time, 2-3 times per day. Minimal equipment, such as music, beanbags, or spot markers, may be used. Involves teacher participation and facilitation to ensure inclusivity and engagement.
#### Constraints
- May disrupt classroom flow if not managed effectively. Some learners may be resistant to physical activity. Requires teacher planning and facilitation to ensure inclusivity and appropriateness.

### Target Learners

- All learner levels, particularly K–12, but also applicable in higher education and professional development settings where sedentary behavior is common.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Increase learner energy and focus, improve blood flow to the brain, stimulate cognitive function, manage stress, and enhance self-regulation.

### Affordances
- Enhances attention and focus by increasing blood flow to the brain. Improves self-regulation by providing structured opportunities to manage stress and release energy. Fosters inclusivity and motivation by empowering students to suggest and lead activities.

### Personalization
- Activities can be adapted to suit various abilities, interests, and limitations. Modifications can include seated activities for mobility-challenged learners, adjustments to game rules, and variations in intensity. Students can also choose activities they find enjoyable and accessible.
