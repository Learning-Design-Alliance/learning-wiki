# Train peer lecturers in teaching and facilitating skills and use locally resonant framing to retain low-baseline-knowledge participants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the study's findings the authors draw two implementable recommendations for male-community health education. First, because attitude change varied across peer-led sessions and some volunteer lecturers reported feeling nervous, "there is a need for training to enhance their teaching and facilitating skills." Second, because drop-outs had low baseline knowledge, the authors suggest replacing abstract introductions about MDGs and national mortality statistics with stories of maternal death within the particular community, and orienting sessions toward small-group discussion so participants can relate information to their individual circumstances.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Volunteer peer lecturers willing to teach other males in their own barangays
#### Constraints
- Recommendations arise from a non-randomized, single-community feasibility study whose outcome measures are not standardized or validated

### Target Learners
- Adult male community volunteers serving as peer health educators

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective peer facilitation of MCH education and participant retention
