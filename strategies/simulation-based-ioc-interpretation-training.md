# Use standardized, simulation-based education with high volumes of normal IOC examples to build trainee IOC interpretation confidence and accuracy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend demonstrating bile duct injury and IOC findings "in a low-stakes environment where interpretations can be verified for accuracy", using standardized education particularly for residents. They further suggest emphasizing "a high volume of normal IOC" because confidence interpreting normal IOC correlated most strongly with overall quiz accuracy, and point to perceptual learning and online modules as precedents.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to educational cholangiogram images; the authors note scarcity of educational cholangiograms remains a barrier to developing effective training tools
#### Constraints
- Future work should assess whether such educational interventions lead to clinically significant improvements in BDI recognition and incidence

### Target Learners
- surgical residents
- surgical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate interpretation of intraoperative cholangiograms
- recognition of bile duct injury on IOC
