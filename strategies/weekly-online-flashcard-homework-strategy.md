# Assign weekly out-of-class online flashcard study as homework to build receptive vocabulary quickly

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the year-long quasi-experiment, the authors conclude that assigning Word Engine use for out-of-class work contributes relatively quickly to learners' receptive vocabulary knowledge. The implementation used weekly monitored homework: one or two hours of Word Engine per week, with "Weekly monitoring of both the vocabulary group and vocabulary and ER group ensured that students were correctly using Word Engine" via the program's admin page. The authors recommend future research explore "the most efficient duration of weekly Word Engine use".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Weekly monitoring of student use through the program's administration system, and exclusion of students who miss three or more consecutive weeks
#### Constraints
- The conclusion rests on a quasi-experimental design with potential selection threats and confounding variables, and the control group's time on task was not fully controlled

### Target Learners
- first-year Japanese EFL university students in compulsory English courses

### Target Learning Goals
- growth in written receptive vocabulary size measured by the Vocabulary Size Test
