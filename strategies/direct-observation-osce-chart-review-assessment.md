# Use direct observation, OSCEs, or chart reviews instead of self-report to assess students' clinical fluid-management competence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because self-reported questionnaires may overestimate competence through recall and social desirability bias, the article recommends that future research and assessment rely on objective measures. It states: "future research should incorporate direct observation methods, objective structured clinical examinations (OSCEs), or chart reviews to validate findings and better capture the translation of theoretical knowledge into practice." It further suggests a randomised controlled trial with experimental and control groups to evaluate educational interventions quantitatively.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to clinical settings or objective assessment infrastructure (OSCE stations, chart records) for observing or verifying actual practice
#### Constraints
- The current study's data are self-reported and may be influenced by social desirability and recall bias, potentially overestimating actual competence, as the article itself acknowledges

### Target Learners
- Nursing students
- Midwifery students

### Target Learning Goals
- Translation of theoretical fluid resuscitation knowledge into clinical practice
