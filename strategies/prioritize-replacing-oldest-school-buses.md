# Prioritize clean bus replacement funding for the oldest, most polluting school buses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that districts and funders direct school bus replacement resources toward pre-1990 vehicles, because the measured educational benefit was concentrated there. The authors conclude that these findings "support prioritizing clean bus replacement of the oldest buses as an actionable way for improving students' educational performance," estimating nearly 5000 pre-1990 buses remain in the US fleet carrying nearly 250,000 students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Funding must be sufficient to overcome districts' financial obstacles, since selected applicants had to cover costs beyond the EPA award
#### Constraints
- The benefit was demonstrated only for pre-1990 model year replacements; no benefit was found for 1990 or later buses
- Findings may not generalize to all districts because applicants needed to fund remaining replacement costs themselves

### Target Learners
- Students in grades 3 through 8 riding school buses in US school districts

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved standardized test performance in reading and language arts and mathematics
