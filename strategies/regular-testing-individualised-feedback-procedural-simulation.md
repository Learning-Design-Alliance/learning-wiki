# Use regular low-stakes testing with individualised feedback and learning-trajectory tracking to increase engagement in procedural simulation training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends testing procedural skills throughout a learning program in conjunction with individualised feedback and tracking of the learning trajectory. Testing "encourage[s] deliberate practice with clear objectives and goals", and feedback during testing is more specific because timed recorded videos allow specific technique feedback. The authors suggest facilitation strategies including allowing learners to test when ready, testing away from peers, self-recorded timings and repeat testing to lower stakes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Weekly face-to-face sessions with surgical supervisors providing verbal, individually tailored feedback
- Timed, video-recorded test attempts at the conclusion of sessions
#### Constraints
- The article notes mastery-style approaches take longer and create logistical difficulties for teachers and trainees
- The observed effect was in medical students and may differ in trainee cohorts

### Target Learners
- Novice medical students learning laparoscopic skills

### Target Learning Goals
- Laparoscopic procedural skills such as thread transfer, dice stacking, paperclip untangling and glove capping
