# Structure group supervision content in advance and pair it with direct, pool-based supervision for novices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Participants recommended concrete changes to the group supervision program: determine and communicate session content before each session so physiotherapists can prepare and contribute, adopt a framework clarifying expectations, document tasks to complete before subsequent sessions, and run structured basics-focused sessions for inexperienced physiotherapists at the start of a rotation. They also recommended complementing group supervision with direct supervision and practical activities in the hydrotherapy pool to apply what was discussed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A senior aquatic physiotherapist facilitator able to guide discussion and create a safe space; pre-session communication of content and tasks
#### Constraints
- Participants perceived little benefit attending when not currently practising in the aquatic environment; videoconferencing reduced interactivity

### Target Learners
- Junior and mid-level physiotherapists rotating through a specialised clinical service

### Target Learning Goals
- Aquatic physiotherapy clinical reasoning, confidence, equipment use and risk management
