# Team Based Learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Team Based Learning (TBL) is a whole-course instructional strategy in which permanent, instructor-formed teams of 5–7 students work through a repeatable sequence: individual preparation, an individual readiness test, a team readiness test with immediate feedback, and a series of team application activities. The instructor's role shifts from delivering content to designing powerful application problems and facilitating inter-team discussion, so that the majority of contact time is spent applying concepts rather than transmitting them.

## Design Implications

TBL operationalizes [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) at scale: the readiness assurance process holds individuals accountable for preparation, freeing class time for team problem-solving [Active learning improves exam performance relative to lecture.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. Its distinctive mechanism is the Readiness Assurance Process (RAP) — individual and team tests on the same questions with immediate appeal and feedback — which combines [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) with peer instruction: teams must reach consensus, which forces discussion of misconceptions [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Application activities must be built around significant, consequential problems where all teams commit to a specific answer simultaneously, enabling productive inter-team debate.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Permanent, strategically diverse teams formed by the instructor and maintained across the course
- Pre-class preparation material (readings, videos) that is genuinely sufficient for the readiness tests
- A Readiness Assurance Process: individual test (iRAT), identical team test (tRAT) with immediate scoring feedback, and a structured appeal process
- Application activities with a *specific* defensible answer that teams must commit to publicly, enabling simultaneous reporting and cross-team discussion
- Peer evaluation that feeds into grades to sustain individual accountability

#### Constraints
- If preparation materials are inadequate or tests are poorly aligned, teams fail the RAP and class time is consumed by re-teaching content [-M]
- Application problems with vague or multiple defensible answers collapse the simultaneous-report discussion and devolve into unstructured talk [~M]
- Free-riding emerges without meaningful peer evaluation and individual accountability components [-M]
- Requires substantial front-loaded design effort; instructors attempting TBL session-by-session without redesigning the course structure report poor results [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Two-stage RAP (individual then team) is standard; some implementations add a third "appeals" stage where teams argue in writing why their answer is correct
- Hybrid models use TBL for part of a course rather than throughout
- Variants in medical education (Team-Based Learning Collaborative materials) emphasize clinical case application; business education variants emphasize decision-making cases

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate and professional students (medicine, business, nursing) who can sustain permanent teams across a term
- Learners who benefit from peer explanation and consensus-building; team discussion during the tRAT exposes and corrects misconceptions [Case-based learning improves exam performance.](../claims/case-based-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+M]
- Less suitable for very short courses or cohorts where team continuity is impossible

### Target Learning Goals
- Concept application and decision-making: applying prepared knowledge to complex, authentic problems
- Collaborative skills: negotiation, consensus-building, and giving/receiving peer feedback
- Accountability and self-regulation: individual preparation enforced by both tests and peer evaluation

### Instructions
1. Form permanent, instructor-assigned teams of 5–7 with strategically distributed characteristics; avoid self-selection.
2. Assign pre-class preparation and communicate that the readiness test will enforce it.
3. Run the Readiness Assurance Process: individual test, then the same test at team level with immediate feedback, then written appeals.
4. Deliver a short targeted mini-lecture only on items the class found difficult — not a full re-teach.
5. Run 1–3 team application activities using significant problems with specific answers; have all teams commit simultaneously, then facilitate inter-team debate.
6. Conclude with structured peer evaluation and periodic team maintenance check-ins ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md)).

## Related Strategies

## Examples
- **[Team-Based Learning Collaborative](https://www.teambasedlearning.org)** — professional community and published TBL modules, especially in health sciences education
- **Medical education (e.g., Vanderbilt School of Medicine)** — TBL used for pre-clerkship basic science courses, with clinical-vignette application exercises and peer evaluation
- **Business education (Michaelsen's original context, University of Oklahoma)** — semester-long TBL in management courses where teams make and defend consequential business decisions
