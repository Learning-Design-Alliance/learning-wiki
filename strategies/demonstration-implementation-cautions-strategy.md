# Practical guidance for running classroom demonstrations: practice, siting, timing, and assessment to avoid jugglery

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Drawing on student feedback, the author distilled implementation cautions for in-class demonstrations. One must "practice these experiments and be engrossed in their development oﬄine" before class; demonstrations "should be carefully sited inside the lecture hall" with a pre-drawn layout sketch so back-benchers see the effect; timing and duration are crucial, with the best experiments having clear, visually observable outcomes requiring little post-processing. To avoid posing demonstrations "as mere entertaining jugglery", students were assessed on them in exams and safety precautions were enforced.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Offline practice and development of the equipment, a pre-planned lecture-hall layout sketch, projection magnification sufficient for all students, and integration of demonstrations into assessed work
#### Constraints
- Demonstrations that cannot be seen by the whole class, that eat into lecturing time with equipment fumbling, or that drag on become counterproductive, per the author's own words of caution

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in large lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining attention and conceptual benefit from live demonstrations
