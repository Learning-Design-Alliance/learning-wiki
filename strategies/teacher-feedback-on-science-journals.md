# Teachers read student journals and give regular individual feedback on them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The teacher collected journals at the start of each lesson, commented on them, and returned them. Feedback addressed negative feelings, suggested learning strategies for poorly understood concepts, corrected misconceptions, and praised innovative ideas, mentioning useful strategies in class. The article states that "the current study considers teacher’s feedback as an essential pillar for successful reflective journal-writing."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular teacher feedback on journal entries each lesson
#### Constraints
- The regular feedback the experimental group received may itself have contributed an expectancy effect, since the control group did not receive journal feedback

### Target Learners
- tenth-grade science students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-regulation of science learning; diagnosing and remediating comprehension difficulties
