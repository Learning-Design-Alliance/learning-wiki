# Implement study-habit improvement programs for children before age 8 to support stable academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "it is important to implement programs which would be focused on improving the study  habits of children before 8 years". Study habits are described as "easy to learn and adapted to compensate for  academic demands throughout the school year", and higher study habits were associated with less change (more stability) in achievement between grades. The strategy targets early acquisition of study habits as a modifiable, noncognitive lever on achievement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should target study habits (general attitude towards study, place of study, work plan, study techniques) as measured by instruments such as the SHTQ.
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence comes from a small two-year follow-up in one state-subsidized school; the negative change-score coefficient for study habits is correlational and interpreted with caution by the authors.

### Target Learners
- Elementary school children under 8-9 years old

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement and stability of performance across school years
