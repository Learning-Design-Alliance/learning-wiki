# Direct Instruction: Phonics

## Description
Direct Phonics instruction connects letters to sounds to decode and comprehend text. It includes explicit teaching of letter-sound relationships and sufficient practice to ensure retention of skills for transfer into long-term memory. Multisensory approaches, such as the Orton-Gillingham approach, can be incorporated to engage multiple senses (hearing and touch) in exploring letters and sounds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires explicit instruction, practice materials (e.g., letter cards, word lists), and a structured approach to teaching letter-sound relationships. Multisensory materials can enhance engagement.
#### Constraints
- May not be suitable for learners who already have a strong foundation in phonics. Requires consistent and structured instruction to be effective.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Adult learners who struggle with foundational reading skills, including decoding and phonemic awareness; also applicable to K-12 learners

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve decoding skills, enhance phonemic awareness, strengthen reading comprehension

### Affordances
- Provides a structured approach to learning letter-sound relationships, improves decoding skills, and builds confidence in reading. Multisensory approaches can enhance engagement and retention.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted to different learner needs by adjusting the pace of instruction and providing targeted practice on specific letter-sound relationships. Multisensory approaches can be tailored to individual learning styles.

## Assessment Evidence
- Assess improvements in decoding skills, phonemic awareness, and reading comprehension through regular assessments. Monitor progress and adjust instruction as needed.

## Impact
- Research supports the effectiveness of explicit phonics instruction in improving reading skills, particularly for learners who struggle with decoding. Early reading instruction assists adult literacy learning, particularly as adult readers may need to unlearn ineffective reading and writing habits.
