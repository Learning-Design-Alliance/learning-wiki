Combine depicting and outlining in sequence: depict first to trigger ideas, then outline to organize them¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Interviewees and the authors propose using the two pre-writing strategies together so each offsets the other's weaknesses. As interviewees put it, "depicting can be used first to trigger ideas, then outlining to help organize the ideas," and depicting can fill outlines where words fail. The article's implication section states a combination of the strategies might assist students more because one can reduce the limitations of the other.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers should examine the strengths and weaknesses of the two strategies and clarify them to students before combined use

Constraints¶

  • The article recommends combination as an implication and future-research direction; it did not experimentally test combined use

Target Learners¶

  • EFL novice adult learners writing descriptive paragraphs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • generating ideas for descriptive writing
  • organizing and developing ideas in writing

Claims¶

  • Depicting training led participants to generate more ideas in their descriptive writings than outlining training [+W]
  • Interviewees reported depicting triggers idea flow and motivation while outlining supports systematic organization and saves time [+W]
  • Participants preferred outlining to depicting overall, with nearly half preferring outlining [~W]
  • Outlining training led to higher content scores than depicting training [~W]
  • Outlining training led to higher organization-of-ideas scores than depicting training [~W]
