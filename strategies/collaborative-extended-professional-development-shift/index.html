Shift from one-day fragmented workshops to collaborative, extended, content-focused professional development¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends replacing traditional single-day workshops with professional development that focuses "on collaborative efforts, on-going or extended days, and build upon content and practices." Traditional professional development is described as "single-shot, one-day workshops" that are fragmented and noncumulative, producing little improvement to teaching pedagogy. The Science-in-CTE model, with seven days of intensive professional development pairing CTE and science teachers, exemplifies this shift and allowed teachers to move from professional development into technical assistance.

Claims¶

  • Professional learning experiences shift teachers' beliefs away from traditional views toward practice-based science and increase implementation confidence, with gains leveling off after initial workshops [+M]
  • A half-day workshop featuring the PPH framework significantly increased dietitians' confidence to undertake pro-environmental change in their personal and professional lives [~W]
  • Most partner professional development relied on traditional short-term workshops with little classroom-level intensive assistance [+M]
  • Only 29% of control agricultural education teachers used the Science-in-CTE method and lessons one year later [+W]
  • 92% of experimental agricultural education teachers continued using Science-in-CTE materials one year after the pilot study [+W]
  • Sustained usage rates in Science-in-CTE were consistent with those reported in the Math-in-CTE follow-up study [+W]
  • 80% of partnered science teachers voluntarily incorporated Science-in-CTE methods or examples into their science classes [+W]
