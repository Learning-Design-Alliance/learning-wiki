# Use the nano board both as a black-box measuring device and as a playground for teaching coding and electronics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article describes two modes of use. In experimental physics classes the board serves as a black box requiring no breadboards or circuit building, connecting rapidly to a student's smartphone. As any Arduino board, it can also serve as a playground to teach coding or electronics, where the quick smartphone feedback helps students understand the measuring process and the limits of the sensors, and the open-source programs let computer-oriented students go further into advanced coding.
