# AI-aware question authoring cycle: stress-test draft questions with GenAI tools and revise when superficial completion is too easy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 4 studies (2 design, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A five-step authoring cycle in which teaching teams draft questions against learning outcomes, attempt them with the GenAI tools students are likely to use (e.g. ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, DeepSeek), inspect answers for "correctness, completeness, and deceptively plausible omissions", revise tasks when GenAI answers too readily, and re-test before deployment. The article used it to move questions toward scenario-based, context-rich, module-specific prompts with local schemas and subtle discriminators.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to contemporary GenAI tools for design-time trials
- Stored design-time GenAI answer files and planning records to document what triggered redesign
#### Constraints
- The stored design-time GenAI trials represent exemplars from each design phase rather than an exhaustive archive of every discarded draft question

### Target Learners
- undergraduate computing students in modules permitting GenAI use

### Target Learning Goals
- questions that elicit interpretation, discrimination, and disciplinary judgement rather than retrieval
