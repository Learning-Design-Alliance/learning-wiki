# Extend AI-assisted grading with LLM ensembles, richer prompts, and full-length essays

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation for building on the grading approach: "Prompt templates need only a handful of instructor-graded examples; once built, they scale to hundreds of SAQs at negligible cost." They suggest follow-up work explore ensembles of LLMs, richer prompts, and extending the approach from short-answer questions to full-length essays.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor-built prompt templates requiring only a handful of instructor-graded example answers before scaling to many questions
#### Constraints
- The study did not directly assess student learning outcomes or critical thinking skills; understanding whether AI feedback improves learning requires long-term studies tracking performance over time

### Target Learners
- instructors of large-enrollment undergraduate courses

### Target Learning Goals
- scaling personalized grading and feedback on written student work
