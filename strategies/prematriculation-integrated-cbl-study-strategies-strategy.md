# Use integrated CBL sessions in prematriculation programs to introduce study strategies alongside integrated content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend CBL sessions as an efficient modality for easing students' transition into medical curricula: such sessions "provide opportunities to incoming first-year medical students to practice, adapt, and evaluate study techniques while delivering integrated anatomy, embryology, and histology content". The strategy pairs a low-stakes environment with closed-book individual quizzes, group case work, and facilitator debriefs so students can test and revise their preparation approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A short instructional period covering the prerequisite lectures and labs before the CBL
- Small groups of six to eight drawn from existing lab groups to provide a safe discussion space
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only in a small, voluntary prematriculation cohort; authors call for follow-up research on whether students implement strategy changes later

### Target Learners
- incoming first-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- adapting study techniques to fast-paced, high-volume curricula
- integrating basic and clinical science content
