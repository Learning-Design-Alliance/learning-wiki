Strategies

Provide parents of high-school students with mailed utility-value materials (brochures and a website) to spur conversations about the relevance of math and science¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The intervention sent parents "a brochure in the fall of 10th grade, another brochure in January of 11th grade, and access to a website in January of 11th grade." Materials gave information about the utility of STEM topics, encouragement to communicate it, and advice on effective communication, such as personalizing information to children's interests and goals. Students were never directly given materials; the intervention worked only through parent–child interactions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Parents must engage with the materials; in the prior report 86% of parents reported remembering and using them and 75% of adolescents confirmed exposure

Constraints¶

  • The sample was recruited in Wisconsin and may not generalize to the overall US population
  • Families had been in a longitudinal study for years, which may have increased engagement with materials relative to newly recruited parents

Target Learners¶

  • high-school students (10th–12th grade)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • increased STEM course-taking
  • improved mathematics and science achievement
  • STEM career pursuit

Claims¶

  • Groups did not differ on preintervention test scores, and the ACT effect remained significant when controlling for them [+W]
  • High-school mathematics and science course-taking significantly predicted all measured posthigh-school STEM career pursuit outcomes [~W]
  • The intervention had significant indirect effects on college STEM course-taking, STEM career aspirations, and perceived STEM value through high-school STEM preparation, but not on desiring a STEM major [+W]
  • The intervention showed no significant direct effects on posthigh-school STEM career pursuit variables [~W]
  • A parent-centered utility-value intervention increased adolescents' mathematics and science ACT scores by about 12 percentile points [+W]
  • The intervention increased 11th- and 12th-grade STEM course-taking by nearly one semester (0.81 semesters) [+W]
