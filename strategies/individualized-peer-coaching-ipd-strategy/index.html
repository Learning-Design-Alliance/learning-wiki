Use individualized peer coaching with follow-up sessions as professional development for inclusive practices.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes an Inclusive Professional Development (IPD) program in which eight teachers received an eight-week intervention combining a 30-minute overview of five core areas (curriculum, assessment, strategies, behavior management, and collaboration) with follow-up coaching sessions tailored to individual needs. "The number of IPD sessions varied from teacher to teacher, and were tailored to the individual needs of each specific teacher and their unique understanding of inclusive practices." A total of 84 IPD sessions were performed across the eight-week span.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must volunteer and have administrator support.
  • A coach must be available to provide individualized sessions and follow-up feedback.

Constraints¶

  • The article notes that more time and support were needed to allow teachers time to reflect and refine their implementation of inclusive strategies, suggesting eight weeks may be insufficient.

Target Learners¶

  • General education elementary school teachers (K-6)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Increasing willingness and perceived ability to carry out inclusive practices for students with disabilities

Claims¶

  • An eight-week individualized peer coaching intervention produced mixed effects on teacher attitudes toward inclusive practices. [~W]
  • All eight participants reported positive experiences and a new appreciation for inclusive education practices. [+W]
  • Cohen's d effect sizes were .07 for the intervention group and .05 for the comparison group on total STATIC scores. [~W]
  • The intervention group's mean RCI was -.03 and the comparison group's was .17, with no significant effect in either group. [~W]
  • In one survey, coached teachers more often report sustained, followed-up professional development than non-coached peers, and more than 81% received at least 30 minutes of one-on-one coaching a week [+W]
