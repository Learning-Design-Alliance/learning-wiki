# Deliver scalable automated talk-move feedback to online instructors as a complement to professional development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend using the talk-move classifiers to give instructors "specific, timely, scalable, and low-cost feedback to improve on their teaching", especially novices teaching online. They caution that models have false positives and negatives, so feedback should be used carefully, and state the models "should complement, and not replace, other forms of professional development". An ongoing study delivers such feedback weekly over a course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful validation of model accuracy before feedback is shown, since incorrect feedback can affect teaching performance and self-confidence
#### Constraints
- The article states the models should not be used to profile instructors or make high-stakes hiring or firing decisions

### Target Learners
- novice instructors on online platforms with limited access to instructional training

### Target Learning Goals
- improving instructors' use of accountable talk moves and, through them, student engagement
