# Incorporate low-fidelity simulation settings into standard training procedures for naval command and control teams.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that maritime organizations "should consider incorporating low-fidelity simulation settings into their standard procedures to effectively support the training of naval C2 teams". Low-fidelity simulations conducted indoors with fewer instructors and reduced equipment offer a more effective approach in both time and costs, allowing for an increased number of training sessions. This strategy is grounded in the study's finding that low-fidelity training results in a workload not distinguishable from high-fidelity training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires low-fidelity simulation materials such as the Emergo Train System with a Navy module, including magnetic symbols, whiteboards, maps, and ship models to replicate the operational environment.
#### Constraints
- The study's small sample size (N=10) and single C2 team limit generalizability of the finding to other Swedish naval C2 teams.

### Target Learners
- Naval command and control team members
- Experienced military personnel

### Target Learning Goals
- Internal battle decision-making
- Combat readiness
- Team coordination under stress
