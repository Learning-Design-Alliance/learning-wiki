# Teachers can step back and use a limited set of discourse moves — providing relevant information, revoicing with uncertainty, and positive supportive utterances — to stimulate and sustain curiosity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy holds that in collaborative student-led discourse environments teachers need only a small repertoire of moves to elicit curiosity: provision of relevant information, infusion of uncertainty via revoicing, and positive supportive utterances. The article reports that "teachers can take a 'step back'and allow curiosity to continueﬂourishing once a student has initiated it," and that "teachers do not need to play extensive discourse roles in order to elicit and sustain students’curiosity." It is derived from network findings in KB classrooms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A collaborative, student-led discourse environment must already be set up, as in Knowledge Building classrooms
#### Constraints
- Findings are from social constructivist KB classrooms; applicability to conventional I-R-E style classrooms is unclear

### Target Learners
- elementary students (grades 2-3, ages 7-9)

### Target Learning Goals
- fostering and sustaining epistemic curiosity and question asking
