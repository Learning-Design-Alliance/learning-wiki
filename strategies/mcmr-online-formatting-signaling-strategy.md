# Formatting changes to signal multiple-response items to students encountering MCMR questions online

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because most students completing the survey for the first time had little or no experience with multiple-choice/multiple-response items, the authors restructured the online instrument: all MCMR items were moved to the end of the survey, an interstitial page stated that remaining questions "might have more than one correct response", a reminder appeared atop each MCMR page, and question stems prompted students to "choose all that apply". The authors conclude these measures were effective at increasing multiple-response selection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness that students unfamiliar with MCMR formats may not realize multiple selections are allowed
#### Constraints
- An increase in answers chosen is not necessarily an improvement in performance, since only two MCMR items had more than one correct response and items were scored dichotomously

### Target Learners
- college students in introductory physics encountering MCMR items for the first time

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate self-report of reasoning on multiple-response assessment items
