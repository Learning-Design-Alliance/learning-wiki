# Use SP feedback, video review, and facilitated group debriefing after each simulated encounter to build HCW skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's training recipe pairs each simulated encounter with immediate non-technical, emotional feedback from the SP actor, followed by a facilitated group debriefing in which videos of the encounters are reviewed and discussed for strengths and areas for improvement. Encounters are video-recorded at stations resembling counseling rooms, and videos are also scored by Kenyan and US-based experts to provide technical feedback on clinical competencies. This cycle of practice, feedback, and reflective debriefing is the core skill-building mechanism of the intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained SP actors able to give structured verbal feedback and complete feedback forms; video-recording capability; a facilitator for group debriefings
#### Constraints
- Feedback from SPs is deliberately non-technical and emotional; technical competency feedback comes separately from expert video scoring

### Target Learners
- Healthcare workers in in-service clinical training

### Target Learning Goals
- Communication, empathy, and clinical assessment skills in adolescent HIV care
