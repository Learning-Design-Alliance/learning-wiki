# Mother-tongue-first bilingual model: begin schooling in the native language and introduce the second language alongside it

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Drawing on first-language and bilingual programs in Peru, Bolivia, and the United States, the review recommends that students begin schooling in their native tongue and be introduced to the second language while continuing to use their mother tongue. In this model learning of new material is not deferred until students become proficient in the second language; the world language serves as a secondary language for accessing advanced education and resources. The monograph presents this as a workable alternative to Haiti's French-only system.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students continue using their mother tongue throughout their schooling while the second language is introduced gradually
#### Constraints
- The review notes obstacles to implementing models that have worked in other countries, including governmental and community support, in the Haitian context

### Target Learners
- Haitian students, the vast majority monolingual Kreyol speakers entering school around age five or six

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition of literacy and academic concepts, including math and science, without deferring learning until second-language proficiency

## Claims

- [The review reports that students acquire literacy most efficiently in their native language and that expecting literacy in a foreign language hampers academic achievement](../claims/literacy-most-efficient-native-language.md) [+W]
