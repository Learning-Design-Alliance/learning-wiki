# Ask students to explain why wrong multiple-choice answers are wrong and why they chose their answer, then have an AI bot analyze the explanations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy adapts existing multiple-choice or concept-inventory questions by adding an explanation step: students explain why they chose their answer and why the other answers are wrong, and a discipline-trained AI bot analyzes those explanations for the instructor. The authors write that they "recommend asking students to explain why 'wrong' choices are wrong and why they chose their answer." The bot's analysis and instructional suggestions are generated essentially immediately, giving instructors time to adapt instruction, introduce background material, and pose targeted in-class questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A discipline-trained AI bot with an adequate training set, and instructors willing to act on the analyses.
#### Constraints
- Requires that actionable formative assessment be embraced by instructors and course designers, which the authors say likely involves rethinking what is taught in a course.

### Target Learners
- large-enrollment undergraduate course students

### Target Learning Goals
- formative assessment
- revealing and correcting student misconceptions
