# Integrate health education and promotion of healthy lifestyles into middle school curricula and school policies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools implement health education and promotion programs so that promoting healthy lifestyles also supports academic performance. It argues "health education and promotion of healthy lifestyles should be integrated into the educational strategies and policies of all educational centers", naming health-promoting schools (HPS) as a multifactorial intervention example. This follows from the observed associations between lifestyle habits and academic performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools able to adopt health-promoting school (HPS) style multifactorial interventions targeting diet, sleep, physical activity, screen use and substance use
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional, so a causal effect of such programs on academic performance is not established by this study; the authors call for longitudinal studies

### Target Learners
- 7th and 8th grade middle school students (ages 12–15)

### Target Learning Goals
- Healthy lifestyle habits and improved academic performance
