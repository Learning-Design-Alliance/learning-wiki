# Provide targeted support and mentoring for widening-participation medical entrants from non-professional backgrounds

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that because non-professional background independently predicts declared depression, widening-participation entrants may need specific support structures. It suggests "more general supportive approaches, such as mentoring or ‘buddying’ systems for students from under-represented groups, will enhance their long term well-being", noting debate about the benefits of formal resilience training. This builds on outreach initiatives following the 'Selecting for Excellence' report.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of students from under-represented (non-professional) backgrounds, e.g. via NS-SEC socioeconomic classification.
#### Constraints
- There is debate as to the benefits of formal resilience training, so general supportive approaches rather than formal training are suggested.

### Target Learners
- UK medical students from non-professional (widening participation) backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- supporting student well-being and reducing risk of depression during medical training
