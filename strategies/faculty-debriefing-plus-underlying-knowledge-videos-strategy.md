# Supplement clinical video e-learning with faculty debriefing and videos that expose the underlying knowledge and skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends two design and use changes for e-learning in clinical skills education. First, "faculty debriefing is suggested to supplement student learning from the clinical videos", with faculty reviewing key points online or face-to-face. Second, "clinical videos should not only demonstrate the procedures of patient encounters but also provide information on the knowledge and skills underlying them". These suggestions respond to students' neutral perceptions and their exam-oriented use of the videos.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty time to run debriefing sessions, either online or face-to-face, reviewing key points from the videos with students
#### Constraints
- The article notes e-learning "needs to be supplemented with practice in real life, which involves receiving feedback on his or her clinical performance"

### Target Learners
- Third-year medical students in clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical competencies: history-taking, physical examination, patient-physician interaction, diagnostic reasoning
