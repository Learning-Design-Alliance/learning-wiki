# Include video-based SA training with interposed questions as one component of a multimodal laparoscopic training curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adding video-based situational awareness training with interposed questions to multimodal laparoscopic curricula for novices, alongside simulation and real cases. Videos of recorded laparoscopic cholecystectomies are stopped without warning and a situation-specific question appears, requiring learners to distinguish tissues and predict outcomes. The authors state "SA training should thus be included as one aspect besides simulation and real cases in a multimodal curriculum".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video clips of the target procedure with interposed, situation-specific questions; active participation is essential
#### Constraints
- Benefit demonstrated only for laparoscopic novices in the early learning curve on a porcine cadaver liver model; transfer to the operating room and to more difficult operations remains unverified

### Target Learners
- laparoscopy-naïve medical students
- novice laparoscopic surgeons in early residency

### Target Learning Goals
- situational awareness during minimally invasive procedures
- error avoidance in critical procedural steps

### Affordances
- Situation Awareness Three Levels Model
