# Future school-based nutrition and PA studies in low-resource settings should use longer supplementation periods and more cognitively demanding assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future trials in similar low-resource settings extend supplementation duration and adopt more sensitive cognitive measures, since the 6-month MMNS period and ceiling-prone Flanker task may have masked effects. They state future studies "should consider longer supplementation periods, more cognitively demanding assessments (e.g., the Stroop task), and multi-level strategies addressing both educational and health-related factors."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supplementation periods longer than the 6 months used here, given high prevalence of undernutrition and micronutrient deficiencies in the study population
#### Constraints
- Findings are specific to peri-urban Tanzania and may not be generalizable to other LMICs, geographic settings, or socioeconomic backgrounds

### Target Learners
- primary schoolchildren aged 6–12 years in low- and middle-income settings

### Target Learning Goals
- cognitive function (inhibitory control, processing speed)
- academic achievement in language and mathematics
