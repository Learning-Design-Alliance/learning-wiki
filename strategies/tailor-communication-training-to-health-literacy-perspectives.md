# Tailor physician communication training to patients' different health literacy levels and perspectives

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that physician training incorporate the five patient perspectives so that communication can be adjusted to patients' differing health literacy levels, cultural values, and expectations of the physician's role. It states that "physician training programs should include interaction techniques tailored to patients' different levels of health literacy" and supports structured communication training integrated into medical clerkships.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Physicians must be able to recognize patients' health literacy levels and adjust communication styles and strategies accordingly
#### Constraints
- Identifying low patient health literacy through assessments can evoke shame, negatively affecting patient adherence and trust, so assessments without proper follow-up could hinder effective communication

### Target Learners
- medical students
- physicians in family medicine and primary care

### Target Learning Goals
- physician-patient communication skills
- health literacy competencies
- patient-centered care
