# Integrate EBM teaching with clinical practice rather than standalone instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors suggest that nominal or uncontextualized EBM exposure may explain why early instruction did not translate into aptitude, citing a systematic review indicating standalone teaching was less effective than clinically based teaching. They note the gap between participants exposed to EBM in undergraduate education and those incorporating it clinically, and recommend graduate medical education institutions identify best practices for educating residents about EBM.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- EBM instruction should be connected to clinical decision-making contexts
#### Constraints
- The authors note the literature indicates EBM instruction increases knowledge and skills but does not itself impact physician behavior or clinical practice

### Target Learners
- medical residents and fellows

### Target Learning Goals
- applying evidence-based medicine in clinical decision making
