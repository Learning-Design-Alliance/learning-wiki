# Actively encourage trainees to analyse and interpret ECGs during clinical training, supported by mobile learning strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that mere presence on ward rounds does not build ECG competence; instead "students and residents should be actively encouraged to analyse and interpret ECGs during their clinical training". It further suggests this self-directed pursuit be supported "by using mobile learning strategies", noting evidence that algorithm-based ECG reference apps may benefit more than unguided Internet exploration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured learning activities in the workplace, including mobile learning, must supplement contextual learning during clerkships
#### Constraints
- The evidence to support these learning strategies is described by the authors as emerging in the literature

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students on Internal Medicine clerkships
- Medical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- ECG analysis and interpretation competence gained through contextualised clinical practice
