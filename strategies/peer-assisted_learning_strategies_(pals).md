# Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS)

## Description
PALS is a structured peer tutoring approach that involves pairing learners to work together on academic activities. In PALS, learners take turns being the reader and coach, and as the reader reads a text aloud, the coach records errors and prompts any miscues during reading. After reading, learners retell information to one another using structured prompts to promote comprehension. PALS often incorporates activities such as partner reading, paragraph shrinking, and prediction relays. The instructor monitors learner interactions and provides feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Appropriately leveled texts, structured prompts, teacher training, classroom time (approximately 35 minutes per session), and monitoring by the instructor.
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness depends on the instructor's ability to pair learners appropriately and provide adequate training and support. Some learners may dominate the activity while others may be passive. Requires careful monitoring to ensure that learners are providing accurate feedback and support.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Increase reading fluency, improve reading comprehension, build summarizing skills, develop peer support, enhance social skills

### Affordances
- Provides structured peer interaction, promotes active learning, allows for immediate feedback from peers, encourages learners to take responsibility for their own and their partner's learning, and increases Motivation.

### Personalization
- Texts can be leveled to match learner reading abilities. Activity roles can be assigned based on individual needs. Structured prompts can be modified to target specific comprehension skills.

## Assessment Evidence
- Reading fluency (words correct per minute), comprehension scores, quality of summaries and retellings, observation of learner interactions, and feedback from learners on their experience with PALS.

## Impact
- Research supports the effectiveness of PALS in improving reading fluency and comprehension for learners in grades 2–6. Studies have shown that PALS can be particularly beneficial for learners with learning disabilities and English language learners.

## Examples
- Partner Reading with Retell: Learners take turns reading aloud and summarizing the text. Paragraph Shrinking: Learners identify the main idea of each paragraph. Prediction Relay: Learners make predictions about what will happen next in the text.
