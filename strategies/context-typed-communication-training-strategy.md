# Organize communication training programmes around different consultation types and train flexible, context-sensitive application of skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that GP specialty training should move beyond generic skill teaching: "training programmes should be organized around different types of consultations and should take into account that patients can be treated by other providers and know what is going to happen". They compare applying communication skills across contexts to "working a mixing table": in a specific context some channels are set to zero and others maximized, while staying ready to adapt to changes in context.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must incorporate the identified consultation types (e.g. preventive, protocol-based, follow-up, multi-person consultations) and co-provider situations
#### Constraints
- Focus should be on flexibility and creativity rather than only on mastering separate skills; future research is needed on consensus about how communication patterns should adapt

### Target Learners
- GP registrars in specialty training

### Target Learning Goals
- Flexible application of communication skills adapted to consultation context
