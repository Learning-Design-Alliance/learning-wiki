# Home-Based Literacy Support

## Description
Home-based literacy support involves schools providing families with resources and guidance to support students’ learning at home. This includes strategies for having discussions about school, creating organized learning spaces, completing interactive assignments, and communicating high expectations. By equipping families with practical tools and knowledge, schools can extend learning beyond the classroom, fostering a collaborative partnership that enhances student outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools need to develop and distribute relevant resources, provide training for staff on how to effectively engage families, and establish clear communication channels.
#### Constraints
- Success depends on the active participation of families. Logistical barriers such as demanding work schedules, transportation difficulties, and lack of childcare can hinder engagement. Some families may require additional support to overcome these challenges.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Schools aiming to improve family engagement and extend learning beyond the classroom, particularly for low-income families and families of color.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance student academic achievement and positive behaviors, improve student motivation and self-efficacy, and empower families with the skills and resources to support learning at home.

### Affordances
- This approach has a stronger positive effect than school-based involvement alone. It supports active engagement by giving families specific tasks and knowledge. It also enhances motivation by showing families that their efforts directly contribute to their child's success.

### Personalization
- Resources can be tailored to the specific needs of different families, such as non-native English speakers, families with different cultural backgrounds, and those with students in special education. Guidance can be adapted to align with the student’s grade level and individual learning goals.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor student academic progress and behavior outcomes. Track family participation in provided activities and feedback on the usefulness of resources. Assess changes in family members' sense of empowerment and well-being.

## Impact
- Research indicates that home-based family engagement practices are often more powerful than school-based practices, leading to improved student outcomes (Thompson, K M, Gillis, T J, Fairman, J, Mason, C A. 2014). Schools that provide specific resources and guidance see greater positive effects.
