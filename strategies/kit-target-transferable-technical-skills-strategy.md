# Target future low-cost home kits at transferable technical skills rather than replicating specific simulator tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future home simulation development focus on building specific deconstructed robotic surgery skills rather than copying individual SimNow tasks. They note expert robotic surgeons "have deconstructed robotic surgery into seven key elements," and that "An ideal home simulation kit can provide specific practice for the technical skills of pick and place, two-handed transfer, and wrist manipulation." They also recommend addressing kit haptics and including residents in further trials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Refined kit design addressing haptics, clutch analogue, and controller resistance
- Fine-tuned model design transferability to the robotic simulator
#### Constraints
- Low-cost models may not replace VR simulators; the authors frame them as a bridge for early trainees with limited simulator access

### Target Learners
- early trainees in robotic surgery, including medical students and surgical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- transferable robotic surgery technical skills such as wrist manipulation and suturing
