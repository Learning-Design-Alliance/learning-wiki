# Explicitly assign and rotate lab group roles, with reflection on role participation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 4 studies (2 associational, 1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
An equity strategy for labs in which students tend to divide tasks: explicitly assign group roles and rotate them so all students participate in all aspects of the work. The review describes that "Heller and Heller devised a scheme by which students would rotate through the roles of manager, recorder, skeptic, and summarizer in cooperative problem-solving and lab-work". Students may also be asked to reflect on their role participation to signal that group-work roles matter.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor commitment to monitoring and rotating roles, and time for role reflection
#### Constraints
- The review reports students prefer to share rather than split up lab-work, and sharing rather than splitting can boost women's self-efficacy in mixed-gender groups

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in cooperative lab and problem-solving groups

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable access to hands-on equipment use and all lab tasks
- collaboration skills
