# Use the agentic engagement behaviour list to train educators to recognize and stimulate agentic behaviours in PBL classes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators participating in PBL classes use the identified behaviour list to become aware of agentic engagement expression in their teaching and adjust the learning environment accordingly. It states the list "may also serve to guide and enhance educator training, in order to help them better recognize and stimulate agentic behaviours in students". This rests on the finding that tutors can prompt agentic engagement by encouraging students to participate constructively.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators need awareness of how agentic engagement is expressed within their teaching
#### Constraints
- Grounded in observations from one medical school with a limited number of students; generalizability is limited

### Target Learners
- second-year pre-clinical medical students in PBL tutor groups

### Target Learning Goals
- stimulating student agentic engagement and autonomy during learning activities
