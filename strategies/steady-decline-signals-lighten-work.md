# Use a steady decline in improvement across exercises as a signal to lighten work or pause the exercises

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Winch proposes a pedagogical criterion based on his data: "Wherever, under first-rate teachers and with earnest work on the part of the children, a series of exercises begins to produce a steady decline in improvement, it is an indication to us of the advisability of lightening the general work or of a cessation of those exercises" until children's reparative activities have had time to act. The criterion rests on the finding that about one-third of the afternoon group's late work was futile.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- First-rate teachers and earnest work by the children
- A series of exercises with per-exercise improvement data to monitor
#### Constraints
- Derived from one class of 13-year-old boys performing mechanical memory tasks; the author notes boredom as a possible competing interpretation

### Target Learners
- Day school children in a class or grade

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining efficient schoolwork across the day and scheduling new subjects
