# Study effort regulation in real-world, personally relevant contexts over longer timeframes, including via ecological momentary assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The commentary recommends moving beyond isolated laboratory tasks toward real-world learning situations where results matter to students' personal goals and engagement spans longer periods. It states we need to "more strongly consider real-world learning situations where results matter to students’ personal goals and where they engage with tasks over longer periods of time," enabling study of temporal dynamics of effort regulation, disengagement, and motivation-dependent effort investment. Ecological momentary assessment with mobile devices is named as a suited method.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Methods capable of capturing intraindividual variability of psychological states over longer timeframes, such as repeated mobile-device ratings
#### Constraints
- In laboratory settings, opting out can hardly be observed, and tasks often hold minimal personal significance for participants, limiting ecological validity of effort-regulation findings

### Target Learners
- students in real-world educational settings pursuing personal as well as learning goals

### Target Learning Goals
- effective allocation and withdrawal of effort across multiple, potentially conflicting goals
