# Pursue three leads for teaching discovery behavior: operant analysis, cognitive simulation, and exploratory-drive research

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
For learning TO discover, Glaser recommends three research leads. Mechner's operant analysis decomposes scientific method into teachable skills such as hypothesis generation, testable in a computer-simulated circumscribed universe. Simon and Newell's simulation work treats heuristics as teachable elementary information processes. Exploratory-drive research shows stimulus change can reinforce and elicit exploration, suggesting instructional environments should vary stimulus characteristics of materials to maintain curiosity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Component behavior repertoires of discovery must first be analyzed before discovery behavior can be instructionally manipulated
#### Constraints
- Discovery behavior is likely specific to subject-matter domain, with much significant specific variance beyond any common repertoires

### Target Learners
- students being taught scientific inquiry and discovery skills

### Target Learning Goals
- hypothesis generation and testing, exploratory and curiosity behavior, discovery strategies
