# Use VR sensitive-exam rehearsal as a stepwise bridge from non-immersive simulation toward live-patient practice, accompanied by physical models and standardized patients

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend implementing the application "via a stepwise simulation-to-live patient learning approach, may be most impactful." They state VR "cannot be a stand-alone curriculum" because it is a non-tactile rehearsal space lacking palpation, so VR curricula should be accompanied by additional learning approaches that include physical models and/or standardized patients for the tactile aspects of procedures. Students likewise envisioned VR as preparatory simulation before working with standardized patients.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- accompanying learning approaches with physical models and/or standardized patients to cover palpation and real-world instrument use
#### Constraints
- VR does not include tactile aspects such as palpation, so it cannot be a stand-alone curriculum

### Target Learners
- healthcare trainees learning sensitive urogenital exam procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- preparation and rehearsal of sensitive exam steps before live-patient encounters
