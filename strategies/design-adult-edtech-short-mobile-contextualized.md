# Design adult-learning ed-tech with short lessons, visualizations, mobile-first simplicity, and contextualized integrated content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on its interviews, the guide recommends entrepreneurs demonstrate value by developing relevant product features for adult learners: design for the unique needs of low-skilled learners by keeping lessons short, using visualizations, providing plentiful resources, increasing learner-instructor connections, and helping learners engage with peers; support anytime, anywhere learning with simple, mobile-first solutions; and create contextualized, integrated content that combines soft skills, job skills, and language skills with basic skills. The guide reports widespread consensus that products for adult learners should be mobile-first, simple to use, and contain high-quality, modular, relevant content, and points entrepreneurs to Digital Promise's two design briefs grounded in adult learning theory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Products must enhance rather than disrupt the learning experience; the guide quotes a provider whose course requiring a new download per assignment detracted from learning
#### Constraints
- The recommendations come from practitioner interviews and design briefs, not from controlled evaluations of specific features

### Target Learners
- low-skilled adult learners
- adult basic education and ESL learners
- independent adult learners

### Target Learning Goals
- basic skills and digital literacy
- job-related and language skills integrated with basic skills
- sustained engagement and retention
