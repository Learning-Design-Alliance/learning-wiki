# Integrate structured, tailored AI training into medical education and continuing professional development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding structured AI training, such as its 1.5-hour asynchronous AI Skills in Medicine course, into medical education, residency programs, and continuing medical education. The course introduced AI applications in "diagnosis, management, predictive analytics, and triage" and instructed participants to "evaluate AI outputs and their underlying rationale carefully rather than accepting them as is." The authors add that ongoing updates and refresher courses are essential given rapid AI evolution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training should cover AI capabilities and limitations, ethical and responsible use, privacy, and optimal AI use in clinical reasoning, as the article's course modules did
#### Constraints
- The article notes structured AI training remains largely absent despite professional-body advocacy, and future work should test customized training for specialties such as emergency and critical care medicine

### Target Learners
- general practitioners
- internists including subspecialty trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- informed and responsible use of AI as a clinical decision-support tool
- clinical decision-making competence with AI assistance
