Build growth-mindset classroom cultures through teacher practices that convey all students can learn and improve¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This strategy treats mindsets as shared classroom beliefs rather than individual traits: teachers create growth-mindset cultures by "expressing high expectations for all students, treating mistakes as opportunities for learning, allowing students to take ownership of their learning process while being a source of support, and providing feedback on the process rather than on ability". The editorial reports this set of practices has been shown to reduce achievement inequality, especially in classes with more low-SES students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must convey that all students can learn and improve; the editorial notes teaching children a growth mindset is unlikely to work if teachers continue to endorse a fixed mindset.

Constraints¶

  • The editorial argues psychological interventions must be supplemented by structural changes at classroom, school, and policy levels to create fertile soil.

Target Learners¶

  • primary and secondary school students, especially those from low-SES backgrounds

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic achievement
  • reduction of achievement inequality

Claims¶

  • Specific observable mindset-signaling behaviors have been identified that convey peers' implicit beliefs about ability [+W]
  • Teachers deliver more inflated praise to low-SES children after success, and observers perceive praised children as less smart [-W]
