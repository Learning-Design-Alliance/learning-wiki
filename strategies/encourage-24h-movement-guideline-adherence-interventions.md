# Interventions aimed at improving literacy and numeracy achievement should encourage adherence to the 24-h movement guidelines, with a literacy focus on limiting recreational screen time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation is that school administrators, policy makers, and intervention designers encourage children's compliance with the 24-h movement guidelines (at least 60 min MVPA, no more than 2 h recreational screen time, 9-11 h sleep) for both health and academic benefit. It states that "Interventions aimed at improving literacy and numeracy achievement should encourage adherence to the 24-h movement guidelines", and that literacy-focused interventions "perhaps should have a focus on limiting recreational screen time (and perhaps replacing it with reading)".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Targets children of primary school age in the guideline range (5-17 years); compliance is defined as 60 min or more of MVPA, 2 h or less of screen time, and 9-11 h of sleep per night
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional, so causality cannot be determined
- The literacy-specific screen-time focus is hedged by the authors and rests on self-reported compliance, which was not replicated in the accelerometry model

### Target Learners
- Primary school-aged children (the CheckPoint sample was aged 11-12 years)

### Target Learning Goals
- Literacy achievement
- Numeracy achievement
