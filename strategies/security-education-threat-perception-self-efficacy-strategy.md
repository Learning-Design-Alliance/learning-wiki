# Design security education programs to build threat perceptions, safeguard effectiveness beliefs, lower safeguard costs and self-efficacy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that IT security awareness, education and training programs be designed around motivational factors from TTAT. As it states, "Individuals are more motivated to avoid malicious IT threats and use of safeguarding measures if these security educational programs help them to develop threat perceptions, comprehend the effectiveness of safeguarding measures, lower safeguard costs and increase their self-efficacy." It further recommends that programs stress both the likelihood of threats and the severity of losses, and that public security education draw attention to emotion-focused coping approaches and train users to stop emotion-focused coping when dealing with malicious IT threats.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should help users develop threat perceptions and self-efficacy and comprehend safeguard effectiveness
#### Constraints
- TTAT suggests emotion-focused coping behaviour confuses users' motivation systems at high threat levels, so high threat perceptions can have negative effects

### Target Learners
- home computer users in non-work settings
- employees in decentralized IT security environments
- bank customers

### Target Learning Goals
- avoid malicious IT threats including phishing
- adopt safeguarding measures
