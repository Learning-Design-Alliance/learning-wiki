# Brain-Based Learning with Movement Breaks

## Description
Incorporating movement, social interaction, and distributed learning to enhance brain function and retention. Strategies include energizing activities, movement breaks, and allowing students to process information in smaller chunks followed by movement-based activities. Emphasis on activating multiple senses and providing opportunities for students to teach each other to deepen understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time for movement breaks and group activities. Access to online resources like GoNoodle. Prepared activities that involve movement and social interaction.
#### Constraints
- May require additional time and planning. Some students may be uncomfortable with movement activities. Requires careful management to ensure that movement breaks are productive and not disruptive.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- K-12 students and adult learners in various subjects. Beneficial for students who have difficulty sitting still or who need movement to reset their brain.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve student engagement, increase retention of information, support focus and attention, promote social-emotional learning, and provide opportunities for students to process information effectively.

### Affordances
- Activates different parts of the brain, enhancing memory and understanding. Provides opportunities for social interaction and peer teaching, which deepens learning. Helps students refocus and maintain attention through movement breaks.

### Personalization
- Activities can be tailored to different age groups and physical abilities. Choice of movement activities can be adjusted based on student preferences and needs.

- Observe student engagement and participation in movement activities. Assess understanding through verbal explanations and visual demonstrations. Monitor student focus and attention after movement breaks.

## Impact
- Research shows that movement and social interaction enhance learning and memory by activating multiple parts of the brain. Frequent breaks and distributed learning can improve retention and focus.
