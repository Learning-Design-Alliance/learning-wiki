# Five-step PBL-plus-3DV session structure for spinal surgery clinical teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article details a five-part implementation: preparing typical spinal cases with a 3D model and surgery videos sent to students 1 week before class; preclass grouping of 8-10 students with a chosen group leader; teacher-guided software manipulation of the model against cases; group discussion and cross-group questioning; and a teacher summary answering common and controversial questions. Each case focused on different knowledge items such as cervical spondylosis and lumbar disc herniation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students without prior PBL experience need a preclass introduction to the PBL process
- Cases, 3D models and surgery videos must be distributed 1 week before class
#### Constraints
- Implemented only in spinal surgery teaching; the authors state it "requires further validation" for other surgical disciplines

### Target Learners
- Fourth-year clinical undergraduate interns in spinal surgery

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical thinking and case analysis
- Spinal anatomy mastery
- Independent and group problem-solving
