# Counter-programming in secondary schools to address physics stereotypes and improve women's physics self-efficacy before engineering coursework

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends intervening before students arrive at university, because the physics self-efficacy gender gap is already large at entry and persists through the degree. It states that "counter programming in secondary schools should be introduced to broadly address stereotypes and improve women's self -efficacy for physics", naming growth mindset interventions as an example. It adds that physics-specific messages could be a pragmatic choice given the particularly large physics gap, and that such interventions could shift the gender imbalance in physics-heavy engineering majors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must target physics-specific stereotypes, since the self-efficacy gap is particularly large in physics
#### Constraints
- The article notes it is unknown whether the effects arise through messages in the home, the media, from friends, or from science teachers, so the sources having the largest effects must first be identified

### Target Learners
- secondary school students
- incoming first-year engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- physics self-efficacy
- equitable participation in engineering majors
