# Design future AR simulation studies with objective performance measures, controlled comparative designs and validated usability instruments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future research "should incorporate these metrics to better evaluate objective training-related outcomes", including procedural time, error rates and structured assessment, and that "Longitudinal designs that assess skill retention, transfer of performance to real patients, and comparative effectiveness against traditional simulation-based teaching are needed". They further suggest standardized usability tools or hybrid instruments to strengthen validity evidence and stratified analyses by prior experience level.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies must add objective measures (procedural accuracy, task completion time, structured assessment) to complement subjective usability perceptions
#### Constraints
- The current evidence base is exploratory: single-group design, small homogeneous sample from a single academic program, and a non-validated instrument

### Target Learners
- graduate healthcare professionals in procedural skills training

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating training-related effectiveness of AR-supported remote guidance beyond perceived usability
