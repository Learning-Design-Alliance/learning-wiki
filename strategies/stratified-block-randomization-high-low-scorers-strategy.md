# Stratified block randomization balancing high and low scorers across teaching-intervention groups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
To test whether a teaching intervention serves both high and low scorers, the article ranked students by prior departmental assessment scores, randomly selected 60 high scorers from the top 113 and 60 low scorers from the bottom 113, then formed sequential blocks of 30 combining blocks so that "an equal number of high and low scorers" sat in each group, with a coin toss assigning the intervention. This design enabled the low- versus high-scorer sub-analyses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prior departmental assessment scores (module and summative tests) for all enrolled students, and an online random number generator for selection
#### Constraints
- The article notes modifications to CONSORT including lack of allocation concealment and absence of blinding of assessors based on study design constraints

### Target Learners
- first-year medical undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable evaluation of a teaching intervention's effectiveness across prior-achievement levels
