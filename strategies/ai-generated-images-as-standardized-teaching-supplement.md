# Use AI-generated disease images as a standardized supplement to limited real clinical teaching materials

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating AI-generated medical images into curricula where real teaching material is scarce or variable. The authors state that "the use of AI-generated images in future medical education offers three advantages: customized disease image generation for targeted medical training, providing a homogeneous and stable teaching environment, and expansion beyond the limitations of existing real image collections." The strategy is a 1-hour preceptor-guided image reading session in which students independently extract and analyze visual features from generated images.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Generated images must be reviewed and affirmed by teachers before use, and teacher guidance is necessary when students interpret the images
#### Constraints
- Tested only for infectious keratitis diagnosis with fourth-year medical students; the test was administered immediately after teaching, so long-term retention and transfer to real patient care remain untested

### Target Learners
- medical students in clinical clerkship

### Target Learning Goals
- visual differential diagnosis of disease categories from clinical images
