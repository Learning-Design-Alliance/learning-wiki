Teach coursebook texts through four intertextual connection categories before creative writing tasks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article applies intertextual reading by having students make four kinds of connections while reading: "1. making connections with other written texts about the same subject, 2. making connections with research outputs, 3. making connections with communicated events, and 4. making connections with other situations that were not explicitly explained, only implied." Each of three coursebook texts was taught for six hours under this approach, after which students wrote narrative or informative essays. The strategy is credited with gains in originality of ideas and vocabulary richness.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Texts from the language course book taught over six hours each
  • Essays written after each text in narrative or informative style

Constraints¶

  • Evaluated only on the Originality of Ideas and Vocabulary Richness rubric dimensions, not all eight subdimensions

Target Learners¶

  • primary school fifth-grade students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • creative writing
  • original idea generation
  • vocabulary richness in word selection

Affordances¶

Claims¶

  • Intertextual reading instruction raises fifth-graders' Originality of Ideas scores in creative writing relative to conventional instruction [+W]
  • Intertextual reading instruction raises fifth-graders' Vocabulary Richness scores in creative writing relative to conventional instruction [+W]
  • Overall post-test creative writing scores differ significantly between intertextual-reading and control groups [+W]
