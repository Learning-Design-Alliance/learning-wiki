# Attend to host-context cultural logistics — daily structure, prayer and fasting schedules, and locally adapted team composition — when implementing simulation activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Implementation Strategies theme collects cultural considerations existing outside the simulation activity itself that affect logistics and delivery. Participants advised adapting scenarios to each environment because healthcare team size and makeup differ, giving people permission to adapt to local context, and understanding "timing and constraints related to specific cultural needs (prayer, fasting, etc.)" which can affect how a simulation is run and implemented.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Culturally inclusive development and pilot testing
- Knowledge of the structure of a day in the host context
#### Constraints
- Grounded in home-faculty interview reports in a small qualitative sample; the article notes implementation was described, not experimentally tested

### Target Learners
- Host-context learners and healthcare teams receiving simulation curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- Smooth delivery of adapted simulation-based education
- Respect for host-context cultural practices during implementation
