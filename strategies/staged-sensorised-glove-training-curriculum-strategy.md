# Incorporate the validated sensorised glove into structured obstetric training curricula after staged refinement and validation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose a staged pathway: refine the glove (make it wireless, standardise the disimpaction model), validate force measurements across prototypes against an expert pool, then "the glove could be incorporated into structured training curricula to evaluate its effectiveness in enhancing skill acquisition among obstetric trainees", followed by regulatory approvals and clinical studies. Participants saw potential for training, understanding technique and forces, and future real-time feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Consistency across glove prototypes, reproducible force measurements, comparison of outputs with an expert pool of clinical judgement, and an in vivo study before training or clinical use
#### Constraints
- The current glove is wired, requires triple gloving, and the expert force benchmark is a reference point rather than a validated safety threshold; the study did not formally evaluate impact on training outcomes

### Target Learners
- Obstetric trainees and clinicians managing caesarean birth

### Target Learning Goals
- Technique and force control in impacted fetal head management; awareness of hand use and applied force
