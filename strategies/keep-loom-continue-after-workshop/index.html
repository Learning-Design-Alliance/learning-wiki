Strategies

Use accessible analog loom technology that youth can keep and continue working on after the workshop ends¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (3 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A specific implementable design choice: provide youth with analog frame looms that are accessible enough for them to keep and continue working on their projects after the formal workshop ends. The article states: "Due to the accessible nature of the looms, youth were able to keep their looms and continue work on their projects after the end of the workshop." This extends learning time and supports sustained engagement with the mathematical ideas embedded in weaving.

Claims¶

  • 30% of youth weavers shifted toward planning or implementing new over/under sequences from first to last project [+W]
  • 76% of youth weavers shifted their practice to experiment with embedding shapes into their designs from first to last project [+W]
  • Twelve of thirteen youth did some experimenting or leveling up in their planned or implemented designs [+W]
  • Neither youth nor loom alone drove the algorithmic production and fabric output; both actively produced it collaboratively [+W]
  • Computation in weaving is characterized by the performance of automation, with student-loom patterns moving from irregular to regular over time [+W]
  • Weaving supports middle school students' performance of the computational concepts variables, conditionals, and functions [+W]
