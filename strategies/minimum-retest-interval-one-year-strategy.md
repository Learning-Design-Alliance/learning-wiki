# Set a minimum retest interval of at least one year to minimize memory-based practice effects on identical test forms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A retesting-policy strategy drawn from the meta-analysis: because practice effects for identical test forms decline with the time interval between administrations, testing programs can reduce memory-driven score gains by enforcing a minimum retest interval. The article states that "organizations may be able to minimize practice effects due to memory by using a minimum retest interval of at least one year." The predicted convergence point for identical- and alternate-form effects is 685 days, just under two years, so one year sits on the safe side of that boundary rather than at an optimum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires the testing program to control the interval between administrations and to track whether identical or alternate forms are used at retest.
#### Constraints
- Grounded in memory-decay effects for identical forms; the article reports practice effects still occur with alternate forms, so score gains are not simply recall effects, and this strategy does not eliminate gains from coaching or mere repetition.

### Target Learners
- job applicants retaking cognitive ability tests in personnel selection
- students retaking admissions tests such as the SAT, MCAT, or GRE

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate interpretation of retest score gains in selection and admission decisions
