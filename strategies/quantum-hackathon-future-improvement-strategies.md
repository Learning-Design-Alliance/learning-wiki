# Improvements for future iterations: extend the event, add unstructured social and practice time, use physical props, and align content with computational thinking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on lessons learned, the authors recommend extending the event beyond two days and rebalancing instructional and break time, since "additional group activities would, at the very least, make students feel more comfortable amongst their peers". They also propose integrating more props because the model Bloch sphere "stirred much interest from participants", aligning lecture notes more closely with computational thinking before release, exploring ZX tools such as quantomatic, recasting objectives in terms of quest-based learning, and adding intermediary materials such as the quantum computer system stack.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Extending the event requires more preparation time for planning lectures and organizing activities, and chaperones to supervise minor participants.
#### Constraints
- Scalability poses a dilemma: a yearly event risks past participants losing interest, while intermediate-level hackathons run counter to the goal of reaching students not previously interested in STEM.

### Target Learners
- high school students from rural communities and underrepresented groups in Nova Scotia

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement, group work, and computational thinking in introductory quantum computing
