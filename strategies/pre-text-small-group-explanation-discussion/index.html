Strategies

Present a problem for small-group explanation discussion before assigning the relevant expository text¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Confront small groups with a description of natural phenomena and ask them to propose explanations in terms of underlying processes, principles, or mechanisms; only after the discussion is a text presented that provides information relevant to the solution. The article reports this activation of prior knowledge through small-group discussion facilitates comprehension of the subsequent expository text, particularly its explanatory content.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A problem describing natural phenomena whose explanation requires the concepts of the target text; small groups with an experimenter or teacher acting as chairperson who summarizes points of view without providing information about underlying mechanisms

Constraints¶

  • The facilitative effect was demonstrated for explanatory propositions in free recall; descriptive facts were not better remembered as a consequence of the analysis of the blood cell problem

Target Learners¶

  • secondary-school and college science students, both novices and experts

Target Learning Goals¶

  • comprehension and recall of causal, explanatory concepts from expository science text

Affordances¶

  • Prior Knowledge Activation Explanatory Models

Claims¶

  • Analyzing a problem before reading facilitates overall free recall of a relevant expository text [+W]
  • Small-group discussion of a problem activates relevant prior knowledge, more than doubling free recall of propositions about the underlying process [+W]
  • The text-learning benefit of prior problem analysis is confined to explanatory propositions; recall of descriptive facts is not improved [+W]
  • Novices and experts benefit alike from problem analysis before reading; the treatment-by-expertise interaction is not significant [+W]
  • Problem analysis through small-group discussion is particularly effective at eliciting explanatory rather than descriptive propositions [+W]
  • Problem-based tutorial discussion of a relevant problem before studying an expository text increases the number of accurate propositions recalled from that text [+W]
  • Providing explanations during a small-group discussion yields better recall of subsequently studied text one month later than only listening to the discussion [+W]
  • Analyzing a problem before reading facilitates subsequent recall of a relevant text for both novices and experts [+W]
