# Evaluate interventions by whether they increase resources or lower extraneous demands while holding rigor constant

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 1 causal, 1 review), `q3`–`q4` · 4 of 5 report an effect size

## Description
The discussion proposes using the two independent dimensions of student appraisals as levers for instructional design: increasing perceived resources, decreasing perceived demands, or doing both. This offers a language for the instructor question of whether making a course less threatening means lowering rigorous learning standards. The article recommends that "researchers can seek to characterize whether proposed interventions increase resources and/or lower extraneous demands while holding the learning demanded of students at the same level of rigor," making resources and demands the explicit topic of instructional decision-making and further research.
