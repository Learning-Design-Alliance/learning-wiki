# Use VR simulation as a supplement to, not a replacement for, traditional audiology training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends deploying immersive VR training alongside traditional clinical training rather than instead of it, because VR enables safe, repeatable practice with feedback without risk to patients, while traditional formats cover skills VR cannot. The authors state that "the goal of VR is not to replace, but to complement other modes of audiological training", reserving precise gestures and patient communication for manikins, teachers and internships. They also advise creating different pre- and post-training questionnaires to reduce procedural learning effects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to VR hardware and the simulator
- Supervision to introduce the system
#### Constraints
- Single-center results needing multi-center confirmation
- Long-term retention not evaluated
- VR unsuitable for precise hand-movement skills and patient-interaction training

### Target Learners
- first-year audiology students
- practitioners in continuing education

### Target Learning Goals
- audiometric procedures and diagnosis
- safe pre-clinical preparation before patient contact
