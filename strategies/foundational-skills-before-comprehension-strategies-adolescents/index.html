Address fluency and word-meaning deficits before or alongside higher-order comprehension strategy instruction for struggling adolescent readers¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that interventions for struggling adolescent readers not rely solely on comprehension strategies such as summarization or graphic organizers, since most such students lack foundational skills. It states "Many students will still require intervention to address skills in reading text efficiently and understanding word meanings before more higher order comprehension processes can be expected." Students with multiple deficits likely need more intensive intervention than students with a single isolated deficit.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interventions must include components building reading fluency and vocabulary knowledge, not only strategy instruction

Constraints¶

  • The article notes comprehension strategy instruction may address only part of students' comprehension difficulties; intensity should match the number of deficit areas

Target Learners¶

  • middle and secondary students with reading comprehension difficulties

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading comprehension
  • reading fluency
  • vocabulary knowledge

Claims¶

  • 96% of middle-school students with low reading comprehension demonstrate deficits in reading fluency, vocabulary, or both [+W]
  • Co-occurring fluency and vocabulary deficits form the largest skill profile among struggling adolescent comprehenders (57%) [+W]
  • Fluency difficulties are more common than vocabulary difficulties among adolescents with low reading comprehension [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use vocabulary screening in early childhood and fluency or vocabulary screening after comprehension tests to catch foundational deficits
