# Use baseline visuospatial and bimanual profiling to personalize laparoscopic simulation training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend incorporating competency screening with baseline psychomotor tests (3D spatial ability, 2HAND bimanual coordination) to personalize training intensity and content, because these measures forecast learning trajectories on specific laparoscopic tasks. They suggest "enhancing visuospatial drills for CN/HE, while coupling bimanual tasks with neuromodulation when appropriate". This strategy rests on the trial's findings that baseline visuospatial ability predicted CN and HE outcomes while tDCS selectively benefited bimanual coordination.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to standardized baseline psychomotor assessment (Vienna Test System 3D and 2HAND tests) before training
#### Constraints
- Outcomes were restricted to simulator-based metrics rather than real-world intraoperative endpoints; the study had a small sample, single-center setting, and short 7-day follow-up without retention testing

### Target Learners
- Medical students and novices in early-stage laparoscopic training

### Target Learning Goals
- Laparoscopic psychomotor skills: camera navigation, hand–eye coordination, bimanual coordination
