# Use longitudinal two-group designs with school marks to control for selection effects in CLIL performance research

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends comparing variations in evaluations across consecutive years rather than single-moment snapshots, because "By comparing the variations in their evaluations instead of instant evaluations as they are given by the school, the effect of such factors can be reduced." This corrects for non-comparable school evaluations and for the higher motivation of students selected into CLIL programs. The author further suggests strengthening the design with multilevel analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Evaluations from two consecutive years of the same students
#### Constraints
- A cross-sectional study would not solve the problems of the longitudinal design used

- primary education students
