# Adopt motion-sensing video-game consoles as a low-cost adjunct to simulator-based laparoscopic training, especially in lower-budget institutions or at home

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend adding structured Nintendo Wii training to standard surgical education based on simulators and the operating room, because the consoles' "economic impact ... is significantly lower than traditional laparoscopic simulators and they provide a basic didactic value". They suggest the Wii "may be adopted in lower-budget Institutions or at home by younger surgeons to optimize their training on simulators before performing real procedures", and hope it triggers development of dedicated software stressing two-hand coordination, depth perception and precision of movements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured, supervised training schedule (60 minutes a day, five days a week, four weeks in the study)
- Games selected for high demands of eye-hand coordination, movement precision, depth perception and 3D visualization
#### Constraints
- The authors note it is hard to suggest Academic Institutions adopt a video-game console as a didactic tool; evidence comes from a pilot study with a relatively short training period

### Target Learners
- Young surgeons and surgical residents in training

### Target Learning Goals
- Laparoscopic skills acquisition before real operating-room procedures
