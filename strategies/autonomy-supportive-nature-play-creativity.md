# Provide autonomy-supportive outdoor play in nature-rich settings to foster children's creativity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Across the reviewed kindergarten and nature-school studies, an important factor coinciding with nature exposure was the autonomy-supportive environment facilitated by teachers: children were encouraged to direct their own play and granted a high level of autonomy to use the natural setting as a canvas for their imagination. The open, ambiguous characteristics of nature kindergartens — lack of fences and manufactured equipment, and raw materials that can be moved and interacted with in different ways — facilitated a dynamic play environment. The review recommends considering autonomy and other situational factors when interpreting results of nature exposure interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to outdoor natural settings with diverse raw natural materials
- Teachers who support child-directed play rather than directing activity
#### Constraints
- The review notes autonomy itself has been found to significantly predict creativity, so it may confound attributions of gains to nature exposure alone

### Target Learners
- preschool and kindergarten children
- primary school students
