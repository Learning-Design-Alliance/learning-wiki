# Brief informational seminars plus start-up and sustainability support to build site readiness for adopting a group-care model

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's translation approach pairs a one-hour informational seminar—covering birth-outcome epidemiology, evidence for CP, patient satisfaction, and the model's essential elements—with follow-up support for interested sites, including "a follow-up two-day training session on the delivery of CP and consultation and support during mode implementation." The authors state that readiness to adopt "will require support for the start-up and sustainability of this model," including adequate patient volume, appropriate group space, trained staff, and a facilitative leadership style.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adequate volume of prenatal care patients, appropriate space for group appointments, staff trained in brief assessments and facilitative leadership, and administrative, provider, and financial support for start-up and sustainability, as stated in the discussion
#### Constraints
- Data are self-report and representative only of people who attended the CP seminar; RE-AIM differences were asked without indicating directionality

### Target Learners
- Prenatal care providers and health care personnel at clinics considering adopting a group prenatal care model

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge of the essential elements and value of the Centering Pregnancy model; readiness to implement the model in practice
