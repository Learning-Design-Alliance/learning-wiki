# Incorporate scenario-based simulation training focused on VAP prevention into undergraduate nursing curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their finding that simulation training significantly improved nursing students' performance in implementing VAP prevention strategies, the authors recommend "incorporating scenario‐based simulation training focusing on VAP prevention into undergraduate nursing curricula". The training used briefing (5 min), simulation (10 min) and debriefing (30 min) with the GAS (Gather/Analyse/Summarise) technique, in a laboratory equipped with a high-fidelity simulator.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulation laboratory equipped similarly to an ICU with a high-fidelity simulator; validated scenarios and a performance checklist; trained simulation educators
#### Constraints
- The authors state further research is warranted before implementing this training in other settings and populations

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students; also proposed for in-service training and postgraduate nursing education

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge and skills for preventing ventilator-associated pneumonia in intensive care
