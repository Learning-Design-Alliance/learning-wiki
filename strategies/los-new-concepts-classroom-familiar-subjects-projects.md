# Use learning objects in the classroom for new concepts and in projects for previously encountered subjects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends matching the LO setting to students' familiarity with the content: classroom use with teacher guidance for concepts students have not faced before, and project-based extracurricular use for subjects students already know something about. The authors state that "if the LOs used for the subjects which the students have not faced before; in classroom activities would be better for this case".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- LOs designed and planned for the specific new concepts before classroom introduction
- project contents prepared in quality when LOs are assigned for extracurricular work
#### Constraints
- Students experienced difficulties preparing projects with LOs for subjects they had not encountered before, because the projects focused on consolidating concept knowledge and solving problems rather than conceptual learning

### Target Learners
- 9th-grade high school mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- learning new mathematical concepts and consolidating previously learned content
