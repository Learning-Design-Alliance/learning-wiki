# Use augmented reality systems with mixed reality capture and motion tracking to teach procedural skills remotely as if mentor and trainee were co-located

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends integrating AR devices such as Microsoft HoloLens, Oculus devices or Google Glass into procedural skills training. A mentor's hand gestures could be captured through motion tracking (such as Leap Motion) and virtually displayed in the trainee's AR space, while the learner's first-person view of a simulated emergency or delivery room is captured through mixed reality capture. The authors state this could "enable procedural skills teaching as if both mentor and trainee were in the same location, even if they work remotely". This is an author recommendation, not a tested intervention in the article.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- AR hardware devices and motion-tracking technology such as Leap Motion must be available to capture mentor gestures and display them in the trainee's AR space
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a proposal for investigation, not an evaluated method; no comparative outcomes are reported

### Target Learners
- pediatric healthcare professionals and trainees, including residency and fellowship trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural skills, communication through the visual channel
