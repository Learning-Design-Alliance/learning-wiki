# Scaffold feedback literacy with short modelling activities so learners can translate reflective prompts into action

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because not all students possess the metacognitive readiness to act on reflective prompts, the article recommends explicit cultivation of feedback literacy. The authors state that "short scaffolded activities-such as modelling how to interpret and apply feedback-can bridge this gap", supporting development of self-regulation and evaluative judgement and moving learners from passive reception to active engagement with feedback. This addresses the trial's finding that metacognitive feedback produced the lowest revision rates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors provide guided practice in interpreting and applying feedback before or alongside reflective AI feedback
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness of reflective prompts depends heavily on students' feedback literacy, per the article's framing

### Target Learners
- higher education students, particularly novices lacking metacognitive readiness

### Target Learning Goals
- feedback literacy
- self-regulation
- evaluative judgement
