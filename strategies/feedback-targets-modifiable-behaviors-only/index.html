Design feedback to reference only modifiable behaviors and avoid mentioning performance to prevent demotivation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's feedback design referenced actionable study behaviors rather than scores: "the message content referenced only modifiable behaviors and did not mention performance." Although low exam scores triggered additional guidance such as campus resource recommendations, messages highlighted productive behaviors like early homework initiation and consistent practice engagement. The authors report this design emphasized productive engagement behaviors without demotivating underperforming students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Analytics variables and thresholds (e.g., practice problem counts, homework start timing) to identify modifiable behaviors per student

Constraints¶

  • Whether students used the additional time effectively as the intervention intended is unknown, per the authors

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in large-enrollment STEM courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • adopting productive study behaviors such as early homework initiation and practice problem engagement

Claims¶

  • Integrating behavioral trace data with outcomes and self-report reveals feedback effects that perception and performance measures alone miss [+M]
  • LA-based personalized feedback increased the proportion of students attempting practice problems during the intervention phase compared to generic messages [+M]
  • Personalized feedback did not increase the number of practice problems attempted among students who engaged, in either phase [~M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Phrase praise modestly and focus on strategies rather than effort to prevent ability-denigrating inferences
