# Teachers should schedule Technology activities with special attention to each Intelligence Style

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation is that the results should encourage teachers to pay special attention to Gardner's Multiple Intelligences Theory when scheduling teaching activities, because a focus on each Intelligence Style enhanced both the emotional performance of the whole group and content acquisition. In practice this means planning a varied activity set — here nine activities, one per IS — alongside traditional instruction, rather than relying only on expository lessons that favour linguistic and logical styles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of students' prevalent IS profiles
- Willingness to vary instructional methodology across the activity set
#### Constraints
- The article notes it is very difficult to isolate a unique reason the intervention worked, citing possible roles of activity variety and teacher motivation

### Target Learners
- Secondary school Technology students, especially those with non-linguistic/non-logical prevalent styles

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved emotional yield and content acquisition in Technology lessons
