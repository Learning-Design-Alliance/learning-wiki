# Use task-supplemental integration prompts (focus questions) to foster structural knowledge integration across domains

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Provide learners with task-supplemental prompts in the form of focus questions that direct attention to integrating information across domain-specific sources. The authors developed "five prompts in the form of focus questions" such as "Can you identify information in the texts that can be linked to conclusions for the design of lessons which are reasonable from multiple perspectives?" These prompts act as "strategy activators" that help learners construct an adequate task model for searching, evaluating, and structurally integrating domain-specific information.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning materials must contain connectable content across multiple domain-specific sources
- Prompts should remain with learners throughout the task performance phase
#### Constraints
- Effects demonstrated on structural measures; semantic-level effects were not significant

### Target Learners
- pre-service teachers
- higher education students

### Target Learning Goals
- integrated understanding across professional knowledge domains
- structural knowledge integration
