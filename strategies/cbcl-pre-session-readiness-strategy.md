# Use pre-session readiness materials and MCQ self-assessment to shift content acquisition out of class time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a flipped-classroom preparation phase in which residents received a short video, an ACR criteria summary, and an introductory case before the session, plus a five-MCQ formative readiness assessment. This was designed to shift "the initial acquisition of foundational concepts to the pre-session moment, thereby allowing face-to-face time to focus on clinical application, collaborative reasoning, discussion, and argumentation." Materials were organized as advance organizers supporting self-regulated learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Preparatory materials intentionally aligned with the educational objectives
- A brief formative readiness assessment to promote minimum familiarity with key concepts
- A brief in-session review of the initial case to support learners with different levels of preparation
#### Constraints
- The MCQs were not formally validated or psychometrically tested
- Adherence to preparatory materials was not formally measured

### Target Learners
- Radiology residents
- Medical trainees in flipped-classroom settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Readiness for collaborative case discussion
- Self-regulated learning
- Foundational concept acquisition before class
