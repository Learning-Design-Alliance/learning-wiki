# Morphological Analysis of Latin and Greek Roots

## Description
Morphological analysis of Latin and Greek roots involves teaching learners about the meaningful parts of words, including prefixes, suffixes, and roots of Latin and Greek origin. This approach emphasizes that many words are constructed from stable and meaningful word parts. For example, learning that the Latin root 'nat' means 'to be born' can help learners understand words like 'natal,' 'native,' and 'nation.' This strategy enhances vocabulary development, reading comprehension, and spelling accuracy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- List of Latin and Greek roots, prefixes, and suffixes, direct instruction from a teacher or tutor, and consistent practice with word analysis
#### Constraints
- Can be challenging for learners with limited background knowledge of Latin and Greek. Requires explicit and systematic instruction. May be difficult to apply if learners lack foundational phonics skills.

- A fifth-grade teacher organizes word study around a common Latin root, such as 'dict' (to speak), to teach words like 'dictum,' 'dictionary,' and 'edict.' A high school teacher uses Greek combining forms to teach scientific concepts and discoveries, such as 'thermodynamics' and 'isotherm.'
