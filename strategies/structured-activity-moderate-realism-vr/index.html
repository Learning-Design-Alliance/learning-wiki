Structured activity design and moderate realism benefit shared VR learning environments¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that shared VR learning environments employ structured activity design and moderate realism to benefit equity, adaptability, and cost-effectiveness. Structured tasks with clear conjecture prompts, guided manipulation goals, and a facilitator directing progress gave groups a shared focus. Moderate realism avoided the costs of photorealistic rendering while supporting meaningful embodied collaboration.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A facilitator to ask participants questions, solve technical issues, and control their progress

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is the authors' design implication drawn from a study of three groups of in-service teachers in one VR geometry environment

Target Learners¶

  • secondary mathematics students
  • in-service mathematics teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • collaborative geometric reasoning and problem-solving

Claims¶

  • Increased perceptual fidelity fails to reliably improve serious game learning outcomes (the fidelity fallacy) [+W]
  • Virtual objects serve as effective media for collaborative knowledge building in shared VR environments [+W]
  • Body movements and positions focusing on virtual objects, and shifts away from or around them, signal key interactional collaborative events in shared VR [+W]
  • Collaborative speech strongly co-occurs with actions on virtual objects and virtual hand gestures in shared VR geometry tasks [+W]
