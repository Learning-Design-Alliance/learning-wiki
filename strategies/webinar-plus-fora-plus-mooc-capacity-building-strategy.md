# Combine live interactive webinars with asynchronous fora, instant-messaging channels and self-paced MOOCs in a modular, open-registration package for global workforce capacity building

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (7 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The initiative paired 15 one-hour live webinars with expert presentations, country case studies, polling, chat and Q&A with TechNet-21 web fora and English/French instant-messaging groups, alongside OpenWHO MOOCs. Sessions were modular, open to all, and materials were posted publicly. Approximately 1000 members joined the instant-messaging channels, which "actively continued beyond the end of the webinar series".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Registration open and unrestricted, with recordings, presentations and Q&A documents posted on publicly accessible websites to keep information transparent and readily accessible
- Technical experts available to answer participant questions live or in compiled Q&A documents
- Partnership platforms (TechNet-21, Project ECHO) to host fora and channels
#### Constraints
- Requires participants to have access to videoconferencing and mobile platforms; the authors note exploring low-bandwidth, low-cost apps for future programs

### Target Learners
- Health workers
- Public health practitioners at national and subnational levels
- Immunization focal points in low- and middle-income countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Preparation and rollout of COVID-19 vaccination programs
- Access to real-time normative guidance and best practices
- Peer-to-peer learning and community of practice building

## Claims

- [A 15-session interactive webinar series reached 3058 individuals in 179 countries, covering 93% of WHO Member States, with the majority from LMICs](../claims/covid-webinar-series-reached-3058-in-179-countries.md) [+W]
- [79.6% of post-series respondents reported confidence in preparing for and rolling out COVID-19 vaccination increased \"a lot\"](../claims/confidence-increased-lot-79-point-6-percent.md) [+W]
- [51.9% of post-series respondents participated in all three training formats (mobile channels, OpenWHO MOOCs, webinars), and low-income-country respondents were more likely to do so (68.0% vs 43.3%, P < 0.001)](../claims/three-format-participation-low-income-countries.md) [+W]
- [The interactive format served as a real-time feedback mechanism that WHO used to identify stakeholder needs and develop new guidance resources](../claims/webinar-series-real-time-feedback-mechanism.md) [+W]
- [Confidence gains differed by profession (frontline providers 72.5% vs public health 86.4% vs other 85.7%, P = 0.04) and by attendance dose (4+ sessions 83.8% vs fewer 65.4%, P = 0.004)](../claims/confidence-gains-vary-by-profession-and-attendance.md) [~W]
- [70% of participants planned to \"definitely use\" the knowledge in their work, with profession differences (public health 75.8% vs other 62.3%, P < 0.001), and relevance also differed by profession (P < 0.001)](../claims/intention-to-use-knowledge-70-percent-profession-differences.md) [+W]
- [Participants applied the learning: more than 60% used session resources, 47% shared them, and 19 respondents reported planning, advocacy and guidance-drafting actions](../claims/participants-used-and-shared-session-resources.md) [+W]
- [Post-session self-reported knowledge of the session topic increased significantly (64.4% reported increase, P < 0.001)](../claims/post-session-knowledge-increase-64-percent.md) [+W]
