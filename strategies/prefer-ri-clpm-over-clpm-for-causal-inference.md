# Use alternatives to the CLPM when causal inference is the goal in longitudinal studies of academic development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 5 studies (4 associational, 1 review), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that longitudinal investigations of development within and between academic domains move beyond the traditional CLPM, which combines between- and within-person variation and is biased by stable unmeasured confounds. It states that "future investigations of longitudinal development including within and between academic domains should consider alternative models to the CLPM if causal inference is a goal of the study," and that researchers should explain conceptually what model estimates mean if causal inference is not the goal. It also recommends comparing a range of models and checking estimates against causally informative benchmarks such as intervention-impact decay.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Panel data with repeated measures of the outcomes across multiple time points, and comparison of model fit statistics (CFI, TLI, RMSEA) across candidate models.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the best design for identifying cross-domain influences remains a randomly assigned intervention with a large impact on one domain and no immediate effect on the other; modeling alone cannot fully resolve confounding.

### Target Learners
- Elementary school students (applied example: K-3 achievement)

### Target Learning Goals
- Estimating causal cross-domain effects of reading and mathematics achievement over time
