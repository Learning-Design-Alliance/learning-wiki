# Teach proper acceleration and time dilation from inertial frames only, using a computational experiment, without resorting to the accelerated observer's frame

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends analyzing the accelerated observer's trajectory entirely from the points of view of inertial frames: "We analyze this situation without resorting to the reference frame of the accelerated observer, thus making it easier for students to understand." Students use excel and python codes applying the Lorentz transformation and finite-difference differentiation to discover the unique comoving event, the invariant proper acceleration, and time dilation graphically.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students familiar with spacetime diagrams and the Lorentz transformation; access to excel or python for the computational activity.
#### Constraints
- The treatment is limited to rectilinear motion with constant proper acceleration, i.e. the Rindler observer's hyperbolic worldline.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understand proper acceleration
- understand instantaneously comoving inertial frames
- understand time dilation
