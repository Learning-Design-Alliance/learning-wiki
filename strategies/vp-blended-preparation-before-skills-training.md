# Combine interactive virtual patient preparation with standard hands-on simulation training for procedural skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's blended approach assigns interactive virtual patients with feedback and media as mandatory pre-training preparation, followed by standard simulation-based skills training structured with a four-step approach and close tutor feedback. The authors conclude this "leads to improved outcomes of practical skill acquisition compared with a standard approach" and may enable more efficient use of resource-intensive training time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- VPs must include interactivity and feedback (e.g., interactive graphics and video clips)
- Hands-on training with standardized individual practice time and tutor feedback follows the preparation
#### Constraints
- Further studies are necessary to understand the specific benefit of VPs for skill acquisition and its sustainability
- Sample size was limited, providing limited power

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students learning emergency procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural knowledge and practical skill performance in basic life support
