# Train social studies teachers and design purpose-built iPad applications to support iPad-based teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article closes with recommendations for implementing iPads in education: raising Ministry of Education officials' awareness of iPad use across educational phases, training social studies teachers through "conducting training courses and workshops", and designing social studies applications and software on the iPad according to quality and efficiency standards with development centers. These follow directly from the study's post-test findings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need training on how to apply the iPad in teaching, per the article's recommendation of training courses and workshops
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a single small quasi-experiment in one Kuwaiti girls' school

### Target Learners
- eighth-grade (intermediate stage) female students in social studies

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of mental skills and performance skills (map and chart skills) in social studies

## Claims

- [Eighth-grade social studies students taught with the iPad scored significantly higher on mental skills at post-test than traditionally taught controls](../claims/ipad-posttest-mental-skills-advantage.md) [+W]
- [Eighth-grade social studies students taught with the iPad scored significantly higher on performance skills at post-test than traditionally taught controls](../claims/ipad-posttest-performance-skills-advantage.md) [+W]
- [Before the intervention, the iPad and control groups showed no significant differences in mental skills](../claims/ipad-pretest-mental-skills-equivalence.md) [~W]
- [Before the intervention, the iPad and control groups showed no significant differences in performance skills](../claims/ipad-pretest-performance-skills-equivalence.md) [~W]
- [Educational games-based iPad applications produced statistically significant higher post-test social studies achievement than traditional teaching in a quasi-experiment with 48 sixth graders](../claims/ipad-games-post-achievement-significant.md) [+W]
- [Achievement gains from educational games-based iPad applications were retained on a delayed test four to six weeks after the post-test, with significant delayed-test differences favoring the experimental group](../claims/ipad-games-learning-retention-delayed-test.md) [+W]
