# Four recommended design features for PLTL programs aimed at broadening STEM participation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on eight semesters of implementation and assessment, the authors enumerate features they recommend for PLTL programs: "1) selection of a diverse group of peer facilitators who have excellent interpersonal skills...; 2) training facilitators in pedagogical principles and practices, including sociocultural methods; 3) providing faculty-generated question sets suitable for group discussion and problem solving; and 4) providing space and time outside the classroom for regularly scheduled workshops." These features operationalize the SGM program's aim of promoting inclusiveness while supporting engagement with lecture material.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Peer facilitators selected for interpersonal skills such as communication and compassion, modeling enthusiasm and self-efficacy in science
- A training course in pedagogical principles and sociocultural methods
- Faculty-generated challenge questions suitable for collaborative problem solving
- Regularly scheduled workshop space and time outside the classroom
#### Constraints
- The program is supplemental to lectures; designers must remain cognizant of the goal of broadening access for underrepresented students

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate introductory biology students, including students from underrepresented minority groups

### Target Learning Goals
- Engagement with lecture material through collaborative problem solving
- Inclusive participation in STEM
