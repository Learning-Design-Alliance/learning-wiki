# Give students two to five minutes to begin a complex problem before presenting the first PI question

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
When scaffolding a quantitative problem with PI, the instructor presents the problem first and lets students work collaboratively for a short bounded interval before any PI question. The author reports: "I have found that students need two to five minutes to work on the problem before I present the first PI question." This interval lets students read, interpret, formulate initial ideas, and realize where they are stuck, so they appreciate the need for the PI questions; the instructor circulates and listens to uncover unanticipated difficulties.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students work in collaboration with neighbors during the interval
- The instructor circulates, listens to conversations, and answers questions
- Enough time to get stuck, but not enough to fully solve, to preserve class time
#### Constraints
- Giving students enough time to actually arrive at a solution first may use a substantial fraction of class time and leave some students disengaged

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate astronomy students

### Target Learning Goals
- quantitative problem-solving
- identifying gaps in one's own reasoning
