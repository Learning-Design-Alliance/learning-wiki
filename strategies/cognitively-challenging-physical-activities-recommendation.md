# Allocate school-aged children to cognitively challenging physical activities such as tag games, team games, or coordinative exercises

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation is that "school-aged children are allocated to participate in cognitively challenging physical activities, such as tag games, team games, or coordinative exercises", which the authors state positively affect executive functions and improve academic performance in school-based settings. This draws on the discussed 'cognitive stimulation viewpoint' that activities requiring high coordination stimulate brain areas responsible for higher-order cognitive processes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School-based settings able to schedule such activities
#### Constraints
- The mechanism is supported by cited prior literature, not tested in this study; the article notes cognitive and executive performance may decline after peak physical strength is reached

### Target Learners
- school-aged children

### Target Learning Goals
- executive function and academic performance improvement
