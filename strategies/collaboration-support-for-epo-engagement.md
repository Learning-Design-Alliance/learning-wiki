# Large science collaborations can support researcher EPO engagement via shared repositories, dedicated funding, infrastructure credit, dedicated staff, and communication training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's survey item Q22 asked how DES and other large collaborations could best support collaboration-wide EPO engagement, and the ranked responses constitute an implementable support menu. The most frequent response (57%) suggested collaborations "build and maintain a repository of talks, slides, curricula, etc." for use in EPO activities; 54% suggested explicitly allocating funding for EPO projects; 52% suggested counting EPO as science-infrastructure time toward data and authorship rights; 48% suggested hiring dedicated EPO staff; and 37% suggested providing communication training for scientists.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration-level organizational infrastructure capable of maintaining shared repositories, allocating funding, and crediting EPO work toward data and authorship rights
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a voluntary survey of one collaboration with likely selection bias toward members already interested in EPO; the article notes results may not apply to the broader population of astronomy researchers

### Target Learners
- Physicists, astronomers, and astrophysicists in large research collaborations, including graduate students, post-docs, staff scientists, and faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- Increase and sustain researcher participation in education and public outreach activities
