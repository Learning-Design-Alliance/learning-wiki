# Self Evaluation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Self evaluation asks learners to judge the quality of their own work, performance, or understanding against explicit criteria before (or instead of) receiving external judgment. It is carried out through rubrics, checklists, exemplar comparison, or structured reflection prompts, and functions as both an assessment activity and a training method for self-regulation.

## Design Implications

Self evaluation improves achievement primarily when learners are trained to use criteria accurately and when evaluation feeds into revision or next-step action [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Untrained self-assessment is systematically biased — novices overestimate their performance, and the accuracy of self-grading improves only with practice, exemplars, and feedback on the evaluations themselves [~M]. Evaluation without a consequential follow-up (revision, re-attempt, planning) tends to become a box-checking exercise with little learning benefit.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit, learner-accessible criteria: a [Rubric](../elements/rubrics.md) or checklist written in language learners can apply independently
- High-quality exemplars at multiple quality levels so learners can calibrate their judgments ([Comparing Cases](../elements/comparing-cases.md))
- A consequential action after evaluation: revision, re-practice, or goal-setting — evaluation must feed the next attempt
- Training in how to apply the criteria, ideally with instructor feedback on the accuracy of early self-evaluations

#### Constraints
- Novices' self-ratings correlate weakly with actual performance and are often inflated [~M] — self evaluation alone cannot substitute for external feedback early in learning
- Simple checklists applied without understanding produce compliance, not judgment [Checklist-based evaluation is ineffective in online contexts.](../claims/checklist-evaluation-ineffective-online.md) [-M]
- Self-grading for high-stakes marks invites strategic inflation; low-stakes or formative use preserves accuracy
- Learners with strong prior knowledge may gain little, already self-monitoring effectively; the benefit concentrates in developing self-regulation [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Before submission:** learners predict their grade or check drafts against criteria, priming revision
- **Exemplar comparison:** learners rate anonymous peer or expert samples before rating their own, calibrating the scale
- **Scripted reflection:** structured prompts (e.g., [3-2-1 Reflection](../strategies/3-2-1_reflection.md)) scaffold what to attend to
- **Collaborative evaluation:** pairs or groups negotiate a shared judgment, surfacing criteria disagreements ([Collaborative Evaluation](../patterns/collaborative-evaluation.md))

### Target Learners
- Developing learners who do not yet accurately monitor their own understanding or performance [~M]
- Adolescents and adults; younger learners need heavily simplified criteria and modeling of the evaluation act itself
- Less valuable for experts, whose self-monitoring is already calibrated [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-regulated learning: accurate monitoring, which precedes effective control decisions
- Quality criteria internalization: understanding *what makes work good*, not just producing it
- Metacognitive skill: judging one's own comprehension and performance ([Articulation](../elements/articulation.md))

### Instructions
1. Provide criteria in learner-friendly language — a [Rubric](../elements/rubrics.md) or checklist co-developed or unpacked with the class
2. Show exemplars at varied quality levels and model evaluating one aloud ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md))
3. Have learners evaluate their own draft against the criteria, citing specific evidence for each judgment
4. Require a consequential next step: revise, re-practice ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)), or set a specific goal for the next attempt
5. Give feedback on the *accuracy* of the self-evaluation, not only the work, so calibration improves over time

## Related Strategies
- [3-2-1 Reflection](3-2-1_reflection.md) — a lightweight structured prompt that operationalizes self evaluation at the end of a lesson
- [Self Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md) — related metacognitive act, but explaining *why* rather than judging *how good*

## Examples
- **Portfolio assessment with rubric self-scores** — students score their own drafts against a rubric and attach a revision plan before teacher review; the self-score is discussed, not graded
- **Exam wrappers** — after a test is returned, students complete a short structured analysis of where they lost points and what study behavior caused it, feeding the next unit's preparation
- **[PALS](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies)** — pairs alternate evaluation roles with scripted prompts, training criteria use through repeated low-stakes judgments
