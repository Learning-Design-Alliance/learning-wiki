# For online lab courses, use interactive virtual labs, simulations, and reflective assignments based on pedagogical journal articles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that lab courses take advantage of interactive virtual labs, simulations and journal articles, noting many virtual labs exist (some free, some costing money beyond a 30-day period). It suggests students read pedagogical AJP and TPT articles about classic experiments such as single-photon double-slit interference, the Millikan oil drop experiment and muon decay, then write about experimental design, troubleshooting, errors and implications, with instructors assessing the writings and discussions and combining the tasks with related interactive simulations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to virtual lab resources or simulations (some free, some paid) and pedagogical journal articles from AJP and TPT
#### Constraints
- Some virtual labs cost money beyond a 30-day period

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in lab courses at all levels and upper-level lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding experimental design and sources of error
- learning to read and reflect upon journal articles
- lifelong learning skills
