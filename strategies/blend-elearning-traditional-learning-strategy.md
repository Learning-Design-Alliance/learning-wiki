# Blend e-learning with traditional learning rather than a sudden switch to e-learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests combining electronic learning with conventional classroom teaching, noting that "The mixture of e-learning and traditional learning was suggested, although interactive learning was dominant in this study." The discussion, citing prior work, states that learners benefit "from a blended learning model, comprising of traditional-style classroom lectures with e-learning aspects, compared with a sudden switch to e-learning". This strategy moderates the transition for learners accustomed to teacher-controlled classrooms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recognition that some intern nurses are more comfortable with traditional-style teaching where the teacher takes control
#### Constraints
- Nurses may face a hard time switching to more active formats; restricted computing access can obstruct e-learning

### Target Learners
- Intern nurses and nursing students accustomed to traditional lecture-based teaching

### Target Learning Goals
- Smooth transition to active, technology-supported clinical learning
