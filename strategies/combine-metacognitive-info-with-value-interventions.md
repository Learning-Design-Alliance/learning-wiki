# Combine metacognitive strategy information with motivational interventions that increase task value, and reduce need frustration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 5 studies (3 review, 2 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q4` · 2 of 5 report an effect size

## Description
The article recommends that self-regulated learning interventions should not rely on metacognitive information about strategy effectiveness alone, because expectancies and values spur motivation multiplicatively and low task value keeps effort-by-allocation low. It states: "Future interventions in the context of self-regulated learning should there- fore incorporate ideas from motivational interventions that try to increase the intrinsic, attainment, or utility value of a certain task or learning material." Ideally these are combined with SDT-based interventions that reduce effort-by-need frustration by supporting needs for competence and autonomy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must ensure the learning task itself is personally meaningful to students, not only that learners know the strategy works
#### Constraints
- Metacognitive information strengthens action-outcome expectancy but does not change task values; close instructional guidance can frustrate autonomy and reduce motivation in real-life settings

### Target Learners
- students in self-regulated learning contexts in school and higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- initiation and persistence of effective learning strategies such as retrieval practice and interleaved practice
