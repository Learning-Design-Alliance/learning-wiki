Teach metacognitive writing strategies through direct instruction, teacher modelling with thinking out loud, and guided application¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends incorporating metacognitive instruction into writing classrooms because it helps students become more self-regulated. It describes a teaching sequence in which teachers give clear explanations of the strategies, model them by using the technique "thinking out loud", and then give students ample opportunities to perform the same task under teacher guidance until the strategies become automatic, with feedback serving as independent practice.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers should give a clear explanation about the strategies to be taught, why they are important and when students will need to use them, and present examples to illustrate instruction

Constraints¶

  • The article reports students needed more time to be trained using metacognitive strategies

Claims¶

  • EFL students who received metacognitive-strategy writing training report positive attitudes because the strategies improve their writing and cultivate learning autonomy [+W]
  • Some EFL students experience difficulties implementing metacognitive writing strategies and report needing more training time [~W]
  • EFL students interviewed after metacognitive strategy training said they had become more self-reliant in monitoring their writing processes and progress [+W]
  • Intervention students poorly utilized brainstorming and need more training in it, while underlining the main idea was a planning technique they found helpful [~W]
  • Time management is the most overwhelming writing difficulty reported by the EFL students, alongside spelling and grammatical mistakes [~W]
