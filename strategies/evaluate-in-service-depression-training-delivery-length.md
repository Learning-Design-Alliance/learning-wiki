# Examine and evaluate hospital in-service depression training, considering delivery methods and length

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that hospitals carefully examine and evaluate in-service training related to depression, because nurses who had recent in-service training held more negative attitudes. The authors state that "in-service training in hospitals mihgt need to consider not only the contents but also the delivery methods and length of the training", citing contrasting evidence on Web-based versus face-to-face formats. They further recommend developing interventions to increase nurses’ late-life depression knowledge and attitudes in acute-care settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training content, delivery method, and length should be examined and evaluated rather than assumed effective
#### Constraints
- The negative training–attitude association is cross-sectional and correlational; causality is not established

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses in acute-care hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- Late-life depression knowledge and favorable attitudes toward depression care
