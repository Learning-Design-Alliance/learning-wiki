# Instructors compile native-speaker video lessons and pair students for peer-supported immersive multimedia learning of L2 oral skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers and lecturers use immersive multimedia learning with peer support to develop oral skills in EFL or ESL teaching, and states that "any instructor can compile similar native speaker video lessons in any language and use them without much hassle". Students work in pairs formed by their own choice of partners while the teacher acts as facilitator, and students are encouraged to practise English with partners or lecturers inside and outside class. Educational institutions are advised to provide facilities such as tablets and headphones and to train teachers to use technology in L2 teaching based on L1 theories.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional provision of facilities such as tablets and headphones
- Training of lecturers/teachers to use technology in L2 teaching based on L1 theories
#### Constraints
- Findings on high achievers may not be fully generalizable because of small sample sizes
- The study is limited to limited peer support activities and learning strategies

### Target Learners
- First-year university EFL students
- Low and high English achievement students

### Target Learning Goals
- English oral production in reading aloud and speaking
- Reduced reliance on code-switching
