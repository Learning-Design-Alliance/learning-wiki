# Develop the EPR argument for pupils in five steps: mathematical tools, entangled experimental setup, classical assumptions, quantum-theoretical outcome, interpretation

## Description
The article lays out a concrete instructional recipe for the EPR–gedanken experiment: Step 1 the mathematical tools (state vectors, operators, eigenvalues and eigenstates using Pauli matrices), Step 2 the entangled two-photon setup sharing one common state, Step 3 the classical assumptions of separability and physical reality, Step 4 the quantum-theoretical outcome, and Step 5 the interpretation that fixed values of properties only emerge in measurements. It builds a bridge "fr om the mathematical structures over the physical phenomena and c onnecting to a philosophical discussion".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students able to compute eigenvalues and eigenstates easily from Pauli matrices
- A spin-based concrete example in place of the original EPR argument
#### Constraints
- The sequence is proposed, not empirically evaluated

### Target Learners
- interested pupils at higher grades (16+)

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the power of the mathematical formalism of quantum theory and its physical interpretation
