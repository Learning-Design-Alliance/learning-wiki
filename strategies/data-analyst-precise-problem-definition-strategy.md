# Assign a data analyst to review school data before team meetings and define problems with precision before selecting solutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that school teams use formal problem-solving protocols, with at least one team member (the data analyst) reviewing school data prior to the meeting to summarize the data quickly and focus the team on areas in need of problem-solving. Teams should define problems with precision (what happens, how often, where, who, when, and why) and select solutions matched to the problem pattern before shifting from problem definition to solution. The authors observed teams becoming more effective and efficient when they followed this sequence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the right information in the right format at the right time
- Clearly defined protocols for how to use data for decision making
- A constant group meeting at planned times with specific objectives, authority, and defined roles
#### Constraints
- Teams with limited time were prone to define problems with limited precision and adopt poorly matched solutions

### Target Learners
- elementary school behavior-support team members

### Target Learning Goals
- precise problem definition and matched solution selection from schoolwide data
