# Small-group estimation and averaging of transit features from a real light curve to determine exoplanet parameters

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students work in small groups at individual computers, read the times and fluxes of transit features directly from the MAST light curve, and estimate the transit length, depth and spacing of overlaps; "By averaging these, the ﬁnal re- sult was obtained", and the averaged values feed the geometric formulas for the system parameters. The procedure works by "avoiding the ﬁtting of complex math- ematical models", giving an illustrative picture of how astronomers determine parameters of transiting planetary systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A school IT classroom where each student has their own computer; the article divided students into smaller groups of 10-12 people determined by the room's capacity.
#### Constraints
- The article warns that these methods provide only an illustrative picture and the results obtained are only approximate, so they should be compared with the actual data in the databases.

### Target Learners
- secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- estimating exoplanet system parameters from light-curve data
- thinking independently and recognizing connections from a real data set

## Claims

- [Students estimated the orbital period of Kepler-2 from two consecutive transits as 2.201631±0.011335 days, close to the literature value](../claims/students-estimate-orbital-period-kepler-2.md) [+W]
- [Students determined the HAT-P-7b planet-to-star radius ratio from the light curve by two methods, both close to the literature value](../claims/students-estimate-radius-ratio-two-methods.md) [+W]
- [Using circular-orbit geometry, students estimated the semi-major axis of Kepler-2 as 0.0366±0.0011 AU, near the accepted value](../claims/students-estimate-semi-major-axis-circular-orbit.md) [+W]
- [From Kepler's third law with the student-derived orbit, students estimated the HAT-P-7b stellar mass as 1.4823±0.4107 solar masses](../claims/students-estimate-star-mass-keplers-third-law.md) [+W]
