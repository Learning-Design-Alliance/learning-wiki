# Teach qualitative physics — dimensional analysis, heuristic methods, graphical reasoning, approximation and guessing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's central remedy: instead of compounding misconceptions, university teaching should "teach dimensional analysis, heuristic methods, graphical reasoning, and the arts of approximation and guessing" — teaching students how physicists think. Methods are illustrated with everyday applications such as stirring tea, which the author reports fascinated students, who spent hours stirring tea and timing the spindown, giving them feedback from the world on their physical picture.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires problems requiring estimation and conceptual reasoning, and exam questions that reward such reasoning, otherwise students will not invest in it
