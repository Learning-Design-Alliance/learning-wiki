# Design peer mindset interventions that leverage adolescents' need for belonging and status, including high-status peer models

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends making growth mindset behaviors socially rewarded rather than socially costly: "One approach is to create environments where growth mindset behaviors, such as sharing struggles, helping peers, or framing effort positively, are not socially costly but instead socially rewarded." A second approach draws on social referent theory, equipping high-status peers to model growth mindset behaviors so interventions harness natural peer influence. Practical formats suggested include peer discussion groups, small advisory circles, and dyadic support models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Growth mindset behaviors must be linked to admired identities and desirable peer roles
- High-status peers should model inclusive practices authentically, not police others' behavior
#### Constraints
- The review states no interventions to date explicitly target peer mindset culture; these strategies are drawn from related work

### Target Learners

### Target Learning Goals
