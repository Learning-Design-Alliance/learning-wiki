# Use randomised dual-presentation designs to address self-selection in quasi-experimental studies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article produced two distinct but closely related presentations (A and B) of equivalent complexity, randomised between simultaneous sessions so volunteers could not sort into a preferred presentation. This generated three groups (non-viewers, A viewers, B viewers) and enabled causal interpretation by using B viewers as a reference group for A, and vice versa, addressing self-selection bias from unobserved motivation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Presentations must cover topics of equivalent complexity and be randomised so participants cannot choose which they watch.
#### Constraints
- Voluntary participation is required where ethics approval prohibits mandating viewing, and self-selection must be explicitly modelled.

### Target Learners
- Tertiary education students

### Target Learning Goals
- Estimating causal effects of instructional innovations on knowledge acquisition
