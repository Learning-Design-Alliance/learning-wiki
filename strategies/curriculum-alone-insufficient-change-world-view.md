# Educators should not rely on an evidence-based curriculum alone to inoculate students against conservative attitudes; address students' world views

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because conservative and magical thinking persisted in an evidence-based program and declined only gradually, the authors argue that curriculum content by itself is insufficient. They state that "it would appear to be overly simplistic to think that providing an evidence-based curriculum alone will inoculate students against conservative attitudes and beliefs" and that changing a person's world view should be the basis for interventions by chiropractic educators.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions targeting students' underlying world view rather than knowledge transmission alone
#### Constraints
- The authors note world-view change is a much more difficult task; the study explains less than 30% of total variance, so other factors are at play; longitudinal designs would be needed to confirm trajectories

### Target Learners
- chiropractic students in evidence-based programs

### Target Learning Goals
- evidence-based clinical reasoning and scope-of-practice decisions
