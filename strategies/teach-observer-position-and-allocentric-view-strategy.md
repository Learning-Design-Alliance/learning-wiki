# Teach observer-position effects explicitly and show the allocentric perspective in the planetarium dome

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
From the null result on observer-position questions, the authors recommend that "If we want students to improve their understanding, we will have to teach specifically about this topic and will have to provide specific exercises", rather than relying on incidental coverage of latitude. They further argue, citing the literature, that "it seems to be essential that an allocentric perspective can also be shown during the presentation in the dome", since their geocentric-only presentation could not support perspective switching on this topic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Specific lesson time and exercises must be allocated to observer-position effects, beyond the latitude-Polaris practice already in the module
#### Constraints
- Time pressure on science teachers limited the original module to 80 minutes, which is why observer position was not treated separately

### Target Learners
- secondary school students aged 13-17

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding how the observer's latitude affects culmination height and rise/set positions of the Sun and stars
