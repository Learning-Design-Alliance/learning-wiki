# Design human-LLM collaborative systems in which LLMs assist diagnostic reasoning while human experts oversee complex decisions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's future-directions section recommends three advancement pathways, with the hybrid human-LLM design as the implementable teaching-and-practice strategy: LLMs assist in diagnostic reasoning while human experts retain oversight of complex, high-stakes decisions. The article states the goal of "reducing reliance on autonomous AI in high-stakes scenarios." The other named pathways are integrating computer vision for radiology images and electrocardiograms, and implementing real-time updates from sources such as the Chinese Medical Association's guidelines to address timeliness gaps.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- LLMs assist in diagnostic reasoning
- human experts oversee complex decisions
#### Constraints
- Current models have fixed training data cutoffs with no mechanism for real-time updates
- Current LLMs cannot directly interpret medical images

### Target Learners
- medical students in Chinese medical curricula
- practicing physicians in clinical training

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnostic reasoning
- clinical judgment under human oversight
