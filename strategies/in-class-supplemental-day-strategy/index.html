Strategies

Schedule the supplemental active session as part of the normal class week rather than out of class¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends embedding the extra active group-work day in the regular class schedule so students attend without conflicts. It argues that "Because SP is part of their normal class schedules, students will not have any class or work conflicts, which they may have with out-of-class sessions," eliminating one reason for not attending. The instructor should prepare a group of class assistants, making the SP day function as an office hour with the whole class and the worksheets act like a tutor.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A group of class assistants ready to help students with the material
  • a weekly slot in the class schedule devoted to the supplemental session

Constraints¶

  • The session only emphasizes material already covered during lecture days

Target Learners¶

  • college students in large lecture introductory mathematics courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving course success and lowering DFW rates

Claims¶

  • The experimental group closed the prior-knowledge gap: control started with significantly higher ACT scores, but post-ACT scores showed no significant difference [+W]
  • The middle prior-knowledge group benefited most from Supplemental Practice, significantly outperforming the middle control group on current points, test 3, final, quizzes, and course GPA [+W]
  • Historical correlational data showed students attending more Supplemental Practice days earned higher course averages and success rates above 65% at nine or more days [+W]
  • Students using worked-example worksheets in weekly Supplemental Practice sessions significantly outperformed control students on test 3 and the final exam in a large College Algebra course [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Schedule CSR within regular ELA and content-area instruction, spreading sessions across teachers in secondary settings
  • Integrate tutoring into the regular school day rather than scheduling it outside instructional time
