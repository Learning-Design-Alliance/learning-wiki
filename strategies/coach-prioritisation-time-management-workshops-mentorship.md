# Coach prioritisation, time management and self-care explicitly through workshops and mentorship programmes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that skills such as prioritisation, time management and self-care "must not only be made explicit to students but can be actively coached through workshops and mentorship programmes". This strategy makes the hidden knower-code demands of internship visible and developable during undergraduate training, rather than leaving graduates to acquire them after qualification.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Making the need for these skills explicit to students
- Workshop and mentorship programme structures
#### Constraints
- Students face competing pressures from high-stakes assessments that can take preference over such development

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- prioritisation and time management
- self-care and work-life balance
