# Use immersive VR with virtual agents and eye-tracking to isolate and manipulate specific social factors (gaze, feedback, embodiment, multimodality) in L2 learning experiments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends immersive head-mounted-display VR as a methodological bridge between ecological validity and experimental control for socially interactive L2 research. VR "allows for the full control of multimodal sensory stimulation (i.e., the onset, location, and duration of presentation of the stimuli)", lets researchers alter a virtual agent's speaking pace, accent, prosody, gaze and feedback valence while holding appearance constant, and enables precise measurement of eye, head, hand and body movements, gaze-following, facial emotion and physiological responses. The authors demonstrate feasibility with a gaze-manipulation word-learning study that would be very difficult to control with physically present actors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experiments requiring complex behaviors such as negotiating meaning or corrective feedback should still use human actors, confederates or naive participants, since reproducing human cues in virtual agents remains challenging.
#### Constraints
- VR cannot replace real human interactions; EEG during HMD use requires participants to remain seated with limited movement to avoid artifacts; and inter-brain synchrony findings may partly reflect low-level perceptual mechanisms.

### Target Learners
- Adult second language learners in laboratory and applied research settings

### Target Learning Goals
- L2 word encoding, lexical-semantic integration, willingness to communicate, and motivation in socially interactive contexts
