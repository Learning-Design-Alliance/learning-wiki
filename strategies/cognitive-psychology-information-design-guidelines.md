# Apply cognitive psychology and information design principles to future guideline development for time-critical emergencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating principles from cognitive psychology, human factors engineering, and information design into guideline development for time-critical neurological emergencies. Guideline format influences memorability, and future designs should apply cognitive psychology and information design principles aided by guideline evaluation tools such as the Reporting Tool for Practice Guidelines (RIGHT). Concise, user-friendly guidelines with visual aids and clear recommendations could improve adherence, especially for less-experienced clinicians.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Application of cognitive psychology and information design principles
- Use of guideline evaluation tools such as the RIGHT framework
#### Constraints
- Large language models may recommend key interventions inconsistently depending on prompting type, so human-centered guideline design remains important

### Target Learners
- clinicians managing time-critical neurological emergencies
- guideline developers

### Target Learning Goals
- improved guideline memorability and usability
- adherence to guideline-recommended interventions
