# Four facilitation strategies for safe intergroup discussions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes four facilitation aspects: attending to participants' emotions, monitoring participants' language, incorporating multiple modes of communication, and furthering the facilitators' own continued education. Facilitators intervened when hearing implicitly marginalizing comments, such as "I don't see race, I only see human beings," which the authors identify as an example of colorblind racism. A three-person team worked well: one facilitating, one taking notes, and a third attending to emotional issues.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A three-person facilitation team so one member can accompany participants who need to leave without disrupting the workshop
- Facilitators who are mandatory reporters with anti-harassment training, plus ongoing self-education between workshops
#### Constraints
- None of the facilitators is an expert in every form of marginalization or every aspect of intergroup discussion

### Target Learners
- Participants in intergroup discussions about race, gender, and other identities

### Target Learning Goals
- Participating productively and safely in discussions of marginalization
