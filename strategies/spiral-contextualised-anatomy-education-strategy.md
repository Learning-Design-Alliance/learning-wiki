# Reform anatomy education with a spiral curriculum, teaching in context, teaching for transfer and assessment for learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors' recommendations follow from students' perceptions: present anatomy topics iteratively with increasing detail (spiral curriculum), contextualise instruction by linking anatomy to clinical manifestations such as radiology or pathology, design explicitly for transfer, and adopt assessment for learning that rewards understanding and higher order cognitive skills. Students also asked for coached laboratory sessions guided by enthusiastic teachers who know students' prior knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires revisiting anatomical topics on more than two occasions across the curriculum, with less detail the first time and more detail added on each repetition.
- Requires controlled introduction of knowledge in the first loop of the spiral to prevent students being overwhelmed by details.
#### Constraints
- The recommendations rest on students' perceptions in one PBL curriculum; the article notes real patient contacts are too complicated to be embarked upon lightly and calls for further research on their impact on learning outcomes.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in a problem-based learning curriculum

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention and clinical application of anatomical knowledge
- Motivation to study anatomy
- Transfer of basic science knowledge to clinical practice
