# Conduct more research on augmented and virtual reality for anatomy learning before drawing overall conclusions about those techniques

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's forward-looking recommendation is that, because the included techniques varied greatly and the evidence base for the newer technologies is thin, "more research into augmented and virtual reality should be performed as the number of studies on those techniques was low." Educators and researchers should treat AR and VR findings as preliminary rather than settled, and prioritize well-designed comparison studies for these two categories, which together accounted for only six of the 21 included articles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- New studies should compare AR or VR tools against traditional methods using test results or student feedback, as the included studies did
#### Constraints
- The review found only two augmented reality and four virtual reality studies, which it states makes an overall conclusion of usefulness difficult

### Target Learners
- medical students learning anatomy

### Target Learning Goals
- anatomical knowledge and spatial understanding
