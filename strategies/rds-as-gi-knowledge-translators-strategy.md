# Position Registered Dietitians as key knowledge translators for GI education, supported by reviewed and co-developed education tools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that clinicians, specifically Registered Dietitians, be treated as the key agents facilitating GI knowledge translation from research to client application, and that their perceived barriers be studied and addressed. It argues clinicians and academics must work together to translate scientific jargon into audience-appropriate phraseology, and that new GI education tools be developed "with the input of clinicians and clients". Research on clinicians' and clients' perceptions, knowledge and application of GI is identified as still needed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reliable, evidence-based GI literature and education materials must be easily available to practitioners
- RDs should be included in the research process on GI education
#### Constraints
- The review notes a perceived deficiency in reliable GI education materials, and that existing tools may contain conceptual errors, e.g. a Canadian tool conflating glycaemic index with glycaemic response

### Target Learners
- Registered Dietitians
- clients living with diabetes

### Target Learning Goals
- applying the glycaemic index concept in clinical nutrition practice
- knowledge translation from research to client education
