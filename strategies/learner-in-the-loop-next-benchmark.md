# Pair a contamination-resistant static benchmark core with a learner-in-the-loop layer for next-generation education evaluation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that because static single-turn question answering reaches a construct-validity ceiling for measuring teaching, future education benchmarks should combine a fixed, contamination-resistant core with direct measurement of effects on learners. The authors state that "the next generation of benchmarks will likely pair a contamination-resistant static core like ELBench with a learner-in-the-loop layer", building on emerging interactive protocols such as simulated-student dialogue, adaptive student personas, and outcome-grounded scoring of learning gains, while acknowledging that simulated learners remain imperfect proxies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interactive or outcome-grounded measurement components, since a rubric applied once to a transcript scores the form of a pedagogical move but not its effect on a learner
#### Constraints
- Simulated learners remain imperfect proxies for real learners

### Target Learners
- Learners interacting with LLM tutors across K-12 and higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Teaching quality and pedagogical moves that produce measurable learning gains rather than only well-formed responses
