# Implement SST as pre-class self-study, a scenario simulation with role players and mannequin deterioration, debriefing, and post-class personalized feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable session recipe: distribute pre-class materials on critical illness assessment (ABC principle) and hypovolemic shock management with quizzes; group residents into three-person teams with a designated leader; run a 25-minute scenario in which family members and nurses advance the storyline while residents adjust treatment plans to condition changes; conduct instructor-led debriefing; then hold group summarization and instructor evaluation with detailed feedback on reflective reports.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors with at least a Master's degree, mid-level or higher title, and systematic SST training
- Teaching assistants familiar with the teaching objectives to play family members and nurses
- High-fidelity mannequins with vital signs controlled via an external monitoring system
#### Constraints
- The study delivered one one-hour session followed by weekly sessions for one month; benefits were assessed only two weeks post-course, so long-term retention is untested

### Target Learners
- second-year residents in emergency medicine rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- applying the ABC principle to identify early signs of shock
- executing emergency management of hypovolemic shock
- team leadership and task assignment under pressure
