Use multiple comprehensive teaching strategies rather than a single strategy to prepare nursing students for the NCLEX-RN¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's synthesis supports combining strategies — active learning (including unfolding case studies, concept mapping, game-based learning), faculty coaching and support, continuous NCLEX-RN-style question exposure, review courses and products, remediation/tutoring, and simulation — rather than relying on one approach. As the review quotes Blakeslee (2019): "nursing education programs are encouraged to develop multiple comprehensive teaching strategies to help nursing students improve their critical thinking skills." The review also recommends faculty use a variety of teaching strategies while focusing on student QOL.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Faculty commitment and unified attitudes supporting student success, as emphasized by Davis and Morrow (2021)

Constraints¶

  • The review found no consensus on a single best practice, and no Level I or II studies among the reviewed evidence

Target Learners¶

  • pre-licensure nursing students (BSN, ADN, diploma programs)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • NCLEX-RN first-attempt success
  • critical thinking
  • clinical judgement

Claims¶

  • Computer-adaptive quizzing software (PassPoint, PrepU) showed a statistically significant positive relationship with NCLEX-RN success (Pence & Wood, 2018) [+W]
  • Concept-based curriculum correlates with critical thinking, which predicts first-time NCLEX-RN pass probability (Harrison, 2018) [+W]
  • Failing two nursing courses is associated with twice the likelihood of failing the first NCLEX-RN attempt compared with failing one or none (Sanderson et al., 2022) [~W]
  • A 6-week NCLEX Coaching Model showed no significant first-time pass-rate improvement but significantly improved confidence/readiness scores (Schlairet & Rubenstein, 2019) [~W]
  • Scoping review of 25 studies finds multiple educational strategies for NCLEX-RN preparation with no consensus on a best practice, active learning being the most frequent [+W]
  • Active learning conditions more often include quizzes, homework, and learning/exam preparation than lecture conditions [+W]
