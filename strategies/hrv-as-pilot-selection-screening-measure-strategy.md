# Use resting HRV as an additional objective screening measure in pilot selection, measured in a low-stress context after existing screening stages

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend adding HRV measurement to existing multi-step pilot selection processes as an objective, rapid, evidence-based indicator of physiological resilience. They propose "using HRV in the screening phase after psychological screening and other physiological indicators to identify the candidates who have the physiological resilience to cope with stressors". The recommendation rests on HRV's significant prediction of course completion alongside, and independent of, the selection formula score.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- HRV must be measured in a state of low or no stress, out of the context of the selection procedures
- measurement approximately two months before training, consistent with the timing in this study
#### Constraints
- The study is historical prospective and correlational; follow-up prospective studies with multiple time points are needed before the predictive value across training stages is understood

### Target Learners
- military pilot course candidates
- candidates for operational positions in military and civilian settings

### Target Learning Goals
- improving accuracy of selection for demanding training programs

### Affordances
- Neurovisceral Theory Hrv Performance Under Stress
