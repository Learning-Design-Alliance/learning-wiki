# Relate Math to Student Strengths

## Description
Connecting math to students' personal strengths involves helping students understand how math is used in the real world in ways that align with their interests and expertise. For example, if students enjoy basketball, teachers can relate percentages to basketball statistics. This allows students to feel more confident with the content because they can bring something to the table that is valued, important, and relevant to them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need to be aware of students’ interests and strengths. They should be prepared to find or create examples and activities that connect math to these areas. This may require some creativity and flexibility.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging to find examples that resonate with all students. Requires a deep understanding of students’ interests and strengths.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Increase student confidence, make math more relevant, activate student expertise, improve engagement

### Affordances
- Increases student motivation and engagement by making math more relevant and meaningful. It allows students to see the value of math in their own lives and to connect it to their personal strengths and interests. It can also help students develop a more positive attitude toward math and increase their confidence in their abilities.

### Personalization
- Teachers can tailor examples and activities to match students’ specific interests and strengths. They can also encourage students to share their own examples of how math is used in their lives.

- Assess students’ engagement and motivation through observation and participation. Collect feedback from students on their perceptions of the relevance of math to their lives. Analyze student work to identify connections to their personal interests and strengths.

Students are more likely to retain information and apply it to new situations when they see the relevance and value of the content.

## Examples
- Relating percentages to the basketball statistics students often look at. Students might not be experts on how to calculate percents, but they could be experts on which NBA players have the highest shooting percentages. When students make that connection, their positionality changes. They’re the ones bringing something to the table that’s valued and important and relevant.
