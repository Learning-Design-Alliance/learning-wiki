# Call-and-Response

## Description
Call-and-response involves the instructor initiating a phrase or word, and the students responding with a pre-determined phrase or word. This technique serves as a cue for students to quiet down and refocus their attention. Examples include: Teacher: "Holy"; Students: "Macaroni"; Teacher: "1, 2, 3, eyes on me"; Students: "1, 2, eyes on you"; Teacher: "The only easy day"; Students: "Was yesterday."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires pre-planning and explicit teaching of the call-and-response phrases. Consistency in using the technique is essential for effectiveness.
#### Constraints
- May lose its effectiveness if overused or if students become disengaged with the chosen phrases. Requires the teacher to be proactive in initiating the call-and-response.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe how quickly and consistently students respond to the call-and-response. Monitor the noise level in the classroom before and after using the technique.

## Impact
- Classroom management: Provides a quick and effective way to quiet a noisy class and regain student attention without resorting to negative reinforcement or disciplinary action.
