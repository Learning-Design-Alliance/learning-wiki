# Respect Diverse Talents and Ways of Learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
One of Chickering and Gamson's (1987) Seven Principles: learners bring different talents, prior experience, and preferred modes of engagement, and instruction should offer multiple pathways into the same content. In practice this means varying representation (text, visual, demonstration), expression (written, oral, applied), and engagement (individual, collaborative, authentic problems) so that learners can display and extend their strengths rather than being penalized for a single mismatched format. Crucially, this is not the same as matching instruction to a "learning style" — a hypothesis with no supporting evidence.

## Design Implications

Offering multiple representations and modes of expression broadens access and gives learners more retrieval routes into the same material [~M]. The benefit comes from *multiple and varied encounters* with content — dual coding, varied practice contexts, and multiple examples — not from diagnosing individual styles and tailoring instruction to them [Learning styles matching yields no measurable learning benefit.](../claims/learning-styles-matching-yields-no-benefit.md) [X]. Varied formats also manage cognitive load when each representation is integrated rather than redundant [Multiple representations improve learning when integrated and explained.](../claims/multiple-representations-improve-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content designed so the same objective can be reached through more than one modality or task type
- Assessment options that allow different demonstrations of competence (e.g., [Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md), [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md))
- Tutor or instructor flexibility: diagnosing where a learner struggles and switching strategy rather than repeating the same explanation louder

#### Constraints
- Matching instruction to self-reported learning styles does not improve outcomes and wastes design effort [Learning styles matching yields no measurable learning benefit.](../claims/learning-styles-matching-yields-no-benefit.md) [X]
- Excessive format variety can fragment attention or add extraneous load for novices [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-M]
- Learners often prefer formats in which they feel fluent but learn less from them; preference is a poor guide to effectiveness [Learning styles matching yields no measurable learning benefit.](../claims/learning-styles-matching-yields-no-benefit.md) [-S]
- Full multi-pathway design is costly; prioritize variety at high-stakes or high-difficulty points rather than everywhere

#### Implementation Variability
- **Representation variety**: pair text with diagrams, worked demonstrations, or [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) linking to prior experience
- **Expression variety**: allow learners to show understanding via writing, [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) demonstration, [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) analysis, or teaching back
- **Engagement variety**: alternate [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), individual work, and hands-on application so classroom-dominant learners and practice-dominant learners each get stretch opportunities
- **Remediation paths**: supply extra background material when prerequisite knowledge is missing rather than assuming a uniform starting point

### Target Learners
- Higher-education and adult learners whose strengths lie outside the default lecture-and-essay format
- Learners with uneven profiles — strong practical experience but weaker academic-theoretical fluency, or vice versa
- Learners at risk of disengagement when a single instructional mode repeatedly fails to connect; inclusive multi-pathway design supports belonging [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding reached through multiple encodings of the same idea
- Transfer: varied contexts and formats prepare learners to apply knowledge flexibly
- Metacognitive awareness of one's own strengths and gaps — learners should be pushed toward modes that do not come easily, not confined to comfortable ones

### Instructions
1. Identify the core objective and list at least two genuinely different ways to represent and practice it (e.g., [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md), worked demonstration, applied problem).
2. Diagnose the learner's entry point — background knowledge, confidence, preferred mode — using a brief [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md).
3. Offer the learner a primary pathway aligned with their current strength, then require a second pass through a less comfortable mode to stretch their repertoire.
4. [Provide guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md) during the stretch mode, since learners operating outside their strength need more scaffolding, not less.
5. [Provide feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) on the stretch attempt, naming the gain ("explaining it aloud exposed the gap the written version hid").
6. Assess via multiple demonstration options so competence, not format fluency, is what is measured.

## Examples
- A statistics tutor lets a learner who struggles with formulas first solve a real-world estimation problem hands-on, then maps the solution back onto the formal notation — the practical route becomes the bridge to the theoretical one.
- **[CAST UDL Guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org)** — the framework operationalizing this principle as multiple means of engagement, representation, and action/expression across K–12 and higher education.
- A writing center offers learners the choice to plan an essay via outline, concept map, or recorded oral brainstorm before drafting, then pushes each to draft in standard academic prose regardless of planning mode.
