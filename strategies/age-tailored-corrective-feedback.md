# Tailor corrective feedback to children's age: use spoken, salient, scaffolded feedback for younger children and written, paper-based feedback only from age 8

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's tentative practice recommendation is that effective feedback for children ages 3-11 goes beyond verification to include the correct answer, is spoken or simple-visual, and is given immediately after each item. For younger children (3-7), content should be supplemented with prompts or explanations and made perceptually salient with audio or physical components; written and paper-based feedback should be reserved for children ages eight and older.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback should be contingent on the child's response to a target task and provided by an instructor or instructional device
#### Constraints
- Strong tailoring recommendations are difficult because the literature is disconnected and some features were never studied in older children

### Target Learners
- children ages 3-11 in preschool through Grade 6

### Target Learning Goals
- improving literacy, mathematics, and problem-solving performance
