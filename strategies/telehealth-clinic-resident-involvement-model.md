# Keep residents involved in telehealth clinics through a staged model of trainee history-taking, attending discussion, joint patient video conference, and post-visit debrief

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
With elective clinics canceled, the article proposes redirecting residents into telehealth encounters using the same video conference software. The model "involves trainees making the initial contact with patients," gathering a comprehensive history and formulating a plan, then discussing the case with the attending surgeon by phone, conducting a multiuser video conference with the patient for final counseling, and debriefing after the patient signs off. The authors present this as an evolving but worthwhile effort to maintain clinic-based education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online video conference software shared by trainee, attending, and patient
- Trainee initial patient contact and history-gathering before the joint encounter
- A post-encounter discussion between attending and trainee to finalize the encounter
#### Constraints
- Patients with limited internet access and technologic capabilities
- Difficulty establishing rapport without in-person encounters
- Lack of a physical exam
- Ambiguity about information security

### Target Learners
- surgical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical evaluation and patient counseling skills
- maintaining clinic-based learning exposure during canceled elective clinics
