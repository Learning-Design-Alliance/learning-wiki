# Design in-class activities so students struggle with a problem before the mini-lecture that shows how an expert organizes it

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy reverses the traditional explain-then-practice order: first give students an example that challenges them to discover the key idea themselves, then give a mini-lecture showing how an expert would organize the problem. The author cites research that learning is "greatly enhanced, often by factors of two or three" when students struggle before being told the solution, and that concepts learned this way transfer better to novel contexts. The practical recipe: decide which topics warrant a mini-lecture, then design a student activity leading into each one.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identify the two or three key ideas each class is built around and design two or three activities around the same ideas
- Activities must let students try out ideas with the instructor present for immediate feedback
