# Use research-based learning tools on Dirac notation, including the guided-inquiry QuILT, reflective problems, and peer concept tests

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the difficulty research, the authors recommend research-based learning tools to help students develop a good grasp of Dirac notation. The QuILT "employs a guided inquiry-based approach and is designed to help students build a good knowledge structure," usable as an in-class tutorial with students working in small groups of two or three, or as a self-study tool for underprepared graduate students. Reflective problems can serve as a homework supplement in undergraduate courses, and concept tests can be integrated with lectures so students learn from peers' expertise.
