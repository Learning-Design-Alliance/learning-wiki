# Implement a bundle of health improvement activities to improve attendance and attainment at age 14 in low-income schools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implications section recommends that, consistent with the HPS and WSCC frameworks, “the present study suggests effective health improvement activities to improve attendance and attainment at age 14 among low‐income schools are: Physical health in the curriculum,” followed by written school health policies, student involvement in policy development, parental involvement across a variety of areas and mechanisms, partnerships beyond statutory requirements, and senior-management prioritisation with written action plans. It also recommends delivering physical health education “in a variety of formats and subject areas, as opposed to delivery solely in separate health education classes.”

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Senior management prioritisation of health and well-being, including production and annual review of written action plans
- Mechanisms for student and parental involvement, including practical support such as transport for engagement
#### Constraints
- The supporting associations were observed at KS3 (age 14) but not KS4, and among high FSM rather than low FSM schools in the regression models

### Target Learners
- secondary school students, particularly in schools with high Free School Meal entitlement

### Target Learning Goals
- attendance and Key Stage 3 academic attainment
