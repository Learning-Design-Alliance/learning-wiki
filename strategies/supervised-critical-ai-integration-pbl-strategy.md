# Integrate generative AI into PBL under educator supervision, teaching students to formulate clinical questions and critically evaluate AI responses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators use AI technologies as supportive methods within PBL rather than avoiding them: AI can support case analysis, patient analysis, and reviewing treatment options, treating the AI system "as a patient, asking questions and getting answers from it" to create real-time clinical scenarios. Rather than fearing misuse, educators should teach students to engage with the tools while "guiding them to formulate appropriate clinical questions and critically evaluate responses to identify accurate treatment solutions". The strategy embeds AI interaction within supervised, small-group discussion so students question, reflect on, and validate AI-generated content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educator supervision and guidance during AI interactions
- A structured orientation to the AI tool including prompt formulation and a competency check
- Small subgroups that collaboratively evaluate AI-generated content
#### Constraints
- AI cannot be the only source of education and cannot substitute for the expertise and guidance of professional academic instructors
- Technological systems may lead to negative outcomes such as technology addiction, avoidance of studying, and academic dishonesty, so ethical guidelines must be clearly defined

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physiotherapy students
- Health professions students

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning and clinical decision-making
- AI self-efficacy and critical evaluation of AI-generated medical information
