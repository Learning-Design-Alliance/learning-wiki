# Train interviewers and use more structured interviewing, particularly the MMI, to improve interviewer reliability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 associational, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that researchers and admissions bodies consider interviewer training and more structured interview formats, particularly the multiple mini-interview (MMI), as ways to address the low reliability and bias concerns long associated with admissions interviews. The review states: "researchers should consider training interviewers and using more structured interviewing—particularly the MMI—to improve interviewer reliability." This recommendation is grounded in the review's finding that interview impact depends heavily on each school's interviewing approach.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interview format and question development, grading and weighting practices should be delineated so the effect of structure can be evaluated
#### Constraints
- The review notes the evidence on the utility of the interview in medical school admissions remains limited, and its findings come from nonrandomized observational studies

- improved interviewer reliability in admissions assessment
