# Design e-learning with user-friendly responsive software, concise modules, and interactive clinical-vignette assessment throughout

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the thematic analysis the author derives three design recommendations for future e-learning courses: prioritise "user friendly and responsive software" (HTML5/H5P rather than older Flash technologies, single-page navigation, saved progress); keep modules concise with short, to-the-point sentences and minimised overall length; and ensure interactive elements throughout with content relevant to practice, for which "clinical vignettes are recommended". These recommendations come directly from participant feedback themes of ease of use, succinctness and interactivity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Modules should contain interactive elements throughout
- Content should be relevant to practice
#### Constraints
- The author notes time taken to complete the course was not tracked, so the under-30-minute perception rests on participant responses

### Target Learners
- medical students
- clinical study investigators in international collaborative studies

### Target Learning Goals
- protocol implementation knowledge
- applying eligibility criteria and classification systems in clinical practice
