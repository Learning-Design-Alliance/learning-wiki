# Math Anxiety Reduction

## Description
Math anxiety — feelings of tension and dread that interfere with manipulating numbers and solving mathematical problems — consumes working memory resources that would otherwise support reasoning [Math anxiety intrudes on the very working memory resources arithmetic depends on.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.130.2.224) [+S]. Reduction strategies operate on two fronts: lowering the threat in the learning environment (timed tests, public error, fixed-ability messaging) and building learners' confidence and regulation skills (reappraisal, expressive writing, mastery-oriented practice). Anxiety and achievement are reciprocally related — poor performance breeds anxiety, and anxiety degrades performance — so instructional design must break the cycle from both directions [Math anxiety and math achievement are negatively correlated and mutually reinforcing over time.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.108.1.80) [+S].

## Design Implications

Because anxious learners' working memory is partially occupied by worry, reducing extraneous load and threat is a precondition for effective math instruction, not an add-on [Cognitive overload degrades learning, and anxious learners reach that threshold sooner.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+M]. The highest-leverage design moves are environmental: removing timed pressure, normalizing errors, and framing ability as growable. Brief regulatory interventions (expressive writing before assessment, reappraisal of arousal) produce measurable gains for anxious students specifically [Brief expressive writing before high-stakes exams improves performance for anxious students.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1198424) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment and grading practices that do not rely on speed or single high-stakes performances
- Norms that treat errors as information — e.g., [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) and low-stakes [Practice](../elements/practice.md) with immediate, private [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md)
- Teacher messaging that avoids transmitting math negativity; teachers' own math anxiety predicts their students' anxiety, especially in early grades [Teachers' math anxiety transmits to students and depresses their achievement growth.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0910967107) [+S]
- Explicit instruction in regulation strategies (breathing, reappraisal, expressive writing) offered before, not during, high-pressure tasks

#### Constraints
- Timed testing reliably depresses performance for high-anxiety learners even when they know the material [Math anxiety intrudes on the very working memory resources arithmetic depends on.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.130.2.224) [-S]
- "Comfort-first" designs that reduce challenge below productive difficulty do not build competence, and competence is the strongest long-run buffer against anxiety [Math anxiety and math achievement are negatively correlated and mutually reinforcing over time.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.108.1.80) [~S]
- One-off motivational sessions without follow-through show fading effects; anxiety reasserts itself when classroom conditions are unchanged [Math anxiety and math achievement are negatively correlated and mutually reinforcing over time.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.108.1.80) [-W]
- Reappraisal and writing interventions help anxious students but can be neutral or slightly harmful for low-anxiety students [Brief expressive writing before high-stakes exams improves performance for anxious students.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1198424) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Environmental redesign (untimed practice, error-normalizing routines) vs. learner-facing regulation training (expressive writing, arousal reappraisal) — most effective programs combine both
- One-to-one cognitive tutoring reduces math anxiety in a single school year, with anxiety-related brain circuitry changes paralleling achievement gains [Individualized tutoring reduces math anxiety alongside achievement gains.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797615570262) [+M]
- Early-grade interventions (number-sense fluency) prevent anxiety from forming; later interventions focus on regulation and reframing

### Target Learners
- High-anxiety learners, who show the largest gains from both environmental and regulation interventions [Brief expressive writing before high-stakes exams improves performance for anxious students.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1198424) [+M]
- Students with high working memory capacity, who lose the most under pressure because they rely on demanding strategies that worry disrupts [Math anxiety intrudes on the very working memory resources arithmetic depends on.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.130.2.224) [+M]
- Young children of math-anxious teachers, who benefit most when the teacher receives support for their own anxiety [Teachers' math anxiety transmits to students and depresses their achievement growth.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0910967107) [+M]
- Low-anxiety students need less of this; over-scaffolding can waste their time [Brief expressive writing before high-stakes exams improves performance for anxious students.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1198424) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency and problem solving under assessment conditions
- Persistence and course-taking in mathematics (anxiety predicts avoidance of STEM pathways)
- Self-regulation: managing arousal and self-talk during demanding cognitive tasks

### Instructions
1. Audit the environment for threat: eliminate timed drills as assessment, replace public board-solving with private or voluntary response, and establish error-normalizing routines ([Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md), "my favorite mistake" discussions).
2. Build fluency through low-stakes, untimed [Practice](../elements/practice.md) with immediate feedback, so competence — the strongest anxiety buffer — grows without pressure [Math anxiety and math achievement are negatively correlated and mutually reinforcing over time.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.108.1.80) [+S].
3. Teach a regulation routine before high-stakes events: 10 minutes of expressive writing about worries, or reappraisal framing ("arousal is readiness"), introduced well before the assessment [Brief expressive writing before high-stakes exams improves performance for anxious students.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1198424) [+M].
4. Reduce unnecessary load in task design — [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) complex procedures, use worked examples for novices — so anxious learners are not pushed past their working-memory ceiling [Cognitive overload degrades learning, and anxious learners reach that threshold sooner.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+M].
5. Support teachers' own math attitudes; provide [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md) and professional development where teacher math anxiety is high [Teachers' math anxiety transmits to students and depresses their achievement growth.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0910967107) [+S].
6. Foster [Belonging](../elements/belonging.md) — social-belonging interventions improve outcomes for students who fear confirming negative stereotypes about math ability [Belonging interventions improve outcomes for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+S].

## Examples
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — mastery-based, untimed practice with hints and unlimited retries removes speed pressure and public failure from math practice.
- **[YouCubed](https://www.youcubed.org)** (Stanford, Jo Boaler) — published classroom tasks and teacher resources built around error-normalizing norms and multidimensional math lessons designed to reduce math anxiety.
- **Ramirez & Beilock's expressive writing protocol** — a 10-minute writing exercise administered before exams, replicated across science and math courses [Brief expressive writing before high-stakes exams improves performance for anxious students.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1198424).
- **[Bedtime Math](https://bedtimemath.org)** — the app-based reading-and-math routine reduced math anxiety growth in children of math-anxious parents [Parents' math anxiety transmits to children through homework help; a math app intervention blocks it.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aac7427) [+S].
