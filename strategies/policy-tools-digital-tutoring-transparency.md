# State and local agencies should use policy tools to ensure transparency and quality of digital tutoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on findings that provider descriptions of digital tutoring vary considerably and that publicly available information can be "diluted and misleading," the authors call for policy tools at the state and local level to ensure greater transparency and continuous improvement of digital tutoring quality and accessibility. They identify priority directions for research aimed at improving digital tutoring models, noting that districts face difficulty monitoring and assessing the wide range of emerging digital tutoring approaches and formats.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- State and local educational agencies with authority over provider approval and monitoring
#### Constraints
- Provider information is partly self-reported, complicating accurate assessment

### Target Learners
- low-income K–12 students eligible for OST tutoring

### Target Learning Goals
- reading and mathematics achievement
