# Clinically-relevant side-effect selection criteria plus a student-driven side-effect research exercise for self-directed learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because remembering all side effects is improbable, the curriculum presents only clinically relevant ones, defined by explicit criteria: "side effects that occurred in more than 25% of the population", bothersome effects causing non-compliance, class-unique effects, and deadly or black-box warnings. Students then complete an interactive exercise researching side effects in reputable resources, cross-referencing them, and determining patient impact. The authors state "This exercise is perfect for self‐directed learning (SDL)" and are investigating SDL modules for other disciplines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to reputable, cross-referenceable drug information resources
#### Constraints
- Designed for medication side-effect knowledge within case-based pharmacology sessions

### Target Learners
- Pre-clerkship medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowing where to find and how to interpret drug-safety information and its impact on patient care
