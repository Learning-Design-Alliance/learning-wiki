# Individualize metaphor choice to the patient's presentation, history, and lived experience when explaining ACEs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose incorporating metaphors into the clinical toolkit, used when individualized to patient presentation and history. Because no single metaphor was superior and effectiveness depended on matching the patient's concerns with the metaphor type, clinicians should select metaphors the patient can relate to, avoid medically anchored metaphors when they do not fit lived experience, and use a preface rather than forcing the metaphor into conversation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Practice with and thoughtful selection of a fitting metaphor; basic communication skills remain necessary
#### Constraints
- The sunburn metaphor may not be culturally sensitive to those with a darker complexion; the dial metaphor was less effective for alcohol use disorder than chronic pain

### Target Learners
- clinicians and health care trainees discussing ACEs with adult patients

### Target Learning Goals
- trauma-informed explanation of ACEs and their health effects

### Affordances
- Metaphor Trauma Inquiry Framework
