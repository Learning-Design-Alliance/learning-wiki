# Instructors and TAs should avoid propagating gender stereotypes and message that physics success comes from effort and deliberate practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using its findings in professional development for physics instructors and TAs to make classes more inclusive. Because agreeing with the gender stereotype was correlated with decreased performance for female students, "TAs and instructors need to be careful to not propagate these types of stereotypes, both in their actions and statements", and should send the message that all students can excel through effort and appropriate learning strategies rather than innate traits such as gender.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Professional development workshops for instructors and TAs that focus on stereotype threat findings and help instructors reflect on encouraging a growth mindset
#### Constraints
- The correlational link between stereotype agreement and female performance is observational; the article notes it is unclear why the effect is stronger in calculus-based courses

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students, particularly female students

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing stereotype threat and the gender gap in physics conceptual assessment performance
