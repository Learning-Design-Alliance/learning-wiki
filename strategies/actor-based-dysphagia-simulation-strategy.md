# Replace or supplement mannequins with coached actors and multidisciplinary role play to increase realism

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (3 qualitative, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
To address mannequin limitations, the authors recommend using real actors who can be coached to display realistic responses such as facial expression or drooling, and incorporating simulations into a peer learning model where senior students facilitate sessions for junior years. Students also recommended that scenarios "include other aspects such as linguistic, cognitive or cultural factors that would mimic real-life" and include multidisciplinary team members such as nurses. The strategy responds directly to student feedback about the simulation focusing on one condition in isolation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Actors must be coached to give specific real-life responses such as facial expression or drooling that a mannequin cannot produce
#### Constraints
- SLEs can be resource and time-intensive and are not feasible for many institutions, particularly in low-middle income countries

### Target Learners
- undergraduate speech-language therapy students

### Target Learning Goals
- holistic patient assessment
- multidisciplinary team communication
- realistic clinical interaction
