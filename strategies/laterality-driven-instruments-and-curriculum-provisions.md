# Adopt laterality-driven provisions: dedicated left-handed instruments, curriculum content, and teaching materials for left-handed trainees

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose concrete accommodations: adopting "laterality driven instruments in undergraduate medical curriculum", adding a laterality-related chapter or SOP manual to surgical textbooks, and creating "A dedicated website for LH medical student's teaching and learning" with LH training videos. These aim to raise laterality awareness among students and faculty and reduce training complexity for LH learners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Availability or development of dedicated LH instruments and pedagogical materials, which the article notes currently do not exist
#### Constraints
- The article notes no pedagogical material is currently available to teach and train LH undergraduate medical students; benefits are suggested, not demonstrated

### Target Learners
- left-handed undergraduate medical students
- medical students generally

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical and clinical psychomotor skills

## Claims

- [Left-handed students scored significantly lower than right-handed students on instrument handling in intravenous cannulation, the only statistically significant laterality difference found](../claims/cannulation-handling-significant-lh-disadvantage.md) [+W]
- [Only 3 of 27 left-handed students (11.12%) voiced laterality concerns or requested instrument changes during training](../claims/few-lh-students-express-laterality-concerns.md) [~W]
- [On laparoscopic simulator tasks, left- and right-handed students performed comparably, with shorter peg-transfer and clipping times and longer cutting times among left-handers, all statistically insignificant](../claims/laparoscopic-skills-laterality-comparable.md) [~W]
- [No significant difference in overall psychomotor skill performance between left-handed and right-handed first-year medical students after identical training in suturing, cannulation and urinary catheterization](../claims/lh-rh-medical-students-no-psychomotor-difference.md) [-W]
