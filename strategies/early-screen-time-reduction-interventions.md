# Develop and test early interventions to reduce screen time exposure to enhance academic achievement in elementary school

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's stated recommendation is that "early interventions to reduce screen time exposure should be developed and tested to promote healthy screen use habits and enhance academic achievement in elementary school." It argues intervention should occur early because screen behavior patterns are established during the early years and academic interventions are more effective when implemented early, and it recommends collaboration among health care professionals, schools, families, and policymakers. The strategy targets young children before formal reading and math instruction consolidates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be developed and empirically tested; current evidence is observational, so causality cannot be definitively assessed.
- Recommendations should be tailored to recognize that different types of screen time may have varying effects on male and female children.
#### Constraints
- This is a recommendation, not a tested intervention result; the article did not manipulate screen time.
- The study population was predominantly urban children from the greater Toronto area, skewed toward higher socioeconomic status, limiting generalizability.

### Target Learners
- Young children (early childhood to elementary school, grades 3 and 6)

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading and math achievement on standardized elementary school tests
