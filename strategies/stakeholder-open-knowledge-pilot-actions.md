## Description
The article's call-to-action assigns each stakeholder group concrete first steps toward modular, machine-readable versions of record. Researchers 'can start small pilots to share and reuse research con- tributions in modular, reusable forms within their communities'; infrastructure providers should build a federated knowledge commons encoding contributions as citable, traceable objects; evaluators, funders, and publishers each receive matching actions to align incentives and validation. Implementing these in small, well-defined communities is framed as a necessary first step before scaling.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Shared, machine-readable building blocks agreed by communities; contributions remain citable, versioned, and machine-readable
#### Constraints
- The article states scaling to larger communities will introduce substantial challenges around interoperability and governance

### Target Learners
- Researchers, evaluators, funders, publishers, and infrastructure providers

### Target Learning Goals
- Enabling learning from research contributions earlier in the research lifecycle through structured sharing and community validation
