Teachers apply project-based learning to other mathematics topics, optionally combined with technology-assisted learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on their finding that PBL was effective in statistical learning, the authors recommend that "teachers apply PBL on other mathematics topics so students can be enthusiastic in mathematics learning in the class". They further suggest that "teachers can use PBL with technology -assisted learning to make learning more interesting for students". They also propose future experimental studies comparing individual work with group work and varying the technologies used.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must create an active student learning atmosphere by having students construct, locate, and develop their knowledge

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is extrapolated from a single statistics topic in one school; the article itself calls for repetition with different topics

Target Learners¶

  • lower secondary mathematics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mathematics learning engagement and enthusiasm across topics beyond statistics

Claims¶

  • Seventh-grade students taught statistics through project-based learning achieved higher learning outcomes than students taught with conventional learning [+W]
  • In the PBL class all students reached the minimum standard score on statistics outcomes, while fewer than half did in the conventional class [+W]
  • Student responses to PBL statistics learning were positive, and more positive than responses to conventional learning [+W]
  • Students in the PBL statistics class were active at every observed interval, while some conventional-class students engaged in irrelevant activities [+W]
  • Students' initial ability influenced their statistics learning outcomes in both the experimental and control classes [~W]
