# Two-part halving revision assignment to force content reorganization

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy asks students to write an essay of a well-defined length, then immediately, without feedback, revise it to half its original length. The article states "This drastic change ensures that simply cutting extraneous words cannot reach the word limit," forcing reorganization rather than revision by excision. Students submit electronically with track changes enabled so revisions are visible. The authors found the draconian shortening necessary to impress upon students the need to reorganize their essays.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Electronic submission with track-changes enabled so inserted and deleted text can be compared
- A length reduction drastic enough that word deletion alone cannot reach the limit
#### Constraints
- The article reports students did not learn to self-revise in first drafts without the forced paired assignment

### Target Learners
- undergraduate non-science majors in introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- content understanding through writing
- coherent essay organization
- reflective revision

## Claims

- [Students did not show evidence of self-revising when asked to write short first drafts without a forced paired revision assignment](../claims/no-self-revision-without-forced-paired-assignment.md) [+W]
