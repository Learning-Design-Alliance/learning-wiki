# Improve LLM grading alignment through LLM-optimized guidelines, calibration examples, and median-of-parallels grading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends concrete steps for future LLM-assisted grading: writing grading guidelines that are easier for LLMs to comprehend with no prior course knowledge, converting qualitative qualities into observable evidence requirements, asking the LLM to cite evidence as short quotes, calibrating with artificial top-, middle-, and low-grade examples, and grading in three rotated parallels to take a reasonable median. It states that "Three parallels of each assessment could ensure a reasonable median."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Grading guidelines rewritten for LLM comprehension with no prior course knowledge
- A few example top-, middle-, and low-grade submissions for LLM calibration
- Multiple grading parallels with rotated batch positioning
#### Constraints
- One prior study reported a minimal effect of nuanced prompt optimization on essay-style grading, suggesting strong reliance on generic characteristics
- The study's guidelines were not written with LLMs in mind and lacked detailed grade mapping or rubrics

### Target Learners
- master's-level public health and health sciences students

### Target Learning Goals
- improved alignment of LLM-suggested grades with human examiner grades on essay-style exams
