# Use AR as targeted early-training scaffolding with progressive withdrawal as competency develops

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the expertise reversal pattern, the review recommends deploying AR-assisted training during the early stages of procedural learning and progressively withdrawing AR guidance as competency develops, rather than using AR as a permanent scaffold. The review states AR "appears to deliver its greatest benefit during the early, high-error phase of skill acquisition, with returns diminishing as experience accumulates". This strategy is described as consistent with the scaffolding framework in educational theory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- AR guidance should be matched to trainee proficiency level, with withdrawal as competency develops
#### Constraints
- The evidence base has small samples (range 8-60 participants), no long-term retention or transfer data, and heterogeneous platforms and outcome measures

### Target Learners
- surgical novices: medical students and early-stage residents

### Target Learning Goals
- early-phase procedural skill acquisition
- error reduction during initial learning
