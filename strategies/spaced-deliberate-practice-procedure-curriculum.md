# Deliver procedural education through spaced simulation with deliberate practice: assisted step-by-step practice, an unassisted evaluation, and return sessions at 3–6 months

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The curriculum schedules individual 1-hour simulation sessions in which a learner first performs the paracentesis step by step with instructor assistance and real-time feedback, then immediately performs an unassisted evaluation scored with the PCAT, and returns 3–6 months later for a timed unassisted paracentesis with no prior demonstration. The authors "create an environment for deliberate practice by offering learners a goal-oriented time to practice with repetition as well as direct and immediate feedback on performance by the instructors", and deliberately space didactic cases several weeks after simulation to exploit the spacing effect for long-term retention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty case instructors complete a training session before simulating
- Learners who miss the Minimum Passing Standard return for a remediation session
- Small-group formats need 90–120 minutes with each learner performing individually
#### Constraints
- The optimal retraining interval is unclear; the authors state they do not have return scores and chose 3–6 month intervals as one approach, offering the framework as a template rather than a required sequencing

### Target Learners
- Internal medicine and medicine-pediatric residents of all training years

### Target Learning Goals
- Psychomotor procedural skill in paracentesis
- Retention and maintenance of procedural skill over months
- Incorporation of feedback to improve performance
