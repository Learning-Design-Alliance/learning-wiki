# Cultivate positive perceptions of pharmacology through case-based, patient-centered teaching and active learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educators "actively cultivate positive perceptions of pharmacology through targeted teaching strategies, such as case-based learning and real-world clinical applications", embedding pharmacology in practical, patient-centered scenarios to strengthen students' belief in its relevance. They also recommend active learning methods, self-reflection, and continuous formative assessments to encourage deep learning strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curriculum and assessment redesign that emphasizes application over recall; faculty training moving beyond lecture-based approaches
#### Constraints
- The effectiveness of these pedagogical interventions is, per the authors, underexplored; the supporting correlations are cross-sectional

### Target Learners
- undergraduate pharmacy students

### Target Learning Goals
- deep learning of pharmacological concepts, clinical application, and academic achievement
