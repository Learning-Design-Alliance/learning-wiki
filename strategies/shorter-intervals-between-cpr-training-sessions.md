# Reevaluate and shorten the time intervals between pediatric CPR training sessions to optimize retention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because simulated pediatric CPR performance deteriorated after a 5-6-week washout in both training arms, the authors recommend that "the time intervals between training sessions should be reevaluated to optimize retention". The strategy is to schedule booster or refresher simulation sessions at shorter intervals than the 5-6 weeks studied, since both RCDP and PSD trained performance that decayed. The article cites consistent evidence that monthly training outperformed training every 3, 6, or 12 months.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to simulation equipment and instructors for repeated booster sessions
#### Constraints
- The optimal interval was not determined in this study; the authors call for future studies at 3, 6, and 12 month intervals

### Target Learners
- pediatric residents

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of pediatric CPR knowledge and skills
