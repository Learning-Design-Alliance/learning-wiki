# Use routine electronic logbook supervision data plus competency assessments to evidence entrustment across a training program

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends analyzing routinely collected operative experience data beyond simple counting: assessing supervision-level progression and formal competency assessment outcomes over the whole course of training to demonstrate increasing trainer entrustment. It states "Other countries utilizing electronic logbooks could adopt similar techniques to further understanding of competency attainment amongst their surgical trainees," noting the method could be "applied internationally to training schemes which electronically record operative experience data."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires electronic logbooks recording procedure type and supervision level, plus a competency assessment instrument comparable to the PBA
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that logbook data alone limit conclusions about entrustment decisions because ad hoc daily supervision decisions are influenced by time of day, staff availability, and urgency

### Target Learners
- Surgical specialty trainees in any scheme with electronic operative logbooks

### Target Learning Goals
- Monitoring competency attainment and changing trainee autonomy
