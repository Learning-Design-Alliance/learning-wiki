Explicit mathematics instruction with challenging questions and mathematical discourse¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes EMI as a systematic approach with clear statements of purpose, clear explanations and demonstrations, and supported practice with feedback until independent mastery. Many EMI teachers also proceed to "a series of challenging questions resulting in active mathematical discourse with students", a practice the author notes is compatible with social constructivism. This combined approach served as the control condition in the experiment.

Requirements¶

  • Clear statements about the purpose of learning
  • Clear explanations and demonstrations of the instructional target

Constraints¶

  • It has been argued that EMI sometimes only increases procedural knowledge and not deep knowledge

Target Learners¶

  • university mathematics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • procedural mastery and conceptual understanding of mathematics

Claims¶

  • The constructive method produced better mean performance than the explicit method when assessed with grey numbers [-W]
  • The constructive teaching method produced better quality performance than the explicit method in a university mathematics classroom experiment [-W]
  • The constructive method benefited higher-performing students more, while the explicit group had a lower failure percentage [~W]
