# Use an active foreign-language control condition when testing auditory-cognitive training efficacy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article addresses methodological shortcomings of prior auditory-cognitive training studies — lacking active control groups, non-naturalistic outcomes, and untrained transfer tasks — by assigning the control group to computer-based Duolingo foreign language training, chosen because prior work showed it to be "cognitively engaging yet orthogonal to the auditory and attentional mechanisms targeted" by the experimental training. Both groups trained at least 20 minutes a day, five days a week for four weeks. The article presents this design as offering stronger evidence for real-world applicability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A control activity matched in time-on-task and engagement but orthogonal to the targeted mechanisms.
#### Constraints
- Control-group training adherence was self-reported in paper-based learning diaries, which the authors list as a limitation.

### Target Learners
- older adults with hearing loss participating in auditory rehabilitation research

### Target Learning Goals
- valid estimation of auditory-cognitive training effects on speech-in-noise comprehension
