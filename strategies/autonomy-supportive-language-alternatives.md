# Replace controlling language with relevance demonstrations and opportunities for pupil opinion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete classroom alternative to psychological control drawn from the article's applied discussion: rather than compelling pupils, teachers should show why activities matter and invite pupil voice. The article advises that "Teachers should not force pupils to do activities, but demonstrate the relevance of learning activities, and provide the opportunity for pupils to give their opinion without using controlling language". It names phrases such as "you must" or "have to" as controlling language to avoid.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be willing to relinquish coercive phrasing and invest time in explaining the relevance of learning activities.
#### Constraints
- The article offers this as a recommendation grounded in prior autonomy-support research and the present correlational findings, not as a directly tested manipulation.

### Target Learners
- Secondary school pupils in teacher-led lessons

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining pupil autonomy, engagement and willingness to participate in class
