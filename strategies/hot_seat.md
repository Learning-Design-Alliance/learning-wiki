# Hot Seat

## Description
One learner assumes the role of a book character, significant figure in history, or concept (such as a tornado, an animal, or the Titanic). Sitting in front of the rest of the class, the learner responds to classmates’ questions while staying in character in that role. Give more learners the opportunity to be in the hot seat while increasing everyone’s participation by having learners do hot seat discussions in small groups, where one person per group acts as the “character” and three or four others ask them questions. In another variation, several learners could form a panel of different characters, taking questions from the class all together and interacting with one another like guests on a TV talk show.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a physical space where one learner can sit in front of the rest of the class, a topic or character to role-play, and clear instructions for learners.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging for learners who are shy or reluctant to speak in front of a group or who struggle with role-playing, requires careful facilitation to ensure respectful communication and avoid stereotyping.
