# Adopt CUSUM analysis as a standardized self-monitoring tool when assessing learning curves of new surgical techniques

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that surgical teams introducing a new procedure use CUSUM analysis of operative time as an objective, standardized way to monitor their own learning curve and to decide when proficiency is reached. They argue the tool "has proven to be useful in objectively assessing learning curves for new surgical techniques", and that establishing such curves is the first step toward building safe and effective surgical training programs. They also call for consensus on a standardized reporting system for learning-curve outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A consecutive case series with recorded operative times and a defined proficiency parameter
#### Constraints
- Results may vary with each surgeon's manual dexterity and experience level
- Further investigation with several surgeons and institutions is needed to define a more accurate and generalized learning curve

### Target Learners
- surgeons adopting a new surgical technique
- surgical training program designers

### Target Learning Goals
- objectively assessing surgeon learning curves and readiness for independent practice
