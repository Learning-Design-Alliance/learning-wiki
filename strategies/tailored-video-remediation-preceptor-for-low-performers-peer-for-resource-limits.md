# Use precepted video review preferentially for underperforming students and peer discussion as a resource-efficient alternative

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that "Peer discussion following individual video review can be a valuable tool, especially in resource-limited settings, but preceptor-led video review needs to be prioritized for students requiring more intensive remediation." Peer discussion under faculty supervision was designed as a less resource-intensive option because preceptor involvement is described as "an authentic and resource-intensive method of remediation." The recommended tailoring includes ensuring availability of trained faculty for one-on-one video-review sessions even if limited, and using peer feedback to supplement these efforts, so that remediation is matched to individual student needs when faculty time is scarce.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured peer discussion session (30 minutes) under a preceptor-supervised facilitator for the resource-efficient arm
- Availability of trained faculty for one-on-one video-review sessions, even if limited
- Video recordings of each student's encounters and a self-feedback diary
#### Constraints
- The peer discussion arm's equivalence was demonstrated in a cohort with relatively high baseline OSCE scores, which the authors say makes educational impact hard to assess from post-intervention scores alone

### Target Learners
- Final-year medical students, with prioritized precepted review for low-performing students

### Target Learning Goals
- Remediation of clinical skills assessed by OSCE, including patient–physician interaction
