# Recruit residency candidates locally from the communities they will teach in, with LEA partners participating in admissions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Both studied residencies recruited candidates who lived in or had roots around partner districts, on the assumption that local ties increase retention, and involved LEA partners directly in interviews and admissions decisions. As printed, "Each residency aimed to recruit residents who lived in or had roots in and around partner school districts and the communities served by the residency, with the assumption that this recruitment strategy would increase graduate teacher retention rates due to the residents' lifelong connections to the area." Kern Urban used joint CSUB–BCSD group interviews; TRRE held virtual advisement meetings with CSUB, TCOE, and district representatives deciding fit collaboratively.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Program coordinators who extend personalized support to applicants navigating complicated application requirements
- LEA partner willingness to participate in interviews and deliberations
#### Constraints
- Recruitment channels differed by context: Kern Urban relied on word of mouth after years of operation, while the geographically disparate TRRE relied heavily on web-based and social media marketing plus community tabling

### Target Learners
- Local prospective teachers, including CSUB liberal studies students

### Target Learning Goals
- Building a stable, community-rooted local teacher workforce
