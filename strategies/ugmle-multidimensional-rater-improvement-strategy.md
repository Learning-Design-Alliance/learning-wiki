# Multidimensional rater-improvement strategy combining expert training, monitoring and evaluation, quality assurance mechanisms, and item banking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that licensure examination directorates improve experts' item-difficulty rating ability "by adopting a multidimensional strategy that includes expert training, monitoring and evaluation of MCQ item development, application of quality assurance mechanisms, and item banking". The strategy targets the validity and reliability of standard-setting in high-stakes medical licensure exams. It is a forward-looking recommendation grounded in the finding that expert ratings explained only 33% of actual difficulty variance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rater training and consistent feedback mechanisms so expert evaluations can be aligned with examinees' actual levels of difficulty
#### Constraints
- The article notes that without a feedback loop where MCQ results are reviewed and improvements made, persistent discrepancies between perceived and actual difficulty may remain

### Target Learners
- Physician item raters and standard-setting experts in medical licensure examinations

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate prediction of MCQ item difficulty for defensible cut-score standard setting
