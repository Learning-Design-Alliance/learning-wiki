# Use the location-independent SJT and e-learning modules to free staff resources for simulated-patient exercises while imparting more theoretical material online

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's outlook recommends integrating the virtual learning environment into different communicative curricula so that various combinations of classroom teaching and virtual teaching units can be implemented. It states: "Thus, with the same staff resources for the exercises with simulated patients, more theoretical material for clinical communication in the form of e-learning modules could be imparted." The strategy addresses the time, staffing and financial challenges that extensive communication-curriculum requirements pose to medical faculties.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An online SJT and e-learning modules available independently of location, plus an existing attendance-based curriculum with simulated-patient exercises
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness of the blended concept compared to pure classroom instruction is planned research for 2020/2021, not yet established

### Target Learners
- medical students in clinical communication courses

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical communicative competence
