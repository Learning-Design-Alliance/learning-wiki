Align future e-assessment interventions in teacher education with a supportive technological infrastructure¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the project's mixed outcomes, the authors recommend that procedures and criteria be re-designed and re-tested before serving as input for curriculum changes, and that interventions account for infrastructure. As they conclude, "future pedagogical interventions in the domain of e-assessment in teacher education should concur with a supportive technological infrastructure." They note teething problems, incomplete interventions, and missing infrastructure (web-video servers, large computer rooms) may explain the absence of positive evaluations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A technological infrastructure that supports the specific e-assessment format (e.g., web-video server for video clips, large computer rooms for digital testing)

Constraints¶

  • Dutch teacher education programs showed a quite conventional technology picture: basic technology available but not commonly used, and more advanced or innovative technology less available

Target Learners¶

  • post-graduate teacher education student-teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • valid, efficient e-assessment of teaching competence

Claims¶

  • E-assessment interventions did not significantly increase teacher educators' use or evaluation of the targeted assessment procedures compared to control programs [+W]
  • A marginal trend suggested teacher educators in the experimental condition tended to increase their use of digital knowledge tests after the intervention [+W]
  • Qualitative evaluation identified per-format conditions: rubrics improved feedback transparency, summative peer assessment was judged infeasible, and video was seen as complementary to live observation [+W]
  • Student-teachers showed no significant differences in evaluation of e-assessment types between experimental and control conditions, with a marginal negative trend for peer feedback [+W]
