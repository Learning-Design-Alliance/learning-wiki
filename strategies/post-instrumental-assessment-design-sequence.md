# Post-instrumental assessment: certify the learner's accountable relation to AI-mediated work via a design sequence of naming the capacity, classifying AI use, and requiring evidence at the point of judgment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy implements post-instrumental assessment through a three-step design sequence: "First, name the target capacity", then decide which forms of AI use would scaffold versus replace that capacity, then require evidence at the point where judgment matters, such as a justification of source selection, a defense of a contested premise, or an explanation of why an AI suggestion was rejected. Documentation should be lean and meaningful, focused on responsibility rather than exhaustive prompt logs, because a short process note, targeted annotation, viva, or in-class transfer task asks the student to reason under a new demand. The strategy aligns the assessment rule with the capacity the assessment claims to certify rather than applying a universal AI ban or permission.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The institution must be able to state what capacity the assessment is meant to evidence before choosing enforcement measures
#### Constraints
- Equity checks are needed: policies treating all AI use as suspicious may punish disabled students, multilingual writers, and students using legitimate support

### Target Learners
- higher education and school students
- instructors and assessment designers

### Target Learning Goals
- demonstrating an accountable relation to AI-mediated work, including framing tasks, evaluating evidence, revising claims, and answering objections

### Affordances
- Post Instrumental Learning
