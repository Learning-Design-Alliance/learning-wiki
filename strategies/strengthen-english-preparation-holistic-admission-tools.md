# Strengthen English language preparation and consider holistic admission tools in English-medium medical admissions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, given the strong role of English proficiency, "targeted strategies—such as enhancing the preparatory year's language curriculum and reducing disparities in secondary-level English education—could improve readiness for medical study". It further suggests broadening admissions reform by incorporating additional predictors, including psychosocial factors, learning strategies, and prior exposure to English-medium instruction, and cautiously integrating non-cognitive assessments such as Situational Judgment Tests and Multiple Mini Interviews.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires institutional preparatory-year English language curriculum capacity and admission-policy authority to add or weight language and non-cognitive measures.
#### Constraints
- The article states the findings are preliminary, course-specific, and hypothesis-generating, not intended to prescribe immediate policy change.

### Target Learners
- prospective and first-year medical students in English-medium Saudi medical programs

### Target Learning Goals
- readiness for English-medium medical coursework, including scientific reading and communication
