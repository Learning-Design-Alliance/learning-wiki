# Use hybrid simulation with high psychological fidelity, including induced stress, when teaching complex intimate-examination skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends preparing healthcare providers for real clinical situations by using simulation with high psychological fidelity that induces stress similar to real medical situations. Qualitative findings showed stress was higher with the hybrid model yet "was perceived as the positive and necessary component", and none of the residents defined it as hindering learning. The authors argue complex learning tasks may begin right from the start of the training curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulated patient integrated with a task trainer to create emotional realism and bidirectional interaction between SP and participant
#### Constraints
- The article states findings are limited by a small number of participants and lack of information about retention of skills and transferability to clinics

### Target Learners
- OBGYN residents
- pediatrics and emergency medicine residents

### Target Learning Goals
- complex clinical examination skills combining technical, communication and interpersonal components
