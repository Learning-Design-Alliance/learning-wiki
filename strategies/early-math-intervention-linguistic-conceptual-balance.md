# Balance linguistic and conceptual support in early math intervention because the source of math difficulties may not be clear until well into elementary school

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that early math intervention should balance linguistic and conceptual support, because RD-related cross-domain math deficits and MD-related deficits produce similar early math presentations that diverge over time. Since "the source of a child's math difficulties may not be clear until well into elementary school," interventions should not assume a single domain-specific origin of math difficulty in the early grades.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Early identification and monitoring of math and reading skills across the elementary years to clarify the source of difficulty
#### Constraints
- The article notes disability-related impairments are already present by the start of Grade 1, before skills can be reliably assessed

### Target Learners
- Children in Grades 1–5 with math difficulties, reading difficulties, or both

### Target Learning Goals
- Early elementary mathematics skills
- Calculation and math fluency
- Applied problem solving
