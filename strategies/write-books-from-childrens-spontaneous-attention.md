# Write children's reading material from direct observation of what children of different ages spontaneously attend to

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The reviewer sketches a method for creating children's books: live close to the children, record what they do and say, and "put into the books the things to which children of the different ages give spontaneous attention." Mitchell's Here and Now Story Book is presented as the product of exactly this approach, applied at the City and Country School, in contrast with the Mother Goose and fairy-story primers then standard in primary schools.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained close contact with children: being "with the children throughout their waking hours, constantly tabulating and interpreting what they did and said."
#### Constraints
- The reviewer frames this as a thought experiment about how one would have to work if stranded without existing children's books; it is not presented as a tested procedure.

### Target Learners
- primary-grade children

### Target Learning Goals
- reading materials matched to children's interests and daily experience
