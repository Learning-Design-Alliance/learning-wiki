# Home School Communication

## Description
Home school communication is the deliberate design of channels through which teachers and families exchange information about student learning — progress reports, conferences, digital platforms, newsletters, and informal contacts. Effective communication is two-way and learning-focused: families receive actionable information about what their child is learning and how to support it, and educators learn about the child's home context. It is a core component of Epstein's framework of "partnerships" among school, family, and community.

## Design Implications

Communication that is specific, frequent, and oriented toward learning tasks produces stronger effects than generic praise or deficit-focused reporting [~M]. The mechanism is partly motivational — families who understand expectations can reinforce them at home — and partly informational, since teachers gain context that improves instructional decisions. One-way broadcast communication (newsletters, grade portals alone) shows weaker effects than interactive contact such as conferences, phone calls, or messaging dialogue [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A predictable cadence (e.g., weekly updates plus event-triggered contact) rather than contact only when problems arise
- Two-way channels: families must be able to respond, ask questions, and share information, not only receive it
- Translation and accessibility for families' home languages and literacy levels ([Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md))
- Content focused on specific learning goals and concrete actions, not just behavior ratings

#### Constraints
- Communication framed around deficits or complaints reduces family trust and engagement [-M]; early negative-only contact predicts disengagement
- Digital portals alone do not build partnership; families without reliable access or digital literacy are systematically excluded [-M]
- Over-communication of low-value updates (mass notifications) trains families to ignore messages [~W]
- Effects are weaker in secondary school than elementary, where subject-matter specialization fragments the family-teacher relationship [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Teacher-initiated positive contact** — brief calls or messages celebrating specific progress; low cost, high trust payoff
- **Learning-focused updates** — sharing what is being taught with suggested home activities (Epstein's "learning at home" type)
- **Two-way journals or apps** — platforms such as Seesaw or ClassDojo that let students and families document and comment on work
- **Home visits** — intensive relationship-building used in programs like the Parent Teacher Home Visits model

### Target Learners
- Elementary students, where a single teacher-family relationship carries most instructional information [~M]
- Students from families facing language, work-schedule, or access barriers — communication must be adapted, not assumed away
- Students at risk of disengagement, where early and positive contact functions as a [Belonging](../elements/belonging.md) signal for both student and family

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement support: aligning home reinforcement with school learning goals
- Attendance and engagement: families who are informed are better positioned to act on early warning signs
- Social-emotional development: consistent adult expectations across home and school contexts

### Instructions
1. Establish a communication plan at the start of the year: channels, cadence, and what families can expect ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md))
2. Open with positive, specific contact before any problem-focused contact is needed
3. Make each update learning-focused: name the goal, show a sample of work, suggest one concrete home action
4. Provide a low-effort response channel and treat family replies as data about the learner ([Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md))
5. Translate and adapt format for accessibility ([Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md))
6. Use patterns from family responses to adjust instruction, closing the loop like [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md)

## Examples
- **Parent Teacher Home Visits** (https://www.pthp.org) — trained educators visit families at home to build relational trust before academic problem-solving
- **Seesaw** (https://web.seesaw.me) — student work portfolios shared with families, who can view and comment, making communication artifact-based rather than report-based
- **ClassDojo** (https://www.classdojo.com) — messaging and photo updates; research reviews caution that behavior-point features can shift communication toward deficit framing
- **Epstein's Six Types of Involvement framework** — used by district partnership programs to structure communication as one of six family-engagement categories
