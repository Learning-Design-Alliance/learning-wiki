Integrate the four core motivational interviewing skills into role-play conversations with virtual humans.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The VOC training integrated the four core MI skills: "(1) asking open-ended questions, (2) providing affirmation, (3) reflective listening (listening closely and periodically confirming comprehension), and (4) summarizing client self-assessments." Learners practiced these techniques in role-plays with virtual student veterans to build trust and motivate the virtual veteran to seek help. A virtual coach provided positive feedback when the learner employed effective MI conversation strategies and corrective feedback in response to non-MI or poor conversation choices.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A virtual coach must provide ongoing positive feedback for effective MI strategies and corrective feedback for non-MI or poor conversation choices, such as being judgmental or critical.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • college and university faculty
  • staff
  • administrators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • engage distressed student veterans in supportive, non-judgmental conversation
  • motivate student veterans to seek support services

Claims¶

  • College faculty, staff and administrators rated their preparedness to support student veterans in psychological distress significantly higher after game-based virtual-human role-play training, and still above baseline at 3-month follow-up, in a pre/post study with no control group. [+W]
  • Game-based virtual-human role-play training significantly increases faculty self-efficacy and behavioral intent to support student veterans, with sustained gains at 3-month follow-up. [+W]
  • Game-based virtual-human role-play training significantly increases faculty military cultural competency, with sustained gains at 3-month follow-up. [+W]
  • Participants rated the VOC training highly on means efficacy, usefulness, and satisfaction, suggesting strong perceived value of the tool. [+W]
  • Self-reported gatekeeper behaviors (identifying, approaching, and referring distressed student veterans) trended upward after training but did not reach statistical significance on the composite measure. [~W]
  • Over one third of participants reported increased conversations with colleagues about student veterans they were concerned about, suggesting potential institutional-level culture change. [+W]
