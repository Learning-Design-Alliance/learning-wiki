# Write new etudes that target difficulties unique to the specific piece rather than relying on general technique methods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors create small, functional etudes from Turkish Music pieces for their own students, and that future etudes be piece-specific. It states that "any etudes that are written should aim at enabling the student to perform musical pieces by helping them overcome difficulties unique to the piece in terms of technique, musicality, and style." It further recommends studies on other Turkish Music genres to address performance difficulties on cello and other instruments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors able to observe the specific mistakes students make on a particular piece and translate them into exercises or melodic patterns
#### Constraints
- Existing methods and etudes for teaching cello technique are already numerous, so the article argues new etudes should be justified by piece-specific needs

### Target Learners
- cello students in Turkish Music conservatory education
- cellists interested in genres other than Turkish Music

### Target Learning Goals
- technical, musical, and stylistic mastery of specific challenging repertoire pieces
