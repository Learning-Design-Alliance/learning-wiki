# Use lab guides that explicitly prompt modeling subtasks, with scaffolding fading over the semester

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In transformed CU lab courses, lab guides explicitly prompted students to engage in modeling subtasks such as constructing models, making predictions and comparisons, and revising models and apparatus. For example, an Advanced Laboratory polarization activity prompted students to use the Jones formalism and derive predictions like Malus's Law, and an Electronics Laboratory guide prompted revision of a circuit schematic to include a multimeter's input resistance. The level of explicit scaffolding faded over the semester to give students more control over how they modeled their circuits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lab guides must be designed around the framework's subtasks, and instructors must accept reduced explicit prompting over time as students take control
#### Constraints
- Notebook evidence indicates most students did not enact revisions unless specifically asked, so fading scaffolding must be managed carefully

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in advanced laboratory, electronics laboratory, and introductory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- engaging in modeling subtasks including model construction, comparison, and revision
