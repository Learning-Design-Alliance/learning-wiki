# Incorporate evidence-based feedback principles into ECTV training and implementation for registrars and ECT visitors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because feedback quality and features were the factors most strongly associated with educational utility ratings, the article recommends that ECTV training and implementation "incorporates evidence-based principles of feedback, including specificity, actionability, timeliness, two-way/collaborative, and underpinned by mutual trust and credibility". It adds that registrars should be supported in developing feedback literacy, defined as appreciating feedback, making judgments, managing affect, and taking action, to maximise learning from the visit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback provided during visits should be focused/specific and easy to translate into action, the quality definition the study used
#### Constraints
- Remote modalities may present additional challenges in fostering the personal connection necessary for effective feedback

### Target Learners
- GP registrars in vocational general practice training
- external clinical teaching visitors

### Target Learning Goals
- responding to and acting on formative feedback
- translating observed consultation feedback into clinical practice change
