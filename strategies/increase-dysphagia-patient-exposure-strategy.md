# Provide increased exposure to patients with dysphagia during SLT training to raise self-perceived competency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that dysphagia training programs, particularly in low- and middle-income countries, increase learners' direct patient-facing experiences, since patient exposure was significantly associated with higher self-perceived competency in both DCVT domains. The authors state programs "are encouraged to provide increased exposure to patients with dysphagia to support increased self-perceived competency scores." They also suggest restructuring the combined cohort course to introduce clinical history taking and oral motor examination earlier alongside theoretical instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to patients with dysphagia for supervised clinical practice during the course
#### Constraints
- Limited variety of conditions seen during the one-month course; language barrier between English-speaking instructors and local-language patients may limit clinical learning

### Target Learners
- Graduate SLT students, particularly in LMIC settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Dysphagia assessment and management competency
