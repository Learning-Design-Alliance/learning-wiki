# Before implementing evidence-based teaching, compile students' initial knowledge, define measurable goals, and design aligned instruction and assessment by working through six guiding questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that before implementing evidence-based teaching, instructors "compile a list of what initial knowledge students have, what are the measurable goals of the course" and contemplate six questions. These cover students' initial knowledge and cognitive and affective attributes (including alternative conceptions, problem solving and reasoning skills, mathematical skills, epistemological beliefs, attitude, motivation, self-efficacy), what students should know and be able to do, what proficiency looks like, what evidence would demonstrate proficiency, how to design instruction building on initial knowledge, and whether goals, instruction, and assessment are aligned.

### Context
