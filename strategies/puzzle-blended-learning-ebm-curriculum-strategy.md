# Incorporate puzzle-based blended learning into undergraduate EBM curricula, particularly for complex clinical reasoning and evidence appraisal skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "Educators should consider incorporating puzzle-based blended learning activities into undergraduate EBM curricula, particularly for teaching complex clinical reasoning and evidence appraisal skills". The puzzle format requires only printed materials, making it low-cost and technology-independent, which facilitates implementation in settings with limited technological infrastructure. The asynchronous pre-class component supports self-paced learning and may accommodate diverse learner needs and learning preferences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Asynchronous pre-class written materials covering foundational EBM concepts
- In-person sessions with small-group puzzle activities
- A trained EBM expert facilitator using a standardized feedback protocol
#### Constraints
- Structured and algorithmic tasks such as PICO-based question formulation may be sufficiently supported through conventional lecture-based instruction without requiring resource-intensive active-learning interventions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in clerkship rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- evidence appraisal, clinical reasoning, and evidence application skills
