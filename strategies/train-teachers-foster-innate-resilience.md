# Schools should train teachers to foster students' innate resilience through communication, relationships and protective-factor activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that resilience can be fostered by teachers in regular classrooms, the authors recommend that "schools should adopt strategies and train teachers to teach youth about their innate resilience, provide meaningful opportunities for communication among students and with teachers". The recommendation set also includes developing positive student-teacher relationships, promoting positive peer relations through classroom and school activities, fostering academic self-determination, confidence and feelings of competence, and promoting creativity, self-esteem, self-efficacy, internal locus of control, sense of humor, stress coping skills, autonomy and optimism.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher training so schools can adopt the strategies
- Meaningful opportunities for communication among students and with teachers
#### Constraints
- The study's intervention lasted only three months; the authors recommend implementing such resiliency training for a longer time period for better results

### Target Learners
- Non-resilient at-risk students in regular secondary classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- Resilience and its protective factors
