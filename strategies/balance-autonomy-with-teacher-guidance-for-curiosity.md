# Balance learning autonomy with teacher guidance when aiming to develop mathematical curiosity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because PBLCCS improved critical thinking but not curiosity, the authors recommend that educators identify ways by which "learning autonomy is proportionally supp orted by teacher's guidance and instruction", and that students need to get accustomed to PBLCCS practices with more focused learning instruments (LKM). This strategy responds to their null curiosity finding and to cited evidence that minimal-guidance methods were less effective.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning instruments (LKM) focused enough to increase students' MCT skill
- Repeated accustoming of students to PBLCCS practices
#### Constraints
- The article cites Kirschner et al. (2006) finding ineffectiveness in constructivist methods providing excessive attention on learner's autonomy and neglecting teacher's guidance

### Target Learners
- undergraduate mathematics education students

### Target Learning Goals
- mathematical curiosity attitude
- mathematical critical thinking
