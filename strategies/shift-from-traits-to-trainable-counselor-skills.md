# Shift counselor education research and selection from personality traits to identifiable, trainable counselor skills and behaviors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends redirecting research and training away from the search for an ideal counselor personality toward specific behaviors, skills, and interactions related to counseling outcome. It endorses Mosher's call for a change in emphasis "from what the counselor is to what he can perform," and points to skills-based training programs such as Carkhuff's systematic skill teaching and Ivey's microcounseling, which teaches interviewer behaviors that can be behaviorally defined, described in a manual, and modeled on videotape.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Necessary counseling skills must first be identified and operationally defined so training can target them
#### Constraints
- The review notes continued research is needed to define more precisely the performance parameters that affect counseling outcome

### Target Learners
- counselor trainees and counselor education programs

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of specific, behaviorally defined interviewing and counseling skills
