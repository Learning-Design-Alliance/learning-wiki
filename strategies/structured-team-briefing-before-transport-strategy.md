# Use a short structured team briefing before transporting unstable patients

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends applying a checklist-based structured team briefing before inter-hospital transportation of unstable patients, arguing that a briefing ensures relevant information is shared and helps the team agree on goals, identify key priorities, and identify ways to reach them. The authors state that "Our study suggests that similar advantages can be made with the transportation of unstable patients", drawing on evidence that checklists and timeouts improve understanding and mitigate errors in elective surgery.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Briefing must be short and easy to administer so it causes no delay in resuscitation attempts
#### Constraints
- Junior physicians often feel time-pressured and may be reluctant to invest time in team briefings

### Target Learners
- junior physicians
- ambulance crews
- resuscitation teams

### Target Learning Goals
- shared mental models
- team coordination
- error reduction during patient transport
