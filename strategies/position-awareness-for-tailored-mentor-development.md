# Use awareness of mentoring positions to identify mentors' learning needs and tailor faculty development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that mentors analyze and reflect on their mentoring and its context so they become aware of the beliefs founding their practice. Once aware of their current position, mentors "can reflect on it and decide whether this position is still appropriate," can make use of multiple positions rather than rigidly holding one, and can strengthen their position through "individual coaching or tailored faculty development."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentors must first become aware of their current mentoring position, e.g., through guided reflection on their practice
#### Constraints
- Positions are contextualized and dynamic, so the article cautions they are not a prescription for ideal mentoring practice

### Target Learners
- faculty mentors of undergraduate students in medicine, biomedical sciences, and health sciences

### Target Learning Goals
- self-awareness of mentoring beliefs and targeted improvement of mentoring practice

### Affordances
- Personal Interpretative Framework Mentoring Beliefs
