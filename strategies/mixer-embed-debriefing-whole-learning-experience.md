# Embed synthetic-culture interaction in teacher-led debriefing and repeated blended sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that experiential learning with synthetic cultures be integrated and delivered as a whole learning experience, incorporating the experiential learning reinforced by teacher-led classroom discussion providing both didactic and experiential culture-general learning. Because most children initially judged the differing rule sets as cheating, the debriefing explicitly explained that neither team was cheating and highlighted the importance of communication in resolving conflict. They further suggest the MIXER interaction and follow-up discussion be repeated and blended with additional didactic learning experiences, and that multiple rule-sets could reduce the focus on cheating.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trained researcher or teacher leads the discussion session after the interaction
#### Constraints
- Similar to most other software for Personal, Social and Emotional classroom-based learning, the software alone did not fully convey the difference-is-acceptable message

### Target Learners
- 9–11 year old school children

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding that different cultural rules are acceptable rather than cheating
- conflict resolution strategies
