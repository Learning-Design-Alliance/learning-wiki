# Sequence uncertainty measures by increasing complexity: start with min-max, then exclude extremes, middle 50%, MAD, and finally standard deviation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends introducing measurement uncertainties with the simplest measure and moving to more complex ones as students' mathematical capabilities grow. Teachers should "start with the min-max measure", refine to exclude extremes, introduce a converging measure like middle 50%, use the MAD as an intermediate step before the standard deviation, and introduce the standard deviation "only in the upper school", tying it to the normal distribution. The concept should be repeated whenever appropriate.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers sequence measures according to the mathematical operations students have mastered in the mathematics curriculum
- The topic of measurement uncertainties is repeated in classrooms whenever appropriate to develop statistical thinking
#### Constraints
- Low-complexity measures describe the variability of the data with lower quality; min-max and exclude extremes remain susceptible to outliers for larger numbers of repeated measurements
- The article recommends 50% over 68% or 76% because it will prove easier for students to determine during the exclusion step

### Target Learners
- high school science students
- upper school students (for the standard deviation)

### Target Learning Goals
- quantifying and interpreting measurement uncertainties
- understanding the statistical nature of repeated measurements
