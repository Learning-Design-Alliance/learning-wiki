# Use the ULTrA fixed factor (or full ULTrA) to measure mindset in science and math undergraduates when survey length allows

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that researchers and practitioners studying science and math undergraduates' mindsets use the ULTrA survey rather than the context-general original measure when survey length permits. "These results suggest that using just the 5 items that comprise the fixed factor of the ULTrA survey can explain substantial variance in outcomes." The author further notes the growth factor adds explanatory power, so the full survey may offer even greater predictive power. The strategy also advises estimating a confirmatory factor model whenever the measure is used in a new sample.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- No strict survey length limitation; ideally a sample size sufficient for confirmatory factor analysis or SEM
#### Constraints
- The ULTrA may not be ideal for researchers constrained in survey length or needing to minimize participant burden
- Latent-factor approaches require larger sample sizes and are not always possible

### Target Learners
- Science and math undergraduates

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate measurement of mindset beliefs to study and support undergraduate motivation, persistence, and achievement
