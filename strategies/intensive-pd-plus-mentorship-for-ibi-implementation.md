# Prepare teachers for inquiry-based instruction through intensive professional development plus ongoing mentorship before classroom implementation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teacher educators model inquiry-based instruction, provide practice similar to the NATAA, offer in-service education, and mentor teachers with guidance and feedback. The authors state that "teacher educators should model inquiry–based instruction and provide practice similar to that of the NATAA", and that strong professional development coupled with encouragement and mentorship provided for clear results. Practitioners are advised to attend NATAA workshops and to use at least four weeks of instruction to let students adjust to the new method.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers receive intensive professional development in the inquiry method before delivering it
- Experienced teachers provide feedback, clarity of content, and mentoring to those learning the method
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is grounded in one quasi-experimental study in secondary agriscience education with teachers already trained through NATAA

### Target Learners
- secondary school agriscience students
- secondary agriscience teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- student content knowledge achievement
- teacher capacity to deliver inquiry-based instruction

## Claims

- [Inquiry-based instruction produced higher content knowledge achievement than the subject matter approach in secondary agriscience classes on six of seven unit posttests](../claims/ibi-higher-agriscience-achievement-than-subject-matter.md) [+M]
