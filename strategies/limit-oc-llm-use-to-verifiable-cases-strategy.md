# Limit OC-LLM use in medical applications to cases where users can independently verify outputs or check cited sources

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 7 studies (3 review, 2 design, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 7 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because flagship models still produced hallucinations on over 12.8% of examination responses and fluent answers make errors harder to spot, the authors recommend risk-reduction approaches for medical applications: "limit use to cases in which users can independently determine the correctness of the output or confirm the supporting source information through the links provided". They also argue health care professionals and educators must acquire skills to recognize and manage hallucinations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Users must be able to independently determine output correctness or confirm supporting source information via provided links.
#### Constraints
- Direct medical support such as selecting personalized pharmacological treatments requires careful consideration of ethical issues including explainability, responsibility, privacy, and patient rights.

### Target Learners
- health care professionals
- medical and pharmacy students
- medical educators

### Target Learning Goals
- safe and effective use of generative AI in clinical and educational settings, including hallucination recognition and management
