Emphasize anticipatory structure strategies and minimize controlling aspects, delivered within broader autonomy and emotional support¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the moderator findings, the article recommends that teachers emphasize anticipatory strategies, minimize the controlling aspects of structure, and consider the broader context including country context, student income background, and whether structure is paired with other psychological supports. Anticipatory strategies proactively communicate expectations, rules, and routines so students can self-regulate, whereas responsive strategies like rewards, intervening, and punishment risk being experienced as controlling. Structure should be delivered "in an autonomy-supportive manner or with positive teacher emotions and caring" so students perceive it as competence support rather than behavioral control.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers must deliver structure with clarity of information and ongoing guidance rather than as demands and sanctions

Constraints¶

  • The benefits may be reduced when structure is applied in a controlling manner or within a broader context that students interpret as controlling rather than competence-supportive

Target Learners¶

  • Preschool through high school students, including students from low income backgrounds and at-promise or special needs students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Academic engagement, competence beliefs, and achievement

Affordances¶

  • Classroom Structure Construct Definition

Claims¶

  • Effects of classroom structure vary with strategy type and context: anticipatory strategies help, controlling aspects should be minimized, and country, income background, and paired psychological supports matter [+W]
  • Teachers' provision of classroom structure correlates positively with students' achievement, engagement, and competence beliefs, and shows a statistically non-significant negative relationship with disengagement [+M]
  • Classroom structure interventions produce significant positive average effects on achievement and engagement and reduce disengagement, with a non-significant effect on competence beliefs [+M]
  • Structure-outcome relationships appear largely universal across grade levels [~M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Build autonomy-supportive learning contexts through perspective-taking, choice, self-initiation, and non-controlling language
