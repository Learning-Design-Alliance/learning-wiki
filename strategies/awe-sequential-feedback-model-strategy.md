# Implement sequential feedback models deploying AWE from surface-level corrections to deeper feedback on cohesion and argumentation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors deploy AWE tools in a sequence, starting with surface-level corrections in grammar and mechanics before progressing to deeper feedback on cohesion and argumentation. This approach leverages AWE's demonstrated strengths in surface-level writing while scaffolding toward higher-order skills where AWE alone is insufficient. The strategy is grounded in the meta-analytic finding that AWE effects are strongest for vocabulary and weakest for argumentation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must sequence AWE feedback from surface-level to deeper levels rather than deploying all feedback types simultaneously
#### Constraints
- Higher-order writing skills require teacher mediation and explicit instruction beyond what AWE can provide

### Target Learners
- Students in elementary through post-secondary education learning writing

### Target Learning Goals
- Writing proficiency including grammar, mechanics, cohesion, and argumentation
