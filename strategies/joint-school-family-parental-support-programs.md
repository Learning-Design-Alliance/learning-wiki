# Design joint school–family work programs that make parents aware of their support for adolescent physical activity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that, given adolescents' increasing screen use, schools and families should collaborate to promote parental support for physical activity. It states "it is vital to design joint work programs between schools and families, in order to promote healthy habits, improve academic performance and achieve social and psychological benefits in schoolchildren", and that parents should see their support as essential to generate adherence to physical activity practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Parents or legal guardians must be reached and informed, as in this study where schools and parents were informed in detail before data collection.
#### Constraints
- Due to its design, this study did not permit the extraction of causality relationships between the analyzed variables.

### Target Learners
- Adolescents aged 12–16 in compulsory secondary education

### Target Learning Goals
- Healthy habits and physical activity adherence
- Academic performance
- School satisfaction with physical and sports activity

## Claims

- [Adolescents with low general parental support (APAF1) report more boredom, less fun, less physical activity, lower academic performance and higher alcohol consumption](../claims/low-general-parental-support-worse-outcomes-adolescents.md) [+M]
- [Higher parental support from active parents (APAF2) is associated with more fun and less alcohol consumption, but not with boredom, physical activity or academic performance](../claims/active-parents-support-fun-alcohol.md) [+M]
