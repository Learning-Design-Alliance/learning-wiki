# Use drone hardware realization of continuum deformation to bridge in-class mechanics learning and physical experiments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends using quadcopter teams as flying demonstrations of strain and deformation theory: students observe axial and shear deformation, principal strains, and Mohr-circle evolution realized physically by drones rather than only through theoretical and numerical analysis or expensive testing machines. The authors state this fills "the gap between in-class learning and hardware realization and experiments, where we introduce new roles for drones as \"teachers\"" providing an opportunity for practicing theoretical concepts of mechanics in an understandable way.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a quadcopter team with indoor positioning and the continuum deformation planning method
#### Constraints
- The approach was demonstrated in a controlled indoor flying area with decimeter-level positioning precision

### Target Learners
- junior undergraduate engineering students in mechanics of materials

### Target Learning Goals
- axial and shear deformation
- principal strains
- continuum mechanics
