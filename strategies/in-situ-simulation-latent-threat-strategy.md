# Use in-situ simulation in the actual clinical environment to observe teams and identify latent threats

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 4 studies (2 design, 1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In-situ simulation is "a training experience that takes place in the actual clinical environment", allowing the clinical team, system processes and environment to be observed. The article reports increasing evidence that in-situ simulation has the potential to identify latent threats, and describes its use across all five Acute Trusts in Northern Ireland, integrated into clinical governance in the Northern Trust.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Good advance planning, communication and organisation are vital to the success of this educational approach
#### Constraints
- Challenges include technical issues, timing around clinical pressure, consideration of other patients and families, and not tying up vital resources needed for real clinical events

### Target Learners
- clinical teams and postgraduate trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- teamworking, system awareness and identification of latent safety threats
