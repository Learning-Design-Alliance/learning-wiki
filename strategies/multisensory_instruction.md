# Multisensory Instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Multisensory instruction teaches content through two or more sensory modalities at once — learners see a letter, say its sound, and trace or write it in the same lesson. The approach originated in the Orton-Gillingham-Stillman tradition for dyslexia remediation and underpins most structured literacy programs. Its core mechanism is not "matching learning styles" but pairing explicit, systematic phonics with simultaneous multimodal encoding and motor output.

## Design Implications

Multisensory instruction is best understood as explicit, systematic phonics instruction enriched with simultaneous multimodal input and motor engagement. The evidence base is strongest for the underlying explicit phonics component; the added value of the multisensory (tactile-kinesthetic) layer itself is less well isolated [Phonics instruction outperforms whole-word approaches for generalization.](../claims/phonics-instruction-outperforms-whole-word-for-generalization.md) [+S]. Dual coding theory predicts benefits when verbal and visual channels carry complementary, non-redundant information [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [~M] — but adding a second channel that merely duplicates the first adds extraneous load rather than benefit.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors trained in a structured, sequential, cumulative scope and sequence (e.g., Orton-Gillingham certification)
- Simultaneous — not merely sequential — use of modalities: see it, say it, hear it, write it within one activity
- Systematic phoneme–grapheme mapping as the instructional core, not sensory activities as an end in themselves
- [Explicit teaching](../patterns/explicit-teaching.md) structure: [demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md), guided [practice](../elements/practice.md), and immediate corrective [feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)

#### Constraints
- Resource-intensive: requires specialized instructor training and small-group or 1:1 delivery, limiting scalability
- Meta-analytic evidence for the Orton-Gillingham approach specifically is promising but limited by few rigorous studies; effects should not be attributed to the multisensory component alone [~W]
- The "match instruction to a learner's preferred modality" rationale is discredited — tailoring to self-reported learning styles does not improve outcomes [X]
- For learners with substantial expertise, added multimodal scaffolding can become redundant and inefficient [Instructional guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Sensory activities without a systematic phonics core (e.g., sand-tray work detached from phoneme–grapheme mapping) consume instructional time without measurable literacy gains [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Structured literacy programs (Wilson, Barton, SPIRE) implement it as a fully scripted, cumulative curriculum
- Classroom adaptations embed multisensory routines (air writing, letter tiles, color-coding) within whole-class phonics lessons
- Extended to content areas: manipulatives in mathematics, gesture-plus-speech in vocabulary instruction — with correspondingly thinner evidence

### Target Learners
- Struggling readers and learners with dyslexia or word-level reading disabilities — the population for which the approach was developed and where evidence is strongest [+M]
- Early literacy learners building phoneme–grapheme mappings, where motor output (writing) reinforces letter knowledge
- Less beneficial for fluent readers or advanced learners, for whom the added sensory layer is redundant [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Decoding, encoding (spelling), and word recognition — automatic word-level skill [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Fluency and reading accuracy as prerequisites for comprehension
- Retention of paired verbal–visual associations generally [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [~M]

### Instructions
1. Begin with [direct instruction](../elements/direct-instruction.md) of the target phoneme–grapheme correspondence, following a cumulative scope and sequence
2. [Demonstrate](../elements/demonstration.md) the sound–symbol link while learners watch the mouth shape and letter formation
3. Guide simultaneous multisensory [practice](../elements/practice.md): learners say the sound while tracing, writing, or building the letter with tiles
4. [Provide feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) immediately and correct errors by re-modeling, not by re-explaining alone
5. Review previously taught patterns in each session (cumulative review) before introducing new material

## Related Strategies
- [Explicit teaching](../patterns/explicit-teaching.md) — multisensory instruction is a modality-enriched form of explicit, teacher-directed instruction
- [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) — cumulative, small-step sequencing keeps each lesson within working memory limits [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- [Cognitive load management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) — simultaneous modalities must complement, not duplicate, to avoid extraneous load

## Related Elements
- [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) — the teacher models sound production and letter formation before learners attempt it
- [Practice](../elements/practice.md) — repeated, distributed tracing/writing/saying routines build automaticity
- [Provide feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) — immediate error correction is integral to Orton-Gillingham-style lessons

## Examples
- **[Wilson Reading System](https://www.wilsonlanguage.com)** — 12-step, cumulative multisensory curriculum for adolescents and adults with word-level deficits; learners finger-tap sounds, use letter tiles, and write simultaneously
- **[Barton Reading & Spelling System](https://bartonreading.com)** — scripted Orton-Gillingham-influenced program designed for parent and tutor delivery
- **[Lindamood-Bell (LiPS)](https://lindamoodbell.com)** — adds articulatory (mouth-shape) awareness to the visual–auditory–kinesthetic triad
- **Classroom adaptation** — whole-class phonics with air writing, sand trays, and color-coded graphemes embedded in a systematic scope and sequence
