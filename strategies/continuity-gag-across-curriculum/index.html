Embed generalisation-querying games across the English curriculum with continuity of approach¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend sustaining the game's impact through "continuity in approach in the English Language class" and note the game "can be employed across the curriculum if teachers themselves are educated in the critical thinking development strategies". They also state the GAG game in its simplicity can be played in any context, at home or in social gatherings, to query statements usually taken for granted.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers educated in critical thinking development strategies for cross-curricular use

Constraints¶

  • Sustainability of impact beyond the experimental period has not been examined

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical thinking
  • reversal of ethnic and religious prejudice

Claims¶

  • Students exposed to the Generalisation Assessment Game had significantly less ethnic prejudice than students not exposed [+W]
  • Students exposed to the Generalisation Assessment Game had significantly less religious prejudice than students not exposed [+W]
  • Within the experimental group, ethnic and religious prejudice scores improved significantly from pre-test to post-test after playing GAG [+W]
