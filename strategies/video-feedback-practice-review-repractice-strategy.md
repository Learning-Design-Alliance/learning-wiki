# Use recorded practice with expert video review and re-practice to improve practical CPR skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The video feedback intervention gave each nursing student three minutes to practice CPR on a mannequin while being recorded; an investigator/CPR expert then reviewed the recording and provided subjective feedback highlighting areas for improvement, after which the student practiced again incorporating the feedback. This observe-critique-repractice cycle is the article's mechanism for skill refinement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recording equipment, a mannequin, and an expert available to review recordings and deliver individual feedback.
#### Constraints
- The feedback was subjective expert feedback rather than real-time objective metric-based feedback such as compression rate, depth, and recoil, which the article notes may offer a more precise approach.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- practical CPR skills
- motor skills for resuscitation

## Claims

- [Post-training CPR skill scores are significantly higher in the video feedback group than in the infographic group](../claims/video-feedback-group-higher-post-training-cpr-skill.md) [+W]
