Remediate children's differential learning needs at the start of primary school to weaken inequality transmission¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors' forward-looking recommendation is that "redistributing education resources to address and remediate the differential learning needs that children present with, at the start of primary school, is key to weakening the transmission of social and economic inequality across generations." This directs schools toward tailoring provisions — for example personalised learning — to individual characteristics and needs rather than teaching all children the same curricula at the same pace by the same methods.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learning needs must be identified early, at the start of primary school, before SES-related differences become entrenched through the high rank-order stability of school performance

Constraints¶

  • The authors caution that achieving equity while preserving equality of educational opportunity is a difficult balancing act for policymakers, and that equity necessarily concentrates resources on children with the greatest needs

Target Learners¶

  • children entering primary school from low SES families

Target Learning Goals¶

  • remediating differential learning needs present at school entry
  • reducing persistent SES-related gaps in school performance

Affordances¶

  • Equity Versus Equality Education Outcomes Policy

Claims¶

  • Family SES correlates 0.28 with children's primary school performance across 16 British cohorts born 1921–2011 [+W]
  • In UK-representative cohorts, the unadjusted SES–school performance estimate was 0.33, with the confounder-adjusted model nonidentified [+W]
  • The association between family SES and children's primary school performance remained stable over 95 years, with no systematic increasing or decreasing trend [+W]
  • Intensive remediation alone will not meet students' needs and, if segregating and stigmatizing, can deepen inequality and trauma [~W]
