# Standardize a single recommended intelligence test per school level with definite norms, selected by a committee of skilled psychologists

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Colvin proposes that a committee of skilled psychologists select the most valuable elements from existing tests, add missing ones, standardize the complete test, and issue it as the one recommended test for each school level, with definite norms for teacher guidance. He argues the current plethora of tests share too many features and are too nearly equal in practical value to make them all necessary. He writes: "I hope the time will soon come when a committee of skilled psychologists will select the elements most valuable in the tests now existing."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful standardization of the complete test and establishment of definite norms for the guidance of teachers.
#### Constraints
- Colvin allows several tests would remain, one each for primary, intermediate and grammar grades, high school, and college, rather than one test for all levels.

### Target Learners
- students at each school level from primary grades through college

### Target Learning Goals
- reliable and standardized assessment of intelligence for prognosis and diagnosis
