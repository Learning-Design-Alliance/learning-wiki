# Integrate hands-on telenursing simulation training into nursing curricula and continuing education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating telenursing education into nursing curricula and extending hands-on training beyond initial education: health care organizations "should provide hands-on telenursing training through structured workshops and regular skill refresher seminars for practicing nurses", medical schools should integrate practical telenursing simulations into core curricula, and professionals should complete continuing education modules with hands-on simulation to maintain virtual care competency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured workshops, refresher seminars, and simulation-based modules require institutional support from health care organizations, ministries, and schools
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence comes from a single-college cohort study without a control group, which the authors state limits generalizability

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students
- practicing nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- remote nursing care competency and virtual care delivery skills

## Claims

- [Change scores showed that gains in awareness and knowledge accompanied more positive attitude shifts, and higher awareness and more positive attitudes accompanied lower perceived barriers](../claims/change-score-correlations-telenursing.md) [+W]
- [Most third-year nursing students had no prior telenursing exposure despite moderate-to-advanced computer proficiency, and most wanted telenursing training](../claims/nursing-students-no-telenursing-exposure-high-interest.md) [+W]
- [At pretest, higher telenursing awareness, knowledge, and perceived skills were associated with more positive attitudes and higher self-efficacy, and more positive attitudes with lower perceived barriers](../claims/pretest-correlations-telenursing-variables.md) [+W]
- [Before training, self-efficacy in telenursing was predicted by awareness and knowledge; improvement in self-efficacy was predicted by change in perceived required skills and attitudes](../claims/self-efficacy-predictors-shift-after-training.md) [+W]
- [In one uncontrolled pre-post study, nursing students' awareness, knowledge, skills, attitudes, and self-efficacy scores rose and perceived barriers fell from pre-test to post-test after simulation-based telenursing training, with moderate to high effect sizes](../claims/telenursing-simulation-training-improves-all-outcomes.md) [+W]
