# Use digitized 3D anatomical models and VR/AR as supplements or alternatives to cadaver-based anatomy teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (7 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 4 studies (3 review, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends incorporating digital anatomical models into the curriculum "to enhance student learning," usable "as stand-alone learning tools or in combination with other learning resources." VR/AR lets users bypass cadaver preparation complexity, explain relationships not clearly discernible in a cadaver, and lets learners "prepare, practice, and review their performance repeatedly in a risk-free environment." The review's What-This-Study-Adds states VR and AR "can serve as effective supplements or alternatives to cadaver-based teaching."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Models must be selectable to focus on features of anatomical structures relevant to the curriculum's educational goals
#### Constraints
- AR serves as an additional training method depending on its implementation; tactile feedback incorporation remains a possibility rather than an established feature

### Target Learners
- medical students
- health sciences students
- surgical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- gross anatomy knowledge
- understanding of anatomical relationships and functions
- surgical skill preparation
