# Explicit Routine Instruction

## Description
Explicit routine instruction is the deliberate, step-by-step teaching of recurring procedures — decoding routines, problem-solving sequences, writing-process checklists, lab protocols — through direct explanation, [demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md), and guided rehearsal. The instructor states the goal, models each step with clear language, leads structured practice, and fades support as fluency develops. It is the routine-focused strand of [Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md): rather than teaching every instance anew, it installs a reusable procedure learners can apply across cases.

## Design Implications

Routines reduce the working-memory demands of recurring tasks by making the sequence automatic, freeing capacity for content and higher-order decisions [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]. Effectiveness depends on unambiguous wording, consistent step order, and immediate guided practice — vague or inconsistent routines produce fragile execution [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S]. Because routines are procedural, they must be rehearsed to fluency, not merely explained [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task analysis identifying the exact steps experts execute, in the order they execute them
- Consistent, unambiguous routine language used identically every time (scripted prompts or stems help)
- Frequent, short rehearsal opportunities with immediate [feedback](../elements/assess-performance.md) until execution is fluent
- A plan for [fading](../elements/fading.md) — from teacher-led modeling, to prompted practice, to independent execution

#### Constraints
- Overly rigid routines can produce mechanical application where flexibility is needed; learners may apply the sequence when it does not fit [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]
- Inefficient for ill-structured or open-ended goals with no stable procedure to install
- Rote execution without understanding can mask non-comprehension — learners follow steps without grasping why they work
- Excessive routine drilling displaces time for [active learning](../principles/active-learning.md) on tasks that build deeper understanding [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Scripted programs** (e.g., Reading Mastery, Corrective Reading) prescribe teacher wording and pacing precisely
- **Routine frameworks** (e.g., Archer & Hughes's explicit instruction cycle) specify structure but leave wording to the teacher
- **Self-administered routines** — checklists or strategy steps (e.g., a revision checklist) that learners eventually run themselves, shifting toward [self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md)

### Target Learners
- Novices who lack the prior knowledge to infer procedures from exposure alone [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]
- Struggling learners and those with processing difficulties, who benefit from predictable structure and reduced search demands
- Less beneficial for advanced learners, for whom established routines become redundant or constraining [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency: accurate, automatic execution of recurring steps
- Foundational skills that gate higher-order work (decoding, arithmetic facts, citation formats)
- Strategy acquisition: installing a usable sequence before learners learn when and why to vary it

### Instructions
1. **Analyze the task** — decompose the expert procedure into its smallest teachable steps and identify prerequisite knowledge.
2. **State the goal and rationale** — tell learners what the routine is for and when to use it.
3. **Model with [demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)** — execute the routine while narrating each step with [think-aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) commentary and identical wording each time.
4. **Guide practice** — lead the whole group through the routine with prompts, then fade prompts step by step ([fading](../elements/fading.md)), checking each learner's execution.
5. **Rehearse to fluency** — schedule brief distributed practice with immediate corrective [feedback](../elements/assess-performance.md) until steps are automatic ([automaticity](../elements/automaticity.md)).
6. **Transfer to varied cases** — apply the routine across new content and gradually introduce cases where it must be adapted, preventing over-rigid application.

## Related Strategies

## Examples
- **Reading Mastery** (National Institute for Direct Instruction, https://www.nifdi.org) — fully scripted decoding and comprehension routines with prescribed teacher wording, choral response, and built-in review.
- **Explicit phonics routines in the science of reading movement** — daily sound–spelling review, blending routines, and dictation sequences taught with identical language each session (see Archer & Hughes, 2011).
- **SRSD writing instruction** (https://srsdresearch.org) — teaches genre-specific writing routines (PLAN, WRITE, REVISE) through modeling and guided practice before independent use.
- **Number Talks routines** (Parrish, 2014) — a predictable daily sequence (problem posed, silent think time, sharing strategies) that becomes a self-running classroom structure.
