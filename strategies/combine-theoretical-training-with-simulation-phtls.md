# Combine standardized theoretical training with high-fidelity simulation-based practical skills training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (7 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that standardized training programs such as PHTLS be combined with high-fidelity, simulation-based practical skills training to build both competence and confidence. Simulation "allows learners to apply their theoretical knowledge in a safe, controlled, and realistic environment," providing opportunities to practice procedural skills, make decisions under pressure, and engage in teamwork. The lower self-efficacy in the trained group is interpreted as a readiness and demand for such practical, hands-on training rather than a failure of the course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Theoretical instruction alone is insufficient; simulation-based practical training must follow or accompany knowledge-focused coursework
#### Constraints
- Evidence for confidence gains from simulation is drawn from prior studies the article cites, not tested in this study itself

### Target Learners
- Emergency healthcare professionals undertaking standardized trauma training

### Target Learning Goals
- Translate theoretical knowledge into practical skills
- Build self-efficacy for high-stakes trauma situations
