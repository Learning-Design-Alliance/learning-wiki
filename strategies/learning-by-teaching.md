# Learning By Teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Learning by teaching asks learners to take instructional responsibility for material — explaining it to a peer, tutoring a younger student, or producing an explanation for an audience. The strategy works through two mechanisms: the *expectation* of teaching drives deeper, better-organized study, and the *act* of teaching forces retrieval, elaboration, and gap detection. It is carried out through structured activities such as reciprocal peer tutoring, jigsaw roles, or "teach-back" moments embedded in lessons.

## Design Implications

Teaching is a highly generative activity: explaining requires retrieving knowledge, organizing it coherently, and monitoring a listener's comprehension, all of which strengthen the tutee's own understanding [Retrieval practice improves retention.](../claims/retrieval-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S]. Merely *expecting* to teach improves learning even before any teaching occurs, because it changes how learners study [~M]. The benefits depend on the tutee actually engaging in substantive explanation and feedback — superficial tutoring behaviors (reading aloud, giving answers) produce little gain.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient prior knowledge to explain the material; tutees need preparation time and source material
- A structured protocol (what to explain, what questions to ask) — unstructured tutoring drifts toward answer-giving
- A feedback or reflection step so the tutor notices and repairs their own gaps ([Reflection](reflection.md), [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))

#### Constraints
- Tutors with weak knowledge can entrench misconceptions and pass them to tutees [-M] — tutor errors go uncorrected without instructor oversight
- Tutors often default to telling rather than explaining; without training in questioning, the interaction degrades [-M]
- High cognitive load for novices: simultaneously managing content, pedagogy, and social interaction can overload working memory [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [~M]
- Benefits shrink when the tutor simply restates source text verbatim rather than generating explanations [~S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Teach-back**: brief end-of-session explanations to a partner; low preparation cost
- **Reciprocal tutoring**: pairs alternate tutor/tutee roles across problems, distributing load
- **Teaching expectancy**: learners study "to teach" but the session is replaced by another task — useful when tutor quality cannot be assured
- **Jigsaw**: each member masters one segment and teaches it to the group

### Target Learners
- Learners with moderate prior knowledge — enough to explain, not so much that teaching is redundant [~M]
- Struggling learners benefit as tutees receiving one-on-one explanation; they benefit as tutors only with strong scaffolding
- Less suitable for complete novices, who lack the knowledge base to generate accurate explanations

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding and self-explanation of relationships, not just recall
- Metacognitive monitoring: teaching exposes "known unknowns" when explanations stall
- Communication and organization of knowledge for an audience

### Instructions
1. Assign the material and a clear audience ("teach X to your partner so they can solve Y").
2. Give structured preparation time with source material and an explanation outline ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md)).
3. Have learners teach in pairs or small groups, using a protocol that requires questions and checks for understanding ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md)).
4. Close with a reflection step where tutors identify what they could not explain well and revisit it ([Reflection](../elements/reflection.md)).
5. Verify learning with an individual assessment so accountability does not rest on the tutoring interaction alone.

## Examples
- **Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS)** — widely researched reciprocal tutoring program for reading and math in K–12 classrooms ([https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs/regions/southeast/plc/PALS.asp](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/edlabs/regions/southeast/plc/PALS.asp)).
- **Jigsaw Classroom** ([https://www.jigsaw.org](https://www.jigsaw.org)) — Aronson's cooperative structure where each student teaches their segment to their home group.
- **Teach-back in medical education** — trainees explain a procedure or concept back to an instructor, who diagnoses gaps before certification.
