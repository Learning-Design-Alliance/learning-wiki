Theory-guided content revisions for adolescent mindset interventions: strategies over hard work, prosocial motives, peer norms, reactance, and self-persuasion¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Beyond A/B-tested changes, the redesign applied psychological theory in five ways: emphasizing "strategies," not just "hard work"; adding prosocial, beyond-the-self motives to address a culture of independence; aligning norms by showing that peers everywhere endorse growth; harnessing adolescent reactance by framing the message as resistance to adult labeling; and increasing writing opportunities for self-persuasion via saying-is-believing. These changes were not individually A/B tested but were built into the revised materials evaluated in Study 1.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Grounding each revision in an existing psychological theory or process model
  • Qualitative knowledge of the target population's values and concerns

Constraints¶

  • These theoretical changes were not subjected to A/B testing individually, so their separate contributions are unknown

Target Learners¶

  • Adolescents entering high school, including students from working-class and racial/ethnic minority backgrounds

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Adoption of a growth mindset and internalization of the message

Claims¶

  • In the pilot A/B experiments, indirect framing outperformed direct framing, refuting the fixed mindset backfired for strong fixed-mindset holders, and celebrity endorsements increased adoption [+W]
  • In Study 1, students rated the revised intervention as more interesting and more informative than the original intervention [+W]
  • In Study 1, the revised intervention reduced fixed-trait attributions and performance-avoidance goals relative to the original, with small effects [+W]
  • In Study 1, the revised growth mindset intervention reduced choosing easy over hard math problems more than the original intervention (d=.19) [+W]
  • In Study 1, the revised intervention reduced hypothetical choice of the easy math assignment from 60% to 51% relative to the original (d=.19) [+W]
