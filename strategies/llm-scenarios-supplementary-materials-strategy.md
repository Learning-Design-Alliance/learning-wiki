# Use expert-reviewed, prompt-standardized LLM-generated scenarios as supplementary materials in simulation-based learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using LLM-generated clinical scenarios, produced with standardized prompts specifying "the target learner level, clinical context, expected competencies, and word limit", as supplementary teaching materials supporting clinical reasoning and active learning. Positive reception from students and expert educators suggests that, guided by thoughtful prompt design and expert oversight, such scenarios may be useful supplements. The authors stress expert review and careful model selection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardized prompts aligned to curriculum competencies; expert clinician review of generated scenarios; blinding/coding when comparing outputs
#### Constraints
- Feasibility and learning outcomes were not evaluated; findings are preliminary, single-center, Turkish-language, and small-sample

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- active learning
- application of theoretical knowledge to clinical cases
