# Use MetaLight in lectures as a visual aid and in exercises and assignments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article identifies two main ways of using MetaLight: as a visual aid during lectures, and as a tool to be used during exercises and assignments. If students can view the application via a projector or desktop-sharing software, a lecturer can talk students through the relationship between individual studies and the resulting forest plot and heterogeneity statistics, discussing the 'anatomy' of a forest plot including visual recognition of heterogeneity through non-overlapping confidence intervals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Silverlight plug-in installed and internet access available
- For lecture use, students must be able to view the application via a projector or software such as Elluminate that enable users to 'share' desktop displays
#### Constraints
- The teaching materials presented focus on the standardised mean difference, though the application supports a range of types of effect size

### Target Learners
- higher education students in research methods courses
- student dissertation writers

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the anatomy of a forest plot
- recognising heterogeneity visually
- understanding the importance of different studies as represented by square size
