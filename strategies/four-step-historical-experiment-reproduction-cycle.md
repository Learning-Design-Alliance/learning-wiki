# Reproduce historical experiments through a four-step cycle: read the original manuscript, perform the experiment, interpret, then compare with Newton's interpretation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy structures each historical experiment replication in four steps: reading a passage from the original scientist's manuscripts describing the experiment "typically including the result of the experiment (but without its interpretation)"; performing the experiment with available lab resources; interpreting the results; and comparing with the original interpretation followed by discussion. A cumulative discussion of results was added as experiments were completed, and no experiment was undertaken unless the previous one was completed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the original scientist's manuscripts or faithful reproductions
- Laboratory equipment and materials for the historical experiments
- Students willing to build interpretations before seeing the historical ones
#### Constraints
- Experiments were not necessarily performed in the laboratory; sometimes they were conducted outside under direct sunlight or with artificial light sources

### Target Learners
- advanced high-school or undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental design and interpretation
- historical understanding of scientific discovery
