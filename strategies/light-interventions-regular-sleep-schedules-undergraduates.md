# Light-based interventions and education about regular sleep schedules to advance circadian rhythms and improve sleep regularity in undergraduates

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's authors propose that irregular sleep in undergraduates could be treated through light interventions used to advance circadian rhythms, together with education about the importance of regular sleep schedules. They note that "Adoption of a stable sleep pattern would regulate light/dark cycles, reinforcing regular behavior", and that "effective light interventions could easily be developed at low user burden". The proposal rests on the modeling result that light exposure patterns largely explain the circadian delay of Irregular sleepers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Further experiments are needed to identify factors that predispose individuals to adopting irregular sleep, as the authors state.
#### Constraints
- The data cannot be used to make causal inferences; the authors note the question of whether imposing a regular schedule improves sleep, health and performance should be revisited experimentally.

### Target Learners
- college undergraduates with irregular sleep patterns

### Target Learning Goals
- improving sleep regularity, circadian timing, and academic performance
