# Use survey findings on graduate student difficulties to inform first-year graduate quantum instruction and research-based tutorial design

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 11 claims (11 for) · 5 studies (4 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors of first-year graduate quantum courses use knowledge of incoming students' shared conceptual difficulties to shape instruction, and reports developing quantum interactive learning tutorials that incorporate paper-and-pencil tasks and computer simulations. The stated goal is to "actively engage students in the learning process and help them build links between the formalism and the conceptual aspects of quantum physics" without compromising technical content. The survey results can also inform undergraduate quantum curriculum design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need awareness of the specific difficulties documented by the survey, such as overgeneralizing stationary-state properties and confusing real space with Hilbert space.
#### Constraints
- The article states the tutorials were under development and assessment at the time of writing, intended as supplements to lectures and standard homework assignments in advanced undergraduate courses.

### Target Learners
- first-year graduate physics students
- advanced undergraduate quantum mechanics students

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of quantum mechanics formalism, measurement, and time-development
