# Provide ongoing in-service training and age-tailored programs to strengthen nurses' infection-prevention knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that health institutions and the Ministry of Health prioritise regular in-service training and workshops on nosocomial infection prevention, since attendance was strongly associated with knowledge, and additionally design training tailored to older nurses' needs. The authors state healthcare institutions "should provide regular opportunities for nurses to attend such training sessions" because they "serve as reinforcement mechanisms for existing knowledge" and improve understanding and implementation of NCI prevention measures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular scheduled opportunities for nurses to attend in-service training or workshops on infection prevention
#### Constraints
- Evidence for the strategy comes from a cross-sectional design, so a causal pathway between training and knowledge cannot be established; the article also calls for qualitative research to understand underlying factors

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses in Ghanaian health facilities, with particular attention to nurses aged 41–60

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge of nosocomial infection preventive measures, including hand hygiene, glove use, waste disposal, disinfection, and diseases transmitted by needle-stick injury
