# Case‐based learning, impairment simulations and outreach placements for gerodontology teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
To make gerodontology learning clinically relevant, the article recommends case‐based learning strategies with varying levels of problem orientation, plus interactive elements. It states: "Interactive elements like simulations of ageing‐related impairments [39], role‐playing with standardised patients, and training in preventive strategies for DOP." Additionally, "clinical‐practical teaching activities at external locations, such as day centres, nursing homes and geriatric hospitals, must be integrated into the curriculum" to provide hands‐on learning in realistic care settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration into the curriculum so learning becomes relevant and available for use in a clinical context
#### Constraints
- Varying levels of problem orientation may be suitable in different situations

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate dental students

### Target Learning Goals
- Addressing the unique needs of care‐dependent older people in realistic care settings
