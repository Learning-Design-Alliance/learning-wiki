# Use QMFPS responses as formative assessment to identify difficulties and guide instructional design

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends administering the validated QMFPS before and after instruction so instructors can benchmark their class against the published baseline data, identify common student difficulties with quantum formalism and postulates, and use those response patterns to design instructional strategies and learning tools. It works as a low-stakes graded quiz so students take it seriously, with data interpreted for the class as a whole.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should receive some credit for completing the survey in order for students to take it seriously
- Relevant concepts must be covered in the course before using it as a post-test
#### Constraints
- It should not be administered as a high-stakes test and the data should be interpreted for the class as a whole

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate quantum mechanics students
- graduate quantum mechanics students

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying and addressing student difficulties with quantum formalism and postulates
