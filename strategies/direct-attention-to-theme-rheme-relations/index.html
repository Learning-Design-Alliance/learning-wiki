Direct students' attention to relating Themes and Rhemes to preceding and succeeding text when writing in English¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article concludes that "pedagogical efforts are needed to direct students' attention to properly relating Theme or Rheme to preceding and succeeding Themes and Rhemes to hold onto important information when writing in English." This strategy shifts focus from sentence-level grammar to discourse-level information flow, implemented in the study through ten 50-minute sessions of explicit TP instruction over 10 weeks.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Explicit instruction in TP patterns delivered over multiple sessions; the article notes TP instruction "required time for instruction results to emerge"

Constraints¶

  • Effects were demonstrated with Chinese college English majors on TOEFL-style essay topics over a 10-week period; the design was quasi-experimental with intact classes

Target Learners¶

  • Chinese college students learning English academic writing

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Using thematic progression patterns to develop coherent English essays

Affordances¶

  • Thematic Progression Patterns Framework

Claims¶

  • Before instruction, Chinese college students used fewer progression-forming Themes and more new and contextual Themes than native speakers [+W]
  • After TP instruction, the experimental group's linear progression use no longer differed significantly from native speakers' [+W]
  • The experimental group moved closer to native speakers in constant progression, though less significantly than in linear progression or new Themes [+W]
  • After instruction, the experimental group used significantly more linear progressions and fewer new Themes than the control group [+W]
  • Instruction in thematic progression significantly increased Chinese college students' use of linear progressions between pre- and post-essays [+W]
  • Instruction in thematic progression significantly reduced Chinese college students' use of new Themes between pre- and post-essays [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Guide reading with targeted questions that direct attention to grammatical, lexical and structural features needed for the writing task
