Plan geography course topics in accordance with the place-based teaching approach to develop science process skills¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Among the article's suggestions for researchers and teachers, it recommends that "The topics of the geography course can be planned and conducted in accordance with the place based teaching approach as it develops science process skills." It further suggests the approach can be applied at every level from pre-school to undergraduate, and can be used by teachers and academics because it is effective and applicable in other courses.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Out-of-class activities such as sightseeing, observation and group work to support the place-based teaching activities

Constraints¶

  • Students reported time and cost problems with out-of-class implementation

Target Learners¶

  • classroom teacher candidates
  • students from pre-school to undergraduate level

Target Learning Goals¶

  • science process skills
  • academic achievement
  • community building

Claims¶

  • The control group showed no significant pre-test to post-test change under the current teaching program [+W]
  • The experimental group's science process skills improved significantly from pre-test to post-test after four weeks of place-based teaching [+W]
  • Teacher candidates expressed mostly positive opinions of place-based lessons, citing retention of information most often, with time and cost as main drawbacks [+W]
  • Students suggested improvements to place-based teaching including visiting more places, applying it in other courses, and increasing allocated time [+W]
