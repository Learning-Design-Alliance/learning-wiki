# Adopt a trial-based, WTP-threshold cost-effectiveness framework (PRECOG steps with net benefit regression) to choose between surgical simulation training strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a four-step economic evaluation for training programs: gather training outcome data, assess total cost split into capital and recurring categories, calculate ICERs, and estimate cost-effectiveness probability via net benefit regression across willingness-to-pay values. The authors state, "We recommend our generalized approach to cost-effectiveness analysis according to established education research methodology and contemporary economic concepts," so stakeholders can identify the most favorable strategy with available resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cost data must be collected for all actual components of setting up and implementing the training curriculum, categorized as capital and recurring cost
#### Constraints
- Adequately powered economic evaluations often require much larger samples than were available; the authors propose estimation rather than hypothesis testing as a pragmatic approach

### Target Learners
- novice ophthalmology resident trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical competency in phacoemulsification cataract surgery
