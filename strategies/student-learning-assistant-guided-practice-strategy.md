# Deploying a high-performing former student as an in-class peer teaching assistant during guided practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study operationalized peer teaching by embedding a female student teaching assistant who had previously taken the course and "scored >95 %" into the experimental group's classes. During the instructor's guided practice phase she followed up with students, "explaining, giving hints, and even teaching whenever it is necessary". This implements the discussion-groups type of peer teaching, in which small groups guided by a student teaching assistant supplement large lectures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A peer teacher who previously completed the course successfully and scored above 95%
- Ongoing supervision so the teacher is available to solve problems arising between students
#### Constraints
- The article states generalization is limited to using the Student Learning Groups model of peer teaching and to the two textbook chapters studied

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students weak in mathematics (midterm scores 8-12 out of 20)

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving mathematics academic achievement
