Use repeated computer-based map-game play as an engaging practice routine for social studies achievement¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends computer-based geography games as classroom tools: "The use of Sheppardsoftware.com to teach the 50 states proved an extremely effective tool that may be used in the classroom or by students at home," and concludes such games "should be considered for use in the social studies classroom." The routine is nine trials of game play across three consecutive days, with students recording and saving scores after each set of trials.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to computers and the selected game, plus a routine of repeated trials with score recording

Constraints¶

  • The article raises implementation barriers cited from prior work, including curriculum integration challenges, technical requirements, and lack of teacher training

Target Learners¶

  • middle school social studies students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • acquisition of geographic knowledge of the 50 U.S. states and increased motivation for social studies learning

Claims¶

  • Repeated play of computer-based geography games over nine trials significantly improves percent correct, distance error, and completion time for seventh-graders [+M]
  • Students rated both map games favorably, with an overall weighted mean of 2.34 on the writing prompt [+W]
  • Students with disabilities improved on the map games in both conditions, with the experimental group gaining 28.33 percentage points in percent correct [+W]
  • Students initially struggled with game questions but acquired the concepts through repeated play [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use a baseline 50-states pretest to inform differentiated geography instruction, and do not rely on CMT spatial-reasoning scores for placement
  • Classroom Games
  • Games + Structured Activity
