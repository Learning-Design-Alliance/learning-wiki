# Actively address status hierarchies through the multiple abilities intervention and the assigning competence intervention.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The essay describes two interventions to foster full participation and disrupt status hierarchies. The multiple abilities intervention "broadens students' conceptions of what it means to be 'smart' in biology" by framing intelligence as a range of scientific skills, emphasizing that no student possesses all of them but everyone possesses some. The assigning competence intervention "involves publicly acknowledging a student's intellectual contribution to a task," elevating the status of students whose abilities may be overlooked.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must identify all of the skills necessary for successful completion of the group task and rely on student trust in the instructor's opinion
#### Constraints
- Assigning competence relies on student trust in the opinion of the instructor
