Adopt student-centered innovative learning models and build teacher capacity to implement them in mathematics and statistics¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the pooled effect, the article recommends that educators "adopt innovative learning models such as problem-based learning, blended learning, and metaverse-based instruction", applied through student-centered and inquiry-based strategies. It further recommends professional development programs equipping teachers with the pedagogical and technological competencies to implement these models effectively, and policymaker support via curriculum revision and digital-infrastructure investment for equitable access.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Professional development programs to equip teachers with pedagogical and technological competencies
  • Investment in digital infrastructure to facilitate emerging technologies and ensure equitable access

Constraints¶

  • The evidence base is limited by a small number of studies, particularly for statistical critical thinking

Target Learners¶

  • mathematics and statistics students at all educational levels

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical thinking skills

Claims¶

  • Innovative learning models produce a large pooled effect on mathematical and statistical critical thinking compared with conventional instruction [+M]
  • Effect sizes across the included studies are highly heterogeneous (I² = 91.37%), indicating moderating factors [~W]
  • Individual study effect sizes range from negative to very strong, with one study showing no effect [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use expert appropriateness assessment of a designed learning model as the basis for further system development and future outcome studies
