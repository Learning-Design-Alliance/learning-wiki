# Structure class time around interactive group work with rotating group assignments to break down barriers between students of different backgrounds

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's findings support incorporating collaborative group work into class structure and deliberately rotating group membership. A Study 3 student suggested instructors change how in-class groups form by "assigning people to different groups and having rotations where people aren't always stuck with the same group", so students meet peers with different backgrounds and break down barriers. In Study 1, faculty de-emphasized lecture in favor of group work and interaction, which students reported enjoying and finding useful for their learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must deliberately build group work and interaction into course structure rather than leaving collaboration to individual instructor choice
#### Constraints
- In Studies 2 and 3, use of active learning and group work was the choice of individual instructors rather than a standardized departmental practice

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics majors
- students from historically marginalized groups in physics

### Target Learning Goals
- fostering peer collaboration and supportive classroom community
- improving equity and inclusion in physics courses
