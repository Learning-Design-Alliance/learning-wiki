# Cultivate Understanding and Respect Across Languages and Dialects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy makes content accessible across linguistic variation while positioning every learner's language repertoire as an asset. Instructors provide translations of key terms, pair language with visuals, gestures, and real-world examples, and explicitly treat dialectal and home-language variation as legitimate rather than deficient. The dual aim is comprehension of academic content and the development of respect for linguistic diversity.

## Design Implications

Comprehension improves when verbal information is supported by nonverbal representation [Dual coding improves recall.](../claims/dual-coding-improves-recall.md) [+S], so pairing key terms with images, gestures, and demonstrations reduces the extraneous load that unfamiliar language imposes [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+M]. Equally important is the affective dimension: classroom practices that affirm students' linguistic identities improve intergroup attitudes and belonging [Building empathy improves intergroup attitudes.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M] [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. Translanguaging approaches — inviting learners to draw on their full linguistic repertoire — support content learning in the new language because skills transfer across languages rather than remaining isolated [Cummins, 1979](#key-sources) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Translation resources or multilingual glossaries for key terms and concepts
- Visual aids, realia, and gesture routines that carry meaning independently of the language of instruction
- Instructor knowledge of learners' linguistic backgrounds and culturally responsive pedagogy
- Norms that frame dialect variation as systematic and rule-governed, not as error

#### Constraints
- Translation alone does not build proficiency; over-reliance can reduce learners' engagement with the target language [-W]
- Visuals that are decorative rather than meaning-bearing add load without aiding comprehension [Decorative illustrations do not improve learning.](../claims/decorative-illustrations-do-not-improve-learning.md) [-S]
- Poorly framed "respect" activities can single out multilingual learners and backfire on belonging [~M]
- Requires instructor time and, for less-commonly-taught languages, resources that may not exist

#### Implementation Variability
- Full translanguaging classrooms vs. strategic first-language support for key terms only
- Peer-mediated support: multilingual learners glossary-build and explain terms to peers
- Discipline-specific variants: labeled diagrams in science, cognate awareness in literacy, gesture routines in early grades

### Target Learners
- Multilingual learners and speakers of non-dominant dialects, who benefit when home-language knowledge is activated rather than suppressed [Activation improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Monolingual learners, who gain intercultural competence from exposure to linguistic diversity [Building empathy improves intergroup attitudes.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M]
- All levels (K–12, higher education, adult education); strongest effects for learners still developing academic language in the language of instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- Content learning through a second language or non-dominant dialect
- Academic vocabulary development in the language of instruction
- Cultural awareness and positive intergroup attitudes
- Metalinguistic awareness: understanding how languages and dialects vary systematically

### Instructions
1. Identify the high-leverage terms and concepts of the lesson and prepare translations, cognates, and visual representations for each ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
2. Open by activating learners' relevant home-language and cultural knowledge so new content connects to what they already know ([Activation](../elements/activation.md))
3. Present new concepts with paired verbal and visual channels — say it, show it, gesture it ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md), [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md))
4. Use [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) grounded in learners' everyday linguistic and cultural experience to bridge unfamiliar terms
5. Structure [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) norms that welcome multiple languages and dialects, with explicit teaching that dialect variation is rule-governed
6. Check understanding through non-language-dependent means (labeled diagrams, gestures, sorting tasks) before assessing in extended prose ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md))

## Examples
- **WIDA standards framework** (https://wida.wisc.edu) — U.S. consortium providing can-do descriptors and multilingual learner supports that operationalize asset-based language pedagogy across content areas.
- **Translanguaging pedagogy in New York City schools** — CUNY-NYSIEB (https://www.cuny-nysieb.org) worked with schools to adopt translanguaging practices, using students' full linguistic repertoires in instruction.
- **Science vocabulary with dual-language glossaries** — teachers pair each unit's key terms with home-language translations and labeled diagrams, so learners build concepts in both languages simultaneously.
- **Dialect-aware literacy instruction** — programs such as the Academic English Mastery Program (Los Angeles Unified) contrast features of African American English with Standard Academic English explicitly, treating contrastive analysis rather than error correction.
