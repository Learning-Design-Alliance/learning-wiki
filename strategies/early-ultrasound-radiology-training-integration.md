# Integrate ultrasound and radiological training early into medical degree programs, combining practical and theoretical cross-sectional imaging teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "students should receive radiological training at an early stage of their studies to benefit from the improved skills as early as possible." It argues that a combination of different teaching methods incorporating different cross-sectional image modalities, including ultrasound, is advantageous because combining practical and theoretical components enables multidimensional, dynamic learning of cross-sectional image representations. It also suggests ultrasound courses be implemented in anatomy training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Voluntary or curricular ultrasound courses with hands-on practice on ultrasound devices and theoretical preparation materials
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence comes from a single-centre prospective observational study without a control group, so randomization was not possible and generalizability may be affected

### Target Learners
- Third-year medical students
- Undergraduate medical students in anatomy and radiology training

### Target Learning Goals
- Visual-spatial ability
- Knowledge of anatomical spatial relationships
- Radiological cross-sectional image understanding
- Theoretical ultrasound competencies
