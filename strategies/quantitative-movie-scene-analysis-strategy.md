# Quantitative scene analysis: computing physical quantities from data visible in a film scene to test its plausibility

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The course's sample analyses have students extract quantities shown in a film and compute whether the depicted event is physically plausible. In the Speed 2 example, students read the ship's speedometer at clock times and compute deceleration as speed change over time change, comparing it with the acceleration of gravity; in the Armageddon example they make reasonable approximations using numbers provided in the film to estimate the deflection of the asteroid pieces.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A scene must display readable quantitative data (such as a speedometer with clock times) or provide numbers usable for approximation
#### Constraints
- The Armageddon analysis deliberately ignores the energy needed to break the asteroid into two pieces as a simplifying approximation

### Target Learners
- college and university general-education physical science students

### Target Learning Goals
- applying conservation of energy, momentum, vector addition, and gravitation
- making reasonable approximations
- estimating physical quantities
