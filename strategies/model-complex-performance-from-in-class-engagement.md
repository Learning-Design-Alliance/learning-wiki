# Model student performance on complex activities starting from in-class engagement measures rather than video clickstream features

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A research-oriented strategy: the authors suggest that 'a good model of student performance on complex activities, like the laboratory assessments in this course, likely starts from other forms of engagement in the course', specifically activities which designers intend for students to engage with in-class, rather than from summary video clickstream features. This redirects measurement effort toward in-class engagement in flipped courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to measures of student engagement with in-class course activities
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a null result for video clickstream features in one introductory mechanics course; offered as a suggestion for future modeling work

### Target Learners
- undergraduates in introductory mechanics courses with laboratory components

### Target Learning Goals
- predicting performance on complex laboratory assessments
