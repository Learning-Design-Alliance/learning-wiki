# Use Particle Dobble as a discussion starter or reflective activity following a lesson on fundamental particles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Educators may deploy the game as a discussion starter or as a reflective activity after a lesson on fundamental particles, in upper secondary and introductory undergraduate courses as well as informal settings such as science festivals and outreach events. The playful format invites students to let go of overly concrete mental models before encountering the formal mathematical framework of quantum mechanics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need only a basic familiarity with atomic structure; no formal background in quantum mechanics or particle physics is assumed
#### Constraints
- The game is designed as an optional learning aid, an additional opportunity for getting in touch with less everyday topics, not a primary quantum mechanics lesson

### Target Learners
- upper secondary school students
- introductory undergraduate students
- informal learning audiences

### Target Learning Goals
- building conceptual foundations for particle physics
- preparing students for formal quantum mechanics instruction

## Claims

- [The speeded spot-and-name mechanic of Particle Dobble is intended to give repeated retrieval practice on particle names and families](../claims/spot-and-name-retrieval-practice-mechanic.md) [+W]
- [Particle Dobble's arbitrary, non-literal symbols are designed to dispel the Newtonian misconception that elementary particles are solid spheres](../claims/symbolic-icons-refute-solid-sphere-misconception.md) [+W]
