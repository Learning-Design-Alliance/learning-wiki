# Use AI-simulated audiences to give students safe, repeated low-stakes communication practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends AI-simulated audiences as a substitute for infeasible real-world practice: "authentic communication practice requires repeated low-stakes interactions with diverse audiences, but traditional methods like community outreach or peer evaluation cannot feasibly deliver within a single semester." During the Elaborate phase, students "gained a safe space to practice their communication strategies... as AI-simulated audiences allowed them to test their messages without real-world pressure."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to AI tools capable of simulating audience interactions and providing feedback on student messages
#### Constraints
- Studied in a medical science communication course; students could also independently access publicly available AI tools outside class, which the authors could not completely exclude

### Target Learners
- medical students
- science communication learners

### Target Learning Goals
- audience-appropriate message design
- communication strategy testing
- revision based on audience response
