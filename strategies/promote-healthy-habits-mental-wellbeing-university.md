# Promote healthy dietary habits and mental well-being among university students as a lever for academic success

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's conclusion urges higher-education policymakers and educators to act on the observed associations: "Educators should plan for interventions, activities, and initiatives to promote a combination of healthy lifestyle and mental well-being." Concretely this means campus programs encouraging regular breakfast and healthy snacking, fewer meals eaten out, and psychological support services, given that distress was the strongest negative correlate of achievement in the regression. The recommendation rests on cross-sectional, self-reported data, so causal claims are not established.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Campus-level programs addressing diet (breakfast, snacks, eating out) and mental health support services
#### Constraints
- Evidence is cross-sectional and relies on a subjective, not Arabic-validated, academic achievement scale; the sample came from a single private Lebanese university

### Target Learners
- university students in Lebanon
- higher-education students generally

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement
- mental well-being
- healthy lifestyle habits
