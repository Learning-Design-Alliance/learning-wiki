# Use effective-potential orbit problems as accessible undergraduate research topics in general relativity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The article recommends identifying applications of general relativity that are accessible to undergraduates as independent study or research contributions: "Problems that involve solving for the behavior of test particles, light rays, and gyroscopes are examples, as are questions involving linear gravitational waves, black holes, and simple cosmological models." Because the 'physics first' approach treats applications first, it enables undergraduate participation in research on topics of contemporary interest within limited time frames.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need intermediate mechanics so orbit calculations are exercises in mechanics
#### Constraints
- Problems must be conceptually and technically accessible and completable in the limited time frame typically available

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics majors

### Target Learning Goals
- research skills in gravitational physics, independent study of relativistic applications
