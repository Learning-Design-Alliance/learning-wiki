# Include all provider types, simulation, legible ACP materials, and one-to-one field coaching in future care-home ACP training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that future ACP training programs in care homes adopt five characteristics: broad inclusion of service providers, simulation activities, user-friendly ACP education material, one-to-one field coaching, and consistent diverse educational initiatives. They state programs should "involve more service providers such as nursing assistants, social workers, service managers, superintendents, and other social and health service providers" and "implement simulation activities such as role play and case scenarios".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Legible, user-friendly ACP education material as a teaching tool and implementation strategy
- One-to-one field coaching that reinforces knowledge, skills, and motivation in ACP
#### Constraints
- Given high turnover rates in most residential care homes, diverse educational initiatives should be implemented consistently to strengthen workforce capacity

### Target Learners
- All residential care home providers, including health assistants, nursing assistants, social workers, service managers, and superintendents

### Target Learning Goals
- ACP engagement and uptake as part of routine clinical practice
- Workforce capacity in ACP
