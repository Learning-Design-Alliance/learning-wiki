# Identify Feelings

## Description
Students identify, label, or match facial expressions and feelings. Students categorize feeling words by the type of feeling. Literature is used to have students analyze characters’ feelings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Emotion charts, picture cards, literature selections, and class time for discussion and activities.
#### Constraints
- Some students may have difficulty connecting with abstract concepts of emotions, especially if they have limited experience or trauma. It's important to create a safe and supportive environment.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- K-12 learners, especially those who struggle with identifying their own and others' emotions

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Identify emotions, categorize feelings, analyze characters' feelings in literature

### Affordances
- Provides a structured way for students to recognize and name feelings, which is a foundational step in emotional regulation. Using literature helps students connect emotions with real-life situations.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted by providing different levels of support, such as matching simple emotions for younger learners or analyzing complex emotions for older learners. Literature choices can be tailored to students' interests and cultural backgrounds.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe students' ability to accurately identify and label emotions in themselves and others. Track their participation in discussions and activities related to analyzing characters' feelings.

## Impact
- Students who can identify and label their emotions are better able to manage them, which can lead to improved academic performance, social relationships, and overall well-being. Research supports the connection between emotional intelligence and positive outcomes.
