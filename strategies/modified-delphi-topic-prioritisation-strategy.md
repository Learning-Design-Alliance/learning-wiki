# Use a two-round modified Delphi survey to prioritise e-learning topics separately for students and educators

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
An expert panel lists existing curriculum topics into a Round 1 questionnaire where participants select topics needing RLOs by checkbox; selection percentages are computed separately for students and educators, and the first quartile of most-selected topics from each group advances to Round 2. In Round 2, participants rate each topic on a 5-point Likert scale (1-not a priority to 5-essential) without knowing which party selected it, and mean scores are compared between groups. The method offers "anonymity and confidentiality" so students can prioritise "free from peer pressure and educators' dominance".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Existing curriculum learning outcomes available to build the Round 1 topic list; separate analysis of student and educator data so both parties' opinions are considered
#### Constraints
- Low educator numbers may not reflect the true needs of educators; low Round 1 consensus (below 50%) indicates weak agreement on topics; results are expected to differ across institutions because curricula and needs vary

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate healthcare students who have completed the specific course

### Target Learning Goals
- Prioritising curriculum topics for development of interactive e-learning objects focused on a single learning goal
