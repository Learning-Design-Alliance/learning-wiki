# Deliver experiential healthy-eating activities through flexible, hands-on sensory play with real food in informal settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 12 claims (9 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 12 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementation approach was a bank of EL activities that staff could select and adapt to each session's cohort, rather than prescribed activities. Staff reported the activities "were simple and easy key messages as well" and fitted their normal operations. Caregivers and staff identified sensory play with real fruits and vegetables — touching, feeling, smelling, tasting, stamping and float-or-sink experiments — as the most enjoyable activities. Future iterations should add taste-testing, take-home caregiver materials and age-separated activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Skilled educators able to choose and adapt activities for the children present
- Alignment with existing exhibits and daily schedule
#### Constraints
- Flexibility may have contributed to key messages being omitted in 40% of observed sessions

### Target Learners
- children aged 0-10 years and their caregivers in a children's museum

### Target Learning Goals
- healthy eating
- physical activity
