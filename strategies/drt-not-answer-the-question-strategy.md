# Allow students to justify not answering written DRT questions, describing what they actually did instead

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because UCL encourages students to deviate from the laboratory script and create their own experiments, the DRT instructions permit students who could not answer a written question to "explain what you did, what you found and what you think about it". Students may answer a different question if they can justify it, provided the relevant events are recorded in the notebook. This develops the skill of defending work and decisions to markers and gives students 'permission to fail'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The relevant activities and difficulties must be recorded in the laboratory notebook
- The allowance applies only to written answer questions
#### Constraints
- Students are told the DRT is the only time they can answer a question that is not on the test, if they can justify this

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- defending experimental work and decisions to others
- developing a divergent experimental mindset
