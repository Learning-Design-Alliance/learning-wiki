# Task Management for Writing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Task management for writing is a [self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) support that helps students break a writing assignment into planned subtasks (brainstorming, outlining, drafting, revising), allocate time to each, and manage distractions during work sessions. It is typically carried out through explicit planning conversations, written task schedules, and agreed routines for breaks and focus. The facilitator's role is to externalize the management function early and progressively hand it to the student.

## Design Implications

Writing is a long-duration, multi-component task that imposes heavy demands on working memory and motivation; students who lack management strategies often procrastinate, then draft without planning, producing weaker text [Writing performance improves when students are taught to manage the writing process rather than only the product.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]. Teaching students to set process goals (e.g., "draft the introduction by Thursday") outperforms outcome goals ("get an A on the essay") for novices [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]. Self-monitoring of progress against a plan improves both regulation and output [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A concrete writing assignment with visible subtasks and deadlines
- A facilitator or structured tool for initial planning ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) or a planning template)
- A way for students to record and check progress (checklist, calendar, or task board)
- An environment or agreement about managing distractions (device norms, break timing)

#### Constraints
- Effectiveness drops when plans are imposed without student input; students follow self-set plans more reliably than assigned ones [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Over-scaffolding can create dependence — students who always receive the plan may not internalize management; supports must be faded [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]
- Students with very low self-efficacy may abandon plans after the first missed milestone; plans should include early, achievable wins [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Rigid time-boxing can fragment drafting flow for experienced writers; management support is most valuable for novices and long projects

#### Implementation Variability
- Whole-class planning sessions vs. individual [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) conferences
- Digital tools (Trello, Google Tasks, [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)-style progress dashboards) vs. paper planners
- Teacher-set milestone schedules vs. student-negotiated schedules with checkpoints
- For students with learning and thinking differences, embed management supports as formal [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md) (chunked deadlines, distraction-reduced work time)

### Target Learners
- Novice writers who treat an essay as one monolithic task and default to last-minute drafting [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]
- Students with executive function challenges, ADHD, or learning differences who need externalized structure [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Less beneficial for experienced writers who already regulate their own process; imposed management can add overhead [Guidance that helps novices can hinder learners with developed strategies.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-regulation: planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's own writing process
- Procedural fluency: internalizing the stages of the writing process
- Persistence: completing long-duration tasks and reducing procrastination [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. [State objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md) — make the assignment's purpose and final criteria explicit before planning begins.
2. Decompose the task with the student into subtasks (prewriting, outlining, drafting, revising, editing) using [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) to keep each unit manageable [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M].
3. Set process goals and deadlines for each subtask, negotiated with the student rather than imposed [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M].
4. Agree on work-session routines: session length, break timing, and distraction-management rules (devices away, defined workspace).
5. During work sessions, have students self-monitor progress against the plan and record deviations [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].
6. Hold brief [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) check-ins at milestones; give feedback on the *process* (planning quality, time use), not just the text [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
7. Fade the scaffolds — shift from teacher-generated plans to student-generated plans with lighter check-ins [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

## Examples
- **SRSD (Self-Regulated Strategy Development)** — Graham and Harris's widely validated writing framework explicitly teaches goal-setting, self-monitoring, and self-instruction alongside genre strategies; students graph their own progress across drafts ([What Works Clearinghouse practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/3)).
- **Purdue OWL + planning calendar** — Instructors using the [Purdue Online Writing Lab](https://owl.purdue.edu) pair its process-based writing guides with a student-completed milestone calendar for research papers.
- **Middle school IEP support** — A special education teacher breaks a five-paragraph essay into daily micro-deadlines with a visual checklist, a formal accommodation for students with executive function challenges.
