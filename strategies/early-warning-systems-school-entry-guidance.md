# Implement comprehensive early-warning evaluation systems before recommending early school entry

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that decisions about flexible age of entry into compulsory schooling be supported by comprehensive early assessment. It states "it seems essential to implement comprehensive and effective early-warning systems that include individualised assessments of the various areas of development in order to obtain valuable information and thus recommend (or not) an early start of compulsory primary education". Such systems should include educator-perceived skills and dynamic tests adapted to preschool stages to reveal learning potential.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Individualised assessments of various areas of development, including neurodevelopment-related aspects
- Dynamic tests adapted to preschool stages and information based on skills perceived by educators
#### Constraints
- The article derives this recommendation from residual RAE effects in eighth graders; its relevance is stated to be greatest in the initial stages of schooling

### Target Learners
- Preschool children and children at the point of entry to compulsory schooling
- Students at risk or with sociocultural disadvantages

### Target Learning Goals
- Informing parents and educators on whether to anticipate entry to the school system
