Use corroboration across multiple sources, including lateral reading, when lacking relevant prior knowledge¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article presents corroboration—verifying the accuracy of information using other information sources—as a strategy readers can apply when their content knowledge is insufficient. As printed, "Corroboration can be considered an essential strategy for laypersons who lack relevant prior knowledge about a specific issue." By corroborating across multiple sources, readers can check whether claims align with expert consensus, and professional fact-checkers practice this through lateral reading, opening additional web resources in separate tabs to cross-reference sources.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to multiple online information sources that can be cross-referenced
  • Awareness that one's own prior knowledge may be insufficient for direct evaluation

Constraints¶

  • The article describes corroboration as essential for laypersons lacking relevant prior knowledge about a specific issue; it does not claim corroboration alone suffices for all evaluation
