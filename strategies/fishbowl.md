# Fishbowl

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Two learners sit facing each other in the center of the room; the remaining learners sit in a circle around them. The two central learners have a conversation based on a pre-determined topic, often deliberately practicing specific discussion skills the class is working on (asking follow-up questions, paraphrasing, elaborating on another person's point). Learners on the outside observe, take notes, or perform another discussion-related task assigned by the teacher. Variations include relay-style swapping between inner and outer circles, outer-circle "coaching" from the sidelines, and outer-circle scoring of the conversation against a rubric followed by a structured debrief.

## Design Implications

The fishbowl makes discussion itself the object of study: the inner circle models conversational moves while the outer circle analyzes them, converting tacit participation norms into observable, discussable behavior [Modeling expert performance supports observational learning.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]. Structured observation with a specific analytic task (rubric, tally of moves, note-taking guide) outperforms unstructured watching, because it directs attention and prompts self-explanation of what made a move effective [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. The debrief is where much of the learning consolidates — outer-circle observations become explicit criteria learners can apply to their own future participation [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A physical space where learners can form a circle around a central area
- A pre-determined topic and clearly named target discussion skills
- Observation guides or rubrics so outer-circle learners have an analytic task, not passive spectating
- A structured debrief in which observers give specific, respectful feedback ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))

#### Constraints
- Can be intimidating for shy or reluctant speakers placed in the inner circle; participation structures that allow entry through the outer circle first reduce this threat [-M]
- Requires careful facilitation to prevent dominance by a few learners and to keep feedback respectful [-M]
- Time-consuming relative to whole-class discussion; the observation and debrief cycle can consume an entire session for a conversation involving only a few speakers [-W]
- Observers without a concrete analytic task learn little from watching alone [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Relay fishbowl**: outer-circle learners periodically swap into the inner circle, keeping the conversation going while broadening participation
- **Coaching variation**: outer-circle members whisper or pass coaching prompts to inner speakers mid-discussion
- **Rubric variation**: observers score the conversation against a discussion-quality rubric, then report out in the debrief
- **Content fishbowl vs. skill fishbowl**: the inner conversation can target subject-matter reasoning (e.g., interpreting a text) or explicit communication skills (paraphrasing, questioning)

### Target Learners
- Learners of all ages and subject areas, particularly those developing discussion and communication skills
- Classes working on group dynamics, where observing peers makes participation norms visible [Modeling expert performance supports observational learning.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]
- Less suitable, without adaptation, for learners with high social anxiety about speaking in front of a group [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Communication skills: questioning, paraphrasing, building on others' points [Accountable talk moves improve the quality of peer dialogue.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]
- Active listening and observation: attending to others' reasoning rather than waiting to speak
- Metacognitive awareness: recognizing and naming effective conversational moves [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Peer feedback and coaching: giving specific, criterion-referenced feedback

### Instructions
1. Select a topic and name the specific discussion skills being practiced; prepare observation guides or a rubric.
2. Arrange seating: two (or three to five) learners in the inner circle, the rest surrounding them.
3. Brief the outer circle on their analytic task — tallying moves, noting paraphrases, or scoring against the rubric ([Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md)).
4. Run the inner-circle conversation while the teacher facilitates minimally, intervening only to protect norms ([Modeling](../elements/modeling.md)).
5. Optionally swap or coach mid-discussion to broaden participation ([Peer Discussion](../elements/peer-discussion.md)).
6. Debrief: outer-circle observers give specific feedback; the class names effective moves for future discussions ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md)).

## Examples
- **EL Education (formerly Expeditionary Learning)** protocols — the fishbowl is a named protocol in EL Education's structure of protocols for student-led discussion, used across K-12 expeditionary curricula. [https://eleducation.org](https://eleducation.org)
- **Face to Face Advisement / discussion-skills curricula** — middle-school advisory programs use fishbowls to teach listening and paraphrasing skills, with observers tracking how often speakers build on prior points.
- **Teacher education methods courses** — pre-service teachers observe a fishbowl of peers leading a discussion, then debrief facilitation moves, making the strategy itself a professional-development tool.
