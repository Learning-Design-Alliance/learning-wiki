# Incorporate formal video-based education curricula and structured video-based education programs into surgical training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that surgical training programs adopt formal, structured video-based education rather than relying on uncurated online content. It argues that "Structured video-based education programs and the development of more objective assessment tools can help mitigate these challenges", and that curricula should give trainees "standardized skills to evaluate online content critically". The recommendation rests on the study's findings of variable quality, low guideline adherence, and poor inter-rater agreement among less experienced viewers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured, peer-reviewed video-based educational programs and standardized reporting practices for surgical videos
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single observational study of one procedure (robotic ventral rectopexy) on one platform (YouTube); the authors state findings cannot be generalized to all surgical videos available online

### Target Learners
- surgical residents and trainees
- fellows in digestive surgery
- inexperienced surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- critical evaluation of online surgical video content
- safe acquisition of surgical technique
