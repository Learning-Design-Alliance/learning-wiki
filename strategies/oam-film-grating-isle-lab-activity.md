# ISLE investigative laboratory activity using film fork gratings to introduce quantum mechanics and OAM

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes using the low-cost film gratings and Class 2 laser pointers as an investigative science learning environment (ISLE) activity, in which instructors use open-ended questions and few instructions while students lead experiments, make hypotheses and observations, and test their own theories. The activity "can be introduced with little to no maths, making it more accessible to students from any background", with mathematics added for physics and optics-focused students. A common diffraction activity, such as determining the laser wavelength with a grating of known period, can precede the OAM activity for school students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors run the activity with more open-ended questions and few instructions, with students leading the experiments
#### Constraints
- The article notes the experiment can be performed on the scale of a table rather than across a room, which is a limitation for printed diffraction gratings

### Target Learners
- school students
- undergraduate physics and engineering students
- interdisciplinary student groups

### Target Learning Goals
- introducing quantum mechanics concepts
- developing contextualised laboratory skills
- understanding OAM
