# Coach students to flag rather than answer difficult items, return after completing the exam, and review all questions — not just missed ones — in one-on-one reviews

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that students who struggle with confidence and rumination flag difficult questions relatively quickly without marking an answer, complete the entire examination, then return to flagged items to commit to an answer. Faculty should review all questions with students during one-on-one examination reviews, not just missed items, because focusing only on missed items reinforces the first instinct fallacy and replaces metacognitive insight about content mastery.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- One-on-one examination review time with faculty covering all items
- Students willing to flag items and defer answer commitment
#### Constraints
- Answer-changing guidance is described as very nuanced and should be tailored to the individual student

### Target Learners
- anxious or lower-performing midwifery graduates prone to rumination

### Target Learning Goals
- metacognition about content mastery; reducing counterproductive rumination during testing
