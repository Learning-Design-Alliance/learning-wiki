Administer the participation style scale before organizing online discussion environments to tailor facilitation to learner styles¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends using the scale diagnostically before discussion design. The authors state: "Instructors and researchers can determine learners' participation styles before organizing discussion environments, and learners who think and gain awareness about their own participation styles can manage their discussion and learning processes more effectively." The scale's Why and How dimensions can be applied separately or together, with 5-point Likert scoring from 0 (not suitable for me at all) to 4 (very suitable for me).

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The scale requires learners who have participated in online instructional discussions; it is administered in Turkish in this study

Constraints¶

  • The authors recommend repeating the study with more diverse student groups and adapting it to different cultures and languages, since the dimensions may give different results in different cultures

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate and graduate students in online courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • diagnosing participation styles to inform discussion design and learner self-management

Affordances¶

  • Participation style why/how dimensions (no wiki page)

Claims¶

  • Confirmatory factor analysis supports a second-level participation style model with four combined styles and acceptable fit indices [+W]
  • The scale's internal consistency is acceptable, with Cronbach's alpha of 0.89 for the full scale and 0.70–0.85 for its four style factors [+W]
  • Focus group interviews with active and non-participating (lurking) students indicated that reasons for participation form a determinant dimension of online participation [+W]
