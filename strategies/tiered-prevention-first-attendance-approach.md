# Implement a tiered, prevention-first approach to reducing chronic absence that invests in positive conditions for learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The brief recommends that to reduce chronic absenteeism among AI/AN students, schools "put in place a tiered approach that begins with prevention." This starts with ensuring positive conditions for learning: students are more likely to come to school when they feel physically and emotionally healthy and safe, experience belonging and support, are academically challenged and engaged, and interact with socially and emotionally competent adults. For AI/AN students this explicitly includes identity safety, Native languages and culturally responsive curricula, inclusive graduation practices such as wearing regalia, and calendar flexibility for cultural events.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration grounded in strategies that build upon the particular strengths and assets of students, families, tribes, and communities
- Professional learning for staff who are unfamiliar with AI/AN histories and cultures
#### Constraints
- The brief states establishing positive conditions for AI/AN students can require explicitly addressing challenges faced especially by AI/AN students and families

### Target Learners
- AI/AN K-12 students and their families

### Target Learning Goals
- Daily attendance, engagement, and reengagement in school
