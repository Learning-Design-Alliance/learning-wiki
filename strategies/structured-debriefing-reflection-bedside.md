# Structured tutor-guided debriefing to drive reflective observation and abstract conceptualisation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Bedside teaching should conclude with structured debriefing, conducted away from the immediate clinical environment, guiding students through reflective observation and abstract conceptualisation. Tutors may use the Pendleton model so reflection is student-led, then link observations to clinical principles; they should scaffold learners who struggle to reflect, use inclusive strategies so all learners participate, revisit session goals and correct misconceptions so student conceptualisations align with learning goals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learner engagement varies; tutors must provide prompts and support for learners who struggle to reflect independently
#### Constraints
- Heterogeneity in learners' capacity and motivation for reflection can significantly limit learning attained from reflective learning

### Target Learners
- medical students following bedside encounters

### Target Learning Goals
- reflective practice
- accurate conceptualisation aligned with session goals
- self-evaluative capability
