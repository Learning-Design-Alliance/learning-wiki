# Use eye movement consistency during face processing as a cost-effective early biomarker for ASD and support visual-routine training for children with face-processing difficulties

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that low eye movement consistency in processing faces "may provide a new early biomarker of ASD that can be obtained through a cost-effective and community-viable eye tracking method". It further suggests that children with face-processing difficulties, such as those with ASD, may benefit from training that aims to facilitate the development of visual routine for faces. This rests on the finding that children with ASD showed higher eye movement entropy than matched TD children, particularly tied to autistic traits in social skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Eye tracking during a face recognition task, with entropy of the HMM summarising each participant's fixations as the consistency measure
#### Constraints

### Target Learning Goals
