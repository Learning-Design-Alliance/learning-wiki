# Train children's reporting by directing attention to essential details and teaching a critical attitude toward one's own report

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends training in giving reports, which it argues is possible only by directing the child's attention to the details adults consider essential, thereby accelerating the developmental transformation of the child's distribution of attention. Suggested means include using report-experiments as memory instruction added to observation instruction, describing objects after they are taken away, and calling attention to essential details during perception itself. By calling attention to misstatements, the teacher builds a critical attitude so that "the answer 'I don't know' is to be preferred to every doubtful answer."
