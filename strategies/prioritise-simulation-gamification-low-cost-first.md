# Prioritise simulation and gamification over flipped classroom models, and implement low-cost physical games before investing in expensive simulation equipment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's practice recommendation is a strategic sequencing of investment: "a strategic approach prioritising simulation and gamification over popular but inconsistently effective flipped classroom models". Institutions with limited resources are advised to start with low-cost physical games before purchasing simulation equipment, and to combine foundational knowledge transmission with high-impact active learning targeted to specific competencies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty development emphasising facilitation and debriefing skills, which the review describes as central to success across modalities
#### Constraints
- High-fidelity simulation carries high resource intensity; the review reports startup costs between US$200,000 and US$1 million for freestanding simulation centres

### Target Learners
- Pre-licensure medical, nursing, and pharmacy students

### Target Learning Goals
- Diabetes clinical competence
- Knowledge acquisition
- Learner engagement
