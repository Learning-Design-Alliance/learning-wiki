# Block Play

## Description
Block play involves using blocks to create structures and designs, either independently or collaboratively. It supports problem-solving, imagination, self-expression, mathematical concepts, continuity and permanence, creativity, scientific reasoning, self-esteem, and social and emotional growth.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a full set of unit blocks, assorted props, open storage shelves, and sufficient space and time for building and rebuilding.
#### Constraints
- May require guidance for children to fully realize the learning potential. Requires adequate space and organization to avoid chaos. Requires time and space.

### Affordances
- Enhances problem-solving abilities, fine and gross motor skills, mathematical skills, and language and literacy abilities. Fosters creativity, self-esteem, and social-emotional development.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted to suit individual interests and developmental levels by providing assorted props tied to children’s current interests and experiences. Open-ended nature allows for solo or group play.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe children's problem-solving strategies, creativity in design, application of mathematical concepts, and social interactions during play. Assess the complexity and stability of structures built.

## Impact
- Studies and educator experiences show block play enhances cognitive, social, emotional, and physical development in young children. Provides opportunities for testing hypotheses and building scientific reasoning.
