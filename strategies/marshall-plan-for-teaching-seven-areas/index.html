A federal Marshall Plan for Teaching focused on seven areas: compensation, debt-free preparation, high-retention pathways, mentoring, expertise-sharing, school redesign, and accountability¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report's central recommendation is 'a nationwide Marshall Plan for teaching, similar to that enacted after World War II to rebuild Europe, but for rebuilding the teaching profession.' It would 'focus the powers of the federal government on supporting recruitment, preparation, support, and retention in teaching in seven key areas': raising net compensation through tax credits, housing subsidies, and salary incentives; making teacher preparation debt-free via service scholarships and loan forgiveness; expanding high-retention pathways such as residencies and Grow Your Own programs; providing high-quality mentoring for all beginning teachers; funding professional learning and collaboration; incentivizing school redesign away from the factory model; and rethinking school accountability around authentic measures of school quality and equity.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sustained federal investment and coordination with states, since state and local professionalization initiatives 'can easily falter without a continuous federal commitment to a strong education profession'

Constraints¶

  • Teacher salaries are determined at state or local levels and typically collectively bargained, 'leaving a limited role for the federal government in determining compensation'

Target Learners¶

  • Early childhood and K–12 educators
  • Students in high-need schools and subjects, including special education, science, and math

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Recruiting and retaining a well-prepared, stable, diverse teacher workforce
  • Supporting student learning recovery and access to qualified teachers

Related Strategies¶

  • Seven-policy comprehensive approach to equalizing student access to certified and experienced teachers
  • Establish high-retention preparation pathways—residencies, Grow Your Own models, service scholarships, and mentoring/induction—to stem turnover in high-need subjects and schools
  • Build high-retention, supportive pathways into teaching for candidates of color
  • State and federal policy recommendations: recruit and prepare more candidates, retain new teachers through early-career mentoring, and improve working conditions
  • Underwrite comprehensive preparation, improve teaching conditions, raise compensation, and expand induction to retain teachers of color
  • Offer targeted service scholarships, loan forgiveness, and 1-year postbaccalaureate residency programs to rapidly expand the supply of well-prepared teachers in shortage fields and locations
