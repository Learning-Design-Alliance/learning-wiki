# Regular Physical Education (PE) Classes

## Description
Regular PE classes provide scheduled, structured periods of physical activity and movement-skill instruction several times per week, taught by qualified instructors rather than left to recess or incidental activity. Quality PE combines moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) with instruction in motor skills, fitness knowledge, and cooperative behavior, making it a curricular commitment rather than a discretionary break.

## Design Implications

Consistent PE supports cognition and classroom behavior, with effects strongest for attention and executive function in school-age learners [Physical activity during school improves attention and on-task behavior.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+M]. Single bouts of exercise produce acute, short-lived cognitive benefits, so the *regularity* of PE — not occasional sessions — is what sustains effects [Timing of acute exercise relative to learning affects memory consolidation.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~M]. PE should complement, not replace, classroom learning time; cutting PE to increase academic instruction typically does not improve achievement and removes a proven attention support [School-based physical activity does not harm and may help academic performance.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Protected scheduling (e.g., 3–5 sessions per week) that is not sacrificed for test preparation or make-up work
- Qualified instructors delivering developmentally appropriate motor-skill instruction, not unstructured free play alone
- Sufficient intensity: quality PE targets sustained MVPA for a substantial portion of class time (SHAPE America recommends ~50% of class time in MVPA)
- Inclusive design so learners of all ability levels participate actively rather than waiting in lines or sitting out

#### Constraints
- Large class sizes and limited equipment reduce activity time; lecture-heavy or turn-taking formats (one ball, long lines) can drop MVPA well below target [-M]
- PE taught as rote calisthenics without skill progression or engagement undermines motivation, particularly for adolescents [~M]
- Substituting recess or classroom activity breaks for PE does not deliver the same skill instruction and structured progression [-W]
- Acute exercise benefits fade within roughly an hour, so PE scheduled far from demanding cognitive tasks yields little same-day academic payoff [Timing of acute exercise relative to learning affects memory consolidation.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Daily PE models (e.g., 60-minute daily schedules) versus minimum-compliance models of 2–3 sessions weekly
- Fitness-education models (e.g., Fitnessgram-based programs) emphasizing self-monitoring of activity
- Sport-education and teaching-games-for-understanding (TGfU) models that embed skill learning in game play
- Integrated approaches pairing PE with classroom [active-learning](../principles/active-learning.md) or movement-based lessons

### Target Learners
- School-age children and adolescents, where evidence for attention and executive-function benefits is strongest [Physical activity during school improves attention and on-task behavior.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+M]
- Learners with ADHD or high activity needs, who show disproportionate on-task gains after physical activity [+M]
- Less effective as a standalone intervention for older adults or purely sedentary adult learners without adaptation

### Target Learning Goals
- Psychomotor: fundamental motor skills, sport skills, and lifelong fitness competence
- Cognitive support: improved attention, working memory, and on-task behavior that indirectly serve academic goals [Physical activity during school improves attention and on-task behavior.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+M]
- Social-affective: cooperation, sportsmanship, and self-regulation through structured group activity

### Instructions
1. Schedule PE at fixed, protected times across the week so regularity — not volume alone — drives effects [Timing of acute exercise relative to learning affects memory consolidation.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~M]
2. Design lessons to maximize MVPA: minimize management talk, lines, and elimination games; use small-sided activities so all learners move simultaneously
3. Sequence instruction with explicit [practice](../elements/practice.md) of motor skills, progressing from isolated drills to game-like application
4. Open sessions with a brief [check-in](../elements/check-in.md) on readiness and close with reflection on effort and strategy to build self-monitoring
5. Coordinate with classroom teachers so demanding academic work follows PE when possible, exploiting the acute attention window [Timing of acute exercise relative to learning affects memory consolidation.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~M]
6. Assess skill progression and fitness growth formatively rather than grading on athletic performance alone

## Examples
- **SPARK (Sports, Play, and Active Recreation for Kids)** — an evidence-based PE curriculum with structured lesson plans and activity promotion, widely implemented in US elementary schools ([https://sparkpe.org](https://sparkpe.org))
- **SHAPE America standards** — national guidelines specifying grade-level outcomes and the recommendation that PE classes achieve ~50% MVPA ([https://www.shapeamerica.org](https://www.shapeamerica.org))
- **Fitnessgram** — a youth fitness assessment and education program used within regular PE to support self-monitoring of health-related fitness ([https://fitnessgram.net](https://fitnessgram.net))
