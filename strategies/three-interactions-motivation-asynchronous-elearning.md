# Use the three interaction types (learner-system, instructor-learner, learner-learner) at necessary stages to sustain motivation in asynchronous e-learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends designing asynchronous e-learning around three interaction types — learner–system, instructor–learner, and learner–learner — drawing on Cheng's finding that learner-learner interaction greatly influences nurses' intrinsic motivation and instructor-learner interaction drives extrinsic motivation. It concludes: "using interactive features at necessary stages can help promote motivation among the learners", addressing the motivation problem inherent in self-directed asynchronous formats.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Evidence-based instructional design principles; interactive elements are required to provide learning guidance, content, and feedback, per Sinclair et al.'s 10 guidelines cited in the discussion
#### Constraints
- Asynchronous e-learning lacks the feeling of being taught directly in real-time, which may affect motivation levels

### Target Learners
- clinical nurses in continuing education

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained motivation and learning persistence in self-directed online learning
