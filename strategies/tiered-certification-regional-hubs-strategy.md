# Tiered certification tracks with regional simulation hubs, mobile units, and shared access to avoid excluding resource-limited centres

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
To implement digitally enhanced certification equitably, the statement recommends a basic track relying on "widely accessible tools, like online content, standardized cognitive assessment, and low-cost task trainers", with advanced certification incorporating high-fidelity simulation where available. Complementary strategies include regional simulation hubs serving multiple centres, EHRA-certified high-volume training organizations, mobile simulation units, shared access arrangements between neighbouring centres, and fellowship grants for trainees from under-resourced environments. Transition periods and tiered requirements are proposed so standardization does not become exclusionary.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Robust quality assurance including minimum simulator specifications, validated metrics, and scheduled curriculum updates
- Certification criteria designed at the European Union level with graduated implementation timelines and alternative pathways not requiring on-site simulators
#### Constraints
- High-fidelity simulators are expensive and unevenly distributed, creating geographic disparities and a risk of two-tier readiness where access, rather than competence, determines progression

### Target Learners
- fellows in resource-limited institutions
- training centres of varying sizes and resource levels

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable access to simulation-based competency training
- standardized certification across European centres
