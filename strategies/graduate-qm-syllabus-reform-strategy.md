# Reform a graduate course syllabus by adding modern content (postulates, density matrices, entanglement) while cutting dated standard topics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors restructured a first-year graduate quantum mechanics syllabus to add "a thorough treatment of the density matrix formalism, partial traces and entanglement", a clear statement of the postulates, quantum field theory introduction, and applications matched to departmental research areas, while cutting topics such as the full Wigner-Eckart theorem treatment and reviews of undergraduate quantum mechanics. Application areas "could be adjusted depending on where the reformed course is being taught". Faculty input was sought to make reform a department-wide effort.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty input and department-head approval before implementing core course changes; a supporting textbook covering the added content
#### Constraints
- Some standard material must be cut to make room; one professor was concerned about loss of the full Wigner-Eckart theorem; the disconnect between low-dimensional (ket notation) and high-dimensional (wave function) portions of the course needs further work

### Target Learners
- first-year graduate physics students, including M.S. and Ph.D. students and non-traditional students

### Target Learning Goals
- preparing graduate students for research in quantum-mechanics-based fields, including entanglement, density matrices, and quantum information
