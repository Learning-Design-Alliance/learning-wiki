# Develop nursing students' compassion competence through role modelling, simulation-based methods, role-playing, and mindfulness techniques within a systematic, continuous curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that nursing education deliberately cultivate compassion, stating it is "necessary to implement effctive methods such as role modeling by clinical educatores and professional nurses, simulation-based methods, role-playing, and mindfulness techniques" for developing compassion competency. It further argues compassion should be an essential curriculum concept and that a systematic, continuous educational program should be developed, citing interprofessional education as increasing compassion and caring behaviours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A systematic and continuous educational program should be developed, with compassion included as an essential concept in the nursing curriculum rather than delivered only as early, lecture-based courses.
#### Constraints
- The authors note their university's baccalaureate curriculum has "no specific courses for compassion or mindfulness practices", and existing training is usually theoretical and lecture-based, which may not be sufficiently effective.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students in their second year and above with clinical courses

### Target Learning Goals
- compassion competence
- caring behaviours
- professional commitment
