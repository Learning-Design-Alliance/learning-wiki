Future research should explore emotion management strategies and exposure to emotionally evocative scenarios to support clinician performance and well-being¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review identifies a lack of research on effective emotion regulation strategies integrated into clinical education, compared to other high-risk industries such as the military. It recommends that "future research should explore emotion management strategies to maximize well-being and patient safety" and that future work examine whether exposure to emotionally evocative simulated scenarios helps trainees deal effectively with difficult patients. It also calls for research on leveraging positive emotions to improve patient outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Research and training programs must differentiate between incidental and integral emotions when designing emotion management interventions.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • clinicians in acute care settings
  • medical trainees

Target Learning Goals¶

  • emotion regulation and management
  • coping with difficult patient encounters

Claims¶

  • Non-procedural skills scores during simulated CPR were significantly lower when external stressors were present [+W]
  • Paramedic trainees randomized to a low-stress condition calculated drug doses more accurately than those in the high-stress group [+W]
  • Negative emotions, most commonly stress, were inversely correlated with clinical performance in some simulated or educational acute care settings [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Medical educators should account for incidental emotions during clinical training by incorporating realistic clinical distractors into simulation
  • Design future pedagogical interventions aligned with the integrative model: case discussions requiring epistemological/ontological reflection, debriefings stimulating emotional and psychomotor awareness, and triangulated multi-dimensional assessment
  • Confront Fears
