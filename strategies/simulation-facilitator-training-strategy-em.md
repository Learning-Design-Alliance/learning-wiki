# Develop facilitator training, fellowships, and scholarly tracks so all simulation facilitators possess skills in simulation pedagogy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building facilitation capacity alongside simulation resources: about half of responding programs lacked fellowship-trained simulation faculty, and lack of faculty expertise was a major barrier. It invokes the INACSL standards that facilitators should possess "specific skills and knowledge in simulation pedagogy" and suggests programs expose residents to simulation theory and facilitation earlier in training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained facilitators with simulation pedagogy knowledge per INACSL standards; institutional support and funding for training materials
#### Constraints
- The article notes some experienced facilitators without formal training become exceptional educators, so formal fellowship training is not stated as strictly necessary

### Target Learners
- EM residents
- EM faculty and simulation facilitators

### Target Learning Goals
- Simulation facilitation and debriefing skills
- Medical education expertise
