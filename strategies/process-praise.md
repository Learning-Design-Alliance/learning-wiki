# Process Praise

## Description
Process praise directs positive feedback at what a learner *did* — their effort, strategy choice, persistence, or improvement — rather than at who they *are* ("you're so smart") or at outcomes alone ("you got an A"). It is carried out in the moment of feedback, typically by naming the specific controllable behavior and linking it to the result: "You tried a new approach when the first one stalled, and that's what got you there."

## Design Implications

Process praise is grounded in implicit-theory (mindset) research: feedback about personal traits pushes learners toward entity beliefs and performance goals, while feedback about process supports incremental beliefs and learning goals [Mueller & Dweck's experiments show trait praise undermines persistence after failure while process praise sustains it.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.75.1.33) [+M]. The mechanism is attributional — it teaches learners to attribute success and failure to controllable causes, which shapes how they respond to subsequent difficulty. Praise that is vague, inflated, or mismatched to actual performance can backfire, so it must be specific and honest [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Observation of actual learner behavior, so the praise names something real (a strategy, a revision, a productive question)
- Feedback language that identifies controllable actions rather than traits or luck
- Alignment with [Action-Oriented Feedback](action-oriented-feedback.md) — praise should point toward what to do next, not just affirm what was done

#### Constraints
- Effort praise without strategy content can teach learners that effort alone suffices, encouraging ineffective persistence [-M] — "you worked so hard" on a failed attempt should be paired with strategy redirection
- Inflated or unearned praise signals low expectations, particularly for lower-achieving students, and reduces motivation [-M]
- Effects on achievement are small and inconsistent at scale; large replications of mindset interventions show modest average effects that depend heavily on implementation quality [~S]
- For learners who already hold strong fixed beliefs, brief praise alone rarely shifts behavior without broader intervention [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Strategy praise** ("your diagram made the relationships clear") — strongest form; names the mechanism
- **Effort praise** ("you kept revising") — useful but weaker; should lead into strategy talk
- **Progress praise** ("your second draft is much tighter than your first") — makes improvement visible and supports self-comparison over social comparison
- Delivered orally in conferencing, in written feedback margins, or as system messages in adaptive software

### Target Learners
- Struggling learners who attribute failure to fixed ability and disengage after setbacks [+M]
- High achievers praised for being "smart," who often develop fear of failure and avoid challenge [-M]
- Younger children are especially sensitive to trait praise, which they readily internalize as a measure of self-worth [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Motivational goals: persistence, resilience after failure, willingness to attempt challenging tasks
- Self-regulated learning: attributing outcomes to controllable strategies [Self-regulated learning improves achievement when learners monitor and adjust strategy use.](../claims/self-regulated-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M]
- Long-term disposition toward learning rather than short-term performance on a single task

### Instructions
1. Observe the learner's actual process — strategy use, revision, help-seeking — so praise can be specific rather than generic.
2. Name the controllable behavior explicitly, linking it to the outcome ("choosing to outline first made your argument clearer").
3. When effort was high but results poor, pair effort acknowledgment with [Action-Oriented Feedback](action-oriented-feedback.md) redirecting to a better strategy, rather than praising effort alone.
4. Avoid trait language entirely ("gifted," "natural") even in positive framing; contrast with [Non-Examples](../elements/non-examples.md) of trait praise during teacher training.
5. Reinforce the pattern over time so learners internalize the attribution, connecting to [Formative Assessment](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) cycles where feedback informs the next attempt.

## Related Strategies
- [Action-Oriented Feedback](action-oriented-feedback.md) — the companion feedback form; process praise tells learners what worked, action-oriented feedback tells them what to do next
- [Growth Mindset Interventions](growth-mindset-interventions.md) — process praise is the moment-to-moment delivery mechanism of mindset theory

## Examples
- **Classroom language (Mueller & Dweck paradigm):** After a successful math set, a teacher says "You must have tried good strategies" rather than "You must be smart at math" — the original studies showed this difference changed children's persistence and performance on subsequent failure trials.
- **Writing conferences:** A teacher praises the specific revision move ("cutting your opening paragraph put the reader straight into the scene") rather than "you're a talented writer."
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — mastery-based progress messaging ("you've practiced this skill 3 days in a row") reinforces process and persistence rather than labeling ability.
