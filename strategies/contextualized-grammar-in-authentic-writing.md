# Contextualize English grammar instruction within purposeful, authentic writing tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding grammar practice within purposeful writing rather than teaching it in isolation. For example, when a class writes a persuasive letter, "the process of engaging in such complex thinking then drives the need for complex written language," and authenticity drives frequent rereading and revising toward a grammatically correct published piece.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Writing tasks must have an authentic purpose and audience so that the need for complex language arises from the composing task itself
#### Constraints
- This interpretation is offered as the authors' speculation about why SIWI students gained on written language while comparison students doing explicit grammar practice did not

### Target Learners
- elementary students, including deaf and hard of hearing students with diverse language needs

### Target Learning Goals
- written language accuracy
- written language complexity
- genre-specific writing skills
