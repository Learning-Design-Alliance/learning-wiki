# Use interactive streamed bedside teaching as a supplement to, not a replacement for, conventional face-to-face teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating interactive electronic visualization formats into teaching as a promising supplement to conventional face-to-face instruction, particularly where presence teaching is restricted, while cautioning that patient contact and practical skills cannot be replaced. It states "Generell kann die Implementierung neuer digitaler Lehrformate die herkömmliche Lehre ergänzen und sollte daher generell in das Lehrkonzept integriert werden." The authors also suggest scaling the model in countries with limited availability of face-to-face teaching places, and note an instructor can supervise more students in the same time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Secure encryption technology for the streaming platform to protect patient data
- Systematic qualitative and quantitative evaluation of newly implemented formats
#### Constraints
- Patient contact and the teaching of practical skills cannot be replaced by this format
- Exam results in the test semester were significantly worse, and the comparison is only indicative because only part of the cohort participated

### Target Learners
- Medical students in clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining clinical teaching during contact restrictions
- Supplementing conventional bedside teaching with digital formats
