Use clinical simulation as a pedagogical strategy for developing cardiovascular assessment competencies in undergraduate nursing education¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2–q4 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review supports using clinical simulation in undergraduate nursing education as an active learning strategy that creates realistic clinical scenarios for practicing cardiovascular physical examination. It reports that simulation "demonstrated consistency in improving cardiovascular physical examination assessments, such as the enhancement of cognitive and psychomotor skills, increased confidence, and knowledge" compared with traditional strategies. The strategy involves scenarios using mannequins of varying fidelity, standardized patients, and 3D organ-overlay applications, with debriefing and repetition in a safe environment.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires realistic simulated scenarios using mannequins, simulators, or standardized patients, with faculty-conducted case presentations and opportunities for repeated deliberate practice.

Constraints¶

  • The review notes the evidence base requires more robust methodological designs and multicenter clinical trials; blinding of participants and personnel was rarely achieved in the included experimental studies.

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate nursing students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Cardiovascular physical examination skills including inspection, palpation, and auscultation
  • Clinical reasoning and confidence in health assessment

Claims¶

  • Clinical simulation improves nursing students' knowledge, confidence and performance in cardiovascular physical examination compared with traditional teaching, per a systematic review of six studies [+W]
  • Simulation-based curriculum with deliberate practice improved cognitive knowledge and cardiovascular assessment skills from pre-test to post-test (Jeffries et al. study) [+W]
  • High-fidelity simulation improved auscultation performance in the intervention group, with no statistical or clinical differences between groups (Menon et al. pilot study) [+W]
  • Simulation training increased nursing students' confidence in cardiopulmonary physical examination skills more than traditional training (Tawalbeh et al. study) [+M]
  • Clinical simulation improved overall cardiovascular examination performance compared with traditional lecture in the Brazilian quasi-experimental study (Fernandes et al.) [+W]
  • Simulation Based Education Improves Outcomes [+M]
