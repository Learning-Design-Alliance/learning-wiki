# Introduce modern physics concepts through conceptually accurate visual models such as Feynman diagrams, suited to animated ICT presentation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A strategy of teaching modern physics concepts through visual models rather than difficult mathematics first. The article presents Feynman diagrams, which 'express the complex physics of Quantum Electrodynamics through 'visual' models which are simple to understand and conceptually accurate', as one route, noting such models may be particularly suited to animated presentation via ICT and need not be unlearned later.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Visual models must be simple to understand and conceptually accurate, and must not have to be unlearned when more difficult mathematical models are added at a later stage
#### Constraints
- The authors do not claim to have the answers to how deepening pupils' understanding of modern physics might best be done

### Target Learners
- school pupils encountering quantum physics concepts, including primary-age children

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding quantum electrodynamics and other modern physics concepts without advanced mathematics
