# Low Stakes Testing

## Description
Low stakes testing is the practice of administering frequent, short assessments — quizzes, clicker questions, exit tickets, practice tests — that carry little or no consequence for grades. The act of retrieving information from memory strengthens retention more than restudying, a phenomenon known as the testing effect [Retrieval practice produces durable learning beyond restudying.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Because consequences are low, the assessments function primarily as learning events and feedback opportunities rather than evaluation.

## Design Implications

Testing is not merely measurement: retrieval itself is a powerful learning event, and low stakes conditions let learners benefit from it without the motivational and anxiety costs of graded assessment [Retrieval practice produces durable learning beyond restudying.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Effectiveness depends on learners actually attempting retrieval — open-book or immediately-answered formats reduce the benefit — and on timely [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) following each attempt.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Questions that require genuine retrieval (short answer, free recall) rather than recognition or rereading
- Low or zero grade consequences, clearly communicated to learners
- Feedback delivered promptly after the attempt, so errors are corrected before they consolidate
- Sufficient frequency and spacing — a single pre-exam practice test captures little of the benefit

#### Constraints
- Benefits shrink sharply when questions are answerable without retrieval (open notes, peer answering before individual response) [~M]
- Repeated testing on the same items can produce rote item-specific learning rather than flexible understanding if question formats never vary [~M]
- Poorly aligned quiz items can encourage surface memorization of isolated facts at the expense of integration [~M]
- If stakes creep upward, anxiety and grade-orientation displace the learning function [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **In-class:** clicker questions, think-pair-share polls, minute papers, exit tickets
- **Online:** auto-graded quizzes in an LMS (e.g., Canvas quizzes, Mobius), spaced retrieval apps like [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net)
- **Pre-class:** reading quizzes that incentivize preparation and enable just-in-time teaching
- **Cumulative quizzing:** each quiz includes items from earlier units, converting quizzing into spaced review

### Target Learners
- Learners of all ability levels benefit from retrieval practice, though lower-performing learners gain the most because testing exposes gaps they would not notice while rereading [Retrieval practice produces durable learning beyond restudying.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]
- Learners prone to illusions of knowing — fluency from rereading is mistaken for mastery; quizzing calibrates their self-assessment
- Anxious learners benefit specifically from the low-stakes framing, which removes evaluative threat while preserving the retrieval benefit

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of factual and conceptual knowledge over the long term
- Metacognitive calibration: accurate judgment of what is and is not yet known
- Transfer preparation: cumulative, varied quizzing supports flexible application better than blocked review

### Instructions
1. Identify the core knowledge and concepts learners must retain; write short retrieval questions at varied cognitive levels.
2. Schedule frequent brief quizzes (weekly or per-unit), explicitly framed as practice, not evaluation.
3. Require individual retrieval before any collaboration or note consultation.
4. Provide immediate [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) on errors, and briefly reteach items the majority missed.
5. Make quizzes cumulative and vary question formats to promote flexible, spaced retrieval.
6. Pair with [Spaced Practice](../principles/spaced-practice.md) and [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md); use results to adjust instruction ([Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md)).

## Examples
- **[Retrieval Practice](https://www.retrievalpractice.org)** (Agarwal & Bain) — classroom guides and research summaries for implementing low stakes quizzing across grade levels.
- **Two-stage cooperative exams** (e.g., as used at the University of British Columbia; see [Carl Wieman Science Education Initiative](https://cwsei.ubc.ca)) — students take a quiz individually, then retake it in groups; the individual stage is low stakes retrieval, the group stage adds discussion.
- **Anki / spaced flashcards** in medical education — programs like [Firecracker](https://www.firecracker.me) schedule daily low stakes retrieval questions across the curriculum.
