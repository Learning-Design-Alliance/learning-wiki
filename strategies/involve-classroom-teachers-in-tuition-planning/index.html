Strategies

Involve classroom teachers in planning and managing tuition so it complements classroom work rather than being a bolt-on¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The evaluation recommends that tuition delivered by external tutors be planned and managed jointly with classroom teachers so that sessions complement ordinary lessons. The report states that "classroom teachers need to be involved in the planning and management of tuition so that it is not viewed as a 'bolt-on' and complements work in the classroom". It also notes the value of involving English and mathematics teachers in planning sessions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Classroom teachers of the tutored subject participate in planning and managing the tuition sessions

Constraints¶

  • Recommendation arises from a quasi-experimental evaluation whose impact findings were not statistically significant

Target Learners¶

  • Year 6 and Year 7 pupils in challenging communities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English and mathematics attainment

Claims¶

  • Participating schools were positive about the tuition and keen to work with the Tutor Trust again, and staff saw a need for more affordable high-quality tuition [+W]
  • Schools perceived Tutor Trust tuition as most effective when tutors had strong pedagogical skills, subject knowledge, and could engage pupils and target sessions appropriately [+W]
  • Pupils receiving Tutor Trust tuition made marginally less progress in English and mathematics than matched comparison pupils, with differences not statistically significant [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Monitor tutor performance and conduct, with schools implementing their own oversight mechanisms, to secure pupil benefit
  • Monitor tutor performance, increase consistency of tuition, and involve classroom teachers in planning and managing tuition
