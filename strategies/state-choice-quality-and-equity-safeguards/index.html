State policymakers should pair any choice expansion with quality controls, equitable access safeguards, integration management, and research and evaluation requirements¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The report's Key Policy Considerations section lays out implementable state actions across sectors: ensure many high-quality options with consolidated, accessible applications and free transportation; authorize charters through a small number of authorizers with strong accountability; maintain special oversight of virtual schools; require accreditation, staff qualifications, and nondiscrimination standards for schools receiving public funds; and require registration, structured programs of study, and assessment for homeschooling. It stresses that "simply providing choices does not automatically provide high-quality options that are accessible to all students or improve student learning."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Choice expansion should be paired with quality review processes, information systems for parents, and monitoring of access for students with disabilities, English learners, and varied backgrounds

Constraints¶

  • The report states outcomes depend on how well districts manage choice and leverage strong school quality; some districts have inadvertently created educational deserts

Target Learners¶

  • All U.S. K-12 students and families choosing among public, charter, private, virtual, and homeschool options

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Equal access to high-quality learning opportunities
  • educational attainment
  • racial and economic integration
  • preparation for civic roles in a democracy

Claims¶

  • Parents' school choices were constrained by disability-linked safety fears and transportation barriers [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use centralized enrollment, controlled choice, and transparency systems to ensure equitable access and integration
  • Research and policy attention should now examine PreK program quality and equitable access across racial, ethnic, and linguistic groups, and how families choose among PreK options
  • Retain, strengthen, and improve statewide choice options, with urgent state action on information, oversight, and funding equity
  • State policymakers should strengthen their own oversight capacity as accreditors' watchdog role weakens
