Use a community of practice to collaboratively develop university-wide online orientation resources¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 design), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends leveraging an established community of practice (CoP) to drive the collaborative development of online orientation resources. The CoP contributed to brainstorming, co-design workshops, content development working groups, and formative review, which the authors state "strengthened the final product and ensured it has wide relevance across the university". This approach also fosters engagement and value creation among staff.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A dedicated, diverse group of academics invested in online education, supported by an Educational Designer to maintain task focus and avoid content duplication.

Constraints¶

  • Creative and innovative ideas from co-design workshops may need to be tempered by practicality and feasibility; the authors recommend "tempered with the setting of realistic expectations of what can be achieved".

Target Learners¶

  • University students studying online

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Orientation to online study
  • Institutional connection and belonging

Claims¶

  • A baseline survey of online course stakeholders found widespread duplication of orientation resources across courses, reflecting siloed development. [+W]
  • The CoP extended its network's reach through national convenings, including a member-facilitated NIEA convention session [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Consult online students directly during the needs analysis phase of orientation resource design
  • Foster communities of practice for AI governance instead of rigid top-down policies
  • Embed AI policy literacy, peer-led communities of practice, and shared AI resources into professional development
  • Invest in ongoing educator professional development and an assessment community of practice for POG success
  • Concentrate AI-edtech adoption priorities through collaborative networks that share tools, policies, and evaluation practices
