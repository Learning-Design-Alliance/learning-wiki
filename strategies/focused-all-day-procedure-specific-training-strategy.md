# Provide focused all-day procedure-specific training covering the entire surgical pathway before surgeons adopt a low-volume procedure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes the exposure that defined the trained surgeon group: "all participating knee surgeons received focused PFA training on September 28, 2007, during an all-day symposium detailing the entirety of the PFA procedure preoperatively, perioperatively, and postoperatively." This focused training, delivered before RCT participation, was the intervention whose association with a lower 6-year revision rate the study observed. The strategy is implementable as a concentrated symposium covering indications, technique, and complication management for a procedure performed in small numbers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training delivered before surgeons take on the procedure, covering the preoperative, perioperative, and postoperative entirety of the procedure as the article describes
#### Constraints
- The article studied training combined with subsequent RCT participation and high procedure volume; it cannot separate the symposium alone from these accompanying factors, and the trained group was a select group of 8 surgeons, which the authors state may reduce generalizability

### Target Learners
- practicing knee surgeons adopting a low-volume arthroplasty procedure

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical competence in indications, surgical technique, and complication management for patellofemoral arthroplasty
