# Add explicit information-literacy modules or quests to gamified open-book assessments to convert engagement into high-quality evidence-based inquiry

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that future iterations of the G-Jump model incorporate explicit information-literacy instruction, because gamified engagement alone did not improve the quality of students' scientific searching: only 26.6% used credible scientific sources. The recommendation is that future versions should "incorporate explicit modules or 'quests' focused on information literacy" so that the model's autonomy yields evidence-based reasoning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- structured training in information literacy, since only one four-hour session on topic-specific searching and database use was provided in this study
#### Constraints
- the recommendation addresses the open-book stage of gamified formative assessment in medical education, where students currently rely on general search engines rather than specialized medical databases

### Target Learners
- undergraduate anesthesia and health-occupation students

### Target Learning Goals
- evidence-based information retrieval
- quality of scientific inquiry
