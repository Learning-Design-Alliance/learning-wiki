# Practical in-class routine for clicker polling: clusters of 3–5 questions, 45–60 second polls, and follow-up discussion when many answers are wrong

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The instructor's how-to routine for integrating clicker questions into 50-minute lectures: pose "between 3 to 5 clicker questions" grouped in clusters of 2–3, give students 45–60 seconds with a 10-second warning, display the response histogram, and if fewer than 75% answer correctly devote extra time to follow-up discussion. Questions are flexibly timed as warm-ups, comprehension checks, wrap-ups, or breaks, and students are encouraged to discuss with neighbours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A student response system integrated with lecture slides; skeleton lecture notes into which questions can be pasted; a few lectures of practice to develop a routine
#### Constraints
- Polling and discussion consume class time; the authors, citing Wieman et al., note instructors cover roughly 10% less material, consistent with their own experience

### Target Learners
- large undergraduate lecture classes

### Target Learning Goals
- real-time formative assessment of conceptual understanding
- increased classroom participation
