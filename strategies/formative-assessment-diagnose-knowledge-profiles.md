# Use formative assessment and a repertoire of adaptive instructional techniques matched to students' knowledge profiles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's practical implication is that teachers cannot assume a uniform knowledge state when beginning a topic. Because some students "might hold integrated naïve theories that have to be rejected and replaced" while others "can have strongly fragmented knowledge that has to be integrated," teachers should diagnose each student's knowledge before or during instruction and respond adaptively. Information about typical knowledge profiles and pathways in a domain, as the study found for floating and sinking, can help teachers prepare assessments and interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formative assessment instruments that capture the coexisting knowledge types in a domain (misconceptions, everyday conceptions, scientific concepts)
- A repertoire of instructional techniques allowing adaptive responses to individual students' differing knowledge states
#### Constraints
- The profile-and-pathway information is domain-specific; the article reports it for floating and sinking in third graders and does not claim generalization to other domains

### Target Learners
- elementary school students with heterogeneous prior knowledge of a science topic

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnosing and adaptively supporting conceptual change in science domains
