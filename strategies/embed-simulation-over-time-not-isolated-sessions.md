# Avoid isolated simulation sessions; embed simulation training over time within a structured medical-education programme

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that cardiac-arrest skills declined significantly at one month after a single simulation session while ACS skills were maintained, the authors recommend against one-off simulation. They state "Il faudrait ainsi éviter de proposer des séances isolées de simulation aux futurs professionnels et il serait préférable d'inscrire la formation dans le temps, ainsi que dans un programme pédagogique de formation médicale initiale", and that renewing the training would keep knowledge and skills at an optimal level. The ultimate goal is full integration of simulation into initial and continuing health-professional education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation sessions renewed or repeated over time rather than delivered once
- Integration into a pedagogical programme of initial medical training
#### Constraints
- The article notes the optimal frequency of sessions and the validity duration of simulator training remain to be determined

### Target Learners
- family-medicine residents and future health professionals in emergency settings

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of technical and non-technical emergency-care skills over time
