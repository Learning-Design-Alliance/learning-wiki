# Structure AI-assisted grading to shift TA effort from routine low-judgment decisions toward conceptually rich responses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that AI-assisted grading be structured so that teaching assistants spend their time on the responses that are most conceptually rich or require careful interpretation, rather than on routine, low-judgment decisions. This preserves the pedagogical benefit that evaluating ambiguous or challenging cases deepens TAs' content knowledge and pedagogical judgment, while reducing overall grading load and freeing TA time for other instructional and scholarly responsibilities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An AI pre-grading pass with confidence filtering must identify which responses need careful human interpretation
#### Constraints
- The article notes encoding all human-discretion contingencies (partial credit for serious attempts, lenient error-propagation handling) in a rubric for an exam taken by several hundred students would be impractical

### Target Learners
- teaching assistants grading large-enrollment university science courses

### Target Learning Goals
- deepening TA content knowledge and pedagogical judgment through grading of ambiguous cases
