# Use the LCAS to compare design features across course types, offerings, and against student outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend instructors and researchers deploy the LCAS for assessment purposes: measuring how collaboration, discovery and relevance, and iteration vary among course types (e.g., CUREs vs. inquiry labs), among courses of the same type, and among offerings of the same course, and for determining the extent to which each design feature relates to student outcomes. They write the LCAS "could also be used for determining the extent to which each design feature relates to student outcomes," informing lab learning experience design and future CURE efficacy and effectiveness studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Courses must be classifiable as CURE or traditional via student and instructor descriptions before known-groups use
#### Constraints
- Future uses should measure factors such as instructor experience and students' prior research experience, which may influence responses
- Student perceptions may diverge from instructor intentions when students do not believe their work is legitimate research

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology students, including non-majors and students early in their undergraduate careers

### Target Learning Goals
- linking lab course design features to outcomes such as communication skills, sense of community, science identity, and persistence
