# Use multisource (360-degree) feedback with confidential sealed questionnaires from six evaluator groups to assess resident professionalism and communication skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article implements MSF assessment in which each resident is evaluated by attending doctors, self-evaluation, resident peers, nurses, office staff and patients. Questionnaires were "kept sealed and confidential when researchers dispatched them to evaluators", and each resident received descriptive data (mean and SD) on their own and the group's results. The authors conclude the MSF questionnaires "may provide an internally valid and reliable way of assessing resident physician competencies".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Uniformly trained investigators
- Ethics approval and confidentiality of questionnaires
- Feedback returned to residents in a confidential, formative manner
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only in large teaching hospitals in China; generalisability to smaller community hospitals or other regions is uncertain

### Target Learners
- first-year resident physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- professionalism
- interpersonal and communication skills
