# Use a first-person 3D procedure video as an educational precursor to simulation-based training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes pairing a short stereoscopic first-person video of a procedure, recorded in real clinical conditions, with hands-on simulator practice. The authors state that "the innovative association of a 3D video providing education about patients installation and full conduct of the procedure in 'real life' and of a simulators training allowing for technical skills acquisition that we propose could be an optimal teaching method." The video is administered once before simulator practice and remains available online for repeated viewing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A recorded first-person video of the procedure, VR headsets or screen devices for viewing, and an existing simulation training session to precede
#### Constraints
- The article states there is no high-quality evidence on the effectiveness of VR-based teaching, and a future study is needed to confirm the video adds value to established simulation training

### Target Learners
- medical students
- residents and attendings in procedural training

### Target Learning Goals
- familiarity with the full conduct of a bedside procedure before hands-on simulator practice, including patient interaction
