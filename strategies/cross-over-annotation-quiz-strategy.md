# Sequential cross-over design alternating individual and collaborative annotation with timed online quizzes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's trial structure alternates the initial annotation condition between consecutive class iterations so "the order of individual and collaborative annotation activities might have affected quiz performance" is controlled. Each class annotates one WSI set under one condition, crosses over after a 30-min washout of traditional classwork, then completes a 5-min questionnaire and 10-min timed online quiz with equal numbers of items per condition, designed by subject matter experts for comparable difficulty.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identical WSI sets across classes
- Quizzes containing an equal number of items related to each annotation condition, of similar difficulty
- A washout period between conditions
#### Constraints
- Carryover effects remain a risk in cross-over designs; the authors argue alternating order and within-cohort class equivalency checks mitigated this
- No pre-annotation test of understanding was logistically possible and could bias results via test-enhanced learning

### Target Learners
- Medical and medical science students in practical pathology classes

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge acquisition of microscopic morphology features
