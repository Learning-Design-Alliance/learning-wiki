# Support faculty transitioning into DBER with get-started information, project-sustaining mechanisms, and a supportive community

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that professional development for faculty entering discipline-based education research include the program elements they found transformative. As they state, "Receiving get-started information, building mechanisms to sustain research projects and engaging with a supportive community help participants transform general interests to speciﬁc questions, articulate concrete next-steps and increase their sense of self-eﬃcacy." These elements can inform professional development opportunities for faculty transitioning into DBER and build capacity in the field.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Program activities such as project mapping, procedural knowledge workshops, individualized project feedback, and peer community structures
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a three-participant case study within one virtual PEER edition

### Target Learners
- Classically trained STEM faculty transitioning into education research

### Target Learning Goals
- Transforming general interests into specific research questions and increasing self-efficacy in DBER
