# Activating and Using Background Knowledge

## Description
This strategy involves readers activating their existing knowledge and using it to comprehend what they are reading. Background knowledge consists of a person’s experiences with the world, including prior reading, and concepts for how written text works, such as word identification, print concepts, word meaning, and text organization. Cognitive scientists' schema theory supports this, stating that as people learn, they develop knowledge structures (schemas) connected to others. Good readers connect their background knowledge to new information in a text, activating relevant schemas as they read, which affects their understanding and reaction to the text. Schemas related to text organization are particularly important for comprehension.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires learners to reflect on their existing knowledge related to the text. Instructors need to facilitate discussions and provide prompts to help learners make connections.
#### Constraints
- Learners may struggle to activate relevant background knowledge if they lack experience with the topic. Misconceptions or inaccurate background knowledge can hinder comprehension if not addressed.
