# Activating And Using Background Knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Activating and using background knowledge is a strategy in which learners deliberately retrieve what they already know about a topic before encountering new material, then connect that knowledge to the new content during and after the encounter. It is carried out through pre-reading prompts, discussion, prediction, [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md), [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md), and ongoing activities such as [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) that force learners to relate new information to what they already hold in memory.

## Design Implications

Comprehension is fundamentally a process of integrating new information with existing knowledge structures; text that cannot be connected to prior knowledge is poorly remembered even when fully "read" [Bransford & Johnson's classic studies show identical text is incomprehensible without relevant context.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(72)80011-8) [+S]. Activation is not a warm-up ritual — it works only when the activated knowledge is *used*: predictions, analogies, and questions that require learners to apply prior knowledge to the new material produce deeper integration than simply recalling it [Dochy et al.'s review shows prior knowledge activation is among the strongest predictors of learning outcomes.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0959-4752(99)00004-8) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Accurate diagnosis of what learners actually know (misconceptions activated as "background knowledge" interfere with learning)
- Prompts or organizers that surface relevant knowledge *before* the new material, not after ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
- Tasks that require learners to make explicit connections — prediction, comparison, [Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md) — rather than passive recall [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding by forcing integration with prior knowledge.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]
- Text and instruction pitched within reach of learners' existing knowledge, with [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md)

#### Constraints
- Activating inaccurate or naive prior knowledge can entrench misconceptions; activation must be paired with [Cognitive Conflict](../elements/cognitive-conflict.md) or [Non-Examples](../elements/non-examples.md) to confront errors
- Learners with genuinely low prior knowledge have little to activate; for them, building knowledge (e.g., through [Case Studies](../elements/case-studies.md) or direct teaching) must precede or accompany activation [Prior knowledge is not uniformly related to performance when it is not activated or used.](../claims/prior-knowledge-not-related-to-performance.md) [~M]
- Generic "brainstorm what you know" prompts can activate irrelevant knowledge that competes with the target content during reading
- For learners with high prior knowledge, extensive activation scaffolds become redundant and can reduce engagement [Expertise reversal: support that helps novices can hinder experts.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Pre-reading:** KWL charts, anticipation guides, prediction tasks, [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md), [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) mapping new concepts to familiar domains
- **During reading:** [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md), text-to-self connection prompts, [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) text into segments with connection questions [Chunking reduces working memory load, freeing capacity for integration.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]
- **After reading:** summarizing, comparing predictions to outcomes, [Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md) of how new ideas revise old ones

### Target Learners
- Learners who possess relevant knowledge but fail to retrieve it spontaneously — the most common case in classrooms [Dochy et al.'s review shows activated prior knowledge strongly predicts learning.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0959-4752(99)00004-8) [+M]
- Novices in a domain, who benefit from organizers and analogies that supply a missing knowledge structure
- Less beneficial for learners with rich, well-organized domain knowledge, who activate spontaneously and may find prompts redundant [Expertise reversal: support that helps novices can hinder experts.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension and conceptual understanding of new material
- Schema construction: integrating new information into existing knowledge structures
- Transfer: recognizing when familiar knowledge applies to unfamiliar problems [Analogical reasoning supports transfer by mapping prior structures onto new situations.](../principles/analogical-reasoning.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Diagnose what learners know about the topic (quick probe, pre-assessment, or discussion) — including misconceptions.
2. Present an [Advance Organizer](../elements/advance-organizers.md) or [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) that provides a structure for the new material.
3. Ask learners to make explicit predictions or connections before engaging the new content ([Activation](../elements/activation.md)).
4. During the reading or lesson, prompt learners to connect new information to the activated knowledge through [Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) or structured questions.
5. After the reading, have learners reconcile predictions with what they learned and explain how the new material changed or extended their prior understanding ([Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md)).
6. Address surfaced misconceptions directly with contrasting cases or [Cognitive Conflict](../elements/cognitive-conflict.md).

## Examples
- **Anticipation guides** — learners agree or disagree with statements about a topic before reading, then revisit them after; the statements activate relevant knowledge and create a purpose for reading.
- **KWL charts** — learners record what they *Know*, what they *Want* to know, and later what they *Learned*, making activation and integration visible.
- **Recht & Leslie's baseball study** — readers with high baseball knowledge comprehended a technical passage about baseball far better than low-knowledge readers of equal reading ability, demonstrating that domain knowledge, not general skill, drives comprehension. [doi:10.1037/0022-0663.80.1.16](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.80.1.16) [+S]
- **[Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org)** — published classroom routines for pre-reading activation, including picture walks and text-preview strategies.
