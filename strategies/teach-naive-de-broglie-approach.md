# Teach the naive De Broglie approach to QM alongside the Schrödinger equation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend correcting what they see as a curricular skew: the original 'naive' De Broglie approach to QM is typically neglected in existing curricula in favor of the Schrödinger equation. They argue "That skew appears rather detrimental; it would be similar to the hardly imaginable scenario of geometrical optics being neglected in favor of wave analysis," and hope their Section IV examples offer enough motivation to present more De Broglie QM. The approach treats stationary states as standing particle-waves and yields quantization conditions with far less mathematical effort.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Willingness to accept approximations accurate to numerical factors (e.g., the harmonic-oscillator quantum comes out about 25% below the exact ℏω)
#### Constraints
- The De Broglie approach neglects coordinate dependence of the wavelength by approximating the potential as constant; the WKB approximation, conceptually close, remains formal and mathematically involved

### Target Learners
- college and graduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- intuitive understanding of quantization
- standing-wave reasoning about quantum systems
