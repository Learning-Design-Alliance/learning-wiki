# Embed brief experiential wellness activities longitudinally within existing health professions curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating wellness strategies into existing coursework rather than adding standalone courses: brief structured activities such as reflective journaling, guided discussion, and applied skill practice can be embedded within existing classes, delivered longitudinally through repeated low-intensity experiential activities that support habit formation, and paired with small-group discussion elements to build psychological safety. The authors state these approaches "require minimal financial investment and can be implemented without specialized infrastructure."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional format matters: small-group and discussion-based learning environments are described as enhancing psychological safety, peer connection, and participation
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from a single faith-based private university with small elective class sizes; the authors call for mixed methods, multisite, and longitudinal research before broader generalization

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing and prehealth students
- Health professions students more broadly
- General undergraduate populations in elective wellness courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Coping and stress management skills
- Health behavior change and habit formation
- Self-awareness and professional resilience
