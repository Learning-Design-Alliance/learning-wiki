# Use structured active learning techniques — think-pair-share, brainstorming, demonstration, and group work — in IPLS teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article details four major active learning techniques for IPLS courses, grounded in a constructivist approach in which students build their own knowledge. Of think-pair-share it notes the technique "is quite simple in terms of preparation but surprisingly successful in terms of students' engagement and learning outcomes across all learning environments [23]." Brainstorming generates ideas in groups with all ideas welcomed without criticism; demonstrations connect theories to applications with student prediction and reflection; group work assigns designated roles such as leader, recorder, and presenter to hold members accountable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- TPS questions must be conscientiously aligned with instructional goals
- Group activities need clear instructions, designated member roles, and timelines to avoid workload imbalance
- Brainstorming needs ground rules and about 5 to 10 minutes for best idea generation
#### Constraints
- TPS can be dominated by one student, can be noisy, and may create time pressure and stress for some students
- Demonstrations require preparation time, appropriate spaces, expensive resources, and risk student inactivity
- Group work can be time-consuming and may lead to inefficiencies if not managed well

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life sciences students in introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- critical thinking, problem solving, communication and teamwork skills
- engagement and collaboration
