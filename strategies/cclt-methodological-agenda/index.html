Methodological agenda for CCLT-inspired CSCL research: new cognitive load measures, multilevel analysis, temporality, and broader methodologies¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article identifies four pressing methodological developments for CCLT-based CSCL research: measuring cognitive load over time (e.g., physiological data such as electrodermal activity, skin temperature, and heart rate variability, plus non-intrusive speech features like pauses and pitch peaks); taking multiple levels of analysis into account (e.g., individual and group prior knowledge, using the Group Actor-Partner Interdependence Model); studying temporality and events over time (e.g., Dynamic Bayesian Networks); and expanding methodological breadth beyond experiments, including design-based research.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Further research is necessary to determine which physiological measures are adequately suited to measure cognitive load

Constraints¶

  • Subjective rating scales such as Paas' 9-point scale give only a single measure of cognitive load, whereas load during CSCL probably varies over time

Target Learners¶

  • students participating in online and face-to-face collaborative learning

Target Learning Goals¶

  • measuring and understanding cognitive load and transactive processes during collaboration

Related Strategies¶

  • Adopt an integrative methodological toolkit using machine learning and computational models to capture SR's multi-level, temporal, and social dynamics
  • Use multimodal data channels combining subjective and objective measures to overcome single-channel constraints in SSRL research
  • Research agenda: investigate affective dynamics with within-person, longitudinal, and multi-channel methods
  • Broaden co-regulation methodology using trace data, server logs, episodic coding and multilevel modelling
  • Integrate physiological, dialog, video, and self-report data to analyze collaboration at individual and group levels
