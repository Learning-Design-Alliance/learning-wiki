# Plan unannounced in situ simulation with management support, cancellation rules, replacement staff, and simulation-only labelling of equipment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
For unannounced in situ simulation the article recommends multifaceted planning: well-established cooperation between educational planners and departmental management, active involvement of representatives from all healthcare professional groups, and preparedness to cancel or postpone during heavy patient loads or staff shortages. Because "unannounced ISS requires multifaceted planning and the need for good management support", extra staff must replace participants so that real patients are not put at risk, and medication and equipment should carry simulation-only labels.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Unannounced ISS must not pose any risk to real-life patients, which means extra staff must replace staff participating in the unannounced ISS.
- Using labels marked "Simulation only" is recommended as a precaution to avoid medication or equipment being mixed up with real clinical items.
- Well-established cooperation between educational planners and departmental management is required, and involving representatives from all healthcare professional groups results in better planning.
#### Constraints
- The average reported rate of cancellation for unannounced ISS is 28–67%, but the percentage seems to go down as training matures.

### Target Learners
- postgraduate interprofessional clinical teams
- simulation instructors and departmental management

### Target Learning Goals
- team training in the real clinical workplace
- organisational learning and detection of latent patient safety threats
