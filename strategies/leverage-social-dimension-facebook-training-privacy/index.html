Professors integrating Facebook should leverage its social dimension while providing training and addressing privacy concerns.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends incorporating Facebook into university learning resources by taking advantage of its social nature to increase communication, collaboration and participation. It advises that "Before using Facebook for class purposes, the professor should provide specific training about the features of Facebook that will be used in the course and address any kind of concern expressed by students," and cites the recommendation to use a "shell profile" for the professor with no personal information.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Specific training on the Facebook features used in the course
  • Addressing student concerns before class use

Constraints¶

  • Students care about privacy when communicating with instructors through social media; "friending" professors is still taboo for most students per the ECAR study

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • communication and collaboration in course contexts
  • increasing student participation

Claims¶

  • Communication, collaboration, and resource/material sharing each have a significant positive effect on the educational usage of Facebook. [+W]
  • Educational usage of Facebook is explained directly by purposes of usage and indirectly by adoption, with purposes mediating adoption's effect. [+W]
  • Facebook adoption has a significant positive effect on the purposes of Facebook usage, explaining approximately 81% of its variance. [+W]
  • Usefulness, ease of use, social influence, facilitating conditions and community identity each have a significant positive influence on Facebook adoption. [+W]
  • Social Influence is the most important factor in predicting students' adoption of Facebook for educational purposes. [+W]
  • Social Relations is perceived as the most important purpose of Facebook usage among students. [+W]
  • Facebook usage in higher education carries potential problems ranging from educators' dominance to privacy concerns [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Institutions should gently promote pre-registration hall and departmental Facebook groups while avoiding aggressive marketing, teaching or pastoral interventions in students' social space.
  • Use social media affordances to scaffold problem finding and solution generation in problem-centered learning
