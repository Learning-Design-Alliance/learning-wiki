# Embed structured self-regulation and time management supports in higher education instruction, advising, and first-year curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that self-regulation and time management relate strongly to academic self-efficacy, the article recommends that "educators are encouraged to incorporate structured interventions that explicitly target students' behavioral self-regulation capacities", including "goal setting, attentional regulation, planning, and task prioritization". Advisors are urged to offer personalized weekly study plans and brief self-regulation coaching, and universities to embed these competencies in first-year orientation programs and academic skills courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
