# Conditionally include more public performance elements in the early medical curriculum or use them in selection, pending stronger evidence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that if the verbal performance effect were stronger or replicated in a larger study, "there could be a case made for including more public performance elements in the early medical school curriculum, or alternatively, for selection of students based partly on these relatively objective criteria". The recommendation is explicitly conditional on replication, since the current evidence is a modest effect from a single cohort. It rests on the parsimonious explanation that high-stakes performance experience mitigates performance anxiety in examinations such as the OSCE.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Replication of the effect in a larger or multi-school study before implementation
#### Constraints
- Current evidence covers a single cohort at a single medical school and justifies only limited conclusions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- medical school applicants

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing performance anxiety in clinical examinations
- selecting medical students on personal qualities
