Use MAPS-based reflection questions to prepare students to critique AI-generated physics solutions¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Before evaluating an AI solution, students critique an AI output to a related preparation problem using structured reflection questions organized around the MAPS rubric categories. In this study, Critique-condition groups used "15 reflection questions organized around five MAPS categories, three per category, rather than the full MAPS rubric or scoring-level descriptors", followed by a common task evaluating a more complex AI solution with MAPS-aligned ratings and written justifications.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A curated AI-generated solution to a related preparation problem, MAPS-aligned reflection questions, and a common evaluation task with scoring questions and written justifications

Constraints¶

  • In this study the critiqued AI solution was fixed rather than student-prompted; the authors call for future work on more authentic AI use including students' own prompts and follow-up questions

Target Learners¶

  • introductory undergraduate physics lab groups

Target Learning Goals¶

  • expert-aligned critique of AI-generated reasoning
  • calibrated trust in AI outputs

Affordances¶

  • Jonassen Prompt Variation Maps Framework For Lrm Reasoning

Related Strategies¶

  • Design PjBL projects so students must evaluate, critique, and improve AI-generated outputs rather than prohibiting AI use
