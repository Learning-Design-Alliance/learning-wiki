# Reduce lecture duration from 60 minutes to 40-45 minutes for students below 21 years

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on measured fatigue effects, the authors recommend timetable and course-structure changes: shortening lecture duration for students under 21. The article states the fatigue seen after an hour of intense Mathematical Physics training "does advocate reducing the durat ion of a lec- ture from 60 minutes to 40-45 minutes for students below 21 years", and adds that reduced response time implies reduced concentration, so much instructor information would anyway not have been absorbed. It also recommends reviewing the methodology used for teaching difficult subjects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Measurement of student reaction time before and after lectures to establish fatigue levels for the student population concerned
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is directed at students below 21 years, and the methodology caveat is reserved for Delhi University, since the authors state they are in no position to comment on scenarios elsewhere

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics/electronics students aged 18-21

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining attention and information absorption during difficult theory lectures such as Mathematical Physics
