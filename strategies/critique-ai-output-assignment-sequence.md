# Scaffolded critique-of-AI-output assignment sequence with paired verification of individual understanding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A strategy from the Evaluating & Fixing AI-Produced Work room in which the object of study is output the student did not write: students read, test, critique, debug, and repair AI-produced code. The room's approaches include giving students AI-generated solutions to critique, improve, or test—"validating behavior, finding defects or unnecessary complexity, identifying missing edge cases, designing unit tests"—and pairing AI-supported coursework with in-class quizzes or examinations that verify individual understanding. Responsibility for formulating prompts and evaluating results shifts gradually onto the student.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assignments must be designed so the student, not the AI, performs the critique, since any text handed to a student can become a prompt.
- Critique activities outside controlled environments remain open to students using AI to complete the evaluations.
#### Constraints
- Oral examinations provide strong evidence of individual understanding but are impractical for large cohorts.
- AI often produces good-quality solutions for introductory programming tasks, leaving little to critique.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate computing students
- students in large programming cohorts

### Target Learning Goals
- code reading, evaluation, and critique
- software engineering practices such as testing and code review
