# Minimise extraneous cognitive load in student MCQ-writing by assigning topics and modelling scenario-based questions without introducing Bloom's taxonomy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend redesigning student MCQ-writing tasks to reduce perceived demands and increase confidence in peer questions. They suggest instructors "model and direct students to write scenario-based clinical questions without introducing Bloom's taxonomy", since students in this study tended not to use the Bloom's guidance during the semester. They also recommend assigning topics directly to students to reduce the load of choosing topics and to ensure the question bank covers the core curriculum evenly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor time to assign topics across the curriculum and to model scenario-based question writing
- A scaffolding session introducing the task and platform before implementation
#### Constraints
- Students in this study tended not to refer to Bloom's taxonomy guidance during the semester, preferring peer feedback and other questions as models
- The task was delivered in the first half of the year while the examination occurred at the end, which may have reduced perceived immediate relevance

### Target Learners
- fourth-year medical students in clinically based years

### Target Learning Goals
- higher-order thinking (application and analysis) in pathology
- knowledge synthesis from multiple sources
