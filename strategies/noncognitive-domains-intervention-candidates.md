# Treat noncognitive domains (self-efficacy, personality, behavior problems) as plausible candidates for intervention to support educational achievement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 6 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 2 review, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 6 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the study found strong connections between noncognitive domains and educational achievement, the authors recommend that "these domains are also plausible candidates for intervention". This is an implementable direction for educational practice: interventions targeting self-efficacy, personality-related traits, or behavior problems, alongside the trend toward personalized learning supported by educational technology.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal research such as cross-lagged analysis is needed to explore causality more explicitly, as the authors state
#### Constraints
- The genetic mediation of these correlations means the article does not establish that changing the domains will change achievement; causality is not yet established
