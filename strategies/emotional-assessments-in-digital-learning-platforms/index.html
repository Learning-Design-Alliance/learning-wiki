Include emotional assessments into new digital learning platforms to address emotional stress and teacher burnout before attrition¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The commentary recommends that as K-12 teachers and administrators make the shift to digital learning, they "includ[e] emotional assessments into the new digital learning platforms in order to address areas of emotional stress and teacher burnout before leading to attrition." The strategy treats emotion measurement as an early-warning mechanism embedded in the platform itself, so that stress and burnout can be identified and addressed before they contribute to teachers leaving. It is offered as an application of Jarrell et al.'s feedback-emotion findings to teacher training.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Digital learning platforms must include emotional assessments as part of the shift to digital learning

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 teachers
  • school administrators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • teacher emotional well-being
  • teacher self-efficacy
  • reduction of teacher burnout and attrition

Claims¶

  • Teacher burnout is linked to contextual and compositional elements of the school environment (as cited by the commentary from Shackleton et al., 2019) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Measure and respond to student emotions continuously in digital platforms and promote mindfulness and emotional awareness
  • Foster positive emotions, support self-regulatory skills, and provide emotional supports for students shifting to digital learning
  • Develop and test interventions and teacher reflective practices to harness emotions as malleable factors in K-12 settings
  • Integrate mindfulness throughout the school day via mindful moments, pausing before reacting, and reflective norms
  • Frustration and Stress Prevention/Reduction Techniques
