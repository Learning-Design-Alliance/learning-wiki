Keep pre-class teaching videos short (10–20 minutes), goal-targeted, and followed by checking homework¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

For the pre-class preparation topic, the article recommends that teachers produce concise, high-quality videos: "The duration of the videos should range from 10 to 20 min." Each video should target a specific chapter goal, be easily understandable, and exploit pause and playback features for autonomous review; a follow-up homework task lets students judge their learning status, and assignments should cover main points without being too extensive.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need software tools and skills to create clear video content within a concise length
  • Videos must fit students' attention time range and developmental characteristics

Constraints¶

  • Low-quality video lectures may lead to poor learning outcomes, per the article

Target Learners¶

  • college and university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • pre-class independent learning
  • timely detection of learning gaps

Affordances¶

  • Flipped Classroom Three Phase Model

Claims¶

  • Evidence on flipped-classroom effectiveness is inconsistent across disciplines, with some studies showing no gains in satisfaction, motivation, or exam results [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Limit flipped classroom video duration to around 20 minutes and use opening quizzes
  • Limit pre-class video length to 20 minutes to reduce student burden
  • Keep online lecture videos under twenty minutes and segment by topic with speed control
