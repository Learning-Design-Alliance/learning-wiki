# Integrate research on licensing effectiveness with research on workforce needs and mobility

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that research on national licensing not be conducted in isolation from workforce planning. It argues that in highly developed countries licensing systems "have to operate within the context of doctor shortages and the increasing mobility of the global medical workforce", and concludes that "Future research should therefore not only be directed at the effectiveness of national licensing to drive up standards, but integrated with broader questions concerning workforce needs and the compatibility of national licensing with workforce planning policy".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Policy-makers and regulators must consider the relative merits of licensing options in relation to doctor shortages that necessitate flows of workers from overseas
#### Constraints
- Framed for highly developed countries; the article notes the developing-world perspective, including brain drain, is equally important but outside this study's scope

### Target Learners
- medical regulators and policy-makers
- medical graduates and international medical graduates

### Target Learning Goals
- standard-setting through licensing assessment
- sustainable medical workforce supply
