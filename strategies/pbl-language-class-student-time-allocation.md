# Implement PBL in language classes so students take control of the learning process and the teacher serves as a facilitator, with more class time allocated to students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educational systems, especially those short of human resources, implement PBL in language classes so that students take control of the teaching-learning process and refer to the teacher mainly for debriefing, allowing a single teacher to deal with a great number of learners. It further advises syllabus designers that "more time should be allocated to students than the teacher" and that assessment type should be congruent with the PBL process used in the classroom.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A precise tutorial process defined and piloted for the class
- Assessment congruent with the specific PBL process used
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on one study of intermediate learners at a single language institute; the article notes gender, age, first language, background, thinking style and learning style may affect results but were not considered

### Target Learners
- intermediate EFL learners
- learners in educational systems short of human resources

### Target Learning Goals
- speaking proficiency
- independent learning and deep understanding rather than memorization
