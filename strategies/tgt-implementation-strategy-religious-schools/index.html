Adopt cooperative TGT as an alternative mathematics teaching approach, supported by teacher training and ministry guidelines¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that mathematics teachers, especially in religious schools, replace lecture-only and individual-drill teaching with cooperative TGT, which combines group activities with tournaments. The authors state that "Teachers should use the cooperative TGT learning approach in ther teaching and learning proceses because this method had been tested numerous numbers of times and it was found that this cooperative approach had helped to increase student understanding and their mathematics communication." They further recommend that education ministries provide inhouse training or short courses for current mathematics teachers and establish guidelines in the form of modules or manuals, since cooperative TGT differs from unstructured conventional group work.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need inhouse training or short courses to assist them in using cooperative TGT learning
  • Ministry-established guidelines in the form of modules or manuals for mathematics teachers
  • Group work activities designed according to students' learning-style preferences

Constraints¶

  • The concept of cooperative TGT learning is different from the unstructured conventional group work
  • Generalization from the supporting study is limited to Grade 11 students in one religious school in Riau with a 10-week intervention

Target Learners¶

  • Grade 11 students in religious secondary schools (Madrasah Aliyah)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Mathematics understanding
  • Mathematics communication
  • Positive attitudes and interest in learning mathematics

Affordances¶

  • Tgt Teams Games Tournament Structure

Claims¶

  • Cooperative TGT significantly improves Grade 11 students' mathematics communication compared with conventional teaching [+M]
  • Cooperative TGT significantly improves Grade 11 students' mathematics understanding compared with conventional teaching over a five-week intervention [+M]
  • Students in Cooperative TGT classrooms learned to socialise while learning mathematics and became more active learners [+W]
  • The TGT experimental group significantly outperformed the lecture-based control group on the mathematics posttest [+W]
  • Grade VIII students taught mathematics with the TGT web-based game technique showed a significant pretest-to-posttest achievement gain over three weeks [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Adopt appropriate mathematical games in junior secondary mathematics teaching
  • Implement TGT game-based cooperative learning in after-school programs with adequately trained teachers and extended treatment duration
  • Mathematics teachers should incorporate concept mapping and cooperative learning strategies in senior secondary trigonometry instruction
