# Explicate, Validate, and Address Negative Emotions

## Description
When learners come into a course with negative emotions and expectations, it can affect not only their learning but also their capacity to learn. If you’re teaching a course which you know learners may have negative emotions about, make it part of your opening course routine to explicate, validate, and address those concerns. Explicate that you empathize with the students that for many of them, there is the perception that the subject is difficult and draining. Professors should be honest with students about what people find hard about the course and recognize that it’s okay, fair, and normal to feel that way. Validate that it may not feel particularly valuable to be studying texts from a time that may not relate to our current events. Address the reason we study ancient works isn’t because those stories happened but because they happen. Make the content relevant. Show that the class will provide opportunities to put on critical lenses that will allow us to better see and understand the events of today.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires instructors to be empathetic and open-minded, as well as knowledgeable about common learner concerns. It is necessary to create a safe space for learners to express their feelings.
#### Constraints
- Addressing negative emotions may take up valuable class time. Some learners may be resistant to sharing their feelings or may not respond to the strategies used by the instructor.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- College and university students, particularly those in courses known for high levels of anxiety or perceived irrelevance, adult learners with prior negative experiences

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve learner engagement and academic performance by acknowledging and addressing negative emotions, creating a supportive learning environment, and increasing the relevance of course content

### Affordances
- Reduces anxiety and resistance by openly addressing negative emotions, validates learners' feelings and experiences, increases motivation and engagement by making content relevant, and fosters a supportive classroom environment.

### Personalization
- Allow learners to share their specific concerns and feelings about the course. Tailor examples and applications to address their individual interests and needs. Offer resources and support for managing anxiety and building confidence.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor learner engagement and participation in class. Collect feedback on learners' attitudes and feelings about the course. Track learner performance on assignments and exams.

## Impact
- Research suggests that addressing negative emotions can improve learner engagement and academic performance. Creating a supportive classroom environment can also reduce anxiety and increase motivation.
