# Introduce the random-sampling experiment at several levels of a physics laboratory course

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes the experiment "as a pedagogical tool to teach probabilistic measurements and their manipulations" and states it "can be introduced in a physics laboratory course at several diﬀerent levels." At the lowest level students demonstrate the zero mean, maximum, minimum and RMS voltage of an AC source; at the next level they calculate voltage probability distributions and reconstruct waveforms; at an advanced level they carry out the statistical analysis and simulations with computer programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Elementary C-programs running on an ordinary PC are sufficient for data analysis, though graph paper, pencil and a pocket calculator also suffice
#### Constraints
- Computer analysis is not essential but is instructive and opens up possibilities of playing around with various parameters

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students at primary or advanced laboratory level

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding probabilistic observations
- probability distributions and their manipulation
- statistical analysis of experimental data
