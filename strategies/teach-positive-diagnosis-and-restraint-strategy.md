# Teach diagnostic restraint through explicit alarm-versus-functional instruction, case-based reasoning practice, bedside modeling of positive diagnosis, and restraint-rewarding assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that DGBI education move beyond lecturing on Rome criteria toward directly targeting applied diagnostic reasoning. It proposes explicitly teaching how to distinguish alarm features from hallmark functional symptoms, practicing positive Rome-based diagnosis under uncertainty via case-based teaching, modeling positive-diagnosis language at the bedside and in small groups, and reforming assessment so that "assessment systems should reward appropriate diagnostic restraint and value‐based reasoning, not only exhaustive differential generation."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educational strategies should explicitly teach how to distinguish alarm features from hallmark functional symptoms, how to make a positive Rome‐based diagnosis, and when diagnostic testing is or is not justified.
#### Constraints
- The article notes case-based exposure attenuated but did not eliminate overuse, and its own evidence is cross-sectional and vignette-based, precluding causal inference.

### Target Learners
- medical students in clinical training years

### Target Learning Goals
- Rome-based positive diagnosis of DGBIs
- accurate alarm-feature interpretation
- appropriate diagnostic restraint under uncertainty
