# Make certified, regularly repeated CPR training with practical skills practice a mandatory component of nursing and health-associated curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that CPR education go beyond theory: students "should be encouraged to learn and to practice CPR procedural techniques beyond theoretical CPR training alone", and that "certified programs training basic skills of CPR should be a mandatory component in the all health-associated fields like medical, paramedical, and nursing colleges and faculties". The recommendation rests on their finding that guideline knowledge and practical approach were outdated. They add that training programs reduce anxiety and increase self-confidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must be certified and cover basic CPR skills, not theory alone
- Programs should be repeated at regular intervals and updated with current guidelines
#### Constraints
- The article cites, rather than tests, evidence that permanent basic and advanced life support training improved nursing professionals' knowledge and skill

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students
- health-associated students in medical and paramedical fields

### Target Learning Goals
- current-guideline CPR knowledge
- practical CPR skills
