# Test whether adding a study skills course to the curriculum reduces depressive symptoms in depressed students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend two follow-up study designs before firm conclusions: a controlled trial of a study skills program among depressed students, and a follow-up of students with high depressive symptoms to see whether treating depression improves study skills. This follows their finding that "poor study skills were found to correlate with higher depressive symptoms". The recommendation is explicitly conditional on further confirmation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Confirmation of the correlational finding in future research before firm conclusions and recommendations
#### Constraints
- The current evidence is correlational and from a single medical school; causality cannot be claimed
