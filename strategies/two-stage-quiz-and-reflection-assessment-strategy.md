# Use two-stage quizzes and reflection-based exam remediation in introductory physics assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The RPS course implemented two assessment strategies replacing standard closed-book individual exams. In the fall, a "two-stage" quiz had students solve real-world problems individually for the first 30 minutes, then form ad-hoc groups and submit a group solution, with the final score never lower than the individual score. In spring, students submitted quiz reflections explaining their mistakes for 50% of missed points back, consulting peers or TAs. Quizzes were entirely open resource in both semesters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Real-world problems suited to group re-solving
- Peer or teaching assistant availability for the reflection component
#### Constraints
- The two strategies were used in different semesters, so their separate effects are not disentangled in this study

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students in introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- problem solving
- learning from assessment mistakes
