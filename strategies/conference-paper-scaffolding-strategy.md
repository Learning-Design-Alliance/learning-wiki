# Retain the conference-style paper with staged scaffolding: checkpoints, draft feedback, sample papers, and scope calibration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends preserving the conference-style paper as the culminating artifact while adding staged support: progress checkpoints, milestone-based expectations, examples of successful papers, and scope calibration by student level. One implementation treats the manuscript "as a sequence of smaller scholarly tasks rather than a single final deadline," such as an early problem statement and annotated source list, a methods update, a results draft, then the full paper. Instructors may also separate writing in a conference style from requiring actual submission to a venue.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Topic approval and scope check early in the semester
- Annotated literature checkpoint early to mid semester
- Methods or progress memo at mid semester
- Draft results discussion in late mid semester
- Sample conference paper or template available throughout
- Optional submission track at end of semester
#### Constraints
- For undergraduates working in groups, narrower project questions, structured templates, and more explicit guidance on reading and synthesizing technical papers are recommended; graduate students may handle greater independence or optional external submission

### Target Learners
- graduate engineering students
- undergraduate senior engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- scientific writing
- research framing
- interdisciplinary project scoping
