# Actively support student community formation through counterspaces, cohort-bonding, and affinity groups, with implicit bias training for advisors

> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article argues the field's status quo promotes an unhealthy, extremely competitive environment, and advisors may suffer from implicit affinity bias, indirectly imposing a two-tiered system between bridge program students and those enrolled directly in graduate school. It recommends robust implicit bias and inclusive communication training for all in support roles, and financial and administrative support for counterspaces and cohort-bonding activities, citing Ong et al. 2018.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implicit bias and inclusive communication training for all serving in a support or advisory role
- Financial and administrative support for counterspaces and cohort-bonding activities
#### Constraints
- The time and financial commitment necessary for a workshop series and other initiatives is substantial, so NASA or a regional partnership should provide significant support

### Target Learners
- bridge program students and early career scientists from under-represented backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- sense of belonging
- navigating and mitigating systemic issues
