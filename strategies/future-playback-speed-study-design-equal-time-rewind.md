# In future studies of video playback speed, give learners equal total time and allow rewinding or re-watching, and assess long-term retention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that future work emulate the full utility of recorded lectures by allowing students "to rewind parts of the lecture they did not understand or re-watch a lecture a second time," possibly by giving both speed groups the same allotted learning time, and by assessing "long-term information retention," which the present study did not test. This design would separate time-on-task from playback-speed effects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies must permit rewinding and re-watching, and control total learning time across speed conditions, to reflect real-world use of recorded lectures.
#### Constraints
- The current study tested only immediate recall without rewinding or re-watching, so its findings may not generalize to real study behavior.

### Target Learners
- medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of novel lecture material
