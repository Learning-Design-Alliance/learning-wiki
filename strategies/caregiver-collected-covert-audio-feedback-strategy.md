# Caregiver-collected covert audio recording protocol adapted for pediatric residency feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A modified audit-and-feedback protocol in which parents or caregivers carry a concealed audio recorder into their child's appointment, recordings are coded with 4C plus supplemental expert review, and findings are shared with residents. The study demonstrates "how a modified protocol was successfully introduced in a pediatrics residency clinic," with caregivers rather than patients consenting, unlike the adult medicine protocol.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Written consent from residents and caregivers; recordings kept on a secure server approved for patient-sensitive data and not shared outside the research team.
#### Constraints
- The article notes inherent logistical and patient privacy challenges, and that stakeholder comfort with audio recording in pediatric settings was not systematically evaluated.

### Target Learners
- pediatric and medicine-pediatric residents (PGY 1–3)

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying learning needs and exemplary performance in routine clinical practice
- contextualizing care
