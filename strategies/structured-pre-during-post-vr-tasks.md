# Design VR lessons with structured pre-, during-, and post-experience tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 causal, 2 review), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends wrapping VR experiences in supporting instructional structure rather than relying on immersion alone. It cites design cases showing that "VR activities benefit from structured pre-, during-, and post-experience tasks, explicit scaffolding, and close alignment between interactive features and subject-specific learning goals." It also suggests embedding captivating scenarios so that potentially mundane facts become engaging storylines. This strategy operationalizes the CAMIL-based principle that immersion must be channeled toward the targeted learning processes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clearly defined learning goals, preparatory activities, guidance during the experience, and follow-up tasks requiring reflection or production
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as an interpretation consistent with pedagogical design cases, not as a manipulation tested in the study itself

### Target Learners
- college students in STEM courses
- novice learners of anatomy

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of factual and spatial science content learned in VR

### Affordances
- Camil Immersive Learning Model
