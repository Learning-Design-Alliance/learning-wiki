# Targeted training emphasizing the importance of each measurement, case-based training on real scenarios, and revised contextual alerts with mandatory justifications for skipped measurements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A set of implementable actions the article recommends for addressing the perceived-irrelevance driver of skipped CDSA measurements. The article states that "targeted training should emphasize the importance of each measurement and provide practical strategies for integrating them effectively into consultations", and proposes case-based training sessions where healthcare workers analyze real scenarios showing the impact of missing or inaccurate measurements. It also recommends revising contextual alerts within ePOCT+ to briefly explain why specific measurements are necessary, and requiring a justification when a measurement is skipped.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration with the ePOCT+ CDSA workflow; training time and facilitation for case-based sessions
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that making the opting-out option more difficult could further encourage entry of false values without taking the measurement

### Target Learners
- healthcare workers in primary health centers
- non-clinical triage staff

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing the clinical relevance of vital-sign and anthropometric measurements
- performing complete and accurate assessments
