# Gather feedback from multiple trainee and expert user groups at each prototype iteration to drive simulator design improvements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The manufacturer tested LSM prototypes across three user groups between 2017 and 2018, using closed-ended 7-point Likert and open-ended questions in an LSM Questionnaire, and used each round's feedback to make concrete design changes: smoother sides and improved areolae after resident testing, medical illustrators for four skin tones and higher-fidelity scars after student testing, and softer tissue, modified nipple tissue, and reduced weight after expert testing. Future unbiased studies are needed to test effects on knowledge and skill acquisition, retention, and transfer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to both novice trainee users and clinical experts, questionnaire instruments, and IRB approval for user-testing
#### Constraints
- The study sponsor was the manufacturer, creating significant conflict of interest and bias; sampling was inadequate and inferential statistics were not possible

### Target Learners
- physician residents
- nurse-midwifery students
- clinical lactation experts

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluation of simulator realism and functionality to improve educational tools

## Claims

- [Physician residents rated the LSM prototype's nipples and breast tissue as realistic (5.5/7) and reported it helped them learn breast assessment in a 2017 workshop](../claims/resident-lsm-prototype-realism-2017.md) [+W]
