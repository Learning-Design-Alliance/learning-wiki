# Add structure and supports for struggling online learners, such as an early strategies module and flexible deadlines with incentivized content ordering

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that failing students applied a non-optimal, quiz-first strategy across the course, the authors recommend course redesign for students who struggle with learning to learn online. They write that "Future versions of the course will focus on building better supports, such as an early module on successful course strategies, and course structure enhancements, such as adding flexible deadlines and enforced or incentivized content ordering." The recommendation responds to the course's intentional maximum flexibility, which the authors note may be detrimental for failing students lacking self-discipline and awareness of personal limitations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recognition that the fully flexible course design depends on self-regulation skills novice learners may not have developed
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is a planned future modification, not a tested intervention in this article

### Target Learners
- non-traditional online higher education students
- novice learners in self-paced online courses

### Target Learning Goals
- adopting successful study strategies and self-regulated engagement in online courses
