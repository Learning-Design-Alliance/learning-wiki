# Identify specific scenarios, learner populations and domains where VR surpasses or enhances traditional trauma training before integrating it into curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that future work concentrate on "outlining specific scenarios, learner populations and domains where VR surpasses or enhances traditional learning techniques", enabling tailored applications, hybrid approaches, and educational innovations that leverage VR's immersive and experiential benefits. Suggested future applications include remote training and telemedicine, integration into assessment and certification, multiplayer interdisciplinary training, and rare scenarios such as REBOA or mass casualty incidents that are difficult to simulate in real life.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Research identifying where VR outperforms traditional methods before curriculum integration
#### Constraints
- VR should not be seen as a complete replacement for traditional training

### Target Learners
- medical students
- healthcare professionals in trauma training

### Target Learning Goals
- trauma management skills
- interdisciplinary team training and communication
