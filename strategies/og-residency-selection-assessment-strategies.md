# Assess social sensibility and multitasking in residency applicants using OSCEs, situational judgment tests and simulated multitasking scenarios

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes concrete evaluation methods for the two top-ranked competence areas. For social sensibility, it suggests simulated patient encounters and Objective Structured Clinical Examinations, which evaluate "clinical skills, communication, and professionalism in a standardized manner", plus situational judgment tests showing promise for noncognitive attributes. For multitasking and psychomotor abilities, it proposes simulated multitasking scenarios with tasks of different priority and difficulty — e.g. managing labor and delivery while monitoring fetal heart rate and coordinating care — and the Multi-Attribute Task Battery, a computerized flight simulator involving system monitoring, tracking, communication and resource management.
