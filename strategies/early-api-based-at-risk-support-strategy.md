# Early identification and support of at-risk first-year students using predicted API

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using the fitted prediction equations at the start of the academic year to estimate each new student's API and flag those at risk, since "if a student with a low academic achievement is detected, the corresponding interventions can be implemented". The authors propose support for all students with an estimated API ≤ 15, deliberately above the low-performance cutoff of 10 so that "all students at risk of failure" are not missed, accepting that some higher-performing students will also be flagged. Suggested interventions include supplemental instruction, tutorial classes, and guidance on study skills and note taking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to incoming students' high school GPA and university access test component grades before the term begins
- Capacity to deliver support services (tutorials, supplemental instruction, study-skills guidance)
#### Constraints
- Flagging at API ≤ 15 deliberately over-includes some students who do not need reinforcement
- Equations are cohort- and degree-specific and validated only at one institution

### Target Learners
- First-year university science students, especially those predicted to be at risk of failure

### Target Learning Goals
- Increasing first-year academic achievement and student retention
