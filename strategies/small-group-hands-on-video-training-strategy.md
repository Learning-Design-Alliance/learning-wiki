# Train point-of-care diagnostic users in small groups with hands-on practice and visual and video content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on observed variation in training delivery and comprehension results, the authors recommend that trainings be conducted in small groups with adequate individual hands-on practice time and visual and video training content. The study's own training combined a presentation with a demonstration video, in-person demonstration, and hands-on practice reviewing the IFU, with materials adapted to locally relevant information. Careful attention should be paid to training content, quality, and delivery, and to assessment proctoring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adequate time for individual hands-on practice
- Training materials reviewed by the local study team for clarity and completeness before delivery
#### Constraints
- Degree of hands-on practice across sites varied based on available time; results are dependent on training quality and vary with language, facilitators, and duration

### Target Learners
- Health workers with varied education and training levels adopting a new point-of-care diagnostic test

### Target Learning Goals
- Operational proficiency with a diagnostic device workflow and accurate interpretation of test results
