# Recommendations for clinical teachers: use reasoning and follow-up questions to elicit elaboration, closed reproduction questions for knowledge recall, and reduce non-response

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article derives concrete questioning recommendations for CBL from its tree analysis and descriptive findings. It states: "When seeking to make students elaborate, teachers should primarily ask reasoning questions or follow-up questions. For eliciting reproductive answers from students, closed reproduction question are suitable." To increase participation, teachers should aim for low non-response, e.g. by avoiding initial reasoning questions on new topics and giving students ample time to reflect.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must distinguish question qualities (initial vs. follow-up, open vs. closed, reproduction vs. reasoning) when planning their questioning in CBL seminars.
#### Constraints
- The recommendations rest on observational associations from one video study; the discipline comparison is explorative and comparison to other formats such as problem-based learning is not possible from the printed evidence.

### Target Learners
- Advanced medical students in clinical (second clinical year) case-based seminars

### Target Learning Goals
- Application of basic biomedical knowledge to patient cases
- Clinical reasoning and elaboration
- Student participation in case discussion
