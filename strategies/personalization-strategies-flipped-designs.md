# Equip instructors with concrete personalization strategies when adopting flipped designs dominated by group activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because structural time reallocation alone did not raise perceived Personalization, the article recommends specific instructor behaviors: "This finding highlights the importance of equipping instructors with concrete strategies—such as individualized feedback on pre-class work, deliberate use of student names, and one-on-one progress check-ins—to maximize personalization when adopting flipped designs." These target the risk that freed class time is spent on standardized group activities rather than learner-specific interaction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must deliberately devote some class or feedback time to individualized, learner-specific interaction
#### Constraints
- The recommendation responds to a null result on the Personalization subscale in a moderately sized class where group-based activities dominated

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- perceived personalization of the learning environment
- individualized instructor feedback
