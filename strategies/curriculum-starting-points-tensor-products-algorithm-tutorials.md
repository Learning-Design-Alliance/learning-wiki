# Start QISE curriculum development with tensor products, eigenvalues/eigenvectors, and tutorials decomposing the most commonly taught algorithms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article names concrete starting points for research-based QISE curriculum development: tensor products and eigenvalues/eigenvectors, which "emerged as particular sites of instructor-perceived difficulty for students", plus tutorials breaking down the most commonly taught algorithms. It also urges the community to broaden focus beyond high-school materials and simulations and to build on projects centering historically underrepresented students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Builds on the core content list of topics covered in ≥ 80% of introductory QISE courses, which the authors call "just one intermediary step toward the development of research-based curricular materials".
#### Constraints
- The recommendations target introductory postsecondary QISE courses; the authors note existing DBER curriculum work "has been relatively specialized, often focusing on teaching quantum computing to high school students" and simulation resources.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students in introductory QISE courses

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational QISE mathematics, including tensor products and eigenvalues/eigenvectors
- conceptual understanding of commonly taught quantum algorithms
