# Show mastery-level visualisations sufficiently infrequently to avoid demotivation while still enabling intervention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that e-learning platforms display visualisations of students' mastery-level evolution sparingly: "visualisations related to mastery level should be shown sufficiently infrequent to avoid potential negative motivational effects, yet frequently enough to allow intervention in case of learning issues." They suggest platforms could facilitate interventions through alerts informing students when it seems advisable to ask teachers for additional support, with those alerts also shown to teachers. This balances the trust and metacognitive benefits observed in their study against the risk of demotivation from frequently showing downward mastery evolutions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A platform that estimates and logs mastery-level changes (e.g. via Elo ratings) so that evolution can be visualised and alerts triggered
#### Constraints
- Some participants' comments about motivation made the authors realise the potentially demotivating effects of frequently showing downward evolutions in mastery levels

### Target Learners
- middle school and high school adolescents using e-learning platforms

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining motivation while practising maths
- enabling timely teacher intervention for learning issues
