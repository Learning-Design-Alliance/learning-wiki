# Anti-oppressive co-design meeting practices: land acknowledgements, shared roles, breakout discussions, multiple input modes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The team adopted an explicitly anti-oppressive and anti-colonial approach to advisory group meetings, listing practices such as "taking turns doing a personal land acknowledgement", checking in as a group, sharing roles to flatten hierarchy, ensuring time for smaller breakout discussion to promote space for all voices, encouraging multiple modes of input/feedback, and actively welcoming a diversity of critical views. The broader process centered people with lived experience via group facilitation techniques, shared decision making, and accountability practices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Early engagement of community members in shaping innovation agendas, structured collaboration, and iterative feedback loops
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge the advisory group composition and facilitation process may have introduced biases, and certain voices facing compounded systemic barriers may not have been fully represented

### Target Learners
- co-design facilitators
- research teams
- healthcare organizations

### Target Learning Goals
- redistributing power in co-design processes by centering people with lived experience of systemic inequity
