# Adopt a hybrid DPBL model combining digital flexibility with in-person collaborative PBL activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review proposes integrating digital and in-person PBL activities as a potential solution to the tension between technological innovation and the social aspects of learning. The authors state that "One potential solution to this issue is to integrate a hybrid approach combining digital and in-person PBL activities" which "may offer a more supportive and balanced learning environment." This strategy addresses concerns about isolation, feedback gaps, and limited tactile realism while preserving the flexibility and autonomy that students value in digital formats.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires careful attention to social dynamics, particularly interactions between students and facilitators, and balancing digital tools with interpersonal interaction.
#### Constraints
- The review notes that DPBL still struggles to reconcile technological innovation with the social aspects of learning that remain pedagogically essential.

### Target Learners
- Medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Collaborative learning
- Social interaction
- Clinical knowledge and skills
