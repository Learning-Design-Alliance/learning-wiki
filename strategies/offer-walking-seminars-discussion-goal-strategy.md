# Offer walking seminars wherever the seminar goal is discussion, and start them early in a programme

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that programmes adopt outdoor walking seminars as a way to embed light-to-moderate physical activity in ordinary teaching, arguing that "if one has the option of conducting a seminar walking or sitting, one should always choose a walking seminar". They report having performed walking seminars with classes of up to 40 students at the same time and suggest the ideal subgroup size is three. One teacher proposed starting with walking seminars at the beginning of the programme to enable all students to get to know each other.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Seminar goals centred on discussion rather than information distribution
- An outdoor venue where discussion can be heard
#### Constraints
- The study could not separate the effects of walking from being outdoors, since both were introduced at the same time
- The method would benefit from being tested in different venues, e.g., ordinary city parks

### Target Learners
- university students
- teachers and course leaders

### Target Learning Goals
- increased physical activity during teaching
- improved seminar discussion and well-being
