# Use integrative prompts to scaffold the quantity of multiple text reading, combined with mapping and repeated delayed writing to support quality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers and instructors use integrative prompts to scaffold the quantity of multiple text reading, combined with other approaches such as mapping and repeated, delayed writing that can support quality. This strategy follows directly from the finding that integrative prompts improved quantity of integration while quality required different supports. The authors note that continued research on reading purpose regarding different aspects of text comprehension is promising and warranted.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prompts must direct attention to integrative, cross-document content rather than superficial details
#### Constraints
- Reading prompts only affect the quantity, but quality can be difficult to influence by prompts or by an expert network reference

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students reading multiple documents

### Target Learning Goals
- multiple document integration
