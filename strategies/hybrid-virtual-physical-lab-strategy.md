# Propose hybrid experiential learning combining virtual and physical laboratories to bridge theory and practice in quantum education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 5 studies (3 design, 1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students repeatedly expressed a desire for more virtual and/or physical laboratory opportunities, including hands-on real equipment. The author argues that implementing physical labs in general education is often impractical due to resource constraints and required specialized expertise, and that "the potential of hybrid experiential learning as a viable approach to bridge the gap between theoretical concepts and practical applications in STEM education" deserves exploration. Qualitative data showed 31.71%, 18.18%, and 33.33% of respondents expressed strong interest in expanding laboratory opportunities, including both virtual and physical formats.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a virtual laboratory platform as the baseline experiential component
- Physical laboratory resources or demonstrations where feasible
#### Constraints
- Implementing physical labs in general education courses is often impractical due to resource constraints and the specialized expertise required for quantum optical experiments

### Target Learners
- undergraduate general education students from science and arts backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting theoretical quantum concepts with practical experimental applications
