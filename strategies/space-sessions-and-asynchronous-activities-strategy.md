# Space blended course sessions over time and deepen asynchronous platform use to increase time on task and flexibility

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on students reporting insufficient time for self-directed learning, the authors propose revising course duration and "spacing the sessions out over time (e.g. once a week)" to allow more time on task for asynchronous activities and give students autonomy over their studying. They further recommend introducing collaborative discussion-forum activities, replacing some synchronous activities with recorded videos integrated with asynchronous discussions, and using shorter real-time sessions for in-depth discussion. These changes were implemented in later ARCADE course iterations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recorded lectures and asynchronous discussion options on the course platform
- Longer course duration than the original one-week intensive format
#### Constraints
- Proposed for future iterations; the original MADAS full-time schedule did not allow much freedom for planning learning outside the classroom

### Target Learners
- doctoral students combining study with employment

### Target Learning Goals
- engaging with learning materials at a feasible pace
- peer-to-peer learning through text-based interaction
