# Hybrid gamification design combining traditional instruction with gamified elements that emphasize individual progress and feedback over intense competition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a hybrid model that balances gamified engagement with individualized feedback and traditional instruction. Its platform used leaderboards for individual progress without fostering intense peer competition, which the authors suggest contributed to the positive reception and effectiveness observed. They argue that overly competitive elements may alienate learners who prefer collaborative or personalized approaches, so flexible gamification designs are needed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Thoughtful design and contextual adaptation of game elements; integration with existing instructional methods rather than replacement
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness is context-dependent; competitive designs have produced null results in other studies, and variations in implementation across settings may lead to inconsistent outcomes

### Target Learners
- medical students in radiology education

### Target Learning Goals
- radiographic interpretation knowledge, clinical skills, and motivation
