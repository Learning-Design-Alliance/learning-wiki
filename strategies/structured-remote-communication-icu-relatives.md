# Support consistent, compassionate remote communication with relatives of ICU patients using structured aids and teleconferencing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
With hospital visits banned during the pandemic, the authors aimed to provide a daily telephone update to each patient's next of kin, a task that usually falls to junior doctors. They tracked daily discussions on a dedicated whiteboard and used structured communication aids, including an infographic developed by a palliative care team, to promote consistent and compassionate telephone communication with families, and used teleconferencing tools on tablet and mobile computers to enable relatives to see and pray for patients at the end of their lives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A dedicated whiteboard or similar tracking aid and structured communication tools must be available; junior doctors need support and training for these distressing conversations.
#### Constraints
- High staff turnover in ICU can lead to inconsistent communication with relatives, and even face-to-face, patient representatives often misunderstand diagnosis, prognosis and treatment plans.

### Target Learners
- Junior doctors communicating with relatives of critically ill patients

### Target Learning Goals
- Breaking bad news and compassionate family communication
- Consistent updates across shifts
