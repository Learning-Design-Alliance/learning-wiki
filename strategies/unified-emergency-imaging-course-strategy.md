# Implement a unified emergency imaging course across medical schools and ensure life-threatening chest X-ray interpretation is taught in all radiology rotations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that medical schools standardize emergency imaging training because diagnostic scores differed significantly between universities. They state the difference "points to the importance of considering a unified emergency imaging course to be implemented by all medical schools", and that identifying life-threatening findings "needs to be well implemented in all radiology rotations", a strategy they note is adopted by program directors of several radiology and medical departments in the USA.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Radiology rotations must include explicit teaching of life-threatening chest X-ray findings for the skill to be well implemented
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is grounded in observed between-university score differences in one national cross-sectional survey, not in a controlled curriculum comparison

### Target Learners
- final-year (sixth-year) undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying acute life-threatening conditions on chest X-ray for emergency triage
