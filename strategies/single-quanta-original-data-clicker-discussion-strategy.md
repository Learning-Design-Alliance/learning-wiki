# Teach single-quanta experiments by presenting actual data from original sources with clicker-question discussion, omitting extraneous technical details.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A lecture strategy for teaching single-photon, delayed-choice, and single-electron double-slit experiments in modern physics. The authors "omitted from our lectures extraneous technical details, while still focusing on the process of designing the experiments", and rather than simply describing what experimentalists meant to demonstrate, "we presented them with actual data from original sources." Student discussion of the implications of each of three single-photon experiments was inspired by clicker questions interspersed throughout lecture, so students need not accept assertions as a matter of faith.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to data and materials from original experimental sources (e.g., Grangier et al., Tonomura et al., Frabboni et al.)
- Clicker questions prepared to inspire in-class discussion of data versus interpretation
#### Constraints
- The experiments are only meaningful to students if the details and results of the experiments are accessible to them

### Target Learners
- introductory modern physics students, engineering majors

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguish experimental data from physical interpretation of that data
- recognize the dualistic nature of light and matter and evidence for nonlocal interpretations
