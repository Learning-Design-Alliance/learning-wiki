# Blend IVR simulation training into an existing e-learning curriculum for procedural skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy adds an IVR-based training simulation focused on a specific procedural subskill to an existing e-learning curriculum, rather than replacing it. All participants first received shared e-learning (2 videos and accompanying text on basic US and PVC placement), after which the intervention group completed approximately 20 minutes of IVR scenarios targeting tip tracking. The study showed "adding an IVR-based training simulation to an existing e-learning curriculum significantly increased the learning efficacy of US-guided PVC placement for medical students."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An existing e-learning curriculum covering the procedure's basics
- Access to IVR equipment (head-mounted display, controllers, VR-ready computer) and simulation software
#### Constraints
- Tested only as a pilot with 19 medical students on a phantom outcome; clinical transfer to patient care not yet demonstrated
- Control group received no further training, so the effect of IVR versus other supplementary training forms (e.g., phantom practice) was not compared

### Target Learners
- Medical students learning US-guided procedures
- Novices to ultrasound-guided vascular access

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved success rate in US-guided peripheral venous catheter placement
- Reduced unnecessary surface punctures during cannulation
