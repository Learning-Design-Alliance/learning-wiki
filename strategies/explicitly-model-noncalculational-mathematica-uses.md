# Explicitly model non-calculational uses of symbolic manipulators in class and discuss them with students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Rather than removing Mathematica from the curriculum, the authors recommend that instructors explicitly model specific uses beyond straightforward calculation — exploring a function's behavior, quickly testing physically meaningful cases, and confirming rather than generating a mathematical conclusion — while talking with students about these uses. They argue this helps students integrate the tool with other mindsets and that making students explicitly aware of the tool's potential roles is a first step to harnessing its power.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must themselves be sensitive to framing effects and willing to address the tool's epistemological power explicitly in class
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that not all student difficulties can be addressed by reframing; sometimes there are genuine gaps in understanding requiring more direct instruction

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students using symbolic manipulators

### Target Learning Goals
- integrating symbolic tool use with chunking and physical-mapping mindsets
- broadening students' math sense
