# Integrate hands-on AI project modules directly into core subjects as an intermediate approach between informal and formal AI education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes an intermediate approach to K-12 AI education that "integrates hands-on AI project modules directly into the core subjects (math, science, English, and history)", combining the adaptability of informal approaches (MOOCs, camps, extracurriculars) with the structure of formal in-class settings while reaching all students in a school. It recommends alliances between schools and local universities, nonprofits, and corporations, likely with federal grant support, to provide long-term support for teaching and designing modules.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Alliances between schools and local universities, nonprofits, and corporations for long-term support; likely federal grant support
#### Constraints
- Existing informal programs are typically short-term, highly selective, and sometimes expensive; free MOOCs are designed for university students and above; AI electives are rare and usually reserved for advanced learners

### Target Learners
- all students in a given school
- K-12 students

### Target Learning Goals
- AI literacy
- core-subject learning through real-world AI applications
