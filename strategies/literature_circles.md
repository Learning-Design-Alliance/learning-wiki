# Literature Circles

## Description
Literature circles involve small groups of students who choose a book, meet to discuss it, and complete projects to demonstrate understanding. The approach emphasizes simplicity, adaptability, and student-led discussion. Teachers provide book choices, observe discussions, and offer feedback, while students use notes and marked passages to prepare for discussions. Activities include reflection, project-based expression, and awareness of common pitfalls like unpreparedness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a variety of books suitable for group reading. Class time for discussions and project work. Teacher facilitation to guide and support students.
#### Constraints
- Requires careful planning to manage discussions and ensure student preparation. May be challenging to assess student learning consistently due to variations in book difficulty and group dynamics.

### Target Learning Goals

- Enhance reading comprehension, promote thoughtful discussion, encourage creative expression, foster student-led learning

### Affordances
- Provides student choice and vested interest in the material. Creates a fun, engaging environment for discussing books. Supports the development of critical thinking and communication skills.

### Personalization
- Offer choices in books, themes, and projects to cater to individual interests and learning styles. Adapt reflection activities and project requirements based on student needs and abilities.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe discussions for evidence of thoughtful engagement and comprehension. Review projects and reflections for understanding and creative expression. Provide feedback on discussions and projects to guide student learning.

## Impact
- The approach is different in every classroom, giving students agency while still supporting literacy. The keys to success are simplicity and adaptability.
