Strategies

Include cartoon and problem-solving learning outcomes in curriculum and coursebooks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the findings, the article recommends that learning outcomes related to cartoons and problem-solving skills may be included in the curriculum and featured in coursebooks. It further suggests preparing supporting materials where cartoons are used and scrutinised, and sharing research data on humour and humorous elements with teachers through panels and symposiums.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Continuous and regular utilisation of activities that help improve students' problem-solving skills

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • primary school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • problem-solving skills

Claims¶

  • Cartoon-supported problem-based learning improves fourth-grade students' perceptions of their problem-solving skills [+W]
  • Cartoon-supported problem-based learning improves fourth-grade students' achievement on two Science, Technology and Society outcomes in Social Studies [+W]
  • Cartoon-supported PBL reduces avoidance, a negative aspect of problem-solving, in the experimental group [+W]
  • Gains in problem-solving self-confidence and self-control persist at retention test, but avoidance and total inventory scores regress [~W]
  • The control group showed no significant change in problem-solving perceptions or achievement over the seven-week period [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Structure cartoon-supported PBL activities in sequential problem-solving steps
