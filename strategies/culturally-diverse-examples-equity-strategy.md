# Use culturally diverse examples, values affirmation, and equity strategies to reduce stress and support students' sense of belonging in science

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends building classroom materials and climates that signal all students belong in science: using culturally diverse examples, introducing stories of diverse scientists, employing values-affirmation exercises, and using equity strategies such as giving all students the chance to think and talk about science. Its neurobiological rationale is that belonging and reduced stress lower cortisol, "removing a factor that can impede neural plasticity and learning."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examples and scientist stories must genuinely reflect students' communities so students can see themselves and issues of concern to their communities in the course
#### Constraints
- The article notes worries about belonging can operate over long timescales and influence large decisions like choice of major, so one-time interventions may not address all belonging concerns

### Target Learners
- first-generation college students
- women and minority students in science
- undergraduate biology students

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing stereotype threat and stress to protect memory encoding and retrieval
- increasing sense of belonging and retention in science

### Affordances
- Synaptic Plasticity Learning Framework
