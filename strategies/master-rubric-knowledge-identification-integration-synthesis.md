# A master rubric with selectable grading scales to assess knowledge identification, integration, and synthesis across disciplines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
To generalize rubrics beyond one subject, the authors built a master rubric assessing performance on identifying concepts or terms classified in categories, integrating identified terms into arrow-connected processes, and synthesizing mechanisms that explain a real-life phenomenon. Instructors "further created the criteria to evaluate students' performance on knowledge identification on the terms of the categories," selecting different sets of scales for different assignments, while knowledge integration and synthesis criteria were unified between the two comparison classes. The authors suggest this "may provide insight in designing a unified rubric to assess knowledge identification, integration, and synthesis across disciplines."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Subject-specific criteria for knowledge identification tasks selected from a set of grading scales
- Study assignments structured so results can be graded as identification, integration, and synthesis tasks
#### Constraints
- Knowledge identification criteria had to be adapted per subject, whereas integration and synthesis criteria could be shared across the two classes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in different science disciplines

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge identification
- knowledge integration
- knowledge synthesis
