# Use explicit metacognitive questioning and visible instructor struggle to shift novice problem-solving approaches

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes Schoenfeld's strategy, motivated by cognitive apprenticeship, in which students "work in small groups and solve complex problems" while the instructor moves around asking "What are you doing? Why are you doing it? How does it take you closer to your goals?". Schoenfeld also began geometry classes with ten minutes devoted to attempting students' challenging problems without prior preparation, letting students see him struggle and fail to solve problems within ten minutes. The article reports this helped students adopt conceptual analysis and planning before formula fitting and raised their self-confidence in persisting with challenging problems.
