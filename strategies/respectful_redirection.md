# Respectful Redirection

## Description
Respectful redirection is a quick, in-the-moment strategy to give corrective feedback to students using a calm tone, neutral body language, and clear, concise wording. The instructor tells students exactly what they’re doing incorrectly and what they should be doing instead with as few words as possible, reducing confusion. It provides brief, consistent, systematic correction with feedback, which has a positive effect on student behavior. Tone, word choice, and brevity affect how students respond.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classroom expectations must be set and regularly reviewed. The instructor must be able to assess behavior in the moment and intervene with a calm and neutral tone.
#### Constraints
- Some students may look off-task when they are using strategies that actually keep them on-task.

## Examples
- If a student is struggling to have their ideas recognized during group work and keeps interrupting another student, the instructor walks to the student and says quietly and calmly, 'Josh, I can tell you really want to share your ideas, but you were talking while Laurel was talking.' Point to a visual of classroom expectations and say, 'In this class, we take turns speaking during discussions. Next time you have an idea to share with the group, wait until the person who is speaking stops, and then share your idea.' Explain why the alternate behavior is a better option: 'It’s easier for people to listen when one person is talking at a time.' Ask the student to demonstrate understanding. When the student follows the redirection, provide feedback and reinforcement: 'Josh, the way you waited to speak until after Laurel was finished talking allowed everyone in your group to listen and share their ideas. Well done.'
