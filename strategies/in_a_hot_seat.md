# In a Hot Seat

## Description
Using a stimulus like a photo or item, learners create a character different from themselves, developing details about the character’s life. They then take turns in a 'hot seat,' answering questions from classmates as the character they've created. This activity encourages perspective-taking, creativity, and the ability to think from a different point of view.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A stimulus (photo, item), a form for character development, a 'hot seat' or designated space for role-playing, and time for questions and answers.
#### Constraints
- Learners may be hesitant to engage in role-playing or may rely on stereotypes when creating characters. It requires a supportive and non-judgmental environment to encourage participation.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learners, particularly those who are interested in exploring different identities and perspectives

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Develop creativity, promote perspective-taking, encourage role-playing, improve communication skills

### Affordances
- Stimulates creativity by creating characters and backstories. Enhances perspective-taking by answering questions from the character's point of view. Improves communication skills through role-playing and discussion.

### Personalization
- The stimulus used to create characters can be tailored to the learners' interests and cultural backgrounds. The level of detail and complexity can be adjusted to their abilities.

## Assessment Evidence
- Assess the creativity and depth of character development, the ability to answer questions from the character's perspective, and the level of engagement in role-playing. Monitor changes in learners' attitudes towards different identities.

## Impact
- Role-playing has been shown to improve perspective-taking, empathy, and communication skills. Creating characters also stimulates creativity and imagination.
