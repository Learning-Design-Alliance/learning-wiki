Design teaching material to embody content, arouse interest, sustain attention, activate students, and feel game-like¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on an open-ended questionnaire with five Turkish teachers, the article lists qualities teaching material should have: "Embodying the information to be taught", arousing students' interest, keeping attention for a long time, making students active, being easy for teachers to use, corresponding to technology, and being perceived by students as a game. The study's animation material was designed based on these qualifications.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Material should be easy for teachers to use and integrated with technology

Constraints¶

  • Qualities derive from five teachers' stated expectations in one school context

Target Learners¶

  • 7th grade secondary school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Turkish grammar learning (moods, verbs, tenses)

Claims¶

  • Students' views on the animated 5E activities clustered around active learning, motivation and enjoyment, with time demands as the main disadvantage [+W]
  • Students overwhelmingly reported that animation use positively affected their grammar success, citing permanence, repetition, understanding, interest and participation [+W]
  • Students reported positive evaluations of CBL, citing aroused learning interest, enhanced self-study, improved communication and cooperation, and improved problem-solving [+W]
  • Students rated the game positively, most often citing fun/interactive features, visuals, and new information learned [+W]

  • Balance entertainment and educational elements when designing or selecting educational games
