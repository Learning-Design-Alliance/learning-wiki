# Relationship Building

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Relationship building is the intentional work of establishing trust, rapport, and mutual knowledge between instructors and learners, and among learners themselves. It is carried out through everyday practices — learning names, [check-ins](../elements/check-in.md), personal disclosure, responsive feedback, and consistent follow-through — rather than through a single intervention. It functions as a precondition that raises the yield of nearly every other instructional strategy.

## Design Implications

Positive teacher–student relationships predict engagement, achievement, and reduced problem behavior across grade levels, with the strongest effects for at-risk and disengaged learners [Roorda et al. meta-analysis of affective teacher-student relationships.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. Relationships are built through small, repeated signals of being seen and known — not through one-off icebreakers — and they matter most at moments of difficulty, when a learner decides whether struggle signals rejection or a normal part of learning [Brief belonging interventions reframe difficulty as common and temporary.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor knowledge of learners as individuals (names, backgrounds, goals) gathered systematically, not incidentally
- Consistency between warm relational tone and fair, predictable treatment — trust collapses when warmth and accountability diverge
- Structures that create peer-to-peer connection, not only instructor-to-learner bonds ([Collaborative Learning](../patterns/collaborative-learning.md), structured [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md))

#### Constraints
- Perceived favoritism or inconsistent treatment damages the relational climate for the whole group, not just the affected learners [~M]
- Relationship building cannot substitute for instructional quality; warmth without clear structure and demands produces liking but not learning [~S]
- In large or fully asynchronous settings, unadapted relational practices (personal check-ins, individual conferences) do not scale and can feel performative; scaled substitutes (discussion presence, personalized feedback) carry less relational signal [-W]
- Effects attenuate when relationships are built early but not maintained; a strong first week followed by transactional interaction yields little [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **2×10 strategy**: two minutes of non-content conversation with a targeted student for ten consecutive days, used for re-engaging disengaged learners
- **Belonging reframing**: structured messages that difficulty is normal and temporary, delivered at high-transition moments (first year, first assessment) [Brief belonging interventions reframe difficulty as common and temporary.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S]
- **Empathic discipline**: reframing corrective conversations around the relationship ("I'm telling you this because I know you can do this work") rather than around compliance [Brief empathic discipline intervention cut suspensions in middle school.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S]
- **Peer relationship structures**: [Assigned Positions](../elements/assigned-positions.md) and rotating group roles that force productive interdependence among learners

### Target Learners
- Disengaged, at-risk, or marginalized learners, for whom relationship quality most strongly predicts persistence [Affective teacher-student relationships predict engagement most strongly for at-risk learners.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Learners in transition (first-year, new school, returning after absence) whose sense of belonging is unsettled [Belonging interventions improve outcomes for students at risk of feeling they don't belong.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+S]
- Learners who need psychological safety to attempt challenging work or expose confusion

### Target Learning Goals
- Persistence and engagement goals: reducing dropout, absenteeism, and disengagement
- Affective goals: empathy, perspective-taking, and intergroup attitudes [Building empathy improves intergroup attitudes.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M]
- Enabling conditions for goals requiring risk: discussion, revision, [Argumentation](../elements/argumentation.md), and productive failure

### Instructions
1. Learn and use every learner's name within the first two sessions; gather brief individual context (goals, prior experience) via an intake survey or [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md)
2. Open sessions with brief low-stakes check-ins that surface how learners are entering the work; respond visibly to what they share
3. Pair relational warmth with high, clearly communicated expectations — communicate "I expect this of you because I believe you can do it" in feedback and corrective moments [Brief empathic discipline intervention cut suspensions in middle school.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S]
4. Structure peer connection through recurring small-group work with defined roles ([Collaborative Learning](../patterns/collaborative-learning.md)), so relationships do not depend solely on the instructor
5. Maintain presence over time: respond to contributions by name, follow up after absences, and keep commitments visibly — trust accrues from consistency, not intensity

## Related Strategies

## Examples
- **Restorative practice circles** in K–12 schools — structured whole-class circles that build peer and teacher–student relationships as the base for community-based discipline
- **[City Year](https://www.cityyear.org)** — near-peer student success coaches build sustained one-on-one relationships with identified students, pairing relational trust with attendance and academic monitoring
- **First-year seminar programs** (e.g., [University of South Carolina's University 101](https://sc.edu/about/offices_and_divisions/university_101/index.php)) — assign a single instructor-mentor to a small cohort, combining belonging reframing with sustained individual contact
