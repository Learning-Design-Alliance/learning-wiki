# Use a structured three-phase debriefing after each resuscitation scenario, including reaction, advocacy-inquiry, and generalization phases

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The course's debriefing followed a standard format applied immediately after each shock scenario: a reaction phase for each participant, then the advocacy-inquiry approach to recognize participant frames, and finally generalization and application of the experience to further patient care. Team performance in crisis resource management was also addressed. The authors credit constructive debriefing with improving confidence and providing effective learning in the students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors available to give an immediate post-action reflection and feedback after each scenario
- A standard debriefing format covering reaction, frame recognition, and generalization phases
#### Constraints
- The article attributes the confidence benefit of debriefing to cited prior work rather than testing debriefing separately in this study

### Target Learners
- fifth-year medical students in simulation-based resuscitation training

### Target Learning Goals
- shock resuscitation knowledge, confidence, and crisis resource management teamwork
