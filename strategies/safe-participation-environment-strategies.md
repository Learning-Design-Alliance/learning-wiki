# Create a psychologically safe participation environment by setting expectations, asking nonthreatening questions, and avoiding putting individuals on the spot

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (3 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete recipe for facilitating active learning in large resident conferences drawn from what residents themselves described as effective. It includes "setting expectations ahead of time, asking questions in a nonthreatening way, not putting residents on the spot to answer questions, and creating a culture where large group discussion is expected." The aim is interaction without the discomfort residents associate with being called on or pimped.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty must give up some control of the learning environment and accept the risk that interactive sessions are less predictable.
#### Constraints
- Residents reported that even with these strategies, faculty may still get little response; the article notes active engagement was not part of the existing conference culture, so resident buy-in is needed.

### Target Learners
- residency faculty facilitating conferences
- internal medicine residents

### Target Learning Goals
- active participation
- engagement in didactic sessions
