# Use self-explanation prompts after example study to boost transfer when instructional time or learner effort is limited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 5 studies (2 causal, 2 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2`–`q4` · 2 of 5 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
After learners study worked/completion example pairs, present short self-explanation sheets with one question per example pair and a text box for a typed explanation, as the study did: "can you explain the underlying principles for choosing electrophysical agents to treat a patient with any pain-related problem?" The authors note learners benefitted "without any prior training in self-explanation", and self-explainers invested less post-test mental effort, making this an efficient route to transfer performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Provide instruction on the potential benefits of self-explanation and encourage learners to do their best when answering the prompts.
- Prompts should ask learners to explain the principles underlying the examples just studied.
#### Constraints
- Self-explanation did not improve conceptual knowledge in this study; use concept map study when conceptual knowledge is the goal.
- The evidence comes from one randomized study with physiotherapy students on electrophysical agents.

### Target Learners
- novice physiotherapy students
- novice learners studying worked examples in health professions education

### Target Learning Goals
- near transfer of problem-solving skills
- understanding principles behind solution steps
