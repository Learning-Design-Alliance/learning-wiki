# Phoneme-Grapheme Mapping

## Description
Phoneme-grapheme mapping is a strategy used to match the letters or letter combinations (graphemes) in a word to the speech sounds (phonemes) they represent. This involves using a grid where each box represents a phoneme, enabling learners to segment words into their constituent sounds and write the corresponding grapheme in each box. This technique helps fix phoneme-grapheme correspondences, supporting spelling, reading, and writing development. It is particularly useful for learners who missed the fundamentals in earlier grades or struggle with explicit sound-to-print mapping.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A phoneme-grapheme grid, list of words for mapping, direct instruction from a teacher or tutor, and practice activities. Regular monitoring and feedback are essential to ensure correct application of the technique.
#### Constraints
- May be challenging for learners with significant phonological processing deficits. Requires explicit and systematic instruction to be effective. Can be time-consuming, especially for complex words.

- Primarily for learners in grades 1–3, but also beneficial for older students and adults who need to improve their foundational spelling and reading skills, including those with dyslexia

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve spelling accuracy, enhance phonological awareness, strengthen reading skills, and develop an understanding of sound-symbol relationships

### Affordances
- Strengthens the connection between sounds and letters, improves decoding and encoding skills, supports memory for whole words, and enhances reading and writing fluency. Provides a structured approach to learning and applying phonics.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted by adjusting the complexity of the words used in mapping exercises. Beginners can start with simple CVC (consonant-vowel-consonant) words, while advanced learners can map more complex words with consonant blends and digraphs.
