# Structure online practice as distributed, interleaved problem solving with retrieval practice rather than massed, blocked review

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The library implements learning-science techniques by distributing rather than massing practice of problem types, interleaving multiple topics question by question, and stimulating retrieval practice through short review quizzes that drive students to draw knowledge from memory. Materials are interwoven so that, on a question-by-question basis, students consider a problem, review a lecture slide, hear supporting audio, and process a hint. The article states this "typically requires more effort" and does not deliver the rapid improvement of massed practice, but leads to better mastery and longer retention, citing work that interleaved practice can be almost twice as effective.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A semester-long series of short review quizzes whose aids (hints, lecture links) are absent from the formal weekly quizzes and exams, so students over time strive to answer by drawing on memories instead
#### Constraints
- The article notes interleaved practice typically requires more effort and does not deliver the rapid improvement associated with massed practice of a single problem-solving strategy

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate non-science majors in introductory astronomy courses with a broad range of mathematical and scientific exposure

### Target Learning Goals
- Better mastery and increased long-term retention of concepts
- Development of problem-solving strategies matched to classes of questions
