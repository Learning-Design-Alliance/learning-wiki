# Use regular low-fidelity in situ simulation as a continuous education tool for first-line caregivers in acute care wards

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's design recommendation: run twice-monthly, 30-minute in situ simulations in the actual clinical environment with low-fidelity equipment, focusing debriefing on crisis resource management skills such as early call for help, iSBAR, closed-loop communication, cross monitoring, and situation awareness. The authors conclude "low-fidelity ISS is a feasible tool for continuous education to build teamwork and teach clinical staff crisis resource management skills" and recommend it for institutions with limited financial resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Fixed trainers to conduct scenarios and debriefings
- Ward staff availability during shift change, with one-day advance notice
#### Constraints
- The study is retrospective; the authors state prospective research is necessary to determine the effect of long-term ISS, and the cardiac arrest decrease was not statistically significant

### Target Learners
- First-line caregivers in acute care wards, especially nursing staff

### Target Learning Goals
- Crisis resource management, teamwork, recognition of clinical deterioration, early reporting and communication with physicians
