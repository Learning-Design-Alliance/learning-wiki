# Use classification results only to support, not to exclude, students: keep interventions open to all while encouraging the most at-risk students to use them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's ethical considerations state that machine learning predictions must not bias instructor treatment of individual students and should not be used to exclude students from educational activities supporting at-risk students. Because predictions are never fully accurate — Fig. 2 shows students with very low predicted probability of earning an A, B, or C can still earn a C or higher — "additional tutoring sessions or similar resources should be available to all"; classification results may instead be used to deliver encouragement to the students most at risk to use these opportunities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors using predictions must take into account that the algorithms will never be 100% accurate
#### Constraints
- Classification results should only direct resources to at-risk students with the goal of improving instruction for all students, per the article's stated ethical principle

### Target Learners
- all students in introductory physics classes, including those predicted at risk

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable use of risk predictions to improve instruction without excluding students
