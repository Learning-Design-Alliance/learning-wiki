# Start EBHC teaching early in preclinical years and scaffold it throughout the programme using real patient scenarios

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Coordinators recommended beginning EBHC teaching in the preclinical years to lay the foundation, then repeating and scaffolding learning throughout the programme to ensure continuity. Key approaches they highlighted include linking teaching to real patient scenarios to engage students and demonstrate relevance, using a variety of delivery and assessment methods, avoiding covering too much content, involving ward clinicians in the teaching team, and including assessment because it drives learning, with feedback provided on performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordination with clinical rotations and clinicians willing to act as part of the teaching team
#### Constraints
- Preclinical students may lack clinical context, making relevance hard to convey; undergraduate students found EBHC abstract

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate and graduate health professions students

### Target Learning Goals
- Applying EBHC steps to real clinical questions and appreciating the relevance of EBHC to practice
