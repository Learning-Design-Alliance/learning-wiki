# Add the concepts of maximum specific cycle net work and optimal compression/pressure ratio to Engineering Thermodynamics teaching and revised textbooks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors propose a curricular change for the gas power cycle unit of Engineering Thermodynamics: alongside thermal efficiency, instruction should present the maximum specific cycle net work and the optimal compression (or pressure) ratio, derived for the Otto and Brayton cycles as worked examples. They state: "it is suggested to add the concepts of maximum cycle specific work and optimal compression ratio (or optimal thermal efficiency) to the teaching process of Engineering Thermodynamics." The aim is to correct the student belief that the most efficient engine is automatically the best one and to give clearer theoretical guidance for engineering applications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must cover the given cycle temperature limits (minimum and maximum cycle temperatures) under which the optimal ratios are derived, as in the paper's derivation sections.
#### Constraints
- The paper notes textbooks currently do not clearly state under what state a gas power cycle should work most economically, so instructors must supplement standard textbook treatments.

### Target Learners
- engineering undergraduates studying energy and power, mechanical, civil, aerospace, cryogenic refrigeration, food, chemical, or environmental engineering

### Target Learning Goals
- correctly evaluate and design gas power cycle devices using both thermal efficiency and specific cycle net work
