# Design physical activity programs aimed at increasing CRF and executive functions to improve academic achievement in schoolchildren

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that "the design of physical activity programs aimed at increasing CRF and executive functions might be a practical strategy to improve academic achievement in schoolchildren". The recommendation rests on the study's findings that higher categories of CRF and executive function were associated with higher language and mathematics grades, and that inhibition and cognitive flexibility partially mediated the CRF–achievement relationship.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must target both cardiorespiratory fitness and executive functions, since the article's recommendation names increasing CRF and executive functions together.
#### Constraints
- The authors state that "the cross-sectional nature of our analyses prevented us from making cause–effect inferences" and that "randomized controlled trials are necessary" to establish a direct, causal relationship.

### Target Learners
- Primary schoolchildren aged 8 to 11 years

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement in language and mathematics
- Executive functioning (inhibition, cognitive flexibility, working memory)
