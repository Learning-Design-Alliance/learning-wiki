# Embed VR lessons in pre-VR lecture and post-VR peer activity using gradually released instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
After observing that starting class with VR felt abrupt and transitioning to new material felt disjointed, the authors revised their classroom approach for the auditory system lesson. They embedded the VR lesson in a pre-VR lecture and post-VR activity, "allowing for gradually-released instruction" (I do it, I do it with you, you do it alone), which also leveraged Mayer's pre-training principle by naming main concepts before the lesson. In the final phase, student pairs enacted the auditory system on an imaginary brain, fostering peer learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A brief instructor lecture on the lesson material before the VR experience, and a structured post-VR activity in which students work in pairs to enact the system.
#### Constraints
- The classroom evaluation could not rule out timing-based alternative explanations, since the embedded lesson occurred later in the semester than the non-embedded one.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduates in a perception course

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning system pathways and structures of perceptual systems
