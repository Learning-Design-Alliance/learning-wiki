# Test students' memory quality and bring all learners' learning to the same point of automatization

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article closes with two educational recommendations drawn directly from its findings: teachers should determine the kind and strength of memory their students possess so students can form proper habits of study, and teachers should "endeavor to bring the learning of all students to the same point of automatization." Because the fast learner retains as well as the slow learner, equalizing the point of learning "will mean more work for some students than others." Students should also learn the value of properly learning and organizing subject-matter as they go, so that retention takes care of itself.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must be able to assess the kind and strength of memory possessed by each student.
- Slow learners must put sufficient time on their work to fix it.
#### Constraints
- Recommendations rest on retention tests given 24 hours (and in some checks one month) after learning of connected thought material with senior and graduate students.

### Target Learners
- senior and graduate students in the author's experiments
- school and class learners generally as discussed by the author

### Target Learning Goals
- retention and permanence of learned subject-matter
- proper habits of study
