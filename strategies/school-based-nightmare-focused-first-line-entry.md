# Use school-based, nightmare-focused screening and intervention as an accessible first-line entry point for trauma-related sleep problems in students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that interventions directly addressing sleep and nightmares in traumatized children can serve as a first-line treatment because they "allow for easy identification of a clinical population in schools due to easily assessed and circumscribed symptoms," carry "less cultural stigma for students and their families," and directly address a core posttraumatic problem interfering with functioning. The Better Learning Program operationalized this by screening students with recurrent posttraumatic nightmares in UNRWA schools and inviting them into a school-based group intervention. The authors also suggest "increasing self-efficacy in relation to the experience of recurrent nightmares may be a good point of intervention."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School-based counselors trained in recruitment and screening procedures to identify students with recurrent trauma-related nightmares
- Written parental consent and voluntary enrollment
#### Constraints
- The study examined only treatment-seeking students, potentially biasing the sample toward a more distressed group; results cannot be generalized to all youth in the setting absent random sampling or a control group

### Target Learners
- war-affected children and adolescents in primary and middle school

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing posttraumatic nightmares and sleep disturbance to improve school functioning
