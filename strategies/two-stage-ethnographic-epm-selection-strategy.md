# Two-stage ethnographic selection of environmental print as learning content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a learner-driven, two-study procedure for selecting learning content: Study-I collects the environmental print most often seen by people with low literacy (77 participants wrote at least eight common EPM items per Urdu alphabet letter, yielding 140 items after removing redundancy), and Study-II (95 non-literate participants) presents the collected items and selects the most often recognized item per letter. This grounds content in learners' actual environment and matches the target population.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants in Study-II should closely match the experiment's target population
- Consent procedures approved by an ethics committee, with consent read aloud and verbal approval recorded for non-literate participants
#### Constraints
- The article notes exposure of non-literates to EPM does not automatically guarantee its recognition, calling for careful planning

### Target Learners
- adult non-literates in a specific cultural and linguistic context

### Target Learning Goals
- selecting recognizable, culturally relevant literacy learning content
