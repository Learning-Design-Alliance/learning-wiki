Integrate computational modelling into basic and translational fear-learning research programs (Computational Psychiatry)¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that the field integrate biophysical computational modelling into both basic and translational research programs on fear learning. The authors state "the field would beneﬁt from the integration of computational modelling in both basic and translational research programs", predicting this will yield cross-level understanding of fear-learning mechanisms and support more specific theories linking circuit dysregulation to psychiatric disorders, an approach they call Computational Psychiatry. Concrete modelling targets they name include BNST–CeA interactions and the multivalent influence of medial prefrontal cortex.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Convergent computational–experimental approaches in which experimental observations constrain models and verify their predictions.
  • Incorporation of interactions between distributed regions such as BNST and CeA, which the review says "will be essential to move forward in this ﬁeld".

Constraints¶

  • Current modelling output measures are adequate only for simulating classical fear conditioning, not more complex and realistic behavioural paradigms.
  • The review notes the field's understanding of amygdala network properties remains superficial, limiting links to mental disorders and therapeutic strategies.

Target Learners¶

  • Fear and anxiety neuroscience researchers
  • Clinical and translational researchers addressing maladaptive fear

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Explaining circuit mechanisms of fear learning and extinction
  • Linking circuit dysfunction to psychiatric disorders and better therapies

Affordances¶

  • Biologically Based Neural Circuit Modelling Framework

Claims¶

  • Computational models of the amygdala are scarce relative to other brain areas, with a PubMed search yielding ~30 times fewer citations than for 'cortex' [~W]
  • Experimental work indicates basolateral amygdala cells activated by appetitive CSs mainly encode behavioural output, not CS identity, challenging amygdalo-centric feedforward accounts [~W]
  • A biophysical model suggested that more excitable LA neurons are preferentially recruited into the fear memory trace via competitive synaptic interactions, validated experimentally [+W]
  • Model ablation of engram neurons showed other neurons emerge to support memory, indicating competitive neuronal interactions underlie learning [+W]
  • The timing rule of fear conditioning contradicts the Hebbian coincidence rule because simultaneous CS–US pairing is less efficient than CS onset preceding the US with co-termination tens of seconds later [+W]
  • A biophysical LA model showed both auditory thalamic plasticity and intra-amygdala plasticity contribute to fear memory, with thalamic responsiveness essential during learning but dispensable after training [+W]
