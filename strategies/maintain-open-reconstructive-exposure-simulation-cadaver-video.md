# Maintain trainee exposure to open and reconstructive procedures using real cases, simulation-based and cadaveric education, and video libraries despite declining open case volume

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Although open surgical volume is decreasing, the authors recommend that training programs deliberately preserve trainees' exposure to open and reconstructive techniques. They write that "we should expose trainees to a broad range of open and reconstructive procedures, as many as possible, utilizing real experiences of clinical cases or simulation‐based education including cadaveric training, or developing video libraries," and ideally teach the technical basics both open and minimally invasive. The rationale is that open conversion may be required when a minimally invasive approach cannot be safely completed, and urologists must manage iatrogenic ureteric injuries.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recognition that open surgical volume is gradually decreasing, so exposure must be deliberately engineered
#### Constraints
- Competency in these procedures was measured subjectively; the recommendation responds to low trainee confidence, not objectively measured skill deficits

### Target Learners
- Urology trainees and residents in postgraduate surgical training

### Target Learning Goals
- Open and reconstructive surgical technique, including management of ureteric injury
