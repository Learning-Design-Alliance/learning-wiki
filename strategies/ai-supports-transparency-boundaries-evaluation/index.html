Design AI-mediated cognitive supports with transparency, clear boundaries, and teacher evaluation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that AI-mediated cognitive supports — such as text simplification and homework tutors — be integrated "with transparency, clear boundaries, and teacher evaluation". Collaborative text simplification improved comprehension and reduced barriers to accessing dense content only when integrated with didactic criteria and human feedback. Ethical design, including data minimization, explicit purposes, and informed consent, is required to maintain learner trust.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Integration with didactic criteria and human feedback
  • Data minimization policies, explicit purposes, and informed consent

Constraints¶

  • Without clear protocols, massive adoption of analytics and traceability erodes learner confidence and capacity for self-directed decisions

Target Learners¶

  • university students
  • university teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • comprehension of dense content
  • digital ethics and data governance awareness

Related Strategies¶

  • Impact Depends On Pedagogical Design Review [+W]
  • Tailor AI deployment to specific applications and stakeholder groups rather than applying a one-size-fits-all integration approach
  • Provide explicit instructional boundary cues, process-oriented assessment, and peer modeling of verification to frame responsible GenAI use
  • Stage GAI use in assessment: permit exploration, restrict independent reasoning, require justification
  • Design AI literacy tools with transparency in development and establish district policies for vetting AI tools
  • Institutional recommendations: shift from detection to process-oriented assessment, transparent AI-use protocols, and revised integrity policies
  • Five reconciliation strategies academics use to manage AI-related guilt: tool framing, critical thinking emphasis, boundary setting, transparency, and denying reconciliation is needed
  • Subordinate digital resources to pedagogical principles: authentic problematization, scaffolding, formative evaluation, and timely feedback
  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
