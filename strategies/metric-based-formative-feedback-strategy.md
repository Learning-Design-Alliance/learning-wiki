# Deliver formative feedback on quantifiable, AI-derived performance metrics with actionable goals during surgical simulation practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that "learning surgical skills in simulation was more effective with metric-based assessment and formative feedback on quantifiable criteria and actionable goals by an AI tutor than remote expert instruction." The strategy interleaves short feedback sessions between practice resections, helping students "achieve higher expertise by bringing awareness to their metric goals during resections and setting measurable performance objectives." It saved approximately 53 hours of expert supervision over 13 weeks while producing comparable OSATS scores.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Validated, quantifiable performance metrics from simulator data; feedback sessions interleaved with deliberate practice
#### Constraints
- Comparing efficacy between intervention arms is limited to the primary outcome measures used; the AI algorithm failed to detect improvements in subjective qualitative OSATS composites such as instrument handling; in-person coaching equivalence untested

### Target Learners
- Medical students (undergraduate years 0-2) in surgical simulation training

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving quantifiable technical performance metrics and skill transfer in simulated surgical procedures
