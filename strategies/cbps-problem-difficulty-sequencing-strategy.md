# Sequence CBPS problems from easy to complex and monitor individual progress

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the Conclusion the article recommends that lecturers using CBPS prepare a learning plan, consider students' ability levels and material difficulty, and sequence problems from easy to complex. Easy problems let students find solutions directly without exploration, while overly difficult problems cause cognitive conflict and frustration, so "The best strategy is to give questions from easy categories to complex ones". Lecturers should also pay attention to each student's progress.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lecturers must carefully determine problems and attend to the difficulty level of the problem
#### Constraints
- Derived from the authors' interpretation of the low problem-solving gain and the study's small sample in one mathematics subject

### Target Learners
- prospective primary school teachers in mathematics content courses

### Target Learning Goals
- higher order thinking skills, particularly problem solving
