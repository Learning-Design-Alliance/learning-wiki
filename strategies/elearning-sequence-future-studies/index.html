Vary the sequence of traditional learning and e-learning in blended course design¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study delivered e-learning after traditional learning because e-learning "was considered as a supporting learning tool that was provided after class." The authors suggest future studies consider e-learning as a tool for course preview, so that "the sequence of the learning activities in future studies can be that e -learning comes before traditional learning," and that results from both sequences be compared and examined. This treats sequencing of blended components as a design variable worth testing.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Blended course design in which the order of e-learning and traditional learning components can be arranged differently

Constraints¶

  • The present study only tested the traditional-then-e-learning sequence; the alternative sequence was not tested

Target Learners¶

  • vocational high school students in blended e-learning courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • electrical machinery learning through blended e-learning

Claims¶

  • Blended e-learning did not significantly affect vocational students' achievement test scores compared with traditional learning after five weeks [+W]
  • Blended e-learning significantly raised students' overall self-assessment scores relative to traditional learning (η2 = .129) [+W]
  • Blended e-learning students reported significantly higher self-assessed cognition and skill than traditional-learning students, with no significant difference in attitude [+W]
  • Students' self-assessment scores rose significantly from before to after studying through blended e-learning, with medium-to-large gains on all three aspects [+W]
  • The self-assessment questionnaire measuring cognition, skill, and attitude showed good validity and reliability in this sample [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Assess blended e-learning outcomes with both achievement tests and self-assessment
