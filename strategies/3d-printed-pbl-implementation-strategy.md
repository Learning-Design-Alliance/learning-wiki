# Implementation strategy for adopting 3D-printed simulator-integrated PBL: cost evaluation, faculty training, case-embedded curriculum, phased training, and ethical compliance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The discussion lays out practical steps for institutions adopting the approach: evaluate initial costs of equipment, materials, and commercial simulator procurement, with cross-institutional collaborations explored to reduce financial burdens; train faculty in technical operations and PBL pedagogy; embed high-fidelity simulators into authentic case-based tasks with standardized assessment tools aligned to learning objectives; use phased training to ease adaptation from low-fidelity models; and mandate strict anonymization of patient-derived data and ethical review protocols such as IRB approval when using clinical imaging.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty specialized training in technical operations and PBL pedagogy
- Standardized assessment tools aligned with learning objectives
- Strict anonymization of patient-derived data and adherence to ethical review protocols (e.g., IRB approval)
#### Constraints
- Successful implementation hinges on balancing resource allocation, faculty expertise, curricular compatibility, and ethical compliance
- The proprietary simulator introduces potential biases related to cost, accessibility, and vendor dependency, which might hinder scalability

### Target Learners
- senior stomatology undergraduates

### Target Learning Goals
- cleft lip repair surgical education
