# Use anatomy-faithful dry-lab simulators as an off-the-job alternative to animal and cadaver surgical training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structured off-the-job training via dry laboratories to replace OJT-dependent surgical education, since OJT "poses a substantial risk to patient safety." Dry labs have no ethical concerns, are inexpensive, and allow repeated training, and their use "has been reported to effectively improve technical surgical skills." COLOMASTER extends dry labs beyond basic skills to full-procedure anatomy-faithful training and, per the authors, "may be utilized as an alternative to surgical training on animals or cadavers."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulator that reproduces the relevant anatomy and membrane layers so that procedure-specific steps, not only basic skills, can be practiced
#### Constraints
- Dry labs have historically been limited to basic skills such as suture ligatures unless the simulator reproduces human anatomy; animal and cadaver training face ethical barriers, cost, infection risk, and regulation

### Target Learners
- surgical residents
- practicing surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- laparoscopic procedural technique
- anatomical structure knowledge
- standardization of surgical procedures
