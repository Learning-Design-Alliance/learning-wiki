# Asset Based Teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Asset-based teaching designs instruction from what learners already know, can do, and bring culturally and linguistically, rather than from a catalogue of perceived deficits. The instructor treats students' home languages, community knowledge, and lived experience as instructional resources to be activated and built upon, not obstacles to be remediated. In practice this means diagnosing strengths first, framing tasks so those strengths are usable, and structuring participation so every learner contributes knowledge, not only receives it.

## Design Implications

Asset-based framing changes the diagnostic starting point of instruction: assessment and [Check-Ins](../principles/check-ins.md) surface what learners can do before identifying gaps, which supports learner self-perception and persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]. Activating existing knowledge as a launch point improves new learning, and the activation itself signals to learners that their prior experience is legitimate classroom content [Activating prior knowledge improves comprehension of new material.](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md) [+M]. The approach aligns with [Constructivism](../theories/constructivism.md): new knowledge is built on existing schemas, so instruction that ignores or devalues those schemas forfeits its strongest building material.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Diagnostic activities that identify learner strengths, interests, and community knowledge before planning ([Check-Ins](../principles/check-ins.md), interest inventories, home-language surveys)
- Tasks designed so diverse prior knowledge is usable — e.g., [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) drawn from learners' own contexts rather than only textbook contexts
- Participation structures that position learners as contributors: peer [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) roles, and sharing routines
- Feedback and [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) practices that name growth and capability, not just error

#### Constraints
- Deficit framing by teachers — attributing struggle to fixed learner traits — depresses expectations and instructional quality, and asset-based language alone does not correct it if underlying beliefs are unchanged [-M]
- Superficial "strengths talk" without redesigned tasks can become performative; if assessments still penalize non-dominant backgrounds, the deficit structure persists [-M]
- Over-reliance on a single assumed "asset" (e.g., treating all students from a group as sharing one culture) risks stereotyping and misalignment with individual learners [~M]
- Requires sustained teacher knowledge of learners' communities; without it, attempts to connect to "funds of knowledge" can misrepresent them [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Funds of knowledge approaches** — household and community practices (e.g., agricultural, mechanical, financial) are mapped and woven into curriculum units
- **Translanguaging / multilingual assets** — learners' full linguistic repertoires are used for reasoning and drafting before producing work in the target language
- **Strengths-first feedback** — feedback sequences open with what the work demonstrates before addressing next steps
- **Interest-driven pathways** — [Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md) and project topics anchored in learner-identified strengths

### Target Learners
- Multilingual learners whose home language knowledge is often treated as a deficit rather than a resource [+M]
- Students from communities underrepresented in curriculum materials, for whom asset framing supports belonging and self-efficacy [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Struggling learners who have accumulated histories of deficit labeling; strengths-first diagnosis prevents instruction from targeting only remediation
- All learners benefit from instruction that activates prior knowledge, but the payoff is largest where prior knowledge differs most from the assumed default [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding built on learners' existing schemas and experience
- Dispositional goals: self-efficacy, belonging, and academic identity
- Transfer: connecting school content to authentic community and life contexts

### Instructions
1. Diagnose assets before gaps: use [Check-Ins](../principles/check-ins.md), interest surveys, and low-stakes [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) to map what learners know and can do.
2. Plan entry points from those assets: open units with [Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating-prior-knowledge.md) routines and [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) that explicitly bridge from learner experience to new content.
3. Design tasks where assets are usable: build [Analogies](../elements/analogies.md) and examples from learners' contexts, and allow multiple entry points and representations.
4. Structure contribution: use [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) and [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) formats in which every learner holds knowledge others need.
5. Give strengths-first feedback: sequence [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) feedback to name demonstrated capability before next steps.

## Examples
- **Funds of Knowledge projects (Moll et al., Tucson, AZ)** — Teachers conducted household visits to map community knowledge (mechanics, farming, finance) and built math and literacy units around those practices, repositioning families as intellectual resources.
- **International Baccalaureate transdisciplinary units** — Units begin with learners' own questions and community contexts, requiring teachers to document prior understanding before introducing disciplinary content.
- **WIDA Can-Do Descriptors (https://wida.wisc.edu)** — Reframes multilingual learners' abilities as what students *can* do at each proficiency level, shifting planning from deficit checklists to asset inventories.
