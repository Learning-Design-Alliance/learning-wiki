# Asset-Based Teaching

## Description
A pedagogical approach that focuses on planning with learners’ strengths at the forefront, recognizing that every learner has something to learn, share, and contribute. It involves proactively planning for instructional opportunities, providing multiple access points for success, presenting a variety of modality choices, and promoting a positive climate for learners to learn from each other. Key strategies include bounce cards (cooperative learning activities), See, Think, Wonder (thinking-made-visible routine to stimulate curiosity and critical thinking), and Which One Doesn’t Belong? (analytic thinking exercise to compare similarities and differences).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Conversation topics, three-column organizer, group of three or four images connected with a math standard
#### Constraints
- Requires intentional planning and a willingness to get to know learners as learners. May be challenging to implement in a classroom with diverse abilities and needs.

### Affordances
- Promotes active listening, turn-taking, and perspective sharing. Normalizes error and stimulates curiosity. Provides multiple access points for success and promotes a positive learning climate.

### Personalization
- Allow learners to choose topics for classroom conversation and plan how to discuss them. Provide a three-column organizer for learners to record their observations, thoughts, and wonders.
