# Dolch Sight Word List - Flash Cards

## Description
Printable flash card sets covering the Dolch sight word list — the 220 most frequent service words compiled by Edward Dolch (1948), organized into pre-primer through third-grade tiers, plus a 95-word noun list. Each set is displayed eight cards to a page for printing and cutting. Cards are used for rapid, repeated recognition drills in which the learner names the word on sight rather than decoding it.

## Design Implications

Flash card drill targets automaticity, not initial word learning. The rationale is that high-frequency words recognized instantly free attention for comprehension rather than decoding [Automatic word recognition frees cognitive resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]. Repeated retrieval from a visual prompt is a form of [Practice](../elements/practice.md) that strengthens word-specific memory traces, but the cards work best when embedded in a broader program that also includes connected-text reading and phonics instruction.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Printable PDF of the target tier, scissors, optional laminator for durability
- Brief, frequent sessions (5–10 minutes) rather than long massed drills
- A record of which words are known to enable removing mastered cards and adding new ones
- Follow-up reading of decodable or predictable texts containing the drilled words

#### Constraints
- Drill alone does not teach orthographic patterns; sight-word memorization without phonics limits generalization to unfamiliar words [~S]
- Massed repetition within a single session produces fast gains but poor retention; spaced review across days is required [~S]
- Whole-word memorization of visually similar words (e.g., *was/saw*, *then/them*) invites guessing; learners need letter-level attention, not shape-based recognition [-M]
- Words taught only on cards may not transfer to reading in context unless the learner also meets them in sentences and books [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Three-pile sorting** (known / hesitated / unknown) to focus time on unmastered words
- **Spaced review schedule**: mastered words reappear after 1, 3, and 7 days
- **Sentence-building extension**: use known cards to compose and read simple sentences
- **Games** (memory match, slap-the-word) to increase engagement while preserving retrieval demands
- **Paired peer drill** with a script so the partner gives immediate confirmation or correction

### Target Learners
- Early elementary students building a base of instantly recognizable high-frequency words [Automatic word recognition frees cognitive resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Struggling readers who need targeted, low-load repetition on a small word set
- ESL/EFL learners, for whom the Dolch words' irregular spellings and grammatical function make explicit drill useful
- Less appropriate for advanced readers, for whom the words are already automatic and drill is redundant

### Target Learning Goals
- Automatic recognition of high-frequency words (fluency subskill, not an end goal)
- Rapid recall of irregular words whose spellings resist decoding
- Building a sight vocabulary that supports reading connected text

### Instructions
1. Select the tier matching the learner's instructional level; pre-assess with the full stack and set aside known words.
2. Show each card for 1–2 seconds; the learner says the word. Correct errors immediately with the correct pronunciation and have the learner repeat it ([Practice](../elements/practice.md) with immediate confirmation).
3. Sort cards into known / unknown piles; keep sessions short and stop before fatigue.
4. Re-drill unknown words within the session, then resurface them on subsequent days (spaced retrieval).
5. Transfer to context: have the learner find and read the drilled words in a sentence or short text, and use them in writing ([Application](../elements/application.md)).
6. Monitor progress by timing and recording accuracy on the full tier periodically ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)).

## Related Strategies
- Phonics and decoding instruction — the necessary complement; sight-word drill covers irregular words, phonics covers the generalizable patterns
- Repeated reading of connected text — where automatic word recognition is consolidated into fluency
- Vocabulary in context — extends recognition into meaning and use
