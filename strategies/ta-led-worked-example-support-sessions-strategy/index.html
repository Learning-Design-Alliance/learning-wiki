Use trained TA-led small-group support sessions with worked and faded worked examples¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (5 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A specific implementable recipe: required weekly support sessions led by TAs working with small groups of at most 12 students, centered on problem-solving approaches and practice problems, using worked examples and faded worked examples as the main study strategy. The article reports that "worked and faded worked examples were the main study strategy for problems utilized in the instructional support sessions," and that fading helps "students understand procedural concepts rather than memorizing a process." TAs must be properly trained for this strategy to work as described.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • TAs participate in an initial training period (15 hours in this study), including 2 hours on metacognitive awareness, motivation, and calculation-based problem-solving strategies
  • Problem sets created for each session to support content presented in class and text

Constraints¶

  • Other strategies may have been appropriate for the calculation-based study, but the chosen strategies allowed focused, organized instruction
  • The study did not describe differences among the skills and understanding of the TAs

Target Learners¶

  • College students identified as academically underprepared in mathematics

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Develop calculation-based problem-solving skills in general chemistry

Claims¶

  • Underprepared students scored significantly lower on early-semester common questions, validating their need for support [+W]
  • Underprepared students in embedded-support chemistry showed no statistically significant difference from college-ready peers on overall common-question results [+W]
  • Entrance measures confirmed the nonequivalence of underprepared and college-ready groups [+W]
  • No significant difference between underprepared and college-ready students on final exam multiple-choice questions [+W]
  • Underprepared students scored significantly higher on final exam open-ended questions than college-ready peers [+W]
  • Attrition from General Chemistry-IS was higher than from General Chemistry and previous fall semesters [-W]
  • Implementation fell short of the intended dosage: very few learners received two 15-minute sessions weekly for 30 weeks, mainly due to timetabling and absence [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Clarify the role of targeted TA-led interventions within a broader teacher-led literacy strategy
  • Make teaching assistant tutorial training more explicit: provide checkpoint learning outcomes and suggested checkpoint questions
  • Allow lead-in time for schools, provide ongoing TA support beyond initial training, and segment comprehension material into shorter, more varied pieces.
