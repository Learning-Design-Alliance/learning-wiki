# Use a level- and subject-structured comfort survey to diagnose students' self-perceived gaps in navigating challenging clinical scenarios

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's 24-item survey organizes challenging scenarios along two axes: three interaction levels (patients, peers, upper-levels) and seven diversity subjects (gender, race/ethnicity, politics, age, sexual orientation/identity, disability, religion), rated on a 5-point Likert scale from "Very Uncomfortable" to "Very Comfortable". This structure let the authors localize comfort deficits to specific relationships and topics, informing targeted teaching modules and case-based education. The survey was developed with expert input to address real-life scenarios.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Expert input to develop realistic scenario items
- Administration during a structured class meeting with informed consent
#### Constraints
- Likert scale data give limited power for discerning nuanced differences; the binary gender construct does not apply to gender identities outside the gender binary

### Target Learners
- third-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing preparedness for diversity-related conflict with patients, peers, and supervisors

## Claims

- [Student comfort is lowest for gender-, race/ethnicity-, and disability-related scenarios and highest for sexual orientation/identity- and religion-related scenarios](../claims/comfort-lowest-gender-race-disability-scenarios.md) [+W]
- [Third-year medical students report lower comfort navigating diversity-related challenging scenarios with peers and superiors than with patients (p < 0.0001)](../claims/students-lower-comfort-peer-superior-than-patient-scenarios.md) [+W]
- [Males report significantly higher median comfort than females for upper-level, gender, and religion related interactions](../claims/males-higher-comfort-upper-level-gender-religion.md) [+W]

- [A higher proportion of males than females report being completely comfortable in 4, 8, and 12 or more of the 24 scenarios (p < 0.05)](../claims/males-more-likely-completely-comfortable-many-scenarios.md) [+W]
