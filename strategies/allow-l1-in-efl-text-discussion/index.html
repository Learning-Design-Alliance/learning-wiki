Allow EFL students to discuss texts in their mother tongue alongside English¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 7 studies (3 review, 2 causal, 2 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 7 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends permitting students to use their first language during text-based discussions rather than enforcing an English-only policy. The author states that "by allowing them to talk in their mother tongue as well as in English, EFL students can be trained to think about the text in the same high-level way as native English speakers." In the study, "group members communicated among themselves in either English or Chinese" and "the English-only policy was not observed."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A bilingual teacher who can explain portions of the text in both the students' first language and English.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • EFL college students with limited English proficiency

Target Learning Goals¶

  • higher-order reading comprehension
  • critical thinking about text

Claims¶

  • Teachers in South African multilingual classrooms use code-switching, offering English content but switching to the mother tongue for elaboration and discussion [+M]
  • Preserving a bilingual student's heritage language supports rather than hinders English acquisition [+W]
  • Preservation of the mother tongue predicts second-language achievement among migrant children [+W]
  • First-language and bilingual programs in Peru, Bolivia, and the United States show students are better off acquiring knowledge in their mother tongues [+M]
  • Mead argued that treating the mother tongue as inferior impedes movement between home and school language structures [+W]
  • Failure to use mother tongue as the medium of instruction during at least the fundamental years has lasting detrimental impacts on students [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Mother-tongue-first bilingual model: begin schooling in the native language and introduce the second language alongside it
