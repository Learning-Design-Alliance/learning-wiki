Future narrative medicine directions: robust evaluation mechanisms plus NM for researchers, burnout-prone contexts, cultural competence training and low-resource settings¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's implications for research set out an agenda beyond current practice: "We advise that NM programming best practices and future directions should include the use of robust evaluation mechanisms; inclusion of writing training as an additional learning outcome; and the development and implementation of NM for researchers, burnout-prone providers/contexts, cultural competence trainings and low-resource settings." Supporting suggestions include blended online/in-person modules using videoconferencing for peer mentoring, and embedding narrative-based learning modules into existing unconscious bias trainings.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Strategic planning, thorough evaluation and ongoing analysis are described as required to integrate relevant NM programming into educational curricula.

Constraints¶

  • The review notes NM programming to date has been based primarily in high-resourced medical areas, with 83.7% of programmes in USA/Canada.

Target Learners¶

  • research faculty and postdoctoral fellows
  • graduate health sciences students
  • clinicians in burnout-prone specialties
  • learners in low-resource settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • communication and relationship-building
  • burnout detection and mitigation
  • cultural competence
  • cross-disciplinary understanding

Claims¶

  • A systematic review through 2019 identified 55 unique narrative medicine programmes in academic health sciences, of which 75% reported some form of evaluation with inconsistent methods [+W]
  • A quarter of NM programmes (n=14, 25.5%) provided no evaluation details, and no programme used a well-described evaluation to assess gains in writing competence or confidence [+W]
  • Well-described qualitative evaluations of NM programmes reported positive impacts most frequently on relationship-building (15 programmes), empathy (10) and perspective-taking/reflection (10) [+W]
  • Among well-described quantitative NM programme evaluations, statistically significant impacts were reported for increased empathy (three programmes) and increased perspective-taking/reflection (one programme) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Expand narrative medicine programme objectives to include writing skills and writing self-efficacy as measurable learning outcomes
