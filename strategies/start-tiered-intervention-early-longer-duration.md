# Start tiered catch-up interventions at the beginning of the school year and run them over a longer period rather than as a brief end-of-year catch-up

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The process evaluation concluded that RTI would have been more effective had it been "started at the beginning of the year and run over a longer period of time", rather than compressed into the busy final weeks of Year 6. Running the intervention at the very end of Year 6 made it necessarily brief, put it in competition with other end-of-school activities, and left some schools delivering very few sessions. The report recommends increasing the time between training and completion to enhance the chances of impact.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough timetabled weeks between training and completion for the full cycle from needs analysis to monitoring and adjustment
#### Constraints
- The compressed timescale was due to funding conditions rather than the preferences of the developer or schools

### Target Learners
- Year 6 pupils at risk of not achieving Level 4 English at Key Stage 2

### Target Learning Goals
- Literacy catch-up (reading) ahead of the primary-secondary transition
