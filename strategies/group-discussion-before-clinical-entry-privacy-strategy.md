# Use facilitated group discussion with clinical cases immediately before clinical entry to teach patient privacy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "Nurse educators are recommended to use group discussion method to enhance the students’ attitude and practice toward patient privacy." The implemented recipe was a two-hour facilitated session per subgroup of six, using four clinical cases mapped to the physical, informational, social, and psychological privacy domains, followed by open discussion of students' views and experiences. The authors suggest holding it "immediately before entering the clinical setting" so students reflect on prior behaviors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator trained in group discussion facilitation
- Small subgroups (six students) during clinical rotations
- Clinical cases covering each privacy domain
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single small trial (55 analyzed) with short follow-up

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students in clinical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- respecting patient privacy
- communication and critical-thinking skills
