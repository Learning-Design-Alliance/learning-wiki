# Sequence VR early and manikin training later in an integrated preclinical dental curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends an integrated, staged approach leveraging both modalities' complementary strengths: "VR being introduced early in the curriculum, helping students visualize procedures and practice basic skills, with manikin training becoming more central as students progress to more complex procedures". VR serves repetitive practice, visualization, and fine motor concepts such as handpiece speed and pressure; manikins provide the hands-on tactile experience needed for complex clinical procedures and real-life patient care.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both modalities available in the preclinical curriculum with equal practice hours and trained faculty supervision
#### Constraints
- VR is not seen as a complete replacement for manikin training; its lack of realism limits use for managing real-life patient scenarios

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate BDS dental students progressing from preclinical to clinical years

### Target Learning Goals
- Bridging theoretical knowledge and practical application; building confidence and skills for real-life patient care
