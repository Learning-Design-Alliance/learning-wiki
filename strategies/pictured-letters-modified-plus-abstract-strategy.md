# Use the pictured letters mnemonics strategy teaching both modified and abstract letters with students with learning disabilities who confuse similar English letters

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends teaching similar English letters by drawing familiar shapes whose form resembles the target letter and whose label begins with the letter's sound, presenting the modified pictured letter alongside the abstract letter. The authors state the strategy helped students "remember the letters by creating a visual link between the modified letter and the abstract one it indicated", and recommend its use for students with characteristics similar to the study's participants, plus further research with other cognitive handicaps.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Letter images validated by specialists and piloted with students; letter-resembling parts highlighted in dark colors against light backgrounds; per-letter educational plans and educational and evaluation worksheets
#### Constraints
- Recommended for students with learning disabilities who have similar features to the study's participants; the study was limited to Jeddah resource rooms in the 2016/2017 second semester

### Target Learners
- Students with learning disabilities in resource rooms
- Students in the first three primary grades and kindergarten (named as curriculum audiences)

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning, remembering, and distinguishing similar English language letters
