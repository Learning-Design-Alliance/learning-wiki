# Monitor student well-being with a validated burnout inventory at course completion to identify cohorts under high mental burden

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 causal), `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article administered the validated School Burnout Inventory (SBI-9), which "measures three domains: exhaustion at school, cynicism towards meaning of school, and sense of inadequacy at studies", within the end-of-course feedback questionnaire. The authors argue that examining burnout and academic performance together lets educators design targeted interventions that reduce burnout while supporting success, and that flexible options such as blended teaching can support students at high risk of burnout.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The inventory must be group-level and optional, respecting privacy; the authors deliberately avoided profiling individual students to protect autonomy and agency.
#### Constraints
- The authors note that burnout inventories, like learning analytics, pose ethical implications, and that self-reported measures can be subject to recall bias.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate university students, particularly those in demanding health-science programs

### Target Learning Goals
- student well-being and sustained academic engagement alongside theoretical learning outcomes
