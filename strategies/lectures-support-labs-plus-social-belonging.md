# Pair lectures that support upcoming labs with social activities that build belonging in a research community

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A summer-school design strategy in which three constant program elements work together: lectures sequenced to prepare students for hands-on labs, special-topic lectures broadening awareness of the field, and social activities building community. The authors state that constant across iterations are "a sequence of lectures designed to support hands-on learning, special topics lectures designed to increase awareness of the breadth of QIST, and social activities designed to foster a sense of belonging among the students and with the broader quantum research community." Social activities include excursions, an institute-wide community gathering, and panels connecting students to industry.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional participation of students, staff, and faculty; a program manager and lab manager with QIST backgrounds; graduate-student facilitators; archived lecture slides for future iterations
#### Constraints
- Exact structure varies year-to-year due to scheduling, equipment availability, and feedback-driven improvements

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students with minimal QIST experience, mostly third year of studies or higher

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational quantum information concepts
- experimental skills across multiple quantum platforms
- sense of belonging in the quantum research community
