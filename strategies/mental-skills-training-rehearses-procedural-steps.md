# Mental skills training (mental imagery rehearsal of procedural steps) as a component of training for high-stakes clinical procedures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Mental skills training (MST) in the program is "a rehearsal involving mental imagery without physically performing the task". The article situates MST as well-established in aviation, elite sports, and the arts, and reports that many systematic reviews show its benefits in learning procedural skills and reducing stress and burnout. In the program, trainees mentally go through the steps of intubation anywhere and anytime, including just before practising on mannequins and real-life intubation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainees learn a structured mental rehearsal of the intubation steps (Supplement S6) that can be applied before mannequin practice and real intubations
#### Constraints
- The article cites systematic reviews of MST in surgical and other procedural fields but reports no trial of MST specifically for neonatal ETI

### Target Learners
- medical trainees performing neonatal intubation
- senior clinicians maintaining procedural skills

### Target Learning Goals
- acquiring and maintaining procedural skills
- reducing acute stress response during procedures
