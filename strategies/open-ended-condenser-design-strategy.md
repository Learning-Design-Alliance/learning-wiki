# Pose condenser design as an open-ended problem with multiple correct solutions that students find by trial and error

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students design the microscope condenser themselves, empirically locating the field-stop and aperture irises by tinkering and linking the empirical placement to the theory of conjugate planes and Kohler illumination. The article stresses that "Any of these designs are “correct” and functional as long as they control the ﬁeld of illumination, the brightness of illumination, and do not create an image of the LED on the sample plane." Life-science students reported that applying Kohler alignment knowledge was the most challenging and most exciting part.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Variable irises for field stop and aperture
- Collecting lens and condenser lenses of various focal lengths for students to tinker with
#### Constraints
- Some iris locations cut rays but do not give maximum control of numerical aperture unless set to a small radius

### Target Learners
- undergraduate optics students
- graduate students in short laboratory modules

### Target Learning Goals
- image formation
- focal planes and collimation
- apertures and field stops
- Kohler illumination
