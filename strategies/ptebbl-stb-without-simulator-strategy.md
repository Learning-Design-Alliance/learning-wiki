# Introduce PTEBL-based Stop the Bleed training without expensive trauma simulators in Chinese medical curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends introducing the PTEBL method into Stop the Bleed training in China, where few STB courses exist and the Caesar trauma simulator is costly and difficult to obtain. The trial showed PTEBL "has equally effectively taught hemostasis techniques in the emergency setting" while being superior for teamwork skills, so the approach can deliver STB training to general medical students without expensive equipment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors provided with scripts to follow, preparing lessons before each class
- A teaching laboratory space with a 1:16–17 teacher-to-student ratio and four hours of course time
#### Constraints
- Effect was evaluated in a small single-institution sample of medical students; the authors state the effect should be evaluated in larger public groups including laypersons

### Target Learners
- Medical students in Chinese five-year training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Bleeding-control skills and willingness to act as first responders
- Teamwork in trauma response
