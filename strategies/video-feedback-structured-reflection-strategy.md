# Use video-recorded simulation sessions followed by instructor-led structured self-reflection and peer review to enable reflection-on-action

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
After the simulation, the entire teaching session is reviewed on video with residents. The article states this stage was "designed in a certain way to encourage Reflective Practice, specifically, the reflection-on-action", guiding residents through "instructor-led structured self-reflection and peer-review" focused on clinical judgment, communication, and teamwork from an observer's perspective to detect cognitive biases and performance gaps. The instructor then gives specific feedback reinforcing appropriate behaviors, and the simulate–feedback–refine cycle is repeated.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Videotaping of the teaching session
- Instructor-led structured debriefing with peer review
- Repetition of the simulation-feedback-refinement cycle
#### Constraints
- Requires access to simulation facilities and standardized patients, which the authors note may be limited by resources at broader scale

### Target Learners
- medical residents in standardized training

### Target Learning Goals
- reflective practice
- identification of cognitive biases
- clinical judgment and communication skills
