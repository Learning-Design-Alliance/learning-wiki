# Assess sleep problems in young children and intervene early to prevent adverse effects on later academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that because persistent childhood sleep problems elevate later academic risk, schools and health services should assess sleep problems early and intervene before they become chronic. They note that "children's sleep problems often are overlooked" yet are associated with identifiable risk factors, making early intervention feasible. They point to trials of school-based sleep education programs as supporting evidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sleep problems must be identified early, since the harm accrues when problems persist over time rather than appearing transiently.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on observational longitudinal associations; the authors call for future studies to disentangle the exact mechanisms.

### Target Learners
- primary school-aged children (ages 7–13)

### Target Learning Goals
- preventing poor academic performance associated with persistent sleep problems
