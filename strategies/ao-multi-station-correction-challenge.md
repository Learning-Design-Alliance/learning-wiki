# Multi-station investigation with a hands-on wavefront-correction engineering challenge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The AMSC activity places students at three stations — wavefront sensing, wavefront correction, and systems characterization. At the sensing station students measure how ray incidence angle relates to focal point location and invert the relation to infer tilt. At the correction station, an aberrated lamp image is blurred by a hidden 'aberrator box' simulating the atmosphere, and "The engineering task was to use the provided materials (foil, plastic, flat mirrors, bendable mirrors, and more lenses) to correct the beam." The challenge fosters group discussion of collimation, image formation, image sharpness and phase conjugation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Low-cost materials (fresnel lenses, lamps, mirrors, bendable mirrors) plus a workbench station for characterization, and an accessible point optically conjugate to the aberrator
#### Constraints
- The amount of introductory terminology depends on student background, from minimal to substantial

### Target Learners
- Akamai Workforce Initiative summer interns
- undergraduate and graduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- wavefront sensing via tilt-focus relationships
- aberration and wavefront distortion
- applying optics knowledge to alignment and correction
