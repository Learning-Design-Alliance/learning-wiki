# Use simulation-based training to prepare allied health practitioners to deliver specialized procedures as an adjunct to surgeon practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extending simulation-based training to allied health practitioners, since they increasingly deliver clinical care. They state that "our study provides support for training these learners, who may serve as a useful adjunct to the surgeon's clinical practice, in specialized procedures", citing prior evidence that physiotherapist-directed Ponseti clubfoot care was effective.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated simulation module and assessment benchmarked to expert performance
#### Constraints
- The study did not evaluate the direct link to clinical outcome

### Target Learners
- physiotherapists
- occupational therapists in pediatric subspecialty care

### Target Learning Goals
- specialized technical procedures such as Pavlik harness application
