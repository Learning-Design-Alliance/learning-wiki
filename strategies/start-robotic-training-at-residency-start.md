# Start robotic surgery training early, at the beginning of residency programs, capitalising on young adults' precision

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors read their Generation Z precision finding as a training-sequencing recommendation: "robotic surgery training might be best to be started at the beginning of residency programs", and note that young doctors using two-thumb typing may develop skills faster. They caution that further studies are needed to determine whether quicker learning leads to better later skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires simulation-based training such as the Da Vinci Skills Simulator as part of residency curricula
#### Constraints
- The performance advantage rested on one small, single-task study with a non-diverse young adult group; longer-term skill benefit is untested

### Target Learners
- residency trainees in robotic surgery

### Target Learning Goals
- acquiring robotic surgical console skills
