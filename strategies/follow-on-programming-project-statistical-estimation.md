# Follow-on programming project in which advanced students implement an algorithmic Scientist player

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
To reinforce conceptual thinking with quantitative understanding, advanced students write a computer program to play the game. Writing the Experiment player is simple practice with random numbers, while writing a good Scientist algorithm is challenging and motivates statistical estimation methods such as the method of moments, maximum likelihood, and Bayesian estimation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should already understand the game mechanics; advanced undergraduates may know some tools of statistical estimation
#### Constraints
- Deciding which measurement to make next or when to guess is not obvious, and derivations and technical comparisons of the statistical methods are beyond the scope of the article

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduate quantum mechanics students

### Target Learning Goals
- practicing statistical estimation techniques
- reasoning about the value of quantum information
