# Post-course refreshers, support and mentorship, plus preparing placement sites and educating nurses, families and policy makers about palliative care

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that palliative care education should not be treated as a single event: "An effective model should incorporate plans for refreshers, support and mentorship post training and as students advance in their career as nurses". They also recommend that nurse educators educate placement sites and mentors about palliative care, lobby for palliative care policies with hospitals, and educate family caregivers and the general public to gain support for palliative care practice, so that students' newly acquired competencies are translated and embedded into clinical practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Nurse educators address the values that underpin care at individual and organizational level
#### Constraints
- Recommended particularly when students have not developed confidence or when enthusiasm and learning from the course may have waned

### Target Learners
- Preregistration nursing students on clinical placement in resource-poor settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Transfer of palliative care learning into clinical practice with dying patients and their families
