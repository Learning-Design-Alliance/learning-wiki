# Strengthen ECMO-specific education, promote nursing autonomy and delineate RN–perfusionist roles to support nurses' participation in paediatric ECMO care

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends targeted professional development for ECMO specialist nurses: strengthening ECMO-specific education, promoting nursing autonomy and supporting effective interprofessional collaboration. It states these findings "highlight the potential value of strengthening ECMO‐specific education, promoting nursing autonomy and supporting effective interprofessional collaboration to optimise ECMO‐related nursing activities", adding that "Clear role delineation between RNs and perfusionists may also help define responsibilities for shared ECMO‐related activities". The strategy rests on cross-sectional associations, not tested interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- RNs working in the ECMO specialist nurse role who have completed standardised ECMO training (in this study, an accredited course with 24 h of theoretical instruction and 16 h of clinical training)
#### Constraints
- The article states the supporting evidence is cross-sectional and exploratory, precluding causal inference, and may not generalise beyond paediatric ECMO or Israeli PICU organisational and cultural contexts

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses practising in the ECMO specialist nurse role in paediatric intensive care units

### Target Learning Goals
- ECMO-related knowledge, confidence and skill
- nursing autonomy in ECMO care
- interprofessional collaboration and role clarity in ECMO teams
