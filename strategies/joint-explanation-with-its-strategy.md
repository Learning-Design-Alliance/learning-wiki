# Instruct pairs to jointly explain problem-solving steps while working with a step-based intelligent tutoring system

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article structures collaboration loosely in two ways: instructions prompting pairs to jointly explain the steps of video-based worked examples, and the use of a step-based tutoring system. The hypothesis tested was that if self-explanation, peer collaboration, and ITS-based problem solving are combined as the context for collaboration, collaboration will be more effective than individual self-explaining. Dyads followed the same procedure as individuals, with only the instructions and prompts to jointly explain differing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A step-based tutoring system that presents worked examples and coaches problem solving
- Instructions and prompts that elicit joint explanation of each example step
#### Constraints
- The article states computer scripts that tightly control dialog have floundered because of their inability to understand natural language, so the structuring here is loose

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- collaborative problem solving
- conceptual understanding of the electric field
