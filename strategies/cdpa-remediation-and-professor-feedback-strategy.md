# Use CDPA response distributions as a remediation tool and report program-level data back to course professors for interpretation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Beyond ranking student understanding across the curricula, the article proposes focusing on the distribution of correct and incorrect responses as an additional layer of analysis, so that CDPA results can be used as a remediation tool to help professors identify gaps in student understanding and refine their teaching approach for subsequent cohorts. The study reinforced the value of collecting program-level data with a common instrument and reporting findings back to course professors for interpretation; engaging professors in data analysis let the group pinpoint the educational benefit of the research. The authors also point to a potential need for more inquiry-guided learning in laboratory courses, or at least more personalized attention in large-scale introductory labs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A departmental partnership structure (a physics education research group with professors and education developers) meeting regularly with cycles of data collection and reflection
#### Constraints
- Misconception identification is limited to the alternatives present in the test

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students across introductory to advanced laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of measurement uncertainty and experimental data analysis
