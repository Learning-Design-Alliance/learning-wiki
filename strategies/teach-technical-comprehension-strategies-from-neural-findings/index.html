Teach technical-text comprehension strategies that engage verbal working memory, spatial visualization, and inference generation (self-explanation)¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors derive teaching strategies from their finding that better technical comprehension involves verbal working memory, spatial mental model construction, and knowledge integration. Recommended strategies include pre-teaching unfamiliar concepts, explicitly teaching visualization strategies, and teaching bridging and causal inference generation: "One of the most effective ways of facilitating such inference generation is teaching self-explanation." These target students who are poorer comprehenders of technical material.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Strategies must explicitly engage the processes better comprehenders used: keeping verbal information active in memory, imagining spatial relationships, and relating new information to previously learned world knowledge.

Constraints¶

  • The strategies are implications the authors state converge with prior behavioral research; the present study did not directly test them instructionally.

Target Learners¶

  • College-age readers of technical expository texts, especially poorer comprehenders

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Comprehension and retention of technical expository text content

Claims¶

  • Activation in four brain regions predicts the comprehensibility of individual technical passages, generalizing across independent presentations [+W]
  • Brain imaging measures predict individual comprehension beyond psychometric cognitive measures, whereas psychometric measures add little beyond brain measures [+W]
  • Activation in four stepwise-selected brain regions predicts individual participants' technical comprehension, generalizing across independent reading presentations [+W]
  • During technical text reading, activation in language, spatial, and knowledge-integration regions is positively related to comprehension, while activation in episodic/autobiographical retrieval regions is negatively related [+W]
  • Activation changes non-monotonically over three readings of technical passages, consistent with a shift from semantic to executive processing [+W]
