Three-point standardized testing schedule for teachers: determine needs at the opening of the term, note progress mid-year, and measure year's gains and vacation losses¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article advises every teacher to use standardized tests on a recurring schedule: "every teacher, whether she is following a particular or unique method or not, can and should utilize tests to determine needs at the opening of the year or term, to determine progress during the year or term, and to measure losses during vacation." Testing at about the middle of the year forms what the article calls the best basis for further teaching, a third testing at the end of the year measures the year's gains, and testing the same pupils at the opening of the following year reveals vacation losses.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Standardized tests available in a form the class teacher can readily utilize
  • Repeated administration at the opening, middle, and end of the year or term, and again at the opening of the following year

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school arithmetic pupils

Target Learning Goals¶

  • diagnosing class and individual arithmetic needs, tracking progress, and measuring vacation losses

Claims¶

  • Remote learning implemented during the spring 2020 closures achieved effects similar to no teaching at all during summer vacation [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Summative Assessment to Measure Learning Over Time
  • Sustain tutoring participation, schedule it close to testing windows, and align content with classroom instruction to preserve gains
