# Implement surgical coaching in otolaryngology using video-based coaching, addressing barriers of ego, cost, privacy, and logistics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The commentary recommends otolaryngology adopt surgical coaching, arguing it is "uniquely poised to incorporate video-based learning due to the various modalities through which we operate—endoscopic, microscopic, robotic, and open—which make recording easy." Implementation should address surgeon ego through confidentiality and goal-oriented sessions, coach training and certification (e.g., the Academy for Surgical Coaching), patient consent for recording, and logistics of observation, video review, and coaching sessions. The authors also suggest integrating artificial intelligence and machine learning for instrument tracking and surgical simulations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Patient consent and adherence to institutional policies for recording
- Coach training or certification
- Institutional investment of time and resources for coaching sessions
#### Constraints
- Program setup has associated costs (the commentary cites ASC certification at $1000 and a program set-up guide at $2500); undefined costs include time away from clinical productivity and purchasing recording devices

### Target Learners
- practicing otolaryngologists
- otolaryngology trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- operative efficiency
- surgical skill refinement
- reduced burnout

### Affordances
- Surgical Coaching Question Driven Goal Focused
