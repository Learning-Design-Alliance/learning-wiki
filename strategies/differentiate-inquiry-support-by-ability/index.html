Differentiate inquiry support by ability level: abstract prompts for gifted, concrete structure for lower-ability¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the usability and exploratory studies, BE COOL! matches the amount and type of inquiry support to ability level. The four topic sets differ in complexity and the amount of structure and support given: gifted children work with more abstract prompts they can access when needed, while average- and low-ability children receive more concrete instructions and step-by-step structure, since the studies showed lower-ability children "preferred and needed more concrete instructions for successful completion of the inquiry assignments." Support is embedded in the five inquiry-cycle steps, with more or less structure depending on the targeted ability level.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Ability levels must be indicated (e.g., via a categorization procedure based on CITO scores and, if available, the Digital Action Protocol for Giftedness)

Constraints¶

  • The article reports average-ability children needed more concrete explanation of learning goals and more structure in experiments, and low-ability children needed structure across the whole assignment, not just experiments

Target Learners¶

  • Gifted, average-ability, and low-ability primary school children

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Successful completion of inquiry assignments at each child's ability level
  • Active and effective inquiry processes

Claims¶

  • A usability study with 37 fourth to sixth graders showed gifted children needed less support and were satisfied with more abstract prompts, while lower-ability children preferred and needed more concrete instructions [+W]
  • In an exploratory study with 478 fifth and sixth graders, children with prompts available showed a more active and effective inquiry process than children without, with ability-related differences in prompt use [+W]
