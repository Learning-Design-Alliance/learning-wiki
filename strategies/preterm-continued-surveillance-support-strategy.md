# Provide continued developmental surveillance, early intervention, and school support services for children born 32-36 6/7 weeks gestation throughout elementary school

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (8 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that children born moderately and late preterm receive continued monitoring and support from birth through elementary school. It states these children "are at risk for learning disabilities and likely benefit from continued support and services to improve achievement throughout school", and suggests pediatricians, early intervention, developmental follow-up clinics, preschools, and state or local tracking databases as vehicles for identifying and serving them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of preterm birth history, e.g. at school entry evaluation, since birth history may flag children at risk of attention disorders and learning disabilities
#### Constraints
- The study's cohort predates the 2020 pandemic and reflects education and neonatal care of that era, and the authors note more resources need to be allocated to preschool centers

### Target Learners
- Children born at 32-36 6/7 weeks gestation entering and progressing through elementary school in the United States

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving academic achievement and learning-disability identification and support for preterm children
