# Secondary-school personality change intervention: teach traits, self-assess, nominate traits, and complete weekly trait-relevant challenges

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (3 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review proposes a concrete classroom recipe adapted from Hudson et al. (2019): students learn about Big Five traits, take a self-report scale with anonymised class results shared, are taught about the malleability of personality, nominate traits they wish to develop, and complete weekly "games" enacting trait-relevant behaviour. Example challenges include reading one book per month for openness and using self-regulated learning strategies for conscientiousness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must choose their own traits to develop, grounding the intervention in individual preference and agency
#### Constraints
- The review suggests this fits secondary school "perhaps not elementary school given the relatively high level of self-reflection required"; the school application is speculative, extrapolated from university-sample studies

### Target Learners
- secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-directed development of chosen personality traits such as conscientiousness, openness, and extraversion
