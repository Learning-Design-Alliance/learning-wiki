Employ residency residents as the second adult in TK classrooms using assistant teacher or paraprofessional budget lines¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The brief recommends that, given TK's child-to-adult ratio requirements, districts employ residency residents, with salary and benefits, as the second adult in the classroom "using assistant teacher or paraprofessional budget lines to cover salary and tuition." This would meet staffing needs under TK expansion, fulfill ratio requirements, and provide a paid pathway for new TK teachers to earn robust preparation and their Multiple Subject credential while working. The brief suggests the pathway may particularly interest current early childhood educators with a bachelor's degree working in state preschool programs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Residency funding streams (federal TQP grants or California grant programs) to cover salary and tuition

Constraints¶

  • Depends on the 12:1 child-to-adult ratio requirement starting in 2022–23 and available budget lines

Target Learners¶

  • Current early childhood educators with a bachelor's degree and new teacher candidates

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Building a credentialed TK teacher workforce with early childhood expertise

Related Strategies¶

  • Reallocate existing district roles and budget lines—substitute teaching, paraeducator positions, and extended-day staffing—to fund and pay residents
  • Strategic staffing models that redirect district instructional-support funds to subsidize paid residency stipends
  • Sustain paid residencies through strategic staffing that reallocates resident instructional support roles
  • Leverage California state grant programs to fund TK-focused teacher residencies
