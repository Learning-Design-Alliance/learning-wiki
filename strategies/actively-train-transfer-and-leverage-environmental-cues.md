# Train transfer actively and leverage environmental cues to strengthen persistence and generalizability of SRL interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Drawing on VPC insights, the review recommends that SRL interventions not take generalizability for granted, since "transfer is more likely when it is actively trained." It further recommends attending to environmental triggers: external supports such as digital reminders or structured study schedules may prompt planning or monitoring, whereas distractions or stressful contexts may disrupt these processes. Future research should examine which environmental cues facilitate or inhibit SRL and how interventions can incorporate strategies to leverage these triggers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must actively train transfer rather than assume spontaneous generalization across academic contexts
#### Constraints
- SRL research typically emphasizes intentional regulation of situational processes over the development and stabilization of habit-like behavioral patterns
- Interventions that ignore contextual triggers may overestimate their generalizability

### Target Learners
- school students
- lifelong learners

### Target Learning Goals
- transfer of self-regulation strategies across contexts
- durable SRL habits
