# Use interactive e-modules with active learning opportunities, rubric walkthroughs, examples, and low-stakes post-module assignments to support large-course assignments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors designed four e-modules consisting "primarily of active learning opportunities to practice skills development", with interactive videos of the instructor explaining the rubric. Each e-module (except the Concept Map) included a minor assignment worth 1% of the total grade to keep students on track. Approximately 95% of students completed these low-stakes post e-module assignments, indicating they accessed these learning opportunities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- E-modules must be available online from the start of the course to allow flexible, self-directed completion.
#### Constraints
- The authors note online learning tends to favor students who are already highly self-motivated, and regularity metrics do not capture depth of cognitive engagement.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in large online or hybrid courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Scientific literacy
- Science communication
- Assignment completion
