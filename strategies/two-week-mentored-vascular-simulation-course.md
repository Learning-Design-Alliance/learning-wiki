# Incorporate a 2-week mentored simulation course into vascular resident training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that simulation training be a standard component of resident education: "the ST should be the basic course for residents, especially in precareer students and residents in rotation". The implemented course combined daily 30–60 min theory lectures with 1-hour mentored simulator sessions, practice of carotid, renal and superficial femoral artery interventions, and pre- and post-course angiography procedures on the simulator. The authors add the course "should be well defined and step planned".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A tutor to deliver the course
- A well-defined, step-planned curriculum; otherwise the ST may result in impaired learning and worse real clinical performance
#### Constraints
- This study delivered ST for only 2 weeks due to teaching funding and time-course limits, differing from advanced vascular centres; other reports cited suggest 8 weeks of ST

### Target Learners
- vascular surgery residents
- precareer medical students and residents in rotation

### Target Learning Goals
- vascular interventional procedural skills
- radiation protection practice
