# Adopt cooperative learning in place of passive lecture-based teaching in Vietnamese higher education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author recommends cooperative learning as a more effective pedagogical technique for Vietnamese higher education, arguing that its interactive approaches advance motivation and that findings equip Vietnamese teachers with empirical support for implementing interactive techniques. The recommendation rests on the study's own nine-week quasi-experimental result favouring cooperative learning over lecture-based teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers implement the cooperative learning technique across a whole course, with students investigating materials, supporting each other, and presenting comprehension
#### Constraints
- The author notes the sample was limited to 72 students and that findings cannot be generalised to all educational levels in Vietnam

### Target Learners
- higher education students in Vietnam

### Target Learning Goals
- learning motivation and learning outcome

## Claims

- [Cooperative learning raised expectancy-component motivation (control of learning beliefs, self-efficacy for learning and performance) more than lecture-based teaching](../claims/cooperative-learning-expectancy-components-motivation.md) [+W]

- [Cooperative learning raised value-component motivation (intrinsic goal orientation, extrinsic goal orientation, task value) more than lecture-based teaching](../claims/cooperative-learning-value-components-motivation.md) [+W]
