# Use trained peer reviewers (subject specialists plus health professions educationists) alongside student evaluations, with structured feedback, a reflection period, and independent revision

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable procedure: faculty deliver a baseline lecture, receive student ratings plus written peer-review feedback from four reviewers (two subject specialists and two trained health professions educationists), get a 20-day reflection (washout) period to revise independently, then redeliver and are reassessed with the same tools. The article reports that "no additional mentors or observers were involved during modification".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Peer reviewers with subject expertise and health professions education training; standardized evaluation instruments; identical lecture topics and durations across groups.
#### Constraints
- The article states the observed changes were primarily associated with the feedback process since no parallel faculty development was introduced; single-institution, small-faculty scope.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical (MBBS) students

### Target Learning Goals
- improved lecture delivery, organization, and student engagement

## Claims

- [After the intervention, the peer-review group showed significantly greater post-lecture knowledge gains than the control group (83 vs 76, p = 0.003)](../claims/peer-review-greater-post-lecture-knowledge-gains.md) [+W]
- [After peer review plus a 20-day revision period, intervention-group lectures received significantly higher student quality ratings than control-group lectures (86 vs 73; p = 0.001)](../claims/peer-review-higher-post-intervention-lecture-quality.md) [+W]
- [Qualitatively, intervention-group students reported improved lecture organization, engagement, and visible incorporation of feedback, while control-group students reported little or no change](../claims/qualitative-improvements-intervention-not-control.md) [+W]
- [Lecture quality scores rose from pre-test to post-test within the intervention group (65 to 86, p < 0.001) and rose without a significant change within the control group (67 to 73, ns)](../claims/within-group-lecture-quality-gain-intervention-only.md) [+W]
