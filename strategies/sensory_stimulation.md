# Sensory Stimulation

## Description
Sensory stimulation involves providing experiences that engage a person's senses to improve regulation, attention, and focus. This is achieved by introducing tactile toys, fidgets, and activities that awaken sensory receptors, helping individuals manage sensory defensiveness and seek tactile experiences as needed. Addressing sensory needs can reduce fidgeting, attention issues, and behavior problems, promoting calmness and focus for higher learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to sensory toys, fidgets, or activities. Implementation benefits from clear rules and guidelines for use, as well as ongoing conversations about their effectiveness.
#### Constraints
- Can be misused as toys if not properly managed, potentially distracting others. Effectiveness varies based on individual sensory needs and preferences.

### Affordances
- Provides immediate sensory input, promotes self-regulation, and supports emotional and behavioral stability. Helps learners calm their bodies and minds for better concentration and learning.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to individual sensory preferences, with options for tactile, visual, auditory, and proprioceptive fidgets and activities. Rules and guidelines can be customized to fit specific contexts and needs.
