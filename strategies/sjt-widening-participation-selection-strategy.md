# Use a locally designed SJT alongside cognitive tests to widen participation in medical selection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A strategy of adding a locally designed SJT assessing non-academic attributes to selection processes that otherwise rely on academic attainment and cognitive tests, to reduce adverse impact on diversity. The article states that "the use of such non-academic tests, targeting important interpersonal attributes required to be a successful clinician, could support the objective of increasing the diversity of medical students."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Test specification grounded in the local curriculum and national competency standards, with contextual modification for other institutions
- Expert concordance to establish scoring keys and item design checked for cultural, ethnicity and gender bias
#### Constraints
- Evidence is from a single-institution pilot; no comparative analysis with other selection methods was conducted

### Target Learners
- medical school applicants from diverse socio-economic, regional and ethnic backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- fair selection of candidates on non-academic attributes such as professionalism, communication and self-awareness
