# Resolve examiner-pair disagreement with simple decision rules such as follow-the-most-experienced, follow-the-most-confident, or a decision tree

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In assessment contexts with few raters and limited resources, examiners first judge performances independently and then resolve disagreement using a simple decision rule. The article's rules include follow-the-most-experienced, follow-the-most-confident, combinations of the two, and a decision tree designed to resolve borderline judgments into a final pass/fail grade. In the study's simulation, all rules outperformed random resolution, with the decision tree most consistently reproducing the ten-examiner verdict for the weaker candidate 'Lars' (over 90% pass).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Independent judgments from at least a pair of examiners, plus information on each examiner's experience and/or confidence to feed the rule
#### Constraints
- No clear-fail performance was included in the study, so the heuristics' sensitivity and specificity for classifying competent versus incompetent candidates could not be evaluated
- Ecological validity may be limited because real examiner pairs typically interact directly after seeing a candidate perform

### Target Learners
- Medical students in high-stakes oral or performance examinations

### Target Learning Goals
- Defensible pass-fail decisions where extensive assessment systems or psychometric adjustments are not feasible

### Affordances
- Ecological Rationality Assessment Framework
