# Run digital skills-maintenance programmes as a monthly self-practise model with practise corners, formal hospital leadership involvement and facilitator training in online supervision

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend reshaping the intervention for future evaluation around a lower practise frequency, stating that a monthly practise model can be considered for further evaluation, alongside structural supports: the creation of practise corners, leadership involvement and facilitator training. A practise corner would allow practise by a single provider, overcoming the difficulty of scheduling group sessions. These measures target the time constraints and feedback delays observed in the pilot.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formally involve hospital leadership and discuss how to integrate self-practise into the daily routine
- Additional facilitator training to increase the number of facilitators and confidence in online supervision
#### Constraints
- Monetary incentives have a sustainability issue and need to be implemented carefully
- The intervention also needs to find a way to compensate the cost of mobile data

### Target Learners
- Newborn care providers (doctors, nurses, midwives, medical assistants) at district hospitals in resource-limited settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining essential newborn care knowledge and skills, including management of non-breathing babies
