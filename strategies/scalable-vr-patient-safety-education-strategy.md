# Offer immersive VR patient safety education as a scalable alternative to live mock simulation for operating room teams

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends immersive VR-based patient safety education programs as a scalable way to reduce patient harm, contrasting VR with live mock simulations whose logistical demands (full team coordination, operating room time, realistic setup, cleanup and reset) make them neither cost-effective nor scalable for multiple realistic experiences per learner. In the conclusions the authors write that "such innovative and immersive patient safety education programs could provide a scalable intervention to help reduce patient harm in the long run." The strategy pairs the VR scenario with TeamSTEPPS-grounded debriefing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- 360° filmed error scenarios, VR headsets, and structured pre- and postdebriefing based on TeamSTEPPS principles
#### Constraints
- Generalizability requires confirmation with larger, more diverse samples; current evidence comes from one single-site pilot

### Target Learners
- surgeons and operating room staff in academic medical centers

### Target Learning Goals
- safety behaviors and teamwork practices that reduce preventable surgical errors

### Affordances
- Vr Sense Making Account Safety Behavior Change
