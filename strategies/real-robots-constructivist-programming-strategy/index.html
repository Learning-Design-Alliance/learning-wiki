Use real programmable robots with constructivist methods to teach programming basics to novices¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends introducing programming through concrete, real devices combined with constructivist pedagogy, so students build knowledge from their own experience rather than receiving behavioral-style instruction. In its conclusion it states that "Real tools and constructivist methods can help students to achieve the abstract element of programming at the level of concrete operations", and that model robots can strengthen students' motivational basis, especially programming self-concept.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • programmable mobile robots or comparable concrete devices
  • a constructivist lesson structure with student activity in real-life-like problem solving

Constraints¶

  • The article warns that the direct motivational effects of educational toys usually are not permanent

Target Learners¶

  • novice programmers at college with no secondary-school programming background

Target Learning Goals¶

  • basic programming knowledge and skills
  • programming self-concept and learning motivation

Affordances¶

  • Concrete Operations Programming Framework

Claims¶

  • Students taught with model robots failed significantly fewer lessons than conventionally taught students [+W]
  • Students using real programmable robots enjoyed learning more and found the material less difficult than control students [+W]
  • Neither experimental nor control groups' programming knowledge developed significantly during the robot-based semester [-W]
  • Programming self-concept developed significantly in the robot group while remaining stable in the control group [+W]
  • Attitudes towards the teacher did not change notably in either group during the semester [~W]
