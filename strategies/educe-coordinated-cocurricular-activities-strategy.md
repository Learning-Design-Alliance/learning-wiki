# Design cocurricular data science activities to be accessible in cost, content, and schedule and coordinated with course modules rather than stand-alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
EDUCE cocurricular activities (workshops, hackathons, directed studies) reinforce course modules outside the classroom. They must be "accessible in terms of cost, content, and schedule" because undergraduates often cannot pay boot-camp costs and resist outside training due to motivational barriers or limited time. Activities "directly reiterate and build on learning objectives from related course modules" and "invoke the same or similar data sets and packages as course modules", since "fully stand-alone programs, such as “boot camps,” do not promote long-term skill development even at the graduate level". Workshops are scheduled in time with course modules to prime participation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained institutional support is needed to maintain the subsidized cost-recovery model, ideally enabling registered students to participate for free
- Active advertisement in undergraduate courses, program websites, and campus listserves
#### Constraints
- Integration with classroom modules makes activities discipline-specific, though the article notes discipline-specific data sets need not exclude participants from other disciplines

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life sciences students
- participants across training levels from undergraduates to industry professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- tidying tabular data
- effective data visualization
- troubleshooting with tools such as R help and Stack Overflow
