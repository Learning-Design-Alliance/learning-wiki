# Partner Reading

## Description
Partner reading is a cooperative learning strategy in which two learners work together to read an assigned text. Learners take turns reading and provide each other with feedback in order to monitor comprehension. This strategy is often used as part of Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS). PALS is a class-wide peer tutoring program in which teachers carefully partner a learner with a classmate. The partner reading strategy allows learners to take turns reading and provide each other with feedback as a way to monitor comprehension. When either partner makes an error while reading, the other will say, “The word is ___. What’s the word?” The reader will repeat back the correct word. Then, the partner will say, “Now reread.” and the reader will go back to the beginning of the sentence, this time reading without error.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mixed-ability partner pairs, assigned reading texts, and an understanding of partner reading procedures. Ensure Partner A is the stronger reader and Partner B is the one more in need of the practice, but do not tell students this logic behind the pairings. Students need only to know whether they are Partner A or B.
#### Constraints
- The feedback can discourage effort and achievement if mostly negative. So, it is important to ensure the tools and protocols used promote feedback that is actionable, timely, focused, proactive, clear, and personalized to promote a growth mindset.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- K-12 learners of varying reading skill levels, including second language learners and learners with learning disabilities

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Improve reading fluency, enhance comprehension, provide corrective feedback, model fluent reading, practice reading with expression

### Affordances
- Provides a model of fluent reading, helps learners practice reading with expression, supports comprehension monitoring, offers opportunities for individualized instruction, improves reading accuracy, and encourages peer feedback and correction.

### Personalization
- Teachers may include story retelling, summarization, or writing activities for varying skill levels. English Learners can be paired with fluent students for language development. Partner reading can be done with cross age/grade partners.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor reading accuracy, fluency, and comprehension during partner reading sessions. Collect data on error correction and student participation. Analyze student summaries and retellings to assess comprehension.

## Impact
- Research shows partner reading promotes speed, accuracy, and proper expression in reading, as well as comprehension skills. Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS) is an evidence-based practice to promote reading achievement. Resource: Armbruster, B., Lehr, F., & Osborn, J. (2001). Put Reading First: The Research Building Blocks for Teaching Children to Read.
