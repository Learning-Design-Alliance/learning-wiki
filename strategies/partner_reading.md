# Partner Reading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Partner reading is a cooperative learning strategy in which two learners work together to read an assigned text. Learners take turns reading aloud and provide each other with feedback in order to monitor comprehension. It is most widely implemented as the fluency component of Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies (PALS), a class-wide peer-tutoring program in which the teacher pairs a stronger reader with a developing reader. When one partner makes an error, the other says, "The word is ___. What's the word?" The reader repeats the correct word, and the partner says, "Now reread," prompting the reader to return to the beginning of the sentence and read it without error.

## Design Implications

Partner reading converts independent reading into structured, high-frequency oral practice with immediate error correction, which increases the number of accurate word-reading repetitions per session — the mechanism most strongly tied to fluency growth [Automatic word recognition frees cognitive resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]. Because feedback comes from a peer within seconds of an error, correction is far more frequent than teacher-led round-robin reading can provide. The strategy also enacts [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) [+M], but its effectiveness depends on tight procedural structure: unstructured "read together" pairings produce far weaker gains than scripted turn-taking with a fixed correction routine [~S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mixed-ability partner pairs, with Partner A the stronger reader and Partner B the one needing more practice; do not disclose this logic — students need only know whether they are Partner A or B
- Assigned texts at an appropriate difficulty level, ideally one the developing reader can read with support but not fully independently
- Explicit teaching and rehearsal of the correction routine ("The word is ___. What's the word?" → repeat → "Now reread.") before the first session
- A predictable session structure: typically 5 minutes of stronger-reader-first modeling, then alternating paragraph or page turns

#### Constraints
- Feedback that is mostly negative discourages effort and achievement [-M]; protocols must keep correction brief, neutral, and paired with genuine praise so that feedback supports rather than erodes self-efficacy [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Texts that are too difficult for the developing reader turn the session into frustration practice; error rates above roughly 10% undermine fluency benefits [-M]
- Poorly matched or unmanaged pairs can drift into off-task behavior; the routine requires active teacher monitoring, especially in early sessions [-W]
- Benefits are strongest for fluency and word-level accuracy; comprehension gains are smaller and depend on adding discussion or retelling components [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **PALS Reading (Fuchs et al.)** — class-wide program pairing partner reading with structured comprehension activities (prediction, paragraph shrinking), grades 2–6
- **Cross-age partner reading** — older students read with younger ones; benefits both fluency practice and the older reader's confidence
- **English learner pairings** — ELs paired with fluent peers for language development, with attention to accessible text [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md)
- **Extensions** — story retelling, summarization, or brief writing after reading to deepen comprehension

### Target Learners
- K–12 learners across reading skill levels, including second-language learners and learners with learning disabilities [+M]
- Developing readers who need high-volume oral reading practice with immediate correction
- Stronger readers benefit as models and correctors, reinforcing their own accuracy and expression [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading fluency: speed, accuracy, and prosody through repeated supported oral reading
- Comprehension monitoring: partners check understanding and correct breakdowns in real time
- Peer feedback and collaboration skills: giving brief, kind, specific corrections

### Instructions
1. Assign mixed-ability pairs and label students Partner A / Partner B without explaining the ability logic.
2. Model the correction routine with a student volunteer, then have pairs rehearse it with a short practice text ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md)).
3. Partner A reads first while Partner B follows the text and corrects errors using the routine; then partners alternate paragraphs or pages ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
4. Circulate and monitor pairs, reinforcing accurate use of the correction routine and redirecting off-task pairs ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)).
5. Close with a brief comprehension exchange — retell, summary, or prediction check — in pairs or as a class ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).

## Examples
- **PALS Reading (grades 2–6)** — a 35-minute cycle: 5 minutes of partner reading with correction, followed by prediction and paragraph-shrinking activities; implemented class-wide two to three times per week.
- **First-grade PALS (Mathes et al.)** — a phonics-focused variant in which pairs alternate decoding activities and partner reading of decodable texts.
- **Cross-age reading buddies** — fifth graders paired with first graders weekly; older students model fluent reading, then the younger child rereads the same passage with correction.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor reading accuracy, fluency rate, and expression during sessions; track error-correction events and participation. Analyze retellings and summaries for comprehension. Experimental and quasi-experimental studies of PALS show reliable gains in oral reading fluency, with smaller but positive effects on comprehension, including for students with disabilities and English learners [+M].
