# Maker-inspired interdisciplinary sensor project where students build, program and collect real data

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The air:bit course is a maker-inspired interdisciplinary strategy in which students participate in all parts of a scientific project: soldering components, programming sensors, collecting local air quality data, and analyzing measurements. Teachers choose how to incorporate the project into their subjects, supported by a two-day teacher workshop, a full-day student workshop for finalizing assembly and programming, and open online guides. The authors offer it as insight for others providing similar projects to students without programming experience and about motivating students to engage in computer science and other STEM subjects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher workshop to teach assembly and programming; online guides and example code; a full-day student workshop to finalize kits; an online platform for uploading and exploring data
#### Constraints
- The authors note analysis of collected measurements was left to teachers, and some students reported little retained programming knowledge; one class shared a complete solution rather than programming the kits themselves

### Target Learners
- upper secondary school students without programming experience

### Target Learning Goals
- computer programming
- electronics
- environmental science
- planning and executing a scientific project
