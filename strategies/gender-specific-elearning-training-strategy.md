# Gender-specific workshops, new-student orientation, and faculty development to improve use of national e-learning resources

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a bundle of institutional actions to raise SDL use: gender-specific training or workshops focused on digital literacy, comprehensive orientation for newly admitted students, encouragement of teaching faculty to motivate students, integration of SDL into teaching and assessment, and a faculty development program on digital literacy. It states that "gender-specific training or workshops can be implemented" and that faculty development is essential to enable faculty to motivate students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training should focus specifically on digital literacy to help students overcome reported barriers
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a single-institution cross-sectional survey with convenience sampling and limited generalizability

### Target Learners
- undergraduate pharmacy students (BSc Pharm and Pharm.D) at a Saudi public university

### Target Learning Goals
- effective use of national e-learning resources such as the Saudi Digital Library for self-directed, evidence-based learning
