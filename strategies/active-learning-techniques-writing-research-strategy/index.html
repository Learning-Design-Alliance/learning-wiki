Implement active learning techniques including peer collaboration, presentations, debates, and online discussion forums in writing and research instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article states that active learning includes several techniques such as demonstration, collaboration with peers, presentations, debates, and cooperative/collaborative activities, and that "All of these strategies were implemented in teaching the writing and research methods course in this research." Concrete implementations included weekly class discussions of parts of the research, a conference-style final presentation with questions from professor and peers, and a Blackboard Discussion Forum on which 10 percent of the final grade was based on the level and quality of participation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors coach students throughout the term to produce the research paper in stages and value maximizing opportunities for students to learn

Constraints¶

  • The authors note Saudi students are accustomed to a more passive role and may need encouragement, at first, to actively shape their own academic experiences

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate preparatory-year students with limited prior research experience

Target Learning Goals¶

  • writing and research skills, oral communication of research findings, and autonomous learning

Claims¶

  • Active learning students outperformed traditional-instruction students on each of the 14 individual writing and research skills, with an average 10.5-point higher score [+M]
  • Active learning instruction produced significantly higher overall final exam scores than traditional lecture instruction in a preparatory-year writing and research course [+M]
  • In the experimental group's skill rankings, search-engine searching, research proposal writing, and results interpretation ranked highest, while annotated bibliography, literature review, and introduction writing ranked lowest [+M]
  • The largest active-learning gains occurred in searching topics and keywords in search engines (skill 6) and writing and interpreting results of research tools (skill 12) [+M]
  • The smallest active-learning gains occurred in writing about previous studies, debating research topics, and using APA format [+M]
  • Four EFL learners interviewed about synchronous online forum discussion described seven themes: positive feelings, motivation, interaction, writing-skill gains and autonomy, alongside practical challenges and a cultural issue [+W]
  • Peer pairing, peer tutoring, and small collaborative/cooperative groupings improved MLs' English phonological awareness across grade levels and were often associated with better English decoding and fluency than teacher-mediated instruction [+M]
