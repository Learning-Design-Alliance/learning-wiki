# Never distribute worked solutions to activity worksheets; offer select answers instead

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
An implementable course-policy recipe from the authors' lessons learned: because worksheets act as the de-facto course textbook, students request worked solutions, but the authors' experience is that "creating worked solutions completely short-circuits the learning process" the flipped classroom works to achieve. Instead, instructors should discuss the prohibition openly in class, respect metacognitive student requests with dialog, offer correct answers to select worksheet questions (as textbooks do), provide alternative feedback mechanisms, and ensure teaching assistants also refrain from creating solutions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The prohibition must be broached with students in class, or it will appear negatively on teaching evaluations
- Teaching assistants must be informed of the prohibition
#### Constraints
- The authors report this from their own experience; it is an experiential recommendation, not a tested manipulation in this study

### Target Learners
- large-enrollment introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- persevering through productive struggle on scaffolded problems rather than seeking correct answers
