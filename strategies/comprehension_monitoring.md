# Comprehension Monitoring

## Description
This strategy involves the ability of readers to know when they understand what they read and to use strategies to improve their understanding when it is blocked. It involves metacognition. Good readers are aware of and monitor their thought processes as they read, while poor readers do not. Strategies employed to improve understanding are called 'repair' or 'fix-up' strategies, such as rereading, reading ahead, clarifying words in a dictionary, or asking for help.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need to be aware of their comprehension levels and be willing to employ fix-up strategies. Instructors should model comprehension monitoring and provide opportunities for practice and reflection.
#### Constraints
- Learners may struggle to accurately assess their comprehension levels. If learners are not familiar with effective fix-up strategies, they may not be able to improve their understanding.

### Affordances
- Improves self-regulation by enabling readers to monitor their understanding, enhances metacognition by increasing awareness of thought processes, and provides strategies for addressing comprehension difficulties.

### Personalization
- Learners can identify and practice fix-up strategies that work best for their individual learning styles and needs. Instructors can provide a range of fix-up strategies and support learners in selecting appropriate ones.
