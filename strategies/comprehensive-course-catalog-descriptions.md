# Describe each laboratory course comprehensively in course catalogs and communicate expectations before registration so students can plan accommodations in advance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Recommendation C-2 directs departments to ensure prospective students know the learning objectives, workload expectations, technical requirements, equipment and software, media types, physical and sensory considerations, and engagement types of each lab course. The rationale is that "The availability of clear and accurate descriptions of lab courses and activities facilitates self-advocacy among students", letting students and support teams plan accommodations before the first day of class. Related recommendations A-3 and C-1 call for communicating early and often about accommodations and regularly discussing which lab components are fundamental.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Academic advisors need sufficient training to support informed enrollment decisions based on workload and cognitive, physical, or sensory expectations
#### Constraints
- Four-year degree plans are not ideal for all students; some students need advising that embraces five- or six-year plans

### Target Learners
- prospective and enrolled undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-advocacy and advance planning of laboratory accommodations
