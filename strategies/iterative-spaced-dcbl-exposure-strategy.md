# Deliver DCBL as iterative, spaced exposure rather than a one-off session to support durable gains in complex clinical reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors argue that the mixed individual response profile "argues for iterative, spaced DCBL exposure rather than a one-off session", consistent with evidence that distributed practice produces more durable gains for complex cognitive skills. They also recommend embedding stewardship reasoning early in the undergraduate curriculum before suboptimal prescribing habits become entrenched.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal scheduling of multiple DCBL sessions with delayed post-tests
#### Constraints
- Durability of domain-specific improvements has not yet been established and requires longitudinal study

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical (MBBS) learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Durable antibiotic-stewardship clinical reasoning
