Invest in high-quality induction and mentoring programs, which are associated with higher retention particularly for teachers of color¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The brief recommends retention programming that provides ongoing support and growth opportunities: "High-quality induction programs are associated with higher retention rates among early-career teachers for all teachers but particularly for teachers of color." Arizona and Kentucky invested ESSER funds in mentoring and/or induction programs for new teachers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Funding for new-teacher mentoring and induction programming

Constraints¶

  • The retention association is stated without cited evidence or effect sizes in the brief

Target Learners¶

  • Early-career teachers, particularly teachers of color

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improving retention of early-career teachers of color

Related Strategies¶

  • Support teacher residency programs, which are associated with higher retention rates for teachers of color
  • Invest in retention of teachers of color through residencies, affinity groups, and induction/mentoring
  • Retain teachers of color through leadership pipelines, multi-year induction and mentoring, and culturally responsive school climates
  • State and federal policy recommendations: recruit and prepare more candidates, retain new teachers through early-career mentoring, and improve working conditions
  • Invest in retention efforts for teachers of color that improve working conditions and provide personal and professional growth opportunities
  • Underwrite comprehensive preparation, improve teaching conditions, raise compensation, and expand induction to retain teachers of color
  • A federal Marshall Plan for Teaching focused on seven areas: compensation, debt-free preparation, high-retention pathways, mentoring, expertise-sharing, school redesign, and accountability
