# Run a half-recitation social-psychological activity combining concern writing, peer quotes, small-group discussion, and a letter to a future student

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a concrete classroom activity implemented by two trained female physics graduate student facilitators in randomly selected recitations. Students write about their concerns, see quotations from prior male and female students who did well, discuss in small groups, hear a class discussion emphasizing that adversity is temporary, and then write a letter to a future student using the "saying is believing" principle.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two trained facilitators introduce the activity as helping the department understand student concerns and foster better learning environments
- Activity is run in half of a recitation class period at the beginning of the semester
#### Constraints
- Implemented in a required introductory calculus-based physics course taken by physical science and engineering majors typically in their first year, first semester in college

### Target Learners
- First-year physical science and engineering majors in introductory calculus-based physics

### Target Learning Goals
- Sense of belonging
- Growth mindset
- Equitable and inclusive learning environment
