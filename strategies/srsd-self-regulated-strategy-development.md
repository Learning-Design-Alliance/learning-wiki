# SRSD (Self-Regulated Strategy Development)

## Description
SRSD is a staged instructional model in which teachers explicitly teach a genre-specific strategy (e.g., POW+TREE for persuasive writing: Pick my idea, Organize notes, Write and say more; Topic sentence, Reasons, Explanations, Ending) while simultaneously building the self-regulation routines learners need to use it on their own. Instruction moves through six recursive stages — develop background knowledge, discuss it, model it, memorize it, support it, independent performance — with the teacher gradually releasing responsibility as students take over strategy use and self-monitoring.

## Design Implications

SRSD combines explicit strategy instruction with [self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md): the strategy supplies the *what* of skilled performance, and the self-regulation component supplies the *when, why,* and *whether*. Meta-analyses show it is among the most effective writing interventions studied, with effects on text quality, structure, and length that persist and transfer across genres and settings [Graham & Perin meta-analysis of adolescent writing instruction.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Its effectiveness depends on the modeling and support stages being genuinely interactive — students must see the strategy used and practice it with feedback before independent performance.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-specified, memorable strategy for the target genre or task, with mnemonic supports
- Instructor modeling that makes strategic thinking visible ([articulation](../elements/articulation.md) of decisions, not just steps)
- Graphic organizers or planning sheets that externalize the strategy during the support stage
- Self-monitoring tools (checklists, graphs of progress) so students can see their own growth
- Multiple practice opportunities across texts and tasks to support transfer

#### Constraints
- Effects shrink when the support stage is skipped — moving from modeling straight to independent writing leaves students unable to execute the strategy unaided [-M]
- Less effective when taught as a single genre-specific script rather than a flexible, generalizable routine; rigid application limits transfer to new tasks [~M]
- Requires substantial instructional time (typically 6–10+ lessons); compressed implementations show weaker and less durable effects [-M]
- Demands teacher training in both the strategy and the self-regulation component; teachers who model only the steps without the reasoning produce mechanical strategy use [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Genre-specific strategies:** POW+TREE (persuasive), POW+WWW (narrative), POW+CSPACE (creative) — the same self-regulation shell with different content strategies
- **Adaptations for other domains:** study strategies, mathematics problem solving, and test-taking have used the same stage structure
- **Small-group and whole-class formats:** originally one-to-one, successfully adapted to classrooms with modest effect attenuation
- **Technology-supported variants:** digital planning tools and self-monitoring apps can carry the scaffolding role of paper organizers

### Target Learners
- Students with learning disabilities and struggling writers show the largest gains [+S]
- Students in grades 2–12 across ability levels benefit, though effect sizes are largest for younger and lower-achieving writers [+M]
- Students who lack strategic persistence benefit from the self-regulation component, which builds task engagement and ownership [autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Advanced writers may find fully scripted strategies constraining; instruction should emphasize flexible, selective strategy use [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Composition quality: producing organized, elaborated text in specific genres
- Strategic processes: planning, revising, and monitoring one's own work
- Self-regulation: goal setting, self-assessment, and independent strategy deployment
- Maintenance and generalization: using strategies on new tasks without prompting

### Instructions
1. **Develop background knowledge** — assess and build prerequisite skills (e.g., what makes a persuasive reason) using [advance organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) and exemplar texts.
2. **Discuss the strategy** — examine current performance, introduce the strategy mnemonic, and set goals with students so they understand why the strategy helps.
3. **Model it** — the teacher writes while thinking aloud, verbalizing strategy steps *and* self-regulation moves ("I'm stuck — let me reread my notes"), making expert reasoning observable.
4. **Memorize it** — students learn the mnemonic steps and self-instructions until they are automatic, reducing [cognitive load](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) during composing.
5. **Support it** — students apply the strategy with graphic organizers, checklists, collaborative practice, and graduated fading of teacher support.
6. **Independent performance** — students use the strategy without supports, self-monitor, graph their progress, and commit to using it in other settings.

## Related Strategies
- Reciprocal Teaching — a parallel strategy-instruction model for reading comprehension with the same modeling-and-fading logic
- Process Writing — SRSD adds explicit strategy and self-regulation instruction to process approaches
- Cognitive Apprenticeship — SRSD's modeling and fading stages enact the same [cognitive apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) sequence

## Examples
- **POW+TREE in elementary persuasive writing** — students learn to Pick an idea, Organize notes, Write and say more, then plan essays with Topic sentence, Reasons, Explanations, and Ending; extensively validated by Harris, Graham, and Mason with students in grades 2–6.
- **SRSD for revising (CDO)** — Compare, Diagnose, Operate: students compare their draft against a rubric, diagnose problems, and operate to fix them, improving revision quality in adolescents.
- **ThinkSRSD** — a widely used teacher-facing implementation framework and lesson materials (https://www.thinksrsd.com) supporting whole-class SRSD across grade levels.
