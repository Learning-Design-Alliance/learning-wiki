# Guide learners through KWIC concordance lines of a verb and its synonym set, then compare prosodic behavior across the set

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete classroom procedure drawn from the treatment condition: for each verb from the coursebook, learners receive its near synonyms from a learners' thesaurus, examine keyword-in-context concordance lines with the keyword centered and fixed left-right context, and determine which set members carry positive, neutral, or negative prosody. Learners may use dictionaries for new words but are encouraged to focus on mid-page keywords. The teacher checks analyses, presents commentaries, and gives quick class-wide feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Concordancing software and corpus access (online or via laptops)
- A dictionary of synonyms such as the Oxford Learner's Thesaurus
- Fewer examples at the beginning to motivate students and prevent overload
#### Constraints
- Semantic prosody must be distinguished from semantic preference: sometimes a word with positive SP collocates with negative words, so SP is a characteristic of the whole unit, not the collocated word

### Target Learners
- EFL high school learners studying coursebooks such as Passages 1

### Target Learning Goals
- Distinguishing prosodic behavior of near-synonyms
- Inductive learning of word-connotation patterns
