# Direct Instruction: Problem-solving Strategies

## Description
Instructors teach and model various strategies for tackling problems, then give learners the opportunity to choose which strategies they use and reflect on the outcomes of their choices. Example strategies include choosing the operation, finding a pattern, creating visual images, guesstimating, creating a table, using manipulatives, working backward, creating a systematic list, etc.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need to be knowledgeable about various problem-solving strategies and be able to model them effectively. Learners need access to a problem-solving notebook.
#### Constraints
- May not be suitable for learners who prefer a more structured, step-by-step approach. Requires learners to actively engage in reflection and self-assessment.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track the strategies learners choose and the outcomes of those choices. Analyze the quality of learners' reflections on their problem-solving processes.

## Impact
- Promotes a deeper understanding of mathematical concepts and enhances problem-solving abilities by encouraging learners to use and reflect on different strategies. Supports the development of a Learner Mindset.
