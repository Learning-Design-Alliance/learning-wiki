# Use structured discussion, argumentation tasks, and guided reflection to bridge AI perception and computational reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend complementing hands-on AI activities with explicit reasoning scaffolds. They write: "Activities that require structured explanations, such as argumentation tasks or guided reflection exercises, could further support the development of reasoning skills." They also recommend structured discussions on AI decision-making and ethics, and addressing misconceptions (students attributing emotions and moral superiority to AI) through guided discussions and real-world AI applications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements

### Target Learning Goals

- recognizing AI limitations and misconceptions
