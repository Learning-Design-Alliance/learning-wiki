# Use think-aloud interviews before a trial to identify regional contextual barriers and tailor a multi-country intervention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends complementing intervention development with think-aloud methods before a randomised trial, stating "Using think aloud methods to complement the development of interventions is a powerful method to identify regional contextual barriers to intervention implementation." GPs verbalise their initial thoughts while viewing the intervention materials, allowing developers to amend content while it can still be changed. The authors also advise creating a core intervention and tailoring aspects where needed, maximising local information and providing country-specific examples.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interviews with target users in every relevant context before implementation, using a think-aloud approach followed by semi-structured questions
#### Constraints
- The article notes participation was voluntary, so the sample may represent GPs more interested in the topic, and self-report data are vulnerable to socially acceptable answers

### Target Learners
- General practitioners in multiple European countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Prudent antibiotic prescribing behaviour
- Acceptability and uptake of web-based professional training
