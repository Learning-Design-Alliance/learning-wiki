# Use the PlaSurIn game as an alternative or complement to traditional lecture-based training for surgical instruments setup

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the study findings, the authors recommend that the PlaSurIn game could be used as an acceptable alternative or complement to traditional training methods to develop knowledge and performance about surgical instruments setup among second-semester OR technology students. Because the game is free of cost and has an easy-to-use platform, the authors suggest it can be used in future training programs to enhance OR novices' knowledge and performance in a limited amount of time with reduced educational resources, especially when routine training methods are unavailable (e.g., during the COVID-19 lockdown period) or when perioperative trainers are under pressure due to work-hour restrictions and large student numbers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have access to a personal computer
- Unique usernames and passwords must be provided to access the game URL
#### Constraints
- Generalizability may be limited by small sample size and group allocation by university
- Further studies are required to evaluate generalizability to other health-related students

### Target Learners
- Operating Room (OR) technology novices
- Second-semester undergraduate OR technology students

### Target Learning Goals
- Surgical instruments setup knowledge
- Surgical instruments setup performance
