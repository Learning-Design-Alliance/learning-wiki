# Embed structured self-reflection activities in brief asynchronous professional training to improve knowledge and observed skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating self-reflection activities into evidence-based intervention training as a structured method for trainees to "critically assess their own skills, identify areas for improvement, and track their progress over time." The RCT showed this worked even in a very brief module, improving knowledge and observed consultation skills, though not self-reported confidence. The authors also recommend combining self-reports with objective performance measures over time, and emphasising scaffolding for time management, probing questions and checking for generality in future iterations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reflection activities should be structured (confidence ratings, goal setting, self-evaluation) rather than unguided
#### Constraints
- Confidence and self-efficacy were not enhanced in the short term; generalizability to in-service practitioners and long-term sustainability remain untested

### Target Learners
- pre-service and in-service health, education, and social care professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical consultation and intake-interview skills in evidence-based intervention delivery
