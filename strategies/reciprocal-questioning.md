# Reciprocal Questioning

## Description
Reciprocal questioning asks learners to formulate questions about material they have just read or studied and to pose those questions to peers, who answer and discuss. The questioner role rotates, so every learner alternates between generating questions and responding to them. It is a core component of [Reciprocal Teaching](../elements/reciprocal-teaching.md) (alongside predicting, clarifying, and summarizing) and can also be run standalone as "question generation" or "reQuest" activities.

## Design Implications

Generating a question requires learners to identify the main ideas, gaps, and relationships in the material — a generative processing activity that outperforms rereading [Generating explanations and self-explanations improves learning.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-learning.md) [+S]. Answering peers' questions adds retrieval practice and exposes learners to alternative framings of the same content. Because question quality reveals comprehension, the strategy also functions as formative assessment: a vague question signals a vague understanding [Assessment that feeds forward improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Text or content substantial enough to support multiple questionable points
- Explicit modeling of good question generation ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) demonstration of "how I decide what to ask")
- A discussion structure in which peers must actually answer, not defer to the teacher ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
- Sentence stems or question-type scaffolds (e.g., "What would happen if…?", "Why does X cause Y?") for learners new to question generation

#### Constraints
- Learners default to literal, low-level questions ("What color was…?") without training in question taxonomy; quality does not improve without modeling and feedback [-M]
- In groups with large knowledge asymmetries, weaker questioners may simply imitate stronger peers' questions, gaining little generative benefit [~M]
- Rotating roles consumes time; for short or simple texts the overhead exceeds the benefit [-W]
- If the teacher reclaims all answering, the "reciprocal" structure collapses into ordinary quizzing and the generative benefit is lost [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **ReQuest (Manzo, 1969):** teacher and students alternately question each other over successive text segments
- **Question-Generation homework:** students write questions before class; the best are used to open discussion
- **Peer questioning in pairs:** each partner writes two questions, swaps, answers, then critiques the questions themselves
- **Reciprocal Teaching full protocol:** questioning embedded with predicting, clarifying, and summarizing in small groups with [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) that fades over time

### Target Learners
- Middle-grade through adult readers; strongest effects documented for struggling readers in grades 3–8 [Reciprocal teaching improves comprehension for struggling readers.](../claims/reciprocal-teaching-improves-reading-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Learners who passively read but do not self-monitor; question generation forces active comprehension checking
- Less effective for novices who lack enough domain knowledge to judge what is worth asking [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension and monitoring of understanding
- Identifying main ideas and relationships in expository text
- Discussion and academic discourse skills
- Metacognitive self-questioning as a transferable study habit

### Instructions
1. Model question generation on a short passage, thinking aloud about why each question targets a main idea ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)).
2. Provide question-type stems and a simple taxonomy (literal, inferential, evaluative) as an [Advance Organizer](../elements/advance-organizers.md).
3. Have learners read a segment and write 2–3 questions individually.
4. Rotate questioners: each learner poses a question to the group or partner; peers answer before the teacher confirms ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)).
5. Discuss question quality briefly — which questions were hardest, and why — as feedback on comprehension.
6. Fade the stems and teacher facilitation as groups internalize the routine ([Fading](../elements/fading.md)), consistent with contingent support [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M].

## Examples
- **Palincsar & Brown's reciprocal teaching trials (1984):** seventh-grade poor comprehenders trained to lead small-group dialogues using questioning, clarifying, summarizing, and predicting; comprehension gains of roughly 30 percentile points over ~20 sessions.
- **ReQuest procedure (Manzo, 1969):** teacher and students take turns questioning each other across successive segments of a story, used clinically with disabled readers.
- **Question-generation study prompts:** college courses where students submit two discussion questions per reading, which seed the next class's discussion.
