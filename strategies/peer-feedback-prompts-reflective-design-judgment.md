# Use peer feedback to prompt students to reconsider design judgments and engage in reflection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy uses peer feedback during design projects as a trigger for students to revisit initial, unreflective judgments and reflect on them. In the case analyses, peer feedback prompted reconsideration: for Annette, "Peer feedback helped her reconsider her judgment and employ reflection," and for Jian, peer feedback helped her consider switching from a 5W1H strategy to draw-and-retell and employ reflection. Instructors can structure opportunities for peer feedback at points where students make early design judgments about materials, strategies, and instruction structure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Design projects in which students make identifiable early judgments that can be surfaced and discussed with peers
- Opportunities for structured peer feedback during the development of instruction, not only at final review
