# Implement suturing/ligature tutoring in parallel with real patient practice, combining expert-led teaching with AI system-assisted self-assessment sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adding a structured tutoring course—expert-led orientation on artificial skin with an OSATS-based checklist, in-training formative assessment with one-on-one feedback, plus voluntary additional practice and automated self-assessment on the WKS-2RII AI system—to the regular surgical curriculum. The authors conclude that "suturing/ligature tutoring should be implemented in parallel with real patient practice to increase medical interns’ confidence in suturing/ligature skills," and that increasing the frequency of AI practice sessions improved interns' confidence and performance. The course emphasized safety (correct needle handling, square-knot ligature), quality, and efficiency (three vertical mattress sutures within 10 minutes).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the WKS-2RII AI assessment system and suturing training kits (needle holder, forceps, scissors, needle, silk, suturing pad)
- Experienced surgical faculty to deliver orientation and one-on-one OSCE-station feedback
#### Constraints
- Participation was voluntary and did not affect grade standing; the evidence comes from a small-scale, single-site pilot study, and the authors note intervention-group performance may partly reflect intrinsic motivation of volunteers

### Target Learners
- Novice medical interns (fifth-year clinical training) without prior basic suturing/ligature training

### Target Learning Goals
- Suturing and ligature technical skill and confidence before real-world practice on patients
