# Community Building

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Community building is the intentional cultivation of relationships, shared norms, and collective identity within a learning group. It is carried out through structured early interactions (introductions, [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md)), ongoing collaborative routines, and design decisions that make learners interdependent — so that participation feels socially meaningful rather than merely required.

## Design Implications

Sense of belonging is a reliable predictor of persistence and achievement, and brief belonging-focused interventions produce measurable gains, especially for at-risk students [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. Community building also supports motivation through relatedness, one of the three basic psychological needs [Autonomy-supportive environments increase intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]. It is not a soft add-on: without trust and shared norms, [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) activities degrade into parallel individual work.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Early, low-stakes opportunities for learners to know one another ([Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md), introductions tied to content)
- Visible, co-developed norms for discussion, feedback, and disagreement
- Interdependent tasks in which learners genuinely need one another's contributions
- Instructor presence and responsiveness, particularly online ([Community of Inquiry](../principles/community-of-inquiry.md) teaching presence)

#### Constraints
- Forced or performative socializing ("icebreakers" unrelated to content) can feel coercive and waste time, undermining credibility [~W]
- Community built only through unstructured social channels tends to exclude quieter or marginalized learners; structure and multiple participation modes are needed [~M]
- In very short courses or high-turnover cohorts, deep community investment may not pay off before the group dissolves [-W]
- Large anonymous cohorts (e.g., MOOC-scale) resist community formation; smaller persistent subgroups work better [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Online:** discussion rituals, small persistent groups, video introductions, instructor weekly presence announcements
- **In-person:** seating and grouping routines, class jobs/roles, shared rituals and celebrations
- **Discipline-based:** communities of practice framing — novices join a disciplinary community through legitimate peripheral participation ([Communities of Practice](../principles/communities-of-practice.md))

### Target Learners
- First-generation, minoritized, and new students, for whom belonging uncertainty is highest [Brief belonging interventions improve academic outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Online and distance learners at risk of isolation and disengagement [+M]
- Less critical for fully self-paced, solitary learning formats, where social structures may add overhead without benefit [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Dispositional and affective goals: persistence, motivation, identity as a member of a field
- Collaborative competence: giving and receiving feedback, productive disagreement
- Sustained engagement in long-horizon learning (degree programs, professional communities)

### Instructions
1. Open the course with structured connection activities — content-linked [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) rather than generic icebreakers.
2. Co-construct discussion and feedback norms with learners in week one; revisit them periodically.
3. Design interdependent [Collaborative Learning](../principles/collaborative-learning.md) tasks with individual accountability (e.g., [Accountability Partners](accountability-partners.md)).
4. Maintain instructor presence: regular announcements, timely responses, and visible participation in discussions ([Community of Inquiry](../principles/community-of-inquiry.md)).
5. Mark progress and milestones collectively — shared celebrations, public artifacts, [Authentic Audiences](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) for group work.

## Related Strategies
- [Accountability Partners](accountability-partners.md) — a lightweight structure that converts community into sustained individual follow-through

## Examples
- **P2PU (Peer 2 Peer University)** — learning circles where small groups meet in person around online courses; community is the retention mechanism. [https://www.p2pu.org](https://www.p2pu.org)
- **El Sistema–inspired ensemble programs** — musical community and collective performance function as the primary motivator for sustained practice.
- **First-year seminar programs** (e.g., learning communities at community colleges) — cohorting students across linked courses to build peer networks; associated with improved persistence [+M].
