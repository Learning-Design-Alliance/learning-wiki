# Deliver simulation-based training of high-acuity low-occurrence procedures remotely via a mobile telesimulation unit with off-site mentor guidance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends mobile telesimulation as a way to provide expert mentorship and overcome geographic, cost, and time barriers to delivering simulation-based medical education in rural and remote locations. The strategy deploys a self-contained unit at a rural or remote site housing the skills training session, with an off-site skilled mentor guiding training through 2-way communication. The authors state that mobile telesimulation could be an effective way of providing expert mentorship and that a collaborative approach drawing on local expertise along with distance-guided mentorship could facilitate advances. They recommend an economic impact evaluation and further validation for other procedural skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An off-site experienced mentor comfortable with simulation-based teaching delivered through telecommunication
- Reliable internet connectivity; the authors note limited connectivity in rural areas may impede delivery
#### Constraints
- Mentor unavailability comfortable with telecommunication-delivered teaching may present a barrier to expanding this approach
- Internet connectivity limitations may particularly affect how learners perceive and rate their remote mentoring experience

### Target Learners
- rural and remote acute care practitioners

### Target Learning Goals
- high-acuity low-occurrence procedural skills
