# Replace visual node identification with audible signals so blind students can actively perform physics-lab measurements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's core adaptation strategy: instead of identifying standing-wave nodes visually, the physical variable is made accessible through sound. The authors note 'a natural alternative to visual readings is to include sounds related to the value of the physical variable to be measured,' implemented here with a guitar pickup and amplifier feeding headphones so the blind student can concentrate on the work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A sensor transducing the physical variable into an audible signal (here a guitar pickup), an amplifier with headphone output, and an audio-reading position sensor on an Arduino platform
#### Constraints
- Some parts of the measurement, such as reading frequency on a computer screen, may still require a sighted collaborator unless separately adapted

### Target Learners
- blind and visually impaired students in undergraduate physics laboratory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- active participation in experimental measurement of wave phenomena
