Islamic Geometry Exploration¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 14 studies (4 causal, 4 review, 3 quant-synthesis, 3 associational), q2–q4 · 3 of 14 report an effect size

Description¶

Students explore Islamic art and architecture — tessellations, rosettes, girih patterns — as a context for geometry standards such as symmetry, congruency, similarity, and composition. A typical entry point is a virtual field trip to the Mosque of Sultan Barquq in Cairo, where students identify patterns and the underlying geometric constructions. Facilitated questioning connects the artistic forms to mathematical ideas and to the cultures that produced them.

Design Implications¶

This is an ethnomathematics approach: mathematics is situated in cultural artifacts so that geometric abstraction emerges from authentic, visually rich cases rather than decontextualized exercises. [+M], and prompting students to explain how a pattern was constructed deepens conceptual understanding Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding. [+S]. The artistic entry point also builds task value for students who are disengaged from formal mathematics, which improves motivation and engagement Task value increases motivation and engagement. Context¶

Requirements¶

  • High-quality images or a virtual field trip (e.g., the Mosque of Sultan Barquq, Cairo) showing patterns at architectural scale
  • Facilitation moves that direct attention to geometric properties — symmetry type, unit cell, rotation order — not just aesthetic appreciation
  • Tools for construction: compass and straightedge, or digital tools such as GeoGebra, so students can reproduce and vary patterns

Constraints¶

  • Without explicit facilitation, students notice surface beauty but not the underlying geometry; open-ended "what do you see?" prompts alone rarely produce transferable mathematical structure [~M]
  • Cultural content presented superficially ("tiles as decoration") risks tokenism; teachers need enough background on Islamic geometric construction to answer "how was this made?" questions
  • Construction of complex rosettes imposes high procedural load on novices; step-heavy compass work can crowd out the geometric reasoning it is meant to serve Chunking reduces working memory load. [~M] — simplify the construction or provide partially completed templates
  • The spectacular 10-fold and quasi-crystalline girih patterns documented by Lu and Steinhardt exceed school-level construction techniques; treating them as reproducible exercises misrepresents both the mathematics and the history [~W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Observation-first: virtual tour and pattern-spotting discussion before any construction
  • Construction-first: students build a simple rosette, then analyze historical examples
  • Digital: GeoGebra or pattern-block apps allow rapid variation of angles and symmetry orders
  • Cross-curricular: pair with social studies units on Islamic civilizations or art units on pattern and ornament

Target Learners¶

  • K–12 students; the observation-first variant works from upper elementary, construction-based variants from middle school onward
  • Students who see themselves as "not math people" often engage through the artistic entry point, building task value before formal notation Task value increases motivation and engagement. [+M]
  • Learners benefit from spatial reasoning practice; structured work with visual-spatial patterns supports spatial skill development Block play predicts math achievement. [+M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Geometric concepts: symmetry (reflectional, rotational), tessellation, congruence, similarity, angle relationships, composition and decomposition of shapes
  • Mathematical practices: identifying structure, generalizing from cases, precision in construction
  • Cultural awareness: mathematics as a human, historical endeavor across civilizations

Instructions¶

  1. Activate curiosity. Open with a virtual field trip or image gallery (Mosque of Sultan Barquq, Alhambra, Topkapı scroll facsimiles). Use Advance Organizers to frame what students are looking for: repeating units, symmetry, angles.
  2. Facilitate pattern analysis. Through Class Discussion, guide students to identify the unit cell, symmetry type, and how shapes fit together. Push from "what do you notice?" to "how would you construct this?"
  3. Construct. Students reproduce a simplified pattern with compass and straightedge or GeoGebra, applying Practice to the geometric relationships just observed.
  4. Vary and generalize. Students change a parameter (number of fold lines, polygon choice) and predict the effect, connecting cases to support abstraction Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction. [+M]
  5. Share and connect. Use Whole-Class Sharing to consolidate the mathematics and discuss the cultural and historical context.

Related Strategies¶

  • Case-Based Learning — historical artifacts function as cases from which mathematical structure is abstracted
  • Activating Prior Knowledge — students bring everyday experience with patterns in tiles, textiles, and screens that the exploration builds on

Related Elements¶

  • Class Discussion — the primary facilitation format during exploration
  • Analogies — connecting rosette construction to students' prior work with circles and angles
  • Application — construction tasks apply the geometric properties identified during observation
  • Collaboration — pattern analysis and construction work well in pairs or small groups

Patterns That Use This Strategy¶

  • Case-Based Learning — the monument or artifact serves as the anchor case
  • Develop Understanding — exploration precedes formalization of geometric definitions

Tools¶

  • GeoGebra — free dynamic geometry software for constructing and varying Islamic patterns
  • Google Arts & Culture — virtual tours of the Mosque of Sultan Barquq and other Islamic architectural sites
  • Pattern in Islamic Art — archive of geometric patterns for analysis

Examples¶

  • A middle school unit begins with a virtual tour of the Mosque of Sultan Barquq; students identify 8-fold rosettes, then construct one with compass and straightedge, naming the angle relationships that make the construction work.
  • Students compare a 6-fold pattern from the Alhambra with a 10-fold pattern from Darb-i Imam, predicting which tessellates the plane and why — connecting to the quasicrystalline girih constructions documented by Lu and Steinhardt [~S].
