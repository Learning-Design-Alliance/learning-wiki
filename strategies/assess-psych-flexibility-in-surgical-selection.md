# Assess psychological flexibility alongside physical, cognitive, and visual abilities when identifying and developing surgical potential

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that prediction of surgical potential should not focus exclusively on innate abilities. It states that "the current results highlight the importance of assessing physical, cognitive, visual, and psychological functioning to predict surgical potential", and that psychological flexibility may be developed: "psychological flexibility is a skill that may be taught and/or increased through mindfulness-based practice". This supports early, individualised preparation of surgical candidates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Measurement of psychological flexibility (e.g. the AAQ-II used here) alongside ability measures in screening or selection contexts
#### Constraints
- The study examined initial training and early skill acquisition only, so the value of psychological flexibility beyond early training is undetermined

### Target Learners
- medical students and surgical trainees (laparoscopic novices)

### Target Learning Goals
- laparoscopic technical skill acquisition and surgical potential identification
