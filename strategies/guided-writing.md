# Guided Writing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Guided writing is a small-group or whole-class instructional strategy in which the teacher scaffolds a composition task: modeling text construction, eliciting ideas through prompts, and providing targeted feedback while students draft. Responsibility for the writing gradually shifts from teacher to student, typically within a gradual-release sequence (model → shared → guided → independent).

## Design Implications

Guided writing works because it reduces the working-memory and self-regulation demands of composing — planning, transcribing, and revising compete for the same limited resources, especially in novice writers [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Meta-analytic evidence shows that explicit, structured writing instruction — including strategy instruction with teacher modeling and scaffolded practice — produces substantial gains in text quality compared with unguided approaches [Graham & Perin, 2007] [+S]. The teacher's role is to make invisible compositional decisions (audience, structure, sentence construction) visible, then fade support as students internalize them.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear model text or live teacher composition that demonstrates decisions, not just the finished product
- Prompts and sentence frames calibrated to students' current control of the genre
- Brief, specific feedback during drafting rather than only after completion ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) embedded in the writing process)
- A plan for fading support — moving from shared composition to independent drafting

#### Constraints
- Over-scaffolding (heavy sentence frames, teacher-composed openings) can produce formulaic, teacher-voiced text and depress motivation [~M] — frames must fade quickly or students never develop independent planning
- Whole-class guided writing is less effective than small-group formats for students with widely divergent skill levels, because prompts cannot be calibrated to individual needs [-M]
- Guiding every sentence undermines the fluency practice that transcription automaticity requires; some independent low-stakes writing must accompany it [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Interactive writing** (early literacy): teacher and students jointly compose a text, sharing the pen
- **Modeled/guided genre writing** (e.g., Writing Revolution, Hochman & Wexler): sentence-level to paragraph-level guided construction within a content area
- **Writing conferences**: brief individualized guidance during independent writing ([5-Minute Writing Conferences](5-minute_writing_conferences.md))
- **Collaborative guided writing**: pairs jointly plan and draft under teacher guidance; peer co-construction improves text quality [Collaborative writing improves text quality.](../claims/collaborative-writing-improves-text-quality.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Emerging and struggling writers, who lack the self-regulation strategies to plan and monitor composition unaided [Graham & Perin, 2007] [+S]
- English learners, who benefit from explicit models of rhetorical and sentence structures in the target language [+M]
- Proficient writers benefit less from structured guidance and more from extended independent composing time [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Genre and text-structure knowledge: how a particular text type is organized
- Sentence-level craft: combining, expanding, and varying sentences
- Self-regulated composing: planning, monitoring, and revising one's own text
- Writing-to-learn: using guided composition to consolidate content knowledge ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) and summary writing in content areas)

### Instructions
1. **Activate prior knowledge and set purpose.** Elicit what students know about the topic and genre ([Activation](../elements/activation.md)).
2. **Model.** Compose a short text (or portion) live, thinking aloud about audience, structure, and word choice — the writing equivalent of a demonstration.
3. **Compose jointly.** Build a shared text with the class, eliciting and negotiating sentences ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) as the elicitation vehicle).
4. **Guide drafting.** Students draft independently or in pairs while the teacher circulates, prompting with questions ("Who is your audience here?") rather than supplying text; use brief individual guidance ([5-Minute Writing Conferences](5-minute_writing_conferences.md)).
5. **Review and revise.** Guide students in evaluating their drafts against the modeled criteria, then fade support across subsequent lessons until students plan and draft unaided.

## Examples
- **[The Writing Revolution](https://www.thewritingrevolution.org)** — Hochman & Wexler's method embeds guided sentence-level and paragraph-level composition within content instruction; teachers model, then guide, then release.
- **Interactive Writing in K–2 classrooms** (Pinnell & Fountas) — teacher and children jointly construct a text on chart paper, sharing control of the pen for known letters and words.
- **[Write to Learn](https://www.readingrockets.org)** routines on Reading Rockets — guided summary and response writing in content-area classrooms.
