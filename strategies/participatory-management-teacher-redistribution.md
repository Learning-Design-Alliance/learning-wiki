# Use participatory management to involve teachers in teacher-redistribution decisions, leveraging the 24-hour minimum teaching load rule

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (4 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Participatory management empowers teachers to participate in decisions about distributing teachers across schools. The article cites four positive values, including that "the opportunity to participate in formulating policies, is an important factor for increasing teacher morale" and that "involvement in decision -making has a positive relationship with teacher satisfaction". Local governments can use the minimum 24 face-to-face teaching hours required for regional and certification allowances to involve teachers in redistribution decisions, with surplus-city teachers choosing rural schools to fulfill their workload obligation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A policy provision (minimum 24 face-to-face teaching hours for allowances) that local governments can leverage to involve teachers in decisions
#### Constraints
- Sharp debates often lead to conflicts that affect the work atmosphere
- Not all teachers are open-minded enough to reach problem-solving

### Target Learners
- Teachers in surplus and shortage areas in South Bangka Regency

### Target Learning Goals
- Teacher participation in redistribution decision-making and equitable teacher workload distribution
