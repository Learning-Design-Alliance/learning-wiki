# Sustain teacher residencies through blended federal and state funding, including TQP grants, ESSER funds, and dedicated state investments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report describes a range of funding sources states use to launch and sustain residencies. The Teacher Quality Partnership grant program is a primary federal vehicle; despite being authorized at $300 million annually, it was funded at just $70 million for fiscal year 2024. States have also used one-time ESSER funds — the Council of Chief State School Officers estimates states spent at least $79.1 million on residencies — Title II, Part A of ESSA, IDEA Part D, and AmeriCorps. California's investment, totaling more than $670 million over 5 years, is the largest in the nation, and New Mexico's grew from $1 million in 2019 to $15.5 million in 2022.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- upfront investment to support paid, yearlong, intensive clinical preparation through district–preparation-program partnerships
#### Constraints
- Federal COVID-19 relief funds are one-time; the report suggests ESSER investments could serve as a model for scaling future federal investments.

### Target Learners
- teacher residency candidates and the PK–12 students they will teach

### Target Learning Goals
- building a well-prepared, equitably distributed, and stable teacher workforce
