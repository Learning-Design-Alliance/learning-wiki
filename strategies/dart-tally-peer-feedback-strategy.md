# Use DART tallies as a conversational substrate for peer feedback and coaching of debriefers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends sharing quantitative DART counts with debriefer colleagues as a concrete basis for feedback conversations, e.g. telling a colleague how many questions versus statements they made and asking about their thought process. Quantitative scoring is positioned as a substrate for direct observation, faculty mentoring, and targeted coaching conversations, and may help debriefers improve at all levels of experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A completed DART tally of the debriefing being discussed
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that DART scores under advocacy-inquiry debriefing could misrepresent effectiveness, and the tool's ratios are not yet mapped to debriefing quality

### Target Learners
- novice debriefers and experienced debriefers in healthcare simulation programs

### Target Learning Goals
- improving debriefing facilitation, encouraging reflection, reducing lecturer-style debriefing
