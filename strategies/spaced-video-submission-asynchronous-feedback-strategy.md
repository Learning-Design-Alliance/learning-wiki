# Structure at-home surgical skills practice as spaced video submission with asynchronous rubric-based faculty feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The curriculum asked residents to submit one task video every two weeks, with adjustable timing and no quality standard on submissions, so practice fit around schedules and duty hours. After each submission, faculty and residents reviewed the video and gave feedback using rubrics plus free-form comments, and residents were asked to review feedback before the next task. The authors describe this as applying deliberate practice with "guiding examples, spaced practice, engaged faculty feedback, and graduated skill development."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty availability to review videos and provide asynchronous feedback
- Reminder emails when residents miss submission deadlines
- Rubrics with formative items and outcomes residents can read to know how to improve
#### Constraints
- Prior qualitative work cited by the authors found suboptimal individualized feedback and poor faculty engagement can prevent trainee progress in at-home practice
- Some residents preferred in-person skills sessions
- Equipment setup challenges led the authors to add a step-by-step box trainer setup guide

### Target Learners
- Junior surgical residents practicing outside scheduled simulation center time

### Target Learning Goals
- Psychomotor laparoscopic skills developed through deliberate practice at an individualized pace
