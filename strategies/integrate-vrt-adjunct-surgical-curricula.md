# Integrate virtual reality training as an adjunct into surgical training curricula for junior trainees

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that virtual reality training could provide a useful training adjunct to integrate into surgical training curricula, based on its meta-analysis showing improved OSATS scores and reduced operating time versus no additional training. It notes VRT "can be delivered without direct trainer supervision, utilizing the VR systems feedback and metrics", offering self-directed practice outside the operating theatre with fewer safety and staffing implications. The recommendation is framed for junior surgical trainees learning laparoscopic cholecystectomy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a VR laparoscopic simulation system; the article notes the cost of VR systems is often cited as a barrier to implementation
#### Constraints
- The article states it is not possible to conclude direct translation to routine clinical practice from research settings, and conclusions are limited by methodological heterogeneity

### Target Learners
- Junior surgical trainees (medical students, foundation trainees, core trainees, registrars and residents)

### Target Learning Goals
- Technical skills and operative performance in laparoscopic cholecystectomy
