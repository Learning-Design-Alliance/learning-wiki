# Train practitioners in active skills for directly inviting fathers and problem-solving attendance barriers, and increase organizational father-inclusive strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends building practitioner capability and organizational father-inclusive practice. It states "future training programs should include active skills training for practitioners in inviting fathers to participate and problem-solving barriers to attendance", and urges organizations to emphasize father attendance at intake, advertise programs for fathers as well as mothers, offer sessions outside working hours, and collect assessment data from both parents. Training should include managerial staff to bring about systems-level change.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training programs should be evaluated using experimental trials examining impact on father participation over time, per the article
#### Constraints
- Providing services outside working hours is unlikely to be feasible for all organizations due to staffing challenges; father-only groups should be used cautiously in two-parent families due to possible reduced effectiveness or negative effects on partner relationships

### Target Learners
- Practitioners delivering parenting interventions
- Managers and organizations delivering child and family services

### Target Learning Goals
- Competence in engaging and working with fathers in parenting interventions
