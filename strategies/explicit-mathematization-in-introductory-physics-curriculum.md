# Make mathematization an explicit part of the introductory physics curriculum rather than relying on prerequisite algebra courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors and education researchers "explore how to make mathematization a more explicit part of the curriculum," because completing mathematics prerequisites prepares students mainly for procedural aspects of mathematics, not for the mathematical reasoning needed for physical sense making. Physicists, with deep knowledge of contextualized mathematics use, are described as best positioned to help students develop this reasoning. The strategy cautions that instructors may unintentionally create barriers by using pre-fabricated explanations without attending to the fabrication process explicitly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must model mathematical sense making in physics while making the fabrication process explicit, rather than presenting pre-fabricated explanations.
#### Constraints
- The article notes there has not yet been sufficient research to establish valid models for improving this instruction.

### Target Learners
- introductory calculus-based physics students, specifically freshman engineering students

### Target Learning Goals
- algebraic reasoning and mathematical sense making in physics

- Cognitive Blending Mathematization Physics
