Implement classroom extensive reading with a graded-reader library, loan records, book reports, and teacher check-in interviews¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q1–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's treatment procedure offers an implementable recipe: equip the institution with a library of graded readers (basic vocabulary from 200–1,000 words) plus story books; have students record borrowed titles in a loan notebook; ask them to write a book report with the title and a brief comment in English; and interview participants mid-study so progress can be checked and advice given. An unstructured post-test interview confirmed students followed the plots and found the books easy enough for reading to be pleasant.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A library of different books with a range of topics students are interested in
  • Graded readers matched to learners' proficiency level

Constraints¶

  • The study lasted 4 months with vocabulary sessions twice per week; effects over other durations are not tested

Target Learners¶

  • High-intermediate EFL high school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Lexical depth
  • Vocabulary knowledge through extensive reading

Claims¶

  • In a one-month case study of one learner of French, extensive reading enhanced knowledge of about two-thirds of 133 target words, with gains varying by how often a word was encountered [+W]
  • Incorporating the extensive reading approach into vocabulary instruction significantly improves high-intermediate EFL learners' lexical depth [+W]
  • Treatment and control groups' WAT means differed by about 14 points (98.05 vs 83.95) with nearly equal standard deviations [+W]
