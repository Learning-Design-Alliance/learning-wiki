# Design online modules to encourage students to take their required first attempts more seriously

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend future instructional designs that discourage strategic guessing on required first attempts in self-paced online modules. They suggest "giving a little bit of extra credit for passing" or not explicitly reducing the number of attempts after the first try, to reduce the perceived cost of attempting to solve the problem and encourage students to take their first attempts more seriously.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online modules that log attempt timing and require a first attempt on the assessment component before instructional access
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a direction for future studies; the proposed designs were not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- students in self-paced online learning environments

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate measurement of transfer ability
- serious engagement with initial assessment attempts
