Prepare learners and balance proficiency in groups when introducing consciousness-raising grammar tasks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's implications section advises that since Vietnamese students were familiar with concept-telling, "they should be prepared to get accustomed to those fa ctors of the new aspects su ch as individual work, team-working, critical thinking operation" and should not expect the right answer from the outset. Teachers should also balance proficiency levels within groups so that less able students gain confidence and mutual assistance occurs, and may let students use English and their first language during tasks, as Ellis (1997) suggests.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers prepare students' state of mind before engaging them in the tasks
  • Group composition balances various proficiency levels
  • Teachers adjust task requirements to suit students' proficiency level

Constraints¶

  • The article warns that if groups are not balanced, "less competent students may become demotivated if they are put into one group or vice versa"

Target Learners¶

  • EFL high school students at pre-intermediate level, including under-average students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Confident participation in inductive grammar discovery tasks
  • Accurate use of English tenses

Affordances¶

  • Consciousness Raising Tasks Iegi Framework

Claims¶

  • EFL students taught with indirect explicit grammar instruction (consciousness-raising tasks) significantly outperformed direct-instruction peers on rule analysis of English tenses [+M]
  • IEGI students significantly outperformed DEGI students in oral production of English tenses one week after treatment [+M]
  • Most experimental students held favorable attitudes toward the consciousness-raising task instruction, including many who overall disliked the method [+M]
  • IEGI and DEGI produced no significant difference in using English tenses in a pre-defined (receptive) grammar-test context [~M]
  • Knowledge of grammar rules correlates positively with subsequent receptive and productive use of tenses in both groups, more strongly in the IEGI group [+M]
