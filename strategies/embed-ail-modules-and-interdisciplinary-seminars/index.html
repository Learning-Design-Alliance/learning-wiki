Embed AIL across disciplines by adding modules to existing Digital Literacy courses and offering interdisciplinary AI ethics and legal seminars¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that universities adopt adaptive curricula integrating AIL across multiple disciplines. Concretely, it suggests "embedding AIL modules into existing Digital Literacy courses and offering interdisciplinary AI ethics and legal seminars", using the Heptagon to decide which dimensions each discipline emphasizes. This targets the implementation gap where AIL is explicitly addressed in only a minority of programs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A shared framework (the Heptagon) to decide dimension emphasis per discipline

Constraints¶

  • Legal requirements are a moving target, so curricula must be adaptive

Target Learners¶

  • all higher education students, with domain-specific extensions for specialized programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • multidimensional AI literacy including critical engagement, responsible application, and legal/ethical knowledge

Affordances¶

  • Ai Literacy Heptagon Framework

Claims¶

  • Students identify navigating ethical and legal dimensions of GenAI as a third priority area for curriculum development [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Embed AI literacy across disciplines by identifying intersections with existing content and using case studies, interdisciplinary modules, and collaborative projects
  • Fund AI literacy initiatives integrating AI ethics into curricula and adapting assessments
  • Embed critical AI literacy into curricula and institutional practices in higher education
  • Integrate AI literacy education as an integral component of first-year curriculum development rather than an add-on
  • Breadcrumb strategy: embed small AI-native skill exercises, nudges, and prompts across multiple courses throughout the four-year curriculum
  • Embed subject-specific web credibility modules into existing courses rather than creating standalone courses
