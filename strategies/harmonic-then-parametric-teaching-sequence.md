# Teach the spring–mass experiment as a harmonic oscillator first, then develop it towards a parametric oscillator

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends a specific classroom sequencing for the spring–mass laboratory: begin with the standard harmonic-oscillator measurements (Hooke's law, ω²=k/m, damping time, sinusoidal solution) using heavy masses, and address the irregular parametric motion only at the end of the lab. As the authors put it, "The spring–mass experiment can be treated in class as a harmonic oscillator and then developed towards a parametric oscillator." This sequence lets students approach complex parametric content gradually.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Apparatus allowing both heavy masses (for stable harmonic oscillation) and lighter masses (for parametric behavior), plus time for a closing class discussion
#### Constraints
- The class apparatus planned for students uses only Sonar1, and students do not elaborate on the complex motion seen during detection; full multi-sonar analysis is reserved for the systematic study

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- harmonic oscillation physics
- parametric instability basics
- research-like investigation activities

### Affordances
- Three Wave Parametric Model Spring Mass
