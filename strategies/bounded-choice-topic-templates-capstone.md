# Bounded 'choose your own adventure' topic selection using ten generic topic templates

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Students select a research topic through a small set of objective questions and a choice among five subject options, then fill in one of ten generic fill-in-the-blank topic statements with subject matter and context. The author describes this as replacing "unlimited" choice with bounded choices, noting students perceive almost limitless possibilities while staff need only a finite knowledge base. Students may alternatively engage an academic staff member to work on a specific topic.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ten generic topic statements with blanks for subject matter and context
- A table mapping subject selection and philosophy outcome to a suggested research methodology
- Clear minimum standards documented for the course
#### Constraints
- Some students prefer to choose not to choose or want prescribed topics; the system permits defaulting the subject to 'thing'
- Growing postgraduate numbers reduced the number of unique topics available from other academic staff

### Target Learners
- undergraduate capstone students in aviation and other disciplines

### Target Learning Goals
- selecting a feasible research topic and methodology
- exercising autonomy in project choice
