# Develop EBM assessments with multiple highly correlated items per topic-level combination or focus on a single topic across all cognitive levels

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends, based on the SPLIT instrument's low reliability, that EBM knowledge tests either concentrate on a single specific EBM topic across all six cognitive levels with several highly correlated questions, or assess multiple topics only at a specific (e.g., high) cognitive level. The authors state "Possible solutions include focusing on a single, specific EBM topic across all six cognitive levels, where several highly correlated questions could be developed to increase precision." They also recommend using separate measures that continually progress in cognitive levels to track knowledge at different learning points.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A greater number of items per each specific domain and cognitive level for more precise assessment
#### Constraints
- Adding items makes the test longer and time-consuming, possibly reducing test-taker motivation; in this study 60% of the expert sample did not complete the test

### Target Learners
- Medical students across curriculum years
- EBM course designers

### Target Learning Goals
- Tracking progression of EBM knowledge across cognitive levels during the medical curriculum
