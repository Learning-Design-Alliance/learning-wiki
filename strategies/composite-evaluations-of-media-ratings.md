# Combine the evaluations of a number of persons into composite evaluations when rating instructional media, because individual raters disagree widely

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy addresses rater unreliability in media evaluation: because there was "widespread disagreement within each group in their ratings of individual items," the article recommends "composite evaluations combining the evaluations of a number of persons." It is an implementable procedure for any group-based instructional media evaluation, converting individually unstable item ratings into aggregated judgments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple raters per item whose ratings can be combined into a composite
#### Constraints
- Recommended in the context of the study's observed widespread within-group disagreement in ratings of individual items

### Target Learners

- high-school instructors
- nonmusic students

### Target Learning Goals
- reliable evaluation of educational music films
