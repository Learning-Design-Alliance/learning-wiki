# Maximize engagement by keeping videos brief, using conversational enthusiastic narration, and packaging videos for the specific course

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The essay recommends keeping each video brief (multiple videos per lesson, each ≤ 6 minutes), using conversational language that creates a sense of social partnership (e.g., "your" rather than "the"), speaking relatively quickly with enthusiasm (185–254 words per minute), and creating or packaging videos to emphasize relevance to the course. It notes videos made for a face-to-face session may feel less engaging online than videos created for that environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- In-video questions, chapters, and speed control give students control over pacing.
#### Constraints
- Reused videos may need contextualizing text outside the video to establish relevance for a particular class.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology students

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained attention and engagement with educational video
