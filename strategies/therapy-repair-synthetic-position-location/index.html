Diagnostic and repair strategy in constructivist therapy: locate where the synthetic position sits and return it to an ordinary voice¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

For constructivist and dialogical therapy, the article adds a diagnostic question — "where in the client's society of mind has the synthetic position come to sit, and has it been installed as the promoter?" Where it has, the aim is repair of location rather than abstinence: helping the client re-occupy the meta- and promoter positions, re-anchoring counter-positions in lived relationships, and returning the system to the rank of an ordinary voice that may be consulted and must be answered.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Repair requires re-anchoring counter-positions in real others who resist, together with the deliberate return of the integrative act to the self

Constraints¶

  • Proposition 6 predicts reversibility and disconfirms it if re-anchoring leaves suspension unchanged or if only reduced volume relieves it; the article notes its individual-authorship premise may not be culturally universal

Target Learners¶

  • Emerging adult clients in constructivist and dialogical therapy

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Restoration of self-authorship and dialogical movement

Affordances¶

  • Authorial Suspension Construct

Claims¶

  • Habitual self-formative dialogue with a synthetic position tends to absorb the integrative authorship of the meta- and promoter positions (synthetic promotion) [+W]

Related Strategies¶
