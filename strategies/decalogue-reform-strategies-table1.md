# Pair each anti-mediocrity critique with concrete reform steps: programmatic assessment, clinical reasoning tasks, relationship-centred care, and interprofessional collaboration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Table 1 converts each satirical commandment into a positive reform direction with practical steps, so that readers who 'insist on doing their best to exercise the exact opposite of everything written in this satirical decalogue' have an actionable map. For example, instead of focusing solely on scores, it says to foster mastery-oriented learning via programmatic assessment with meaningful feedback loops and portfolios to track longitudinal growth; instead of rote memorization, develop clinical reasoning through authentic challenges in clinical case discussions and problem-solving sessions; instead of disregarding communication, prioritize relationship-centred care by including patients as teachers and practising narrative medicine; instead of disdaining teamwork, foster interprofessional collaboration through shared learning spaces and team-based projects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment to shift from scores-oriented isolated study-for-the-test assessment toward systems-oriented programmatic assessment with feedback loops
#### Constraints
- The commentary notes evidence alone is insufficient to instigate change and that implementation of reforms has been surprisingly slow

### Target Learners
- health professions students (medicine, nursing, pharmacy, physiotherapy and beyond)
- residents
- health professions educators and institutional leaders

### Target Learning Goals
- deep understanding and clinical reasoning rather than rote memorization
- communication and empathy in patient care
- interprofessional collaboration and teamwork
- professional well-being and resilience
