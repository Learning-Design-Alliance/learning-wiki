# Implementation recommendations for asynchronous two-stage exams: onboarding, complex questions, 15-20% collaborative weighting, groups of four, and clear communication

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on implementation experience across courses at two institutions, the authors provide key recommendations in Table 1 for coordinating both exam stages and fostering productive collaboration. These include student onboarding on the rationale, complex conceptual questions where "a high score is obtainable with a group of 3 or 4 students," allocating 15-20% of the exam score to the collaborative stage, restricting group sizes to four with intentional assignment of ungrouped students, forming groups at least a week ahead, and clear LMS announcements, policies, and exam office hours with an upper time limit of about 3 hours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Student onboarding explaining the benefits and rationale of two-stage exams
- Complex, conceptual exam questions rather than factual recall
- 15-20% of the overall exam score allocated to the collaborative stage
- Group sizes restricted to 4 with intentional assignment of ungrouped students
- Groups formed at least one week before the exam with coordination emails
- LMS announcements of policies and deadlines, a policy for unreachable students, and exam office hours
#### Constraints
- The authors state instructors must manage academic integrity due to reduced direct supervision, remaining available for questions and designing exams that emphasize collective problem-solving over answer sharing

### Target Learners
- undergraduate STEM students
- online-only course students

### Target Learning Goals
- productive collaboration during assessment
- student participation and accountability
- formative feedback through group exams
