# Departments should stress student portfolio development and reflect on required learning-theory coursework given variable graduate skill sets

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their finding that programs provide foundational understanding to all students while allowing wide flexibility, the authors recommend that "departments might stress development of portfolios by which students may distinguish and clarify the training they have received." They also argue that the scarcity of required teaching/learning theory courses "may warrant departmental reflection" in a field that expects graduates to understand how to teach. The strategy is inter-departmental introspection: departments learning from each other's curricular trends.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Departments must offer flexible programs of study where students choose much of their coursework, as the surveyed programs do
#### Constraints
- The authors state there is not necessarily one proper way for departments to train students; rankings matter less than reflection about what the field values

### Target Learners
- graduate students in instructional design and technology programs

### Target Learning Goals
- clarifying and documenting the training graduates have received
- understanding how to teach through learning theory coursework
