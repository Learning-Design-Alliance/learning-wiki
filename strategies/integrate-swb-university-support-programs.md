# Integrate spiritual well-being into university student support and health promotion programs, targeting meaning, coping, and emotional resilience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that universities incorporate spiritual well-being into student support and health promotion efforts, because SWB was associated with healthier behaviors and lower depression and indirectly with academic performance. Interventions "may focus on enhancing meaning, coping skills, and emotional resilience rather than assuming direct behavioral change", alongside addressing academic pressure, living conditions, and economic challenges.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should target enhancing meaning, coping skills, and emotional resilience rather than assuming direct behavioral change
#### Constraints
- The authors state spiritual well-being alone cannot fully compensate for structural and socioeconomic disadvantages
- Cross-sectional design restricts causal conclusions
