# Increase transfusion medicine education from medical school onward, combined with electronic pop-up alerts on RBC orders

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's policy recommendation for reducing unnecessary RBC transfusions rests on its finding that physician knowledge is low. The authors state that "education in transfusion medicine must be increased, starting in medical school and continuing in designated educational programs, including periodic hospital-wide lectures and clinical scenarios discussed in group meetings". They additionally recommend an electronic "pop-up" alert with each RBC transfusion order containing relevant literature, modeled on the real-time clinical decision support used in the Stanford study, and suggest a computerized educational program focused on transfusion indications for Israeli medical staff.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designated educational programs including periodic hospital-wide lectures and clinical scenarios discussed in group meetings
- An electronic pop-up alert with each RBC transfusion order containing data regarding relevant literature
#### Constraints
- Israeli Ministry of Health regulations were most recently updated in 2002 and do not replace the lack of RBC transfusion guidelines in Israel

### Target Learners
- physicians in internal medicine and surgical departments
- medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of transfusion medicine physiology and indications
- familiarity with restrictive blood management policy
