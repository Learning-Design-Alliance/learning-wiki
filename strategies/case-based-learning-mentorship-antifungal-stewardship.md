# Targeted educational interventions including case-based learning and mentorship to bridge the intention-practice gap in antifungal stewardship

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends targeted educational interventions to bridge the intention-practice gap in antifungal stewardship among pharmacy students. These include curriculum enhancement with comprehensive modules on AFR mechanisms, interactive case-based learning with simulations from real-world case scenarios, mentorship programs, and structured feedback with follow-up assessments. The authors state these interventions aim to equip future pharmacists with the skills necessary for rational antifungal use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pharmacy curricula should be updated to include comprehensive modules on AFR mechanisms, global epidemiology, and evidence-based countermeasures
#### Constraints
- Interventions should be tailored to institutional and cultural contexts, recognizing that approaches effective in private-sector institutions may require modification for public-sector universities

### Target Learners
- undergraduate pharmacy students

### Target Learning Goals
- antifungal stewardship competency
- rational antifungal use
- clinical decision-making
