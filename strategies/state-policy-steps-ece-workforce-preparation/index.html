Six state policy steps to support early educator preparation, paired with adequate compensation and retention policies¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends states: build higher education capacity for a diverse workforce; support high-quality clinical practice such as "apprenticeships, lab schools, and paid practicum experiences"; develop clear 2-year to 4-year transfer pathways; fund specialized advising and academic supports; offer financial assistance for permits, degrees, and credentials; and set clear, high expectations through an early childhood-specific teaching credential like New Jersey's P-3. It stresses that "compensation increases must accompany increases in qualification requirements or taxpayers will be filling a leaky bucket."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • State funding and technical assistance, plus data collection on the ECE workforce to understand educator pathways, completion, barriers, and wages

Constraints¶

  • The report warns that raising qualification requirements without supports may harm workforce adequacy, stability, and diversity and cause educator shortages

Target Learners¶

  • Current and future early educators of children birth to age 5, particularly students of color and low-income students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Attainment of early childhood credentials and degrees and effective, developmentally grounded teaching practice

Related Strategies¶

  • Federal strategy to develop and support a well-qualified ECE workforce through compensation, career-ladder scholarships, preparation programs, and coaching
  • Four-part state policy agenda: coherent administration, universal affordability, a well-qualified workforce, and quality improvement for all programs
  • Build high-retention, supportive pathways into teaching for candidates of color
  • Six state policy recommendations to stabilize, support, and expand the early childhood workforce and build diverse TK pathways
