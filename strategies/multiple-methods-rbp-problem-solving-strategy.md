# Solve rigid-body problems by multiple methods and present complete derivations rather than energy-conservation shortcuts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend presenting complete derivations in introductory courses, including explanations based on the interaction between rod and disk (torque and contact force) and on basic physical laws, and solving problems by several methods. They write: "We recommend to ﬁnd the solution of the problems using diﬀerent methods such as the angular momentum/torque, work and energy theorem, energy conservation, etc." This helps students see relations among concepts, e.g. that non-conservative forces do not necessarily imply energy is not conserved.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must present the full derivation including the rod-disk interaction description, not just the final result
#### Constraints
- Direct energy-conservation equations, while easy to use, can hide information such as the existence of non-conservative forces performing translational and rotational work

### Target Learners
- novice students in introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the relation among different physical concepts such as work, energy conservation, torque and angular momentum

## Claims

- [Professors specify multiple solution methods and multiple categories per problem more often than Ph.D. students and introductory students, who often place two-principle problems in only one category](../claims/faculty-multiple-categories-two-principle-problems.md) [+W]
- [On Q16, 35% of upper-level students selected only one principle (conservation of momentum alone, 21%, or mechanical energy constancy alone, 14%) as needed to solve the ballistics-collision problem](../claims/emcs-q16-single-principle-selection-upper-level.md) [+W]
- [For the rolling-hoop problem pair, no significant difference between paired and single presentation, attributed to failure to use conservation of energy correctly](../claims/hoop-pair-no-difference-energy-errors.md) [+W]
