# Develop structured in vitro training curricula with objectively evaluated procedural metrics for endovascular skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend using the HANNES framework to build structured training scenarios covering coil embolization fundamentals, with cases selected to represent the spectrum of clinically encountered aneurysm morphologies and locations, accompanied by theoretical background on safe handling of catheters, y-adapters, flushes and wires. "Objective evaluation of procedural metrics similar to what is demonstrated here may then supplement training programs", with video analysis for detailed evaluation of each procedure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulator with customizable patient-specific vascular models; fluoroscopy recording and video analysis capacity; tasks matched to trainees' skill level.
#### Constraints
- The current experiments were not designed as a curriculum to teach the basics of coil embolization; in vitro training requires fluoroscopic control, limiting flexibility.

### Target Learners
- beginning neurointerventionalists
- advanced physicians maintaining skills

### Target Learning Goals
- safe handling of catheters, y-adapters, flushes and wires
- selection and navigation of catheters along the vascular tree
- selecting, sizing and delivering neurovascular implants
