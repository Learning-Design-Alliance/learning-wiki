# Implement interprofessional simulation trainings and internships as longitudinal programs within the curricula of all involved health professions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation from their Lessons Learned section: because practice is important for realizing change, "interprofessional simulation trainings and internships should be implemented as longitudinal programs in the respective curricula of all involved health professions." In their own pilot, longitudinal integration succeeded for nursing and paramedic trainees, who attended both modules as part of their class schedules, but not for medical students, whose attendance was voluntary and non-recurring. The authors also note that planning such training requires considerable time, coordination, and resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time slots integrated into the curricula of the participating professions
- Interprofessional instructor planning team
- Considerable time, coordination, and resources for planning
#### Constraints
- The article notes lack of time, lack of practical training, and unsupportive colleagues and teachers in the workplace can prevent students from change

### Target Learners
- emergency nursing trainees
- student paramedics
- medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained interprofessional teamwork and communication skills
- transfer of learning into workplace practice
