# Responsible Decision-Making Practice

## Description
Learners engage in problem-solving scenarios in pairs or small groups, connecting to their everyday lives (e.g., cheating, bullying), to make decisions, analyze situations, and consider possible consequences. They then share their decisions with the whole class and reflect on how they arrived at them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Relevant problem scenarios, facilitation to guide discussions, and time for group work and class sharing.
#### Constraints
- Scenarios must be carefully selected to be relevant and inclusive. Facilitation is needed to ensure respectful and productive discussions.

### Target Learners

- K-12 learners and adult learners in social skills development programs.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Apply responsible decision-making skills in real-life scenarios, enhance collaborative problem-solving, and promote self-reflection.

### Affordances
- Provides practical application of decision-making skills, promotes peer learning and collaboration, and encourages reflection on reasoning.

### Personalization
- Scenarios can be tailored to match the age, interests, and experiences of learners. Support and scaffolding can be provided to learners who struggle with analysis or reflection.

## Impact
- Engaging in real-world problem scenarios improves decision-making skills and fosters ethical reasoning. Participative decision-making can help students develop skills for responsible citizenship (Ross & Bondy, 1993).
