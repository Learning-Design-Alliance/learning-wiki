# Use brief five-minute mini-simulation videos with small-group empathy mapping to reduce large-group simulation anxiety

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy replaces one large-class simulation with repeated short scenario-based activities: "This study implemented mini-simulations as concise, five-minute scenario-based learning activities focusing on clinical situations requiring empathic communication". Students watch videos of nurse-patient interactions, analyze them with empathy maps in small groups of 4–5, and practice expression through discussion and simulated question-answering. The small-group format is intended, following Bandura's social cognitive theory, to reduce anxiety before a large audience and let every student participate.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenario videos calibrated by a multidisciplinary expert panel, structured empathy map worksheets, and experienced instructors providing feedback
#### Constraints
- Tested over one semester with second-year nursing students; the article notes limited sample size and single-institution scope

### Target Learners
- novice nursing students lacking patient-communication experience

### Target Learning Goals
- empathic communication
- confidence in communicating with patients
