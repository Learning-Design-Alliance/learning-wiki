# Target recovery resources to low-performing students via tutoring and high-quality enrichment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Given estimated global learning losses, the authors recommend that "it would make sense to target resources to low performing students with appropriate and high-quality enrichment opportunities". They note online tutoring reduced losses significantly in several randomized controlled trials and that high-dosage one-to-one tutoring was effective before the pandemic, while cautioning that online variants need more research and that much higher absenteeism makes reaching disadvantaged students challenging.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutoring and enrichment programs must be high-quality and appropriately targeted to low-performing students
#### Constraints
- The evidence base for online tutoring is limited and more research is needed; low-tech parent interventions were tested only in a few low- and middle-income environments and effects are limited when not implemented well; high absenteeism makes recovery efforts challenging

### Target Learners
- low-performing students, especially from disadvantaged backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- recovering mathematics, reading, and science achievement lost during COVID-19 school closures
