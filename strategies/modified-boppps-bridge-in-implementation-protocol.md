# Standardized experiential Bridge-in protocol: 45-minute ward visit plus thoracoscopy simulation within a fixed total teaching time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A reproducible recipe for the experiential Bridge-in: a 45-minute ward visit (5-minute patient background briefing, 15-minute bedside observation, 15-minute instructor Q&A, 10-minute intra-group exchange) guided by a unified 8-item observation checklist, plus thoracoscopy simulation in which each student trains 15 minutes across three stations (instrument holding, tissue manipulation, basic suturing), with the system automatically tracking "operation time, instrument movement path, and tissue damage rate". A pre-test (n = 20) showed 45 minutes per practical activity was the minimum duration to ensure students could observe key clinical details and master basic thoracoscopic instrument manipulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The same senior thoracic surgeon teaches both arms after a 2-hour standardized training session with signed compliance records
- Written informed consent, de-identification, confidentiality agreements, and strict behavioral norms for ward visits
- Third-party quality-control monitoring of randomly attended sessions with a required compliance rate ≥ 95%
#### Constraints
- Requires a Grade A tertiary thoracic surgery ward with high annual lung tumour case volume and simulation equipment (standard 5-mm thoracoscopic instruments, a 3D-printed thoracic cavity module, a calibrated high-definition display)

### Target Learners
- fifth-year clinical medicine students in thoracic surgery coursework

### Target Learning Goals
- embodied thoracoscopic instrument manipulation
- linking observed clinical phenomena to theoretical knowledge
