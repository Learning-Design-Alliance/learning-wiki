# Match CME training content and format to the demands of each target audience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that E-learning effectiveness in continuing medical education varies across training formats, organizational environment, and target audience, and explains the differing outcomes by supply–demand matching: training supply and demand for clinical physicians and primary care personnel were well matched, while content for public health physicians was not consistent with what they demanded. The implied strategy is to align training content, timing, and location with health workers' needs before deploying E-learning CME.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An appropriate CME training format that could increase access to high-quality training resources, considering health workers' needs in terms of content, timing, and location
#### Constraints
- High quality content is the key facilitator of the behaviour change, while learning content difficulty, relevancy, and hardware constraints are key barriers

### Target Learners
- TB health workers in LMICs
- primary care workers
- clinical physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- continuing medical education knowledge updating
- application of learning to daily practice
