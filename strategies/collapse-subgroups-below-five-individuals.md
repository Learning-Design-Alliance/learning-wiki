# Protect anonymity in EDI data collection by not reporting or collapsing subgroups with fewer than 5 individuals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A data-reporting practice for EDI surveys and progress tracking: when a respondent subgroup is very small, do not report it separately, to protect anonymity. The article recommends "following the guidelines of the National Science and Engineering Research Council (NSERC) and either not report any instances where there are fewer than 5 individuals in a group, or collapse them into a shared, larger group". This responds to the risk that identification data could harm groups such as Indigenous and First Nations peoples in Canada.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular data collection to track the efficacy of EDI initiatives, with great care taken to protect the anonymity of respondents, particularly for subgroups with the lowest numbers
#### Constraints
- Data collection can be problematic for groups whose identification was historically used by the government to cause them harm, such as Indigenous and First Nations peoples in Canada

### Target Learners
- physics students and professionals from underrepresented groups in Canada

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate, ethical tracking of EDI progress in physics education and careers
