# Incorporate a tremor-measuring smartwatch app to support self-supervised training of surgical residents

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that tremor measurement could be delivered as a smartwatch application giving residents "real-time feedback on hand steadiness", enabling them to monitor progress and refine skills over time without peer comparison. The strategy couples self-supervised learning — where residents set their own pace and focus on individual strengths and weaknesses — with data-driven tremor metrics from the wearable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a tremor-measuring app within a smartwatch providing real-time feedback on hand steadiness
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that some residents might resist monitoring, particularly if compared to peers, due to concerns about privacy and individual autonomy

### Target Learners
- surgical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- self-monitored improvement of hand steadiness and microsurgical dexterity
