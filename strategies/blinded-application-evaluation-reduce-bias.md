# Blind identifying and demographic information in application evaluation to reduce implicit bias

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
In its initial review process, IWA experimented with blinding name, nationality, gender, and age from evaluators with the goal of reducing implicit bias. Some evaluators reported they felt they were not getting the full picture of applicants without those details, and the organizers are "exploring ways to ﬁll in those gaps with out exposing ourselves to pitfalls of internally-held stereotypes." The strategy sits among other inclusivity-oriented application-process changes cited from the literature.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reviewers must be able to evaluate applications on motivation and merit without seeing name, nationality, gender, and age
#### Constraints
- It is difficult to assess explicitly whether the blinding achieved the bias-reduction goal, and some evaluators reported feeling they lacked the full picture of applicants

### Target Learners
- outreach program applicants aged 16-24

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable access to informal science learning opportunities
