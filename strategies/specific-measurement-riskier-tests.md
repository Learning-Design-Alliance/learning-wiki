# Improve measurement specificity in longitudinal studies to enable coherent pattern matching of skill gains to later knowledge states

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that future assessments use more cognitively complex and diagnostic measures rather than single scores, since children assigned the same score may have very different knowledge states — for example, intervention children versus controls who reached the same score through far more extensive experience. Specific measures let researchers relate gains in specific knowledge states to subsequent knowledge states and predict where effects should appear, how big they should be, and when they should vanish, an approach Shadish, Cook, and Campbell call coherent pattern matching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Precision and completeness in measurement and theory
- Formulating the riskiest tests possible
#### Constraints
- Better measurement in one domain often costs sample size or worse measurement elsewhere; some theories are open-ended enough that wide ranges of effects seem plausible after the fact; early cognitive individual differences may be non-differentiated

### Target Learners
- children in longitudinal studies of cognitive development

### Target Learning Goals
- early academic skills and knowledge states
