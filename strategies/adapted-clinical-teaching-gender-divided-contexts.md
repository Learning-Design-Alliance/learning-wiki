# Adapt clinical teaching in conservative, gender-divided contexts using simulation, models and standardised patients to build intimate examination skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that in contexts with prominent gender divisions, clinical teaching must adapt rather than simply adopt Western curricula, since students "are left deficient in exposure and skills". It proposes teaching intimate examinations "through lectures, videos, models, simulation or standardised patients", citing reviews that patient involvement and simulation improve skills and reduce anxiety, while noting recruiting simulated patients is highly challenging in their setting.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness among faculty of gender's influence on clinical skill attainment
- Research on gender influences in conservative cultures to guide adaptation
#### Constraints
- Recruiting simulated patients is highly challenging, if not impossible, in the authors' setting; simulation for cardiovascular examination does not necessarily improve practical clinical skills, and interaction with a human is more likely to translate into bedside skills

### Target Learners
- final-year undergraduate medical students in conservative, gender-divided contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical examination skills including intimate examinations
