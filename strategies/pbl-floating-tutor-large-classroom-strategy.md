# Adopt PBL variants with one floating tutor in large classrooms where instructor shortages or implementation time constrain pure PBL

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that where PPBL implementation is time-consuming and tutor-intensive, educators adopt PPBL or HPBL with a single floating tutor serving a large classroom. This follows from the study's demonstration that both PBL variants, run with one facilitator in classes of 30–35 students, outperformed lecturing on performance, mental effort, and instructional efficiency. The authors state that "the HPBL and PPBL with one floating tutor in a large classroom may be adopted to overcome these concerns."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator able to serve a floating tutoring role across small groups in a large class
- Structured lesson plans, scenarios, and Patient Information Sheets prepared in advance
#### Constraints
- The article limits generalization to courses of similar content and level
- The intervention lasted only 8 weeks on four organ-dysfunction topics

### Target Learners
- undergraduate junior nursing students in large classes

### Target Learning Goals
- problem solving and higher-order cognitive skills in pediatric nursing
- efficient learning with reduced mental effort
