Design sustainable, future-oriented feedback and structured community-partner collaboration in authentic assessment¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Drawing on the review's gaps, the authors recommend that educators design feedback that emphasises the learning process and self-regulation, fostering students' evaluative judgement and application of knowledge over time, and provide 'more structured opportunities for students to engage in collaborative projects with workplace or community partners'. Such partnerships advance a broader mission of societal transformation and help students define their societal roles and gain recognition for their contributions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Feedback practices must extend beyond grading to offer actionable insights applicable to future contexts

Constraints¶

  • Only five of the reviewed studies currently connected students with industry partners and community stakeholders, so implementation examples are scarce

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate and postgraduate students in higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • evaluative judgement, self-regulation, societal role definition and employability

Affordances¶

  • Learning Oriented Collaborative Aa Design Framework

Claims¶

  • Formative feedback dominates authentic assessment designs while sustainable feedback is rare [+W]
  • Social collaboration in authentic assessment is mostly peer- and teacher-based, with few connections to industry or community partners and rare equitable teacher-student collaboration [+W]
