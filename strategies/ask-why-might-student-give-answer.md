# Ask teachers why a student might give an answer rather than what is wrong with it, to focus attention on the student's construction of ideas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the professional development session on potential energy, the facilitator asked "why might a student give answer A?" so that teachers would attend to the student's construction of ideas and discuss the good ideas students had even when answering incorrectly. The author argues that asking what is wrong with an answer would lead to a discussion of correct ideas instead, which does not help teachers listen to their students; focusing on what students can do and working with that is more consistent with teachers' classroom activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator who frames prompts around student construction rather than correctness, keeping responsibility to participating teachers foremost
#### Constraints
- The strategy emerged in a professional development context with in-service middle school teachers and was not designed as a research intervention in advance

### Target Learners
- in-service middle school teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- responsive teaching
- noticing productive ideas in incorrect student answers
