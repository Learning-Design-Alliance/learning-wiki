# Deploy LLM-powered ACLS simulations as a structured, scalable adjunct to traditional resuscitation leadership training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes LLM-based simulation as a complement to mannequin- and instructor-based resuscitation training, offering remote access, adjustable team sizes, and standardized AI-driven assessment with real-time feedback. It concludes that "While they are not yet a replacement for traditional methods, they provide a structured and scalable approach to developing and assessing team leadership in resuscitation scenarios." The strategy responds to traditional training's need for trained instructors, physical spaces, and co-presence of participants and instructors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Guideline-bound agent outputs and standardized checklist-based scoring so that AI-driven assessment remains objective
#### Constraints
- Further research with larger participant samples is necessary to evaluate long-term educational impact and scalability
- The pilot tested the platform with only one ACLS-certified user, limiting generalizability

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine physicians and trainees in ACLS team leadership roles

### Target Learning Goals
- Development and assessment of resuscitation team leadership and ACLS algorithm adherence
