# Journal Reflections

## Description
Students think back on their experiences and outcomes to gain a greater understanding of themselves. Students think and write about their experiences throughout a day or class period and look for patterns, as well as create and manage goals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Journals or notebooks, prompts for reflection, and class time for writing and discussion.
#### Constraints
- Some students may be resistant to journaling or struggle with self-reflection. It's important to create a safe and supportive environment and provide guidance and support.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Middle and high school students, as well as adult learners, who want to develop self-awareness and improve their ability to manage their emotions and behaviors.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Develop self-awareness, identify patterns in behavior, improve goal-setting skills

### Affordances
- Promotes metacognition by encouraging students to reflect on their experiences and identify patterns in their behavior. Supports goal-setting and self-management skills.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted by providing different prompts and structures for reflection, such as focusing on specific events or emotions. Students can also choose different formats for recording their reflections, such as writing, drawing, or audio recording.

## Assessment Evidence
- Review students' journal entries for evidence of self-awareness, reflection, and goal-setting. Track their progress toward meeting their goals.

## Impact
- Journaling has been shown to improve self-awareness, reduce stress, and promote emotional well-being. It can also help students develop self-management skills and improve their academic performance.
