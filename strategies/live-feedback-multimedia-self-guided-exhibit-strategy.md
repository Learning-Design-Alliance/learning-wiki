# Use a combination of live instrument feedback, short multimedia content, and physical interaction buttons to support self-guided learning at a museum exhibit.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes combining a live video feed of the interference pattern with real-time graphing of the photodiode signal, short videos (under 90 s), brief text pages (under 80 words), and a short quiz to enable "varying levels of self-guided interaction within a museum setting." The strategy is designed so that "a short engagement allows a little information about gravitational waves to be gained; however, much more detailed information is available if a visitor chooses a prolonged interaction." Content was developed in collaboration with museum staff to ensure accessible language, and design choices included colour-blind-friendly palettes, sans-serif fonts, subtitled video, and directional speakers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content must be self-explanatory without an expert present
- Information must be quick and easy to access whilst also providing depth and variety for longer interactions
#### Constraints
- Museum visitors often spend limited time at any particular exhibit, so content must work at multiple engagement depths

### Target Learners
- museum visitors of all ages including children
- self-guided learners without science backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- self-directed learning about gravitational-wave science
- building understanding of detector technology through multiple media formats
