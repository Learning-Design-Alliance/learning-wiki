# Human-Centered Design co-design process for developing a departmental-culture survey

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends grounding survey development in collaborative design with stakeholders rather than expert-driven methods alone. The authors "used a Human-Centered Design (HCD) approach," which "emphasizes the creation of tools based on a deep understanding of user needs." They ran four two-hour virtual co-design sessions over one month with 15 stakeholders including initiative leaders, department chairs, committee members, faculty, and student representatives, who collectively identified critical indicators of departmental culture. Findings were synthesized with literature review to define constructs and draft items.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to stakeholders holding multiple roles relevant to the change initiatives; sessions with shared documents for real-time note recording
#### Constraints
- The co-design process details are described in a separate publication; the article reports the sessions as a design activity, not an evaluated intervention

### Target Learners
- Physics faculty and staff; developers of departmental assessment instruments

### Target Learning Goals
- Creating survey items that capture culturally relevant indicators of systemic change and equity in departments
