# Deliver PAL as short (about 10-minute) moderate-intensity movement bursts embedded in normal lessons, using simple, randomly presented movements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's PAL sessions were about 10 minutes within a 20-minute maths lesson, targeting 3–6 METs with movements such as "on the spot stepping, marching, jogging, jumping, arm pulsing". Movements were deliberately "not presented as a routine" so children had to keep shifting focus between learning and movement, and the teacher delivering PAL was already familiar with the approach, which the article cites as a prerequisite of successful delivery.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A class teacher familiar with PAL as part of normal daily practice
- Movements simple, uncomplicated and easily replicable, at moderate-to-vigorous intensity
#### Constraints
- The small single-class sample and narrow age range may limit generalisation; the authors note PAL replication may have limitations in other schools

### Target Learners
- primary school children (mixed age 9–10 class)

### Target Learning Goals
- multiplication tables recall and applied recall
- attention and self-regulation during lessons
