# Schedule guided self-study in low-workload periods and integrate it into the curriculum rather than adding it on top

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the feasibility findings, the authors recommend that future G-SS implementations be timetabled where student workload is normal, since scheduling in high-workload periods was the main reason attendance fell to 23% against an 83% target. They state: "G-SS study should be scheduled in time periods where workload is in normal range (< 40 h per week)" and that the intervention "should not be planned on top of an existing curriculum, but preferably be integrated as a part of the curriculum", with learning workload evenly distributed across the module. They further propose making tutor meetings mandatory in the first three cycles and voluntary in the last three.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Academic calendar planning that leaves adequate preparation time for clinical cases
- Integration as a curriculum component rather than an added workload
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a single feasibility study whose exam-score results are preliminary; the authors state the study design was not appropriate to definitively assess knowledge changes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physiotherapy students in fulltime degree programs

### Target Learning Goals
- self-directed learning of practical clinical skills
- changes in knowledge and skills
