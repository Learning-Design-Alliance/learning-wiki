# Give frequent, purposeful practice writing integrated into class time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because the first hurdle for beginning writers is not writing at all, the manual directs GED teachers to give frequent writing opportunities that are purposeful, expected as a regular part of class time, and understood as necessary. Suggested forms include freewriting, note-taking, questioning, outlining, summarizing, comparing/contrasting, practice essays, journal writing, and reading journals. Practice essays should avoid the essay-a-week trap, with ample time to revise and timed essays near test readiness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Practice must be purposeful and a regular, expected part of class time
#### Constraints
- Requiring too many individual papers emphasizes product over process

### Target Learners
- Adult GED preparation students

### Target Learning Goals
- Build writing fluency, organizing skills, and test readiness for the 45-minute GED essay
