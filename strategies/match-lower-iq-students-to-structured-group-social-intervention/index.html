Match students with lower-average cognitive and verbal abilities to structured, group-based social interventions like SCI-A¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that educators measure students' cognitive and behavioral profiles and use them to match students to intervention, because SCI-A was effective specifically in the lower average IQ range. They write that "it appears as though SCI-A is particularly appropriate for those students with cognitive abilities in the lower average range" and that "a group-based intervention like SCI-A may be a more effective choice" than individually based or less structured services. The strategy is to screen for FSIQ, VIQ, and VIQ–NVIQ discrepancy and assign students with lower-average profiles to structured group-based social programming.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators need access to quality information to assess students' cognitive and behavioral profiles and understand how these profiles are associated with intervention response
  • Reliable screening tools whose scores predict treatment response

Constraints¶

  • SCI-A was not consistently effective across the range of IQ; additional inquiry relative to group-based programming for students with higher abilities remains warranted
  • Conclusions apply at the cluster (group) level rather than individual student level due to the CRT design

Target Learners¶

  • Early adolescents with social competence deficits and cognitive abilities in the lower average range (e.g., FSIQ or VIQ about 1 SD below the mean, or VIQ at least 11 points below NVIQ)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Improved social cognition, communication, and motivation to be social

Claims¶

  • FSIQ significantly moderated SCI-A response only on communication, where lower-FSIQ students did better in SCI-A than in BAU and higher-FSIQ students did worse [+W]
  • VIQ moderated SCI-A response: lower-VIQ students benefited on communication, while higher-VIQ students showed worse outcomes on social cognition, communication, and motivation [+W]
  • The VIQ–NVIQ discrepancy moderated SCI-A response: students with VIQ at least 11 points below NVIQ showed large positive effects, while those with VIQ above NVIQ showed large negative effects [+W]
  • SCI-A and BAU showed no significant differences on most SRS-2 subscales at postintervention, with BAU significantly better on Social Cognition (d = 0.31) [~W]
