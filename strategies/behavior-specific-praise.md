# Behavior Specific Praise

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Behavior specific praise (BSP) is a brief, positive verbal statement delivered immediately after a learner exhibits a target behavior, explicitly naming what the learner did ("You showed your work on every step — that made your error easy to find"). Unlike general praise ("good job"), BSP identifies the behavior precisely, functioning as contingent positive reinforcement within a [Behaviorism](../principles/behaviorism.md) framework while also giving learners informational feedback about which actions are valued.

## Design Implications

BSP works through two mechanisms: it increases the future frequency of the praised behavior via reinforcement, and it clarifies expectations by labeling exemplars of desired performance [~S]. Effectiveness depends on contingency and specificity — praise delivered indiscriminately or without naming the behavior loses both reinforcing and informational value. Because BSP communicates *what* was done well, it aligns with evidence that [feedback is most effective at the task and process levels](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S] rather than at the level of the self ("you're so smart"), which can undermine motivation.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear operational definition of the target behavior so praise is contingent and consistent
- Immediate delivery (within seconds of the behavior) with an explicit behavior label
- A ratio of positive to corrective interactions of roughly 4:1 or higher in classroom settings
- Genuine, developmentally appropriate tone — learners detect and discount hollow praise

#### Constraints
- Praise directed at the person rather than the behavior ("you're brilliant") can promote fixed self-views and reduce persistence after failure [-M]
- Overuse or non-contingent praise loses reinforcing power and can feel manipulative, especially with adolescents [-M]
- Praise for easy, already-mastered tasks can signal low expectations and lower engagement for high-achieving learners [~M]
- Public praise can backfire for learners sensitive to social attention; quiet, private delivery preserves the function [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Group-level BSP** ("I see three tables with materials ready") for efficiency in whole-class settings
- **Nonverbal BSP** (thumbs up, proximity, written margin comments) for learners who find verbal attention aversive
- **Token-linked BSP** where verbal praise accompanies a point or token in a token economy
- **Peer-mediated BSP** where learners are taught to deliver specific praise to classmates

### Target Learners
- Young learners and novices who need explicit labeling of desired behaviors [~S]
- Learners with behavioral or attentional challenges, for whom BSP is a core component of positive behavioral interventions and supports (PBIS) [+S]
- Adolescents and adults, for whom BSP must be delivered privately and framed as informational rather than evaluative [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Behavioral and procedural goals: classroom routines, participation norms, study habits
- Self-regulation: naming strategies ("you checked your answer before moving on") builds awareness of [self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) moves
- Classroom climate: establishing psychological safety and predictable expectations

### Instructions
1. Define 2–3 target behaviors in observable terms (e.g., "begins the warm-up within one minute").
2. Scan actively for occurrences; deliver praise within seconds, naming the behavior and its effect ("You re-read the question before answering — that's how you catch misreads").
3. Pair praise with [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) or a brief [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) when a learner needs to understand *why* the behavior matters, not just that it was noticed.
4. Fade frequency as the behavior becomes habitual, shifting from teacher-delivered praise to learner self-monitoring.
5. Maintain the positive-to-corrective ratio; if corrective statements rise, re-teach the expectation rather than increasing reprimands.

## Related Strategies
- **Token economies** — BSP often serves as the verbal accompaniment to token delivery; the praise carries the informational content while the token carries the exchangeable value
- **Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS)** — a school-wide framework in which BSP is the primary universal-tier practice
- **Praise-to-reprimand ratio monitoring** — coaching teachers to sustain ~4:1 ratios through observation and feedback

## Examples
- **PBIS (Center on PBIS, University of Oregon)** — universal expectation-teaching with high rates of specific praise across school settings ([pbis.org](https://www.pbis.org))
- **Early literacy small groups** — a teacher praises decoding attempts specifically ("You broke that word into chunks") rather than saying "good reading," making the successful strategy visible to the whole group
- **Online discussion moderation** — an instructor replies to a forum post naming the move ("Your counterexample directly tests the claim"), reinforcing argumentation behaviors in an asynchronous course
