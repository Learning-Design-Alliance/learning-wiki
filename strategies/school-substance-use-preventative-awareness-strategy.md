# Schools should tailor preventative initiatives with awareness of adolescent substance use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that secondary schools attend to substance use when designing preventative initiatives, because in this adolescent sample substance use was the variable most clearly tied to academic outcomes. The authors state that since this "variable was the only significant predictor of academic achievement, high schools should be especially aware of the impact of substance use when tailoring preventative initiatives." Substance use experimentation often emerges in adolescence, making this age range the relevant target for such initiatives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of substance use frequency among students, as measured in the study by self-reported times of experimentation in the past month
#### Constraints
- The supporting association is observational and correlational; the study design does not establish that reducing substance use would improve grades

### Target Learners
- adolescent high school students aged 13-18

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement (Math grades)
