# Use a flashlight, three linear polarizers and a translucent screen to let students experimentally swap the order of two polarizers and observe that the order of factors alters the product

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A concrete classroom activity in which a fixed vertical polarizer generates vertically polarized light, and a diagonal (D+) and a horizontal (H) polarizer are swapped relative to one another. The authors describe that "it is only necessary to move one of the polarizers, which is placed in front or behind the one that remains stationary", making the order-of-factors question directly observable. The activity requires only a flashlight, three linear polarizers, and a translucent screen.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A flashlight, three linear polarizers (purchasable as pieces of glass or mica), and a translucent screen
#### Constraints
- The activity addresses only linear polarization; the authors omit circular polarization for simplicity

### Target Learners
- high school students
- college students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding noncommutativity of matrix multiplication
- using matrices to model physical phenomena
