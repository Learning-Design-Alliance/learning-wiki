# Offer an interprofessional simulation-based course as an elective option in undergraduate operating room nursing education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the trial's positive attitudinal and knowledge findings, the authors recommend that the integrated course with interprofessional education and simulation "may be a valuable elective option for undergraduate nursing students in operating room nursing education", and suggest the IPSE designated for pre-health professional students may be applied broadly to enhance team-based training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty team from nursing and medical schools to develop and review scenarios
- Simulated operating room with animal-model surgical protocols approved by an animal committee
#### Constraints
- Synchronizing sessions for small groups of students in different professions is not easily achieved; the challenge increases exponentially as modules and professional groups grow
- Longer-term follow-up may be required to evaluate whether short-term benefits translate into workplace improvements

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students
- Pre-qualification health professional students

### Target Learning Goals
- Operating room nursing knowledge
- Team-based competencies
- Interprofessional collaboration

## Claims

- [Nursing students in the IPSE group scored significantly higher on an operating room nursing knowledge questionnaire than students in the traditional course group](../claims/ipse-higher-or-nursing-knowledge-scores.md) [+M]
- [Nursing students in an interprofessional simulation-based education program had more positive self-reported attitudes toward interprofessional learning on four of nineteen RIPLS items from pre-test to post-test within the IPSE group](../claims/ipse-improved-ripls-attitudes-four-items.md) [+W]
- [Qualitative analysis of open-ended responses identified four themes in nursing students' perceptions of the IPSE experience: communication, role awareness, a better way of learning, and future IPSE](../claims/ipse-open-ended-four-themes.md) [+W]
