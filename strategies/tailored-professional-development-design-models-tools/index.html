Provide university teachers with specially tailored professional development in systematic design, including design models and tools¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (3 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The commentary highlights Bennett et al.'s identification of "key areas where university educators require support and professional development, to engage in systematic design of learning, in particular the use of design models and tools". It notes the lack of bespoke resources and tools for teachers' design work, and that while generic design training may be useful, "teachers may need specially tailored approaches that are both relevant and responsive to the particular exigencies and requirements they face". This is framed as especially important given the pandemic-driven shift to online education.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Bespoke resources and tools for teachers' design work; professional development tailored to teachers' particular exigencies and requirements

Constraints¶

  • Generic design training alone may be insufficient; the reviewed evidence base is limited to the Australian university sector

Target Learners¶

  • university teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • educational design and redesign of units of learning

Claims¶

  • Research on university teachers as educational designers is an emerging field, and the professional development of educational designers is neither well understood nor effectively supported [+W]
  • Bennett et al.'s qualitative study found university instructors' design process is top-down, breadth-first, iterative, responsive, and reflective [+W]
  • Bennett et al.'s study establishes faculty roles as designers of learning, an essential consideration when institutions seek digital design talent. [+W]
  • Bennett et al.'s study engaged a diverse cross-section of university teachers across disciplines and modalities, yet reported common shared design practices [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Cultivate faculty design talent through professional development and strategic faculty hiring in responses to large-scale or rapid shifts to digital learning.
  • Make university teachers' design work more explicit through conceptual tools and design models to navigate top-down and bottom-up design tensions
  • Pursue explanatory then prescriptive research on the rationale behind teachers' design decisions to strengthen design implications
