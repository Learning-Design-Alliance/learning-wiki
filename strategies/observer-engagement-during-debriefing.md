# Engage observers as active learners throughout the post-simulation debrief, seeking their input before the performing participant

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article holds that observer learning "pivots on the effectiveness of the debrief, where the debriefer must ensure constant engagement of the observer alongside the active participant". In the trial, debriefs used plus/delta followed by the Advocacy/Inquiry framework; observers were asked for input on strengths before the performing participant, then discussed what they would do differently, and each debrief closed with take-home lessons from each participant.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Debriefers trained in a debriefing framework; the study's facilitators were institutionally trained in plus/delta and Advocacy/Inquiry
- Observers treated as active learners and engaged at all points of the debrief conversation
#### Constraints
- Observer learning depends on debrief effectiveness, so a weak debrief undermines the strategy

### Target Learners
- Observers and active participants in interprofessional simulation sessions

### Target Learning Goals
- Reflection on counselling performance, dissemination of observations the active participant may not have noticed, and transfer of take-home lessons to practice
