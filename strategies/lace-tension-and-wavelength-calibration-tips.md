# Apply unequal tensions to equalize lace wavelengths and measure D in both meters and wavelengths for accurate results

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors give practical measurement tips for the exercise. The wavelength can be found most accurately by measuring the distance D both in meters and in wavelengths and taking the ratio of those two numbers. Because the two trial laces' wavelengths differed by about 1%, they "hung 100 g on one lace and 200 g on the other" to bring the wavelengths into agreement before collecting the Table I data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Weights (100 g and 200 g) to tension the laces
- Measuring D in both meters and wavelengths
#### Constraints
- Without the tension correction, measurements of y differed by several centimeters from the calculated positions of the maxima

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate measurement of pathlengths and maxima positions in the model exercise
