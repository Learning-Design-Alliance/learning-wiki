# Nine classroom strategies for reducing working-memory load during instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article reproduces Gathercole and Alloway's (2007) list of nine teacher strategies for learners with WM limitations: monitoring the student, reducing memory load by breaking tasks into smaller steps, repeating and reviewing, advance organizers such as KWL charts, memory aids like handouts and posters, pausing to paraphrase and summarize, color coding, key words in listening tasks, and reinforcing learning preferences with software. The authors adopted these because they fit regular language lessons without major adjustments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers estimate the WM demands of tasks and look for warning signs of memory overload
#### Constraints
- The article frames these for learners displaying signals of WM problems

### Target Learners
- learners with indicators of limited working memory capacity
- undergraduate EFL students

### Target Learning Goals
- processing and retaining instructional content
- vocabulary retention in a foreign language
