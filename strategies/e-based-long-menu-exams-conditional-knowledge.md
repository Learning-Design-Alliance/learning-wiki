# Use problem-solving free-text tasks to test competency-based learning objectives, with e-based long-menu examinations to make evaluation feasible

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating problem-solving tasks into examinations because the dyadic-learning effect was clearest on conditional-knowledge problem-solving questions. Since objective evaluation of free-text answers is not scalable with limited teachers, the authors suggest it "would be more realistic to develop e-based examinations with long menu options that could be modified and further adapted to allow the possibility of providing free text answers".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An objective, scalable evaluation mechanism for student answers is needed; handwritten free text would require character recognition and semantic extraction systems needing large training corpora.
#### Constraints
- Computer-based evaluation of handwritten answers is problematic; deep-learning semantic extraction requires large corpora of training data.

### Target Learners
- medical students in large university cohorts

### Target Learning Goals
- conditional knowledge
- problem-solving competency assessment
