# Implement a 2-hour guided extracurricular outdoor physical activity program after school on school days

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's intervention is a structured 2-hour (4-6 pm) after-school physical activity program on school days, "guided and monitored by their head teachers and physical education teachers", with recommended activities including basketball, soccer, badminton, and table tennis. Implementation was supported by a 1-day intensive teacher training session and daily diary-form recording; activities moved to semiopen or indoor spaces in bad weather. The trial showed this program was noninferior for mathematics scores and improved physical fitness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School sports facilities sufficient for 2 or more classes to play sports at the same time
- Teacher training and daily diary-form recording of implementation status
- Head teachers and physical education teachers to guide and monitor sessions
#### Constraints
- Sessions may need to move indoors or to semiopen areas during inclement weather
- The trial was conducted in grades 3-4 in a single country; the authors caution about generalizability to other countries, ethnicities, and age groups

### Target Learners
- Elementary school children in grades 3 and 4 (ages 7-11)

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintain academic performance (mathematics) while increasing physical activity
- Improve physical fitness
