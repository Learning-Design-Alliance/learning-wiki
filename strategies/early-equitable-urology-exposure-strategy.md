# Combine urology interest groups, mixed-model skills sessions, and targeted mentorship to increase equitable early exposure for medical students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that medical school curricula increase urology exposure in both pre-clinical and clinical years through student-led interest groups offering case conferences, journal clubs, career panels and mentorship, mandatory didactic-plus-simulation skills sessions, and mentorship programs for students without home programs or from underrepresented backgrounds. It states these efforts "must also advocate for underrepresented student participation" and can be achieved through integrating urology content into the core curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration of urology content into the core curriculum and skills-based training sessions so all students receive equitable exposure, as the review states
#### Constraints
- Only 17% of schools offer required urology clerkship experiences and only 51% offer preclinical urology lectures, per the review

### Target Learners
- Medical students, especially those without home urology programs and those underrepresented in medicine

### Target Learning Goals
- Early career exposure to urology
- Foundational urologic clinical skills such as catheter placement and pelvic examination
- Improved residency match preparedness
