# Graphic Organizers and Visual Aids (Attention)

## Description
Graphic organizers and visual aids structure content spatially — through mind maps, advance organizers, comparison matrices, flowcharts, and diagrams — so that learners can see which information matters and how it relates. As an attention strategy, the organizer functions as a pre-selection device: it directs the learner's limited attentional resources toward essential relationships rather than surface details.

## Design Implications

Graphic organizers work by externalizing structure, reducing the working-memory burden of holding relationships in mind while processing new content [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Their attentional value comes from emphasis: visually highlighting what is central directs selective attention and improves later recall of emphasized content [Relevancy of emphasis directs attention.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [+M]. Combining verbal and visual representations supports dual coding, producing better recall than either channel alone [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [+M]. Meta-analytic evidence shows knowledge maps and similar graphic organizers outperform text-only or list formats for retention, with the largest gains when learners actively construct the organizer rather than only study a provided one [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear analysis of the content's structure (hierarchy, sequence, comparison, cause–effect) so the organizer type matches the relationships being taught
- Explicit instruction in how to read and use the organizer — learners unfamiliar with a format need modeling ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
- For maximal benefit, a step where learners complete or construct the organizer themselves rather than passively receiving it ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))

#### Constraints
- Poorly designed organizers with excessive boxes, colors, or cross-links add extraneous load and depress learning [~M] — visual complexity must be pruned, not accumulated
- Provided (completed) organizers can encourage shallow copying; learners who fill them in without generating connections show smaller gains [~M]
- Mismatched structure harms comprehension: a hierarchical map applied to non-hierarchical content distorts the relationships [~M]
- Benefits shrink for learners with high prior knowledge, who can impose structure on text themselves and may find the organizer redundant [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Advance organizers** presented before text to prime relevant schema ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
- **Partially completed organizers** that learners finish during instruction, fading support over time
- **Learner-constructed maps** (concept mapping) as an active-learning and assessment activity
- **Signal-enhanced materials** — headings, color-coding, and callouts embedded in existing texts or slides rather than separate artifacts

### Target Learners
- Novices who cannot yet distinguish central from peripheral information in a domain [Relevancy of emphasis directs attention.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [+M]
- Learners with limited working-memory capacity who benefit from externalized structure [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Less beneficial for advanced learners, who may experience redundancy effects [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Selective attention: identifying the key ideas and relationships in complex material
- Relational knowledge: comparing, sequencing, and classifying content
- Retention of structured information [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Analyze the content to determine its dominant structure (hierarchy, sequence, comparison, cycle).
2. Select an organizer type that matches that structure; keep the design minimal ([Cognitive Load Management](../elements/cognitive-load-management.md)).
3. Present the organizer before or during instruction as an advance organizer, modeling how to read it ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md)).
4. Have learners complete or construct the organizer themselves, then check it against a reference version ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
5. Fade the organizer as learners internalize the structure, eventually asking them to generate their own from raw material.

## Examples
- **Inspiration / Kidspiration (https://www.inspiration.com)** — concept-mapping software widely used in K–12 for learner-constructed graphic organizers across content areas.
- **Comparison matrices in history instruction** — a grid with civilizations as rows and institutions (religion, economy, government) as columns directs attention to comparable features across cases; multiple contrasting cases presented in such a matrix support abstraction of shared principles [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M]
- **Khan Academy unit maps (https://www.khanacademy.org)** — visual course maps showing lesson hierarchy, orienting learners to where the current lesson sits in the unit's structure.
