# Develop discipline-specific transparency toolkits coauthored by first-generation faculty covering the unwritten mechanics of academic chemistry careers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The editorial proposes that chemistry departments and professional societies such as ACS and RSC develop toolkits that make explicit the hidden rules of academic careers, including editorial board appointments and PI start-up package negotiations, which are "not taught formally, even at the postdoctoral stage." The authors propose toolkits "coauthored by first-generation faculty who have navigated these pathways themselves." They state that "Improving transparency at this specific career stage is a key mechanism for leveling the playing field for first-generation scholars in chemistry."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Content must cover the unwritten mechanics of academic chemistry careers and be coauthored by first-generation faculty with lived experience of these pathways
#### Constraints
- Existing programs such as the Sutton Trust Summer Schools, Cambridge's Widening Access scheme, and the ACS Bridge Program address transitions but "none of these programs addresses the ongoing discipline-specific opacity of academic chemistry progression"

### Target Learners
- first-generation undergraduate, graduate, and postdoctoral chemists

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of academic career structures, expectations, and evaluation systems
