# Continuously tailor rehearsal difficulty by adjusting skill criteria and task demands

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents ongoing adjustment of practice difficulty as a component unique to DP: "DP is an ongoing process of tailoring practice to the right level." Practice can be made easier by removing skill criteria, shortening video segments, or simplifying responses, and made harder by lengthening segments or adding criteria as performance improves. The supervisor also monitored difficulty ratings (the therapist reported 8-9, then 7, then 6-7) to keep practice challenging but doable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ongoing assessment of the trainee's experienced difficulty (e.g., 1-10 ratings)
- Skill criteria that can be decomposed and recombined
#### Constraints
- Repeated practice risks overfitting responses to specific moments from past sessions, limiting flexibility; practicing a range of responses to diverse client statements helps therapists remain adaptable

### Target Learners
- psychotherapy supervisees across skill levels

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining engagement and preventing plateau during repeated behavioral rehearsal

### Affordances
- Deliberate Practice Supervision Model
