Use beat synchronization assessment and early music and rhythm training as early indicators and supports of reading readiness¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes that nonverbal rhythm assessment, possibly combined with the neural FFR metric, can identify children at risk of reading struggle before formal schooling. Based on differences in preliteracy skills and auditory processing in children as young as 3 years old, the authors "suggest that interventions, which may support these areas of development (e.g., music and rhythm training), be implemented early in childhood".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Assessment requires a conga-drum drumming task administered by an experimenter who reliably produces synchronous rhythms to a pacing tone, with hits recorded and processed via circular statistics

Constraints¶

  • Classification reliability is reduced for children who synchronize at only one rate, where attentional difficulties, fatigue, or tempo preference may confound performance; the relationships were established in typically developing children, not yet those with language disorders

Target Learners¶

  • preschool children aged 3–5 years

Target Learning Goals¶

  • preliteracy skills: phonological awareness, auditory short-term memory, rapid automatized naming

Claims¶

  • Preschoolers who synchronize well to a beat outscore poor synchronizers on phonological awareness, auditory short-term memory, and rapid automatized naming [+W]
  • Synchronizers show more precise speech-envelope encoding in the FFR, with the group difference modulated by presentation mode, time region, and stimulus [+W]
  • Synchronizers show higher phase-locking consistency than Non-synchronizers, especially from transition to steady-state and at higher frequencies [+W]
  • Synchronizers have more consistent FFRs than Non-synchronizers, with group differences shaped by polarity, presentation mode, and time region [+W]
  • Synchronizers outperform Non-synchronizers on musical rhythm discrimination but not on melody discrimination [+W]
  • Children who synchronize at only one beat rate fall statistically between the other two groups, with mostly non-significant differences [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use a 30-minute neurophysiological assessment of consonants-in-noise coding for early identification of children at risk for language and literacy problems
  • Use learning-based neurophysiological indicators of speech-print mapping difficulty for early diagnosis and intervention of developmental dyslexia
  • Offer supplementary reading programs to children at substantially elevated genetic risk for dyslexia years before formal diagnosis
