# Educators should expose students to accurate depictions of physics careers and underemphasized skills such as interdisciplinary communication and computational problem-solving

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators give students an accurate picture of the skills, practices, motivations, and daily routines of different physics careers to support career decision-making. It notes theorists mentioned skills that "may be underemphasized in many classrooms," including interdisciplinary communication, linking theory to applied problems, and computational problem-solving, and encourages educators to use the paper's topics as inspiration for supporting student career decisions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators must communicate a clear picture of what it means to work in different areas of physics and allow students opportunities to explore those options
#### Constraints
- Further research is needed on incorporating authentic research practices into classroom instruction and on whether assessments emphasize a sufficiently wide breadth of skills and attitudes

### Target Learners
- prospective physics majors and students making career decisions

### Target Learning Goals
- informed career decision-making about physics subfields
- interdisciplinary communication and computational problem-solving skills
