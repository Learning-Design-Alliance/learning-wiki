# Daily self-led skill drills with automated simulator feedback to maintain neonatal resuscitation skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy is to have health workers perform brief daily resuscitation skill drills on a high-fidelity feedback manikin before starting clinical work, with the simulator's app scoring each attempt and giving immediate feedback. The article reports that "self-training with a high-fidelity, feedback simulator can maintain the neonatal resuscitation skills" and that "at least 8 skill drills are optimal to maintain the neonatal resuscitation skill for a period of 3 months".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A high-fidelity manikin with sensors and automated objective feedback
- A routine slot for daily practice before clinical work
- Recording of drill counts per participant
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a 3-month observational study in one Nepali referral hospital; the authors state further evaluation is required in a scale-up setting and that the waning effect between drills over time cannot be estimated

### Target Learners
- Nurses and midwives in low-resource maternity and newborn units

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining neonatal resuscitation and bag-and-mask ventilation competency

## Claims
