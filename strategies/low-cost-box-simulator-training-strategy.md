# Use low-cost physical box simulators to provide safe, cost-effective laparoscopic skills training, especially in low and middle-income settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing laparoscopic physical simulation as "a safe and cost-effective environment to enhance surgical skills", enabling low and middle-income countries to adopt it. The strategy rests on building simulators from simple materials: "Using simple materials and replacing specific devices such as monitors and camera to generic one allow an expressive reduction in cost", with the ESTBS unit estimated at one thousand dollars versus $2850.00 for the standard trainer. Training before operating-room practice can shorten anesthetic and surgical time spent on intraoperative training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A skills surgery laboratory or equivalent space with camera, monitor and suture materials
#### Constraints
- The authors note laparoscopic physical simulation cannot substitute operating room practice

### Target Learners
- surgical residents
- practicing surgeons in low and middle-income settings

### Target Learning Goals
- laparoscopic suturing and psychomotor skills acquisition outside the operating room
