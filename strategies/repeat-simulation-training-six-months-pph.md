# Repeat simulation training 6 months after initial training to sustain PPH knowledge and skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that simulation-trained providers' skills "remain effective for at least 6 months" but decline thereafter, and therefore recommend that "simulation training should be repeated in our learning environments 6 months after initial training". The strategy schedules booster simulation sessions at a 6-month interval after the initial theory-plus-simulation course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An initial simulation-based training on PPH management as delivered in the study, followed by scheduled reassessment to time the booster session
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted in situ and the authors cannot confirm the same results in vivo or an impact on maternal mortality; broader applicability requires larger numbers of providers from all professional categories

### Target Learners
- Maternity hospital healthcare providers: obstetricians, general practitioners, midwives, birth attendants, nurses, anesthesiologists and laboratory technicians

### Target Learning Goals
- Postpartum hemorrhage diagnosis and management, blood transfusion, and team communication
