# Implement weekly reflection-and-feedback cycles in small sections of 10 to 20 students per instructor

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing the GRF in courses with small student-to-teacher ratios, or in large courses with small sections led by teaching or learning assistants, because responding individually to each student can be prohibitively time-consuming in large classes. The strategy pairs weekly student reflections with individualized instructor responses, sustaining an ongoing written dialogue. The authors also suggest the GRF could be implemented in informal educational contexts, including academic advising or mentorship programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors read each reflection and write an individualized response, taking about 10 to 15 min per student in this study
#### Constraints
- In large classes the time required to respond to each student individually can be prohibitively large

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- reflection, goal-setting, and strategy development for academic difficulties
