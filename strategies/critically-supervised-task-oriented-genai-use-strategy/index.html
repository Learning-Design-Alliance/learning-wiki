Graduate AI literacy training should promote purposeful, task-oriented, critically supervised GenAI use rather than targeting overall use levels¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article's practical implications recommend that universities avoid treating GenAI dependence as uniformly desirable or undesirable and instead "promot[e] purposeful, task-oriented, and critically supervised use." Recommended practices include incorporating critical thinking into AI literacy training through "veriﬁcation of AI-generated claims, identiﬁcation of hallucinations and bias, comparison of alternative outputs, source checking," and encouraging students to document how AI content is evaluated, periodically conduct research tasks independently of GenAI, and use human supervision for high-stakes decisions. The goal is calibrated, transparent, human-directed AI-assisted research.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional AI literacy initiatives and supervisor involvement; students retain responsibility for problem formulation, evidence evaluation, methodological decisions, and final scholarly judgments

Constraints¶

  • Grounded in cross-sectional associative findings; the article notes it did not directly measure interaction frequency, accumulated experience, or familiarity, so some explanations remain tentative

Target Learners¶

  • graduate students
  • university AI literacy program designers
  • supervisors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical evaluation of AI-generated outputs
  • maintaining cognitive agency in AI-assisted research
  • calibrated trust in GenAI

Affordances¶

  • Functional Existential Genai Dependence Differentiation

Related Strategies¶

  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
  • Redesign assessment reflectively for possible AI use, using supervised tasks for lower-order skills and intentional AI use with reflection for higher-order work
  • Redesign assessment toward supervised, oral, process-based and authentic formats to verify students' own reasoning under GenAI
  • Teach AI literacy as epistemic calibration and design assessment that preserves visible spaces for human reasoning and ownership
