# Provide explicit, formal instruction in physics problem solving at all levels

## Description
The article's closing recommendation to physics instructors: help students recognize that physics problem solving is both complex and teachable, and provide explicit, formal instruction in it. The authors also invite students to see introductory physics courses as opportunities to cultivate powerful habits of thought beyond the physics content itself. The strategy rests on the essay's nine-lesson account of what problem solving trains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must treat problem solving as complex and teachable rather than as an implicit byproduct of content coverage
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is an argued position of the essay, not a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- physics students at all levels
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- transferable problem-solving strategies
- metacognitive awareness of problem-solving processes
