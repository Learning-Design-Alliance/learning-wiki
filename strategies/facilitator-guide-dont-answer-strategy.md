# Faculty facilitate by questioning and guiding rather than answering, with random group call-ons and peer assistance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the classroom session, 3–5 prepped faculty advisors with an answer key circulate among groups, asking questions and guiding without directly answering. Groups are called on randomly, and if a group errs or stalls, facilitators use leading questions, invite another group to assist, or pose the question to the whole class. Students receive the case 3–5 days ahead and answer basic questions individually beforehand; allowing any resources pushes students toward reliable sources because they must defend answers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty prepped on the case with an answer key
- Groups of 4–5 students, preassigned per the literature on small-group effectiveness
- Students answer a few basic individual questions before class
#### Constraints
- A smaller room with moveable desks works better than a large lecture hall

### Target Learners
- medical students in a class of 150 (or two sessions of 75)

### Target Learning Goals
- defending answers with reliable sources
- consolidating basic-science concepts through group discussion
