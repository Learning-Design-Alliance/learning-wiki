# Blend asynchronous and synchronous sessions with technical support and virtual simulations when delivering flipped classrooms remotely

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that institutions delivering remote education mix asynchronous and synchronous formats, provide robust technical support, and use virtual simulations and remote practicums for practical skills. As the authors state, "a mix of asynchronous and synchronous sessions is ideal to balance flexibility with crucial real-time interactions." Faculty running remote workshops should give clear instructions, encourage webcams, and facilitate technical support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Robust technological support infrastructure
- Clear instructions and webcam encouragement in remote workshops
- Video demonstrations, virtual simulations, and remote practicums for skill-based training
#### Constraints
- Findings derive from a single Asian university pharmacy program during COVID-19; the authors note regional educational practices may influence perspectives

### Target Learners
- undergraduate pharmacy students in remote higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- communication skills
- practical/clinical skills such as compounding, OSCEs, and device counselling
- content learning in flipped-format courses
