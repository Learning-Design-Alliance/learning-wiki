# Use equity-oriented quiz rewording and course redesigns to address gendered gain differences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article notes phrasing matters for equity and inclusion, citing McCullough & Meltzer (2001), who reworded the Force Concept Inventory to use more daily-life or female-oriented contexts and demonstrated it elevated female scores on some questions; it also suggests avoiding mechanics questions about frictionless motion on ice at UH Hilo, where many students have no ice experience. Where a gender gap appears in gains, the authors propose redesigns: more formal values affirmation, projects connecting content to learners' majors or a community problem, and qualitative exploration of male and female experiences to guide changes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Quiz phrasing must be reviewed for equity, e.g. ensuring questions provide sufficient information and using contexts familiar to the learners
#### Constraints
- The female–male gain difference in the authors' own data was not statistically significant, and the sex labels used were the instructor's perceptions, not students' self-identification

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science students, particularly female learners in physics and astronomy courses

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable conceptual learning gains across learner groups
