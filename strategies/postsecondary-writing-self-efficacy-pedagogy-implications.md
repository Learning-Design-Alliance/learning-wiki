# Practical implications for postsecondary writing pedagogy synthesized from included studies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's narrative analysis of the implications researchers drew from their studies identifies recurring pedagogical themes: students' tendency to overinflate their writing self-efficacy, recognition of the developmental nature of writing ability, the importance of teacher attitudes and instructional climate, the influence of feedback on writing self-efficacy, and approaches to teaching and guiding writing. These implications were identified by the researchers of the included papers based on their findings and written in their discussion and conclusion sections. The review offers them as potential directions for future writing instruction in postsecondary settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implications should be grounded in studies meeting the review's quality threshold of at least 50% of possible quality points
#### Constraints
- The implications are exploratory and drawn from studies with varied settings, designs, and methodological choices, so they are not established causal recommendations

### Target Learners
- postsecondary undergraduate and graduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- writing self-efficacy and writing performance in academic writing contexts
