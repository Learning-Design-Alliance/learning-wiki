# Improve instructional design in online courses, since stable satisfaction over nine years suggests design improvements were not translating to better learner experience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend continued instructional improvement in online courses. They state plainly that "Our findings suggest the need for improved instructional design in online courses," reasoning that the stability of experience ratings over nine years — despite evolving theory and evidence — implies either a failure to incorporate design improvements or rising student expectations. The strategy is to apply evidence-based design improvements and monitor learner experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course design should adapt to research questions and incorporate instructional improvements identified in earlier iterations, as the annual studies did.
#### Constraints
- Limited research suggests enhanced course design is associated with improved satisfaction, and worsening attitudes have been observed after poor course design, per the article's cited evidence.

- postgraduate medical trainees in online courses
