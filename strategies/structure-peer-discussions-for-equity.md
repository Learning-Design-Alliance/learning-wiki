# Structure in-class peer-discussion activities purposefully to promote equitable participation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because social identities predict preferred roles and barriers differ across student groups, the authors recommend that instructors deliberately design peer-discussion activities rather than leave participation voluntary and unstructured. The article states: "We encourage instructors to consider structuring their in-class activities in ways that promote equity, which may require more purposeful attention to alleviating the current differential student experiences with peer discussions." Identifying which barriers affect which groups enables targeted interventions encouraging participation from students who participate less in unstructured settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor awareness of which barriers contribute to differential participation patterns across social identities in their classroom
#### Constraints
- The article notes that in most large STEM classrooms participation during peer discussions is generally voluntary because instructors cannot enforce individual engagement

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large active-learning STEM courses, particularly students from historically underserved groups

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable opportunity to engage in explaining and interactive engagement during peer discussions
