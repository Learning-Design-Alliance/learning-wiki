# Use virtual patient simulations in large lecture-hall classes to train clinical reasoning with minimal lecturer supervision

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing VPS programs for whole cohorts in a shared-screen lecture hall, with faculty instructed to "intervene as little as possible during the class, especially regarding knowledge and clinical reasoning skills that were directly relevant to the answers." The authors conclude that "VPS software programs could thus help medical students improve their clinical decision-making skills with minimal supervision from lecturers," and suggest combining VPS with traditional methods for further benefit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Uniform conditions across participants (same hall, same experience level), an audience response system for anonymous data collection, and minimal operational guidance from one faculty member
#### Constraints
- The authors state it is not possible to complete all medical instruction through simulation education, and that fully immersive VR for all participants simultaneously requires tremendous money and high-tech equipment

### Target Learners
- large cohorts of pre-clerkship medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- clinical decision-making
