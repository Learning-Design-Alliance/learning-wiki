# Rebalance AIED review processes to require evidence of impact on student learning beyond feasibility demonstrations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 2 studies (2 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size

## Description
This strategy is a concrete implementable change to the AIED publication and review process: reviewers should ask what evidence a paper offers of impact on student learning, and how a model's predictions can actually be used to make things better for teachers, students, and schools. The article states that "papers showing feasibility are fine, but papers can't be just showing feasibility." The author motivates it by noting large language models that answer questions at 70 to 80% correct produce C or D grade work, so impressive examples do not guarantee effectiveness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A review process willing to ask for learning-impact evidence
- A broad portfolio that still admits purely technical innovative papers that are truly a first
#### Constraints
- The author notes some computer scientists say you cannot write a technical paper in AIED anymore, and calls for rebalancing rather than excluding technical work

### Target Learners
- Students ultimately affected by AIED systems evaluated under this review standard

### Target Learning Goals
- Ensuring AIED research demonstrates impact on student learning rather than prediction alone
