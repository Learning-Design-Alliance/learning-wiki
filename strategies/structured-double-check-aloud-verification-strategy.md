# Structure the double-check systematically using the original prescription and drug, speaking each step aloud, and do not proceed with unresolved inconsistencies such as a documented allergy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the RoH findings and participant feedback, the authors recommend verifying the 5R rule strictly at each step using the original prescription, the original drug used for preparation and the final preparation, while speaking aloud each step to facilitate shared understanding. Participants should consult physicians when facing unavailability, formulation discrepancies or unclear prescriptions, and should not administer in the presence of unresolved inconsistencies such as a documented allergy. Volume checks should follow confirmation of the correct drug and concentration rather than serving as a dose proxy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional guidelines mandating an independent double-check for high-risk drugs such as opioids
- Educator-led debriefing to deliver these corrective recommendations after observed errors
#### Constraints
- The authors note a truly independent double-check was limited in this set-up because both participants performed the exercise simultaneously, mirroring imperfect real-life practice

### Target Learners
- Nurses
- Healthcare assistants
- Physicians administering or double-checking high-risk medications

### Target Learning Goals
- Rigorous application of the 5R rule
- Systematic, reproducible double-check performance
- Escalating unresolved inconsistencies before administration
