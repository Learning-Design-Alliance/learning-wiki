# Residency programs should combine simulation-based training, early surgical exposure, enhanced supervision, competency-based benchmarks, and high-risk scenario practice to reduce intraoperative complications

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (7 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's discussion recommends a bundle of training methodologies for ophthalmology residency programs: "Simulation-based training, including the use of high-fidelity surgical simulators, could offer residents an opportunity to practice critical steps of cataract surgery in a controlled, risk-free environment", alongside enhanced live-surgery supervision with real-time feedback, competency-based curricula with clear skill benchmarks, and deliberate exposure to high-risk scenarios such as mature cataracts or zonular instability. It also suggests advanced technologies like FLACS could simplify challenging surgical steps in resident training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adequate supervision during live surgeries so residents receive real-time guidance and feedback
- Structured curricula with clear benchmarks for skill acquisition before advancing to more challenging cases
#### Constraints
- The review notes conclusions should be interpreted with caution due to the small number of studies, high heterogeneity, and variability across settings

### Target Learners
- ophthalmology residents

### Target Learning Goals
- cataract surgical competence
- reduction of intraoperative complications such as anterior vitrectomy
