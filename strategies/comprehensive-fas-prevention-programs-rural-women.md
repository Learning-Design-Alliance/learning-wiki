# Implement comprehensive community prevention programs targeting women of child-bearing age alongside support for FAS-affected children in high-risk rural populations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend large-scale interventions in rural Western and Northern Cape areas that pair prevention of prenatal alcohol exposure with support for affected children. They cite a Western Cape clinic prevention program for women with high-risk drinking behaviour that "was effective in helping women stop drinking, or drink less, during pregnancy, reducing the risk of FASD", and argue such interventions are urgently required in high-risk populations like Clanwilliam.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must reach women of child-bearing age in rural communities; the article notes existing programs do not reach small towns like Clanwilliam
#### Constraints
- The article notes there is no children's home or special needs school in Clanwilliam and alcohol treatment services are poorly distributed and unaffordable for the poor

### Target Learners
- Children with FAS
- Women of child-bearing age in high-risk rural communities

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing prenatal alcohol exposure and supporting classroom learning for FAS-affected children

## Claims

- [Children with FAS are shorter, lighter, and have smaller head circumference than children without FAS](../claims/fas-children-smaller-physical-measurements.md) [+W]

- [FAS prevalence in a rural Western Cape farm school is 127 per 1000 learners (12.7%)](../claims/fas-prevalence-127-per-1000-clanwilliam-school.md) [+W]
- [FAS prevalence among farm children (16.2%) is substantially higher than among town children (6.6%)](../claims/fas-prevalence-farm-higher-than-town.md) [+W]
