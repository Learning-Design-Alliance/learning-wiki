# Advise particular student types to delay specific difficult courses until a later stage of their academic career

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends using persistence-gap analysis by student type and stage to guide advising and placement. Where first-term transfer students taking a course persist 15-20% less often, "keeping new transfer students out of this particular course would be an obvious, and easily accomplished step toward higher retention"; the same course posed no problem in later terms.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Persistence data broken down by student type and stage for individual courses
- Advising and placement mechanisms with flexibility, e.g., curricula with flexibility in course choice and timing
#### Constraints
- The article notes the general education requirement structure gave flexibility that is often not present in highly structured majors

### Target Learners
- first-term transfer students
- undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- retention
- successful course completion
