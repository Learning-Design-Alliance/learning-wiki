Use structured training pathways with supervised early practice and experience-informed patient counselling in hip arthroscopy¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's clinical implications translate its learning-curve findings into a training approach: hip arthroscopy "can be performed safely during the early learning phase under appropriate supervision", while continued experience yields efficiency gains. The authors recommend "structured training pathways, careful case selection during early practice and more informed patient counselling regarding expected outcomes and surgeon experience", and point to simulation-based training and standardized surgical pathways as future directions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Supervision during the early learning phase
  • Careful case selection during early practice
  • Structured training pathways

Constraints¶

  • Findings are observational with low to very low certainty and should be interpreted as indicative rather than causal
  • Experience thresholds associated with optimal outcomes remain undefined pending prospective multicenter studies

Target Learners¶

  • Orthopaedic surgeons in training for hip arthroscopy

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Surgical technical proficiency in hip arthroscopy
  • Operative efficiency and safe early-phase practice

Claims¶

  • Overall complication rates do not differ significantly between late and early hip arthroscopy experience phases [+W]
  • Late-experience hip arthroscopy cases achieve higher functional improvement in MCID units than early-experience cases [+W]
  • Conversion to total hip arthroplasty is significantly lower in late-experience hip arthroscopy cases than in early cases [+W]
  • Late-experience hip arthroscopy cases show modest but significant gains in mHHS and NAHS compared with early cases [+W]
  • Surgeon experience in hip arthroscopy is associated with significantly shorter operative time in late versus early experience phases [+W]
  • Late-experience hip arthroscopy cases show significantly shorter traction time than early-experience cases [+W]
  • Revision arthroscopy rates do not differ significantly between late and early hip arthroscopy experience phases [+W]
