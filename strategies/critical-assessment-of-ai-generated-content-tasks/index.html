Design literature-class tasks in which students critically assess AI-generated content and propose improvements¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that, as AI enters literature education, assessment should shift toward creativity, critical thinking and evaluative judgement, and that literature classes use tasks "where students critically assess AI-generated content, identify potential biases, and propose improvements." The authors argue this strengthens critical and evaluative skills and encourages deeper engagement with literary analysis. This study's rubric-based group analysis of alternative endings is an instance of such evaluative work.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • An instructional design that assigns students authentic AI-generated texts to evaluate against criteria such as fidelity, context and character integrity

Constraints¶

  • The article flags that educators should ensure careful integration to avoid overreliance on AI outputs and preserve students' personal creativity and critical thinking

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate English literature students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • critical and evaluative judgement of texts
  • literary analysis skills

Claims¶

  • Students analyzing ChatGPT-generated endings were somewhat more likely than peer-text readers to believe the ending could be Orwell's original [+W]
  • Students were far more likely to accept peer-written alternative endings as valid conclusions to the novel than ChatGPT-generated endings [+W]
  • Students trusted the plot plausibility of peer-written alternative endings more than ChatGPT-generated endings [+W]
  • Students' main AI challenges are content reliability and depth, and most expect AI to serve as tutor, creative partner, and critical thinking partner rather than mere content generator [+W]
  • Both writing-based and AI-based alternative-ending activities motivated students, though with distinct concerns in each group [+W]
  • Students rated ChatGPT-generated narrative endings equal to or higher than student-written endings on setting, themes, clarity of language and tone [~W]
  • Both student- and AI-text groups similarly affirmed contextual and character integration, with greater uncertainty in the ChatGPT-text group [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Redesign assessments to require critical thinking, originality, interactive activities, and real-time or proctored formats
  • Design curricula giving students opportunities to critically engage with AI and AI-generated content through validation, interpretation, and revision tasks
  • Embed AI as a learning partner and redesign assessments for AI-rich contexts
