# Deploy screen-based simulation as an accessible training alternative when face-to-face clinical education is disrupted

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends screen-based simulation for contexts where face-to-face education is not feasible, such as disasters or pandemics, because it runs on widely used devices and is less expensive than high-fidelity simulators. In the trial, the intervention group used it in subgroups of ten in a computer laboratory after a four-hour verbal lecture, with the control group receiving identical simulation training afterward for ethical reasons.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Computer, tablet, or phone access; the article notes simulations were run in a computer laboratory without internet problems
#### Constraints
- The study was limited to students taking the pediatric nursing course at one university, so "the results can only be generalized to this study group"

### Target Learners
- nursing students
- second- and third-year undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- pediatric medication administration training during disrupted or distance learning conditions
