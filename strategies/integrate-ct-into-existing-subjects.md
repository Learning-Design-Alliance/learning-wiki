# Integrate CT into existing subjects such as sciences, maths and English when a standalone CS course is not feasible

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review's Research Question 3 section recommends that where schools cannot run a full CS curriculum, CT be incorporated into existing subjects, summarising studies of integration into sciences, biology, physics, maths, English (journalism, writing, literature) and dance. It states the hope that "the studies outlined can help educators who lack the time or resources to teach CT as a standalone subject to incorporate this important skill into their classrooms." Examples include an Introduction to Computational Thinking course for science majors taught through Python with physics, bioinformatics and simulation projects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Subject teachers need time and resources, and awareness of how CT skills apply beyond CS or science
#### Constraints
- For some schools or colleges it might not always be possible to run a complete CS curriculum in which CT can be taught separately

### Target Learners
- Primary and secondary students in non-CS subjects
- Educators lacking time or resources for standalone CT teaching

### Target Learning Goals
- Computational thinking applied within science, maths, English and arts subjects
