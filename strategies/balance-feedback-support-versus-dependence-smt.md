# Balance objective visual feedback with appropriately dosed support to avoid over-reliance on feedback during motor skill learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that providing objective feedback on force modulation can support students' development of motor strategies for mastering performance skills, but that educators must find a balance between supporting motor learning and making students overly reliant on feedback. It observes that "the high confidence and the limited ability to perform specific target impulse forces suggest a dependence of students on visual feedback", noting that when deprived of feedback students' motor performance is affected. Future work should identify "the ideal level of feedback to optimize motor learning".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators must calibrate the amount of visual feedback so students develop force modulation without becoming dependent on it
#### Constraints
- Structured or progressive laboratory feedback has shown no improvement in SMT target performance in some prior studies, and self-controlled feedback may be more efficacious than constant feedback

### Target Learners
- chiropractic and manual therapy students learning psychomotor clinical skills

### Target Learning Goals
- retention and transferable modulation of manual therapy force–time characteristics
