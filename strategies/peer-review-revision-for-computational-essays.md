# Add peer review and revision cycles to computational essay courses to supply feedback and correctness checking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on stated limitations—essays were not checked for correctness and students received little feedback beyond one or two presentation questions and TA grading—the authors propose having students peer-review each other's work in future iterations. They write that "one option for addressing this limitation is having students peer-review each other's work, and we are currently in the process of exploring this idea", noting it would also address correctness and add scientific authenticity. They further propose layering on explicit requirements for higher-order computational methods, writing guidelines, and revisions in response to critiques as a learning progression.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A baseline implementation of computational essays with presentations must already be in place before layering on critique and revision criteria.
#### Constraints
- In the implemented design no room for critique was built in; some students used clearly-incorrect physics, especially regarding energy conservation within magnetic fields.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in computational courses

### Target Learning Goals
- giving and receiving scientific critique
- revising computational work
- physics computational literacy
