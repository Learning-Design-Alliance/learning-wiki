# Thirteen-session IM dialogic argumentation intervention pairing engagement, practice, and reflection on transcripts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (7 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
A implementable intervention recipe in which same-side pairs argue with a succession of opposing pairs over IM chat software, then analyze printed transcripts of their own dialogues with 'Other Argument' and 'Own Argument' reflection sheets, prepare for a culminating 'showdown' debate, and receive an argument map with feedback. The article reports: "The intervention took place during thirteen 40-minute sessions occurring twice per week in the participants' classroom." Participants receive a constrained list of "some possibly relevant facts" equated across topics as an argumentation resource.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A constrained knowledge base of possibly relevant facts equated across participants and topics
- Adult coaches who facilitate without offering direct instruction or feedback
- IM software providing a permanent record of the discourse for reflection
#### Constraints
- Because intact classes, rather than individual students, were randomly assigned to control and experimental conditions, comparisons between the two groups should be interpreted with caution
- The method involves no software-based scaffolding of argument construction and evaluation beyond the IM software itself

### Target Learners
- middle school students (sixth grade, ages 11-12)

### Target Learning Goals
- dialogic argumentation skills including counterargument and rebuttal
- epistemological understanding of the purpose and goals of argument
