# Systematically evaluate the degree of implementation of health promotion programs in HPS and review accreditation requirements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that the degree of implementation of health promotion programs in accredited health promoting schools be systematically evaluated and that the accreditation process and requirements be reviewed. The authors argue that partial implementation may explain the absence of an HPS advantage, noting HPS programs are "rarely implemented as planned due to their complexity". Accreditation criteria should follow WHO or other health authority standards, with periodic certificate renewal.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- HPS accreditation requirements should be based on specific criteria as determined by the WHO or other health authorities
- The process of HPS accreditation should be ongoing and systematic and HPS certificates should be renewed periodically
#### Constraints
- HPS programs are rarely implemented as planned due to their complexity, resulting in some HPS implementing their health promotion programs only partially
- Few studies have evaluated the degree of implementation of health promotion programs in HPS, and their results are inconclusive

### Target Learners
- 7th and 8th grade middle school students aged 12–15

### Target Learning Goals
- Health promotion, life skills, and healthy lifestyle habits in compulsory education
