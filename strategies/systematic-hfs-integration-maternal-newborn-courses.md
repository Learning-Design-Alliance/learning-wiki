# Systematically integrate high-fidelity simulation into maternal-newborn courses with faculty development and gender-inclusive support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (7 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that nursing programs "systematically integrate HFS into maternal-newborn courses, using diverse, realistic scenarios to enhance learning outcomes". It pairs this with specialized faculty development in designing and facilitating simulation activities, awareness programs and mentorship to address gender bias, culturally sensitive curriculum revisions, and policy advocacy for flexible clinical placements for male students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Specialized faculty training to design and implement effective simulation-based activities
- Diverse, realistic simulation scenarios aligned with course objectives
#### Constraints
- The article calls for further research on the long-term impact of HFS on clinical competence and on gender-specific experiences

### Target Learners
- male undergraduate nursing students in maternal-newborn courses

### Target Learning Goals
- maternal-newborn clinical skills
- confidence and professional readiness
