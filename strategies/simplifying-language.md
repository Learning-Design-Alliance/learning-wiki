# Simplifying Language

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Simplifying language means rewriting instructional materials — text, narration, instructions, and assessment items — so that the linguistic surface demands less of the learner: shorter sentences, common vocabulary, explicit connectives, and one idea per sentence. The goal is to free working memory for the actual content rather than for decoding prose. Simplification targets the *language wrapper* around content, not the conceptual difficulty of the content itself.

## Design Implications

Simplifying language reduces extraneous cognitive load imposed by dense syntax and unfamiliar vocabulary, allowing learners to allocate resources to constructing meaning [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S]. However, wholesale simplification can backfire: texts that are too easy or too coherent can reduce the effortful processing that drives deep learning, particularly for learners with relevant prior knowledge [~M]. The most robust finding is that *targeted* simplification — clarifying syntax and vocabulary while preserving conceptual challenge — outperforms both dense prose and oversimplified text [~S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A readability baseline (e.g., sentence length, vocabulary frequency) and a defined target audience
- Retention of technical terms where they are the learning goal, with definitions on first use
- Consistent terminology — one concept, one word — across materials ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure-presentation.md))
- Review by someone who did not write the text, to catch unstated assumptions

#### Constraints
- Over-simplified text can lower engagement and reduce inference-making for knowledgeable readers; coherence effects reverse with expertise [~M]
- Simplifying away all technical vocabulary prevents learners from acquiring the discipline's language, harming later reading and communication [-M]
- Simplified language does not compensate for missing background knowledge; vocabulary support without [Activating Prior Knowledge](../strategies/activating_prior_knowledge.md) yields limited gains [-M]
- Machine or formulaic simplification can distort meaning or strip necessary qualifiers, especially in technical or legal content [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Plain-language rewriting**: short sentences, active voice, common words — suited to instructions, interfaces, and assessment items
- **Scaffolded complexity**: begin with simplified text, then introduce authentic disciplinary prose as proficiency grows
- **Bilingual/ESL adaptation**: simplified syntax plus glossaries and visuals, rather than reduced content
- **Simplification of directions only**: leave content texts intact but simplify task instructions and rubrics

### Target Learners
- Beginning readers, language learners, and learners with reading-related disabilities, for whom decoding costs otherwise crowd out comprehension [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Novices in a technical domain who lack the vocabulary to parse dense source material
- Less beneficial — and sometimes harmful — for advanced learners, for whom simplified text is redundant and reduces productive struggle [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Comprehension of factual and procedural content delivered through text or narration
- Vocabulary acquisition, when key terms are defined and reused deliberately
- Not well suited to goals requiring learners to interpret complex, ambiguous, or authentic texts as the skill itself

### Instructions
1. Identify the linguistic demands of the material: sentence length, vocabulary rarity, syntactic embedding, and passive constructions.
2. Rewrite for clarity: one idea per sentence, active voice, explicit connectives, and consistent terminology — apply [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md).
3. Break long passages into labeled sections and lists to support [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S].
4. Remove decorative or tangential material that competes for attention, consistent with the coherence principle [Irrelevant material hurts learning.](../claims/coherence-principle-irrelevant-material-hurts-learning.md) [+S].
5. Preserve the conceptual challenge: keep the hard ideas intact, define necessary technical terms, and pair simplified text with tasks that demand deep processing.
6. Pilot with representative learners and check comprehension before full deployment.

## Examples
- **Plain language guidelines (U.S. government, [plainlanguage.gov](https://www.plainlanguage.gov))** — systematic rewriting of public documents into short sentences and common vocabulary; the same techniques apply to instructional materials and assessment items.
- **Newsela** — publishes the same news article at multiple reading levels, letting teachers match linguistic complexity to reader proficiency while keeping content constant.
- **Khan Academy** — uses short, plainly worded sentences in exercise hints and instructions, reserving technical vocabulary for the concept being taught.
