# Support self-efficacy through positive reinforcement, peer modeling, constructive feedback, and early mastery experiences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes concrete instructional strategies to strengthen students' self-efficacy in clinical skills training: "Supporting self-efficacy through positive reinforcement, peer modeling, constructive feedback, and early mastery experiences may help students engage more actively in clinical skills training." These strategies are framed as beneficial for all learners, not only those with high neuroticism or low self-efficacy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors or peer tutors able to deliver structured, behavior-focused feedback and model skills
#### Constraints
- Feasibility and resource implications (staff time, tutor training) were not evaluated in this study

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Physical examination skills
- Self-efficacy for hands-on clinical performance
