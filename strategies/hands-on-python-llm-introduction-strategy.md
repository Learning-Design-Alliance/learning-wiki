# Introduce LLM concepts through hands-on Python activities that progress from simple examples to increasing complexity with physics-related tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A teaching strategy in which students learn how LLMs work by actively experimenting in Jupyter Notebooks rather than by lecture: they start with simple examples and progressively increase in complexity, working in groups of two or three on physics-related tasks. The article states the activities "are designed to be used in the classroom with minimal computing resources and without the need for paid software or advanced hardware, ensuring accessibility for all students" and that they "follow the principles of active learning methods". The sequence moves from Word2Vec embeddings to GPT-2 generation, linking AI mechanisms to physics concepts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to Google Colab and the provided Jupyter Notebooks; an introductory notebook supports students with limited Python knowledge
#### Constraints
- The GPT-2 activity is limited to three parameter variations (temperature, max length, top-k) to focus and simplify the exploration

### Target Learners
- University physics students, including those without specific knowledge of machine learning

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding foundational LLM concepts such as embeddings, tokenization, and stochastic next-token prediction
- Fostering critical engagement with AI tools, including awareness of bias and factual unreliability
