# Differentiated training programs by academic background for young new farmer development.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study recommends that universities and training institutions design differentiated training programs that combine the strengths and weaknesses of different academic backgrounds. Science and engineering graduates need communication and market management improvement; economics and management graduates need technical adaptability and sustainability development; humanities graduates need agricultural production knowledge and innovation capability enhancement. The authors argue this targeted approach promotes comprehensive ability improvement more effectively than uniform training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of trainees' academic backgrounds and ability profiles before program design
#### Constraints
- Conclusions based on self-report data from Guangdong Province may be affected by regional contextual differences

### Target Learners
- Young new farmers from science and engineering, economics and management, and humanities backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- Communication skills
- Technical adaptability
- Career sustainability
- Practical innovation
