# Use an airtrack thought experiment with two sticking gliders so students discover traveler/map time differences and relativistic energy and momentum in their own terms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The article proposes that rather than simply being told the metric equation, students first ponder how to explain data on traveler/map time differences, in the spirit of Piaget and modeling workshops. It states that "an airtrack thought experiment, with adjustable kinetic-energy source (e.g. a spring) and two gliders that stick on collision, is adequate to give students data for experimental discovery (in their own terms)" of phenomena underlying the metric equation and of relativistic kinetic energy K = (γ − 1)mc2 and momentum p = mw. A student exploration might uncover the relation holding constant the sum of squares of coordinate speeds through space and time.
