# Connect quantum physics learning to everyday life, citizenship and human relationships rather than applications alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that acknowledging technological applications is not enough for students to see the societal relevance of learning quantum physics, the authors recommend giving students tools to see connections between quantum science and their everyday life, better decisions as citizens, concepts usable as analogies for other life problems, and bonding with others. They also propose exploring a 'middle' layer of human relationships between the individual and societal dimensions, and report that Quantum Rules! is adding a collective game connecting experiments with applications and controversies in society.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must go beyond showing technological applications to address why students themselves should learn the science.
#### Constraints
- Curricula with nature-of-science or history-and-philosophy-of-science objectives are very hard to assess in a standardized manner; outreach settings without fixed assessment may be more suitable for exploring societal relevance.

### Target Learners
- upper secondary school students learning quantum physics

### Target Learning Goals
- perceiving the societal and personal relevance of learning quantum physics

### Affordances
- Stuckey Three Dimension Relevance Model
