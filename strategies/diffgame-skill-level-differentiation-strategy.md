# Use DiffGame as a flexible teacher tool for skill-level differentiation and easy introduction or repetition of differentiation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends deploying DiffGame in classrooms for "both fun introduction to and easy repetition of the topic of differentiation as well as a flexible teacher tool for skill-level differentiation". Teachers can let students pick which function categories to train on, delivering level-differentiated teaching, and the short two-hour session format makes it easy to fit alongside existing instruction. The authors note this holds "given the small amount of time needed for the activity and the broad scope of the material covered".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the hosted DiffGame exercises and time-stamped game data so progress can be followed
- A blended-learning classroom session with teacher and instructors available to help and guide
#### Constraints
- A future study with a control group receiving traditional lecture and training exercises is stated as essential to determine comparable efficiency in motivation and learning curve; predicted transfer and alignment with other modes of learning will take some work

### Target Learners
- high-school students
- university students

### Target Learning Goals
- introduction to and repetition of differentiation
- skill-level differentiated practice
