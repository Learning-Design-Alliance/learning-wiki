# Time educator intervention to the anger tipping point rather than suppressing anger from the outset

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators treat anger as a signal to monitor rather than immediately suppress: science talk increased "in parallel with anger talk, until an apparent tipping point." Facilitation should target the moment tilting begins, when discussion quality plummets, rather than early moments of anger which can accompany productive argumentation. The abstract states the findings "offer guidance on the timing for educators' intervention in collaborative discourse when anger arises among students."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators need ways to recognize anger-expression codes and tilt indicators (disengagement, repeated angry interruptions, withdrawal) in real time or from recordings.
#### Constraints
- Findings are preliminary and based on two focal teams in one hybrid simulation; the Dual-Threshold Model offers only limited guidance for young students' impropriety thresholds given classroom norms and developmental differences.

### Target Learners
- middle school students working in collaborative game-based teams

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining productive collaborative discourse during emotionally charged group learning
