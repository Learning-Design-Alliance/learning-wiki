# Increase Latency Periods When Questioning Students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Latency (or "wait time") is the pause an instructor holds between posing a question and calling on a respondent, and between a learner's answer and the instructor's reply. Typical teacher latency is under one second; Rowe's classic research showed that extending it to three or more seconds lengthens and deepens student responses, increases voluntary participation, and disproportionately benefits low-achieving learners [Rowe's wait-time research on extended pauses improving response quality.](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.3660110204) [+S]. The strategy is carried out by deliberately pausing after questions, silently counting, and resisting the urge to rephrase or answer one's own question.

## Design Implications

Extended latency converts questioning from rapid recall checking into genuine [cognitive activation](../principles/cognitive-activation.md): learners must retrieve, compose, and commit to an answer rather than waiting for the teacher to supply it [Active participation during questioning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. Wait time also functions as an equity mechanism — teachers systematically give less time to students they expect to struggle, and low-achieving learners' contributions improve markedly when pauses are equalized [Rowe's wait-time research on extended pauses improving response quality.](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.3660110204) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A genuine question worth thinking about; latency cannot rescue yes/no or rapid-recall items
- A discipline technique for the instructor: silently count 3–5 seconds, maintain a neutral expectant posture, and do not rephrase mid-pause
- Some form of monitoring — a peer observer, video review, or a tally of who is called on and how long they were given — because teachers are poor judges of their own latency patterns
- A classroom norm that silence means thinking, not failure

#### Constraints
- Latency applied to trivial recall questions wastes time and can feel like stalling [-M]
- Learners with strong fluency may find a fixed long pause tedious; the pause should scale with question demand [~M]
- Without norms, extended silence can raise anxiety for some learners, who may feel singled out as "slow" [~W]
- Teachers typically revert to sub-second latency within weeks without deliberate practice or peer observation [Rowe's wait-time research on extended pauses improving response quality.](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.3660110204) [+M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Wait Time 1** — pause after the question, before a student is named; combine with no-hands strategies (cold calling after the pause) so all learners prepare an answer
- **Wait Time 2** — pause after a student answers, before the teacher responds; invites elaboration and peer response
- **Think-pair-share** — inserts structured partner talk into the latency period, lowering the stakes of the public answer
- **Think time in writing** — learners jot a response during the pause, which supports learners who need to compose before speaking

### Target Learners
- Low-achieving learners, who receive systematically less wait time under default teacher behavior and show the largest gains in response length and correctness when it is equalized [Rowe's wait-time research on extended pauses improving response quality.](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.3660110204) [+S]
- Anxious or slower-processing learners, for whom the pause removes the speed pressure that suppresses participation
- All learners benefit, but gains are largest where participation is currently dominated by a confident few

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding and reasoning: latency supports composed, elaborated answers rather than one-word recall
- Communication and academic discourse: longer, more hedged, more evidence-based student utterances
- Self-efficacy and participation: successful public contributions build confidence that supports persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Prepare questions in advance that are open enough to warrant thinking time; pair them with [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) routines.
2. Pose the question to the whole class *before* naming a respondent, then hold a silent 3–5 second count (Wait Time 1).
3. After the learner answers, pause again before evaluating or redirecting (Wait Time 2), and use [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) moves — "say more," "how did you get that?" — instead of immediate judgment.
4. Ask a colleague or use video to chart latency per learner and identify which students you are cutting short; share the data with the teacher.
5. Fold the questioning cycle into [Formative Assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md): use the extended answers as evidence for the next instructional move, and close the loop with [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md).

## Related Strategies
- [Cold Calling](cold_calling.md) — pairing cold call with prior wait time captures the equity benefit without the anxiety cost
- [Think-Pair-Share](../patterns/think-pair-share.md) — structures the latency period with peer rehearsal
- [No Hands Up](no_hands_up.md) — distributes answering after the pause rather than letting volunteers dominate

## Examples
- **Rowe's original protocol** — teachers audiotaped their lessons, charted their own latency with a peer observer, and set a target of 3+ seconds; within weeks student response length tripled and "I don't know" responses dropped sharply ([Rowe, 1974](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.3660110204)).
- **Cognitively Guided Instruction mathematics classrooms** — teachers pose a problem, give extended silent and written think time, then elicit multiple solution strategies before [Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) on the target method.
- **Socratic seminar and discussion-based humanities courses** — facilitators hold Wait Time 2 after each contribution so peers, not the teacher, respond first, enacting [Discussion-Based Learning](../patterns/discussion-based-learning.md).
