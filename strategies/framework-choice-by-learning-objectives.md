# Choose domain-general vs. domain-specific diagramming frameworks according to the relative importance of learning objectives

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advises that framework selection follow learning goals: "Given that the unique effects of a domain-specific diagramming framework were small, a good argument could be made for using more domain-general frameworks," which students could reuse across disciplines. However, because validity is a central concept in research, writing-to-learn goals may warrant the domain-specific framework despite only trend-level gains. This strategy operationalizes the finding that both frameworks aid relevance and opposition while only the specific framework aids validity writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must identify which objective (transferable argument skills vs. discipline-specific validity reasoning) matters most for their course
#### Constraints
- The unique domain-specific effects were small and only trend-level in this study; more research is needed to determine their size

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory science courses, most of whom will not become scientists

### Target Learning Goals
- argument diagramming skills that transfer across disciplines and writing about scientific validity
