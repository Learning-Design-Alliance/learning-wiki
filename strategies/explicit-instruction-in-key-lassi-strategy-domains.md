# Explicitly teach Information Processing, Selecting Main Ideas, and Time Management within the curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that practitioners use early LASSI data to inform "targeted workshops, individualized coaching, and course-embedded instruction that explicitly teaches students how to process large volumes of information, prioritize core concepts, and structure study time effectively". It further cites evidence that formal instruction in learning strategies is associated with stronger LASSI performance, supporting intentionally teaching these skills rather than assuming students develop them independently.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Early administration of the LASSI (e.g., during orientation) to identify students' strategy profiles before academic difficulty occurs
#### Constraints
- The article notes the one-time administration of the LASSI is a study limitation, as students did not complete it again later to assess changes in study strategies

### Target Learners
- medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- study strategy use
- self-regulated learning
