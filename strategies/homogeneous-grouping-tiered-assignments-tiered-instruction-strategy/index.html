Implement differentiated instruction via homogeneous grouping, tiered assignments and tiered instruction across content, process and product¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's concrete recipe: divide students into homogeneous groups by pre-test proficiency, then deliver tiered assignments and tiered instruction in content, process and product. Content was modified with supplementary materials, process used "homogeneous groups of different levels, below average, average and above average", and product used "different choices from which students can choose were given to show students' learning". This package produced the study's significant gains.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher training on the strategies (the study used three pre-treatment plus six in-treatment sessions)
  • Pre-test data to form homogeneous groups
  • Supplementary materials such as differentiated grammar sheets and reading texts

Constraints¶

  • Findings limited to a small sample, two schools, two teachers, and eight weeks of treatment

Target Learners¶

  • mixed-ability grade 8 EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • overall English achievement including reading, grammar, vocabulary and writing

Affordances¶

  • Di Four Areas Framework

Claims¶

  • Differentiated instruction had a very large effect size (Cohen's d = 2.5132) on the experimental group's achievement while one-size-fits-all had a negligible effect (d = 0.009295) [+W]
  • Differentiated instruction reduced the experimental group's score standard deviation (6.30 to 4.73) while the control group's stayed nearly unchanged (6.44 to 6.47) [+W]
  • Differentiated instruction strategies produced a statistically significant post-test difference in overall English achievement favoring the experimental group over the one-size-fits-all control group [+W]
  • The experimental group's pre-to-post achievement gain was statistically significant (t = 9.79473, P = 0.0001) [+W]
  • The one-size-fits-all method produced no statistically significant change in the control group's achievement from pre-test to post-test [+W]
