Structure mutual learning teams with heterogeneous ability within teams and equality across teams¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 theoretical, 1 causal), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes forming mutual learning teams by distributing students according to achievement scores so that each team contains a range of abilities while teams remain comparable. The 25 experimental learners were grouped into 5 teams of 5, with the highest scorers seeded first into each team. The article states this distribution "would enable achieving considerable equality among the teams in each group" while "heterogeneity of learner ability within a team is maintained as per the requirement of the Mutual learning approach."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teams must be composed based on learners' prior achievement scores, with high scorers distributed across teams rather than clustered.

Constraints¶

  • The article applies this formation procedure specifically to B.Ed students learning Optional II English in teams of five.

Target Learners¶

  • B.Ed student teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Optional II English content mastery (Advanced Grammar, Teaching Vocabulary, Reference and Study skills)

Claims¶

  • Homogeneous grouping for reading instruction and heterogeneous grouping for discussion groups each serve distinct purposes [~W]
  • A practice-framework article, quoting Johnston and Wildy (2016), reports that the literature generally finds attainment grouping (streaming) harms learning outcomes, while noting the research is contested [-W]
  • Mutual learning approach outperforms conventional instruction in academic achievement among B.Ed Optional II English students [+W]
  • Experimental group shows significant pre-to-post achievement gains under mutual learning [+W]
