Use the Bogardus Social Distance Scale as an active classroom activity to engage students in learning about prejudice and diversity¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The APA Task Force (1998) recommended a classroom activity using the Bogardus Social Distance Scale in which the instructor administers the scale, shuffles and redistributes responses for anonymity, divides the room into areas matching the seven BSDS levels, and facilitates discussion. The article tested this activity, a Display variant, and lecture-only control, finding "All three methods--lecture only, lecture plus seeing peer attitudes, and lecture plus the BSDS activity--appear to be equally effective at positively shifting student attitudes towards marginalized groups".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A target marginalized group selected by the instructor, the seven-level BSDS administered to the class, and a facilitated class discussion about placement and diversity issues

Constraints¶

  • The article found the activity was no more effective than lecture alone or merely displaying peer responses, so the activity itself cannot be credited as the causal agent

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in an introductory family science course

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding prejudice, stereotyping, and discrimination
  • attitude change toward marginalized groups

Claims¶

  • The observed attitude-change effect sizes exceed Hattie's zone of desired effects threshold, which the authors interpret as pedagogical success [+W]
  • Students' self-reported attitudes warmed over time while perceptions of campus difficulty and safety for target-group members worsened [+W]
  • Students' BSDS attitudes toward marginalized target groups become more positive from pretest to posttest and remain stable at a delayed posttest [+W]
  • Students in the Control condition held less positive BSDS attitudes averaged across time than students in the Display and Activity conditions [+W]
  • Students held more positive attitudes toward homosexuals than toward atheists on the BSDS and feeling thermometers, but attitude change was similar for both targets [+W]
  • A Time x Condition x Target interaction on the Open variable occurred only for the atheist target, where the Display condition showed no pretest-to-posttest change [+W]
  • Lecture only, lecture plus seeing peer BSDS responses, and lecture plus the BSDS activity were equally effective at shifting student attitudes [-W]
