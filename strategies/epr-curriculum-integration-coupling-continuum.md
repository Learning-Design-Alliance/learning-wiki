# Guide integration of EPRs into undergraduate healthcare programmes using the six domains along a loose-to-tight coupling continuum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that academics use the six domains of competence and associated learning outcomes to guide integration of EPRs into undergraduate healthcare programmes, since "The six domains of competence and associated learning outcomes can be used by academics to guide the integration of EPRs into undergraduate healthcare programmes." Integration can follow Kushniruk et al.'s continuum: "the first is “loose coupling”, where the EPR is demonstrated to students and assignments involve the EPR outside of the classroom, and the second is “tight coupling” where the EPR is fully integrated into teaching, assignments and assessment." The learning outcomes can guide educational initiatives at any point along this continuum, allowing gradual, ongoing development and refinement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation requires the technological resource to facilitate delivery—that is the EPR technology for educators to implement for students to interact with
#### Constraints
- The article notes changes to healthcare curricula can follow “fads” and need to be appropriately evidenced for inclusion since many programmes are already working at capacity
- Integration of the technology into teaching can be a gradual process

### Target Learners
- undergraduate healthcare students

### Target Learning Goals
- safe and effective use of EPRs
- digital competence of the graduating workforce
