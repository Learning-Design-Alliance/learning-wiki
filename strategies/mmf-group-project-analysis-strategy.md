# Run group projects spanning risk-factor and mathematical analyses, with presentations that reveal how methods complement each other

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
After the outbreak, participants clean raw datasets into analyzable form, form small groups, choose research goals, and select appropriate methods (Table 1): risk-factor studies via logistic or Poisson regression, estimation of R0 and infectious periods from contact tracing data, and stochastic SIR outbreak simulation. Groups then present results, and 'these presentations revealed methods' assumptions and utilities, clarifying how the various risk factors and mathematical methods differ from and complement each other'. Presentations are followed by evaluative questions (Box 3) scaled to student background.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Datasets from the outbreak must be available for cleaning and analysis
- Groups must choose research goals and matching methods from both traditions
#### Constraints
- The article notes many participants were well trained in dynamical epidemiology but lacked strong empirical skills, so an emphasis was placed on understanding all steps from data generation to analysis

### Target Learners
- advanced undergraduates
- graduate students
- high school students with adequate training

### Target Learning Goals
- confounding, selection bias, information bias, and random error
- survey design
- R0 as an epidemic threshold
- stochasticity in outbreaks
- verbal communication of results
