# Disassemble-and-rebuild consensus task for teaching optical conjugation and relays

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the AOSS lab activity, students first trace the light path, identify parts, and draw a quantitatively accurate ray trace and block diagram. During a break, two to four beam-expander lenses are removed, extra lenses and confocal but non-conjugate post holes are added, and the DM may be misaligned. Students must then rebuild the workbench and build team consensus on each placement: "students must realize that there is only one combination that both expands the beam to the proper size as well as re-images the aberrator onto the DM." Facilitation emphasizes articulating why beam diameters must be preserved and how relays re-image wavefront shapes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple confocal mountings and beam sizes so only one combination works; students' original drawings from the observation phase; a DM on a tip-tilt-translation stage to introduce registration
#### Constraints
- Nominally finished in 1-2 hours with students having significant background knowledge; facilitation must manage dominant talkers, disengaged learners, and unquestioned assumptions

### Target Learners
- graduate students
- college faculty
- industry professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- optical conjugation and relays
- system design reasoning
- team consensus-building and technical communication
