# Use a bilateral passport system to scale cross-departmental laboratory access without a central mandate

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Each new space joins the network through a bilateral agreement led by departmental space-owners who establish acceptable usage, and users meet each space's safety requirements "in a manner akin to acquiring visas for different countries in a passport". This allowed the programme to cross between departments and grow. The authors note it is crude but rapidly scalable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Good relationships with departmental space-owners and technicians; repeated safety inductions for each space
#### Constraints
- Requires repeated inductions for different spaces and produces heterogeneity in the kinds of access different users can have to each space

### Target Learners
- university members seeking access to laboratories outside their discipline

### Target Learning Goals
- access to interdisciplinary research resources
