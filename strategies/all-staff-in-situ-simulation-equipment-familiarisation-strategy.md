# Deliver in situ simulation including all staff, with repeated low-time-cost equipment familiarisation sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends team training through in situ simulation that includes all staff within the GP surgery, since participants reported that everyone on site should be able to use emergency equipment. Staff suggested additional opportunities for using centre-owned equipment during training sessions, and expressed that such trainings "would not need to be time-consuming in order to achieve greater familiarisation with equipment". Providing more opportunities for clinical and non-clinical staff to train together is recommended to enhance interprofessional working and reinforce understanding of others' roles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participation from both clinical and non-clinical staff
- Access to the centre's own emergency equipment during training
#### Constraints
- Offering trainings for all staff together was felt to not always be practical, in part due to difficulties in closing the centre
- Apprehension about filmed simulation may impact recruitment in future studies

### Target Learners
- Whole primary care centre teams including receptionists and administrators

### Target Learning Goals
- Emergency equipment use
- Interprofessional teamwork in medical emergencies
