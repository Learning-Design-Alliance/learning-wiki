# Adopt ICT-based multifaceted alternative assessment (peer, mentor, expert feedback) in writing classrooms over extended durations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators adopt ICT-based alternative assessments combining anonymous peer review, mentor guidance, and expert evaluation with rubric-structured iterative drafting. It states that "adopting ICT-based alternative assessments in the actual classroom over a longer duration or a semester may have a positive impact on the students". Implementation should include clear rubrics, anonymous feedback exchange, and structured feedback cycles across multiple drafts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured rubrics, orienting peer assessors, and digital infrastructure supporting the platform
#### Constraints
- Policy makers are advised that commission studies across diverse populations and contexts are needed before large-scale implementation; infrastructure limitations, digital literacy gaps, and educator preparedness challenges were evident

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in higher education writing courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved writing proficiency through formative, learner-centered assessment
