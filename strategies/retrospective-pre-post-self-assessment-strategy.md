# Use retrospective pre-course self-assessment surveys alongside OSCEs to reveal unknown deficits

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 5 studies (3 design, 1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 5 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article uses five-point Likert self-assessment surveys administered pre-course, post-course, and retrospectively after the course, asking participants to re-rate their pre-course proficiency. This design, the authors write, captures "information that trainees 'didn't know they didn't know.'" Averaged responses are stratified into low, medium, and high categories for comparison with stratified OSCE scores.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Five Likert-anchored items per joint
- Administration before, after, and retrospectively after the course
#### Constraints
- In this cohort, stratified self-assessment did not correlate with OSCE scores

### Target Learners
- post-graduate trainees
- health professions students

### Target Learning Goals
- self-assessment of clinical examination ability
