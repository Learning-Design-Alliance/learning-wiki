# Vary tense-explanation procedures to place learners along context-embedded to context-reduced conditions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on Acton (1979), the article illustrates five procedures for teaching tense: memorizing the course-book definition, analyzing examples, oral explanation with blackboard graphic presentation, contrasting tenses on the board, and searching for similar examples in a reading text. 'While the context is largely embedded in either /3/ or /5/, it remains partly reduced in /2/ and largely reduced in /1/ and /4/', with cognitive demand also varying across procedures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Procedure choice should depend on learners' current proficiency level and field independence/dependence
#### Constraints
- Less proficient, field-dependent learners 'simply need a situation revealing far more context embedded educational conditions'

### Target Learners
- Intermediate and less proficient foreign language learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding target-language tense use
- Context-search sensitivity
