# Counting Based Arithmetic Strategies

## Description
Counting based strategies are procedural methods for solving arithmetic problems by constructing quantities through counting rather than retrieving memorized facts. The developmental sequence runs from *counting all* (enumerating both addends, e.g., 3 + 5 → "1, 2, 3… 4, 5, 6, 7, 8"), to *counting on* (starting from the larger addend and counting the other: "8… 9, 10, 11, 12, 13"), to *counting up/down* for subtraction and flexible decomposition. These strategies are the dominant solution methods in early elementary arithmetic and are studied extensively through strategy-choice models such as Siegler's overlapping-waves theory [Siegler, R. S. (1987). The perils of averaging data over strategies. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 116*(3), 250–264](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.116.3.250) [+S].

## Design Implications

Counting strategies are developmentally valuable but computationally expensive: they consume working memory and execution time, and their efficiency depends on the learner's counting skill and the problem's magnitude [chunking-reduces-working-memory-load](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]. Instruction should treat them as transitional, self-adaptive procedures — not as end goals — and design practice so that learners discover the efficiency of counting on over counting all, then shift toward decomposition and retrieval as problem frequency builds associative strength in memory [Siegler, R. S., & Shipley, C. (1995). Variation, selection, and cognitive change. In T. J. Simon & G. S. Halford (Eds.), *Developing cognitive competence* (pp. 31–76). Erlbaum.] [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Solid cardinality and one-to-one correspondence (counting principles) before strategy instruction
- Concrete or visual counters ([Manipulatives](manipulatives.md), fingers, number lines) during the counting-all phase
- Problem sets that make the inefficiency of counting all salient (larger addends) so learners adopt counting on [Groen, G. J., & Resnick, L. B. (1977). Can preschool children invent addition algorithms? *Journal of Educational Psychology, 69*(6), 645–652](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.106.1.63) [+M]
- Explicit prompts to compare strategies and explain why one is faster ([Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md)) [self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Counting all becomes untenable for large operands; time and error rates grow with problem size [-S]
- Learners who remain reliant on counting past second grade show slower fact retrieval and are overrepresented among children with mathematical learning difficulties [Geary, D. C. (1993). Mathematical disabilities: Cognitive, neuropsychological, and genetic components. *Psychological Bulletin, 114*(2), 345–362](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.114.2.345) [-M]
- Drill that forces premature retrieval before counting strategies are consolidated can produce error-prone guessing [~M]
- Finger counting, while functional, is limited to small quantities and can become a hard-to-break habit [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Counting all → counting on:** modeled via [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) and [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md), then discovered through practice
- **Min strategy (count on from larger):** most children adopt this adaptively by age 5–6 without direct instruction [+S]
- **Derived facts / decomposition** (e.g., 8 + 6 = 8 + 2 + 4): the bridge from counting to retrieval, supported by [Cognitively Guided Instruction](../patterns/cognitively-guided-instruction-cgi-for-math.md)
- **Counting up for subtraction** (13 − 9 → "9… 10, 11, 12, 13") is more accurate than counting down for small differences [+M]

### Target Learners
- Kindergarten through grade 2, when counting strategies are the normative solution method [+S]
- Struggling learners who have not automatized facts — counting on is a functional compensatory strategy, but instruction should pair it with fact-building practice rather than accept it as terminal [-M]
- Less appropriate for older students, for whom continued counting signals a fluency deficit requiring intervention [Geary, D. C. (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.114.2.345) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency with single-digit addition and subtraction
- Number sense: understanding the relation between counting and cardinal quantity
- Transitional scaffolding toward fact retrieval and decomposition-based strategies

### Instructions
1. Verify counting principles (cardinality, one-to-one correspondence) with concrete tasks.
2. Model counting all with counters on a representative problem, narrating each step ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md)).
3. Pose problems with large second addends so counting all is visibly slow; prompt learners to find a faster way ([Practice](../elements/practice.md)).
4. Model and have learners rehearse counting on from the larger addend, using a number line to mark the start point.
5. Ask learners to compare strategies aloud and justify which is faster and why ([Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md)).
6. Introduce decomposition (make-a-ten) as problems exceed counting range, and build retrieval through spaced practice [spaced-repetition-improves-retention](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S].

## Related Strategies
- Fact retrieval practice — the intended endpoint; counting strategies should fade as retrieval strength builds
- Make-a-ten / decomposition strategies — the conceptual bridge out of counting
- Number line estimation activities — ground counting sequences in spatial magnitude

## Examples
- **Cognitively Guided Instruction (CGI)** — teachers diagnose each child's counting strategy from problem-solving interviews and sequence problems to advance strategy sophistication ([CGI for Math](../patterns/cgi-for-math.md)).
- **Number Talks (Parrish, 2010)** — whole-class routines in which students share how they computed (e.g., counting on vs. making tens), making strategy differences visible and norming efficient choices.
- **Assessment use:** asking children to solve addition problems and observing min-strategy use (latency scaling) is a standard diagnostic for early numeracy screening.
