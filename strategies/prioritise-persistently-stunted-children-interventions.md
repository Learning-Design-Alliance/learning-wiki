# Prioritise nutritional and learning needs of persistently stunted children in future interventions, and consider improving nutrition beyond age 2

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that while first-1000-days nutrition programmes remain crucial, interventions should also target children who remain stunted beyond early childhood. They state "The nutritional and learning needs of those persistently stunted may need to be prioritised in future interventions" and that "improving nutrition beyond age 2 may also be beneficial", because growth improvement between early and late childhood was associated with better schooling outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must address both nutritional status and learning needs of children persistently stunted through early and late childhood
#### Constraints
- The study followed children only to age 11, so longer-term associations including school dropout could not be established

### Target Learners
- Children in early and late childhood in low- and middle-income settings
- Children in primary school in sub-Saharan Africa

### Target Learning Goals
- Timely school enrolment
- Age-appropriate grade progression
- Reduced grade repetition
