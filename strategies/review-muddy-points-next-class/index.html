Open the next class with a discussion of the most frequently mentioned muddy points¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A specific implementable teaching routine following each one-minute paper collection: the instructor reads and analyzes all responses, then begins the subsequent class with a short discussion of the most frequently mentioned muddy points from the previous class. The article states this demonstrates to students that their feedback matters and can encourage active involvement in the learning process.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Regular collection and analysis of one-minute paper responses before the next class meeting

Constraints¶

  • In the study, feedback from a previous experimental class was not analyzed until after the subsequent controlled class had concluded, to avoid transferring feedback across sections

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate statistics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • clarifying poorly understood key points
  • demonstrating respect for student opinion

Claims¶

  • The one-minute paper's effect does not differ by student ability level as measured by GPA [+W]
  • The one-minute paper significantly enhances students' statistics knowledge in an OLS controlled-vs-experimental comparison [+W]
  • The one-minute paper finding is robust to using post-test minus pre-test as the dependent variable [+W]
  • Seemingly Unrelated Regression on Fall 2005 sections confirms the one-minute paper as equally effective [+W]
