# Pair rich-media training formats with objective recognition or performance tests before adoption

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because richer formats inflated judgments of learning without changing memory performance, the article's findings support evaluating any candidate rich-media instructional format with an objective outcome measure alongside learners' impressions. In the studies, judgments were collected on a 1–100 slider after study and learning was indexed with three-alternative forced-choice recognition questions probing middle and late assembly steps, which revealed the dissociation that subjective ratings alone would have hidden.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A dependent measure of actual learning (such as the recognition tests used here) must be collected in addition to judgments of learning.
#### Constraints
- The article notes its probes examined only a small subset of steps, so actual construction accuracy could have been even lower than measured.

### Target Learners
- adult trainees learning procedural assembly tasks

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural assembly knowledge and calibrated metacognitive monitoring
