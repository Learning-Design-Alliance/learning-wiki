# Design robot-assisted STEM instruction with sustained duration (over one month), instructor support, group interaction, and game-based or project-based learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), `q3`–`q4` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the meta-analytic findings, the authors recommend that robot-assisted STEM education be designed with attention to instructional context: they report that "the best intervention duration for robot-assisted STEM education was more than one month", that effects were more positive with instructor support, group-level interaction, and game-based or project-based learning, and that programming robots outperformed social robots. These recommendations translate the subgroup patterns into design guidance for educators and developers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient intervention length (more than one month) for effects to emerge
- Instructor assistance and guidance during robot-assisted activities
#### Constraints
- Most moderator variables were non-significant in omnibus tests, so these recommendations rest on descriptive subgroup comparisons
- The CT outcome showed no significant benefit, so robotics should not be assumed to build computational thinking

### Target Learners
- K-16 students in STEM courses

### Target Learning Goals
- STEM learning performance
- STEM learning attitudes
