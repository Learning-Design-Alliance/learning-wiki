# Prioritize larger sample sizes, multicentre designs, and rigorous reporting in future simulation-based CRM transfer and patient-outcome research

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review's forward-looking research recommendations: moving forward, "larger sample sizes, more multicentre studies, and studies with less risk of bias are required to provide a precise measure of the effect that simulation-based education has on healthcare provider skills in the workplace and patient outcome." The authors also suggest the simulation community commit to rigorous research reports, that researchers consider risk of bias at an early stage when designing the protocol, and that larger and multicentre studies could balance the risk of contamination. They identify frequency of retraining, skill retention, and instructional design as research priorities for Level 3 and 4 outcomes, call for universally recognized rigorous assessment tools, and suggest future research explore the cost-effectiveness of simulation CRM training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Study designs examining patient-outcome improvements require larger sample sizes and a control group; only an RCT with a control group could show that the teaching intervention is the reason for better survival
#### Constraints
- It may be challenging to design studies on simulation-based CRM without risk of bias when investigating transfer of learning to the workplace and patient outcome, since in complex organizations it is very difficult to ensure risk of contamination is nonexistent and intervention is independent of other changes

### Target Learners
- Healthcare providers across acute care specialties at all levels of practice, from trainees to staff

### Target Learning Goals
- Transfer of CRM skills to the workplace and improvement of patient outcomes following simulation-based CRM training
