# Target perceived recognition, not received recognition, when designing interventions to close gender gaps in perceived peer recognition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that intervention experiments aim to vertically shift men's and women's perceived-recognition levels to overlap, because students' varying internalization of recognition better explains perceived-recognition gaps than bias in nominations. It states that "intervention experiments should target shifts in perceived peer recognition, such that all physics students appropriately internalize recognition from their peers." In lecture contexts, where women receive significantly less recognition, interventions targeting received recognition could supplement this. The authors caution their data do not allow specific recommendations for what such interventions may look like.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be tested as experiments; the article states future research must identify causal mechanisms of the correlational findings before specific intervention designs can be recommended.
#### Constraints
- The article states shifts in received recognition would not produce overlapping lines in perceived recognition, and that a received-recognition intervention may be particularly ineffective in lab contexts where recognition is proportional; in lecture it could only supplement a perceived-recognition intervention.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- physics identity development
- perceived peer recognition
- persistence in physics
