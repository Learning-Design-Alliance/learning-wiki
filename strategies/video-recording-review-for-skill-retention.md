# Use video recording and post-session video review to support skill retention and self-directed review

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends incorporating a video recording component into clinical skills training: sessions are recorded, and after the session residents review the video content for self-directed learning. The authors state video recording offers “convenience, storability, replayability, and ease of identifying errors,” enabling residents to review and consolidate skills during spare moments and to troubleshoot recurring problems, thereby mitigating skill decay.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recorded video of the practice session available to residents after training
#### Constraints
- In the trial, all experimental-group residents reviewed their own recordings before the one-month assessment; retention advantage was shown only against traditional Peyton

### Target Learners
- resident physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural skill retention
- self-directed learning
