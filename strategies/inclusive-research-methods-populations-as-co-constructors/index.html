Make research methods inclusive by engaging studied populations as co-constructors of knowledge, including adapting EEG research for all hair types¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The editorial calls for research that develops and implements inclusive methods: involving studied populations in every step, from conceptualizing questions and developing materials to interpreting findings, so they become "co-constructors of knowledge rather than mere objects of study", with proper compensation and credit. As a case in point, it cites Adams et al.'s recommendations for conducting EEG research with participants whose thick, voluminous, and curly hair types and certain hairstyles have led to disproportionate exclusion from EEG research.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Populations involved in the research must be properly compensated and credited for their efforts.

Constraints¶

  • The editorial notes this inclusive approach is often discouraged because it is believed to interfere with objectivity and can be mistaken for bias, especially when the researcher is part of the population studied.

Target Learners¶

  • Black and Latinx students and other minority populations historically excluded from educational neuroscience research

Target Learning Goals¶

  • equitable participation in educational research
  • valid neuroscience evidence for education theory and policy

Related Strategies¶

  • Editorial vision: embrace diverse technologies and methodologies, rigor appropriate to each paper's methods, and CSCL in new contexts

  • Change stakeholder roles and investments so marginalized participants can engage as co-experts in R&D
