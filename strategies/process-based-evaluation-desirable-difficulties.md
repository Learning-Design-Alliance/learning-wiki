# Process-based evaluation strategies: desirable difficulties, think-aloud protocols, annotated drafts, and iterative revision logs that render reasoning legible

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Drawing on Bjork and Bjork, the article advocates "desirable difficulties, such as in-class, resource-restricted problem solving", making the path to the answer as important as the answer so cognitive labor occurs. Practical instantiations include think-aloud protocols, annotated drafts, and iterative revision logs, which render reasoning visible to evaluators and yield richer formative data for early intervention. The authors report pilot programs at several institutions showed more accurate representations of understanding and higher student satisfaction with fairness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Investment in faculty development, assessment infrastructure, and institutional cultures that reward pedagogical innovation over administrative convenience
#### Constraints
- The authors acknowledge such methods are more resource-intensive than single-submission grading

### Target Learners
- higher education students

### Target Learning Goals
- demonstrable cognitive labor and reasoning process
- accurate representation of student understanding
