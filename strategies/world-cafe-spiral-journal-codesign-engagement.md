# Use liberating structures — World Café rotations and Spiral Journaling — to balance power dynamics in co-design workshops

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The co-design workshops used interactive discussions based on liberating structures techniques to "balance power dynamics, foster collaborative idea generation, and facilitate structured feedback". In the World Café method, participants were divided into four groups that rotated through themed tables in 20-minute rounds, building on the insights of previous participants; café etiquette guided participation. The Spiral Journal activity prompted reflection on who should use the framework, how it might be implemented, and challenges in participants' areas. The article reports these methods supported rich dialogue and consensus-building and aligned with a 'creative PPI' model for inclusive research.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires skilled facilitators and co-facilitation by an academic/PPI representative and a HSE/healthcare professional lead
#### Constraints
- Face-to-face workshops were held in Dublin per panel preference, requiring participants to travel from across the country; some sessions were run online so time-poor staff could remain involved

### Target Learners
- multidisciplinary healthcare staff co-design participants
- patient and public partners

### Target Learning Goals
- collaborative generation and refinement of competency content
- shared decision-making and consensus-building
