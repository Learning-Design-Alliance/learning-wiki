# Three-category extension framework: optimise, understand, hypothesise

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Students who finish the core analysis are encouraged to extend their work in three named directions: improving results, deepening understanding, and testing new hypotheses. The article states these improvements "can be categorised into three general areas: enhancing their results (optimise), developing a better understanding of the results (understand), and testing novel or alternative theories and ideas based on this understanding (hypothesise)." Concrete options include selection optimisation up to genetic algorithms, Shannon-entropy binning, systematic-uncertainty studies, and hypothesising about apparatus changes such as a near detector.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Completion of the core experiment within the allocated time
- Guidance from laboratory demonstrators
#### Constraints
- Given the provided samples, there is a limit to how much selection optimisation can realistically be achieved; over-aggressive cuts let statistical uncertainties dominate and weaken exclusion power

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate physics students who complete the core analysis early

### Target Learning Goals
- open-ended enquiry and independent extension of an analysis
- machine-learning optimisation
- systematic uncertainty evaluation
- hypothesis generation
