# Improve OSCE communication assessment by training examiners, revising the scoring range, and adding items covering low communication-skill levels

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes concrete revisions to communication-skill assessment in OSCEs: it is "advisable to revise with the examiners how to adequately use the entire 5-level Likert scale for each item", possibly reducing scoring to binary pass/fail options, using single-item scores instead of a summated score across stations, and adding or modifying items that discriminate among very poor and lower-average students. These actions follow directly from the study's invariance and IRT findings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examiners must be revisited and trained on use of the full rating scale, since examiners were not formally trained to use this scale during OSCEs at the studied faculty
#### Constraints
- The article notes it remains an open question whether communication competencies can be assessed by a unique scale across different specialties and practice conditions

### Target Learners
- third- and fourth-year undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- communication skills assessment in OSCE performance-based examinations
