# Facilitate life grid interviews with thoughtful introduction, flexible movement through the grid, and reflection on blank entries

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends specific facilitation practices for realizing the life grid's benefits: introduce the grid as a collaborative narrative-building tool, encourage respondents to backtrack and cross-reference, point to columns rather than asking direct questions, prompt reflection on blank entries at the end, and avoid abbreviations so the grid stays comprehensible. The authors state that "how the life grid is structured, introduced, and used during interviews is paramount in realizing the benefits described above," and that the way researchers introduce and use the grid is highly likely to influence the depth of their data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The grid must be visible and comprehensible to respondents, with abbreviations avoided.
- Interviewers must frame grid completion as a collaborative activity and describe rows and columns before beginning.
#### Constraints
- Bell (2005) found a large, temporally broad grid with broad indicators produced quick, detail-poor interviews; the authors recommend limiting grid size and using specific direct indicators.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate STEM students

### Target Learning Goals
- Eliciting detailed, accurate retrospective narratives of student experiences in education research interviews
