# Use fused curriculum knowledge graphs to support teaching planning and student concept navigation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using its constructed graphs and derived statistics as practical supports for teaching and learning: correlation degrees help teachers "arrange appropriate teaching programs", clustering sorts disciplines into a knowledge framework, and concept rankings highlight core knowledge. The authors state the method "improves learning efficiency by helping students to understand the knowledge concepts between various courses".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Constructed graphs must first undergo data cleaning with a language error correction model and fusion to ensure entity accuracy
#### Constraints
- Derived from Electronic Information curricula data; the article presents no controlled evaluation of learning outcomes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in Electronic Information
- university teachers planning curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- macro-level understanding of course relationships
- identifying pivotal knowledge concepts
