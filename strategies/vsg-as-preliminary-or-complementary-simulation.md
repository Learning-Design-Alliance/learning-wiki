# Use virtual simulation games as a preliminary or complementary method to face-to-face simulation and clinical practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating well-designed VSGs into nursing education as preparation for or a supplement to in-person simulation and clinical placements, particularly for high-risk clinical skills and for Generation Z learners who expect flexible, repeatable practice. The trial supported this by giving the VSG group 7 days of game access after standardized training, before skills were assessed. The game's decisions "having no impact on real life, safeguard patient safety and enhance clinical skills".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-designed VSG application aligned with simulation design standards
- Technical infrastructure with reliable internet and device capacity, or an offline option
#### Constraints
- Findings from a single institution, small sample, and short-term follow-up; effectiveness may vary with the comparison condition and simulation quality

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students
- healthcare professionals in hospital education programs

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical skill performance before clinical practice
- patient safety in high-risk procedures such as urinary catheter care
