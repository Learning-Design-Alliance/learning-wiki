# Alternate on-site and remote teams every 14 days to maintain education and clinical continuity during a disruption

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Divide residents, faculty, and advanced practice providers into two teams that alternate clinical in-hospital duty every 14 days: while one team performs essential on-site clinical activities, the other works remotely with no face-to-face interaction, serving as reserve personnel and completing research and the remote curriculum. The authors state this separation "reduces the risk of disabling the entire team if quarantine is required," and the 14-day remote rotation mirrors recommended home quarantine time. A comprehensive teleconference hand-off with closed-loop communication occurs before switching duties.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough residents and faculty to staff two parallel teams at two main hospitals
- a comprehensive teleconference hand-off between teams before switching duties
#### Constraints
- Applied during the COVID-19 pandemic with elective cases cancelled and clinical volume decreased; the authors note individual programs' staffing constraints may warrant modifications

### Target Learners
- orthopedic surgery residents
- orthopedic faculty and advanced practice providers

### Target Learning Goals
- continuity of resident education and research during a system disruption
- clinical education through telemedicine consultations
