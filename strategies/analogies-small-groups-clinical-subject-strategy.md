# Teaching clinical subjects using analogies in small groups of about 6 students as a student-centered alternative to traditional instruction.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 5 studies (5 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
In an experimental cross-sectional study, the experimental group worked in small groups of 6 people each and was taught using analogies, while the control group was taught in the traditional way; both had four sessions of four hours each. Descriptive results favored the analogy group though the difference was not statistically significant. The strategy seeks to change from teacher-centered strategies to student-centered strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both groups had four sessions of four hours each to review the didactic unit
- Final evaluation with clinical cases and multiple choice questions on the same day and at the same time and place for both groups
#### Constraints
- The statistical analysis showed no significant differences between the two groups (t(64) = -.56, p>0.05)
- The average grade with which students enrolled differed: control group 8.2, experimental group 7.6

### Target Learners
- medical students studying Surgery I in the second academic course

### Target Learning Goals
- improve students' learning in a clinical subject
- integration of basic sciences with clinical sciences

### Affordances
- Analogies Promote Knowledge Transfer Medicine
