# Embed an endeavour-based gamification layer on the VLE to raise timely engagement with pre-lecture activities in flipped courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends embedding a gamification strategy on the virtual learning environment hosting flipped-learning materials, awarding points for participation rather than achievement. Full points were given only for activities completed before the associated session, with half-points thereafter, explicitly targeting timely engagement. The authors conclude that "gamification strategies, when effectively implemented, can have a positive impact on student motivation and engagement" and that implementation should be considered across a student's programme of study to ensure a diversity of learning approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time and attention from staff to set up the system, including allocating experience points to tasks and manually resetting points after deadlines
#### Constraints
- Potential downsides include increased competition, task evaluation difficulties, demotivation for students placed low on the leaderboard, and unknown effects if the novelty of a leaderboard wears off or gamification is implemented widely across multiple courses

### Target Learners
- Year 1 undergraduate students in large flipped mathematical sciences courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Motivating completion of out-of-class pre-lecture work before scheduled sessions
