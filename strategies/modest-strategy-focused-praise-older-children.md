# Phrase praise modestly and focus on strategies rather than effort to prevent ability-denigrating inferences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 2 review), `q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, for older children (from about age 10) who understand that ability and effort are compensatory, teachers "phrase their praise modestly and focus the praise on children's strategies rather than their effort". Inflated praise, even without mentioning effort, implicitly signals that a student had to work hard because of low ability, leading peers to see the student as less smart. Focusing praise on strategies (e.g., "You found a good way to do this") is offered as a way to avoid these ability-denigrating inferences.
