# Use video clips portraying conceptual hurdles and mathematical confusion to provoke inquiry in professional development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The LTG materials select unedited clips from unstaged lessons across grade levels, locations, and student populations, deliberately choosing segments that "typically represent a conceptual hurdle or portray some degree of mathematical confusion, based on the expectation that they are likely to provoke inquiry and discussion within the PD setting." Clips offer a window into content, student thinking, and pedagogical moves. Facilitators pair clip selection with focus questions and probes so viewing leads to substantive examination of classroom interactions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Focus questions and probes that guide viewing and analysis toward subject matter content
#### Constraints
- Without guidance such as an analytic framework and explicit tasks, teachers watching videos rarely address subject matter content

### Target Learners
- Mathematics teachers serving grades 5-10

### Target Learning Goals
- Discriminating how learners comprehend subject matter and developing instructional responses to student errors
