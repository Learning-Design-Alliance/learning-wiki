Use differential item functioning analysis and psychometric equating studies to validate FCI gender fairness¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article proposes that forthcoming studies apply differential item functioning (DIF) analysis to establish whether FCI gender gaps reflect measurement variance, noting that item-level data are required. It states that "FCI gender gaps will indicate the potential of measurement variance must be validated by the lens of psychometric studies such as in differential item functioning (DIF) analysis". It further recommends an equating study comparing a reformed FCI version with the original, examining item and person parameter invariance using classical test theory (CTT) and item response theory (IRT).

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Item-level FCI data, which the article notes are out of scope for the present meta-analysis
  • Psychometric frameworks such as classical test theory (CTT) and item response theory (IRT)

Constraints¶

  • The present meta-analysis is limited to summarizing average FCI scores and cannot establish conclusions at item level

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate physics students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • valid and fair measurement of Newtonian mechanics understanding across gender

Claims¶

  • Mean FCI scores differ significantly between male and female students at a moderate magnitude favoring male students (g = 0.61) across international PER studies [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use a decision template weighing assumption fit, DIF uniformity, simultaneity, significance testing, and ease of use when choosing a DIF method for polytomous performance assessments
  • Use DFIT indices with empirically established cutoffs and iterative linking to screen polytomous tests for DIF and DTF
