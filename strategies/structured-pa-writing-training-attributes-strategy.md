# Structured P&A writing training with authentic tasks, low-stakes practice, genre discussion, expert collaboration, and iterative feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structured writing education specific to physics and astronomy, listing attributes "identified as helpful and desired by the community": authentic writing tasks (ideally piggybacking on a paper in progress), low-stakes practice, discussion of genre for technical and popular audiences, guidance from both writing and disciplinary experts, constructive prompt iterative feedback, regular offering, good advertising, and being required or incentivized. These echo best practices from disciplinary education research cited by the author.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training should be specifically focused on physics and astronomy and the writing tasks of P&A careers, such as proposals and fellowship applications
#### Constraints
- The article notes many lab instructors are not currently trained to provide writing education, and further work is needed to understand mentors' needs as writing instructors

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate physics and astronomy students

### Target Learning Goals
- discipline-specific scientific writing, including research papers, grant and telescope-time proposals, and science communication to the public
