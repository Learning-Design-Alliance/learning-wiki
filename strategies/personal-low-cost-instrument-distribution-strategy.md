# Distribute personal, low-cost rheometry instruments to each student to accelerate engagement in advanced fluid mechanics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends giving each student personal, indefinite access to laboratory-level instrumentation by distributing printable flexure devices costing roughly 25 cents each. Used in MIT course 2.341J, UMass Polymer 704, UIUC TAM 534/498 and a Cargèse summer school with over 70 participants, the authors report that distributable tools increased engagement, observing "participants engaging with the subject matter differently".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruments must be sufficiently inexpensive (e.g., much less than 1 USD) such that each student can independently ask scientific questions and keep the instrument for use outside the classroom.
- Designs must be operationally simple so participants focus on fluid measurements rather than debugging software or sweeping parameter space.
