# Explicitly surface the rubber sheet analogy's misconceptions at the start of the activity and repeat the corrections throughout

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The activity opens by introducing the analogy's simplifications: that any mass or energy deforms space-time, that the sheet is a two-dimensional representation, and that curvature has no privileged direction, so there is no up and down. The authors state "We believe that bringing these misconceptions to light helps participants to overcome them", an idea they say was confirmed by the questionnaire results. They also recommend that "a greater attention must be paid in ﬁghting this idea throughout the activity with the rubber sheet", since the big-mass misconception persisted for 34% of students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The limitations of the analogy must be discussed explicitly, ideally at the beginning of the activity
- Corrections of the big-mass misconception should be emphasized more than once during the activity
#### Constraints
- Even with explicit treatment, the big-mass misconception was not solved completely in this implementation

### Target Learners
- High school students learning gravity and General Relativity

### Target Learning Goals
- Avoiding misconceptions about the direction of gravity and the sources of space-time deformation
