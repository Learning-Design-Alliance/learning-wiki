# Use matrix grading on team assignments to penalize free-riding and reward carrying the team

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 4 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 4 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Matrix grading is applied to team-produced assignments (research briefings and proposal parts): each student tells the TA what percentage each teammate contributed beyond their own work, penalizing non-participants and rewarding students who carry the team. The authors apply it to only a percentage of the assignment grade because "applying it to the entire grade had a too-big effect". Severely lopsided results trigger a TA-led team conversation during tutorial to realign expectations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A TA who receives the contribution percentages and initiates conversations when grading comes back severely lopsided
#### Constraints
- Applied to only a percentage of the assignment's total grade, not the entire grade

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students working in research teams of four

### Target Learning Goals
- effective teamwork and interpersonal communication
