# Choose complexity and difficulty measures based on the intended use case rather than seeking a single best measure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that rather than trying to find the correct measure of item difficulty or complexity, it is meaningful to focus on useful measures that enable improving learning systems. The usefulness of measures depends on the specific goal: some applications need complexity measures that predict difficulty well where predictive accuracy is the primary criterion, while other cases benefit from multiple interpretable measures that are not necessarily strongly correlated. Each use case requires measures with slightly different properties.
