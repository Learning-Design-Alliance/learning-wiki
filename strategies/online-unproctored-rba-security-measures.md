# Security and motivation measures for online unproctored RBA administration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 review, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a set of implementable measures for administering a research-based assessment online without a proctor: presenting it as a low-stakes survey, awarding credit for participation rather than correctness, using a time limit, showing each item in its own browser window, preventing backtracking, and not showing answers afterward. The article states that security "may be addressed by properly motivating the use of the RBAs with students, using a time limit, limiting the number of items students can view at one time". A short embedded video explained the purpose and credit basis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A survey/quiz platform that can show one item per window, block backtracking, and enforce a time limit
- Reminder emails and course credit for participation to sustain participation rates
#### Constraints
- Randomizing answer choices was not used, because validation of items only holds for the versions used during validation
- The platform allowed students to leave the survey open indefinitely, though no new responses could be entered after time expired

### Target Learners
- college students in large-enrollment calculus-based introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- quantitative reasoning in physics contexts
