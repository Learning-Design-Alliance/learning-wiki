# Use regulated, supervised interactive tablet games as a low-cost stimulation support for early childhood educators

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that, if used in a regulated way, interactive technologies may provide stimulation support "serving as a useful tool for early childhood educators working in educational environments where human resources are scarce". They argue a tablet-based game is low-cost, attractive for young children, and familiar to parents and caregivers. The strategy is implementable as brief individual game sessions in nursery rooms with minimal educator oversight and a tutor application for remote monitoring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Use regulated in time and supervised by caregivers or educators
- A supervising educator to oversee sessions and evaluate vocalizations
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that screen-time greater than 1-5 h per day may be associated with cognitive difficulties, and that further studies with larger sample sizes are mandatory

### Target Learners
- Toddlers and preschoolers from low-income families in public preschool educational centers

### Target Learning Goals
- Vocabulary acquisition
- Pre-reading letter-sound knowledge
- Verbal communication and speech comprehension
