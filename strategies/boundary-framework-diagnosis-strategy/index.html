Use the four-boundary framework to diagnose a community and focus resources on the aspects most important to its goals¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that designers, teachers, and leaders use the boundary framework to evaluate a specific community, identify where it is in its development, and decide which boundary needs strengthening. The authors write that "its designers and leaders can focus on building the aspects of the community (conceptual congruity, functional collaboration, presence awareness, or relational cohesion) that are most important for achieving its goals". For example, an online community may lack presence while sharing conceptual boundaries of vision.

Context¶

Requirements¶

Constraints¶

  • The authors caution the framework is for sharpening and focusing a community's possibilities, not for limiting its potential

Claims¶

  • Learning-community research suffers from unclear and inconsistent definitions of community [+W]
  • Access boundaries of learning communities can be physical or virtual, with the required amount and nature of presence still undefined [+W]
  • Functional boundaries: learning communities can be defined by what members do together, which is particularly useful for online communities [+W]
