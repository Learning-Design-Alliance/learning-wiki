# Pair surgical simulation practice with active bilateral M1 tDCS to accelerate technical skill acquisition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend augmenting surgical simulation training with active tDCS over bilateral primary motor cortex delivered concurrently with practice, based on their finding that bM1 stimulation produced significantly greater learning than sham. They argue that given tDCS's safety profile and ease of administration, "this novel technique could be a useful and practical augmentation to current technical skill training within surgical residency", potentially offsetting restricted training time under work-hour limits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stimulation must be delivered concurrently with task practice using parameters in the range previously reported to affect motor skill training (2 mA, 20 min)
#### Constraints
- The authors state results are preliminary and call for replication, extension to surgical residents with higher baseline skill, and determination of generalizability beyond the single bimanual task studied

### Target Learners
- General surgery residents and novices in surgical simulation laboratories

### Target Learning Goals
- Accelerated acquisition of basic laparoscopic technical skills within competency-based curricula
