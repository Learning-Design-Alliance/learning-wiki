# Develop tests of native aptitude for each school subject separately and question the Accomplishment Quotient

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Given the low intercorrelations among school subjects, the article recommends attacking "the problems of discovering tests of native ability for each subject separately," with tests for native aptitude for arithmetic, writing, drawing, spelling, and so on. It also argues this finding "raises a question with regard to the validity of the concept of the 'Accomplishment Quotient'" and similar practices assuming slight specialization, and suggests correlating tests with abilities developed to the limit rather than with school-practice-dependent achievement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assumes a specialization of native abilities among school subjects, for which the article says there is evidence though greater abundance exists for the former view
#### Constraints
- The article notes its data represent merely the facts for one school, and that correlations would probably not rise near +1.00 even if each subject were developed to the limit

### Target Learners
- elementary school pupils in grades I to VIII

### Target Learning Goals
- measurement of achievement and aptitude in reading, arithmetic, spelling, and writing
