Use argumentation activities built around claims, reasons, supporters and rebuttals tied to curriculum achievements, with students designing experiments¶

Description¶

The article describes argumentation-based teaching implemented through researcher-prepared worksheets aligned to the 7th Grade Science Curriculum, using "expression table, use of evidence, concept cartoon, competing theories" activities. In a week-10 example, students were given a story with a claim and asked to explain whether they agreed "by providing reasons, supporters and rebuttals", then designed group experiments and discussed results. The researcher acted only as a guide while students discussed and presented ideas.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Worksheets reviewed by two experts and aligned to curriculum achievements
  • Preparatory activities giving students general knowledge about creating arguments
  • Science classroom and laboratory access

Constraints¶

  • Students reported difficulty recording ideas on worksheets in writing and disliked worksheet completion; activities were described as time consuming

Target Learners¶

  • 7th grade secondary school science students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • scientific process skills
  • constructing arguments and counter-arguments
  • experiment design

Claims¶

  • Argumentation-based teaching shows no significant effect on 7th graders' scientific process skills compared to the current program when pretest scores are controlled [-M]
  • Experimental and control groups differed significantly in scientific process skills at pretest [~M]
  • Students found argumentation-based activities fun and instructive, with experimenting the most-liked part [+W]
  • Students reported learning to construct arguments and design experiments during the activities [+W]
  • Most students wanted argumentation-based activities continued, mainly in science lessons [+W]
