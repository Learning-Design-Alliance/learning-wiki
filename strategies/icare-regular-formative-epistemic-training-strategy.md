# Use iCARE regularly as a formative assessment and epistemic training tool in inquiry-based teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends administering iCARE at the end of each class period or learning section so teachers can formatively assess cognitive conflict and anxiety in everyday teaching. Because its four sections "follow the flow of a progressive activation of meta-cognitive self-reflections of one’s learning," repeated use prompts students to reflect on learning processes and can "help train students to think more on the metacognitive side of their learning." It is also positioned as a tool in professional development for pre-service and in-service teachers learning to deliver inquiry-based instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regular repeated administration (e.g., weekly) so students become fluent in reflecting on their learning
- An inquiry-based or group-learning course context with which the context items align
#### Constraints
- Self-report method subject to the drawbacks of that type of method
- Students select only one reaction option, which limits the richness of feedback

### Target Learners
- University students in inquiry-based science courses
- Pre-service and in-service K-12 teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Formative assessment of cognitive conflict and anxiety
- Metacognitive and epistemic awareness of the constructive nature of knowledge
