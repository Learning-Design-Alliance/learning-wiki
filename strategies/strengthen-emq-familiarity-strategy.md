# Strengthen students' familiarity with EMQs before high-stakes use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's conclusion recommends preparing students for the more complex EMQ format, since unfamiliarity with its long scenarios and many similar options lowers performance and perception. It states that "strengthening students’ familiarity with EMQs can help improve the quality of assessment." This implements the finding that students performed weakest on EMQs and that prior familiarity with assessment tools shapes performance, as the authors note citing Nalini et al.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Exposure to the standard EMQ structure (theme, lead-in, 2–5 stems, 8–10 options) before summative use
#### Constraints
- Based on a single-institution study with 29 students; perception did not predict performance, so familiarity alone may not raise scores

### Target Learners
- senior dental students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- differential diagnosis
