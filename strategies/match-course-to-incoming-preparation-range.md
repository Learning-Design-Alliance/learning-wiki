# Better match introductory course design and resources to the range of incoming student preparation to improve outcomes and eliminate demographic performance gaps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends addressing the variation in incoming preparation rather than demographic status per se. It suggests adjusting course level and pace, which currently appear targeted towards better-prepared students, and providing greater resources such as more instructor time or additional course sequence options. As the authors put it, "It is plausible that adjusting the course level to better match the preparation of the less prepared students would improve their performance and reduce the sensitivity to preparation, while having a very small impact on the learning of the best prepared."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors or departments need access to measures of incoming preparation, such as math SAT/ACT percentile scores and concept inventory pre-scores, to characterize the preparation range of their students
#### Constraints
- The article frames these as potential suggestions for future work, not tested interventions; the underlying analysis is correlational and does not demonstrate that weak preparation causes low exam performance

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory calculus-based physics (physics 1) across the full range of incoming preparation, including students from weaker K-12 STEM backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- improve final exam performance and success for all students across the preparation distribution
- eliminate demographic performance gaps in introductory physics
