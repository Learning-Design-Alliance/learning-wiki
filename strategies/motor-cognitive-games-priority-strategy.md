# Promote gross motor activities and games that also demand cognitive processes, particularly for boys

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers and educational policy promote physical activities combining motor competence with cognitive demand, especially for boys whose GMC–AA link ran through EFs. It states that "the promotion of physical activity and games that involve both motor competence and cognitive demand should be a priority for teachers" and that promoting gross motor skills involving EFs in boys "should be a priority for educational policies in early ages."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Motor tasks must require cognitive processes (executive functions) alongside gross motor execution to plausibly influence academic achievement via EFs
#### Constraints
- Recommendation derives from a cross-sectional mediation analysis; the authors state it prevents causality and directionality inference and call for intervention studies

### Target Learners
- Children aged 8–10 in primary school (fourth and fifth grades)

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement in language and mathematics
- Executive functions (inhibitory control, cognitive flexibility, working memory)
- Gross motor competence
