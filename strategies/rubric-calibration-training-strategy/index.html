Train faculty and students in rubric use with calibration strategies to maximize agreement and self-assessment accuracy¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that "Faculty and student training in rubric use is essential to maximize inter-rater agreement and minimize subjective bias, particularly in self- and peer-assessment contexts." It specifies calibration strategies such as video benchmarking and reflective exercises to synchronize evaluator interpretation and improve self-assessment accuracy. Institutions are advised to embed rubrics routinely in OSCEs, clinical skill sessions, and reflective portfolios, and to invest in structured training programs on rubric interpretation for both faculty and students.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Structured training programs for both faculty and students on rubric interpretation
  • Calibration mechanisms such as video benchmarking and reflective exercises

Constraints¶

  • The review notes rubric development and application can be time-consuming, and faculty reported loss of flexibility for complicated cases

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate dental students
  • postgraduate dental students
  • dental faculty assessors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • inter-rater reliability
  • accurate self-assessment
  • grading consistency
  • student metacognition

Claims¶

  • Faculty calibration with instructional rubrics yields high inter-rater reliability, rising after repeated calibration [+W]
  • Organized training with rubrics reduces the gap between student self-assessment and educator judgment [+W]
  • Students tend to overestimate their own performance when self-assessing with rubrics [+W]
  • Exemplar-based calibration is expected to align student ratings with expert ratings at the criterion level [+W]
