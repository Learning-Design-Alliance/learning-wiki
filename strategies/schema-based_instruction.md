# Schema-Based Instruction

## Description
Schema-based instruction involves teaching learners to recognize and understand the underlying structure (schema) of problems, particularly word problems in mathematics. By identifying the problem structure, learners can better understand the relationships between the problem's elements and select appropriate strategies to solve it. This strategy emphasizes identifying key words associated with different operations and modeling the problem structure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional materials with various word problems, explicit teaching of problem structures and associated keywords, modeling of problem-solving strategies, and opportunities for practice.
#### Constraints
- May not be suitable for all types of mathematical problems, and the over-reliance on keywords can sometimes lead to misinterpretation of problems. Requires explicit instruction and practice to be effective.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Middle school learners, particularly those struggling with mathematical word problems

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve problem-solving skills, enhance comprehension of word problems, facilitate the translation of word problems into mathematical equations, develop strategic thinking in problem-solving.

### Affordances
- Provides a structured approach to problem-solving, enhances understanding of mathematical concepts, promotes active engagement with word problems, and can improve confidence in solving mathematical problems.

### Personalization
- The strategy can be adapted to different types of word problems and mathematical operations. Scaffolding can be provided for learners who struggle with identifying problem structures, and more challenging problems can be presented to advanced learners.
