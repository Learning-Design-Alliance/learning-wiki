# Pair DCBL modules for URTI and diarrhoea stewardship with complementary strategies such as communication skills training, shared decision-making simulations, or reflective debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the DCBL format aligned best with discrete, guideline-amenable decision points (as in the UTI case), the authors recommend that for URTI and diarrhoea, "complementary strategies such as communication skills training, shared decision-making simulations, or reflective debriefing may be needed alongside the DCBL module". This tailors instructional design to domain-specific reasoning demands.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Domain-specific analysis of which reasoning patterns the branching-logic format can support
#### Constraints
- URTI stewardship primarily requires withholding antibiotics and managing diagnostic uncertainty, a reasoning pattern less amenable to structured feedback alone

### Target Learners
- Third- and fourth-phase MBBS learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Antibiotic stewardship for URTI and diarrhoea
- Managing diagnostic uncertainty about viral versus bacterial aetiology
