# Foreground SDG 12 with a functional definition of sustainability and teach the SDG 8–12 conflict across disciplines as a necessary minimum of sustainability education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The chapter's central recommendation is "That SDG 12, centered on our functional definition of sustainability, be foregrounded in education," with the SDG 8–12 conflict emphasized "as a portal to thinking about alternatives." Table 1 maps the argument's components to existing topics: material footprints in data exercises, exponential growth beyond math class, thermodynamic efficiency limits in physical science, adding "refuse" to the three Rs, limits to recycling, and documenting scant decoupling success.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Education should extend beyond students to citizens, policymakers, and government and corporate leaders
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that extolling recycling without discussing its inherent limits risks co-option to justify incremental change

### Target Learners
- high school and college students
- citizens, policymakers, and government and corporate leaders

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the growth–sustainability contradiction
- considering alternatives that embrace human well-being and long-term sustainability
