# Run the feedback session with teacher-led recommendations: clear introduction, explain the feedback dilemma, encourage open discussion, avoid the word 'feedback' for student roles, and end with a debrief

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Box 1 sets out what the teacher can do to make feedback a success: "Providing a clear introduction at the beginning with explanations on aims and learning outcomes may promote students' participation"; elaborating on the feedback dilemma and students' expectations; encouraging open discussion in other sessions; avoiding the words 'feedback' or 'peer feedback' when introducing students' roles, since the word may carry socio-culturally negative connotations; and providing a short debrief at the end discussing challenges to improve the next session. These recommendations arise directly from the study's identified challenges.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher to introduce the session, frame student roles and facilitate discussion
- Students with prior training in giving and receiving constructive feedback
#### Constraints
- Benefits and challenges identified may differ with a more experienced group, as the study's participants were unfamiliar with most components of the process

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- students in formative practical skills assessments

### Target Learning Goals
- giving and receiving constructive peer feedback
- self-reflection on performance
- open collaborative discussion
