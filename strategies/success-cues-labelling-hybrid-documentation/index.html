Provide success cues, component labelling, and hybrid documentation combining video, still images, and step-by-step diagrams for buildable robot kits¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends three concrete design moves for open hardware learning platforms. First, hardware feedback can be explicit: designers can provide success cues, such as an audible click, to help users distinguish a secure fit from a potential break. Second, labelling actual parts would reduce first-time builders' mental load during assembly. Third, remaining friction should be addressed through hybrid documentation that combines video, still images and step-by-step diagrams, with static anchors to avoid the constant backtracking of a video-only format.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Documentation must pair video guidance with static step-by-step diagrams so users can re-establish their position after pausing to act.

Constraints¶

  • The recommendations respond to friction observed with an alpha-stage video-only guide and snap-fit resistance ambiguity; the article does not report a test of the proposed fixes.

Target Learners¶

  • first-time builders of open-source hardware kits
  • university students in social robotics projects

Target Learning Goals¶

  • successful independent assembly of open-source robots
  • reduced mental load during wiring and part identification

Claims¶

  • First-time builders completed the v4.1 build in a mean of 39.1 minutes with SUS 74.6 (Good), but showed higher assembly Mental Demand than developers and tactile fragility concerns around snap-fit components [~W]
