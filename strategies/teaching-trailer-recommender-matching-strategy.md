# Use brief teaching trailers to build online recommender systems that match students to professors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes an online system, similar to commercial recommender systems, in which students view and rate brief videos of professors' teaching and receive individualized feedback about which professors they would find especially effective. The authors state these findings 'support the possibility of developing online systems that would provide personalized recommendations that specific students take courses from specific professors.' Forecasting uses each student's trailer ratings with professor averages and relationship scores.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Brief video trailers of professors' teaching that students can rate before enrolling
- Ratings from multiple students per professor so professor and relationship effects can be isolated
#### Constraints
- Trailers were carefully constructed and might be too expensive to justify cost-effectiveness
- Results are from 40-min psychology lectures and may not generalize to semester-long courses or other fields
- Professors were confounded with lecture topic

### Target Learners
- college students in introductory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- matching students to professors under whom their learning and satisfaction are unusually effective
