# Repurpose labs toward experimentation skills and nature-of-science goals rather than verifying physics content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using labs for goals uniquely served by hands-on work: "labs may be more eﬀectively used for focusing on skills that can be uniquely learned through the activity associated with labs", such as coordinating equipment, analyzing data and its variability, navigating teamwork, and communicating results. It points instructors to the AAPT recommendations for undergraduate physics lab curricula as a starting point, and argues content reinforcement is more efficiently achieved with short enhanced demonstrations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scheduling labs before instruction and training lab instructors to facilitate constructive and interactive engagement, which the article notes are complicated and non-trivial
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that removing extraneous-load activities may remove valuable aspects of experimentation, and that modifications to verification labs while maintaining verification goals may not be worth the effort

### Target Learners
- introductory physics undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- experimentation skills
- nature of science
- critical thinking
- data analysis and communication
