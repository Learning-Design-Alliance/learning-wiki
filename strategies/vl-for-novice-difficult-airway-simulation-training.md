# Use videolaryngoscopes when training novices to intubate difficult airway scenarios in simulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 10 claims (7 for, 3 mixed) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 10 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's own recommendation is that non-physician novices facing difficult airway situations benefit from videolaryngoscopes: "McGrath VL and Pentax AWS compared to Macintosh laryngoscope show advantages with novices in easiness and first attempt success rate in difficult airway situations." Simulation curricula for novice intubators should therefore include VL practice for cervical-immobilized or difficult-airway variants, while noting no clinically significant time advantage was shown.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Novice trainees without prior intubation experience
- Manikin simulation including difficult airway variants such as cervical immobilization
- Availability of McGrath VL or Pentax AWS alongside Macintosh laryngoscope
#### Constraints
- Advantages were demonstrated in a manikin model and quiet operating room; the authors caution results "may not be replicated in real patients" and call for studies in combat or disaster settings

### Target Learners
- novice anesthesia nurses
- novice prehospital airway providers

### Target Learning Goals
- first-attempt intubation success in difficult airway scenarios
