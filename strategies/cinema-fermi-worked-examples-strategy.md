# Give instructors worked Cinema Fermi Problem examples to develop skill in recognizing Fermi problems and making Fermi calculations in their own courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's stated intent is 'to provide instructo rs with examples they can use to develop skill in recognizing Fermi problems and makin g Fermi calculations in their own courses.' The strategy is to screen popular films, identify scenes with extractable quantitative claims, and work the resulting estimation problems in class, with the goal of training students in critical thinking and reasoning. The article supplies four fully worked examples as models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must screen movies and work the problems themselves beforehand; the authors report having 'screened hundreds of movies and worked tens of such problems' for their course.
#### Constraints
- Class problems are deliberately more sophisticated than introductory warm-ups because the goal is to train students in critical thinking and reasoning.

### Target Learners
- non-science general education undergraduates in physical science and astronomy courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Fermi estimation skill
- critical thinking about scientific claims in media
