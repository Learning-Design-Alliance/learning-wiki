# Five-step iterative process for designing, implementing, and monitoring PLAI-LOs via Backward Design

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article prescribes a recurring five-step cycle: assess the state of GenAI technology and practice; use "a Backward Design Process [27]" for program leaders to translate developments into PLAI-LOs using the revised Bloom's taxonomy; have instructors apply PLAI-LOs to courses and tag learning artifacts; develop artifact-level GenAI policies; and report student success on tagged artifacts back to program leaders for monitoring. The cycle "repeats from step 1 in the following academic term," aligned with institutional calendars.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The design, implementation, and assessment cycle must be "recurring and iterative, with a time frame aligned with institutional calendars to avoid cycles of confusion among faculty and students"
#### Constraints
- The authors note full course redesigns "may become outdated quickly given the rapid pace of GenAI development," which is why the process ties outcomes to individual artifacts for gradual intervention

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in degree programs

### Target Learning Goals
- program-level GenAI readiness defined, implemented, assessed, and revisited as the technology changes
