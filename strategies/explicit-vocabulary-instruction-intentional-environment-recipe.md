# Teach target vocabulary explicitly with read-aloud, repetition, context guessing, word lists, and multi-slide word-meaning-L1-picture presentations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The experimental treatment combined explicit instruction with an intentional learning environment. The researcher told students about the importance of vocabulary, had them attend to bolded words, read words aloud for repetition and spelling, prompted guessing from context before giving the meaning, built a board word list, and projected four-slide sequences pairing each word with its textbook meaning, contextual sentences, the Tamil (L1) equivalent, and a picture.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must be made aware they are involved in learning new words (intentional learning environment)
#### Constraints
- The vocabulary test at the end of the session was not disclosed to students in advance

### Target Learners
- fifth-grade second language learners of English

### Target Learning Goals
- reception of prescribed target vocabulary

## Claims

- [Explicit instruction combined with an intentional learning environment produced higher self-reported receptive knowledge of target vocabulary in fifth-grade ESL learners than the same lesson taught by another teacher](../claims/explicit-instruction-intentional-learning-improve-vocabulary-reception.md) [+M]
- [After regular instruction on Unit 1, the class's receptive knowledge of the prescribed vocabulary was partial, averaging 14.23 out of 40 on the VKS](../claims/partial-reception-unit1-vks-average.md) [+M]
