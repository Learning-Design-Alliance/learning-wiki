# One-on-One Conference

## Description
One-on-one conferences involve providing individualized feedback to a learner in a private setting. This approach allows for personalized attention, facilitates open communication, and provides an opportunity for the learner to ask questions. Conferences are typically structured around a shared artifact (a draft, a project, a portfolio) and a small set of goals, with the learner doing substantial talking — explaining their reasoning, self-assessing, and setting next steps — rather than passively receiving the instructor's evaluation.

## Design Implications

Conferences concentrate the conditions under which feedback works best: it is dialogic, task- and process-focused, and delivered at a moment when the learner can act on it [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. Because the instructor can probe understanding and adjust support in real time, conferencing is a natural vehicle for contingent scaffolding — support that responds to the learner's current state rather than a predetermined script [Scaffolding that is contingent on learner responses improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]. The private, relational setting also builds the learner's belief that effort will pay off, supporting persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Protected time and a private (or at least low-distraction) setting; short, frequent conferences often beat long infrequent ones
- A concrete artifact or performance to anchor the conversation — feedback attached to specific work outperforms general praise [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- A structure that makes the learner an active participant: self-assessment first, then instructor input, then jointly set next steps ([Self-Assessment](../elements/self-assessment.md), [Goal-Setting](../elements/goal-setting.md))
- Instructor skill in [Active Listening](active_listening.md) — questioning and eliciting before telling

#### Constraints
- Highly time-intensive; at scale, conference time crowds out other instruction, so frequency must be rationed or the format adapted (peer conferences, small-group conferences, brief check-ins)
- Effectiveness drops when conferences become one-way evaluation delivery; students who perceive grades as the focus engage superficially [Grades can undermine the learning-oriented response that comments are meant to produce.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [~M]
- Power asymmetry can suppress honest disclosure; without deliberate rapport-building, learners may hide confusion rather than surface it
- Benefits depend on follow-through — a conference that ends without a concrete next action rarely changes behavior

#### Implementation Variability
- **Writing conferences** (Atwell, Calkins): brief, frequent, focused on one revision move per conference
- **Reading conferences**: learner discusses independent reading, instructor checks comprehension and coaches strategy use
- **Advising/mentoring conferences**: longer horizon, focused on goals and self-regulation rather than a single artifact
- **Peer conferences**: learners use the same structure with each other, trading instructor time for rehearsal of evaluative judgment
- **Micro-conferences / check-ins**: 2–5 minute conversations during work time, preserving individualization at scale

### Target Learners
- Developing writers and other learners producing complex, revisable work, who benefit most from process-level guidance [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- Learners with weak self-regulation, for whom the conference externalizes monitoring and planning [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Anxious or disengaged learners who will not ask questions in public settings; the private format lowers the social cost of admitting confusion
- Less necessary for learners with strong self-regulation, who can extract the same information from written feedback

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving the quality of complex products (writing, projects, portfolios) through iterative revision
- Self-regulated learning: planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's own work [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Metacognitive and reflective skill: articulating one's own reasoning and learning needs
- Relationship-building and motivation, particularly self-efficacy [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Schedule and anchor.** Set a predictable cadence and ask the learner to bring a specific artifact and one question ([Check-Ins](../principles/check-ins.md)).
2. **Open with the learner's self-assessment.** "Tell me about your process — what went well, what's giving you trouble?" ([Self-Assessment](../elements/self-assessment.md), [Reflection](../elements/reflection.md)).
3. **Probe before advising.** Use questions to locate the actual source of difficulty before offering input ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md)).
4. **Give one or two process-level feedback points.** Focus on strategy and next moves, not the person or the grade [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S] ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)).
5. **Co-set a concrete next step.** End with one specific, achievable action the learner owns ([Goal-Setting](../elements/goal-setting.md)).
6. **Follow up.** Open the next conference by checking on the agreed action, closing the feedback loop ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md)).

## Examples
- **Nancie Atwell's writing workshop** (Center for Teaching and Learning, Maine): the teacher holds brief daily conferences with individual student writers, focusing each exchange on a single revision decision. See [https://www.c-t-l.org](https://www.c-t-l.org)
- **Lucy Calkins' Units of Study** (Teachers College Reading & Writing Project): conferring routines with scripted structures ("research-decide-teach") are embedded in published K–8 curricula. See [https://www.unitsofstudy.com](https://www.unitsofstudy.com)
- **PhD advising**: regular one-on-one meetings between advisor and advisee, where feedback on drafts and goal-setting for the next work cycle mirror the conference structure at an adult level.
