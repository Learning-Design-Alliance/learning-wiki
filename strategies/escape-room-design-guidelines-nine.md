# Nine practical guidelines for designing and conducting educational escape rooms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on lessons learned, the article suggests nine guidelines for educators: participate in teams to develop soft skills; design for 1–2 hours; provide clear initial guidance on rules, goal, and help mechanisms; use a linear puzzle structure; connect puzzles through an engaging narrative; intertwine educational content with game mechanics so trial-and-error solving is minimal; increase puzzle difficulty gradually; use purpose-built software tools like Escapp; and deliver hints via self-assessed quizzes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Purpose-built software tools to ease enrollment, content delivery, solution checking, monitoring, and hint management
#### Constraints
- Shorter durations could prevent mastering meaningful objectives; longer durations increase the risk of students becoming tired or losing interest

### Target Learners
- higher-education students

### Target Learning Goals
- topic-specific knowledge delivered through game-based learning
