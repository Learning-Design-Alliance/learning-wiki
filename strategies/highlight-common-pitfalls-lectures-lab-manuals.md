# Address troubleshooting in lectures and lab manuals by highlighting common pitfalls for each lab activity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A low-effort strategy reported by instructors: rather than building dedicated troubleshooting labs, they embed troubleshooting talk into existing course structures by addressing it in lectures and lab manuals, typically by naming the common pitfalls associated with a particular lab activity. This supplements the apprenticeship-style in-lab interactions that form the primary vehicle of troubleshooting instruction in these courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Existing lecture and lab-manual materials into which troubleshooting content can be incorporated
#### Constraints
- Few instructors described labs specifically dedicated to improving students' ability to troubleshoot; this strategy supplements rather than replaces in-lab practice

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in electronics lab courses

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing common circuit faults and pitfalls
- developing troubleshooting strategies during circuit-building activities
