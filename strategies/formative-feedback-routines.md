# Formative Feedback Routines

## Description
A formative feedback routine is a scheduled, repeatable cycle — produce, receive feedback, revise, resubmit or apply — embedded into the flow of instruction rather than appended to it. The feedback is *formative* in Black & Wiliam's sense: it is used to shape subsequent learning, not to certify final achievement. The routine aspect matters as much as the feedback itself; a single feedback event rarely changes behavior, but a predictable cycle that learners expect and act on does.

## Design Implications

Feedback improves learning when it answers "Where am I going, how am I doing, and where to next?" — and when learners actually use it to close the gap [Formative assessment practices improve achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Routines institutionalize the "use it" step: revision, resubmission, or immediate re-attempt is built into the cycle, not left to learner initiative. Feedback that is specific, task-focused, and actionable outperforms praise or grade-only feedback; grades appended to comments can cause learners to ignore the comments entirely.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Low-stakes or ungraded opportunities to produce work early and often
- Feedback that arrives while the task is still relevant — within days, not weeks
- A built-in action step: revision, re-attempt, or application of the feedback ([Application](../elements/application.md))
- Feedback language focused on the task and process, not the person

#### Constraints
- Feedback delivered with grades is often ignored; learners attend to the grade and skip the comments [-M]
- Feedback on work learners have no chance to revise produces little measurable gain [-M]
- Overly frequent or voluminous feedback can overwhelm novices and trigger defensive disengagement, especially for learners with low self-efficacy [~M]
- If the routine is unpredictable or the feedback is vague ("good work"), learners stop treating it as information [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Peer feedback cycles** — structured protocols (e.g., gallery walk, two-stars-and-a-wish) distribute the feedback load and build evaluative judgment
- **Self-assessment checkpoints** — learners compare work against criteria or exemplars before submission
- **Automated feedback** — platforms deliver immediate hints and correctness signals at scale
- **Conferencing** — brief individual conversations ([5-Minute Writing Conferences](5-minute_writing_conferences.md)) for high-stakes or struggling learners

### Target Learners
- Struggling learners benefit most, provided feedback is specific and actionable [Formative assessment practices improve achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]
- High-achieving learners may gain less from routine feedback and more from delayed or minimal feedback that forces self-evaluation [~W]
- Learners with fragile self-efficacy need process-focused framing ("your strategy for X") rather than person-focused framing ("you're good at this") to avoid disengagement after critical feedback [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Skill refinement: closing the gap between current and target performance through iteration
- Self-regulation: internalizing the monitor–evaluate–adjust cycle ([Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md))
- Evaluative judgment: learning to judge quality in one's own and others' work

### Instructions
1. Set clear success criteria or exemplars before learners begin, so feedback has a shared reference point ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md))
2. Schedule a low-stakes production event — draft, problem set, rehearsal ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
3. Deliver timely, task-focused feedback tied to the criteria; prioritize one or two high-leverage points over exhaustive correction
4. Require an action on the feedback: revise, re-solve, or explain what was changed ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))
5. Repeat on a predictable cadence so learners come to expect and rely on the cycle ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md))

## Examples
- **Draft–feedback–revision cycles in writing instruction** — Graham & Perin's meta-analysis of writing instruction found strategy instruction combined with structured feedback and revision among the most effective practices for adolescent writers.
- **[ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org)** — free math platform delivering immediate feedback on homework problems, with teacher dashboards showing which items need reteaching the next day.
- **Peer review with calibrated exemplars** — students rate sample papers against a rubric, compare their ratings to instructor ratings, then exchange drafts; used widely in writing-across-the-curriculum programs.
