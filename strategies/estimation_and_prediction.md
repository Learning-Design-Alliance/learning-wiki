# Estimation and Prediction

## Description
Asking students to use estimation and prediction skills. Asking them how they might do on an upcoming project or how long it might take them to complete a task or assignment. Recording estimates and reflecting on the accuracy of their predictions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Projects or assignments, recording sheets, and class time for estimation and reflection.
#### Constraints
- Learners may struggle to accurately estimate their performance or time required. Some learners may find it challenging to reflect on their predictions. Estimation and prediction can be influenced by factors beyond the learners’ control.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- All learner levels, including K–12, higher education, and adult learners in professional development programs

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Enhance self-awareness, improve estimation skills, promote goal-setting, encourage self-monitoring, and foster accuracy in predictions.

### Affordances
- Provides opportunities to practice estimation and prediction, encourages self-monitoring and reflection, promotes goal-setting and self-regulation, and supports personal growth.

### Personalization
- Projects and assignments can be chosen to align with different age groups, interests, or real-world situations. Learners can be given options for how to record their estimates and reflections. Support can be provided for learners who struggle with estimation or self-monitoring.

## Assessment Evidence
- Review students’ recorded estimates and reflections. Observe students’ ability to set goals and monitor their progress. Evaluate students’ accuracy in predicting their performance or time required.

## Impact
- Estimation and prediction skills can improve self-awareness, promote goal-setting, and enhance performance. Regular self-monitoring can help learners adjust their strategies and achieve their goals.
