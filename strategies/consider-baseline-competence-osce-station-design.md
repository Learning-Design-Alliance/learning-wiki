# Consider baseline competence when designing OSCE stations, since well-established skills may show limited measurable change

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the small gain observed at the Oral Hygiene Education station, the authors recommend attending to baseline competence when designing OSCE stations: "This highlights the importance of carefully considering baseline competence when designing OSCE stations, as skills that are already well-established may show limited measurable change". Stations assessing skills students already perform well from prior academic and personal exposure are less sensitive to improvement, so designers should anticipate ceiling effects or choose more refined evaluation tools for such topics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of students' prior exposure to each station topic
- Awareness that ceiling effects reduce station sensitivity
#### Constraints
- Ceiling effects are a recognized limitation of OSCE assessment; rater variability at subjective stations can further affect score sensitivity

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate dental students

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate assessment of clinical skill improvement in OSCE design
