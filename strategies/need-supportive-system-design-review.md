# Institutional design review to reduce pressure transmitted through assessment, communication, and workload systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that institutions examine the conditions that make controlling teaching seem necessary, including "assessment practices that heighten impression management, administrative communications that rely on compliance without rationale, workload and scheduling arrangements that reduce educators' capacity for dialogue." Leaders can explain rationales for requirements, invite appropriate challenge, and ensure documentation supports dialogue rather than replacing it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educational leaders must examine whether systems inadvertently reward compliance, certainty, or impression management over curiosity and dialogue
#### Constraints
- Many controlling experiences originate outside direct teaching encounters, so interpersonal training alone is insufficient

### Target Learners
- Medical learners, residents, and the educators who supervise them

### Target Learning Goals
- Internalizing professional standards and values rather than merely complying with external demands
