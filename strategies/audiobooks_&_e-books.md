# Audiobooks & E-books

## Description
Audiobooks and e-books can be used to enhance literacy skills, comprehension, and engagement, especially for emerging readers, English language learners, and students with reading challenges. Audiobooks bridge the decoding gap, allowing learners to access age-appropriate stories and complex content. E-books offer customizable features like adjustable fonts and text size, catering to individual learning preferences. Following along visually while listening can enhance word-recognition ability, while listening alone can expand vocabulary.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to audiobooks and/or e-books, devices (e.g., computers, tablets, smartphones), reading apps (e.g., Sora, Libby), and library cards (for some platforms).
#### Constraints
- Some may argue that listening to audiobooks is not equivalent to traditional reading. The selection of free titles may be limited to older classics. Requires reliable access to technology and digital resources.

### Target Learners

- Emerging readers, K-12 students, English language learners, students with reading difficulties (e.g., dyslexia), and adult learners.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance literacy skills, improve comprehension, expand vocabulary, increase engagement with reading, and support learners with reading challenges.

### Affordances
- Provides access to complex texts, supports decoding skills, strengthens comprehension, offers a stigma-free reading experience, and accommodates different learning preferences through customizable features. For educators, it offers a tool to engage reluctant readers and provide differentiated instruction.

### Personalization
- Learners can adjust the playback speed, font size, and background color. Digital platforms enable text-to-speech capabilities, and learners can choose between listening alone or following along with the text.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor learners' reading progress, comprehension levels, and engagement in class discussions. Observe their ability to follow along with the text while listening and track their recommendations to peers. Collect data on learners' attitudes toward reading and their self-perception as readers.

## Impact
- Studies show audiobooks can improve reading skills, vocabulary, and comprehension. They also provide access to books above learners' reading levels and can spark interest in reading. Can also create feelings of relaxation and stress reduction.
