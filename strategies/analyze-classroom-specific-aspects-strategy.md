# Future research should analyze classroom-specific aspects—class climate, teacher-student relationship, and teaching strategy—and their effects on achievement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that twins in different classrooms showed reduced twin-shared environmental effects, the authors recommend that research examine the classroom-level factors that the twin-shared environment appears to reflect. The article states: "Future research should therefore analyze classroom specific aspects like class climate, teacher-student relationship and teaching strategy and their effect on the achievement of students in greater detail." It also encourages examining twins taught by the same teachers versus those with different timetables, given the small sample used here.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designs that can identify classroom membership and teacher assignment, since teacher names were unknown in this study
#### Constraints
- The classroom-separation comparison rests on a rather small sample and yielded somewhat unclear results across grade variables

### Target Learners
- School students, particularly twins and siblings in shared or separate classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding how classroom-related environmental factors contribute to differences in academic achievement

## Claims

- [Twins assigned to different classrooms show larger additive genetic and non-shared environmental variance and smaller twin-shared environmental variance than twins sharing a classroom](../claims/classroom-separation-lower-twin-shared-environment.md) [+W]
- [Twin-shared environmental effects, not sibling-shared (family-wide) effects, explain part of grade variance, consistent with grades reflecting individual achievement](../claims/twin-shared-not-sibling-shared-environment-grades.md) [+W]
