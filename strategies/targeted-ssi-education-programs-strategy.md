# Deliver targeted educational programs updating nurses' SSI-prevention knowledge to positively affect attitudes and adherence to evidence-based practices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends targeted educational activities aimed at the knowledge areas found most lacking, on the grounds that knowledge and attitudes were the strongest predictors of the investigated evidence-based practices. It states that "an update in knowledge regarding SSI prevention performed through educational programs could positively affect attitudes and, ultimately, tendency to perform current evidence-based practices". Education should cover SSI outcomes, risks, and methods enabling adherence to effective prevention practices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educational content must target the specific knowledge gaps identified (e.g., preoperative hair-removal timing, bundle definition, antibiotic prophylaxis duration).
- Behavior change must also be aimed at abandoning outdated practices and adopting and maintaining evidence-based practices.
#### Constraints
- The article notes changing behavior is a very complex process.
- Support for the knowledge-to-practice pathway comes from a cross-sectional self-report survey, not from a tested educational intervention.

### Target Learners
- nurses working in general or specialist surgical wards, operating rooms and critical care units

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of evidence-based SSI prevention recommendations
- adherence to evidence-based prevention practices
