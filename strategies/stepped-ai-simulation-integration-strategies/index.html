Implement AI simulations as complements within a stepped simulation continuum, with layered interaction modes and blended feedback¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The review's synthesized design strategies position AI simulations as complements rather than substitutes to traditional simulation, creating a "stepped" simulation continuum where AI serves prelearning and basic skills practice while traditional simulations address advanced interpersonal and affective competencies. Strategies include authenticity enhancement, layered instructional design (menu-based interactions for early stages, voice-controlled for advanced critical thinking), technical stability optimization, targeted feedback mechanisms, hybrid interaction modalities, blending automated with facilitator-led feedback, and phased curriculum integration.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • clear communication to students of AI simulations' pedagogical objectives and curricular positioning to manage expectations
  • continuous improvement of response speed, dialog accuracy, and platform stability

Constraints¶

  • moderate authenticity (eg, animated virtual characters) may be more acceptable than overly realistic but technically unstable systems

Target Learners¶

  • prelicensure nursing students
  • practicing nurses in formal educational activities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • foundational clinical reasoning
  • communication skills
  • clinical confidence

Affordances¶

  • Ai Simulation Four Modality Taxonomy

Related Strategies¶

  • Phased implementation of AI in nursing education: simulation for emergency care, adaptive platforms for at-risk learners, automated formative feedback, and multi-site pilots
  • Use fidelity-aware simulated patients as a low-risk complement to supervision for deliberate psychotherapy practice
  • Deploy AI-assisted classroom observation as a complementary first-pass screening and reflection tool, with raters retaining interpretive judgment
  • Combine real people and artificial agents as complements in blended learning environments
