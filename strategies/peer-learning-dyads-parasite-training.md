# Targeted training programs pairing peer-learning dyads and prioritizing weak competency areas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends targeted, competency-based training to address identified weaknesses in practitioner skills. It explicitly recommends "establishing peer-learning dyads that pair high-performing female participants with male participants requiring improvement in future training programs," and calls for standardized competency-based training in western regions, equitable resource allocation, and educational technology training for microscopic techniques.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires assessment data identifying which competency areas and practitioner subgroups are weakest, as the article's own assessment provided.
#### Constraints
- The authors note the sample size was relatively small and the assessment methodology could not capture the full spectrum of real-world diagnostic scenarios.

### Target Learners
- CDC and hospital laboratory practitioners responsible for parasitic disease diagnosis

### Target Learning Goals
- Malaria blood smear preparation and staining
- Microscopic identification of Plasmodium species and parasite eggs
- Theoretical knowledge of rare parasitic species
