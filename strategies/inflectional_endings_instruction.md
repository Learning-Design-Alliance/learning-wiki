# Inflectional Endings Instruction

## Description
Inflectional endings instruction focuses on teaching the rules for adding suffixes (e.g., -ed, -s, -es, -ing, -er, -est) to base words. These suffixes change the number, person, or tense of a word without changing its part of speech. The instruction involves making learners aware of the different sounds the suffixes make, sorting words based on the sound of the past tense ending, and explicitly teaching the rules for adding endings, such as the doubling rule, the drop -e rule, and the change y to i rule. This type of instruction improves spelling accuracy and understanding of grammar.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- List of words and suffixes, direct instruction from a teacher or tutor, and consistent practice with word decomposition and combination
#### Constraints
- Can be challenging for learners with significant language processing deficits. Requires explicit and systematic instruction. May be perceived as rote learning if not connected to meaningful contexts.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Primarily for learners in grades 2–4, but also beneficial for older students who struggle with spelling or grammar, including English language learners

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve spelling accuracy of inflectional endings, enhance understanding of grammatical rules, and develop skills in decomposing and combining words

### Affordances
- Enhances spelling skills, improves understanding of grammar, supports word building, and provides a structured approach to learning and applying morphological rules

### Personalization
- Can be adapted by varying the complexity of the words and suffixes used in the instruction. Scaffolding can be provided for beginners, while more advanced learners can tackle more complex word transformations.
