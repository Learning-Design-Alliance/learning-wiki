# Structured spiritual care training, interdisciplinary collaboration with chaplains, and institutional integration strategies for ICU nursing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structured education and institutional strategies to strengthen spiritual care in intensive care units. It states that "structured training programs and professional development initiatives could enhance nurses’ competence and confidence in providing spiritual care", and further proposes integrating spiritual care assessments into routine nursing documentation and care plans, strengthening interdisciplinary collaboration with chaplains or psychologists, and workload management through appropriate staffing and equitable task distribution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Nurses need structured training programs and professional development initiatives to build spiritual care competence and confidence
- Institutional support is needed, including spiritual care professionals such as chaplains or psychologists and integration of spiritual care into routine documentation, care plans, and policies
#### Constraints
- The article notes that high workload, the ICU environment, and lack of institutional support may limit integration of spiritual care even among highly competent nurses
- Generalizability is limited to Turkish critical care nurses recruited via a social media platform, with self-reported data

### Target Learners
- Critical care nurses in intensive care units

### Target Learning Goals
- Spiritual care competence
- Providing spiritual care interventions to critically ill patients
