# Use creative drama techniques across different grades and courses to support achievement and affective outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educators apply creative drama — improvisational, process-centered structured play with beginning, middle, and conclusion phases — as an instructional technique in varied grades and subjects, since it appeared to have positive impact on achievement in various domains, skills, and attitude. They state it is "recommended that creative drama techniques be utilized in different grades and courses" and advise education experts to encourage educators to explore its classroom benefits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher who facilitates structured plays (beginning, middle, conclusion) and familiarity with the method; the article notes teachers may initially perceive it as requiring much time, energy, or effort.
#### Constraints
- The article reports reported negative aspects from student views: creating confusion, trouble in understanding due to crowded classes, and being considered time-consuming.

### Target Learners
- Students from preschool through university across science, math, social sciences, and foreign language courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic achievement
- creativity and critical thinking
- social and affective outcomes
- attitude toward school
