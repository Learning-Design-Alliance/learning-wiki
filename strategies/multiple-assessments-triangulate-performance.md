# Use multiple assessments, possibly mixing methods, to triangulate on a student's performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because student performance on performance assessments is volatile across tasks and occasions, single snapshots are unreliable indicators. The authors propose that "multiple assessments are needed to gauge performance, perhaps using a mix of methods to triangulate on a student's performance". The strategy pairs the revised exchangeability finding (direct observation, notebook, and computer simulation converge) with the volatility finding, suggesting mixed-method repeated measurement for individual student inference.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple measurement occasions or task samples, since single-occasion scores carry large task-occasion error.
#### Constraints
- The authors note volatility, perhaps arising from partial knowledge, limits the exchangeability of any of the methods.
- Sampling more than one occasion is very expensive and unlikely to occur in practice.

### Target Learners
- fifth and sixth grade science students

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate gauging of individual student science investigation performance
