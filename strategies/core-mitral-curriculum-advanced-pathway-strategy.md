# Structure surgical training around a defined core mitral curriculum for all trainees plus a separate advanced pathway for future mitral specialists

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that training requirements distinguish core competence expected of all cardiothoracic trainees from advanced subspecialist competence in complex mitral repair. All trainees need exposure to "assess valve pathology, understand repair strategy, perform essential operative steps under supervision", while trainees intending a mitral subspecialty require concentrated experience in high-volume valve centres. Nationally audited logbook data and proficiency-based assessments could identify programmes with inadequate core exposure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient trainee exposure to valve pathology assessment, repair strategy and supervised essential operative steps
#### Constraints
- The article states it may be neither feasible nor necessary for every trainee to complete training as an independent operator for the full spectrum of complex mitral repair

### Target Learners
- UK cardiothoracic surgery trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- Operative competence in isolated aortic and mitral valve surgery
