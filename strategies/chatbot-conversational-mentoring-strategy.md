# Deliver writing-assignment feedback via a conversational chatbot to emulate mentoring at scale

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because classic mentoring takes place in conversations, the article chooses a conversational interface to digitally emulate mentoring processes: chatbots "are known to be relatively easy to use and intuitive with a low entry barrier and good accessibility", suiting users of different digital literacy levels. A chatbot is inherently adaptive to some degree since it reacts to user input during conversation, and adaptiveness towards the mentee is a key factor of mentoring efficacy. Students upload written texts in the chat and receive a feedback document through the same interface.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- an underlying knowledge base the chatbot can adapt to
- trust and respect within the mentoring relationship, reportedly influenced by design details such as human-likeness, humor or professional appearance
#### Constraints
- the elements the chatbot needs to adapt to through the course of conversation still need to be determined
- ethical factors, transparency, degree of human-likeness and opportunities for playfulness remain subject to future consideration

### Target Learners
- university students with different levels of digital literacy

### Target Learning Goals
- support for learning from texts during self-study, on demand
