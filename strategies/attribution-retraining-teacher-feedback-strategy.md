# Attribution retraining in teacher feedback and classroom practices to promote internal, controllable attributions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers identify students who blame external factors for poor performance and implement brief classroom interventions promoting internal, controllable attributions. It gives concrete feedback wording: "emphasizing “Your improvement shows your hard work is paying off” rather than “You’re naturally good at this.”" Classrooms should emphasize effort and strategy over ability and give students multiple opportunities to succeed through their own efforts, which the authors argue would foster healthier attribution patterns and reduce dropout risk.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher training in recognizing attribution patterns through classroom observation and in attribution retraining within feedback practices
#### Constraints
- Recommended on the basis of cross-sectional correlational findings; the article did not test these interventions experimentally

### Target Learners
- adolescent students in middle and vocational high schools

### Target Learning Goals
- adaptive attribution styles for academic success and failure
- reduced internalizing and externalizing problem behaviors
- reduced dropout risk
