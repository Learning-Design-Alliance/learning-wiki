# Deliver suturing instruction through self-directed video courses with the teacher limited to supervision, requiring no faculty guidance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's course design assigns the teacher a strictly supervisory role: during classroom sessions and the independent practice stage, "the teacher's involvement was limited to supervision, devoid of any instructional guidance". Students replay videos as needed and practice each technique seven times, a repetition count informed by prior research indicating performance plateaus after seven to nine attempts. This design lets novices acquire suturing skills without faculty instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Videos must be replayable on demand during practice, and students must complete the current stitching technique before moving to the next
#### Constraints
- The study emulated remote teaching with a uniform skin model, which may differ from the individualized practice aids students would use in actual remote learning

### Target Learners
- preclinical medical students learning basic surgical skills

### Target Learning Goals
- acquiring suturing technique proficiency through self-directed practice
