# Use needs assessment, flexible modalities, and protected time to implement palliative care education for clinical nurses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing education from identified practice gaps and learning needs, offering multiple participation modes, and securing organizational support. It states attendance "can be improved in part by dedicating protected time and resources to educational purposes and offering support to encourage staff participation, especially for novice nurses". It further recommends strategies for mandatory PC education and collaboration between multidisciplinary teams in tertiary care, and engaging physicians and nurses jointly in QI initiatives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A culture that prioritizes learning and supports continuing education for professional growth
#### Constraints
- The article notes identifying preferred learning methods during needs assessment and a longer implementation time could have increased participation

### Target Learners
- Nurses caring for heart failure patients
- Novice nurses in tertiary care

### Target Learning Goals
- Palliative care knowledge and perceptions
- Symptom management in heart failure
