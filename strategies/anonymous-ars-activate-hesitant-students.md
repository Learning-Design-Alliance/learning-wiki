# Use an anonymous audience response system to activate students who hesitate to answer questions publicly

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating an anonymous smartphone audience response system into teaching to reach learners that traditional questioning misses. The authors conclude that audience response systems "specifically allow activating students which cannot be reached by the traditional way of asking questions without such an anonymous tool", and that their intervention "could be easily adapted into other classes and courses". The supporting evidence is the questionnaire subgroup analysis showing students uncomfortable answering publicly profited especially.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An anonymous, smartphone-based audience response system such as eduVote integrated into the presentation
- Questions posed during class with aggregated answers displayed and discussed
#### Constraints
- In this study the system did not improve summative assessment results, so the strategy targets engagement and motivation rather than measured long-term persistence

### Target Learners
- higher-education students, including those uncomfortable speaking in front of peers

### Target Learning Goals
- student activation and engagement
- motivation to deal with course content
- willingness to ask questions
