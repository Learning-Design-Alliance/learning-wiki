# Structure pre-clerkship communication education as a lecture-based theory section followed by standardized-patient simulation with feedback and reflection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study's curriculum comprised two parts: a lecture-based curriculum (LBC) covering general communication and theoretical knowledge over 9 class hours in 4 weeks, and a clinical skills-based curriculum (CBC) in which "groups of six to eight students practiced for 2 hours at a simulation center" with standardized patients, recorded simulations, and tutor/classmate feedback for self-reflection. This design is intended to build both knowledge and application of medical communication before clerkship.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires instructional variety in the LBC (e.g., lectures, role playing, workshops, case studies, feedback, self-reflection) and access to a simulation center with standardized patients and tutors for the CBC
#### Constraints
- Perceived MC competency did not significantly increase post-CBC; the authors note students had difficulty applying MC theories during simulations and recommend active feedback and strengthened LBC-CBC linkage

### Target Learners
- Second-year pre-clerkship medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Medical communication competency, including questioning, active listening, empathy, and explanation with patients
