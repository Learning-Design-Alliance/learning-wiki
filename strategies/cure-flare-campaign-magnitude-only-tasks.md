# Embed a flare-focused multi-instrument observing campaign in an introductory course as a student-led CURE with constrained, magnitude-only analysis tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article presents a transferable model for course-based citizen science in which "The course instructor and students led the design of the observing campaign, the configuration of instruments, data curation, the processing pipeline, and manuscript preparation". Student analytical workload was intentionally constrained to reading off total-field values and computing simple magnitude differences (ΔF) rather than formal identification of magnetic crochets, aligning with course learning outcomes in quantitative reasoning and data literacy. Students synchronized clocks, compared records across sites, and related measurements to satellite X-ray flux, practicing authentic scientific skills within a broader multi-parameter Sun–Earth observing campaign.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a research-grade reference instrument, external benchmark datasets (USGS/INTERMAGNET and GOES-18), and time-aligned CSV files and plots prepared for a first- or second-year audience.
#### Constraints
- The analysis design was scoped to magnitude-only tasks; students did not attempt formal identification of magnetic crochets or advanced ionospheric diagnostics.

### Target Learners
- introductory-level undergraduate students at community colleges

### Target Learning Goals
- quantitative reasoning
- data literacy
- authentic scientific data practices
- Nature of Science understanding
