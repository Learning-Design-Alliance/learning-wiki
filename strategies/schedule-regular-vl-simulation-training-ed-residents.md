# Schedule simulation-based VL intubation training regularly in emergency medicine and intensive care residency curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that "This kind of training should be introduced in the training course of emergency medicine and intensive care residents to generalize the use of VL in the ED and to improve conditions of intubation in the ED. It also should be scheduled regularly to maintain knowledge and skills." The recommendation rests on reduced intubation time and fewer attempts after a single session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to videolaryngoscope devices and simulation equipment
- Instructors able to run the four-step procedural session with debriefing
#### Constraints
- The article notes it was not possible to assess skill retention after the study period, so the appropriate interval for repeating training remains to be determined

### Target Learners
- emergency medicine residents
- intensive care residents

### Target Learning Goals
- videolaryngoscope orotracheal intubation skill
- maintenance of knowledge and skills over time

## Claims

- [Simulation-based training improved theoretical knowledge of intubation and videolaryngoscopy among ED residents (mean pretest 12.9/20 vs post-test 14.6/20, p<0.001)](../claims/simulation-improves-vl-intubation-theoretical-knowledge.md) [+W]
- [The mean number of attempts for successful intubation decreased from 3.81 before simulation training to 1.84 after the session (p<0.001), in one group with no comparison group](../claims/simulation-reduces-intubation-attempts.md) [+W]
- [Mean intubation time fell from 149.81±108 sec before to 51±96 sec after the simulation session in one uncontrolled pre-post study (p<0.001)](../claims/simulation-reduces-vl-intubation-time.md) [+W]
- [The source described shared visualisation between instructor and learner and immediate corrections during videolaryngoscopy intubation training](../claims/vl-shared-view-supports-instructor-feedback.md) [+W]
