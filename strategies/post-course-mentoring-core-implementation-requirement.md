# Treat post-course mentoring and institutional supports as a core implementation requirement when running innovation courses for novice nurses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that nurse managers and innovation mentors treat post-course support as central, not incidental: "Practical supports include facilitator preparation, protected learning time, transparent project‐selection criteria, access to evidence and patent‐search assistance, staged feedback and referral of feasible projects to quality‐improvement or innovation platforms." This strategy extends the course's in-class PBL cycle into sustained translation of projects into patents, competitions and research proposals. Organisations should monitor feasibility, equity of access, learner burden and project quality when adapting the model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitator preparation, protected learning time and context-appropriate mentoring
#### Constraints
- Because the pilot cannot establish effectiveness, organisations should distinguish educational outcomes from the effects of mentoring and institutional resources

### Target Learners
- Newly recruited nurses
- Nurse educators and clinical educators

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained innovation project development
- Translation of learning into patents, publications and research proposals
