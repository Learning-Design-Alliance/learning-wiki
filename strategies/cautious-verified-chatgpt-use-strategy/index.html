Cautious, verified use of ChatGPT with critical cross-referencing of AI-provided sources¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 7 studies (3 qualitative, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 associational, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 7 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends using ChatGPT as a thesis-writing assistant while verifying its outputs: participants found inaccurate responses and fake auto-generated references, and the authors suggested cautious use. Users themselves recommended "double-checking every piece of literature the AI provided to ensure accuracy," and one adopted a proactive strategy of presenting an expanded set of questions to the supervisor to curate optimal items. This treats ChatGPT as a support for drafting and idea generation rather than an authority on facts.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Human verification of AI-provided references and factual claims against independent sources such as Google Scholar or Zotero

Constraints¶

  • Inaccurate responses and fabricated references occur frequently; plagiarism detection tools could not distinguish AI-generated content, and AI use in education lacked a robust regulatory framework at the time of the study

Target Learners¶

  • Master's thesis students
  • Young researchers in higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Academic writing
  • Research methodology decision-making

Claims¶

  • ChatGPT users report fabricated references and wrong sources from auto-generated literature content [+W]
  • Students report negative experiences with ChatGPT including inaccurate outputs, lack of emotional connection, and risk of overreliance, which constrain competence [+M]
  • Reviewed literature reports risks of overreliance, AI errors in complex tasks, and students' uncritical acceptance of AI-generated outputs [+M]
  • Gen-AI hallucinates sources and values and produces convincing but incorrect explanations, risking student misinformation [+M]
  • Of 30 verified hallucinated references, 13 appeared entirely fabricated and 17 were hybrid references combining a real title with fabricated authorship or metadata [+M]
  • LLM responses that are incomplete or incorrect prompt students to question, verify and improve the information, fostering critical thinking through cycles of validation and correction [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Support learners to evaluate AI outputs by verifying sources, reporting errors, and overriding recommendations
