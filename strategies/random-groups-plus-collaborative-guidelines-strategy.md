# Use random group assignment and provide collaborative guidelines for effective group processes in physics classrooms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article draws two managerial and instructional recommendations from its quasi-experiment. First, random group assignment is viable: "The similar performance observed on both control and ex- perimental groups, specially on Activity 1, is an encouraging sing for using random groups in physics classrooms." Second, teachers should "include col- laborative guidelines for effective performance", especially for students unaccustomed to positive interdependence, individual accountability, coordination and decision-making. The strategy pairs group-formation decisions with explicit support for collective processes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Group activities should be open-ended and additive so that they demand collective coordination rather than being solvable by the most capable member.
#### Constraints
- Collaborative effects were negative early in the hybrid year, so gains from strong ties required continuity and persistence across semesters before appearing.

### Target Learners
- secondary school physics students in remote or hybrid modalities

### Target Learning Goals
- effective collaboration, group-level coordination, and physics performance
