# Use simultaneous high-arousal episodes to locate emotionally relevant moments in collaborative learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends tracking group-level physiological activation as a way to find emotionally meaningful situations worth studying and supporting. It states that high arousal episodes "might indicate emotionally relevant situations by revealing the situations in which group members’ jointly experience an activating affect with high intensity," and that recognizing such critical moments could be especially important in online learning settings where socio-emotional cues are limited.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Physiological data (e.g., EDA) collected from multiple group members simultaneously
- A complementary data channel providing valence context for the arousal episodes
#### Constraints
- Simultaneous high arousal episodes are rare, so they capture only a subset of emotionally relevant situations

### Target Learners
- collaborative learning groups in classroom and online settings

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying emotionally relevant situations for support of socio-emotional collaboration
