# Anchor an experimental problem in a widely used technical patent so students connect school physics to real devices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing olympiad problems around technical patents implemented in contemporary devices. The EPO10 set-up "illustrates the principle of chopper stabilized DC amplifiers described in the patent by Edwin Goldberg and Jules Lehmann", and "Now this principle is implemented in many contemporary operational amplifiers. In such a way this student problem is related to the operation of widely used technical device and many participants successfully solved the problem." The authors note EPO problems are related either to fundamental physics or to technical patents used in contemporary devices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A patent or technical principle realizable with a simple, inexpensive experimental set-up
- The application question is posed only after the experimental solution of the whole L problem
#### Constraints
- The application task proved unsolved by any participant, indicating the need to complement physics education with application-oriented thinking

### Target Learners
- secondary and undergraduate physics students seeking careers in science or engineering

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the physics behind widely used technical devices
- developing invention-oriented thinking
