# Administer the QMCS as a posttest only in modern physics courses, not as a pretest

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy prescribes when and how to give the QMCS in a modern physics course. Because students enter with almost no knowledge of the concepts measured, the article recommends "giving the QMCS as a posttest at the end of a course only, and not as a pretest at the beginning of the course". Giving it as a pretest was found to be meaningless and demoralizing: students reported feeling discouraged even when told they would not be graded.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course must cover the standard topics measured by the QMCS so posttest scores reflect instruction
#### Constraints
- Pretest administration is meaningless and demoralizing in modern physics courses due to students' lack of prior knowledge
- In graduate courses pretest and posttest scores do not differ, so pre/post gains cannot be measured there

### Target Learners
- sophomore-level modern physics students (engineering and physics majors)

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring conceptual understanding of quantum mechanics for course reform assessment
