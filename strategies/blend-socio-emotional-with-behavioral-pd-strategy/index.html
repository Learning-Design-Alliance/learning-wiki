Blend socio-emotional components with behavioral approaches in professional development for teachers of students with autism¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that when the goal is to increase teaching repertoires for applying instructional methods, strategies, and learning supports to students with both mild/moderate and severe autism, professional development should blend behavioral and socio-emotional approaches rather than rely on a singular approach. It suggests "socio-emotional components when blended with behavioral approaches provide teachers with a more comprehensive repertoire of instructional methods, strategies, and learning supports to apply in the instructional setting with students with autism", because the blend offers a broader model for meeting heterogeneous learning needs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training that explicitly covers both behavioral techniques (reinforcement, prompting, three-term contingency) and socio-emotional elements (interactive teaching style, self-regulation, joint attention)

Constraints¶

  • The advantage over singular behavioral training appeared on the application measure but not on knowledge, self-efficacy, or self-report measures; the study used a small sample of 48 teachers and the authors call for replication with a larger sample

Target Learners¶

  • Teachers of students with autism, including those in specialized classes, inclusion programs, and early intervention settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Increasing teachers' repertoires for applying instructional methods, strategies, and learning supports across autism severity levels

Claims¶

  • Both Blended and Behavioral professional development training significantly increased teachers' knowledge, self-efficacy, and self-reported use of instructional methods compared with a no-training control [+W]
  • The Blended training group significantly outperformed the Behavioral training group on post-test application of instructional methods, strategies, and supports to hypothetical cases [+W]
  • Teacher application of instructional methods did not differ significantly by autism severity level, and no treatment-by-severity interaction emerged at post-test [+W]
  • Post-test measures of teacher knowledge, self-efficacy, self-reported use, and application were significantly and positively intercorrelated, with the strongest link between self-efficacy and self-reported use [+W]
  • At pretest, teachers' application scores were significantly lower for severe than mild/moderate hypothetical cases, with no group differences or interaction [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Train teachers of students with autism on evidence-based strategies applied in the natural environment
