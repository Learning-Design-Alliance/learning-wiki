# Combine tutored, student-tutored and tutorless PBL formats within a curriculum rather than choosing one

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes mixing PBL formats across a curriculum to balance faculty resource demands with learning support. It states: "An appropriate and effective curriculum can be administered in every school by combining tutored PBL and student-tutored PBL or tutorless PBL." The mixed course itself gave students alternating experience of tutored and tutorless sessions, which the authors suggest may prompt peer-assisted learning between groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty availability for tutored sessions at least in early stages
- Awareness that tutorless PBL differs from faculty-tutored and student-tutored PBL and should not be used interchangeably
#### Constraints
- Tutorless PBL was judged comparable only "at least in the early stages"; PBL2 for clinical years was kept tutored because faculty-led PBL was reported to perform better on higher-order cognitive skills

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students
- third- and fourth-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical problem solving
- knowledge acquisition
- self-directed learning
