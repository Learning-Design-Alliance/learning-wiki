# Integrate surgical simulator training into the undergraduate medical curriculum before residency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that "practical experience and specifically surgical simulator experience should be integrated into the medical school curriculum to help better prepare students and help them find the right discipline that suits both their skills and interests." They argue early exposure can "expose students to surgical fields, identify those that have particular talent and aptitude for surgery, and assist them in deciding which specialty to pursue."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a surgical simulator such as Eyesi Surgical during clinical rotations
- Structured sessions with an introductory lecture and demonstration, as implemented in this study
#### Constraints
- Scheduling conflicts with other clerkships limited attendance in this study, causing a loss to follow-up of 36 of 115 students

### Target Learners
- medical students in clinical rotations, particularly those undecided about specialty choice

### Target Learning Goals
- informed specialty and career decision-making
- early acquisition of basic microsurgical skills
