# Select laboratory experiments by considering whether their qualitative behaviour is interesting, to enable student autonomy and genuine note keeping

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends identifying experiments whose interesting behaviour is qualitative and where numerical agreement with a simple model is impossible, so that success is defined by students' satisfaction with their experimental process rather than by agreement with theory. It suggests that "considering qualitative behaviour may be a fruitful strategy for identifying experiments that are both amenable to student autonomy and embedding skills such as laboratory note keeping in a flexible and genuine way".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experiments where careful observation identifies interesting qualitative behaviour but "numerical agreement with a simple theoretical model is impossible"
#### Constraints
- Requiring students to take numerical measurements in such experiments "is likely to lead to more frustration than understanding"
- The instructor should withhold the theoretical description until after data collection, since its absence "prevents students from referring to values from a model that they may view as more credible than the data"

### Target Learners
- introductory university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- scientific critical thinking
- student autonomy in experimental design
- laboratory note keeping
