Balance on- and off-computer mathematics activities in early childhood classrooms¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Drawing on prior research, the authors recommend maintaining a balance between computer-based and hands-on mathematics activities rather than providing one type alone. In their study, teachers used software once weekly while providing hands-on rekenrek, ten-frame, and counter activities on remaining days. The authors note the scholarly dialogue should shift from whether computers should be used toward how to use them effectively and appropriately.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to both computer-based activities and hands-on manipulatives within the same mathematics program.

Constraints¶

  • The article notes educators and researchers still debate whether computer use in early childhood classrooms is appropriate.

Target Learners¶

  • preschool and early childhood learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • early mathematics skills such as problem solving and computation

Related Strategies¶

  • Provide Manipulatives
  • For young learners, adapt instructional support level rather than only task difficulty, and investigate adaptive features in isolation
