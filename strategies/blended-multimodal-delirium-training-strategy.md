# Embed tailored e-learning in blended, multimodal delirium training that combines face-to-face interaction with regular repetition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that, following refinement, the e-learning program be embedded in a multimodal training approach combining online modules with face-to-face training, which they call "a blended learning format". Drawing on the PRECEDE model, they argue educational programs "should not only provide knowledge (predisposing factors) but also promote practical application and interaction (enabling factors) and be repeated regularly (reinforcing factors)", so that structured, repetitive, interprofessional training formats can sustainably anchor delirium care in nursing homes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- E-learning alone is not sufficient to change behavior in direct resident care or enhance clinical delirium detection skills; an active face-to-face component and repetition are needed
#### Constraints
- The article notes the observed benefit among nurses with previous face-to-face delirium training may partly reflect participation in a related prevalence-study training, so the mechanism is an assumed explanation rather than a tested result

### Target Learners
- nurses and nursing assistants in nursing homes
- interprofessional nursing home teams

### Target Learning Goals
- sustainable integration of delirium management into routine nursing home practice and sustained behavior change in delirium care
