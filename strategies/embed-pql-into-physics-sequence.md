# Embed physics quantitative literacy into the introductory physics sequence instead of requiring remedial math prerequisites

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 5 studies (3 design, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 1 of 5 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends: "Instead of requiring students to complete remedial math before enrolling in physics, departments can embed physics quantitative literacy (PQL)--the contextual, applied math skills that physics truly demands--directly into the physics sequence." Optional instructor-led support courses or extended, credit-bearing pathways offer a more inclusive alternative; programs using this approach report improved outcomes for all students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors identify when students need specific skills associated with PQL and weave them into instruction, which the article says can boost learning without lowering expectations.
#### Constraints
- The article notes physicists "cannot single-handedly fix the deep disparities in U.S. pre-college math education" and that rethinking course designs requires sustained effort and institutional support.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate introductory physics students with varied math preparation
- students from low-SES districts lacking access to advanced high school math

### Target Learning Goals
- develop physics quantitative literacy within the physics course
- succeed in calculus-based physics regardless of prior calculus access
