Build online multimedia lessons with slide-sharing, survey, and video-sharing tools in a three-stage workflow¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends a workflow using only commonly available tools: create concise, narrated animations in slide-sharing programs such as PowerPoint, applying the multimedia, coherence, redundancy, contiguity, signaling, and personalization principles; divide them into user-paced segments and embed them in survey programs such as Google Forms or LMS quiz tools with interpolated practice-testing and self-explanation prompts; and host them on video-sharing sites such as YouTube for pace control and captioning.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors must explicitly teach learners how to use the control features if they want learners to use them effectively

Constraints¶

  • Captions, if used, should not obscure important learning information such as on-screen animations or labels

Target Learners¶

  • instructors designing online courses
  • adult online learners
  • undergraduate and graduate students

Claims¶

  • Applying CTML to authentic online instruction exposes gaps concerning working memory duration, retrieval, organization, and affective outcomes [~W]
  • Learner control over pace improves learning while control over pedagogical aspects undermines it, with prior-knowledge and outcome-type moderators [+W]
  • Increasing narration speed by up to 1.8 times has no negative impact on learning and reduces instruction time [+W]
  • Personalized, conversational narration shows moderate-to-large effects on friendliness, cognitive processing, retention, and transfer [+W]
  • Segmenting online multimedia lessons into short user-paced segments improves transfer and reduces cognitive load [+W]
