# Use a morphograph-based rule-teaching routine: introduce the rule, apply it in whole-group instruction, then assign independent worksheet practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Darch et al. (2006) intervention sessions followed a three-activity routine for rule-based spelling instruction: teachers first introduced "the spelling rule in whole group instruction time (5–7 minutes)," then applied "the spelling rule, also in whole-group instruction (10–12 minutes)," and finally had students complete "independent worksheet practice while the teacher circulated to assist as necessary (5–7 minutes)." Each 30-minute session focused on six words, and each week's sessions focused on a different type of word, such as phonetically regular words, regular words with a prefix or suffix, and irregular words. This routine operationalizes the morphemic strategy of teaching students to spell and blend bases and affixes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires teachers trained to deliver scripted lessons; in the study, teachers were graduate students who received three 1-hour training sessions and had to demonstrate adequate performance during role-playing before implementation
#### Constraints
- The routine was implemented in sixteen 30-minute sessions over a 4-week period with all words taught at a fourth-grade level of difficulty; effects were substantively important but not statistically significant in that trial

### Target Learners
- Elementary students with learning disabilities in grades 2–4

### Target Learning Goals
- Spelling words composed of learned morphographs and applying consistent spelling rules
