# Use vortex and rigid rotational motion of dust grains, induced by a charged probe or an external magnetic field, to teach vortex flow and rotational motion of many-body systems

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In a dust grain medium, vortex motion can be induced around a charged probe (metal wire) in unmagnetized RF discharge, or by an external magnetic field; dust particles in either DC or RF discharge configuration also exhibit rigid rotational motion under a weak magnetic field (B < 0.05 T). The article states "The dusty plasma device either in unmagnetized or magnetized RF discharge conﬁguration can be used to demonstrate the vortex motion in ﬂuids", letting students visualize vortex motion at the particle level and estimate angular frequency of rotating particles.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Unmagnetized RF discharge with a biased probe, or an external magnetic field applied along the discharge axis
- Free available particle image velocimetry (PIV) code and ImageJ software to obtain velocity profiles and angular velocity distributions
#### Constraints
- Rigid rotational motion is described for a weak magnetic field (B < 0.05 T); the article's vortex examples reproduce images from prior published studies (Law et al.; Choudhary et al.)

### Target Learners
- graduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding vortex flow in fluids and the differences between translational and rotational motion of many-body systems
