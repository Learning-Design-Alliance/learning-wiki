Improve student perception in learner-centered courses through honest dialogue, preparation accountability, maximized teacher-student interaction, and a risk-safe class culture¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends a set of instructor actions to ameliorate lower student perception in flipped and learner-centered courses: honest dialogue with students about how they learn and what instruction is valuable; finding effective methods for holding students accountable for watching lecture videos; maximizing and improving teacher-student interactions, including praising effort per Dweck's growth mindset framework; and cultivating a classroom culture where taking risks and being wrong is accepted, using Brown's engaged feedback practices. These are the authors' forward-looking recommendations grounded in the observed perception deficits.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors must work to find the most effective methods for holding students accountable for their outside preparation.

Constraints¶

  • The article notes accountability methods will not guarantee that all students come prepared.
  • Every class has its own unique chemistry, so culture-building cannot be uniform.

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in required mathematics courses such as college algebra

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improved achievement of learning outcomes in mathematics
  • improved student perception of learner-centered instruction

Claims¶

  • The author proposes that students in flipped classes perceive less learning because they experience difficulty during class-time problem solving, whereas lecture students perceive understanding while following lectures [+W]
  • Students in flipped college algebra rated 'The instructor encouraged my interest in the course' significantly lower than lecture-based students (p = .008) [+W]
  • Students in flipped college algebra rated the course overall significantly lower than lecture-based students (p = .04) [+W]
  • Students in flipped college algebra rated 'Overall, the instructor effectively facilitated my learning' significantly lower despite higher exam achievement (p = .04) [+W]
