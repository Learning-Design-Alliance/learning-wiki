# Guided Reading

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Guided reading is a small-group instructional format in which learners read a text at their instructional level — challenging but manageable with support — while the teacher observes, prompts, and coaches. The teacher introduces the text, supports word-solving and comprehension during reading, and leads a brief discussion or teaching point afterward. The goal is to make the reader increasingly independent by gradually transferring strategic control from teacher to learner.

## Design Implications

Guided reading operationalizes [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) in the reading domain: support is contingent on observed learner behavior rather than pre-scripted [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]. Text selection is the critical design decision — texts must sit in the learner's zone of proximal development, hard enough to require strategy use but not so hard that decoding consumes all working memory [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Fluent word recognition is a precondition: learners who must labor over every word lack the cognitive resources for comprehension work [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Accurate assessment of each learner's instructional reading level (e.g., running records, benchmark assessments)
- Leveled text sets spanning a gradient of difficulty
- Small groups (typically 4–6) of learners with similar needs, regrouped as learners grow
- Teacher skill in prompting and [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) — offering the least support needed, not immediately supplying answers
- A follow-up structure for independent application ([Practice](../elements/practice.md) at independent level)

#### Constraints
- Ineffective when text difficulty is misjudged: text too hard produces cognitive overload and guessing habits [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-S]; text too easy produces no growth
- Fixed ability grouping can entrench achievement gaps if groups never change; regrouping based on ongoing assessment is essential
- Whole-class time cost: guided reading requires the rest of the class to work independently on meaningful tasks; without that structure, implementation collapses
- Less effective for learners still building basic decoding accuracy, who need more explicit, teacher-led phonics instruction than guided reading provides [-M]
- Over-prompting ("sound it out" for every word) can fragment reading and destroy comprehension; prompts should target strategy use, not single-word rescue [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Fountas & Pinnell guided reading** — leveled text gradient (A–Z), detailed prompting guides, running-record-based grouping
- **Reciprocal teaching** — a guided reading variant in which learners take turns leading comprehension strategies (predicting, questioning, clarifying, summarizing) with teacher fading
- **Book club / literature circle formats** — shift the guidance role from teacher to peer discussion as readers mature
- **Digital variants** — platforms such as [Raz-Kids](https://www.raz-kids.com) and [Reading A–Z](https://www.readinga-z.com) deliver leveled texts with audio support and comprehension checks

### Target Learners
- Emergent and developing readers who can decode most words but need strategic support with meaning-making [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Struggling readers in small groups, where contingent prompting can be calibrated to individual needs [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- English learners, when paired with [Accessible Vocabulary & Syntax](../principles/accessible-vocabulary-syntax.md) work before and during reading
- Least appropriate for advanced readers working well above grade level, who benefit more from independent reading and discussion of complex texts [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Fluency and automatic word recognition [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Comprehension strategy acquisition (predicting, inferring, summarizing, monitoring)
- Self-regulated reading: internalizing the prompting language so learners prompt themselves [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. **Assess and group.** Use running records or benchmark assessments to form small groups with similar instructional levels; plan to regroup every few weeks.
2. **Select text.** Choose a text at instructional level (roughly 90–95% word accuracy with support) that connects to learners' background knowledge; pre-teach 2–3 critical vocabulary items [Activation improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M].
3. **Book introduction.** Briefly orient learners to the text's gist, structure, and tricky vocabulary — enough to support problem-solving, not so much that reading becomes retelling.
4. **Observe and prompt during reading.** Learners read softly or silently while the teacher listens in, prompting with the least invasive support (e.g., "Does that make sense?" before "That word is ___") [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md).
5. **Discuss and teach.** Lead a short comprehension conversation and name one explicit teaching point drawn from what was observed.
6. **Extend and fade.** Send learners to reread the text independently or with a partner, and progressively shift prompting responsibility to the reader [Fading](../elements/fading.md).

## Examples
- **Fountas & Pinnell Benchmark Assessment System and leveled readers** ([Heinemann](https://www.heinemann.com)) — the most widely implemented guided reading system in U.S. elementary schools, with leveled texts and prompting guides
- **Reading Recovery** ([https://readingrecovery.org](https://readingrecovery.org)) — a one-to-one descendant of guided reading for the lowest-achieving first graders, using running records to drive moment-to-moment teacher decisions
- **Raz-Kids** ([https://www.raz-kids.com](https://www.raz-kids.com)) — digital leveled library supporting guided reading rotations with audio models and comprehension quizzes
