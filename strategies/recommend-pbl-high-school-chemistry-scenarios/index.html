Recommend PBL for high school chemistry teaching with daily-life scenarios¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend adopting problem-based learning in formal education institutions and high school chemistry teaching because the method is thought to raise skills such as "bringing the problem or problems which students may encounter in daily life into the class environment as scenarios, defining the problem in the scenario, structuring in their minds information regarding the problem and its solution, developing ways out for the problem and coming out with a solution."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Problem scenarios drawn from students' daily life; teacher guidance; small heterogeneous groups

Constraints¶

  • Recommendation grounded in one 48-student study on the mixtures topic plus cited literature

Target Learners¶

  • High school chemistry students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Problem defining and solving; structuring knowledge of chemistry topics

Claims¶

  • PBL produced significantly higher mixtures achievement than teacher-centered traditional instruction in 10th-grade chemistry [+M]
  • Before instruction, the PBL and control groups did not differ significantly in mixtures achievement [+M]
  • The researcher-built two-stage Mixtures Achievement Test showed high reliability in the pilot and research groups [+M]
  • Prior chemistry studies cited by the authors also favor PBL over traditional methods [+W]
  • Neither PBL nor PBL with concept cartoons significantly changed students' levels of relating knowledge with daily life [-W]
  • Problem based learning, with or without concept cartoons, raises sixth graders' inquiry learning skill perceptions more than the standard science programme [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Adopt PBL-based techniques and strengthen curriculum coverage so linear programming is taught to all learners
  • Scaffold method choice by student progression: CBL early, transitioning to PBL and TBL
  • Use real-life scenarios requiring learners to produce goal-directed language with target vocabulary
