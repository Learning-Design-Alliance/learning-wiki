# Debate for the Opposite Team

## Description
Learners are assigned to argue a position that is opposite to their own, requiring them to see things from a different perspective. This activity promotes critical thinking, perspective-taking, and the ability to understand and argue opposing viewpoints.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A controversial topic, resources for research, and time for debate and discussion.
#### Constraints
- Learners may resist arguing a position they disagree with, or they may not take the activity seriously. It requires a skilled facilitator to manage the debate and ensure respectful communication.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learners, particularly those who tend to be rigid in their thinking and resistant to alternative viewpoints

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote critical thinking, develop perspective-taking skills, encourage argumentation, improve communication skills

### Affordances
- Forces learners to consider opposing viewpoints, promoting critical thinking and perspective-taking. Enhances argumentation skills by requiring learners to defend a position they may not personally agree with. Improves communication skills through debate and discussion.

### Personalization
- The topics chosen for debate can be tailored to the learners' interests and the curriculum content. The level of research and argumentation can be adjusted to their abilities.

## Assessment Evidence
- Assess the quality of arguments, the ability to understand and articulate opposing viewpoints, and the level of critical thinking demonstrated in the debate. Monitor changes in learners' attitudes towards different perspectives.

## Impact
- Studies suggest that debate can improve critical thinking, perspective-taking, and communication skills. Engaging with diverse viewpoints also promotes tolerance and understanding.
