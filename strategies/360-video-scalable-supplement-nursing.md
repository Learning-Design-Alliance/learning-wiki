# Use screen-based 360° contextual simulation videos as a scalable supplement when supervised practice opportunities are constrained

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that "screen-based 360° contextual simulation videos may offer a scalable supplement for undergraduate nursing skills education, particularly when supervised practice opportunities are constrained." The videos allow learners to control viewing direction on a laptop without a head-mounted display, providing standardized exposure to clinical procedures. The authors call for blended designs integrating video-based observation with structured hands-on practice and feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Brief orientation covering learning objectives and 360° navigation instructions
- Facilitated contextual debriefing after viewing
- Delivery via an accessible eLearning platform on standard laptops
#### Constraints
- The level of immersion and interaction is lower than fully immersive headset-based virtual reality; objective performance and long-term retention were not assessed

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students in resource-limited settings

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural action observation
- preparation before supervised clinical practice
