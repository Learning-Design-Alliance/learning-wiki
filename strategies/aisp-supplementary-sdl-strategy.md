# Use AISPs as a supplementary self-directed learning resource where direct patient contact is limited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating AI simulated patients into undergraduate curricula as a supplement to, not a replacement for, real patient interaction: "The AISP cannot replace the face-to-face patient experience but has an important role in supplementing curricula and assessments when direct patient contact is not possible." Educators can script scenarios within a pedagogical framework and increase scenario complexity as appropriate, and AISPs can also expose students to rare conditions they might not see on the ward.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- LLM-based AI used within a pedagogical framework defined by educators
- Internet access and platform licensing
#### Constraints
- Did not replicate the patient–doctor relationship or nonverbal communication nuances
- Financial feasibility was not assessed and requires additional study

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in early clinical years

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- history taking
- self-directed learning
