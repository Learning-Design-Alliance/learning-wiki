# Seven Blind Mice

## Description
Learners hear the story 'Seven Blind Mice,' stopping after each mouse’s perspective to take notes or engage in jigsaw reading. They then discuss and speculate on what the object might be based on the different perspectives, exploring how different viewpoints contribute to a fuller understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The story 'Seven Blind Mice' or a similar narrative, a space for discussions and reflections, and facilitation by an instructor to guide analysis.
#### Constraints
- Learners may struggle to understand the characters' perspectives or may impose their own biases on the interpretation. It requires careful guidance from the instructor to ensure thoughtful and respectful analysis.

### Target Learners

- All learners, particularly those who are learning about perspective-taking and collaboration

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote perspective-taking, enhance comprehension of storytelling, encourage collaborative problem-solving, improve communication skills

### Affordances
- Illustrates the importance of considering multiple perspectives to understand a situation fully. Encourages collaborative problem-solving through discussion and speculation. Improves communication skills through sharing ideas and perspectives.

### Personalization
- The level of analysis and discussion can be adjusted to the learners' abilities. The activity can be adapted for different age groups by using more complex stories or films.

## Assessment Evidence
- Assess the ability to understand and articulate different perspectives, the quality of collaborative problem-solving, and the level of critical thinking demonstrated in the analysis. Monitor changes in learners' attitudes towards different viewpoints.

## Impact
- Collaborative problem-solving has been shown to improve critical thinking, communication skills, and perspective-taking abilities. Exploring different viewpoints also promotes tolerance and understanding.
