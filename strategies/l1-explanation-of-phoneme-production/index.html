Explain how to produce target phonemes in students' first language during pronunciation instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article identifies teacher explanation in students' own language as the distinctive technique of QDPR: the teacher explains how to produce the target phonemes in Bahasa Indonesia while simultaneously demonstrating them. The article argues that "Teacher explanation in students' own language makes them easier to understand the procedural knowledge of language, particularly the system of English phonology." Explanation and demonstration are done simultaneously so students both understand and can produce the sounds.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher proficiency in the students' L1 (here Bahasa Indonesia)
  • Simultaneous explanation and demonstration of phoneme production

Constraints¶

  • Grounded in learners whose L1 is Bahasa Indonesia/Sasak; the article notes some phonemes are absent from or categorised differently in the L1

Target Learners¶

  • EFL university students with persistent segmental mispronunciations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Understanding how to produce target English phonemes
  • Accurate production of target phonemes

Affordances¶

  • Qdpr Learning Model

Claims¶

  • Common serious mispronunciations of Indonesian learners stem from absent L1 phonemes and different sound categorisation in the L1 [+W]
  • QDPR significantly improved Indonesian EFL learners' knowledge of the target English phonemes in a one-group pretest-posttest study [+W]
  • QDPR significantly improved Indonesian EFL learners' ability to pronounce the target English phonemes [+W]
  • QDPR accounted for 53% of variance in phoneme knowledge and 24% of variance in pronunciation ability in simple regressions [+W]
