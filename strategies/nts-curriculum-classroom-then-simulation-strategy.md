# Structure NTS curricula as classroom introduction first, then simulation once basic technical proficiency is reached, with debriefing and level- and specialty-specific adaptation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review's Recommendations section prescribes a sequencing strategy: "To introduce NTS concepts, teaching should be classroom based; once this is completed and there is proficiency in basic technical skills, an appropriate form of simulation can be undertaken". Curricula should be specific to seniority level and subspecialty while remaining logistically viable, use debriefing adapted to scenario and resources, and repeat NTS teaching throughout surgical training with monitoring in the real OR. The evidence includes the UK 'core trainee boot camp', which introduced NTS and NoTSS in the classroom to early trainees, and S-TEAMS, which excluded immediate post-graduate doctors lacking technical proficiency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Significant advance planning, since the review notes frequent short sessions are better but harder to organise and non-compliant or under-prepared supervisors are a problem
#### Constraints
- Training NTS at the same time as technical skills detracts from teaching quality, and training juniors alongside seniors mainly benefits juniors while detrimentally affecting seniors' teaching quality

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees at all levels
- medical students
- qualified surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- non-technical skills including decision making, leadership, teamwork and situational awareness
