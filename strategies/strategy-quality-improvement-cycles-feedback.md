# Use quality improvement cycles with two-way feedback to reinforce guideline implementation and monitor progress

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that quality improvement cycles linked to guideline recommendations may "reinforce implementation, monitor progress and provide feedback" to the whole process of guideline implementation. Cycles work when target standards have been agreed to and owned through the implementation process and the process feels reflective and appreciative rather than judgemental and critical, with good quality feedback that includes feedback from patients receiving care and serves as ongoing motivation rather than punitive action.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Target standards must be agreed to and owned through the guideline implementation process
- Feedback should be reflective and appreciative rather than judgemental and critical, and should include feedback from patients

- using feedback to change clinical practice
