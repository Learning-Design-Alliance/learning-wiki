# Represent entangled states with pairs of attached Bloch Cubes that rotate together

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because a single Bloch Cube does not naturally lend itself to entanglement, the article describes using pairs of Bloch Cubes attached side-by-side and able to rotate together forward or backward. The accompanying video shows how entanglement leads to correlations in measurement outcomes, with the number of outcomes reduced by the correlation though quantum uncertainty remains. Two types of entangled states are shown, corresponding to correlated outcomes or anti-correlated outcomes, and measurement outcomes are independent of the order in which each qubit is measured.
