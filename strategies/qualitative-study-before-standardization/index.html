Use Downey's will-profile technique as a model requiring exact qualitative study of the test situation before quantitative standardization¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

A proposed methodological attack suggested by Downey's will-profile technique: her methods of constructing test materials differ from those used to build many intelligence tests, and they suggest the need for a much more exact qualitative study of the test situation than is ordinarily made as a preliminary to final standardization in quantitative form. The article also calls for more adequate methods of representing test results, noting the profile gives a good deal of information but is difficult to interpret and rarely used in providing data for generalization.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A detailed qualitative analysis of the test situation must precede final quantitative standardization

Constraints¶

  • The article also notes a need for better methods of representing results of test performances, since current profiles are hard to interpret

Target Learners¶

  • children and adults taking intelligence and character tests

Target Learning Goals¶

  • accurate measurement and interpretation of individual differences in test performance

Related Strategies¶

  • Direct research effort toward measuring temperament and character factors, which operate largely independently of intelligence and may be more educable
  • Use qualitizing data transformation to convert quantitative results into narrative profiles for comparison with qualitative data
