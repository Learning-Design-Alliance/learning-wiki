# Redesign TBL sessions for repeat participants to restore novelty and sustain engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the management–participation link was weaker for students with two or more TBL experiences, the authors propose concrete redesigns to sustain engagement. Suggestions include adjusting "the difficulty level of case-based problems depending on the sequence in which TBL sessions are presented", strengthening feedback mechanisms, clarifying role assignments within small groups, and increasing the interval between TBL sessions to mitigate prior-exposure effects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- For students who have participated in TBL multiple times, objectives, structure, and evaluation criteria explicitly aligned with core TBL principles
#### Constraints
- The article notes a lack of empirical evidence regarding the optimal frequency and timing of TBL implementation within an academic term

### Target Learners
- Medical students with prior TBL experience

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustaining active participation and motivation across repeated TBL sessions
