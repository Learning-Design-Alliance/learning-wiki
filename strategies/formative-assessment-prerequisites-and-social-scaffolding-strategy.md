# Use formative assessment to identify missing prerequisite skills and social scaffolding to lower perceived task difficulty

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because students perceived tasks as difficult partly because they lacked prerequisite skills, the authors recommend that mathematics teachers implement formative assessment "for the very purposes of collecting data on ‘where students are?’ and ‘what prerequisite skills do they lack?’" and start instruction from students' actual level. They further recommend social scaffolding as "a means to help students benefit from the afforded opportunities to engage in and to learn more meaningfully", and an inductive approach to exercises so success on lower-order tasks builds confidence and effort.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must collect formative-assessment data on students' current level and missing prerequisite skills before teaching new content
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a correlational study in one Ethiopian secondary school; generalizability beyond that setting is limited by the authors' own geographic delimitation

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students in grades 10 and 11

### Target Learning Goals
- Mathematics achievement
- Reducing perceived task difficulty
