# Focus reduction efforts on high schools, racial/achievement disparities, and conflict prevention/de-escalation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report identifies three priority areas for districts seeking to reduce exclusionary disciplinary practices: targeting high schools (which account for 56 percent of OSS districtwide), addressing disparities affecting African American students and those with low incoming achievement, and building capacity for "prevention and de-escalation of conflict, especially between students and teachers." The authors recommend increased staff training in de-escalation and social-emotional skill development, particularly at schools with high suspension rates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires changes in high school discipline practices specifically, since efforts aimed at lower grades alone will do little to reduce overall exclusionary practices
#### Constraints
- The report notes that high suspension rates persisted for certain groups despite policy efforts aimed at reducing exclusionary practices, suggesting a need for better support in schools serving those groups

### Target Learners
- Students in grades 6-12 in urban public school districts

### Target Learning Goals
- Reduce loss of instructional time due to exclusionary discipline
- Improve school climate and safety
