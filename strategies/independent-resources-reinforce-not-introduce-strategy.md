# Do not use multimodal independent study resources in a condensed course to introduce new material

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's second lesson learned is to "resist the urge to use these resources to introduce new material" when providing multimodal independent study resources in a condensed course. In the first iteration, faculty-written supplemental readings introduced 17 new topics not covered in lecture and overwhelmed students. In the second iteration, readings enhanced lecture content rather than introducing new material, and workload perceptions improved substantially.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A succinct and manageable list of resources
- All new content introduced via face-to-face educational opportunities such as lectures and laboratories
#### Constraints
- The article notes, citing Prober and Heath, that there are limits on the amount of additional homework that can be assigned

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students in condensed preclinical courses

### Target Learning Goals
- managing workload in compressed curricula
- reinforcing lecture content through independent study
