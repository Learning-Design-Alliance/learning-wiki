# Provide structured, multi-layered university support programmes combining curricular structuring, mentoring, psychosocial support and financial support for quota-admitted students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that universities and policymakers go beyond allocating study places and offer structured support to quota-admitted students, because work-study conflicts, financial burdens and uncertainty about future obligations threaten academic success and the programme's rural-workforce goal. It states that "comprehensive university-based programmes that combine curricular structuring, mentoring, and psychosocial support are essential to mitigate these risks and enable RDQ students to successfully complete their studies." Clear, updated information about underserved areas and transparent internship and scholarship criteria are named as essential for building trust.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional resources for curricular structuring, mentoring and psychosocial support
- Targeted financial support at federal or state level
- Clear and updated information about underserved areas and transparent scholarship criteria
#### Constraints
- Many uncertainties about future working regions cannot be reliably predicted due to future dynamics within the healthcare system

### Target Learners
- Medical students admitted through a rural admission quota
- Older students with prior vocational training and family obligations

### Target Learning Goals
- Successful completion of medical studies
- Long-term retention of doctors in rural general practice
