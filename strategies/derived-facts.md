# Derived Facts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The derived facts strategy asks learners to compute unknown facts by reasoning from known ones — for example, deriving 7 × 8 from 7 × 7 + 7, or 9 + 6 from 9 + 1 + 5. Rather than drilling every fact to immediate recall, learners build a small core of automatic facts and a repertoire of decomposition and compensation strategies to derive the rest. Over repeated use, derivation itself becomes fast, and many derived facts consolidate into direct recall.

## Design Implications

Derived facts leverage existing knowledge as a scaffold for new knowledge, reducing the memory burden of learning large fact sets by brute repetition [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. The strategy builds number sense and flexibility alongside fluency: learners who derive facts understand *why* the answers are correct, which supports transfer and error correction [~S]. It also functions as formative assessment — the derivations a learner chooses reveal their conceptual understanding.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A secure base of known facts (typically small doubles, tens facts, or 2s, 5s, 10s in multiplication)
- Explicit instruction in a small set of derivation strategies (near doubles, adding one, compensation, partitioning)
- Timely practice so derivations become efficient rather than laborious ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))

#### Constraints
- If the known-fact base is weak, derivation overloads working memory and produces errors [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-S]
- Learners who remain slow derivers under time pressure may experience frustration and avoid the strategy; fluency development must not be neglected [~M]
- Over-reliance on counting-based derivation (e.g., counting on by ones) predicts persistent low achievement and should be redirected toward decomposition [~S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Interview-based diagnosis**: teachers ask "How did you figure that out?" to identify which facts are known and which strategies are available (the basis of [Cognitively Guided Instruction](../patterns/cognitively-guided-instruction-cgi-for-math.md))
- **Strategy modeling**: the instructor demonstrates a derivation aloud, then prompts learners to generate alternative derivations for the same fact
- **Fact families**: presenting related facts together (6 + 7, 7 + 6, 13 − 6, 13 − 7) so each known fact supports several others

### Target Learners
- Elementary mathematics learners moving from counting strategies to reasoning-based recall
- Learners with gaps in fact knowledge who need a functional workaround while fluency develops
- Less appropriate for learners who already have automatic recall — derivation adds unnecessary steps [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Fluency with basic facts grounded in understanding rather than rote memorization
- Conceptual structures: properties of operations (commutativity, distributivity, compensation)
- Strategic competence: selecting and adapting computation methods flexibly

### Instructions
1. Diagnose the learner's known facts and current strategies through a brief oral interview; record which facts are automatic, derived, or counted.
2. Select an unknown fact that is *near* a known fact (e.g., 8 + 6 near the double 7 + 7, or 6 × 7 near 6 × 5).
3. Model a derivation with explicit reasoning ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)), making the relationship between known and unknown facts visible.
4. Ask the learner to derive the same fact a second way, and compare the strategies ([Comparing Cases](../elements/comparing-cases.md)) to build flexibility.
5. Repeat the derived fact in varied contexts over subsequent sessions until it is recalled directly; log progress and retire mastered facts.

## Examples
- **Cognitively Guided Instruction (CGI)** — teachers use children's derived-fact strategies as the basis for instruction in number and operations; see [Cognitively Guided Instruction](../patterns/cognitively-guided-instruction-cgi-for-math.md).
- **Number Talks (Parrish, 2010)** — daily classroom routines in which students mentally compute a fact or problem and share their derivation strategies, making multiple derivation paths public.
- **Math Recovery** — one-to-one intervention programs that assess whether a child derives or counts, and target instruction at moving them toward derived-fact strategies.
