# Use active patient-side surgeon roles in robotic surgery to maintain trainee motivation and laparoscopic proficiency when laparoscopic case volume declines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends giving assistant surgeons active roles in robotic gastrectomy so that training continues despite declining laparoscopic case numbers. It argues that "DST can solve these problems by giving assistant surgeons roles in robotic surgery while maintaining the quality of surgical education", and that active involvement is expected to increase trainee motivation. Because the surgical process is nearly identical for LG and RG, skills gained on the patient side transfer when the trainee becomes console-side surgeon.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A robotic gastrectomy setup in which the patient-side surgeon can perform clipping, stapling, energy-device dissection and field development
- A console-side surgeon available to guide and take over when needed
#### Constraints
- The strategy was evaluated in a small retrospective single-center cohort, not a randomized controlled trial
- Many hospitals lack the equipment for robotic gastrectomy

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees in hospitals where robotic cases are increasing and laparoscopic cases are decreasing

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition and maintenance of laparoscopic gastrectomy proficiency and preparation for robotic gastrectomy
