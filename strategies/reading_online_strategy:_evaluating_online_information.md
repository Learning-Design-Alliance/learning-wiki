# Reading Online Strategy: Evaluating Online Information

## Description
Students learn to analyze and evaluate the information they encounter online by assessing the reliability of websites. This includes mini-lessons on the differences between .com, .gov, .org, and .edu domains. Students examine selected websites (both reliable and unreliable sources) and check for the following elements: can the information presented be corroborated elsewhere, is the writer of the information reliable, is the information current, is the information documented, is the website advocating for something and therefore potentially challenged as a neutral source, and is there a conflict of interest present? Students also examine famous website hoaxes and search for clues that suggest it is a hoax, as well as mini-lessons on propaganda techniques and identifying the use of the same techniques in online advertising.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a variety of websites, time for explicit instruction, and guided practice.
#### Constraints
- May require significant instructional time for learners to develop evaluation skills. Requires ongoing practice and application of the strategies.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Middle school, high school, and adult learners who need to improve their digital literacy skills.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance critical thinking skills, develop evaluation strategies for online information, identify reliable sources, recognize propaganda techniques, understand website domains

### Affordances
- Provides a clear set of criteria for evaluating online information, supports critical thinking and reasoning skills, and enhances digital literacy.

### Personalization
- Adapt the complexity of the websites examined based on the learner's level. Provide different levels of support for identifying and evaluating information.
