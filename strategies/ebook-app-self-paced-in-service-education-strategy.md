# Offer interactive E-book apps for nurses' self-paced in-service education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends interactive E-book apps as an in-service education strategy for nurses facing workload and staffing barriers to face-to-face courses. E-book apps "allow individuals to control the time and place of learning" and let nurses "self-regulate their learning, manage their time, seek assistance, and perform self-evaluations". The trial combined app-based self-learning with a shared face-to-face lecture and found greater knowledge, attitude, and confidence gains than lecture alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Systematic, evidence-based content (built on the 2014 PI guidelines) and interactive multimedia features such as notes, bookmarks, and question prompts
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness was assessed over only a four-week follow-up in one hospital; the authors recommend longer follow-ups and multi-setting studies

### Target Learners
- registered nurses in clinical in-service education

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical knowledge, attitudes, and care confidence for pressure injury prevention and wound care
