# Pair VR visualization interventions with constructive interactivity (e.g., controller-based input) rather than passive viewing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the meta-study account and their own null treatment effect, the authors recommend making VR interventions "much more constructively interactive through the use of a Bluetooth controller paired to the phone, allowing more diverse input." The strategy treats passive look-around visualizations as insufficient and adds student-controlled change in the virtual environment as the design target for future VR learning materials.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- VR hardware supporting diverse input, such as a Bluetooth controller paired to the smartphone
#### Constraints
- The article notes Madden et al. and Brown et al. found small treatment differences even with more interactive AR/VR interventions, so the strategy's benefit is not yet established

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students learning 3D magnetostatics

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding magnetic fields, forces, and torques in three dimensions
