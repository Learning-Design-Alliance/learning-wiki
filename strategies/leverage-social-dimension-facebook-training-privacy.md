# Professors integrating Facebook should leverage its social dimension while providing training and addressing privacy concerns.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating Facebook into university learning resources by taking advantage of its social nature to increase communication, collaboration and participation. It advises that "Before using Facebook for class purposes, the professor should provide specific training about the features of Facebook that will be used in the course and address any kind of concern expressed by students," and cites the recommendation to use a "shell profile" for the professor with no personal information.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Specific training on the Facebook features used in the course
- Addressing student concerns before class use
#### Constraints
- Students care about privacy when communicating with instructors through social media; "friending" professors is still taboo for most students per the ECAR study

### Target Learners
- undergraduate university students

### Target Learning Goals
- communication and collaboration in course contexts
- increasing student participation
