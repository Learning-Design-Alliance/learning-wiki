# Evaluate AI literacy interventions with behavioral measures of LLM interaction in addition to self-reports

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that AI literacy interventions be evaluated with behavioral measures in addition to self-reports, because self-reports "may miss important differences in actual use". The recommended behaviors include prompt acceptance or reformulation, correctness judgments of LLM responses, and follow-up questioning, treated as indicators of applied, procedural AI literacy rather than declarative knowledge alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Task settings in which students interact with an LLM so that prompt decisions, correctness judgments, and follow-up questions can be logged
#### Constraints
- The article frames prompt acceptance as an experimental probe, noting students would often write their own prompts from scratch in ecological classroom use

### Target Learners
- middle-school students using LLM-based chatbots for school tasks

### Target Learning Goals
- applied regulation of LLM interaction during problem solving
