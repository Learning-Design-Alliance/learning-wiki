# Design lab instruction to strengthen inclusive climates and structured opportunities for student-authored experimental reasoning, foregrounding epistemic quality over participation alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implications-for-practice section recommends interventions that "strengthen inclusive lab climates and create structured opportunities for student-authored experimental reasoning, while foregrounding the epistemological quality of engagement rather than participation alone." It also proposes treating critical thinking as a configuration rather than a score, so instructors can identify which specific PLIC dimensions students struggle with and tailor instruction accordingly. The recommendation rests on the CLPM findings that belonging prospectively predicts epistemic profile membership and that agency may not be uniformly beneficial unless directed toward model testing and evidence evaluation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lab settings where belonging, self-efficacy, agency, and PLIC critical-thinking outcomes can be measured pre and post
#### Constraints
- The cross-lagged panel model cannot establish causation in the absence of experimental manipulation
- Identity measures were brief embedded items, not full validated scales
- Dataset dominated by North American institutions

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics lab students in introductory and beyond-first-year courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Critical thinking about experimental data, methods, and next steps
- Physics identity constructs including belonging, recognition, self-efficacy, and agency
