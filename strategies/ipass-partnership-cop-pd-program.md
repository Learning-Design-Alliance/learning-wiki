# Sustained university–school partnership PD with material provision and peer support to shift teachers toward open-ended labs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The IPaSS partnership program builds a physics teacher Community of Practice around prolonged, physics-specific professional development: teachers receive "100 hours of in-person and online professional development for up to four years," research-based university-level lab materials including class sets of iOLab devices, structured opportunities to share course materials with one another, year-round implementation support from developers and peers, and a pathway toward becoming program leaders and mentors. The strategy aims to give teachers the tools to overcome barriers to open-ended lab instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained physics-specific PD of 100 hours over up to four years, a university partnership providing research-based materials and device access, and peer sharing and support structures throughout the school year.
#### Constraints
- Teachers are not required to choose an open-ended style for their labs or to use the university labs.

### Target Learners
- high school physics teachers, including novice and out-of-field teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- implementing open-ended inquiry labs
- overcoming structural and perception-related barriers to lab reform
