# Use a non-directive approach during PAT dosing sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a non-directive approach as the standard posture during PAT dosing sessions, where most protocols encourage facilitators to minimize interventions. The participant is empowered to lead the process rather than the facilitator imposing a pre-loaded agenda. Chaplains develop expertise in this through CPE, restraining impulses toward advice-giving and directive leadership. The article states that "Psychedelic practitioners from other disciplines often find this non-directive approach counter-intuitive and must adapt to and develop the skill of non-directive presence."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training in holding space with compassion, patience, and steady strength
- Developing the skill of non-directive presence, especially when strong emotions are expressed
#### Constraints
- In dosing sessions, participants cannot be expected to have a typical, rational, and/or linear conversation

### Target Learners
- Chaplains
- Facilitators in psychedelic-assisted therapy

### Target Learning Goals
- Supporting participants through non-ordinary states of consciousness
- Allowing participants to lead their own process
