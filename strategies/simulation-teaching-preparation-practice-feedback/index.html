Provide preparatory materials, scenario introductions, adequate practice time, and constructive feedback in simulation teaching¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that simulation teaching be supported by "providing preparatory materials and scenario introductions" to enhance students' theoretical foundation and psychological readiness, alongside "adequate practice time and appropriate training frequency" and "timely and constructive feedback" after simulation to help students understand their performance and identify areas for improvement.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to simulation infrastructure and materials
  • Sufficient time for repeated practice and feedback sessions

Constraints¶

  • Simulation teaching requires certain teaching infrastructure and materials
  • Students may focus on completing practical tasks rather than deep theoretical application

Target Learners¶

  • Medical students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Clinical knowledge acquisition and skills development through simulation

Claims¶

  • Simulation teaching improves medical students' multiple-choice question scores compared to traditional teaching [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Embedding online simulation throughout curricula and providing more student access to online simulation approaches
  • Design and implement VS teaching with simplified interfaces, multimodal feedback, multilingual diverse case libraries, pre-training, and controlled VR session lengths
  • Use ANN-based adaptive feedback to provide scalable, process-oriented real-time feedback at scale in higher education
