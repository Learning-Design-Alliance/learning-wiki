# Use particularly challenging procedural steps as the basis for targeted simulation-based training enabling deliberate practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that step-level proficiency findings from real-life supervised operations be fed back into training design: "Particularly challenging steps of a procedure can be used as basis for developing specific training opportunities, i.e. by the use of simulation-based training". This targeted simulation work is presented as enabling deliberate practice — training focused on improving a particular task — which the authors call a prerequisite for competency-based surgical education. In this study, identifying the appendix in challenging cases was the step with lowest proficiency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Step-level proficiency evaluation of each supervised procedure to identify which steps are challenging
#### Constraints
- The authors note many contributing factors to performance cannot be controlled for but should be considered when deciding whether a patient is suitable for a trainee

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees in structured appendectomy training programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- improving performance on the most challenging procedural steps toward proficiency
