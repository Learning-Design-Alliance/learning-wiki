# Teach algebra word problems through the three-phase sequence of modeling, scaffolding, and collaboration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's treatment recipe for each algebra word problem: first modeling, in which the trained teacher solved the problem "using loud thinking technique so that learners can observe and imitate" how to convert the word problem into an equation; second scaffolding through step-by-step teaching, two-way questioning, discussion, and audio-visual aids; third collaboration, with the class divided into six groups of no more than four students, each containing at least one bright student, brainstorming for five minutes, solving collaboratively, and presenting. The article reports this sequence produced significantly better outcomes than one-way teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher trained in the three constructivist steps
- existing intact class groups whose members are familiar with one another for collaborative work
#### Constraints
- Tested only on the ten word-problems of Exercise 7.10, Unit 7 'Algebra' of the Sindh Textbook Board grade-7 mathematics curriculum, over six 40-minute periods in one boys high school

### Target Learners
- grade-7 public secondary students in Sindh, Pakistan

### Target Learning Goals
- solving algebraic word problems using operations of variables
