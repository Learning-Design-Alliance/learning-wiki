# Do-it-yourself black hole workshop: build and compare sector models to introduce curved space descriptively

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A workshop sequence in which participants first classify surface curvature with the tearing-or-buckling criterion, then accept the plane-laid-out representation via the flatlanders thought experiment, and finally build three-dimensional cardboard sector models of euclidean space and of the space around a black hole from cut-out sheets. The concept is "presented in a descriptive way in order to promote a qualitative understanding of the properties of curved spaces". The black hole is chosen because relativistic effects near it are large enough to be clearly visible in a true-to-scale model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cut-out sheets for the euclidean and black hole models (available in the online resources)
- At least three slices should be built per model so quarter-spheres can be assembled
#### Constraints
- The workshop has been run with high-school students, undergraduates and interested adults; the authors report development "in several cycles of testing and revision" but print no controlled evaluation data

### Target Learners
- high school students
- undergraduate students
- interested adults
- students intending to become physics teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- determine the sign of curvature from model behaviour
- understand that euclidean geometry fails in curved space
