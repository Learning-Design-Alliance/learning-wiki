# Teach self- and peer-assessment by starting with rubrics, then glows, then scaffolded grows

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A sequenced recipe for building students' assessment skills: first teach students to use a rubric or checklist independently by self-assessing and comparing their rating with the teacher's; then have students exchange positive feedback ("glows") using prompts for active engagement, contributed intellectual ideas, active listening, and question quality; finally scaffold to improvement feedback ("grows"), using "I wonder" phrasing to make feedback less direct. This empowers students to monitor their own and others' progress and become more independent, self-directed learners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A classroom culture of learning and groups set up with collaboration skills, with the teacher intentionally guiding and supporting the process
#### Constraints
- Teacher must intentionally guide and support the process; peer assessment fails without this support

### Target Learners
- K-12 and STEM students in collaborative learning settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-assessment and peer-assessment skills
- reflection
- collaborative communication
