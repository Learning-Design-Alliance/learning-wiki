# Target academic and language skills at high intensity over a long period, and favor longer duration over higher weekly hours for executive function skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (5 for, 3 mixed) · 5 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 2 associational), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article translates its domain-specific dose–response findings into dosage-allocation guidance for clinicians designing individualized ABA programs. Because duration coefficients exceeded intensity coefficients across all domains, and the academic and language domains showed the strongest responses, the article recommends that "clinicians should target academic and language skills at a high intensity, over a long period of time." For executive function, it suggests applying fewer hours per week in favor of a longer treatment period.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clinicians must be able to monitor mastered learning objectives per domain using a treatment-tracking system such as Skills, and distribute treatment hours across domains according to individual skill deficits.
#### Constraints
- The article states there is no empirical evidence that high intensity without consideration for duration yields optimal results; treatment hours were not randomly assigned, and hours per domain were estimated proportionally rather than recorded directly.

### Target Learners
- Children with autism spectrum disorder aged 18 months to 12 years receiving community-based ABA services

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition of academic, language, and executive function skills as measured by mastered learning objectives
