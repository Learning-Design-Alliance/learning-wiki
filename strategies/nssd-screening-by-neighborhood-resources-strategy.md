# Target youth with NSSD from resource-depleted communities for executive-function screening and monitor all NSSD youth for academic difficulties

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors translate their mixed findings into differentiated screening guidance: because the working-memory interaction showed "an additive adverse impact of environmental resource depletion for youth with NSSD", youth from resource-depleted environments should be prioritized for executive-skills monitoring, while academic difficulties warrant screening for all NSSD youth regardless of neighborhood resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of youth's neighborhood resource level (e.g., via census-based indices such as the Child Opportunity Index)
#### Constraints
- The article states academic intervention efficacy has not been specifically tested in NSSD
- Cross-sectional design precludes causal interpretation

### Target Learners
- Youth with Noonan Syndrome Spectrum Disorder ages 4-17

### Target Learning Goals
- Executive functioning (working memory, daily executive skills)
- Academic achievement (reading, math, spelling, comprehension)
