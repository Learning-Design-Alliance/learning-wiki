Support struggling readers and writers with increased practice time, skill diagnosis and guidance, and targeted feedback during practice¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (3 review), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The editors argue that corrective measures for poor reading and writing skills should include increased time for students to practice, diagnosis of weak skills with guidance, and targeted feedback during practice, citing prior work. Because teachers and schools typically lack the resources for such supports, they recommend developing automated methods for evaluation, guidance, feedback, and instruction deployable across educational technologies.

Requirements¶

  • Sufficient practice time, diagnostic guidance, and targeted feedback during practice, which the editors note teachers and schools typically lack the resources to provide

Target Learners¶

  • middle school through college students below grade-level standards in reading and writing

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading and writing proficiency, including reading and writing in STEM subjects

Claims¶

  • Corrective word-reading feedback effects depend on timing and reader skill: delayed feedback enhanced word-reading accuracy, and phonics feedback helped poor readers while better readers performed best with no feedback [~M]
  • The National Reading Panel provided no evidence that phonics helps the majority of struggling readers above grade 1 [~M]
  • Combining reading comprehension and writing strategy instruction in an ITS is significantly more effective than either strategy alone [+M]
  • The ten special-issue papers collectively indicate near-term feasibility of automated guidance and feedback on reading and writing skills [+M]
