Multi-format teacher professional development for implementing the Facing History and Ourselves curriculum¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The program's professional development model combines an intensive Summer Institute, introductory workshops, online courses, and ongoing follow-up support. The report states that 'A Summer Institute provides an intensive five- or six-day seminar focusing on issues related to identity, violence, bigotry, power, and conformity,' in which teachers explore ways to apply the content and approaches to their own teaching. Follow-up classroom support continues during the school year in person, by phone and email, and on the website, supplemented by a lending library, guest speaker lists, videos, and technical assistance.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • In the evaluated study, each FHAO teacher had attended the FHAO Institute and had taught the FHAO curriculum for at least three years before the study

Constraints¶

  • Professional development carries costs: Summer Institute fees of $650 for commuting and $900 for resident participants, online courses $300, workshops $35-$250

Target Learners¶

  • middle and high school teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • applying Facing History and Ourselves content and approaches to teaching history and ethics

Related Strategies¶

  • Design professional development that is intensive, on-site, peer-based, responsive, and grounded in teachers' real classroom experiences
  • Redesign workshop structure, content, timing, and location in response to non-participant barriers
  • Support curriculum implementation with initial multi-day training plus ongoing onsite consultation during the school year
  • Layered implementation support for teachers adopting a new science curriculum: preservice training, follow-up training, and a science coach
  • Provide structured multi-phase professional development with ongoing support for intervention teachers
  • Provide 70 hours of professional development in three phases: intensive training, practice, and implementation
  • Layered professional development for curriculum implementation (webinars, workshops, coaching)
  • Multi-format professional learning for curriculum implementation (on-site, virtual, blended, coaching)
  • Online asynchronous professional learning series completed in teams across the school year
  • Combine summer professional development with school-year on-site coaching and provision of complete instructional materials
  • Provide initial training plus multi-year in-classroom coaching when implementing Carnegie Learning Algebra I
  • Implement EWIMS through combined in-person and online training with ongoing technical assistance liaisons
  • Provide on-site first-year training of 4-5 hours plus building-level ongoing support each year of ST Math implementation
  • Implement Reading Edge with two days of initial teacher training plus monthly in-class coaching and peer support structures
  • DCPS recommends regular AP teacher participation in the Advanced Placement Summer Institute to promote quality AP instruction
