# Teach an early, actively delivered learning-strategies module in medical school with spaced follow-up modules

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 mixed, 1 unmarked) · 6 studies (2 causal, 2 quant-synthesis, 2 review), `q3`–`q4` · 4 of 6 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The author proposes a curriculum program conveying the testing effect, active recall, and spaced repetition, taught "actively by posing questions and quizzing students" and itself repeated at expanding intervals. A basis module of several hours presents the concepts and scientific background, followed by short repetition and application modules restudied at expanding intervals such as 7, 15, 30, and 60 days later.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A basis module of several hours presenting the concepts and scientific background, plus short follow-up modules for repetition and application
#### Constraints
- Time in the medical curriculum is scarce since the length of medical education remains constant despite growing knowledge; the author notes content may not be the most important issue, the key being a constructive atmosphere and awareness of learning

### Target Learners
- Medical students, especially those in year 1

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective learning and long-term retention of factual medical knowledge
