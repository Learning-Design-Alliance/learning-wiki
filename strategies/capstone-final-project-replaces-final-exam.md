# Replace the final exam with a capstone-style programming project with code, report, and presentation components

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course replaced a final exam with a capstone project in which teams selected and defined a problem, developed a Q# solution runnable on the simulator, wrote a short research-style report, and gave a 10-minute presentation with live demo. The article states this aligned with its "hands-on, practical introduction to quantum computing" and taught research skills, collaboration, writing, and public speaking alongside quantum software development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teams of about three students working over four weeks
- Project code must solve at least some problem instances on the QDK quantum simulator
#### Constraints
- Students must analyze the problem to find a small but non-trivial instance solvable in simulation (e.g., around 30 qubits on a laptop for a graph coloring project)

### Target Learners
- 3rd and 4th year undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- quantum software development
- scientific communication
- collaboration
- public speaking
