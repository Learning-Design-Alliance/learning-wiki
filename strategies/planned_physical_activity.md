# Planned Physical Activity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Planned physical activity is the deliberate scheduling of movement — sport, active transport, family activity, structured play — into a learner's daily and weekly routine, rather than leaving it to unstructured spare time. For pre-teens and teenagers, who need roughly 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity per day, planning is what converts good intentions into consistent behavior. The planning process considers available spaces, low-cost options, active friends, and activities the family can do together, and balances activity against homework, work, and social commitments.

## Design Implications

Physical activity is not time taken from learning; it supports it. Regular activity is associated with improved attention and executive function in school-age learners [Classroom physical activity improves attention.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+M], and single bouts of acute exercise can transiently enhance memory encoding shortly afterward [Acute exercise timing affects memory.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~M]. Because adolescents' schedules fill quickly, activity that is not planned is reliably displaced — making the schedule itself the active ingredient of the strategy.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of age-appropriate activity guidelines (e.g., 60 min/day for ages 6–17)
- A realistic weekly schedule that names when, where, and with whom activity will occur
- Support from family, friends, or a program that makes planned sessions socially self-sustaining
- Low-barrier options: walkable routes, free or cheap facilities, equipment at hand

#### Constraints
- Requires discipline and commitment; plans decay quickly without social or family support [-M]
- Adolescents with heavy homework, caregiving, or work loads may find rigid plans unsustainable; over-scheduling can crowd out sleep, which itself supports learning [-W]
- Activity planned at fixed times may conflict with natural variation in energy and motivation; rigid adherence can undermine autonomy and intrinsic motivation [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Active transport** — walking or cycling to school embeds activity in the existing routine at zero cost
- **Family-based activity** — shared outings (hikes, bike rides, backyard games) build the social support that sustains adherence
- **School-embedded activity** — active recess, classroom movement breaks, or before-school sport shift the planning burden to the institution
- **Timing for learning** — scheduling demanding academic work shortly after moderate activity may exploit acute exercise effects on attention and memory [Acute exercise timing affects memory.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~W]

### Target Learners
- Pre-teens and teenagers, who must accumulate substantial daily activity but control little of their own schedules
- Adolescents whose activity has declined with transition to secondary school, when structured sport often drops off [-M]
- Less applicable to learners with medical restrictions without clinical adaptation

### Target Learning Goals
- Health and physical literacy: meeting daily activity requirements and building lifelong habits
- Self-regulation: planning, scheduling, and balancing competing commitments
- Academic support: improved attention and readiness for learning in adjacent study time [Classroom physical activity improves attention.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Review guidelines and current activity levels to identify the gap to close.
2. Inventory resources: available spaces, low-cost options, active friends, family activities.
3. Build a weekly plan that names specific days, times, and activities, balancing against homework and social commitments; use [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) routines to review adherence weekly.
4. Anchor activity to existing routines (active transport, after-school slots) to reduce reliance on willpower.
5. Track activity and well-being, and adjust the plan rather than abandoning it when a week goes off-schedule.
