# Reintroduce Rasch analysis across selection processes, potentially augmented by artificial intelligence, to minimise differential attainment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes Rasch analysis as a psychometric approach to reduce differential attainment in selection by ensuring all candidates are assessed on the same scale and adjusting for differential item functioning, thereby correcting for unconscious biases. It notes Rasch analysis "has been used previously in General Surgery selection, but could helpfully be reintroduced across all processes", and suggests artificial intelligence might be employed where trained human statisticians are scarce.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Appropriately trained statisticians or AI augmentation, which the article notes are scarce
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a proposal for future processes; its effect on equity in ST3 selection was not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- Applicants to UK ST3 surgical national selection

### Target Learning Goals
- Fair, bias-corrected assessment of all candidates on the same scale
