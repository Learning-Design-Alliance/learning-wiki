# Use a satellite-data-driven, web-accessible dose tool to connect space-weather theory with real-time radiation-protection practice for undergraduates

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend teaching space radiation protection through a tool whose inputs are directly taken from operating satellites, so that students tackle real observational data rather than only statistical worst-case scenarios. They report that space-weather courses "rarely allows students to get in touch with the daily practical issues faced by technicians" in the field, and devised DREADCode to fill this educational gap between theoretical/observational study and its effects on the human environment. The strategy asks students to configure shields, choose data sources and interpret dose outputs in a web GUI.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the online GUI on the European Space Weather Portal and to the satellite data feeds it uses
- Students need background spanning astrophysics, material engineering and radioprotection, the disciplines the authors say the subject combines
#### Constraints
- The tool is educational, not an operational or shield-design instrument
- Satellite data sources have limited life-times comparable to few years, which the authors note as a reliability limit over long periods

### Target Learners
- undergraduate university students
- advanced secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting radiation-protection theory with real-time observational practice
- assessing dose exposure for interplanetary missions
