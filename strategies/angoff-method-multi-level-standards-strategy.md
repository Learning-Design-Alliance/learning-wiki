# Use the Angoff method to set passing standards for multiple levels of proficiency, not only minimal competence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that when constructing CBE curricula, educators attend to assessment methods, since "The Angoff method is a widely accepted method of standards setting". The original method "uses expert judges to determine an expected passing score for a level of proficiency", while the modified version uses multiple rounds of iterations to enhance agreement between experts, with test data further enhancing credibility of the passing score. The review argues the method "could be used for calculating scores for all levels of expertise", countering criticism of promoting mediocrity and minimum standards.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Expert judges participating in standard-setting rounds
- Test data from resulting assessments to enhance credibility of the passing score
#### Constraints
- The included studies used the Angoff method to determine the minimal passing score, although only for one level

### Target Learners
- Residents at different levels of expertise in procedural training

### Target Learning Goals
- Documenting trainee progress across multiple proficiency levels and countering minimum-standards criticism
