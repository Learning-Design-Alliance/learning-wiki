# Use small groups of well-known co-students with rotating roles as simulator and respondent to foster psychological safety and peer learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a strategy in which students are divided into small groups of well-known co-students, with different roles during simulation: either conducting the dialogue simulation or acting as a respondent listening and responding based on feedback and feedforward principles. The article reports that "The fact that the groups were small and familiar was important for the students to help them create a safe environment", and that students appreciated observing how fellow students solved the task.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Groups small and composed of well-known co-students
- A facilitator present in the same room who could see and hear the simulating student and the avatar responses
#### Constraints
- A few students who did not feel safe or accomplished noted they wanted more information beforehand

### Target Learners
- Second-year bachelor physiotherapy and occupational therapy students

### Target Learning Goals
- Communication skills and peer learning through observation and reflection
