# Identify absence-related academic problems in urban students during early elementary grades, before related problems worsen in later schooling

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (3 associational, 1 review), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that because more robust estimates of the absence-achievement relationship can be obtained with family fixed effects methods, educators and researchers can identify the negative academic issues tied to missing school earlier in students' schooling careers. It states that "the negative academic issues pertaining to absences can be more accurately identified before these young students enter into later grades where academic, sociological, and economic problems related to missing school only worsen." The strategy rests on the article's finding that the negative relationship is already detectable in third- and fourth-grade observations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to longitudinal district records linking absences to standardized test outcomes across years
#### Constraints
- The article notes the negative relationship between absences and achievement tends to worsen for students as they progress into the later grades of schooling, per prior literature it cites

### Target Learners
- urban elementary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- early identification of students whose absences are harming reading and math achievement
