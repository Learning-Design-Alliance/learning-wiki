# Support EBM training graduates beyond the course with follow-up support, exchange opportunities and counselling training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because 35% of interviewed participants reported not practising any EBM skills six months after the course, and because barriers included "a lack of further support and possibilities to exchange experiences beyond the programme", the authors conclude that implementation of EBM skills needs support beyond the training course. Nearly a quarter of participants expressed the need for long-term support related to counselling and communication training or further education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training graduates with basic EBM competencies who intend to apply them in advocacy or counselling roles
#### Constraints
- The article's evidence is a descriptive six-month follow-up with unblinded assessors, so intervention-related effects cannot be quantified

### Target Learners
- Patient and consumer representatives who completed EBM training

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained implementation of critical appraisal and risk-communication skills in advocacy and counselling practice
