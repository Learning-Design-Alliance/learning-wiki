# Use structured scenario prompts with diagrams and explicit list-anything prompts to broaden the uncertainty sources students access

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's comparison of its survey with a prior interview study suggests that adding structure — a diagram of the experimental setup, a more detailed description of equipment and procedures, and an explicit prompt to "list as many causes as you can think of" — made a greater variety of uncertainty sources accessible to students, including limitations in quantum contexts that interviews had missed. Instructors and researchers can use such structured prompts when they want students to consider both limitations and principles rather than only the most accessible source.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A specific experimental scenario with diagram and detailed equipment description
#### Constraints
- Even with careful structure, students did not consistently draw on both limitations and principles in all relevant contexts

### Target Learners
- Upper-level undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Broadening the range of sources of measurement uncertainty students consider
