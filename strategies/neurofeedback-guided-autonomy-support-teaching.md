# Use INS as an online marker of learning quality to trigger dynamically increased autonomy support during teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that as portable neuroimaging matures, teacher–student INS can serve as a real-time indicator of interaction quality rather than only a post-hoc measure. It suggests that educators could "dynamically adapt their teaching strategies—such as increasing autonomy support—when a dip in neural alignment is detected", enabling responsive, personalized neurofeedback-guided teaching. In the article this is a forward-looking practical implication of the finding that lPFC INS marks autonomy-supportive interactions and tracks students' need satisfaction and positive emotions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Portable fNIRS or EEG technology capable of measuring teacher–student neural alignment in near real time
- An established link between the neural marker (lPFC INS) and the teaching behavior to be adjusted
#### Constraints
- The proposal is prospective; this study used a 20-minute lab-simulated one-to-one setting and did not implement real-time neurofeedback
- INS in this study did not correlate with test scores, so the marker reflects process outcomes (need satisfaction, positive emotions) rather than achievement

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in one-to-one instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- autonomous motivation
- positive academic emotions
- autonomy need satisfaction
