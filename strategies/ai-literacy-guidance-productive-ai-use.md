# Treat AI literacy as educational support by teaching students to question AI responses, attempt problems first, and avoid substituting AI output for engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that educators and policy makers guide students toward productive AI-use behavior rather than merely granting or restricting access. It states: "Policy makers and educators should treat AI literacy as educational support, teaching students to question AI responses, attempt problems before consultation, and avoid substituting AI output for active engagement." The recommendation rests on the study's finding that engagement pattern, not access alone, is associated with exam outcomes, and that equal access can produce unequal learning returns.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators or systems able to monitor and guide students' AI-use behavior during learning
#### Constraints
- The article notes longitudinal and intervention studies with much larger samples are needed to address whether targeted guidance can improve learning behavior

### Target Learners
- university students using generative AI in coursework

### Target Learning Goals
- productive AI-use learning behavior supporting knowledge acquisition rather than answer substitution
