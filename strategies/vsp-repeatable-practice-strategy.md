# Use repeatable virtual standardized patient practice with real-time feedback as a low-cost supplement to bedside and standardized patient teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating virtual standardized patient systems into clinical skills courses because they are repeatable and provide real-time feedback: "real-time feedback offered students valuable insights into the overall quality of their performance and identified areas that needed further improvement." Because the web-based system requires only one-time development with small maintenance funds, the authors conclude it is "a feasible, practical, and cost-effective alternative, and it is worthy of promotion," especially where occupational or student standardized patients are too costly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A developed case database of foundational diseases, an intelligent human-computer interaction environment accepting text or voice commands, and teacher training before the course
#### Constraints
- The system should be more consistent with the characteristics of TCM, such as simulating patients with different syndromes instead of diseases; it cannot convey some physical signs of real patients

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical and TCM students in clinical skills courses

### Target Learning Goals
- medical interviewing, syndrome differentiation and treatment, interpersonal communication, and autonomous learning
