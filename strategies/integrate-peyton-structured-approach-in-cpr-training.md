# Integrate Peyton's structured four-step approach into CPR training programs, particularly for critical procedural skills, and consider combining it with Kolb's experiential cycle

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend incorporating Peyton's structured, stepwise instructional approach into standardized CPR training programs because it emphasizes guided demonstration and repetitive practice under supervision. They further suggest that "combining elements of both the Kolb and Peyton methods, such as experiential learning with structured repetition, could further optimize CPR education". The recommendation rests on Peyton's significantly greater skill gains in this study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured sessions with demonstration, step-by-step guided practice, and supervised repetition on a model, as delivered in the study's six-hour sessions
#### Constraints
- Drug therapy skills did not improve under either method, so the authors note medication administration during resuscitation may require more targeted interventions or practical exposure

### Target Learners
- Nurses across various departments
- Healthcare providers in CPR training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural CPR skills, particularly cardiac massage, breathing, and defibrillator use
