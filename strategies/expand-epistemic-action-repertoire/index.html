Expand students' repertoire of epistemic actions and teach when each evidence type is appropriate rather than replacing purportedly poor beliefs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article proposes that epistemic-cognition instruction should not simply replace purportedly undesirable beliefs with more sophisticated ones. Instead, "it would be better to (a) expand the repertoire of epistemic actions available to a student and (b) develop in the student an appreciation both the limitations of different types of epistemic actions as well as situations in which they are useful and appropriate to invoke." This reframes empirical and authoritarian evidence as sometimes appropriate for certain epistemic aims rather than always unproductive.
