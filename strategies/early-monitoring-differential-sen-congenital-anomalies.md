# Provide early monitoring and differentiated special education support for children born with congenital anomalies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review concludes that because children with non‐syndromic congenital anomalies underperform academically and have higher SEN rates than peers, support should begin early and be differentiated. It states that "Early monitoring and development of differential SEN are important to promote academic progress in these children." The strategy targets timely identification of academic difficulty during the child's school life so that effective support can be provided by families, health professionals and school teams.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identification of children with congenital anomalies at school entry or earlier so academic progress can be monitored over time
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is observational studies of selected major structural congenital anomalies (spina bifida, CHDs, craniofacial anomalies); syndromic conditions were excluded

### Target Learners
- school‐aged children (4–6 to 18 years) born with major non‐syndromic structural congenital anomalies

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement in reading and mathematics
- access to appropriate special education needs provision
