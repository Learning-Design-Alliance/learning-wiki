Use directed educational games sessions to teach social skills to educable mentally retarded children¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's recommendation is that, given the positive effect of direct educational games on social skills and "considering the applicability of this program", teachers use new educational methods to teach these skills to mentally retarded children. The tested implementation consisted of 7 sessions of 45 minutes each of directed educational games, with content validity confirmed by psychology professors, rehabilitation-center teachers and kindergarten teachers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Content validity of the games should be confirmed by expert panels as the article did
  • Sessions delivered as directed games rather than conventional class training

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from one small rehabilitation-center sample of 20 girls aged 8-12 in Ardabil, Iran

Target Learners¶

  • educable mentally retarded children aged 8-12

Target Learning Goals¶

  • social skills, self-help in eating and dressing, socialization

Claims¶

  • Directed educational games significantly improve overall social skills of 8-12-year-old educable mentally retarded girls compared with conventional class instruction [+W]
  • Educational games significantly improve socialization skill of 8-12-year-old educable mentally retarded girls [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use direct-experience formats — simulation games, extracurricular activities, action learning, peer teaching — to pursue relational-domain objectives
