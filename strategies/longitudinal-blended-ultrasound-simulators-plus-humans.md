# Combine simulator-based ultrasound training with hands-on human training in a longitudinal blended learning concept

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that because simulators cannot fully reproduce the realistic environment of human subjects, future training concepts should combine modalities: "A practical strategy to achieve this would be the implementation of a longitudinal blended learning concept, which should include simulator self-learning programmes in paired teams and practical training on humans." The strategy aims to close the observed simulator-to-patient transfer gap while retaining the simulator's safe, forgiving environment for early learners. The authors additionally suggest integrating "artificial intelligence, virtual reality and telemedicine" to facilitate multimodal ultrasound training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The combined concept should include simulator self-learning programmes in paired teams and practical training on humans.
#### Constraints
- The article states training on humans is irreplaceable at the moment and offers a realistic environment that cannot be fully reproduced by current simulators, and participants did not accept simulators as a replacement for human training.

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate medical students
- medical students in the clinical phase of their degree program

### Target Learning Goals
- transfer of FATE echocardiography skills to real patient examinations
