# Introduce complex semi-quantitative assessments after trainees establish foundational skills, using targeted image review sessions for challenging parameters

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a staggered learning approach in which complex semi-quantitative assessments are introduced only after foundational skills are established, potentially enhancing learning efficiency and skill retention. The authors also suggest that standardized vascularity assessments and loss of bowel stratification "may benefit from focused educational interventions, such as targeted image review sessions emphasizing vascularity gradations". This strategy operationalizes their parameter-specific learning curve findings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainees must first establish foundational skills in basic IUS parameters before progressing to complex semi-quantitative assessments
#### Constraints
- Based on learning curves observed in trainees with prior abdominal ultrasound experience; optimal sequencing for novices not established

### Target Learners
- Gastroenterology trainees in structured IUS training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition of semi-quantitative vascularity assessment (Limberg score) and challenging parameters such as bowel wall stratification
