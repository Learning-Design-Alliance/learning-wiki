Editors and reviewers should reward transparency about limitations so researchers are not incentivized to overstate implications¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article proposes that transparency about limitations "needs to be rewarded by editors and reviewers or else researchers are incentivized to overstate implications and hide away weaknesses". This is an implementable editorial-policy action: review practices should create incentives for honest communication of how limitations and assumptions affect practical implications, rather than penalizing authors who voice caveats.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Editorial and review processes must actively reward transparent communication of limitations and assumptions

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • educational researchers
  • journal editors
  • peer reviewers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • accurate communication of research limitations and practical implications to practice

Related Strategies¶

  • Future trend reviews should code the type and strength of practice recommendations and the degree to which limitations were voiced
  • Shift journal editorial and review practices to reward theoretically integrative work
