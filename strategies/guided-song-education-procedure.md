# Implement song education through in-class guided listening and lyric analysis plus out-of-class singing and appreciation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's song education procedure pairs classroom and out-of-class work. In class, students listen with guidance, first focusing on melody and "the phonological handling of lyrical content", then analyzing lyrics for theme and cultural message. Out of class, students practise melodic singing of the songs and appreciate further songs by the same artist, theme or genre. This recipe was used with first-year English majors across one semester alongside the regular curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Songs must be introduced in addition to the same learning tasks the comparison group completes, within the regular EFL teaching-learning process
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only with first-year English majors at a single Chinese university over one semester

### Target Learners
- first-year university English majors in China

### Target Learning Goals
- English pronunciation and intonation
- vocabulary and grammar
- cultural knowledge and interest

- [Experimental-group students reported larger cultural gains than control-group students, including respect for values and beliefs (92% vs 80%) and awareness of social issues (88% vs 71%)](../claims/song-education-cultural-gains.md) [+W]
