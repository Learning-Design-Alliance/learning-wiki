# Use equation (4) to plan the number of assessment opportunities and to diagnose and improve a Keller-type course

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article exemplifies how a teacher can use equation (4) for planning: given Nu = 9 units and an estimated average success of 50% per assessment, giving (alpha, beta) = (1/2, 1/2), the teacher can ask 'how many assessment opportunities are needed for most students to reach mastery at the end of the course?' and read the answer from the mastery curves. After applying a course, the teacher can fit the (alpha, beta) parameters to collected data, 'which allows an improvement in the choice of the number Na of total assessment opportunities'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must divide the content into Nu units and define or estimate the average success probability per assessment
#### Constraints
- The formula gives average behavior under the no-dropout and one-unit-per-opportunity approximations

### Target Learners
- students in planned Keller-type courses

### Target Learning Goals
- course design so that most students reach mastery by the end
