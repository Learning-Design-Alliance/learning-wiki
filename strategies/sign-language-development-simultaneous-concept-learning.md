# Develop subject-specific sign language simultaneously with conceptual learning when teaching deaf students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that to teach a subject to deaf students, sign language for that subject should first be developed with methods and techniques suitable for the subject, and that conceptual learning and acquiring the sign language should happen simultaneously. In the project, new concepts such as telescope, planet and axial tilt were introduced using visuals, videos, a planetarium and hands-on models, starting from students' own perceptions, so that words were formed in sign language about the concepts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers of deaf students need preparation identifying which concepts students already know in sign language before deciding which new concepts to teach
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a study with students whose sign-language and daily-life vocabulary the author describes as poor, limiting the data collected

### Target Learners
- deaf students

### Target Learning Goals
- subject-matter conceptual learning
- sign-language vocabulary for science topics
