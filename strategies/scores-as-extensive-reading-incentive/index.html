Allocate scores for extensive reading as motivation rather than penalty¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

From her observation of the trial, the author concluded that allocating some scores for extensive reading, for example for classroom participation, could have been an incentive for students to become alert as active participants. She notes it is controversial whether marks should come in, and that assigning scores should aim for motivating or rewarding students rather than penalizing them.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Scores framed as reward or motivation, not penalty

Constraints¶

  • The article earlier notes that when marks come in, reading for pleasure becomes reading for credit, making this controversial

Target Learners¶

  • secondary EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustained motivation through extensive reading tasks
