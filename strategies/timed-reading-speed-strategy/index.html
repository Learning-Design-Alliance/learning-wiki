Use timed reading activity with level-appropriate passages to build reading speed, while providing separate vocabulary-focused activities for comprehension¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends employing timed reading activity specifically for enhancing reading speed, using passages written within the 1000 most frequently used words of English, each followed by ten comprehension questions. It simultaneously advises that teachers or curriculum designers "need to employ other remedial techniques to improve the language learners' comprehension", since the timed treatment did not significantly raise comprehension scores.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Passages matched to learners' level; the study used 550-word passages from Asian and Pacific Speed Readings for ESL Learners

Constraints¶

  • The article reports slight anxiety at first sessions that disappeared as learners got used to the activity

Target Learners¶

  • intermediate EFL learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading speed
  • reading fluency

Claims¶

  • Timed reading activity significantly improves the reading speed of intermediate EFL learners compared to untimed reading of the same passages [+M]
  • Timed reading activity does not significantly improve the reading comprehension of intermediate EFL learners [-M]
  • Timed reading initially raises learner anxiety, which fades with familiarity, and learners judge fast reading unhelpful for comprehension of difficult passages [~W]
  • Experimental participants report positive attitudes toward timed reading, citing better concentration, confidence, and motivation to read more [+W]
