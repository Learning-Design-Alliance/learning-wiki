# Summer reading programs should provide books matched to students' reading levels, encourage oral reading practice, and include scaffolding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on its review of experimentally evaluated voluntary summer reading programs (e.g., Kim, 2006; Kim & White, 2008), the article identifies the program features found effective: "programs that provide books matched to students’ reading levels, encourage children to practice reading orally, and are scaffolded". Parent scaffolding means adults assist beginning readers only when necessary, providing "just enough" help. The article contrasts these with programs that merely hand out books.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Books must be matched to students' reading levels
- Parental or teacher support (scaffolding) must accompany book provision
#### Constraints
- Programs that provide books but no parental or teacher support are generally found to be ineffective

### Target Learners
- Elementary school-aged children, particularly low-SES and low-performing readers

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading and literacy skills maintained or gained over summer vacation
