# Run a pre-matriculation summer bridge program with an open-ended inquiry curriculum to build community before freshman year

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's founders chose to intervene "over the summer between the students' final year of high school and first year of college," combining lessons from Berkeley's PREP program with physics education literature. Incoming freshmen lived and learned together for two weeks, working in groups on a curriculum organized around an open-ended question—"What can earthquakes tell us about the interior of the Earth?"—through activities and experiments exploring wave mechanics and modeling. The community formed before classes begin is presented as helping students adjust and form study groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A team of graduate student teachers, curriculum designers, resident assistants, and logistics coordinators
- Residential format so students live and learn together before the fall semester
#### Constraints
- The article describes this in the specific pre-matriculation window; it does not claim the format works at other times

### Target Learners
- Incoming first-year physical-sciences students, including those from underrepresented backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- Building peer community and study networks before college starts
- Developing modeling and experimentation skills through open-ended inquiry
