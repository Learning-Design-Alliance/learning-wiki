# Adapt open-endedness to learner skill level and scaffold exploration with graduated guidance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes personalizing open-endedness by learner proficiency and scaffolding exploration over time. For novices, "Novice learners who are less familiar with the tools and tasks may benefit from a more structured and guided approach initially," with freedom increasing as learners gain proficiency. Designers can also scaffold exploration by providing feedback and suggestions about alternate task-completion approaches, demonstrating different problem-solving options with their trade-offs, and gradually reducing these nudges as learners self-explore solutions. This responds to the study's finding that VR participants reported lower self-efficacy despite performing better.
