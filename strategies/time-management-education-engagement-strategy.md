# Enhance study engagement through time management education, tools, self-monitoring and home-school collaboration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that colleges improve students' time management as a route to higher study engagement, listing concrete actions: "enhancing time management education, promoting the use of time management tools, reinforcing students' self-monitoring and feedback mechanisms, fostering a positive learning attitude among students, and establishing a collaborative home-school partnership". It grounds this in its finding that time management predicts engagement directly and via self-control and mobile phone dependence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions implement time management education and tools alongside self-monitoring and feedback mechanisms for students.
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional, so the article itself notes it cannot establish definitive causal relationships.
