# Manipulatives: Sentence Combining

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Sentence combining asks learners to merge two or more simple sentences into one more complex sentence using coordination, subordination, embedding, or appositive structures. The manipulative variant externalizes this process: learners physically or digitally move, sort, and connect movable cards or tiles representing sentences, clauses, and connectives — often color-coded by grammatical role — so that syntactic relationships become tangible objects to manipulate rather than abstractions to hold in mind. Meta-analytic evidence identifies sentence combining as one of the most effective writing interventions for adolescents [Graham & Perin's meta-analysis of adolescent writing instruction.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.99.3.445) [+S].

## Design Implications

Manipulatives reduce the working memory burden of sentence construction by letting learners hold partial solutions externally while experimenting with clause relationships [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+S]. The physical act of arranging and rearranging supports hypothesis-testing about syntax: learners can compare two arrangements side by side and judge which reads better, converting grammar from rule-recitation into a decision-making activity. Effectiveness depends on the manipulative being faded toward independent written combining — learners must ultimately produce sentences without the cards [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A bank of movable sentence/clause cards (physical strips, sticky notes, or digital tiles in tools like Google Slides or [NoRedInk](https://www.noredink.com))
- Connective cards (conjunctions, subordinators, relative pronouns) so learners must make the logical relationship explicit
- A progression from guided combining with models to independent combining in authentic writing ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
- Instructor knowledge of target structures (e.g., appositives, subordinate clauses) so feedback targets the intended syntax

#### Constraints
- Concrete manipulatives can become a crutch: learners who always combine with cards may fail to internalize the structures for independent writing unless the support is systematically faded [~M]
- Learners lacking basic word-class knowledge cannot benefit from color-coding; the color scheme is meaningless without prior vocabulary of parts of speech [-M]
- Poorly designed card sets (one obvious correct answer) reduce the activity to matching rather than syntactic reasoning [-W]
- Physical manipulation itself adds extraneous load for complex sentences; with many clauses, learners spend effort shuffling cards rather than reasoning about structure [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Physical strips** (sentence strips, sticky notes) suit younger learners and small-group work
- **Digital tiles** (Google Slides drag-and-drop, NoRedInk's interactive prompts) allow automated feedback and adjustable scaffolding
- **Color-coding by grammatical role** (subject, verb, modifier, connective) adds a second visual channel [Media combinations affect recall and retention.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [~M] — helpful when colors are consistent across the curriculum, distracting when arbitrary
- **Erroneous examples**: include a flawed combination for learners to diagnose and repair [Erroneous examples build conceptual knowledge.](../claims/erroneous-examples-build-conceptual-knowledge.md) [+M]
- **Contrasting cases**: present two acceptable combinations of the same sentences and discuss the difference in emphasis [Multiple contrasting cases support abstraction.](../claims/multiple-contrasting-cases-support-abstraction.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Struggling writers and adolescents with underdeveloped syntax, who show the largest gains from sentence-combining instruction [Graham & Perin's meta-analysis of adolescent writing instruction.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.99.3.445) [+S]
- Elementary learners transitioning from oral to written complex sentences [+M]
- English learners, for whom movable, color-coded components make English clause structure visible [+W]
- Less beneficial for proficient writers, for whom the manipulative layer is redundant and slows production [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Syntactic knowledge: understanding coordination, subordination, and embedding
- Writing fluency and sentence variety in composition [+S]
- Reading comprehension of complex sentences, by making clause relationships explicit [+W]

### Instructions
1. **Model**: Combine two sentences aloud at the board, narrating decisions ("These show cause, so I'll try *because*…") — a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) that makes syntactic reasoning visible
2. **Guided manipulation**: Give pairs a card set (2–3 simple sentences plus connective cards); learners physically arrange combinations and read each aloud to test it ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
3. **Compare and evaluate**: Display two learner combinations side by side; discuss which is clearer and why, prompting learners to explain their reasoning [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]
4. **Fading**: Move from card-based combining to combining on paper without cards, then to revising sentences in the learner's own draft writing [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]
5. **Apply**: Require learners to use the target structure in an authentic writing task and self-identify it in revision
