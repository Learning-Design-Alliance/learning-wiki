Implement computer-based teaching as a complement to the teacher that individualizes learning pace and provides immediate feedback¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes CBT as educational content or activities conveyed through the computer, functioning as "a complementary and supportive part of the educational process, not as an alternative to the teacher." It reports that CBT lets each student learn at their own pace, provides immediate feedback and reinforcement, and makes learning more interesting through game-like activities. To reach effective results, the article specifies requirements including well-planned programs, internet connections, clear instructions, and computer-competent teachers.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Well-planned and developed computer programs, internet connections, clear and specific instructions, and teachers competent in the use of computers are required (Bakar, 2007, p. 34).
  • Students are required to have prior knowledge of computer use and come to class well-prepared.

Constraints¶

  • The computer should be regarded as a complementary and supportive part of the educational process, not as an alternative to the teacher.

Target Learners¶

  • primary, secondary, high school, and university students in Turkey

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic success across subject areas including science, mathematics, social sciences, and undergraduate courses

Claims¶

  • The effect of computer-based teaching on academic success differs significantly by grade level, with the largest effect in secondary schools (ES = 1.31) and the smallest in primary schools (ES = .66) [+W]
  • The effect of computer-based teaching on academic success does not differ significantly by type of course, though social studies showed the largest effect size (1.54) and mathematics the lowest (1.06) [+W]
  • The effect of computer-based teaching on academic success does not differ significantly by implementation period, with the largest effect size in the nine-to-eighteen week period (ES = 1.87) [+W]
  • The effect of computer-based teaching on academic success does not differ significantly by publication year of the studies [+W]
  • Computer-based teaching has a large, positive, significant effect on students' academic success compared with traditional teaching methods in Turkish studies from 2006-2014 [+W]
  • Adaptive learning functions as the algorithmic engine that operationalizes personalized learning [+W]
