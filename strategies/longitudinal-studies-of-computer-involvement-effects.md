# Undertake cross-sectional and longitudinal studies of the long-term effects of children's involvement with computers now

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Learning from television research's costly omission, the authors recommend that long-term, cumulative effects of computer involvement be studied before the opportunity is lost: "there is in principle no reason why we could not undertake today cross-sectional and longitudinal studies of the long-term effects of various kinds of involvement with computers". Television research relied on short-term, often single-shot studies that told little about long-range effects, and with no no-TV population remaining the error could never be corrected. Acting early on computers would avoid repeating this and prevent unwarranted later attribution of educational and social ailments to computers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies must begin while variation in children's computer involvement still exists, before a baseline comparison population disappears.
#### Constraints
- The authors note mounting such a research program would be expensive and difficult.

### Target Learners
- children and students using computers in and out of school

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term cognitive and educational effects of sustained computer use, including transfer to new contents and situations
