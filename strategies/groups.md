# Groups

## Description
The teacher calls out a number (e.g., 3), and the students have 10 seconds to get themselves into groups of that size. It might be impossible for everyone to get in a group every time, but each new number gives everyone another chance.  In the basic game, just call out single numbers. Once students get the gist, the teacher can call out addition or subtraction problems (i.e., “get into groups of 7-4.”). Calling out a group of 1 and a group of however many students are in the entire class at some point in the game is beneficial.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An open space where students can move freely. The teacher needs to be able to call out numbers and sums or differences.
#### Constraints
- May not be suitable for students with physical limitations. Can be challenging for students with social anxiety or difficulty with group work.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Reinforce number recognition, encourage quick mental math, promote social interaction, support kinesthetic learning

### Affordances
- Quick, fun, and adaptable activity.  Movement refreshes students and provides another take on math concepts. Incorporates collaborative learning

### Personalization
- Adjust the complexity of the math problems based on the students' skill levels. Allow students to create their own variations to the activity.
