# Integrate interactive, competency-based diabetic foot care training into continuing professional development, supported by organizational resources

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that structured, interactive and hands-on diabetic foot care training be embedded in institutional curricula and professional development, emphasizing experiential learning over passive lectures or purely online teaching. It further recommends tailoring content to nurses' prior experience, progressing from basic definitions to complex cases, and pairing training with available assessment tools and formal authority for nurses to conduct foot examinations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interactive workshops and practical classes rather than traditional lectures or online-only delivery
- Tailoring to nurses' specific needs and prior experience
- Organizational support including equipment (10-g monofilaments, tuning forks, portable Doppler devices) and allocated time for training
#### Constraints
- The review notes this is a reasonable interim strategy pending higher-quality randomized trials; certainty of evidence is very low
- Educational programmes alone are insufficient; adequate resources and organizational support are required

### Target Learners
- Practicing nurses in hospitals, outpatient clinics, primary care centres and home-care settings
- Community nurses and care workers

### Target Learning Goals
- Diabetic foot risk assessment and neurovascular assessment
- Correct use of monofilaments and tuning forks
- Footwear assessment and patient counselling
