# Avoid jargon and redefine common terms like planet and star in astronomy outreach, aiming at understanding rather than fact lists

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends avoiding technical terms and re-defining everyday astronomical words during outreach. It recalls that "avoiding jargon, or technical terms, is always a golden rule in the outreach domain," noting that interest rose when "fate of the Universe" replaced "cosmology," and recommends always recalling what a planet is. It also states outreach "should thus aim at" true understanding "rather than increasing the known fact list, which only provides superficial knowledge."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Outreach materials and talks that substitute everyday phrasing for technical terms and briefly redefine words such as planet and star
#### Constraints
- Derived from a Belgian French-speaking audience survey; the article notes simple words like planet and star are still partly misunderstood there

### Target Learners
- general public

### Target Learning Goals
- deep conceptual understanding of basic astronomical concepts rather than recall of facts
