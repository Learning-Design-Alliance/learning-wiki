# Consider differential test effort when interpreting achievement gap estimates among student subgroups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy follows from the brief's central concern: because "test effort differs substantially across student gender and racial subgroups," achievement gap estimates drawn from low-stakes or standardized test data may reflect differences in engagement rather than true achievement differences. Educators and analysts using gap estimates as a policy metric should therefore ask whether observed subgroup differences could be contaminated by differential test effort before acting on them. The brief treats achievement gaps as "an important policy metric" whose interpretation is at stake.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to information or indicators about student test effort on the assessments used to compute gap estimates
#### Constraints
- The supplied text does not print a method for measuring or adjusting for test effort; it only establishes that effort differs across subgroups

### Target Learners
- K-12 students assessed with standardized achievement tests

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate interpretation of achievement gap estimates across gender and racial subgroups
