# Sustain inquiry innovations through partnerships with business, universities, thought leadership, or institutional policy adoption

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The chapter distinguishes scale (spreading a practice) from sustainability (keeping it in use), noting sustainability requires a mechanism for covering recurring costs. It states that "One way to address sustainability is through partnerships with business," giving examples such as Read180, Carnegie Learning, graphing calculators with Texas Instruments, and probes and sensors with the Concord Consortium. Alternatives include a university-affiliated group such as the Lawrence Hall of Science sustaining FOSS, intellectual thought leadership such as the Exploratorium, and institutional policy adoption.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mechanism for covering recurring costs must be identified for an innovation to be sustained
#### Constraints
- Few scholarly reflections on the nature of educational public-private partnerships are available

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in reading, mathematics, and science programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Long-term continued use of inquiry learning approaches beyond initial adoption
