Three design recommendations for gender-responsive AI literacy programs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 causal), q2–q3 · 3 of 3 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article closes with three recommendations for AI literacy program design grounded in its gender-disaggregated findings: "(1) leverage workshops to increase female STEM career interest; (2) incorporate gender-specific deepfake safety modules, particularly for male students; and (3) ensure that workshop evaluations monitor equity of impact by gender." The recommendations follow directly from the observed baseline gaps, the female-biased gains, and the male-skewed deepfake sharing behaviour.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Workshop evaluations must collect gender-disaggregated pre/post data to monitor equity of impact

Constraints¶

  • Evidence base is limited to two co-educational regional NSW schools, which the authors state limits generalisability

Target Learners¶

  • Australian secondary students in Years 7, 8, and 10

Target Learning Goals¶

  • AI literacy
  • STEM career aspiration development
  • deepfake safety and ethical synthetic-media behaviour

Claims¶

  • Deepfake behaviours show gender patterns: males more likely to have shared deepfakes, females more likely to have reported them [+W]
  • Male secondary students report significantly higher STEM career interest than female students across AI, computer science, and engineering at baseline [+W]
  • Gender is associated with small differences in GenAI use, literacy, and monitoring, while educational level shows no significant differences [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Develop gender-specific well-being strategies, especially literacy support for male students
  • Build students' mathematics ability, alongside STEM exposure, to foster STEM career interest during middle school.
