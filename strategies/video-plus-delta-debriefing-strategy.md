# Video-based structured debriefing with a plus-delta approach immediately after each simulation scenario

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
After each team scenario, residents reviewed a video recording of their performance and then took part in a structured debriefing with the attending neonatologist. As the article states, "A debriefing strategy with a plus-delta approach was used to identify what went well and what needed to be changed." The facilitator "encouraged the residents to reflect on what went well and what could be improved", and the cycle was repeated with a second, more advanced scenario. The article cites evidence that both oral and video-based debriefing increased participants' behavioral and technical skills, so the best method depends on context and learning needs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A video recording of the scenario for accurate recall, a facilitator, and a second scenario to repeat the debriefing cycle
#### Constraints
- The article reports no significant difference between oral and video-based debriefing in a cited clinical trial, so the choice of method should follow context and specific learning needs

### Target Learners
- senior pediatric residents in team-based simulation

### Target Learning Goals
- neonatal resuscitation teamwork, reflection, and technical skills
