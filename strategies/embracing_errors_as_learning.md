# Embracing Errors as Learning

## Description
This strategy involves creating a classroom culture where errors are seen as opportunities for learning rather than indicators of failure. When a student makes a mistake, instructors should focus on what is correct in the student's thinking, find out what is right about that student’s thinking and provide an opportunity for the student to uncover their misconception. When a student voices a wrong answer, teachers can get excited about that.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a shift in instructor mindset and classroom culture. Instructors should be trained to facilitate error-based learning and provide constructive feedback.
#### Constraints
- May require significant time investment to change established beliefs and behaviors. Some learners may initially resist a focus on errors.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learners, particularly those who struggle with math and have developed a negative self-perception.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Shift learner perceptions of math ability from fixed to growth-oriented. Encourage risk-taking, resilience, and persistence in problem-solving.

### Affordances
- Creates a safe environment for learners to experiment and learn from mistakes. Boosts confidence by focusing on what learners understand rather than what they do not. Reinforces reasoning skills over rote memorization.

### Personalization
- Instructors can adjust feedback to address individual learning styles and needs. Activities can be tailored to build on learner strengths and interests.
