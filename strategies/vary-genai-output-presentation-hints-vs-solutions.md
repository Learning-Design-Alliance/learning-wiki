# Design experimental studies that systematically vary how GenAI outputs are presented, comparing full solutions against structured hints or constructive feedback guardrails.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that future research on GenAI as tutoring systems should employ experimental designs that systematically vary how GenAI outputs are presented, such as providing full solutions versus guardrails that present structured hints or constructive feedback. This strategy aims to distinguish when GenAI encourages superficial problem solving and when it supports conceptual understanding. It directly addresses the meta-analytic finding that GenAI assistance does not reliably improve learning outcomes, proposing a concrete experimental manipulation to identify boundary conditions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experimental designs must systematically vary output presentation format (full solutions vs. structured hints/feedback).
- Studies should investigate mechanisms and boundary conditions through which GenAI assistance influences learning.
#### Constraints
- Current evidence base is small (11 learning effect sizes) and heterogeneous, limiting granularity of moderator analyses.
- The article notes actual GenAI use was often not controlled for in existing studies, capturing treatment assignment rather than adherence.

### Target Learners
- Programming students across expertise levels, from novice programmers to experienced developers

### Target Learning Goals
- Programming skill acquisition
- Conceptual understanding versus superficial problem solving
