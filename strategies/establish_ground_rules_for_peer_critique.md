# Establish Ground Rules for Peer Critique

## Description
Establishing ground rules for peer critique ensures that the process is productive, respectful, and beneficial for all learners. These ground rules typically include: picking work that matters, being kind, being helpful, being specific, keeping it moving, and holding everyone accountable. These rules are designed to promote effective communication and provide objective feedback that supports the presenter's goals for the work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear communication of the ground rules, consistent reinforcement of the rules, and a supportive classroom environment.
#### Constraints
- Requires ongoing effort to ensure that learners adhere to the ground rules. May be challenging to address situations where learners violate the rules.

### Target Learners

- All learner levels, including elementary, middle, high school, and higher education

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Create a positive and supportive environment for peer critique, promote effective communication, and enhance learners' ability to provide and receive feedback

### Affordances
- Promotes a respectful and constructive environment for peer critique, helping learners feel safe and supported. Provides clear guidelines for effective communication and ensures that feedback is focused on the presenter's goals.

### Personalization
- The ground rules can be adapted to fit the specific needs of the learners and the classroom environment. Teachers can involve learners in creating the ground rules to promote ownership and buy-in.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observation of group interactions to assess learners' adherence to the ground rules. Analysis of feedback provided by learners to assess the quality and effectiveness of their critique.

## Impact
- When learners aren’t used to the process, a teacher presents it to them as though she’s offering them a code to effective adult communication. At first, following the rules starts out as a performance, but over time kids internalize it and it becomes part of them.
