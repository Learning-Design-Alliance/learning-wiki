# Check In_Check Out

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Check In/Check Out (CICO) is a targeted, small-group behavior support in which a student meets briefly with a consistent adult mentor at the start of each day to review goals and collect a point card, then again at the end of the day to review points earned and take the card home. Teachers award points throughout the day against 2–3 positively stated behavioral expectations, giving frequent, low-stakes [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) tied to clear criteria.

## Design Implications

CICO works because it combines high-frequency adult attention, explicit behavioral expectations, and immediate contingent feedback — a practical application of [Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md) principles wrapped in a relationship that itself supports engagement [Positive adult–student relationships improve engagement and reduce problem behavior.](../claims/teacher-student-relationships-improve-engagement.md) [+M]. The daily cycle also builds student self-monitoring: tracking one's own points against goals is a foundational [self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) routine [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A consistent, non-stigmatizing mentor who is not the student's classroom teacher
- A simple daily report card with 2–3 positively stated expectations aligned to school-wide rules
- Brief (2–5 minute) morning and afternoon check-ins run the same way every day
- A system for taking the card home and returning it, with parent signature or comment
- Data entry so point-card trends can be reviewed weekly and used to fade or intensify support

#### Constraints
- Ineffective for students whose behavior is maintained by escape from academic demands rather than adult attention [~M] — CICO's attention-based reinforcement does not address avoidance-motivated behavior
- Fails when teachers award points inconsistently or treat the card as punishment, converting a positive feedback routine into public negative marking [-M]
- Gains often fade when the intervention is withdrawn abruptly; support must be faded gradually (e.g., self-managed point cards, reduced check-in frequency) [~M]
- Less effective for students with internalizing needs or those who find daily adult attention aversive rather than reinforcing [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **CICO with individualized goals** — point card criteria tailored to the student's function of behavior
- **Self-monitored CICO** — the student scores themselves before the teacher confirms, shifting toward self-regulation as an exit path
- **CICO with social skills instruction** — pairing the point card with a small-group skills component for students who lack the target behaviors
- **Check & Connect-style mentoring** — a longer-term variant emphasizing relationship persistence across school transitions

### Target Learners
- Students with mild, attention-maintained problem behavior who respond to adult mentoring [+S]
- Students at risk of escalation to intensive (Tier 3) supports; CICO is designed as the standard Tier 2 entry point in [PBIS](../patterns/positive-behavioral-interventions-and-supports.md) systems
- Roughly 60–70% of referred students respond; non-responders need function-based individualized supports [~S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Behavioral self-regulation: meeting explicit daily expectations
- Self-monitoring and goal tracking as transferable executive routines
- School belonging and connection to at least one trusted adult [Belonging interventions improve outcomes for at-risk students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Identify students through office discipline referral data and confirm the behavior is plausibly attention-maintained ([Behaviorism](../theories/behaviorism.md))
2. Assign a mentor and hold a brief orientation with the student and family explaining the point card
3. Run the morning check-in: greet, review yesterday's points, set today's goal ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md))
4. Have each teacher award points at scheduled checkpoints against the card's expectations, with brief positive feedback ([Feedback](../elements/feedback.md))
5. Run the afternoon check-out: total points, compare to goal, celebrate or problem-solve, send card home
6. Review point data weekly; fade support (self-monitoring, fewer check-ins) once goals are met consistently, or intensify for non-responders ([Fading](../elements/fading.md))

## Examples
- **[PBIS CICO](https://www.pbis.org)** — The national PBIS Technical Assistance Center publishes standard CICO point-card templates, fidelity checklists, and fading guidance used across thousands of US schools.
- **Check & Connect** — A University of Minnesota–developed mentoring model ([https://checkandconnect.umn.edu](https://checkandconnect.umn.edu)) that extends the check-in/check-out structure into long-term dropout prevention with a persistent mentor.
- **Elementary Tier 2 rollout** — A school identifies students with 2+ office referrals per month, assigns them to a counselor-led CICO cycle with a 3-item card ("Be safe, be respectful, be ready"), and reviews graphed daily points in weekly grade-team meetings.
