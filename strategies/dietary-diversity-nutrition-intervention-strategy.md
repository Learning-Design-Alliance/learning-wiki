# Recommend nutrition interventions on dietary diversity for school-age children, alongside efforts to raise mothers' educational level and economic empowerment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend nutrition interventions targeting dietary diversity among school-age children, arguing these would be "crucial for increasing academic achievement", and tie the recommendation to food and nutrition policy, the national nutrition strategy, and national nutrition programme. The strategy also includes increasing mothers' educational level and economic empowerment to promote dietary diversity intake. It is offered for policymakers and stakeholders in pastoral, low-income settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must operate through food and nutrition policy, the national nutrition strategy, and the national nutrition programme as the article frames them
#### Constraints
- The study's own SEM found no direct, indirect, or total effects of DDS, HAZ, or BAZ on academic performance, so the achievement link is asserted by the authors rather than supported by their models
- The cross-sectional design limits identifying cause and effect relationships, and findings may not generalize beyond the two pastoral districts studied

### Target Learners
- School-age children (7–14 years) in pastoral communities of Southeast Ethiopia

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving academic achievement of school-age children through nutrition interventions
