# Use automated surgical video analysis to supplement nontechnical skills assessment and feedback in surgical education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating motion-feature analysis from computer vision into nontechnical skills assessment, because conventional NOTSS-style rating depends on retrospective subjective accounts, requires highly trained observers, and is time-intensive and costly to scale. Their stated Meaning is that "automated surgical video analysis could enhance nontechnical skills assessment" and be "used for surgical education and quality improvement initiatives." The strategy positions automated motion metrics as a complementary tool to existing expert rating techniques rather than a replacement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video capture infrastructure in the operating room, pose-estimation software, and expert NOTSS ratings as the criterion for validating the automated measures.
#### Constraints
- The authors state further investigation across different hospitals and specialties is necessary to validate the findings, which come from one specialty at one tertiary hospital with a small sample.

### Target Learners
- surgical teams and surgical trainees in operating room settings

### Target Learning Goals
- objective evaluation and feedback on team nontechnical skills during surgical skills development and quality improvement
