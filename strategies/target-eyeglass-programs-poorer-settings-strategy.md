# Target free-eyeglass and vision care programs at poorer settings and disadvantaged students to maximize learning impact

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q4` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that organizations aiming to raise children's learning and reduce educational inequality through eyeglasses should deliver them in poorer settings, where the intervention is more productive, and that governments can subsidize eyeglasses for poor myopic children. It states that "doing so in a poorer setting will be more productive" and that intervention design and upscaling must take full account of context.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should identify disadvantaged subgroups (lower baseline scores, less wealthy households, left-behind children, schools with high blackboard use) where eyeglasses are likely to have the largest effect.
#### Constraints
- The article acknowledges it has no data to verify all the mechanisms of the substitution effect of wealth, such as multimedia learning.

### Target Learners
- Myopic primary-school students (grades 4–5) in rural China

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved standardized mathematics achievement
