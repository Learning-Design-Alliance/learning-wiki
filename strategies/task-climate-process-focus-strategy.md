# Create a task-oriented motivational climate focused on the learning process to protect mental health and favour academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In its pedagogical perspectives the article recommends educating students that "it is not the end result but the process that is most important", and states that "Through the creation of a motivational climate focused on learning (homework), it is possible to act in order to take care of the mental health of young people and to favour their academic performance." This follows from the finding that task climate relates negatively to anxiety while ego climate relates positively to it, so shifting classroom emphasis from competitive, extrinsic outcomes to task mastery is proposed as a lever on both wellbeing and grades.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educational environments where teachers can shape the motivational climate towards learning and process rather than competitive outcomes
#### Constraints
- The article notes the educational environment itself sometimes increases stress and anxiety levels of young people; the supporting evidence is cross-sectional and does not establish causal intervention effects

### Target Learners
- university students
- pre-service physical education teachers
- young people in education

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing anxiety
- improving academic performance
- fostering intrinsic task orientation
