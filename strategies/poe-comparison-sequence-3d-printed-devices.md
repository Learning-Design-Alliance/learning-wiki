# Structure device activities in prediction, observation and explanation stages with comparison-focused tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article organises classroom use in three stages — prediction, observation and explanation — following the Predict–Observe–Explain approach "used to probe students' understanding and to promote the revision of their initial explanations". Tasks should guide comparison between situations: free versus magnet-perturbed motion, different springs or pole inclinations, two near-identical releases. Comparison shifts attention from superficial description towards identifying variables, parameters and operating conditions, supported by small teams, written records and teacher-led plenary discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks that lead students to anticipate, justify, record and revisit their own ideas rather than merely observe
- Written records preserving initial anticipations for later comparison with observations
- Teacher guidance before, during and after the activity, including a closing plenary discussion
#### Constraints
- When the activity is reduced to observing that the motion is strange or hard to anticipate, part of its educational potential is lost

### Target Learners
- secondary education students
- pre-service teacher trainees
- introductory university students

### Target Learning Goals
- constructing and revising physical explanations of dynamical phenomena
