# Involve attending physicians in simulation-based training and conduct frequent needs analyses to fit training to local context

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends directing simulation-based training resources toward attending physicians, because their buy-in is needed for new practices to be implemented, and conducting frequent needs analyses so training matches locally available resources. It notes its own curriculum already trained condom-catheter UBT given limited Bakri balloons, and suggests future training may benefit from focusing even more on condom-catheter UBTs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training programs must reach attending physicians who sit at the top of decision-making hierarchies for their buy-in to enable implementation.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a single-institution study in Guatemala where group composition limited causal attribution of comfort differences.

### Target Learners
- OB/GYN residents and attending physicians in low-resource teaching hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- Management of postpartum hemorrhage with B-Lynch suture and uterine balloon tamponade
