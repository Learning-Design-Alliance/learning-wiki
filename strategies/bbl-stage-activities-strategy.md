# Use mind mapping, group discussion, music and brain games, and celebration across BBL stages to support conceptual understanding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes concrete classroom activities for each BBL stage: teachers make mind maps "to facilitate the information organization" and connect to previous learning; group discussion lets students explain concepts to peers; incubation uses rewriting of concepts plus classical music and brain games; verification uses question-and-answer checks; integration ends with applause and gifts. These are the implementable steps the authors report using in the experimental group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher acts as facilitator, approaching groups when students do not understand well
#### Constraints
- Described as implemented in one month of polyhedron instruction at one junior high school

### Target Learners
- grade VIII junior high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding and retaining mathematical concepts, reducing misconceptions
