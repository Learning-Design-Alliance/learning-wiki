# Provide easily modifiable instructional materials that expect and support faculty customization

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A dissemination strategy in which instructional materials and designs are developed with the expectation that faculty will engage in local customization. The authors recommend that "instructional materials and designs should be developed with the expectation that faculty will engage in local customization" and that faculty "should be treated as participants in the development process." Providing easily modifiable materials also communicates to instructors that they can and should use their own expertise to integrate materials into their unique teaching situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Materials must be editable or modifiable by faculty
- Explanations about the instructional design should accompany materials
#### Constraints
- Represents a move toward the invention side of the continuum, away from the adoption model

### Target Learners
- college physics faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- adoption and adaptation of research-based instructional materials
