# Frame the activity as measuring the speed of light rather than as a distance measurement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A framing strategy for the teacher: although metrologically the activity measures the refractive index of air or a light-pulse distance measurement, presenting it as measuring the speed of light increases student motivation. The article reports that "students found the idea of measuring the speed of light to be significantly more motivating than interpreting the experiment as a distance-measurement method." It connects the technique to LIDAR systems used in industrial, automotive and satellite contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A time-of-flight measurement activity whose interpretation can be presented either way
#### Constraints
- From a rigorous standpoint the measured quantity is not the defined exact speed of light in vacuum

### Target Learners
- high school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement and motivation in experimental physics
- connecting classroom measurement to modern metrology techniques such as LIDAR
