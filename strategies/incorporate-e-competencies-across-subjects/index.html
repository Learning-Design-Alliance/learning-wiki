Incorporate socio-emotional e-competencies into schooling as a transversal element of digital-competence learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on their finding that e-competencies explain a high percentage of the variability of habitual digital behaviors, the authors recommend that primary and secondary schools go beyond educating about digital risks and "incorporate the acquisition of socio-emotional e-competencies in order to encourage balanced, responsible use of the Internet as a transversal element in the learning of digital competencies across all subjects". Concretely they suggest encouraging prosocial online behavior and online conflict management within group academic tasks, adding specific modules on socio-emotional competencies to training programs with age-appropriate emotion-regulation strategies, and helping families supervise children's screentime within a framework of responsible social-network use.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • School-wide integration across subjects rather than isolated digital-risk instruction

Constraints¶

  • The article's transversal design precludes establishing causal relations, so program effects should be verified longitudinally

Target Learners¶

  • Secondary education students aged 12-16
  • Primary and secondary school students generally

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Balanced, responsible Internet use; emotional regulation online; independence from virtual popularity indicators

Affordances¶

  • Socio Emotional E Competencies Model

Claims¶

  • Socio-emotional e-competencies correlate negatively with habitual digital behaviors in secondary students, except social e-competency (weakly positive) and emotional e-awareness (no significant correlation) [+W]
  • Emotional e-independence predicts habitual digital behaviors only in girls and older students, while emotional e-awareness predicts them only in boys [+W]
  • Emotional e-regulation and e-self-control of impulsiveness are the strongest SEM predictors of fewer habitual digital behaviors across genders and age groups, in cross-sectional data [+W]
  • Social e-competency is a positive statistical predictor of habitual digital behaviors, invariant across gender and age groups, in cross-sectional data [~W]
