# Apply CLT-derived design principles in medical education: integrated information sources, oral explanations for animations, completion tasks, worked examples, and variable practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 4 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q4` · 3 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents CLT-derived design recommendations for medical education, drawn from fifteen recommendations discussed in the journal Medical Education on how external load can be reduced, intrinsic load controlled, and germane load optimized. "The split-attention principle states that multiple spatially or temporally distributed information sources should be replaced by an integrated information source", which supports giving device instructions at the moment of use and distributing lecture slides beforehand. Further recommendations include supplementing visual animations with oral explanations (modality effect), partially completed tasks (completion principle), pre-made exemplary solutions to criticize (worked-example effect), and varied practice contexts (variability effect).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recommendations should be adapted to the exact circumstances of the learning situation, as negative consequences may arise otherwise
#### Constraints
- The worked-example effect reverses with increasing learner competence, becoming superfluous or counterproductive

### Target Learners
- medical students
- inexperienced clinical learners

### Target Learning Goals
- learning complex clinical content and procedures
- transfer of clinical knowledge
