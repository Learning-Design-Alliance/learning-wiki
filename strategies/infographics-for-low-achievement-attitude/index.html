Use infographics in geography lessons where visual and information content are given together, especially where achievement and attitudes are low¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that "Infographics can be effectively and widely used in geography lessons in different grade levels and learning areas when visual and information are to be given together" and specifically suggests using them "in cases where achievement and attitudes of students in geography lesson is low". It further calls for studies to increase the use of infographics in geography syllabuses and textbooks and for nationwide studies in different branches.

Claims¶

  • Control-group students' attitudes towards geography declined significantly over the six weeks while experimental-group attitudes rose [+W]
  • Across all four Solomon groups, achievement post-test scores differ significantly, with both infographic groups outperforming both control groups [+W]
  • Using infographics in geography lessons improves students' attitudes towards the geography lesson relative to usual instruction [+W]
  • Using infographics as instructional materials in 10th-grade geography lessons raises academic achievement post-test scores relative to usual materials [+W]
  • Before treatment, the experimental and control groups were equivalent on both achievement and attitude [~W]
  • Students taught with infographic materials scored higher on chapter quizzes than students taught the same chapters conventionally in both colleges [+W]
