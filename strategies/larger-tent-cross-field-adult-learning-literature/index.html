Draw across adult-learning literatures ('larger tent' approach) rather than over-relying on field-specific literature in teacher education¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors argue that teacher education research benefits from incorporating instruments and constructs shared across professions and adult-learning fields, a "larger tent" approach they call a hallmark of the adult education movement. They perceive that "the failure to incorporate the rich traditions and literatures across the fields engaged with adult learning has become an obstacle to professional renewal and growth in their field, that of teacher education." Insularity from over-limitation of literary categories is described as intellectually stifling within their own college setting.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Willingness to cite and apply instruments validated in other professional and adult-learning populations

Constraints¶

  • The authors frame this as their perception and an implication of the study, not a tested result

Target Learners¶

  • teacher educators
  • adult learners in professional contexts

Target Learning Objectives¶

  • professional renewal and growth
  • reflective practice research design

Claims¶

  • The Reflective Judgment Model coding rubric achieves high inter-rater reliability (alpha .93) when applied to teacher educators' interview narratives [+W]
  • Teacher educators scored near the center of the epistemic scale (means about 4.2-4.3), contrary to expectations of a highly constructivist orientation [+W]
  • Every teacher educator participant produced narrative from every RJM level, with a preponderance near a typical level slightly above 4.0 [+W]
