# Routinely screen health professions students for academic stress, anxiety, depression, and sleep disturbance during virtual learning, and incorporate sleep hygiene and resourcefulness training in curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educational institutions routinely use early-detection tools for subtle signs of academic stress, anxiety, depression, and sleep disturbance, refer students with alarming patterns to counselling, and design curricula and support services targeting sleep quality and both personal and social resourcefulness skills. "Incorporating sleep hygiene and resourcefulness training in health professions education is highly indicated" per the authors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Screening tools must be routinely utilized by educational institutions so subtle underreported symptoms can be detected early
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a single-institution, predominantly female, high-GPA sample, limiting generalizability per the authors

### Target Learners
- undergraduate health professions students (nursing, medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, applied health sciences)

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining psychological well-being during virtual learning
- maximizing academic performance while managing academic stress
