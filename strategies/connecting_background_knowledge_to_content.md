# Connecting Background Knowledge to Content

## Description
A method for making instruction more culturally relevant by connecting content to learners’ experiences. Teachers can enhance their curriculum using a variety of resources in order to make the material more culturally relevant and accessible, helping learners make important connections to content. Steps include:

1.  **Assess Existing Knowledge**: Find out what background knowledge learners have on a particular topic related to the lesson. This can be done through discussions in their home language, drawing, writing, or using a graphic organizer such as a K-W-L chart.
2.  **Connect to Familiar Experiences**: Look for ways to connect the content to things that are familiar and learners' experiences. Open up the thinking of the rest of the class to the different cultures of their peers.
3.  **Enrich Instruction**: Use resources that go beyond the textbook, such as artwork, music, dance, culture kits, special traditions, celebrations, recipes, and realia (concrete objects).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Resources that go beyond the textbook, such as artwork, music, dance, culture kits, realia, etc. Understanding of learners' cultural backgrounds and experiences.
#### Constraints
- Requires time and effort to research and prepare culturally relevant materials. Risk of making assumptions about learners' experiences or putting them on the spot. May be challenging if the teacher has limited knowledge of learners' cultural backgrounds.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance learner engagement, improve content retention, foster cultural understanding, make content more accessible

### Affordances
- Draws on learners' existing knowledge and cultural backgrounds to facilitate deeper understanding. Promotes inclusivity by valuing diverse experiences. Enhances engagement by making content more relevant to learners' lives.

### Personalization
- Adapt the content and resources used based on the specific backgrounds and experiences of learners. Allow learners to contribute their own cultural artifacts and stories to the lesson.
