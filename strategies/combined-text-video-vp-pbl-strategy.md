# Use a combined text-plus-video approach in virtual patients for PBL, reserving video for elements to which it is particularly suited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 3 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that "a combination approach may be a superior one within the context of undergraduate PBL": video used only for elements it suits (such as displaying procedures) may reduce negative impact on session pace and reduce extraneous cognitive load, while text efficiently delivers information learners must review and appraise. Several participants suggested video first, then the text equivalent, or a text transcript.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video content must be reviewed for suitability and accuracy before delivery, as the module leads did in this study.
- Media should be tested in the actual PBL rooms to ensure sufficient audio clarity, per the article's recommendation for future studies.
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that a text transcript accompanying video may conflict with the redundancy effect in Mayer's cognitive theory of multimedia learning, though that work concerns individual self-directed materials and the group dynamic in PBL may warrant an alternative approach.
- Findings come from a single intervention at one institution with learners experienced in text-based PBL; generalization requires further research.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in transition-year PBL tutorials

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning and patient-management decision making
