# Plan university teaching around the psychological needs specific to each knowledge area, prioritising teacher involvement and fostering students' self-efficacy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, because students in different knowledge areas show different psychological needs related to achievement, an in-depth study of these needs should inform how university teaching is planned and managed in each area. Its own results indicate that, generally, what matters most is "the teacher’s involvement in the student learning process and students’ feelings of competence (self-efficacy)", so teachers should be dependable and available to help and should enhance students' recognition that they are learning. The authors add that teachers can learn to become more supportive but need considerable help and expert guidance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of the psychological needs of students in the specific degree areas being taught
#### Constraints
- The authors state the analysis is limited to higher education, so care should be taken about generalising to primary or secondary education

### Target Learners
- higher education students across Humanities, Social Sciences, Sciences, Life Sciences, Medical Sciences, and Architecture/Engineering

### Target Learning Goals
- improving student achievement
- supporting student motivation
