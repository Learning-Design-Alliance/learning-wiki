# Identifying Self-Talk

## Description
Students record the messages from their inner voice over the course of a week in a journal or notebook. Reminding students that self-talk is incessant and happens all the time, encouraging them to record a minimum of five inner voice messages per day.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Journals or notebooks for recording self-talk. Time for students to reflect and write down their thoughts. A reminder system to ensure students record their thoughts regularly.
#### Constraints
- Students may forget to record their thoughts. Some students may find it difficult to articulate their inner voice. The accuracy of the recordings depends on students' honesty and self-awareness.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- K-12 students, particularly those who are learning to become more aware of their thoughts and feelings.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- To enable students to recognize and document their internal dialogue over a period of time.

### Affordances
- Promotes self-awareness by encouraging learners to pay attention to their thoughts. Supports self-reflection by providing a structured way to document and review their inner voice. Helps students recognize patterns and themes in their self-talk.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to different age groups and learning styles. Can be modified to focus on specific situations or emotions. Educators can provide different types of journals or notebooks to suit individual preferences.

- Review students' journals or notebooks for patterns and themes in their self-talk. Assess students' ability to accurately identify and document their inner voice. Observe students' engagement and participation in discussions about self-talk.

## Impact
- Research supports the benefit of self-monitoring strategies, especially when those strategies include journaling. Identifying self-talk can give students insights into their own beliefs.
