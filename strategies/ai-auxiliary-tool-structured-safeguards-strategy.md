# Deploy AI as an auxiliary tool supplemented by structured measures including transparent auditing of algorithmic bias and data-processing privacy protocols

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that although AI provides scalable educational support, "its deployment must be supplemented by structured measures, including transparent auditing of algorithmic bias, data processing protocols to protect privacy, and positioning AI as an auxiliary tool." This strategy responds to three stated risks: AI may amplify existing differences in medical knowledge or language expression leading students to internalize inaccurate knowledge frameworks; continuous input of student questions and case data may disclose sensitive information; and excessive dependence on AI in early clinical training risks weakening basic cognitive ability if students accept AI-generated explanations without critical thinking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Transparent auditing of algorithmic bias
- Data processing protocols to protect privacy
- Positioning AI as an auxiliary tool rather than a replacement for independent judgment
#### Constraints
- The article warns that if students are used to accepting AI-generated explanations directly without critical thinking, their diagnostic reasoning, comprehensive information analysis, and interpersonal communication inquiry abilities may not be fully developed.

### Target Learners
- Medical interns in early-stage clinical training

### Target Learning Goals
- Preservation of independent diagnostic reasoning and critical thinking while benefiting from AI-supported learning
