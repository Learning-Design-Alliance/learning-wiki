# Prioritize longitudinal, international, and higher-education ethnic-racial socialization research on racial climate and belonging

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q4` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the discussion the authors call for expanding the evidence base in three directions: "the need for more longitudinal research, more research outside of the USA, and more ethnic-racial socialization research in higher education." These gaps follow directly from the corpus, in which 83.1% of studies were US-only, most effects were cross-sectional (first-timepoint cross-sectional effects were selected), and postsecondary socialization research was sparse. The strategy directs future study design and funding toward designs that can support stronger causal and generalizable conclusions about how racial climate shapes belonging.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies measuring perceived racial climate and belonging in educational institutions, with attention to within-school variation in students' perceptions
#### Constraints
- The current evidence base is dominated by US, cross-sectional, student-reported perceptions, limiting causal and international generalization

### Target Learners
- students in primary, secondary, and postsecondary settings worldwide

### Target Learning Goals
- building evidence on how racial climate dimensions shape belonging over time
