# Have students measure wavelength with the Google Earth distance tool and calculate wave speed from the gravity-wave formulas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a classroom activity using a Google Earth tool that allows measuring the distance between two geographical points. Students measure the wavelength of visible waves and calculate wave speed: "students can measure the wavelength, and calculate the wave speed in deep water by applying Eq. (1)". For λ = 100 m this gives v_phase = 12.5 m/s; if depth can be estimated, speed in shallow water is computed with Eq. (3), e.g. d = 1 m giving v_phase = 3.1 m/s.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to Google Earth imagery showing waves of measurable wavelength, and water depth estimates for the shallow-water calculation
#### Constraints
- The calculation applies only in the deep-water or shallow-water regimes for which equations (1) and (3) hold

### Target Learners
- pupils in elementary physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- relating wave wavelength to wave speed
- applying wave velocity formulas to real data
