Strategies

Have students develop and train AI agents and simulations in the domains they are learning, with validation and scaffolding¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

In its scenario on developing professional practices, the article proposes coaching students to develop their own customizable AI agents for exploring a content area, or leveraging AI coding and rendering capacities to represent a domain. It reports that "Recently we've had students (who have no coding experience) designing computer simulations to represent basic to advance physics phenomena", requiring students to validate AI-based approaches for outcomes and scientific accuracy while learning the strengths and limitations of the technologies.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Careful guidance and scaffolding is necessary, per the article, and students must validate AI-based approaches in terms of outcomes and scientific accuracy

Constraints¶

  • The article presents this as a scenario with an early example rather than a systematically evaluated intervention

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate physics students, including those with no coding experience

Target Learning Goals¶

  • communication, discernment, metacognition, trust, and understanding of the strengths and limitations of AI technologies

Related Strategies¶

  • Extend simulated teaching practice to student assessment and teacher screening uses
  • Provide differentiated scaffolding matched to teachers' behavioral-cognitive profiles when supporting multi-agent AI workflow design
