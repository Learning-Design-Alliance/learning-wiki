# Combine FTE with Term Prompted Term Entry tasks to build detailed maps of students' conceptual knowledge structure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose an assessment sequence in which students first complete an FTE task to collect a vocabulary for a subject, then receive a battery of TPTE tasks prompted by terms from their own FTE responses. Where the FTE explores widely the concepts a subject associates with a topic, the TPTE probes connections in more detail, potentially allowing construction of a detailed, quantitative map of each student's conceptual knowledge structure. Comparing such maps across students and experts could reveal patterns correlating with subject mastery.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires computer-based task administration recording response terms and timing, and complementary tasks beyond FTE alone
#### Constraints
- The approach could be defeated by students priming themselves with memorized vocabulary lists beforehand, so it suits ongoing formative assessment rather than high-stakes summative assessment

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- formative, cognitively diagnostic assessment of conceptual knowledge structure
