# Let students train—not only prompt—language models to build AI literacy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating transparent, trainable AI systems into introductory computing education so learners act as investigators and builders rather than passive users. The authors conclude that "enabling students to train—and not only prompt—language models can shift how they think about AI," and that making training visible and manipulable helps learners see language models as statistical systems shaped by training data. The activity is implementable as a short homework sequence: choose datasets, select configurations, train, observe output changes, and reflect in writing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A browser-based, client-side training tool so no specialized hardware, large-scale datasets, or cloud pipelines are needed
#### Constraints
- The brief intervention alone falls short of producing complete or fully expert mental models

### Target Learners
- introductory computing (CS1) students
- K-12 learners in AI literacy education

### Target Learning Goals
- data-centered reasoning about language model behavior
- critical AI literacy
