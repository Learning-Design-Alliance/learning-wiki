Teach an academic toolbox combining study strategies with self-regulation skills such as goal setting, time management, and anxiety coping techniques¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Within the SSC, after the diagnostic assessment component, instructors teach a specific package of strategies: study strategies including rehearsal, elaboration, organization, and critical thinking, alongside self-regulation instruction. The article describes the self-regulation portion directly: "the academic toolbox included instruction in self-regulation; that is, how to maintain motivation (e.g., through personal goal setting), manage time and effort (e.g., using thought-stopping to refocus concentration to the task at hand), and cope with test anxiety and stress (e.g., by relaxing, using positive self -talk, and visualizing success )". Strategies are practiced through in-class exercises, homework assignments, peer discussion, thought papers, and individual appointments with the instructor across the 14 weeks.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Multiple opportunities for application of the learning strategies and reflection over the term
  • Instructors who refer students for personal counselling when necessary

Constraints¶

  • Post-SSC test anxiety still remained relatively high, which the authors attribute to students approaching final exam week while needing to perform well to avoid dismissal

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate students on academic probation with rudimentary learning strategies and poor time management

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Effective encoding, storage, retrieval, and application of information
  • Time management and reduced procrastination
  • Coping with test anxiety and stress

Claims¶

  • The combined training-plus-diary condition improved specific SRL components: SRL knowledge, metacognitive strategies, cognitive strategies (elaboration, organisation, critical thinking), and resource management strategies (time-management, effort regulation) [+W]
  • Students on academic probation showed significant pre- to post-course improvement on the LASSI learning strategies inventory after the Student Success Course [+W]
  • MSLQ learning strategy scores improved significantly pre- to post-course, with practice effects ruled out for cognitive and resource management strategies but possibly contributing to metacognitive strategy gains [+W]
  • After the Student Success Course, test anxiety significantly decreased while intrinsic goal orientation and self-efficacy significantly increased, but task value and control beliefs did not improve [+W]
  • General academic procrastination declined significantly from pre- to post-course, and of the six procrastination patterns only the oppositional pattern significantly declined [+W]
  • Participants' GPA improved significantly over the school year, with 81% improving and 66% improving enough to have academic probation status removed [+W]
