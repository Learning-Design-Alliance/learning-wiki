# Use less-structured, multi-part problems with scaffolding that is gradually faded, plus grade incentives, to develop expert-like problem solving

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that physics instructors give students problems of different cognitive complexity, including ill-specified problems requiring decisions about what to calculate, and provide scaffolding support that breaks multi-part problems into sub-problems. Support "should be gradually reduced to help them develop self-reliance" so students eventually decompose problems on their own. Students should be given "grade incentives and opportunities for solving multi-part problems as well as less structured and out of the box problems" rather than only algorithmic assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scaffolding must be incorporated in an instructional context using multiple pedagogical considerations consistent with supporting student learning; lower-level content can be moved outside class via pre-lectures to free in-class time
#### Constraints
- Multi-part problems in small groups can cause cognitive overload due to limited information-processing capacity, so scaffolding is needed to reduce load

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students
- introductory through advanced physics course students

### Target Learning Goals
- expert-like problem-solving decisions
- combining conceptual and quantitative reasoning
- making approximations and estimations
