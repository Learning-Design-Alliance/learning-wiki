# Provide peer process feedback earlier in the curriculum, when students are still developing their tutorial behavior

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy recommends delivering midterm peer process feedback to first-year rather than second-year students, because first-year students lack a clear picture of their own individual contributions and are still developing their tutorial behavior, whereas second-year students are already more or less set in their ways and less susceptible to feedback. Students in the focus group proposed this change and suggested first-year students would take the feedback more seriously.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A curriculum stage in which tutorial behavior is still developing
#### Constraints
- Stated for second-year students in this study; effectiveness for first-year students was suggested, not tested

### Target Learners
- first-year students in problem-based learning programs

### Target Learning Goals
- development of effective tutorial group behavior
