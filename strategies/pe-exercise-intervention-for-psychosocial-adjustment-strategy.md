# Implement exercise-based intervention programmes within physical education to support adolescent psychosocial adjustment and reduce anxiety

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that educators, parents and policymakers use exercise-based interventions in physical education to enhance adolescent psychosocial adjustment and decrease anxiety, because "the findings suggest that existing exercise -based intervention programmes may have a positive influence on psychosocial adjustment and decrease anxiety." The strategy consists of purposefully applying a structured complex of modified PE lessons over an extended period (seven months in this study), combining vigorous team sports with Pilates and periodic theory instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained delivery over months within regular physical education lessons
- Purposefully construed programme content targeting psychosocial adaptation-related behaviours
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one quasi-experimental study of 14–15-year-olds in a single school; the authors note it would be worthwhile to extend implementation to students of other age groups

### Target Learners
- adolescents in secondary school physical education

### Target Learning Goals
- psychosocial adjustment
- anxiety reduction
