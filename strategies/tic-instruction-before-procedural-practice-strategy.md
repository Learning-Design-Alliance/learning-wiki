# Embed trauma-informed care instruction before procedural simulation rather than after

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's intervention design sequences a short interactive didactic and case-based discussion before learners practice procedures on a standardized patient, so that trauma-informed behaviors are encoded prior to performance. The authors state that educators can use their curriculum as "a reproducible means by which educators can incorporate training on these skills in a low-cost, time-effective format in the medical student population," aligned with newly established EM TIC core competencies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty with TIC training and expertise
- Trained standardized patients or volunteers
#### Constraints
- Clerkship curricula must accommodate a roughly 50-minute pre-simulation instructional block; pilot settings were tertiary academic centers

### Target Learners
- Senior medical students in emergency medicine clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- Performing common ED procedures using a trauma-informed approach
