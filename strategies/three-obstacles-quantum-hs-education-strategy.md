# Address the three obstacles to HS quantum education: level-appropriate materials, funded teacher professional development, and state curriculum standards

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (3 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article identifies "three distinct obstacles" to widespread introduction of QC and QIS topics in HS and middle school: "(a) lack of materials at the right level for students and instructors, (b) funding and support for professional development for teachers, and (c) lack of state standards". It addresses all three, with focus on quantum virtual labs and ZX calculus. Next steps include adding Quantum to the state HS curriculum, forming a national stakeholder taskforce, and helping College Board develop an AP Quantum Information Science course and exam.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional leverage of talent-development strengths; dual-credit structures so one college class yields three HS classes of credit.
#### Constraints
- As of today, quantum topics appear on HS curriculum standards in just two states: OH (Computing) and TX (Physics).

### Target Learners
- High-school students
- Middle school students and their teachers
- Incoming college students

### Target Learning Goals
- Workforce development for the quantum industrial ecosystem; pathways into quantum computing specializations
