# Match shared-control teaching to intermediate student self-regulation skill

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article analyzes which teaching strategy fits which level of student self-regulation. Shared regulation yields congruence when students master a learning activity to an intermediate degree, constructive friction when they do not yet master it but can with guidance (compared to Vygotsky's zone of proximal development), and destructive friction when students already use the activity spontaneously. The article concludes the shared-control strategy "is best suited for those situations in which students have already acquired some skill in employing a particular learning activity, but have a need for further skill development".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must know students' current mastery level of the relevant learning activity
#### Constraints
- It is less suited for students who already have expertise in the self-regulated use of particular learning activities; stimulating such students is described as superfluous and can irritate them

### Target Learners
- higher education students, including higher vocational education students

### Target Learning Goals
- developing skill in learning activities students use only partially or not spontaneously

### Affordances
- Teacher Regulation Strong Shared Loose Control
