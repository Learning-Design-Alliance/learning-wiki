# Avoid excessively frequent praise of high-achieving students while using praise to reduce antisocial behavior

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that administrators and teachers apply praise deliberately but cap it. It found "a certain level of praise can effectively reduce antisocial behavior; however, if the degree of praise exceeds a threshold, it will encourage students to conduct antisocial behavior." Teachers are told to be "good at applying praise in the classroom", for example adopting an "encouraging and guiding" approach, while specifically avoiding praising students with excellent academic performance too frequently, which the authors say breeds negative psychology and leads to antisocial behavior.
