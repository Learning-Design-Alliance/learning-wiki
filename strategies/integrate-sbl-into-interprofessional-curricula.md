# Integrate simulation-based learning into interprofessional curricula to cultivate collaboration early in training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating simulation into interprofessional curricula, concluding that the findings "support the integration of SBL into interprofessional curricula and suggest its potential as a scalable strategy in resource-constrained educational settings". They further highlight "the importance of structured learning activities that actively engage all participants" for developing teamwork, communication and collaborative decision-making, and support WHO recommendations for integrating SBL into health professional training programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured learning activities with trained facilitators and debriefing sessions
#### Constraints
- The article notes the potential as a scalable strategy is suggested, not directly tested; future research should explore long-term impact on clinical performance and patient outcomes

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduate health professions students

### Target Learning Goals
- Readiness for interprofessional collaboration and collaborative clinical practice
