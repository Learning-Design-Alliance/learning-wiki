# Tiered lab curriculum: single-session demonstration through semester-long student-designed projects on a shared pendulum platform

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a progression of uses: "A compelling short sophomore-level lab might employ an instructor-setup pendulum and optics, using a single illumination power", letting students observe laser-induced deflection and compare amplitudes to expectations. Multi-session versions add overnight data collection, multiple datapoints, Fourier-transform exploration, and noise analysis. Advanced students choose project directions across multi-week to semester-long labs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- For the short lab, an instructor-set-up pendulum and optics so students can collect sufficient data quickly
- For extended projects, students determine experimental details, implement the design, and characterize pendulum behavior themselves
#### Constraints
- Once the pendulum is set up, the authors advise staying away from the optical table while taking datapoints to avoid vibrations
- The authors recommend using a climate-controlled room to avoid temperature-drift issues entirely

### Target Learners
- sophomore-level undergraduate physics students
- advanced undergraduates in multi-week labs

### Target Learning Goals
- building intuition about oscillator systems
- demonstrating the power of Fourier analysis
- developing student-designed experimental projects
