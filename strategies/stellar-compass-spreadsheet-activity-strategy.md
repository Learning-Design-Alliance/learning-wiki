# Stellar-compass construction activity using a spreadsheet of bright stars' rising and setting azimuths

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Students enter their latitude and a chosen star elevation (10 degrees is suggested) into an Excel file that converts sky coordinates to azimuths, then plot each star's rising and setting azimuth on a circular template with a protractor. The spreadsheet "consists of 57 bright stars plus the Pleiades which is a very prominent group of stars." The resulting compass shows symmetric azimuths about the meridian and lets students steer courses as ancient navigators did.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Computer with MS Excel and the supplied spreadsheet astroedu1645_AncientMediterranean_BrightStars.xlsx; protractor and the compass template
#### Constraints
- Stars whose cells show #NA never rise or set (circumpolar or below horizon); the exercise targets stars that just rise or set at the chosen elevation

### Target Learners
- Secondary school students aged 14–19

### Target Learning Goals
- using rising and setting positions of stars to determine cardinal directions and maintain a ship's course
