Strategies

Provide in-service training equipping mathematics teachers with game- and poem-based teaching skills¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that government bodies and the National Mathematical Centre run in-service training so mathematics teachers acquire game- and poem-based instructional skills. It states the NMC and government "should embark on in-service training for mathematics teachers to equip them with new skills, such as games and poems needed for effective teaching." It also recommends that curriculum developers include games and poems as activities in the mathematics curriculum and that textbook authors write books of mathematical poems.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training bodies (NMC, government) and curriculum agencies (NERDC) must organise and mandate the training and curriculum changes

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation is directed at the Nigerian primary mathematics context

Target Learners¶

  • Primary school students taught by trained mathematics teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Interest in mathematics learning

Claims¶

  • Instructional strategy has a significant main effect on primary students’ posttest interest in mathematics [+W]
  • Poem-enhanced instruction produced higher posttest interest than game-enhanced and modified lecture instruction [+W]
  • Treatment and verbal ability interact significantly in their effect on students’ interest in mathematics [~W]
  • Verbal ability has a significant main effect on primary students’ interest in mathematics, with higher verbal ability groups scoring higher [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Implement TGT game-based cooperative learning in after-school programs with adequately trained teachers and extended treatment duration
  • Adopt active-participation strategies such as CRAIS for mathematics teaching and train teachers through seminars and workshops
  • Integrate classic African board games into teaching mathematics, STEM skills, and AI topics, with teacher training
  • Provide prolonged, sustained professional development so in-service teachers can deliver digital game-based learning effectively
  • Employ educational games in primary science teaching and train teachers to design them
