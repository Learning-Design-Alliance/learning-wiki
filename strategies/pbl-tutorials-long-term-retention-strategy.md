# Use PBL tutorials to support long-term retention of basic-science knowledge before clinical training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's conclusion recommends PBL as a learning tool for durable learning: the "non-significant reduction in the pre-clinics knowledge and confidence scores resulting from PBL proves it to be a potent learning tool for long term retention of knowledge, and sustainability of confidence". The authors suggest continuing the study to determine application of PBL-gained basic science knowledge in solving clinical problems, and running similar studies throughout the curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- PBL tutorials must be embedded within a hybrid program so that factual knowledge is still covered through didactic lectures.
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single-course pilot restricted to female students at one institution; the authors call for curriculum-wide studies to provide a true reflection of PBL effectiveness.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students preparing to enter clinical courses

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term retention of basic science knowledge
- sustainability of self-reported confidence

## Claims

- [Fifteen months after the course, lecture-based knowledge and confidence scores decreased significantly from post-course to pre-clinics, while problem-based knowledge and confidence scores showed no significant decrease in one group with no comparison group](../claims/pbl-long-term-retention-pre-clinics.md) [+M]
- [At post-course evaluation, lecture-based and PBL knowledge scores did not differ significantly, but lecture-based confidence scores were significantly higher than problem-based confidence scores](../claims/post-course-knowledge-parity-confidence-gap.md) [~M]
- [Post-course knowledge and confidence correlated moderately and positively for both pedagogies (r = 0.514 lecture-based; r = 0.495 problem-based), but no significant correlation existed at pre-course or pre-clinics evaluation](../claims/post-course-knowledge-confidence-moderate-correlation.md) [+W]
