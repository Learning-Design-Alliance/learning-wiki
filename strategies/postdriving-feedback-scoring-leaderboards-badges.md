# Use postdriving feedback mechanisms (drive scoring, leaderboards, achievement badges) instead of feedback during driving to avoid distraction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The review recommends that to implement gamification without distracting the driver, "postdriving feedback is suggested instead of feedback during driving", and that "drive scoring, leaderboards, and achievement badges could be used as feedback mechanisms". This strategy shifts gamified feedback to after the trip, since the reviewed evidence showed instant visual feedback during driving can increase cognitive workload and slow reaction times. It is offered as a design direction for future gamified safe-driving interventions rather than a tested design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback mechanisms must fulfill users' expectations of challenge, interaction, social connection, and personalization without causing distraction.
#### Constraints
- The review states there is a lack of evidence of the effectiveness of gamification design and in real-world driving implementations, and that gamification has its own risks, including possible adverse effects from increasing competition.

### Target Learners
- adolescent and young novice drivers

### Target Learning Goals
- safe driving behavior change
- sustained engagement with safe-driving apps
