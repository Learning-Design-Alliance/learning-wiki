# Integrate support practices such as tutoring, learning communities, supplemental instruction, experiential learning, and early intervention into the introductory astronomy curriculum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The chapter recommends that community college astronomy instructors deliberately embed student-support structures into Astro 101 rather than leaving them optional, because many community college students lack academic study skills and will not seek out optional supports. It argues that to a college student, optional translates to not going to happen, so engagement must be "a deliberate and focused eﬀort." Specific practices it names as integrable include "tutoring, learning communities, supplemental instruction, experiential learning, and early intervention."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional structures such as tutoring centers, learning communities, and early-intervention systems must exist and be connected to the course
#### Constraints
- Some supports, such as first-year experience and orientation programs, are described as largely out of the hands of most astronomy instructors

### Target Learners
- Community college students in introductory astronomy, many part-time, working, or first-generation

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic success and persistence in introductory general-education science
