# Instructor-provided physical artifacts plus gesture prompts to help students translate between representations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
During the oral exam the instructor introduces a durable physical artifact (a sheet of paper standing for the sheet of current) and gives gestural and verbal prompts, such as showing the current direction with a hand and asking the student to draw his Amperian loop on the board. These moves prompt the student to use gestures and words to build meaning on durable representations and to progress toward mathematics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor must supply a relevant durable artifact and allow students to gesture and talk through the solution
#### Constraints
- Observed in an individual oral exam setting with one highly verbal student

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students in oral exams or small-group problem solving

### Target Learning Goals
- translating between diagrammatic and mathematical representations
