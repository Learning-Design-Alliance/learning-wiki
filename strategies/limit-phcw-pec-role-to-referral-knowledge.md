# Limit the primary health care worker's primary eye care role to knowing where to refer patients with eye complaints

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the trial's weak results for diagnosis and management skills, the authors recommend narrowing the PHCW's primary eye care role. They write that "it might be better limited to knowledge of where to refer people they see with eye complaints, particularly in settings where few eye patients present to PHC facilities". This repositions task shifting away from training frontline workers to diagnose and manage eye conditions toward referral knowledge alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- an urgent need to define more precisely what is meant by primary eye care, what skills are required to provide it, and how realistic it is to train general PHCWs to gain proficiency in the corresponding skills
#### Constraints
- more evidence on the effectiveness of current concepts of PEC is required before advocating for their expansion

### Target Learners
- general primary health care workers in settings where few eye patients present to PHC facilities

### Target Learning Goals
- referral knowledge for patients presenting with eye complaints
