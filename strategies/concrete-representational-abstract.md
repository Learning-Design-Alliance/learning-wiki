# Concrete Representational Abstract

## Description
Concrete Representational Abstract (CRA) is an instructional sequence — rooted in Bruner's enactive–iconic–symbolic progression — in which learners first explore a concept with physical manipulatives, then with drawings or diagrams, and finally with abstract notation. Each phase builds on the previous one, with explicit links made between representations so the abstract symbols inherit meaning from the concrete and pictorial stages.

## Design Implications

CRA manages the transition from embodied, situated understanding to symbolic fluency, reducing the risk that symbols are manipulated without meaning [Cognitive overload degrades learning when novices must process abstract notation without supporting representations.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+M]. Its effectiveness depends on the *connections* drawn between phases: manipulatives and pictures help mainly when instruction explicitly links them to the target concept, not when they are used as standalone activities [Manipulatives support learning when instruction makes the concrete–abstract link explicit.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2012.12.001) [~M]. The sequence should fade — learners should not remain in the concrete phase longer than needed, and the abstract phase is the actual instructional goal.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Manipulatives or concrete materials that genuinely model the target structure (not merely decorative objects)
- Explicit mapping language at each transition ("the three counters are the same as the 3 in the equation")
- A plan for fading: each phase is temporary, with the abstract phase as the destination
- Consistent representations across phases so learners can map one onto the other

#### Constraints
- Manipulatives left unconnected to symbols can become a distraction; learners may play with materials without extracting the intended structure [Concrete materials can increase extraneous load when their perceptual richness is not tied to the target concept.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2012.12.001) [-M]
- Staying too long in the concrete phase delays fluency with symbols and can produce dependence on physical supports
- Highly realistic or feature-rich concrete materials can divert attention from the deep structure to surface features [Perceptually rich manipulatives can reduce transfer compared with bland ones.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2012.12.001) [~M]
- Less suitable for learners who already operate fluently at the abstract level, for whom the sequence is redundant [Guidance and support sequences become less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **CRA-I** (with explicit integration lessons between phases) — used in mathematics interventions for students with learning disabilities
- **Partial CRA** — starting at the representational phase when learners already have concrete experience with the concept
- **Virtual manipulatives** — digital versions (e.g., Brainingcamp, Didax) that can scaffold the concrete phase at scale
- **Simultaneous linking** — showing concrete, pictorial, and symbolic forms side by side rather than in strict sequence, useful when time is limited

### Target Learners
- Novices in a symbol-heavy domain (typically early mathematics, but also science notation and music) [Concrete representations reduce working-memory demands for learners without existing symbolic schemas.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Learners with learning disabilities or limited prior knowledge, for whom CRA-based interventions show consistent gains in computation and word-problem performance [CRA sequences improve arithmetic outcomes for students with mathematics difficulties.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022466909359114) [+M]
- Less beneficial for advanced learners, who can often enter directly at the abstract phase [Guidance and support sequences become less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of abstract symbol systems (place value, fractions, algebraic variables)
- Procedural fluency grounded in meaning rather than memorized steps
- Representational flexibility — the ability to translate among concrete, pictorial, and symbolic forms

### Instructions
1. **Concrete phase:** introduce the concept with physical materials learners can manipulate; model the target structure with them ([Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) if available, otherwise direct modeling).
2. **Link explicitly:** name the correspondence between the materials and the concept while both are visible; do not assume learners will see the mapping on their own.
3. **Representational phase:** replace objects with drawings, bar models, number lines, or diagrams of the same structure; have learners draw before they compute.
4. **Abstract phase:** introduce symbols and connect them back ("show me the 3/4 in your picture"); require learners to explain the mapping.
5. **Fade:** reduce reliance on drawings as fluency grows, following the same logic as [Fading](../elements/fading.md) — support is temporary and withdrawn as competence develops [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility to the learner.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

## Examples
**CRA mathematics intervention (Butler, Miller, Crehan, Babbitt & Pierce, 2003)** — Algebra instruction for secondary students with learning disabilities sequenced from concrete tiles to diagrams to symbolic equations; the CRA group outperformed a representational-abstract group on posttests and maintained gains at follow-up.

**[Cognitively Guided Instruction](../patterns/cognitively-guided-instruction-cgi-for-math.md)** — Builds instruction from children's informal, concrete solution strategies toward formal abstract procedures, treating learners' own concrete methods as the starting point.

**[Brainingcamp](https://www.brainingcamp.com)** — Digital fraction tiles and other virtual manipulatives paired with symbolic notation on the same screen, enacting the concrete-to-abstract link in a single view.
