# Make teaching entrustment explicit through direct observation, didactic training, and inclusion of teaching EPAs in training programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that postgraduate training programs "explicitly include teaching as a skill to be learned and practiced" and that teacher training programs include concepts for entrusting student supervision as a defined teaching activity. Courses could address risks and promote direct supervision; where time is scarce, recording teaching sessions with subsequent feedback could improve implementation. Teaching EPAs could be incorporated into medical school peer teaching programs so junior doctors know the concept before postgraduate training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit space and protected time must be set aside for trainee teaching, as interviewees stressed
- Observation of student supervision is needed to assess knowledge, didactic skills, strain and overload
#### Constraints
- The article notes direct observation "may seem time-consuming and not easily feasible" in a busy general practice context
- Didactic qualifications are not carried out regularly due to a lack of resources

### Target Learners
- GP trainees and postgraduate medical trainees
- Medical students in peer teaching programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Teaching and student supervision skills
- Constructive feedback skills
- Explicit entrustment and safe delegation of teaching

### Affordances
- Teaching Entrustment Model General Practice
