# Shift outreach from increasing enrollment to increasing freedom: present all career alternatives fairly so girls can make a personal informed choice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a "paradigm shift" in outreach programs toward K-12 girls: instead of selling science and engineering to as many girls as possible, programs should empower free choice. Telling girls "all they need to know about the many alternatives (science, engineering, law, medicine, business, journalism, teaching, art, staying at home , etc.)" may enable "a personal informed choice". The author notes this is no longer science outreach but fair and neutral career counseling.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Presenting competing choices in a fair way, including non-scientific alternatives, so the choice is informed and neutral rather than promotional
#### Constraints
- The article states that assessing the match between a possibly unexpressed preference and a career is more difficult than assessing enrollment, and that allowing free choice would be a practical nightmare to implement

### Target Learners
- K-12 girls, generally high-school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Informed, free career choice congruent with abilities and desires

### Affordances
- Ethical Underrepresentation Fairness Criterion
