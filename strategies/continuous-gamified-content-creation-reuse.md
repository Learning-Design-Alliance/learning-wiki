# Plan for continuous creation of additional gamified content and reuse it across years or sections, tying it to participation marks rather than full course grades

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's implementation guidance for managing the workload of a gamified course. The authors found that gamification requires content beyond a traditional course: "additional conten t must be continually created beyond that which would be created in a traditional course", including multiple extra quizzes and quests per week because teams completed quests at different speeds. They conclude gamification is an intense human resource endeavor that becomes manageable when content is reused across years or sections and tied to the participation mark rather than the full course grade.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A continuous stream of new quests must be provided to keep students engaged as different teams complete quests at different speeds
#### Constraints
- When the entire course is gamified and applied to the final grade, each activity must maintain a standard of rigor and relate to course and program outcomes, making content hard to recycle year after year

### Target Learners
- undergraduate media studies students

### Target Learning Goals
- student engagement with course material and activities
