Sequence simpler desirable difficulties (retrieval, spacing, interleaving) during initial L2 vocabulary encoding, reserving generation for post-encoding consolidation¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that during initial vocabulary acquisition, teachers use less complex desirable difficulties first, since generation's added complexity did not yield the expected long-term recall enhancement. It cites recent research indicating "generation may serve best as a consolidation strategy post-encoding" and proposes providing contextual examples at initial exposure, then retrieval practice such as flashcards with spacing and interleaving, before learners generate and transfer vocabulary knowledge to new contexts.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learners must first have the vocabulary embedded in long-term memory via retrieval, spacing, and interleaving before generation tasks are introduced.

Constraints¶

  • The article states the present study's choice to prioritize generation over simpler methods did not yield the expected enhancement in recall over the longer period investigated.

Target Learners¶

  • First-year Japanese university ELLs at B1 to B1+ CEFR level

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Long-term retention of academic L2 vocabulary for sustained use and transfer

  • Research agenda: use process-oriented assessments, study encoding-consolidation-retrieval effects, and examine longitudinal outcomes of educational generative AI
