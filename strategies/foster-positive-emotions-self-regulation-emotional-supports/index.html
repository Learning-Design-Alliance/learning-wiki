Foster positive emotions, support self-regulatory skills, and provide emotional supports for students shifting to digital learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (1 review, 1 associational, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

From the Ge perspective on practical and policy impact, the editorial notes that teachers and students shifting rapidly to digital learning may experience negative emotions. It suggests multiple extensions: "developing and testing strategies to foster positive emotions," "supporting students with self-regulatory skills that can reduce the negative emotions," and providing emotional supports for students, along with multiple data collection methods to measure emotions.

Claims¶

  • Restrictive digital-use policies may reduce short-term exposure while doing less to build long-term self-regulatory capacity [+W]
  • Affective reactivity can override adolescents' regulatory intentions for digital engagement [+W]
  • Adolescents proactively use online technologies to unwind, escape stress, and regulate emotions [+W]
  • Pre-actional metacognitive, motivational, and positive-emotional components are positively associated with regulatory behavior in the actional phase, while anger and anxiety are negatively associated [+W]

Related Strategies¶
