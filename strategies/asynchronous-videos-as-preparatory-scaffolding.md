# Use asynchronous instructional videos as preparatory scaffolding before simulation-based workshops to enable more efficient use of supervised training time.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article conceptualizes asynchronous videos not merely as supplementary resources but as "a form of preparatory pedagogical scaffolding within clinically demanding training environments." This scaffolding enables a redistribution of cognitive and instructional effort, allowing supervised sessions to focus more explicitly on technical refinement and feedback. The strategy involves assigning short instructional videos before hands-on simulation workshops so that learners arrive with foundational procedural understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Videos should be short and concise to minimize review time
- Videos should remain accessible during practice
#### Constraints
- Requires adequate instructor-to-student ratios for individualized feedback
- Physical space and audio conditions must be adequate
- Viewing was recommended but not mandatory and engagement was not formally monitored

### Target Learners
- Medical students during surgical internships

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural skills acquisition (suturing)
