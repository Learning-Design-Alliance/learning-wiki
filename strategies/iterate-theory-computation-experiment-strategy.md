# Design lab courses so computation, experimentation, and theory iterate and inform each other across multiple lab periods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend structuring labs so that experimental observations feed back into theoretical and computational models, and students revisit earlier work with new information. They write that "Allowing computation to inform experimentation, experimentation to inform computation, and both together to inform the mathematical model, helps students have a better understanding of the scientific method and how the scientific community knows what it knows." Concrete extensions include adding background radiation to the model after students observe ambient counts, and exploring Poisson regression after least-squares bias appears.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple lab periods or reserved discussion time so students can circle back to previous theory and modeling work
- Instructor acting as a coach who probes whether students' understanding, implementation, and analysis are robust
#### Constraints
- Constraints of the lab-period length influence pedagogical choices; the authors reserve deeper bias exploration for interested or more advanced students
- Students' sophistication limits some statistical discussions (e.g., transformation bias) at the beginning of their second year

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in modern physics lab courses

### Target Learning Goals
- a more realistic understanding of how physical models develop and evolve
- understanding the relationship between theory, models, and physical systems
