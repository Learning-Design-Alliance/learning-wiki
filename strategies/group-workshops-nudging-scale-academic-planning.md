# Scale academic planning through counselor-led group workshops with targeted nudging rather than one-on-one appointments alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable recipe for scaling academic planning under severe counseling capacity limits (a national median student-to-counselor ratio of 441 to 1) is to run counselor-led group sessions with standardized curriculum in a computer lab, where students complete and often leave with counselor-approved plans, combined with personalized electronic nudges and guaranteed appointment slots held for two weeks. The district believed "that the best way to achieve this goal was to run counselor-led group sessions, rather than require students to schedule an appointment or wait in line for a one-on-one counselor meeting." The RCT found the workshop raised completion by about 20 percentage points at the lowest cost per plan completed, with exploratory evidence it performed as well as one-on-one counseling.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A counselor-designed workshop curriculum and counselor review and approval of each plan
- Guaranteed appointment slots and a nudge delivery platform (Sherpa) integrated with scheduling software
- Counselor training to standardize quality across formats; the majority of counselors received six to eight hours of training
#### Constraints
- A majority of students in all groups still did not complete a plan, so the article states additional intervention is needed to ensure all students engage in academic planning
- Early workshop attendance was low due to inadequate publicity and a first nudge sent after the first workshop

### Target Learners
- First-time community college students subject to academic plan registration requirements

### Target Learning Goals
- Completion of a counselor-approved, semester-by-semester academic plan
