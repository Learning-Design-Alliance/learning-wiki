Strategies

Implement modified Moore lessons with patient guidance, adequate time, and enriched materials¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's recommendations for implementing the modified Moore learning approach: lecturers 'should be more patient in giving guidan ce and presenting excersices task so that students were motivated to compose mathematical proof by the mselves', conduct the lesson in one semester, and continuously cultivate positive learning dispositions. Learning materials should be completed with more examples, illustrations, and exercises of varied difficulty with guiding questions, and difficult proof problems can be assigned as homework or group tasks.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Materials completed with examples, illustrations, and exercises at various difficulty levels with relevant guidance and questions
  • Discussion of student presentations and selected exercise tasks during lessons

Constraints¶

  • Findings were lower than prior studies because this study ran half a semester on a more complex course with medium-low ability students

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate mathematics students, including those with low prior mathematics ability

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mathematical proving ability
  • mathematical understanding ability
  • critical and creative thinking, communication, reasoning, problem solving, self-regulated learning

Affordances¶

  • Modified Moore Learning Approach

Claims¶

  • More MLA students reached medium/high understanding scores while conventional students did so more often on proving scores [+W]
  • Modified Moore learning approach and conventional teaching produced no significant difference in undergraduate mathematical understanding or proving ability in advanced abstract algebra [-W]
  • Students held positive dispositions toward the modified Moore approach, but low prior-ability students disliked it and struggled [~W]
