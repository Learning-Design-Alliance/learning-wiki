# Use classroom action research and teacher–researcher collaboration to bridge the DDL research–practice gap

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends classroom action research as a way to fill the gap between research and instruction in corpus-based DDL, with "teachers become more involved in applying the corpus-based DDL approach and evaluating its efficacy". It further proposes joint research projects between teachers as direct users of DDL and corpus linguistics researchers to connect empirical evidence with classroom practice, and notes experimental studies with large populations promise generalization and replication across settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher involvement in applying and evaluating the DDL approach
- Collaboration between teachers and corpus linguistics researchers
#### Constraints
- Case studies are valuable for detailed accounts but experimental studies with large populations are needed for generalization within given contexts and replication in different settings

### Target Learners
- L2 and EFL students across instructional contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective adoption of corpus-based DDL for language development
