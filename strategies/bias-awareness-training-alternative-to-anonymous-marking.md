# Train assessors' awareness of unconscious bias as a cost-effective alternative to anonymous marking, and reconsider all-coursework modules

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that anonymous marking had negligible effects on group differences, the article recommends that "training to increase assessors' awareness of their own potential unconscious biases might prove to be a more cost-effective option for ameliorating performance differences", since anonymous marking is potentially costly to implement. It also recommends practitioners "reconsider their position on all-coursework modules", because the findings suggest any fairness gain from replacing written examinations with coursework is "borderline at best" while potentially exacerbating plagiarism and collusion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Further research should first target the impact of anonymous marking on performance differences before revoking or extending the practice.
#### Constraints
- The authors state it would be reactionary to recommend anonymous marking be revoked based on the present study's findings alone.

- higher education students
