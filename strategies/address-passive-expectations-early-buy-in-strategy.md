# Address student expectations of passive instruction early in the semester to promote buy-in

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on Expectancy Violation Theory, the article recommends that instructors address student expectations of a passive instruction style early in the semester by sharing the motivation and data behind active-learning instruction. This introduction may help students connect new active-learning experiences to positive impacts on their learning and increase buy-in, which the authors say is crucial to maintain classroom interactions and possibly improve performance, and could reinforce changes in institutional culture.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must explicitly explain how different components of the course relate to student learning, as emphasized in the IBIS faculty and peer-mentor training sessions
#### Constraints
- A barrier to buy-in may be the novelty of active learning; resistance can occur when class requirements conflict with expectations based on previous experiences

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduates new to active-learning classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- student buy-in to active learning
- engagement in active learning activities
