# Calibrate AI-generated material complexity and organize access to avoid overwhelming students, and identify high-cognitive-load students early

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's lessons-learned section recommends that educators using teacher-prompted AI generation manage cognitive demand and organization: "educators could reduce the length of videos, the text on infographics and the length of reports, and calibrate the language in the materials". It also advises organizing materials coherently rather than dumping them in a folder, and identifying early the students who find materials difficult. The strategy rests on the observed negative correlation between cognitive load and grades.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators need access to a RAG-based AI tool such as Google NotebookLM and course materials as generation input
#### Constraints
- The article notes generation limitations of the AI tool, such as the number of infographics or videos allowed daily, would certainly be a constraint

### Target Learners
- EFL associate-degree students in high-stakes academic pathways

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic writing and presentation skills in English for Academic Purposes
