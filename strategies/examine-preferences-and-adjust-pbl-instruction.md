# Teachers examine students' environment preferences and continually adjust PBL instruction to student perceptions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that before and during problem-based instruction, teachers actively account for how students prefer and perceive the learning environment. "First, teachers need to examine students' preferences for the learning environment and familiarise themselves with PBL before providing them with PBL instruction." "Second, teachers need to consider how students perceive the learning environment" and continually adjust instruction so students engage with PBL activities and gain satisfaction and a sense of achievement. They also argue nursing education should improve the professional development of PBL teachers, blending content and pedagogical knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must have knowledge of students' preferences and of PBL practice before implementing problem-based instruction
#### Constraints
- Recommended in the context of problem-based learning becoming a focus of nursing educational reform in Taiwan; formal professional development policies rarely exist for medical educators

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- improving knowledge achievement, self-efficacy and attitudes in problem-based nursing curricula

### Affordances
- Four Quadrant Preferred Perceived Congruence Framework
