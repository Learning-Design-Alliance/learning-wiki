# Evaluate curriculum reform with long-term follow-up surveys after graduation, not student evaluations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The author argues that student evaluations "are good at gauging student satisfaction but do not accurately measure the effectiveness of learning", because students may not immediately appreciate educational benefits. He proposes evaluating reform success through "follow-up survey several years after graduation", asking whether the reforms improved intellectual development and preparation for diverse careers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Long-term engagement with students, specifically follow-up surveys several years after graduation
- Carefully worded evaluation questions
#### Constraints
- An accurate evaluation of the success of curriculum reform is not easy, per the article

### Target Learners
- undergraduate science students

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating intellectual development and career preparation outcomes of curriculum reform
