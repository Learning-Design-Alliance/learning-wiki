# Address voluntary-recruitment failure through mandatory or risk-based enrollment structures, while weighing the risk of reducing students' self-recognized need for remediation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because voluntary recruitment enrolled only about 11–17% of high-risk students, the authors discuss alternative recruitment structures. Some institutions "build supplemental instruction into the mandatory structure of the lecture course," while others require only at-risk students to participate, identified via high school GPA, SAT scores, and socioeconomic status, or via placement exams before entrance. The authors caution that obligate remediation may prevent students from concluding on their own that they need to change study habits, increasing resistance to the strategies taught.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A way to identify at-risk students before or early in the course, such as placement exams or pre-college metrics
#### Constraints
- Obligate remediation may keep students from recognizing their own need for remediation, which the authors suggest could increase resistance to the ideas

### Target Learners
- High-risk first-year students in introductory STEM courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Adoption of effective study strategies
- Retention in STEM majors
