# AI-assisted quality-control tools that flag radiocapitellar line deviation and inadequate projections as supports for junior clinicians

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that AI-based tools trained to detect alignment abnormalities, such as radiocapitellar line deviation, and to assess the adequacy of radiographic projections could act as real-time quality-control systems. Such systems would flag suboptimal images and prompt closer evaluation, supporting—rather than replacing—clinical judgment, particularly for junior physicians or staff in high-volume emergency settings. The article stresses this is a future research direction requiring development and validation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training data covering radiocapitellar alignment on both AP and lateral views
- Automated recognition of whether an image represents a true AP or lateral view
- Validation tailored to pediatric musculoskeletal imaging with attention to usability in acute care and educational environments
#### Constraints
- Proposed as a complementary role only; the article presents no tested AI system or performance data
- Proper radiographic positioning is critical, as malrotated or misaligned images may obscure anatomical landmarks

### Target Learners
- Junior physicians
- Clinicians working in high-volume emergency settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Reliable capture of diagnostic landmarks in pediatric elbow radiographs
- Closer evaluation of radiocapitellar alignment to detect radial head dislocation
