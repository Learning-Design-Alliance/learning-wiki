# Evaluate school research-mentorship programs by collecting objective indicators, with interviews of students, teachers, and mentors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that in evaluating an educational project like Tan-Q, "it is desirable to collect objective indicators and data soon" and proposes that "Interviews are a possible method to manage the outcomes." It enumerates three guiding questions: how students' interest in physics-related fields changes, how school teachers evaluate the program, and what mentors gain as learners and educators. This gives programs a concrete evaluation recipe combining indicators with stakeholder interviews.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Identify stakeholders in three roles — students, school teachers, and mentors — and formulate questions for each
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a planned future method, not an evaluation already carried out

### Target Learners
- junior and senior high school students in research-mentorship programs
- university mentors

### Target Learning Goals
- program-level assessment of inquiry-based research education outcomes
