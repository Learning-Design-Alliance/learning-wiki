# Apply metacognitive resolution methodology to assess the accuracy of effort appraisals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends applying well-established metacognitive methodologies for studying biases in self-appraisals to investigate effort appraisals. Specifically, it proposes "to determine the covariation of effort appraisals and objective effort measures as an indicator of the resolution of effort appraisals." Resolution is calculated as a within-participant correlation between judgment and objective measure across items, requiring simultaneous administration of both measure types for several items.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Administering subjective and objective effort measures simultaneously for several items
- At least ten items per participant for statistically robust within-participant correlations
#### Constraints
- Objective effort measures lack an absolute minimum and maximum score with clear meaning, precluding calibration

### Target Learners
- students performing multi-item learning or problem-solving tasks

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate monitoring of effort investment across varying task demands
