# Use the historical reasoning framework to design and analyze tasks, tools, and assessments in history education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes using the six-component framework "both in future research and in educational practice". In research it serves to code and compare reasoning in essays, chat discussions, and classroom talk; in practice it can guide the design of tasks, questions, and tools that provoke rich historical reasoning, such as evaluative questions and representational tools like argumentative diagrams.
