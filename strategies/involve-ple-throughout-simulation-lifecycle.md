# Involve people with lived experience throughout simulation, from defining objectives and codesigning scenarios to training actors, observing simulations and contributing to debriefings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy moves PLE beyond narrative sharing into sustained partnership across the whole simulation lifecycle. In the article, six PLE informed learning objectives, scenario design and debriefing, four revised the script and validated patient profiles, and one trained actors, observed simulations and joined debriefings. The authors state their approach 'demonstrates the value of engaging them throughout the process—from defining objectives and codesigning scenarios to training actors, observing simulations and contributing to debriefings'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- PLE must be recruited with relevant lived experience, trained on feedback expectations and simulation-based teaching principles, and remunerated, as the article describes
#### Constraints
- Logistical constraints limited direct observation and personalised feedback to a few teams; students wanted more PLE presence

### Target Learners
- third-year pharmacy students
- health professions students in simulation-based education

### Target Learning Goals
- inclusive, person-centred communication on sensitive topics
- reducing stigma and bias
- PrEP consultation skills
