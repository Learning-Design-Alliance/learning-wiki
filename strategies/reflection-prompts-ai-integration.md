# Use student reflection on subject-related datasets and AI model behavior to address both common core standards and AI competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
This strategy embeds AI concepts into core curriculum through structured student reflection on content and AI methods, so one activity addresses both common core standards and AI literacy competencies. The article shows that reflection on patterns in a constellation classification model's input and output addresses the standard 1-ESS1-1 and the AI concept "Learning from Data". In the exemplar curricula, "students were asked to reflect on what did and did not work and why, and on the real-world implications of a biased dataset in airplane development", addressing 3-5-ETS1-3 plus AI Strengths & Weaknesses, Critically Interpreting Data, and Ethics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- reflection prompts must be designed into the curriculum rather than improvised; teachers recognized even younger students are capable of deep reflection
#### Constraints
- developed with a small group of teachers in non-technical subjects; the article cautions against generalizing beyond this context

### Target Learners
- K-12 students across grade bands, including younger students

### Target Learning Goals
- AI competencies including AI Strengths & Weaknesses, Critically Interpreting Data, and Ethics
- common core standards such as 1-ESS1-1 and 3-5-ETS1-3
