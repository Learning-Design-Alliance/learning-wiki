# Embodied carbon-cycle bathtub activity to teach steady state versus non-steady state

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A classroom activity (Box 1) in which students play carbon dioxide molecules moving through a taped rectangle representing the atmosphere, with two ushers acting as carbon sources and sinks. When "the first usher is putting students into the rectangle as fast as the second usher is bringing them out, there is generally a constant number of students in the rectangle" — a steady state; when the entry usher speeds up, observers record a rising graph, a non-steady state. Observers plot students-in-rectangle versus time, and the activity is followed by think-pair-share applying the result to Figures 1 and 2.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A floor rectangle large enough for about a third of the class; ushers and student observers; follow-up discussion connecting to the CO2 graph
#### Constraints
- The article notes a possible misconception that the atmosphere is 'getting crowded with carbon dioxide' if the rectangle is too small; CO2 concentration is around 0.04%

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate non-science-majors physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding steady state versus non-steady state and anthropogenic disruption of the carbon cycle
