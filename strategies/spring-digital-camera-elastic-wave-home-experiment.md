# Use a released plastic spring filmed with a digital camera as a home experiment to teach elastic waves

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes substituting a plastic spring for a metallic bar so that deformations are large and slow enough to record with a standard digital camera. Students film the released spring, extract consecutive frames with freeware, and compare observed tag motion with the analytical wave solution. The authors state the experiments "can be easily performed in the classroom and repeated at home by the interested student".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A plastic spring (stretched it behaves much like the elastic bar), a digital camera or camcorder able to record short video sequences, and freeware to process the video sequences
#### Constraints
- The spring's elastic properties change completely when the loops touch one another, so the bar model applies only qualitatively or for limited time spans; attaching a mass on top extends the valid interval

### Target Learners
- students in introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding elastic wave propagation and the initial motion of a released elastic bar
