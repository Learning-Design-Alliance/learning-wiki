# Rich Library: Audio & Braille

## Description
Using audiobooks and Braille texts to reinforce and inspire literacy skills. This includes providing access to literature and other reading selections in audio form for all students, including emerging readers and ESL/ELL learners. Some resources provide accompanying text to follow along, while others include options to interact with the book through clicking, responding, and watching animations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to audiobooks and Braille texts, digital devices or players, and internet access for online resources.
#### Constraints
- Requires careful selection of resources to ensure quality and alignment with curriculum goals. May not be suitable for all types of content or learners.

### Target Learners

- All students, including emerging readers, ESL/ELL learners, and students with visual impairments.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Reinforce literacy skills, improve English skills, study literature in new ways, cater to diverse learning needs, and enhance comprehension.

### Affordances
- Provides auditory learning experiences, supports learners with visual difficulties, reinforces literacy skills through multiple modalities, and allows for personalized learning experiences.

### Personalization
- Learners can adjust settings like speaker voice and speed to suit their needs. Resources vary in format, offering audio-only, text-to-follow, and interactive options.

## Assessment Evidence
- Monitor student engagement with audiobooks, assess comprehension through quizzes and discussions, and evaluate improvements in literacy skills over time.

## Impact
- Research shows that audiobooks can improve comprehension, vocabulary, and reading fluency, especially for struggling readers and English language learners. Braille texts support literacy development for visually impaired students. Interactive resources can enhance engagement and motivation.
