Interventions targeting cognitive stimulation may help reduce SES-related cognitive and educational disparities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review discusses that cognitive stimulation is a particularly important determinant of children's cognitive development and that interventions targeting cognitive stimulation would be beneficial for children from low SES families. It cites evidence that intervention studies designed to increase cognitive stimulation have consistently shown benefits for cognitive outcomes, including school-based stimulation programs and a randomized controlled trial from Jamaica. The review suggests that children with lower initial cognitive abilities may benefit from structured cognitive stimulation interventions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interventions should be designed to enhance cognitive stimulation for children from low SES families.

Constraints¶

  • The review notes that reciprocal interactions exist between children's cognitive abilities and the stimulation they experience, adding complexity to intervention design.

Target Learners¶

  • Children from low SES families

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Cognitive development
  • Executive function
  • Academic achievement

Claims¶

  • Cognitive stimulation mediates the association between SES and executive function, language ability, and academic achievement [+W]
  • Preschool attendance, home learning activities, and parental support buffer the association between low SES and lower cognitive and language outcomes [+W]
  • SES is negatively associated with children's cognitive and academic performance, leading to long-term educational and economic disparities [+W]
  • SES is a powerful predictor of executive function, language ability, and academic achievement [+W]
  • Stress, support, and stimulation mediate the association of SES with EF, language ability, and academic achievement [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Leverage cognitive strengths of low-SES children via group work, real-world application, and oral/narrative learning and assessment
  • Use knowledge of the specific points and lengths of exposure where children are susceptible to contextual effects to structure interventions aimed at reducing learning inequities
  • Interventions targeting educational expectations in low SES families and schools may help bridge the achievement gap
