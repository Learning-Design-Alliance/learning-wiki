# GEMS can adopt HEMS-style simulation-based NTS training practices, adjusted to the ground environment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that the Norwegian ground emergency medical service strengthen its focus on NTSs by learning from the helicopter service's practices, stating that "The low frequency of training in and assessment of NTSs in GEMS suggests that there is a great potential to learn from HEMS and to strengthen the focus on NTSs". It points to HEMS initiatives such as Camp Torpomoen, an intensive training programme in which pilots, HCMs and anaesthesiologists practise together on rare and challenging tasks in safe environments, and calls for research on establishing GEMS training standards motivated by those in HEMS.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulations need to be specifically designed to incorporate significant differences across the two domains
- Standards that specify requirements of training may inhibit natural disincentives such as time-consumption, interruption of daily duties and increased overall expenses
#### Constraints
- The practice applied in HEMS should be adjusted to better fit the GEMS environment

### Target Learners
- GEMS personnel: paramedics, EMTs, EMT apprentices, EMT nurses and nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- non-technical skills relevant to safe prehospital emergency care
