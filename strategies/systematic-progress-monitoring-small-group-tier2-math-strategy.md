# Schools should conduct systematic progress monitoring and small-group Tier 2 early mathematics instruction for struggling first-graders

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the Educational Implications section the authors recommend that, in the absence of widespread evidence-based Tier 2 mathematics interventions, schools begin providing services to struggling students now. They state "First-grade teachers should conduct systematic progress monitoring on essential mathematical ideas that they are responsible for teaching" and that small-group instruction is a necessary component of early mathematics intervention, deliverable by general education teachers or interventionists with supported coaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to progress-monitoring tools, for which the authors point to the National Center on Response to Intervention website
- Supported coaching for general education teachers or mathematics interventionists implementing Tier 2 interventions
#### Constraints
- The study's intervention was delivered by trained research staff; the authors state scaling up to classroom teachers and interventionists must be conducted and replicated to learn what makes sense for classroom implementation

### Target Learners
- first-grade students at risk for mathematics difficulties

### Target Learning Goals
- early numeracy and whole-number computation performance sufficient to exit Tier 2 intervention
