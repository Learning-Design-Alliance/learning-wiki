Train teachers in argumentation-based science instruction through sustained multi-year workshop programs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The study's intervention model trained all teachers in randomized treatment schools in the SWH technique starting in summer 2009 at workshops in four geographic regions of Iowa. Training "took part over three days and included specific training on the SWH approach including how to foster argumentation and science inquiry skills for students in the classroom," with follow-up training the next summer, within-year training days, and two days before classes began.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sustained training across two summers plus within-school-year training days
  • District superintendent consent for school participation

Constraints¶

  • Implemented in a cluster randomized design with rural and larger-population Iowa elementary schools; geographic clusters of schools were formed to control for free and reduced lunch and minority population differences

Target Learners¶

  • elementary teachers of grades 3-5 science

Target Learning Goals¶

  • fostering classroom argumentation and science inquiry skills
  • students posing questions, gathering data, and making claims supported by evidence

Affordances¶

  • Science Writing Heuristic Approach

Claims¶

  • Students in SWH treatment schools gained about one-tenth of a standard deviation in Mathematics scores per quarter of exposure, erasing an initial disadvantage of roughly 0.4 SD after four semesters [+M]
  • The SWH implementation showed no effect on Science scores [-M]
  • The authors conclude that argumentation-based science teaching produces spillover benefits into Mathematics and Reading via critical thinking rather than content acquisition [+W]
