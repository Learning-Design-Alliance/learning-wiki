# Using Reflection for Habit Formation

## Description
This strategy promotes reflective classrooms to ensure learners are fully engaged in making meaning. Reflection enhances the meaning of work, encourages insight, and fosters complex learning. Teachers act as facilitators of meaning-making, guiding learners to approach activities strategically, monitor individual progress, construct meaning, and apply learnings to other contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Reflective role models, intentional structuring of classroom activities, and facilitating teachers.
#### Constraints
- Students need to be taught how to reflect to derive rich meaning from their experiences. The students must understand that the students will now look back rather than move forward.

### Target Learning Goals

### Affordances
- Promotes active engagement, enhances meaning-making, develops metacognitive skills, and facilitates application of learning.

### Personalization
- Teachers can use discussions, interviews, questioning, and logs and journals to guide student reflection. Students also need to see adults—parents, teachers, and administrators—reflect on their practice.
