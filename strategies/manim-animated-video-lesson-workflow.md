# Workflow for producing animated video lessons: identify the problem, build animations first, then add narration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article prescribes a production sequence for animated video lessons: first precisely identify the concept or problem, then develop explanations and visualizations, render the full video, and finally add narration. Implementing animations during planning lets the educator "actively participate in the learning process, mirroring the student experience." Narration can be skipped if the educator explains live, but recorded narration eliminates repeated instructor explanations during playback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Precise identification of the concept or problem to be explained before building visualizations
- A rendered full video before narration is added
#### Constraints
- Recording narrations is very time-consuming and repetitive; a single misspoken word requires restarting the recording from the beginning

### Target Learners
- students learning STEM topics such as sorting algorithms, mathematical derivations, and data structures

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of specific STEM problems and topics through animation
