# Structure experimental work so students discover physics rules, keeping them in a loop of higher-order thinking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article argues that experimental work yields more quality knowledge only when structured so students discover physics rules themselves. Students first analyze the situation, synthesize and create a research procedure, evaluate it, then return to analyzing results, synthesizing formulas or laws, and evaluating what they obtained, cycling repeatedly through higher-order thinking levels of Bloom's taxonomy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experiments must be structured to allow students to discover physics rules rather than follow cookbook-style procedures that merely confirm known laws.
#### Constraints
- The article notes textbook experiments are often explained poorly, without directions for performing, analyzing, additional questions or activities.

### Target Learners
- High school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Higher-order skills: analyzing, synthesizing, drawing conclusions, evaluating procedures
