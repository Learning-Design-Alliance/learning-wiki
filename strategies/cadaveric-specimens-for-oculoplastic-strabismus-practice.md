# Use cadaveric specimens for oculoplastic, orbital, and strabismus surgery practice to provide more realistic anatomy than synthetic or animal alternatives.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q4` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using cadaveric specimens for practising oculoplastic, orbital, and strabismus surgery, noting that "Our course uses cadaveric specimens for practising oculoplastic, orbital and strabismus surgery, with more realistic anatomy for students to learn from". It contrasts this with prior wet lab models that used pig eyes, Styrofoam, bacon, or chicken meat for strabismus surgery with limited validation data, and notes no human cadaveric strabismus surgery lab had been previously reported in the literature.
