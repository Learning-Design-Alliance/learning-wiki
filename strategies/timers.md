# Timers

## Description
Timers, both visual and auditory, are used to help learners gauge how much time remains during an activity, supporting greater attention and task completion. Visual timers, in particular, help learners see and understand time passing, developing a conceptual understanding of time and improving their ability to maintain attention. Timers can be used to limit turn times during games, allocate time for group projects or writing assignments, or manage classroom transitions. Classroom timers with a visual representation of the time passed and time remaining also helps children to develop a sense of time and employ important time management skills. Classroom timers have nearly endless uses: use one minute timers to limit turn times during an educational game, give students a specific amount of time to complete a group project, provide a specific time for a writing assignment, give the class a time to ask the teacher questions, and much more.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Timers (physical or digital), a designated activity or task, and a structured environment to use the timer effectively.
#### Constraints
- Some learners may become anxious or distracted by timers if not used appropriately. The timer should be relevant and meaningful to the task. Some timers may be disruptive if alarms are too loud or frequent.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the learner's ability to stay on task and complete activities within the allotted time. Track the learner's use of time management skills. Monitor the reduction of distractions or anxiety related to time constraints.

## Impact
- Visual and auditory timers can significantly improve attention and task completion, especially for learners who benefit from structured environments and clear time management cues. They support the development of time management skills and can reduce anxiety related to deadlines.
