Use writing-based pre-reading tasks as a cost-effective classroom strategy for teaching reading comprehension¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends prw tasks as a teaching strategy for reading comprehension because, in the author's words, "it is cost-effective and does not require any special training or expensive technology" and "can also be easily adopted in any classroom setting". The author also observed increased student interest, with students able to imagine passage content beforehand and then check whether the passage matched the ideas they generated in writing, making them active participants in discovering meaning.

Requirements¶

  • Students write about the topic before reading the passage

Constraints¶

  • Generalizability is limited because the study was located in a specific setting with a specific group of students

Target Learners¶

  • ESL learners, especially those with low proficiency in English

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading comprehension and autonomous reading in a second language

Claims¶

  • A one-way ANOVA on the same data verified the t-test finding that the experimental group outperformed the control group on total marks [+W]
  • The study rejects the null hypothesis that writing-based pre-reading tasks have no impact on sub-skills of inferential reading comprehension [+W]
  • The prw group scored higher on six of seven inferential-comprehension sub-skills, but only the sequence sub-skill difference was statistically significant [+W]
