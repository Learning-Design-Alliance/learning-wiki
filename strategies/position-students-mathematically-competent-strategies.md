# Position students as mathematically competent and build collaborative peer relationships to foster mathematics belonging

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report recommends teacher practices that foster social and mathematics belonging: positioning students as mathematically competent by highlighting the value of contributions and inviting students to justify thinking; supporting collaborative peer relationships through "well-structured collaborative work in heterogenous ability groups"; and examining teacher beliefs about mathematical ability while cultivating inclusive, culturally responsive practices. The goal is not false competence but drawing attention to existing competence to encourage further competency-building learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must examine their own beliefs about who typically excels at math, since these beliefs shape practices and signal belonging to students
#### Constraints
- Positioning should draw on students' areas of existing competence rather than create a sense of false competence

### Target Learners
- K–12 mathematics students, particularly those from negatively stereotyped racial and ethnic groups and female students

### Target Learning Goals
- Mathematics belonging and identity
- Classroom engagement
- Math achievement
