# Integrate mixed-reality visualization with physical interaction rather than relying on a single modality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's first design guideline recommends combining tangibility and mixed reality in engineering learning tools, because participants valued the combination and reported improved conceptual understanding and hands-on exploration. The authors warn that "relying on a single modality could reduce engagement over time due to diminished novelty, underscoring the need for integrated multimodal designs." The guideline translates empirical findings into recommendations for mixed-reality and tangible learning tools in engineering education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tools must support both visualization and hands-on interaction to sustain engagement
#### Constraints
- Based on a small-sample study of one mechanics topic; engagement may still decline as novelty wears off

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students learning foundational mechanics

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding and sustained engagement with beam bending concepts
