Use one or two suitable metacognitive strategies rather than many in primary reading lessons¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

In its conclusion the article advises that primary students do not need many strategies: it cites Keene (2008) that "By using one or two suitable metacognitive strategies to read a text can give positive impact on the students’ comprehension performance". The study's own strategies were basic forms — guessing, question-building, highlighting difficult words, dictionary reference and context-based meaning connection — yet proved suitable for primary school students. The strategy is to select a small number of appropriate strategies matched to the text.
