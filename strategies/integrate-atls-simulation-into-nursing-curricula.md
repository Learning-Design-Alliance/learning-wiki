# Integrate ATLS-based simulation programs into nursing curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that "ATLS-based simulation programs can significantly improve nursing students’ readiness for real-life trauma situations and should be integrated into nursing curricula". The strategy uses a standardized, internationally recognized ATLS protocol delivered as simulation-based, immersive training (lectures, videos, scenario simulations, skill stations) in a safe environment. In resource-constrained settings, the article argues, such programs provide standardized, safe, and resource-efficient trauma training opportunities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Use of the validated international ATLS protocol from the American College of Surgeons
- Structured, immersive training formats with repetition
- Trained instructors and simulation facilities (skill stations, moulages)
#### Constraints
- The study's evidence comes from a single institution with a relatively small sample and lacks long-term follow-up and direct comparison with other teaching methods, per the article's own limitations

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- Readiness for real-life trauma situations
- Trauma clinical decision-making
