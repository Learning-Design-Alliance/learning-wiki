# Use AI to monitor and support peer collaborations rather than replace them, combining modalities by affordance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that AI in education detect and nurture quality interactions rather than substitute for peers. Because discourse markers of interaction quality appear early, "AI could monitor collaborations and provide support when struggling patterns emerge (extreme turn imbalance, low questioning rates)". Future systems might leverage peer interaction for curiosity generation and AI for knowledge consolidation when peer dynamics falter.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Real-time discourse analytics capable of detecting early markers such as question-asking rates and turn imbalance
#### Constraints
- Translating these findings into practice will require close partnership with educators; sessions in the study were single encounters of approximately one hour, so longer-term patterns are untested

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students
- university learners using AI tutoring systems

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining curiosity and engagement in collaborative learning
- knowledge consolidation
