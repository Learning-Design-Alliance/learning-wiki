Use structured input activities in the ESL classroom to shift grammar teaching from output-based drills to input processing¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (4 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends exploiting Processing Instruction's structured input activities in the ESL classroom, since the beneficial effects of PI are attributed to "the structured input activities" as a key component. Teachers present the target form and meaning, warn learners against non-optimal strategies such as relying on time adverbials, then have learners work through referential and affective structured input activities that require attending to the verb form for meaning before any production is required.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Activities must be developed and validated following the guidelines in Lee and VanPatten (1995) and VanPatten (1996, 2002)

Constraints¶

  • Production of the targeted form is deferred; the approach is an adjunct to communicative language teaching rather than a replacement for the whole language program

Target Learners¶

  • Filipino ESL learners, including tertiary-level students with persistent grammar errors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • L2 grammar acquisition through form-meaning connections in processed input

Affordances¶

  • Input Processing Theory Processing Instruction

Claims¶

  • Replication studies attribute PI's effects mainly to structured input activities rather than to explicit information or explicit feedback [+W]
  • The review reports PI's superiority over TI is generalizable across structures and languages, including Spanish, Italian, French, and English [+W]
  • Both PI and TI produced significant gains in post and delayed production of the English present perfect, but only the PI group gained significantly on delayed interpretation [+W]
  • Prior studies reviewed in this article report PI superior to mechanical output practice for acquisition, especially on interpretation tasks [+W]
  • Explicit information mediated PI's superiority over PBI on interpretation: PI-EI and PBI-EI performed equally, so the PI+EI advantage was attributed to EI, not structured input alone [-M]
  • Processing instruction produces significant gains in both sentence-level comprehension and production [+W]
