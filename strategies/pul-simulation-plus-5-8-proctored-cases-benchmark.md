# Use a benchmark of roughly 5 to 8 supervised PUL cases, preceded by simulation training, to structure supervision and credentialing for naïve urologists

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that its learning-curve findings guide training structure for urologists adopting PUL, stating "Our findings could help inform supervision and potentially credential programs for naïve urologists aiming to adopt a novel technique." In the study, all PUL-naïve urologists "completed a PUL simulation training program, and were supervised by a PUL-experienced urologist during their initial PUL cases," and proficiency was reached after 5 to 8 cases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Naïve urologists complete a PUL simulation training program before live cases
- Supervision by a PUL-experienced urologist during initial cases
#### Constraints
- Supervision in the early cases was not standardized, which the authors note may introduce observer bias
- Small single-center sample limits detection of rare complications or subtle intergroup differences
- Operative time as sole learning-curve surrogate may not capture implant positioning accuracy, patient satisfaction, or long-term outcomes

### Target Learners
- PUL-naïve urologists experienced in endourologic procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- Achieving procedural proficiency in prostatic urethral lift
