# Use biophysics exam problems linking electricity concepts to cell physiology as a learning-by-synthesis capstone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article presents a collection of examination problems that connect basic concepts in electricity to cellular phenomena, arranged to follow a course syllabus progressing from electrostatics to the Hodgkin-Huxley model. The structure and complexity of these problems are configured to make the final examination a "learning by synthesis" according to Bloom's taxonomy, with similar problems assigned as open-book homework during the semester.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should have already studied both introductory physics and calculus for one year before attempting these problems.
#### Constraints
- The models are based on ideal conditions and do not contain details of real voltage/current clamp experiments such as Ag/AgCl-electrode potentials, liquid-junction potentials, or series resistance compensation.

### Target Learners
- second-year premedical and biomedical sciences undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of electrical processes in cells
- application of electric circuit concepts to cellular phenomena
