# Use cognitive interviewing with think-aloud and verbal probing to evaluate established questionnaires in new linguistic and educational contexts, revising conservatively

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates cognitive interviewing as "an evidence-based questionnaire evaluation method to identify potential sources of misunderstanding and response error prior to broader administration of the instrument". Participants think aloud while answering and respond to verbal probes, revealing how items are interpreted; findings then inform item-level decisions. The authors emphasize avoiding overcorrection: where the intended scope of an item could not be established confidently, the original wording was retained rather than imposing a new interpretation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interviewers experienced in cognitive interviewing who did not participate in the learners' teaching or assessment; independent review of transcripts and team consensus on item-level decisions
#### Constraints
- Proposed revisions require cognitive retesting before being considered confirmed; the method identifies and characterizes item-level problems rather than estimating their prevalence

### Target Learners
- learners completing questionnaires in a language other than their first language
- medical students in English-medium programs

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate measurement of learner experience with immersive learning technologies
