# Explicitly expose students to the confusions about the moving-charge field through guided discussion questions, including asking them to draw field lines of an instantaneously stopped charge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose a discussion-based teaching approach in which confusions are introduced deliberately so as to "arouse the students' interest in what may otherwise be seen as a dry subject" and then resolved insightfully. A central diagnostic activity asks students to draw the field lines of a point charge instantaneously stopped from uniform motion; the common intuitive answer (field lines centered on the resting charge everywhere) is wrong because outside the transition sphere the field must keep evolving as if the charge were still moving. The authors report developing this discussion through active interaction with generations of students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must be aware of the confusions in advance, in order to address them in a timely manner before students' struggles get out of hand
#### Constraints
- The discussion is restricted to the electric field of a point charge in uniform motion, entirely excluding magnetic fields

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate physics students in General Physics and Classical Electrodynamics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- deep understanding of relativistic electric field transformations
- internalization of the signal retardation effect
- appreciation of the internal consistency of electrodynamics and special relativity

## Claims

- [After traditional instruction, only about 35% of students found the electric field direction at point A and only 14% at point B on a pre-test combining a line charge and charged cylinder](../claims/pretest-direction-line-cylinder-low-success.md) [+W]
- [Even after the tutorial, only 60% of tutorial students found the field direction at point A inside one of two adjacent charged cylinders, which the authors call extremely difficult](../claims/adjacent-cylinders-point-a-extremely-difficult.md) [+W]
