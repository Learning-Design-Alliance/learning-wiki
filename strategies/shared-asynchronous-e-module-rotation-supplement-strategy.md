# Deliver standardized e-learning modules asynchronously as a supplement to clinical rotations, shared across institutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating an asynchronously delivered e-module into clinical rotations as an effective, flexible, self-directed supplement to real-time clinical experiences, and sharing e-learning tools across institutions to deliver standardized education on core clinical topics. Trainees complete the module during the rotation without time constraints or due dates, with content not otherwise covered in the rotation's formal learning experiences. The authors state that "e-learning collaborations like the one employed for our project offer an innovative solution for delivering content to trainees at multiple institutions."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hosting platform for delivery and consent (Canvas was used)
- Content addressing knowledge gaps not covered elsewhere in the rotation
- Multi-institutional collaboration for content development and editing
#### Constraints
- Trainees were not required to complete the e-module early in the rotation
- Clinical rotation experiences likely influenced postmodule ratings and could not be separated from the e-module effect

### Target Learners
- medical students
- residents on clinical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- standardized education on core clinical topics less commonly addressed in medical training, such as perioperative medicine
