# Implement a student-run ADR management team by recruiting volunteer students within an existing student-run clinic, training them on the BAT-M diagnostic criteria, and routing reports through a national pharmacovigilance center

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete implementation recipe drawn from the article: assign pairs of volunteer medical students weekly within a learner-centered student-run clinic; have healthcare professionals contact the team by phone or email about suspected ADRs while the team also screens ward records; apply the four BAT-M diagnostic criteria (consistency with the known adverse effect profile, temporal relationship, exclusion of other causes, explainable pharmacological mechanism) with clinical pharmacologist review; report confirmed ADRs to the national center and feed results back to physicians and the patient record.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clinical pharmacologist to review each suspected ADR with students and attending physicians
- An existing student-run clinic or equivalent extracurricular structure to host the team
- Access to patients' electronic patient records and the national pharmacovigilance reporting system
#### Constraints
- Physician supervision time, though minimal (less than 5 minutes per patient), must be arranged; single-ward pilots are advised before scaling

### Target Learners
- medical students (1st–6th year, voluntary participants)

### Target Learning Goals
- ADR detection, diagnosis, and reporting competence; medication safety awareness
