# Two Stars and a Wish

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Two Stars and a Wish is a peer-feedback protocol in which each reviewer identifies two specific strengths of a peer's work and one suggestion for improvement, phrased as a wish ("I wish you would…"). The fixed structure lowers the social and cognitive barriers to giving feedback, ensures every review contains both affirmation and a next step, and is commonly used in writing workshops, design critiques, and primary classrooms.

## Design Implications

The strategy operationalizes formative feedback principles: effective feedback is specific, task-focused, and actionable rather than evaluative [Feedback that addresses the task and process improves learning more than praise directed at the person.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. The two-stars requirement forces reviewers to attend to what *worked* — building evaluative judgment — while the single wish keeps improvement guidance focused and prevents feedback overload. The fixed format also reduces the social threat of peer critique, which supports the trust conditions peer feedback depends on [Peer assessment can produce learning gains comparable to teacher assessment when criteria and training are provided.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A shared rubric or success criteria so "stars" and "wishes" reference the task, not taste
- Training in specific, descriptive language ("Your opening states a claim" vs. "It's good")
- Time for learners to act on the wish in a revision cycle, or the feedback is wasted
- A [check-in](../elements/check-in.md) or norm-setting step so critique feels safe

#### Constraints
- Without criteria, stars collapse into generic praise ("nice job") that carries no information [Feedback that is vague or person-directed produces little learning benefit.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [-M]
- The 2:1 ratio can train learners to expect praise and discount improvement suggestions; over time the wish should carry equal weight
- Novice reviewers may lack the expertise to identify genuine strengths or diagnose problems, producing confident but inaccurate feedback [Peer feedback quality depends on reviewer expertise; untrained peers often misdiagnose work.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [~M]
- If no revision time follows, the protocol becomes a ritual with no learning payoff

#### Implementation Variability
- **Two stars and a step:** replace "wish" with a concrete next action to increase actionability
- **Silent gallery walk:** written sticky-note feedback on displayed work, reducing social pressure
- **Teacher-modeled rounds:** instructor demonstrates the format on anonymous work before peers try it
- **Escalating ratio:** shift from 2:1 toward 1:1 or 1:2 as learners develop evaluative skill

### Target Learners
- Elementary and secondary learners new to giving and receiving critique — the structure scaffolds the social skill
- Peer reviewers building evaluative judgment; reviewing others' work against criteria improves their own subsequent performance [Peer assessment improves both reviewer and recipient learning when criteria are explicit.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M]
- Less suitable for advanced learners who can handle open critique formats without a fixed ratio

### Target Learning Goals
- Evaluative judgment: recognizing quality features in work against criteria
- Feedback literacy: giving and receiving specific, non-evaluative critique
- Revision behavior: translating feedback into concrete improvements

### Instructions
1. Share success criteria or a rubric and review what specific, descriptive feedback sounds like.
2. Pair learners or set up a gallery walk; each reviewer studies the work silently first.
3. Reviewer states two **stars** — specific features that meet the criteria, quoting or pointing to evidence.
4. Reviewer states one **wish** — a single, actionable improvement phrased as a suggestion, not a verdict.
5. Recipient paraphrases the feedback (no defending) and records it.
6. Build in a revision cycle so the wish is acted on; revisit revised work to close the loop ([assessment](../elements/assessment.md) for learning).

## Related Strategies
- Gallery critique — a whole-class variant using the same star/wish structure on displayed work
- Author's chair — oral sharing format where the protocol structures audience response
- Rubric co-construction — building the criteria that make stars and wishes specific

## Examples
- **Writing workshops (elementary):** after drafting personal narratives, students trade papers and complete a Two Stars and a Wish slip tied to the class's narrative checklist before revising.
- **Design critiques (secondary STEM):** teams present prototypes; audience members post sticky-note stars and wishes on a feedback board, which the team prioritizes for the next iteration.
- **[Peergrade](https://www.peergrade.io) / Peerstudio-style platforms:** structured peer-review prompts can be configured with the two-strengths-one-suggestion template, with rubric criteria attached to each field.
