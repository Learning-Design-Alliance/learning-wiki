# Use seed-and-feed tactics — seeding forums before members arrive, posting during lulls, and replying to unanswered posts — to launch small on-demand online communities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
A seed-and-feed strategy has staff create initial content before members arrive, start new topics during conversation lulls, and reply to member posts that would otherwise go unanswered, so that a small community appears active and responsive. In this case study, staff authored 44% (405/929) of all posts, seeded 12 posts before members arrived, started 75 of 133 total topics, and the effort was estimated at "just under 245 staff hours, or the equivalent of about 6 weeks full-time for 1 staff member."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Staff time available to seed forums, monitor for lulls, and reply to unanswered posts on most days
#### Constraints
- The article notes this was a single case study whose design choices are not guaranteed to be reproducible in other settings

### Target Learners
- adult participants in an Internet-mediated walking program

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained participation and engagement in an online community supporting behavior change

## Claims

- [Adding the online community was associated with improved retention in the walking program, with a 21% versus 34% dropout rate, while both arms increased walking with no difference between arms](../claims/community-arm-improved-retention-suh.md) [+W]
- [Concentrated recruitment and limiting the forums to a small number helped the community appear active, and the authors would in retrospect have used a single forum](../claims/concentrated-membership-few-forums-appear-active.md) [+W]
- [Contest threads generated high reply counts: 42, 26, 46, and 37 replies for the staff-favorite, first-time-poster, healthy-snacks, and walking-goal contests](../claims/contest-thread-reply-counts-suh.md) [+W]
- [Occasional contests with small prizes were the most effective single tactic at generating forum participation, with prize threads drawing the most replies](../claims/contests-small-prizes-most-effective-forum-participation.md) [+W]
- [Over the study, 45% of community members posted at least once, 20% were lurkers, and page views correlated strongly with posting (r = 0.65)](../claims/posting-lurking-and-viewing-rates-suh.md) [+W]
- [Staff questions directed back at the original poster had mixed success at eliciting further replies, while solicitations addressed to the whole community were somewhat more effective](../claims/staff-question-tactics-mixed-success.md) [~W]
