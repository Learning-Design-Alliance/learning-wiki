# Embed the shared competencies into undergraduate and postgraduate interprofessional education curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating the six shared competencies into interprofessional education (IPE) at undergraduate and postgraduate levels. IPE gives learners from different disciplines structured opportunities to develop communication, teamwork, and problem-solving skills together. As the article states, "Embedding the identified competencies into undergraduate and postgraduate curricula can foster early appreciation of each profession' s roles, strengthen interprofessional relationships, and reduce hierarchical barriers in clinical settings." Continuous IPE-based training in healthcare institutions is also recommended to enhance collaborative readiness and care coordination.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A defined shared-competency framework to embed; structured IPE opportunities for learners from different disciplines
#### Constraints
- The article notes fee-for-service payment models reduce incentives for collaboration and IPE, and hierarchical culture can cause communication breakdowns

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate and postgraduate health professions students
- Practicing healthcare professionals in continuing education

### Target Learning Goals
- Communication, teamwork, and problem-solving for collaborative primary care practice
- Role clarity and mutual role understanding across professions
