# Diagnose PBL gaps with a quality questionnaire, then revise problems with the 3C3R model via a multidisciplinary revision committee

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy is the article's three-phase intervention: gap identification via a Quality Assessment Questionnaire, targeted faculty and student training, and problem revision. A PBL revision committee adopted the 3C3R model so that "a total of 136 PBLs of different blocks from different academic years were revised according to the 3C3R model", correcting, changing, or re-objectiving problems across 12 blocks. The curriculum committee assessed and approved the revisions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A PBL revision committee comprising medical education experts, faculty representatives, and curriculum committee members
- Faculty development workshops and student orientation sessions before gap assessment
#### Constraints
- The authors state the interventions were successful in their context and their applicability to other settings should be evaluated individually

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical (MBBS) students

### Target Learning Goals
- Alignment of PBL problems with learning outcomes
- Clinical reasoning, teamwork, and self-directed learning

## Claims

- [Revising PBL problems and delivery with the 3C3R model significantly improved perceived PBL quality, with mean QAQ scores rising from 2.7 to 4.0 on a five-point scale](../claims/pbl-3c3r-revision-qaq-improvement.md) [+M]
- [Stakeholder feedback identified gaps in PBL spanning problem design (misalignment, simplicity, contextual relevance, inter-PBL connection) and delivery (training, assessment alignment)](../claims/pbl-gap-taxonomy-design-delivery.md) [+M]
