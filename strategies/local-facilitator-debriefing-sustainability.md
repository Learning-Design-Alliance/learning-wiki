# Use a locally trained facilitator to lead debriefings and sustain the curriculum, with debriefing time lengthened as learners gain experience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A local Nepali ED faculty member with prior simulation training led all debriefings and tracked the simulations, which the authors identify as key to sustainability and to accommodating local learners. Debriefing occurred in the same room after each case, covering teamwork, communication, and medical management. Initially equal time was given to case and debriefing; after one to two cases, debriefing time was lengthened as learners became more confident.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Availability of a local provider who has simulation and debriefing training
#### Constraints
- The authors note availability of a locally trained simulation provider is a challenge to generalizability

### Target Learners
- Medical officers
- Medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Reflecting on teamwork and communication
- Reviewing medical management decisions
