# Students search out their own wave-phenomenon examples on Google Earth and compare them with textbook images

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The concluding section recommends that "Students can search out their own examples of wave phenomena on Google Earth", which the author calls very instructive and entertaining. Students, together with their teachers, compare their own pictures with traditional images in textbooks. Teachers could suggest looking for waves near big towns on the sea coast, big harbors, marina resorts and big rivers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to Google Earth imagery, which the article notes is free if not used in commercial products; an option with a clock icon allows seeing old pictures
#### Constraints
- Google Earth Company periodically updates its maps, so images change over time

### Target Learners
- pupils in elementary physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- recognising reflection, refraction, diffraction and interference in real satellite imagery
