# Formative Self Assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Formative self assessment asks learners to judge the quality of their own work against explicit criteria — rubrics, checklists, exemplars, or success criteria — while the work is still in progress and revisable. The purpose is not grading but calibration: learners compare their current performance to a target, identify gaps, and act on them. It is carried out through structured activities such as rubric-guided drafts, self-checklists, confidence ratings, or comparing one's work to annotated exemplars.

## Design Implications

Self assessment works when it functions as an act of [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — evidence gathered by the learner to inform their next step, not a verdict [Formative assessment practices improve achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Accuracy is the central design problem: unguided self-ratings are often inflated and weakly correlated with actual performance, so self assessment must be anchored to external referents such as rubrics and exemplars [~M]. Training and practice improve self-assessment accuracy over time, and accuracy in turn supports self-regulated learning [Self-regulated learning theory](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit, learner-accessible success criteria (rubrics, checklists) developed or unpacked before the task
- Exemplars at multiple quality levels so learners can calibrate their judgments ([Comparing Cases](../elements/comparing-cases.md))
- Time and structure for revision — self assessment must be followed by an opportunity to act on the judgment ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
- Low-stakes framing so honest self-evaluation is not penalized

#### Constraints
- Unanchored self-ratings ("How do you think you did?") produce inflated, uninformative judgments [~M]
- Novices lack the knowledge to discriminate quality; without exemplars or criteria their self-assessments are unreliable [~M]
- If self-assigned grades count toward marks, learners game the ratings and the formative function collapses [-M]
- Self-assessment consumes time that may exceed its benefit for simple, well-specified tasks where external checks are cheaper

#### Implementation Variability
- **Rubric-guided self-scoring**: learners score a draft against a rubric and write one revision goal
- **Exemplar comparison**: learners rank their work against annotated samples before revising
- **Confidence-weighted judgments**: learners rate confidence per item, not just correctness, surfacing illusions of knowing
- **Self- and peer-assessment pairs**: peer feedback first provides an external anchor that improves subsequent self-judgments

### Target Learners
- Intermediate learners who have enough domain knowledge to perceive quality differences; pure novices need exemplars and modeling first [~M]
- Learners developing self-regulation — self assessment is a core monitoring phase of the learning cycle [Self-regulated learning theory](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) [+M]
- Less effective for learners who are highly anxious or grade-focused unless stakes are explicitly removed

### Target Learning Goals
- Metacognitive monitoring: accurately judging one's own understanding and performance
- Quality criteria internalization: learning what "good" looks like in a domain
- Revision and iteration skills: closing the gap between current and target performance

### Instructions
1. Unpack the success criteria with learners before the task; co-constructing the rubric improves ownership and comprehension ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
2. Study annotated exemplars at several quality levels so learners can recognize the criteria in real work ([Comparing Cases](../elements/comparing-cases.md))
3. Have learners produce a draft, then score it against the rubric, citing specific evidence for each judgment
4. Require one concrete revision action per identified gap, then a second self-check after revision ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
5. Follow with [Action-Oriented Feedback](../strategies/action-oriented_feedback.md) from the instructor or peers to correct persistent miscalibration

## Examples
- **Portfolio assessment in writing workshops** (e.g., [Reading & Writing Project approaches](https://readingandwritingproject.org)): students score drafts against rubrics and revise before teacher review.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** mastery practice: learners self-select when to retry skills, with confidence and correctness data surfaced to them.
- **Exam wrappers** (widely used in postsecondary STEM, e.g., [Carnegie Mellon Eberly Center](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching)): students complete a structured self-analysis of their exam preparation and errors, then set goals for the next assessment cycle.
