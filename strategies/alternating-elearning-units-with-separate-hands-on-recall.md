# Alternate short e-Learning units with separate hands-on exercises that require active recall of the content away from the screen

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementable session recipe for blended medical device training: divide the e-Learning content into short units and follow each with a hands-on exercise conducted apart from the screen. The article "implemented an elaborative learning procedure by dividing the learning phase into short e-Learning units followed by an associated hands-on exercise", with the pump placed behind the learner so content "had to be actively retrieve[d] from memory during the hands-on part". Learners who forget a step may return to the e-Learning program to look it up.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Timing of units and exercises (pilot-derived mean times with sound cues), a separate pump location that prevents simultaneous viewing, and learner compliance with the procedure
#### Constraints
- The skills benefit was significant immediately after learning but not at the two-week follow-up
- Tested with a single, rather elaborate e-Learning program, so transfer to other programs is questioned by the authors

### Target Learners
- nursing students
- undergraduate students in medical device training

### Target Learning Goals
- procedural skills for operating an infusion (syringe) pump
