# Apply memory-weakening reactivation with sleep disruption soon after a traumatic experience, before a long delay intervenes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose TMR-SD as a candidate intervention for weakening unwanted memories such as in post-traumatic stress disorder, exploiting the finding that recently formed memories are vulnerable whereas older memories resist. They write that "TMR-SD soon after an emotional or traumatic experience may weaken those memories and could serve as a potential treatment for PTSD, though only if a long delay is not interposed between trauma and TMR-SD." The strategy requires presenting memory-linked cues during sleep at intensities that provoke brief arousals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Auditory cues previously associated with the target memory
- Sleep (nap or overnight) during which cues are delivered at arousal-provoking intensity
- Timing soon after encoding, before consolidation confers resilience
#### Constraints
- The article's evidence is for week-old spatial memories in afternoon naps, not for trauma memories; the PTSD application is the authors' postulate, not a tested result

### Target Learners
- adults with unwanted or traumatic memories

### Target Learning Goals
- weakening or degrading targeted declarative memories
