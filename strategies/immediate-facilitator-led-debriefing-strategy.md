# Use immediate, facilitator-led structured debriefing after simulated neonatal resuscitation scenarios; video assistance is optional, not required

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends debriefing performed immediately after each simulation and assisted by a facilitator, since in this context "the strategy structured by video is not superior to the oral structured strategy". Teams should be formed as in the real clinical context, with each professional executing the actions of their profession, and debriefing conducted by an instructor with expertise in the relevant resuscitation program and learning objectives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator/instructor with expertise in the learning objectives and the resuscitation program conducts the debriefing
#### Constraints
- The study lacked a no-debriefing control group; some variability in debriefing delivery existed between the two instructors

### Target Learners
- Experienced clinical staff in neonatal critical care teams

### Target Learning Goals
- Neonatal resuscitation technical/cognitive and behavioral team skills
