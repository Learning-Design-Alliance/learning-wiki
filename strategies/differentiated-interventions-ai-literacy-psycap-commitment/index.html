Differentiated interventions integrating AI literacy training, psychological capital cultivation, and professional identity reinforcement¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that universities design interventions addressing technological competencies and psychological resources together rather than AI skills alone. Its findings "offer practical guidance for designing educational interventions that synergistically integrate AI training, psychological capital cultivation, and professional identity reinforcement in the era of AI-enhanced education", with professional commitment treated as a malleable target that amplifies the psychological-capital-to-engagement pathway.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interventions should target professional commitment alongside AI skills; the article reports that raising commitment from a low to a high level "can nearly double the efficiency of psychological capital in promoting learning engagement".

Constraints¶

  • The article itself notes the moderating effect "fell within the small-to-medium range" (f2 = 0.018); the evidence is cross-sectional and drawn from Chinese undergraduates.

Target Learners¶

  • university students with varying levels of professional commitment

Target Learning Goals¶

  • learning engagement
  • AI literacy
  • professional commitment

Affordances¶
