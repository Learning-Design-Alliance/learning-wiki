Align taught discussion strategies with learning tasks and provide timely individual feedback on strategy and label use¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the study's findings, the article recommends that instructors "make sure that th e strategies chosen are aligned with the learning tasks" and pair strategies with appropriate discussion tasks. It also recommends providing "timely feedback to individual students regarding how well they use the strategies and labels," since whole-class feedback left some students without full grasp of the strategies.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructors select strategies appropriate to the discussion's purpose (e.g., counterclaims and rebuttals for argumentation)
  • A mechanism for monitoring individual students' strategy and label use

Constraints¶

  • In the studied implementation, feedback was general and whole-class only, and some students still did not fully grasp the strategies

Target Learners¶

  • online course students at the master's level

Target Learning Goals¶

  • effective use of discussion strategies in asynchronous online discussion

Claims¶

  • The strategy-instruction-and-labels intervention increased discussion posts in Phases I and II of the interaction analysis model but produced no significant increase in Phases III to V [~W]
  • Students in the experimental group perceived that strategy instruction and labels positively impacted their online discussion and learning [+W]
  • Students adopted the post type labels extensively but did not always label accurately, with building posts correctly labeled only 64% of the time [+W]
  • Students taught discussion strategies with post type labels used more elaborating-and-clarifying, making-connections, challenging, and questioning strategies than control students, with no significant difference in building [+W]
  • Student comments identified organizing thoughts, establishing discussion expectations, and difficulty differentiating labels or challenging peers as major themes of the approach [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Shift from matching teaching to learning styles toward teaching students adaptable, effective learning strategies aligned to task complexity and goals
