# Use brief social-psychological classroom interventions (e.g., belonging and mindset interventions) to raise women's belonging, self-efficacy, confidence and interest in physics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 5 studies (2 review, 1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends classroom interventions as one way to improve the learning environment: "Brief social-psychological classroom interventions, e.g., mindset and sense of belonging interventions, have been shown to increase women's sense of belonging and self-efficacy in the classroom, as well as boost their confidence and interest in physics". It reports the authors implemented a 25 minute belonging intervention in calculus-based physics classes that eliminated the gender gap in physics performance, and suggests it could be adapted for algebra-based courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The intervention must be implemented within the physics class and address students' sense of belonging and feelings of being recognized by peers, TAs and instructors.
#### Constraints
- The 25 minute belonging intervention eliminating the gender gap was implemented in calculus-based physics classes; the article states it could be adapted for algebra-based courses but reports no direct test in that setting.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory physics courses, particularly women

### Target Learning Goals
- sense of belonging
- physics self-efficacy
- confidence and interest in physics
