# Family-involvement strategy: pair child experiential nutrition activities with caregiver participation and home recording tasks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The protocol targets primary caregivers alongside children to create a supportive home food environment: children shop for healthy snacks with parents, cook collaboratively at home, and jointly log meals and sugar intake. The article states "By targeting primary caregivers alongside children, this intervention aims to create a supportive home food environment and enhance the sustainability of behavior change." Family recording tasks include the "Energy Recharge Station" weekly food-energy log and the "Sugar-Free Day," with class follow-up discussions after each activity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Parents must participate in the home-based activities; the article notes activities "were designed to be feasible within typical family routines with minimal time requirements" and supported by WeChat parent-group communication.
#### Constraints
- Family participation dependence is a stated fidelity risk; the relationship between family engagement intensity and outcomes will be quantified in later analyses.

### Target Learners
- Primary school children and their parents/primary caregivers

### Target Learning Goals
- Healthy eating behaviors, awareness of caloric and added-sugar intake, family nutrition interaction
