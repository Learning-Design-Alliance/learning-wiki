# Use vocational training as a complementary, not decisive, student-selection criterion, e.g. via a quota for the professionally experienced

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 2 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating vocational training in health-care professions into medical student selection because waiting-list students with such experience excelled in practical OSCE stations and the policy widens access. The authors "emphasize that vocational training should be utilized as one complementary, but not as the decisive selection criterion", and argue that "including criteria such as vocational training facilitates access to university for groups that are under-represented in 'classic' selection procedures". Since 2020 German schools use a 10% professionally-experienced quota ranked by study-ability test.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Applicants attest experience in selected health-care professions; cognitive ability is still assessed (e.g. via study-ability test or Abitur) because cognitive abilities remain important for study success.
#### Constraints
- The article stresses the criterion should be complementary, not decisive; career choices and job performances of graduates were not observable, and results come from one medical school.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical school applicants, including vocationally trained health-care professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- Fair student selection; recognition of practical clinical and communicative competence
