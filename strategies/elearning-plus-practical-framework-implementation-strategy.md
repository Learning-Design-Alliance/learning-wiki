# Pair brief e-learning with practical frameworks, downloadable materials and behaviour change techniques to support implementation into practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that e-learning interventions aiming to change clinical communication behaviour should not deliver knowledge alone: "Including a practical framework to facilitate and structure sexual wellbeing conversations, and including behaviour change techniques such as reminders and prompts, may have contributed to the changes in important attitudes and beliefs". They advise that such tools "should include tangible support in the form of downloadable materials to use in practice", and note that learning may need to be repeated and applied in everyday practice, with simulated practice and rehearsal, to produce measurable behavioural change.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Practical framework to structure conversations, downloadable practice materials, and behaviour change techniques such as reminders and prompts
#### Constraints
- Goal setting techniques by themselves may not be sufficient to change practice; longer-term effectiveness and impact on clinical behaviour remain untested

### Target Learners
- healthcare professionals in cancer care

### Target Learning Goals
- routine delivery of sexual wellbeing support and sustained changes in clinical communication behaviour
