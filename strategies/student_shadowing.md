# Student Shadowing

## Description
An adult (educator, administrator, or school leader) follows a student throughout their school day to observe their experiences, interactions, and environment firsthand. The goal is to gain a deeper understanding of the student's perspective, identify challenges, and inform strategies to increase engagement, motivation, and learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A willing student and their parent/guardian, a shadowing protocol or template, a scheduled day for observation, and a commitment to objective note-taking and reflection. Access to digital platforms for recording observations can enhance efficiency.
#### Constraints
- Requires significant time commitment, relies on the observer's ability to remain objective and avoid influencing the student's behavior, and may not capture the full complexity of a student's experience if conducted only once.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Educators, administrators, school leaders, and product designers seeking to understand student experiences and improve learning environments, particularly for students of color, English learners, or those who may be overlooked.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Understand students’ lived experiences, identify areas for improvement in teaching practices and school environment, gather qualitative data to inform equity initiatives, increase educator empathy and awareness of student needs.

### Affordances
- Provides real-time qualitative data, uncovers hidden challenges and disparities, promotes empathy and perspective-taking, informs targeted interventions and support, and can reveal biases or unintended consequences of existing practices.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted by focusing on specific student populations or learning goals. Protocols and templates can be modified to address particular areas of concern, such as academic needs, social interactions, or access to resources.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observations of student engagement, interactions, and access to resources; educator reflections on insights gained; changes in teaching practices or school policies; student feedback on implemented changes.

## Impact
- Studies show that student shadowing can uncover disparities in access to learning opportunities, promote more equitable practices, and improve student outcomes. It also enhances educator awareness of student needs and challenges, fostering a more supportive learning environment.
