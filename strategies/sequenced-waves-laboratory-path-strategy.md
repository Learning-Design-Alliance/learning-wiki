# Sequenced extracurricular laboratory path: Shive machine, sound with oscilloscope, acoustic interference, then resonance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes a laboratory sequence in which students first explore wave behaviour qualitatively with the Shive wave machine, guess what can happen and then test their hypotheses, with quantitative measures obtained from camcorder-captured images. Sound waves are then studied with a microphone and oscilloscope, further verifying superposition and studying beats and Moiré fringes; acoustic interference is studied as an experiment complementary to laser-light interference that allows measuring the speed of sound; and resonance is introduced with a ceramic magnet hanging from a string over an electromagnet driven by a variable-frequency square wave. Meetings last 3 hours almost every month and are planned to continue for the last 3 years of high school.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Joint planning by teachers and university, with the student as the main character of learning, per the PLS guidelines
#### Constraints
- The path is optional and extracurricular, realized with few, interested and talented pupils

### Target Learners
- Selected high-school students (third class of Liceo Scientifico) in deepening laboratories

### Target Learning Goals
- Characterization of oscillatory motion, wave energy and energy transport, superposition, interference, and resonance
