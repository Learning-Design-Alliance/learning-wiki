# Design assessments from the start around the intended inferences, observations, and reasoning chain

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report recommends that assessment designers adopt a construct-centered, evidentiary approach from the outset rather than building tasks first and scoring them later. It states: 'One must design a complex assessment from the very start around the inferences one wants to make, the observations one needs to ground them, the situations that will evoke those observations, and the chain of reasoning that connects them.' This follows Messick's construct-centered approach, in which the nature of the construct guides task selection and the development of construct-based scoring criteria and rubrics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Designers must coordinate the work of statisticians, task authors, delivery-process developers, and interface designers through a common design architecture
#### Constraints
- More complex statistical models may be required, but they evolve from the substance of the assessment problem, jointly with purposes and task design

### Target Learners
- any examinee population for which a new assessment is being designed

### Target Learning Goals
- valid inferences about knowledge, skills, or other attributes tied to instructional objectives
