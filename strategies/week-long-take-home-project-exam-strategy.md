# Replace traditional time-limited graduate exams with week-long project-based take-home questions that allow professional tools and choice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy is to set realistic, professionally framed physics questions requiring about two student-days, give students five or six days including a weekend, and allow use of "the research literature, libraries, and colleagues". Topics are covered beforehand in lectures; students may approach questions theoretically or computationally, and may choose among several questions. The author trialled this in place of traditional examinations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Topics must have been covered beforehand in lectures.
- Questions should be designed to require roughly two student-days of work.
#### Constraints
- Effort demanded of students is significant; clashes with other demands on student time should be watched for.
- Instant-answer risks: for a shared first exam question, most elements could be covered in under five minutes by an expert.

### Target Learners
- honours/masters level physics coursework students

### Target Learning Goals
- professional practice skills
- application of core course content
- use of research literature
