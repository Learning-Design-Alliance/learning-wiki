# Integrate sharp object injury management modules into the preclinical curriculum and implement a simplified mandatory reporting system with increased faculty oversight

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's discussion sets out three recommendations for reducing sharp object injuries among dental students. First, "it is crucial to increase faculty oversight during high-risk procedures", because faculty shortages can leave first-time practical experiences insufficiently supervised. Second, "implementing a mandatory reporting system that simplifies the reporting mechanisms can help reduce stigma and ensure that appropriate protocols, such as the PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis) protocols, are activated promptly". Third, integrating SOI management modules into the preclinical curriculum is described as "essential for better preparation and awareness among students".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty capacity for oversight during high-risk procedures, and a simplified institutional reporting mechanism that activates PEP protocols promptly
#### Constraints
- The article frames these as suggestions to mitigate risks; their effectiveness was not tested in the study, which lacked a control group and long-term follow-up

### Target Learners
- Dental students in clinical training years

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe management of sharp object injuries, post-exposure prophylaxis activation, and injury reporting compliance
