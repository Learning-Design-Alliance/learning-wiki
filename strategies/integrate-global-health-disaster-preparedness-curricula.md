# Integrate student-centred global health and disaster preparedness courses into undergraduate medical curricula at both pre-clinical and clinical stages

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that medical institutions systematically embed pandemic-preparedness education in undergraduate curricula. It states it is "imperative for medical institutions to integrate student-centred and systematic courses to the undergraduate curricula – both in pre-clinical and clinical stages, to ensure that prospective health professionals are adequately prepared for future public health emergencies". Courses should develop multidisciplinary preparedness, health information management, emergency decision making, crisis leadership, and basic competencies such as life support, vaccination and triage.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recognition of global health emergency-related competencies in national competency standards and other learning resources
#### Constraints
- In Indonesia's highly diverse medical schools, integration must maintain the existing curriculum process to achieve relevant competencies

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in pre-clinical and clinical stages

### Target Learning Goals
- Global health emergency preparedness
- Disaster medicine competencies
- Emergency decision making and crisis leadership
