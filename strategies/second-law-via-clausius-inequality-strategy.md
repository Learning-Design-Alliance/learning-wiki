# Derive the Clausius and Kelvin-Planck statements and Carnot's theorem from the Clausius inequality for a unified treatment of the second law

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Instead of starting from the two historical statements of the second law, whose standard equivalence proof looks rather involved and not intuitive, the proposal starts from the observation that the second law can always be expressed in terms of the Clausius inequality. Both the Clausius and Kelvin-Planck statements, as well as Carnot's theorem, are derived from it, providing a highly unified picture of the second principle of thermodynamics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be able to work with the Clausius inequality as the general mathematical formulation of the second law.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the historical Clausius and Kelvin statements are strongly linked to the historical context of heat engines, which transmits the idea that the second principle mainly concerns the efficiency of heat machines.

### Target Learners
- high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the second law of thermodynamics and its generality
