# Six practical solutions for implementing interprofessional in situ simulation on a working ward

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article documents six challenges to running in situ interprofessional simulations and the solutions it used (Table 3): scheduling around rounds and breaks with an interprofessional planning team; treating the simulation as the protected daily teaching activity with nursing backup; a 30-minute total session covering prebrief, simulation and debrief; using the ward procedure room; low-fidelity mannequins with unit equipment and an 'iSimulate' monitor app; and structured GAS debriefing with focused objectives to manage large groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Leadership support from nursing, pharmacy and medicine to protect and schedule the activity
- An interprofessional planning team familiar with each profession's schedules
- Unused ward space such as a procedure room to preserve context fidelity
#### Constraints
- Previous studies report 28%–67% simulation cancellation rates and 20%–38% participation rates without such supports
- Large groups in short sessions limit depth of reflection; the authors suggest smaller groups for deeper reflection in future sessions

### Target Learners
- interprofessional clinical teams on inpatient wards

### Target Learning Goals
- sustain regular simulation participation despite clinical schedules
- maintain engagement and feasibility in short interprofessional sessions
