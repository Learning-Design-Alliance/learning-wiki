# Implementing school-wide SEL curricula such as Second Step to improve school culture, climate, engagement, and achievement in high-need schools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (3 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 5 studies (4 quant-synthesis, 1 causal), `q3`–`q4` · 3 of 5 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that "implementing programs such as Second Step in schools appears to be a worthwhile investment to improve school culture and climate to support school engagement, learning, and achievement especially for high-need or high-poverty schools". The strategy involves school-wide adoption of a comprehensive SEL curriculum over multiple consecutive semesters, supported by teacher training and systematic behavioral observation systems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School-wide implementation across consecutive semesters
- Teacher training and buy-in
- Systematic behavioral measurement systems
#### Constraints
- Effects on prosocial behaviors may not be sustained without booster or supplemental curriculum
- Schools self-selecting into implementation may differ systematically from non-implementing schools

### Target Learners
- students in high-need or high-poverty schools
- students in grades 5-8

### Target Learning Goals
- school engagement
- academic achievement
- positive school climate
- reduction of problem behaviors
