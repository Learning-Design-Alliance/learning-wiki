# Explicit or Direct Instruction

## Description
Explicit instruction is a structured teaching approach where instructors make goals, objectives, expectations, and instructional content explicit. They clearly communicate the purpose of each learning activity and explain how it relates to broader learning goals. Instructors address all aspects of the learning task, including how to think about it, how and when to perform the task or use the information, and how to evaluate the task. This involves explaining and modeling the skill or concept, guiding learners as they practice, and providing opportunities for application to ensure they can transfer their learning to other contexts and situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires instructors to plan lessons carefully, articulate learning goals clearly, and provide detailed explanations and models. Requires allocation of time for guided practice and application.
#### Constraints
- May be perceived as overly structured or rigid by some learners. Requires significant planning and preparation time for instructors.

### Target Learners

- Adult learners in basic skills programs, individuals with reading disabilities

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Ensure learners understand the goals and objectives of each task; facilitate transfer of learning to new contexts; model effective problem-solving

### Affordances
- Provides clarity and structure, reduces ambiguity, and ensures learners understand the purpose and application of skills and concepts. Clearly demonstrates expectations and models effective strategies.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted to individual learner needs by adjusting the level of explicitness and providing varied examples and practice opportunities. Scaffolding can be tailored to support learners with different levels of prior knowledge.
