# Separate learning-outcome and faculty-agreement questions in future evaluation of AI oral assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that prospective evaluation of the system split two distinct validation questions rather than pooling them. It states "A future evaluation should separate two questions. First, does access to CALLIOPE formative practice improve a common, unaided learning outcome relative to an appropriate comparator?" and, second, how AI scores agree with blinded faculty judgements on the same responses. It further advises fixing topic choice, baseline measures, assessment forms, treatment allocation and analysis rules prospectively, using a justified power or precision analysis for a planned sample near 50, and having independent raters record first-pass judgements before reconciliation with agreement analysis that distinguishes absolute agreement from consistency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A justified power or precision analysis, a restrained estimand, specified rubric for independent raters, and an intraclass correlation model matching the design
#### Constraints
- Oral-project assessment and individual topic learning should not be pooled without a defensible design; correlation alone is insufficient to establish interchangeability

### Target Learners
- students in planned participant studies of AI-supported oral formative practice

### Target Learning Goals
- unaided learning outcomes and agreement between AI scores and blinded faculty judgement
