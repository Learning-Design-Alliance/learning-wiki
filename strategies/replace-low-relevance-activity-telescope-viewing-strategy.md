# Replace low-relevance take-home artifacts with telescope viewing in astronomy museum programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
After evaluation, the developers determined that the signal stick activity's low astronomical significance did not reflect an ideal approach to teaching Aboriginal astronomy, and that the sticks did not serve a useful purpose once students left, with many left behind. They replaced the signal stick activity with telescope viewing in one of the domes: daytime programs view the Sun through a solar telescope, and nighttime programs view objects of significance to Aboriginal people, letting guides discuss both Aboriginal and scientific significance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to telescopes in an observatory dome, including a solar telescope for daytime programs
#### Constraints
- Daytime and nighttime programs offer different viewing targets (Sun versus Moon, planets, stars, and star clusters)

### Target Learners
- Primary school students on observatory visits

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning Aboriginal and scientific significance of astronomical objects
- Understanding the physical structure of the Sun
