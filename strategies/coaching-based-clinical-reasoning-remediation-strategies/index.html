Remediate clinical reasoning deficits through case-based coaching, deliberate practice, guided reflection, and structured reasoning frameworks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review identifies a recurring bundle of instructional strategies for remediating clinical reasoning deficits. It states that "Case-based coaching allows learners to engage with authentic clinical scenarios while practicing hypothesis generation, data gathering, differential diagnosis, and management planning under guided supervision." Deliberate practice reinforces these skills through repeated targeted exercises with timely feedback; additional approaches include structured reasoning frameworks or diagnostic algorithms, guided reflection, and verbalization of thought processes. Selection depended on institutional context, faculty expertise, and available resources.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Substantial institutional investment including protected faculty time, sustained engagement, and financial resources

Constraints¶

  • Most reported remediation programs were conducted outside the formal residency curriculum; clinical reasoning deficits often require longer remediation periods than other competency domains

Target Learners¶

  • underperforming medical residents with clinical reasoning deficits

Target Learning Goals¶

  • hypothesis generation, data gathering, differential diagnosis, and management planning

Claims¶

  • In a standardized faculty-guidance and coaching remediation program, 96% of learners with clinical reasoning deficits passed reassessment [+W]
  • In a program combining coaching, deliberate practice, formative feedback, and enhanced supervision, 55% of residents and fellows with primary clinical reasoning deficits graduated in good standing [+W]
  • Across remediation programs reviewed, 55%–100% of residents successfully completed remediation and finished residency in good standing, while 4%–23% did not [+W]
