Use existing after-school programs with paraprofessional staff as a scalable setting for foundational-skills interventions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends expanding learning opportunities into after-school contexts to build foundational EF and VS skills when school-day time is scarce. It argues the curriculum is implementable with non-school personnel: "this curriculum can easily be implemented with after-school staff members (e.g., paraprofessionals, college students) providing both organizational structure and tangible goals", using low-cost, reusable, commercially available materials.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Existing after-school program infrastructure
  • Week-long staff training in behavior management and group leadership plus additional curriculum training

Constraints¶

  • The authors note free after-school program operating costs are high and vary widely once food and transportation are considered, and prior after-school programs for under-served students have often been poorly organized and lacked clear goals

Target Learners¶

  • kindergarten and first-grade children from low-income communities entering school with skill gaps

Target Learning Goals¶

  • executive function
  • visuospatial skills
  • classroom behavior

Claims¶

  • An after-school EF/VS intervention improves directly assessed executive function in kindergarten and first-grade children (intent-to-treat) [+W]
  • An after-school EF/VS intervention improves directly assessed visuospatial skills in kindergarten and first-grade children (intent-to-treat) [+W]
  • Improvements in executive function mediate the intervention's effects on learning-related behaviors and problem behaviors [+W]
  • The after-school EF/VS intervention showed no direct intent-to-treat effect on teacher-rated classroom behavior composites, with a trend for problem behaviors [~W]
  • Initial EF and VS skill levels do not significantly moderate intervention effects on classroom behavior [~W]
  • Visuospatial skill improvement does not mediate intervention effects on classroom behavior [~W]
