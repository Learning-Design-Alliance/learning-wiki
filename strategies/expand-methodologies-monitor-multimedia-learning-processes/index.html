Expand research methodologies to monitor learning processes during multimedia instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

To incorporate social, affective, motivational, and metacognitive components into CTML, the article recommends expanding measurement beyond post-lesson outcome tests. The article states: "we need to expand the research methodologies used to monitor learning processes, including the use of eye-tracking, biometric, brain monitoring, and survey techniques." Examples include eye-tracking of attention allocation, heart rate variability and electro-dermal activity in immersive virtual reality, portable EEG measures of distraction, and brief survey items injected at pauses within an ongoing lesson.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • New process measures should complement existing techniques to measure learning outcomes

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • students learning from multimedia lessons in laboratory and classroom settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • detecting cognitive, social, and affective learning processes during instruction

Related Strategies¶

  • Complement outcome measures with continuous process measures to assess social effects and enable adaptive pedagogical agents
  • Incorporate potential moderators into experimental designs for multimedia testing research
  • Collaborate with stakeholders and with ethicists and lawyers to enable classroom eye-tracking under data-protection law
