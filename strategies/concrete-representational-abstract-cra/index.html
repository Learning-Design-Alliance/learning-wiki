Concrete-Representational-Abstract (CRA)¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 9 studies (4 causal, 2 quant-synthesis, 2 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 2 of 9 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

CRA is an instructional approach that uses concrete materials, visual representations, and abstract symbols to teach math concepts. Learners first manipulate concrete objects to understand a concept, then draw pictures representing the objects, and finally use abstract numbers and symbols. As instructors move through the sequence, abstract symbols should be used in conjunction with concrete materials and drawings — not after them — to promote association between symbolic notation and the underlying quantities. The approach descends from Bruner's enactive–iconic–symbolic sequence and is a staple of special education mathematics instruction.

Design Implications¶

CRA manages intrinsic cognitive load by giving abstract notation a referent learners can see and touch, reducing the risk that symbols are manipulated procedurally without meaning Overloaded working memory degrades learning outcomes. [+M]. Pairing representations with symbols also exploits dual channels of processing Dual coding of verbal and visual information improves recall. [+M]. However, concrete materials are not automatically helpful: poorly chosen manipulatives can distract from the target structure rather than illuminate it Manipulatives support learning only when they make the target structure salient. [~M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Concrete materials matched to the concept (e.g., base ten blocks for place value, fraction tiles for equivalence), drawing materials, and explicit teaching methods
  • Instructor modeling and description at each level, with ample practice and feedback before transitioning
  • Deliberate overlap between levels: abstract symbols introduced alongside, not after, concrete and pictorial work
  • A mastery criterion at each level before fading to the next

Constraints¶

  • Transitions that move too quickly leave learners with symbols but no underlying concept; learners with weak fact fluency or memory difficulties may stall at the abstract level
  • Concrete materials that are themselves complex (e.g., money for teaching decimals) can add extraneous load Overloaded working memory degrades learning outcomes. [-M]
  • For learners with strong prior knowledge of a concept, the concrete phase is redundant and slows progress Guidance and support become less effective as learner expertise increases. [~M]
  • Requires more instructional time than symbol-first teaching; impractical as a universal default for every topic

Implementation Variability¶

  • Gradual vs. simultaneous: classic CRA fades each level out before the next; a simultaneous variant presents all three representations side by side throughout
  • Virtual manipulatives: apps such as Brainingcamp and The Math Learning Center's Number Frames replicate concrete materials digitally with comparable outcomes in several studies
  • CRA for algebra: Witzel's CRA sequence uses concrete cups and counters for integers and equation solving, extending the approach beyond arithmetic

Target Learners¶

  • Learners with mathematics learning difficulties and disabilities — the strongest evidence base CRA instruction improves math outcomes for students with learning disabilities. [+M]
  • Young learners encountering a concept for the first time, for whom symbols alone carry no meaning
  • Less beneficial for advanced learners who already operate symbolically Guidance and support become less effective as learner expertise increases. [~M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Conceptual understanding: why a procedure works, not just how
  • Procedural fluency grounded in meaning, supporting retention and transfer
  • Representational flexibility: connecting symbols, pictures, and quantities

Instructions¶

  1. Activate relevant prior knowledge and preteach prerequisite skills (Direct Instruction, Supportive Information)
  2. Concrete phase: model the concept with physical materials while narrating the mathematics; learners manipulate and explain (Practice)
  3. Representational phase: replace objects with drawings or diagrams of the same objects, explicitly linking each mark to the material it stands for
  4. Abstract phase: introduce symbols alongside the drawings, then fade the supports (Procedural Information, Simple-to-Complex Sequencing)
  5. Provide immediate corrective feedback at each level and assess performance against a mastery criterion before advancing

Related Strategies¶

  • Cognitively Guided Instruction (CGI for Math) — shares CRA's commitment to grounding symbols in learners' own models of quantities
  • Explicit Teaching — CRA is typically delivered through explicit modeling, guided practice, and independent practice
  • Manipulative-Based Instruction — the concrete phase generalizes beyond mathematics to science and other domains

Examples¶

  • Place value: learners build 342 with base ten blocks, then draw flats/longs/units, then write the numeral — with the numeral introduced while blocks are still visible
  • Fraction equivalence: fraction tiles compared physically, then bar diagrams, then the symbolic procedure for finding common denominators
  • Witzel's CRA algebra curriculum: solving x + 3 = 5 with cups and counters before pictorial sketches and finally symbolic manipulation
  • Number Frames / Number Pieces apps — free virtual manipulatives supporting the concrete and representational phases digitally
