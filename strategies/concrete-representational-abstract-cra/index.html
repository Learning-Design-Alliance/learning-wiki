Concrete-Representational-Abstract (CRA)¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 7 claims (4 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 21 studies (10 causal, 5 quant-synthesis, 5 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 5 of 21 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

CRA is an instructional approach that uses concrete materials, visual representations, and abstract symbols to teach math concepts. Learners first manipulate concrete objects to understand a concept, then draw pictures representing the objects, and finally use abstract numbers and symbols. As instructors move through the sequence, abstract symbols should be used in conjunction with concrete materials and drawings — not after them — to promote association between symbolic notation and the underlying quantities. The approach descends from Bruner's enactive–iconic–symbolic sequence and is a staple of special education mathematics instruction.

Design Implications¶

CRA manages intrinsic cognitive load by giving abstract notation a referent learners can see and touch, reducing the risk that symbols are manipulated procedurally without meaning Overloaded working memory degrades learning outcomes. [+M]. Pairing representations with symbols also exploits dual channels of processing Dual coding of verbal and visual information improves recall. [+M]. However, concrete materials are not automatically helpful: poorly chosen manipulatives can distract from the target structure rather than illuminate it Manipulatives support learning only when they make the target structure salient. [~M].

Constraints¶

  • Transitions that move too quickly leave learners with symbols but no underlying concept; learners with weak fact fluency or memory difficulties may stall at the abstract level
  • Concrete materials that are themselves complex (e.g., money for teaching decimals) can add extraneous load Overloaded working memory degrades learning outcomes. [-M]
  • For learners with strong prior knowledge of a concept, the concrete phase is redundant and slows progress Guidance and support become less effective as learner expertise increases. [~M]
  • Requires more instructional time than symbol-first teaching; impractical as a universal default for every topic
  • Manipulatives left unconnected to symbols can become a distraction; learners may play with materials without extracting the intended structure Concrete materials can increase extraneous load when their perceptual richness is not tied to the target concept. [-M]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Gradual vs. simultaneous: classic CRA fades each level out before the next; a simultaneous variant presents all three representations side by side throughout
  • Virtual manipulatives: apps such as Brainingcamp and The Math Learning Center's Number Frames replicate concrete materials digitally with comparable outcomes in several studies
  • CRA for algebra: Witzel's CRA sequence uses concrete cups and counters for integers and equation solving, extending the approach beyond arithmetic
  • CRA-I (with explicit integration lessons between phases) — used in mathematics interventions for students with learning disabilities
  • Partial CRA — starting at the representational phase when learners already have concrete experience with the concept
  • Virtual manipulatives — digital versions (e.g., Brainingcamp, Didax) that can scaffold the concrete phase at scale
  • Simultaneous linking — showing concrete, pictorial, and symbolic forms side by side rather than in strict sequence, useful when time is limited
  • Concreteness fading: start concrete and fade to abstract within a single lesson sequence (Fyfe et al., 2014)
  • Simultaneous linking: show concrete, pictorial, and symbolic forms side by side rather than strictly sequentially
  • Virtual manipulatives: digital versions (e.g., Brainingcamp, Didax virtual manipulatives) trade tactile experience for scalability and clean fading
  • CRA with explicit instruction: common in special education, pairing the sequence with scripted teacher demonstration and student verbalization
  • Gradual vs. simultaneous: fade stage by stage, or present all three representations side by side from the start for learners who can handle the load
  • Virtual manipulatives: digital tools (e.g., Brainingcamp, Didax virtual manipulatives) can substitute for physical objects, with similar effects in meta-analytic comparisons [~M]
  • Domain extension: CRA originated in mathematics but the logic applies to science (physical models → diagrams → formal notation) and programming (physical artifacts → flowcharts → code)
  • CRA-I (CRA with explicit instruction): teacher demonstration at each stage followed by guided practice — the most researched variant for students with learning disabilities

  • CSA (Concrete–Semiconcrete–Abstract): an older variant where "semiconcrete" uses pictures of real objects rather than schematic drawings

Instructions¶

  1. Activate relevant prior knowledge and preteach prerequisite skills (Direct Instruction, Supportive Information)
  2. Concrete phase: model the concept with physical materials while narrating the mathematics; learners manipulate and explain (Practice)
  3. Representational phase: replace objects with drawings or diagrams of the same objects, explicitly linking each mark to the material it stands for
  4. Abstract phase: introduce symbols alongside the drawings, then fade the supports (Procedural Information, Simple-to-Complex Sequencing)
  5. Provide immediate corrective feedback at each level and assess performance against a mastery criterion before advancing

Examples¶

  • Place value: learners build 342 with base ten blocks, then draw flats/longs/units, then write the numeral — with the numeral introduced while blocks are still visible
  • Fraction equivalence: fraction tiles compared physically, then bar diagrams, then the symbolic procedure for finding common denominators
  • Witzel's CRA algebra curriculum: solving x + 3 = 5 with cups and counters before pictorial sketches and finally symbolic manipulation
  • Number Frames / Number Pieces apps — free virtual manipulatives supporting the concrete and representational phases digitally
  • Cognitively Guided Instruction — builds arithmetic understanding from children's informal, concrete modeling strategies toward abstract number sentences
  • Concrete-Representational-Abstract instruction for fractions — Butler, Miller, Crehan, Babbitt & Pierce (2003) compared CRA and representational-only treatments for teaching fraction concepts to secondary students with learning disabilities; both improved, CRA strongest for subtraction
  • National Library of Virtual Manipulatives — free virtual manipulatives (base-ten blocks, algebra tiles) supporting the concrete-to-representational transition in digital contexts
  • Singapore Math / Math in Focus — published curriculum built on the concrete–pictorial–abstract sequence, using bar models as the representational bridge to algebraic reasoning
  • Witzel's CRA algebra curriculum — algebra tiles used to teach linear equation solving to middle school students with math difficulties, moving from tile manipulation to drawn area models to symbolic steps.
  • Butler et al. fraction intervention — fraction tiles and circles to teach equivalent fractions and fraction addition, with measured gains over representational-only instruction.
  • Bridges in Mathematics — a published K–5 curriculum that systematically sequences hands-on models, visual models, and symbolic notation across units.
  • Numicon — structured number tiles used in UK primary classrooms as the concrete stage of early arithmetic instruction.
