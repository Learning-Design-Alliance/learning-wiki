# Prevent closely knit homogeneous-attitude communities to reduce bias by increasing productive friction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that in collaborative knowledge construction and learning environments, designers should prevent the emergence of closely knit communities with very homogeneous attitudes toward certain topics. According to the article, this "can be a means of reducing biases by increasing productive friction" (Jirschitzka et al. 2017). The strategy targets platforms and learning environments where diverse viewpoints can be incorporated and exposure to others' perspectives can support knowledge development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A platform or learning environment that allows contributors with different attitudes and backgrounds to encounter and edit shared artifacts
#### Constraints
- The article states more research is needed to understand dynamics in homo- and heterogeneous knowledge communities and to disentangle effects of specific digital environment forms; exposure to different viewpoints is not a guarantee for successful knowledge construction

### Target Learners
- contributors and learners in large-scale online knowledge construction platforms

### Target Learning Goals
- reduction of biases and radicalization, and productive resolution of socio-cognitive conflicts

### Affordances
- Productive Friction Concept
