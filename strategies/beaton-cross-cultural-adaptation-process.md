# Follow a staged cross-cultural adaptation process with translation, synthesis, back-translation, and pilot testing when adapting a scale to a new language

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The study adapted GRAS from Dutch to Danish following the process suggested by Beaton and colleagues. Stage 1 used independent expert translations of the Dutch and English versions; stage 2 synthesised a single Danish version by consensus; semi-structured pilot interviews with three students on wording, translation, relevance, understanding, and time consumption produced a pre-final version; stages 3 and 4 used back translation to Dutch, "which did not lead to any alterations"; stage 5 pilot tested electronic distribution on two student groups (n = 35).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple independent expert translators and a consensus synthesis step
- Pilot testing to ensure the electronic distribution, administration, and reminder procedure functioned well
#### Constraints
- The authors note that transfer to a different international setting could still result in subtle differences in linguistic nuance, though they judged these most likely minor

### Target Learners
- medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- valid measurement of reflection across languages and settings
