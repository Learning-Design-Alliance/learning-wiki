# Arabic-speaking medical students should exercise caution, attentiveness and heightened scrutiny when using ChatGPT for exam preparation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that Arabic-speaking medical students using ChatGPT to prepare for licensure exams apply heightened scrutiny to its answers, because "the tool's efficacy in aiding preparation for medical exams in the Arabic language falls short in comparison to its performance in English". The recommendation rests on the study's own measured accuracy of 43.9%, below the exam's passing grade of 60. It is framed for exam preparation, not for clinical use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students or educators using ChatGPT for Arabic-language exam preparation must verify its answers rather than rely on it as a primary tool
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is tied to ChatGPT-3.5 at the time of writing and to Arabic-language multiple-choice OBGYN questions; the authors warn against generalizing to other languages or future model versions

### Target Learners
- Arabic-speaking medical students preparing for Israeli OBGYN licensure exams (foreign university alumni)

### Target Learning Goals
- Medical knowledge and knowledge application in obstetrics and gynecology assessed by licensure examinations
