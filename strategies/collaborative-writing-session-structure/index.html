Run a collaborative writing session with teacher instruction followed by timed pair writing on a nominated topic¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's experimental syllabus structures each session as: about 45 minutes of teacher instruction on writing principles and materials, then the teacher nominates a topic and assigns students into pairs, who receive "45 minutes to work on the topic and to write about it", followed by reading of the writings and take-home writing with teacher feedback the next session. The teacher circulates during pair work providing guidance. This same structure, minus pair work, ran in the control class.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Same syllabus, materials, topics, and session duration across groups; a teacher who circulates and provides feedback on both individual and pair writing

Constraints¶

  • Evaluated over 12 sessions with low-intermediate learners in an EFL institute setting; the article does not claim effectiveness beyond this scope

Target Learners¶

  • low-intermediate EFL students aged 15-18

Target Learning Goals¶

  • L2 paragraph writing skill across content, organization, grammar, vocabulary, and mechanics

Affordances¶

  • Sociocultural Co Constructivism Collaborative Writing

Claims¶

  • Collaborative writing improves each of the five writing components (content, organization, grammar, vocabulary, mechanics) in low-intermediate EFL students [+W]
  • Collaborative writing instruction improves overall writing performance of low-intermediate EFL students more than individual writing instruction [+W]
