# Combine technique education with ongoing monitoring, reinforcement, and quality assurance to sustain blood pressure measurement proficiency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that remediation of nurses' BP measurement deficiencies go beyond one-off instruction: "efforts to address these deficiencies should include not only education on proper technique but also ongoing monitoring and reinforcement of skills through regular training and quality assurance initiatives." This strategy pairs initial guideline-based technique education with recurring observation-based checks. It responds to the observed pattern in which 74.7% of nurses showed poor skills and 96% poor knowledge despite a mean 9.7 years of experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a standardized checklist based on AHA guidelines for observation-based skill assessment
- Organizational capacity for regular training and quality assurance initiatives
#### Constraints
- The article notes its single-center design and relatively small sample size may restrict generalizability of the findings motivating this strategy

### Target Learners
- Practicing nurses responsible for blood pressure assessment in clinical settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate blood pressure measurement technique
- Knowledge of standardized BP measurement guidelines and protocols
