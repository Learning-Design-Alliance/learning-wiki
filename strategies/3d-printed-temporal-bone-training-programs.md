# Incorporate 3D-printed temporal bone models into surgical training programs to accelerate the learning curve

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (4 for, 3 mixed) · 4 studies (2 design, 1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating 3D-printed temporal bone models into surgical education programs, arguing they provide a safe, controlled practice environment and hands-on learning without relying on cadaveric specimens. They state that "incorporating 3D-printed temporal bone models into surgical training programs can significantly accelerate the learning curve for trainees, improve surgical skills, and enhance surgical outcomes." Recommended uses include preoperative planning and customization of patient-specific models from medical imaging for malformed or pathological temporal bones.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- 3D-printing capability with resin resembling human bone; models customized to simulate specific pathologies or surgical scenarios where needed
#### Constraints
- The article calls for future research to further validate effectiveness across different surgical procedures (such as cochlear implantation and labyrinthine dissection) and settings (microscopic vs. endoscopic approaches)

### Target Learners
- ENT residents and surgeons in otologic training

### Target Learning Goals
- otologic surgical skills
- temporal bone anatomy
- preoperative planning of complex ear procedures
