Institutional leaders should examine their own data, identify troublesome equity trends, and engage faculty, staff, and students in deliberate action to support all students¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The brief argues that closing gaps "takes deliberate action from institutions," exemplified by Washington State University's intentional early outreach and campus-community building and by University of Nebraska–Lincoln's proactive advising, degree-planning, first-year communities, data-driven curricular improvement, and a diversity taskforce. Leaders are told to "look at their data, find the troublesome trends, and engage faculty, staff, and students themselves in identifying ways to best support all students toward success."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional leaders must examine their own graduation-rate data by race/ethnicity to find troublesome trends

Constraints¶

  • The brief states overall improvement alone does not translate into gains for underrepresented students without deliberate action

Target Learners¶

  • first-time, full-time undergraduate students at four-year colleges, particularly underrepresented minority students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • college completion and closing white–URM graduation rate gaps

Related Strategies¶

  • Foster communities of practice for AI governance instead of rigid top-down policies
