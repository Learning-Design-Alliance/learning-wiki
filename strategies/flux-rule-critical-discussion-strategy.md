# Accompany any use of the 'flux rule' with a full critical discussion of its physical and epistemological drawbacks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
If instructors choose, as tradition and textbooks imply, to teach electromagnetic induction with the 'flux rule', the paper recommends that this choice be accompanied by a full discussion of its drawbacks: it cannot be interpreted causally, it misuses the field concept, it cannot localize the induced emf, it may require an ad hoc contour choice, and it is falsified by Blondel's 1914 experiment. What should be avoided is presenting the rule as the law of induction without any critical discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must themselves understand the derivation showing the flux rule holds only in the Galilean limit for inertially moving rigid and filiform circuits
#### Constraints
- The paper states instructors face a choice depending on their teaching context; the microscopic Lorentz-force description is the alternative

### Target Learners
- upper high school students
- elementary physics course students

### Target Learning Goals
- understand the status and limits of the flux rule in electromagnetic induction
- develop critical reasoning about physical laws
