# Bridge the gap between the conceptual and quantitative aspects of quantum mechanics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 4 studies (2 associational, 2 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that because many faculty recall making sense of calculations without conceptual understanding, and student preparation has grown more diverse, it is "important to consider strategies to bridge the gap between the conceptual and quantitative aspects of QM". The strategy pairs practice with the formalism and problem solving against explicit conceptual sense-making of quantum systems in upper-division courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Suitable for upper-division undergraduate QM courses where the diversity of students' prior preparation in the classrooms has increased greatly over the years
#### Constraints
- Derived from a small survey of thirteen instructors, so the basis is indicative rather than definitive
- Instructors surveyed rated students' mathematical preparation as average or below average, so quantitative readiness cannot be assumed

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of quantum mechanics alongside applying the formalism to solve diverse problems
