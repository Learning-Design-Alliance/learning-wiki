# Open a lesson on spherical geometry by asking learners to flatten a familiar spherical object such as a soccer ball

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The chapter opens with a familiar object, a soccer ball made of pentagons and hexagons, and asks the learner to predict what happens when the pattern is flattened. When the shapes no longer fit, the discrepancy motivates the key idea that "shapes on a sphere behave very diﬀerently than shapes on a ﬂat surface." This creates cognitive disequilibrium from an everyday artifact before any formal machinery is introduced.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A familiar spherical object whose surface pattern is visibly composed of polygons
#### Constraints
- Introduced at the start of the lesson as a motivation, before formal definitions

### Target Learners
- school students beginning geometry

### Target Learning Goals
- recognize that planar intuitions fail on curved surfaces
