# Integrate virtual and augmented reality as supplementary tools rather than full replacements for traditional learning methods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that immersive technologies like VR and AR be used as supplements to, not substitutes for, traditional hands-on learning. Evidence shows virtual labs increase confidence and engagement but "did not satisfactorily replace hands-on experimental learning", and AR improves engagement without consistent test-score gains. Careful implementation is needed to manage cognitive load.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must carefully design VR-based curricula to ensure cognitive load remains manageable and learning objectives are clearly defined
#### Constraints
- Excessive visual stimuli in VR environments can reduce learning efficiency if students are not adequately guided
- Cost of implementing high-fidelity simulation and VR-based learning can be a limiting factor for low-resource settings

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students (DO/MD/MBBS/MBChB)

### Target Learning Goals
- biomedical science knowledge acquisition
- clinical skills development
