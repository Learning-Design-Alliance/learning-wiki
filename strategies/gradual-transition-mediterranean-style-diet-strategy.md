# Promote gradual transition toward a Mediterranean-style dietary approach, starting with low-sugar intermediate patterns, as an initial nutrition and public health strategy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that in non-Mediterranean food cultures, where full MedDiet adherence is difficult, schools and public health actors first promote intermediate Mediterranean-style patterns marked by low sugar consumption, then move toward fuller adherence. The Conclusions state that "a gradual transition toward a Mediterranean-style-based dietary approach could emerge as a plausible initial strategy in nutrition and public health, linked to more desirable outcomes in brain health and educational achievements". The discussion adds that low-sugar intermediate patterns "could serve as a kick-start in transitioning toward a Mediterranean-style dietary pattern in non-Mediterranean regions".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recognition that full Mediterranean-style adherence is difficult in non-Mediterranean food cultures due to cultural and socioeconomic factors
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional, so the article itself notes it "precludes the establishment of causality even with meticulous adjustments for pertinent variables"

### Target Learners
- Adolescents in non-Mediterranean countries, e.g., Chilean schoolchildren aged 10–14

### Target Learning Goals
- Supporting cognitive performance and academic achievement through dietary quality
