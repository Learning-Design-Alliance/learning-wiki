# Maximise trainee-as-trainer opportunities by giving CTs and JRs operative preference under SR supervision, starting TT early, and selecting simpler in-hours cases

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a concrete service-delivery recipe for near-peer intraoperative teaching: prioritise junior trainees as operators with senior registrars as supervising teachers, begin TT early in training, and entrust simpler cases during daylight hours. The authors state that "Opportunities can be maximised by giving preference to CTs or JRs for appendicectomies supported by SRs, starting TT early in training, and using appropriate case selection for simpler cases, performed during daylight hours." They estimate raising TT from 20% to 35% would create 37 additional teaching opportunities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supervising senior registrar available to assist, and an ad hoc entrustment decision weighing learner capability, patient risk, timing and urgency for each case
#### Constraints
- Case selection restricted TT to simpler histology, children over five, working hours and weekdays; quality of learning could not be ascertained in this retrospective study

### Target Learners
- Core Trainees (PGY3–4) learning appendicectomy
- Junior Registrars (PGY5–6) acting as trainers

### Target Learning Goals
- Laparoscopic appendicectomy operative skill
- Intraoperative teaching and supervision skill
