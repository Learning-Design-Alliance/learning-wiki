Design pharmaceutical care learning environments that combine settings inside and outside the university and adapt to learning styles and contexts¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 4 studies (3 review, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that learning environments for pharmaceutical care combine real and simulated settings inside and outside the university. It found environments inside (ability labs) and outside the university (hospitals, outpatient clinics, and community pharmacies) that contribute to professional skill development by promoting early contact with patients. The conclusion states environments should "be reproducible in scenarios both inside and outside the university and have the capability to be adapted to different learning styles, organizational models, and social and cultural contexts".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Environments must provide a safe environment with essential conditions for learning success
  • Selection of virtual environments must be guided by specific learning objectives

Constraints¶

  • The review reports a lack of studies about developing learning environments for teaching Pharmaceutical Care

Target Learners¶

  • Pharmacy students in early and later course years, including interns

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Patient-centered practice competencies, medication management, professional skills

Claims¶

  • Virtual environments favor clinical reasoning, self-regulation and peer collaboration, while physical environments engage critical reasoning, professionalism and communication abilities [+W]
  • The closer training content is to authentic clinical scenarios, the smoother its translation into practice [+W]
  • Experiential learning in authentic clinical environments (community engagement, home visits, outreach rotations) was reported to produce the greatest positive impact on student attitudes, confidence, and perceived competence, but is the most resource-intensive [+W]
  • Student-centered innovative strategies (PBL, EBL, simulation, TBL, blended and online learning) showed positive effects in pharmaceutical care learning [+W]
  • The review finds flipped learning, PBL, gamification, and collaborative learning adapted to digital environments produced positive effects on motivation, participation, and transversal competences [+W]
