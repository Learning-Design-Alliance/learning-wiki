# Deliver the remote lab course through asynchronous Slack lectures plus Zoom Q&A sessions with individual student interviews

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
To convert the course to remote teaching, the instructors used a Slack workspace and Zoom meetings. “Lectures were mostly asynchronous, delivered as posts on the Slack space, which students had to go through on their own.” In each Zoom “Questions and Answers” session every student was interviewed about the work and exercises already done, any difficulty encountered, and plans for the coming days, and the Slack forum carried bi-directional communication throughout the week.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A Slack workspace and Zoom meetings for posting material and bi-directional communication
- Instructions to install the free course software at home, or remote connection to Linux-based university machines where all software is available
#### Constraints
- Five students dropped out when the switch to remote was announced or during the course

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate physics students studying remotely during lockdown

### Target Learning Goals
- independent work through asynchronous material and coding exercises
- bi-directional communication with instructors and supervisors
