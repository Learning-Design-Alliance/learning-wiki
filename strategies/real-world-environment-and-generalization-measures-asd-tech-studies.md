# Design technology-based ASD social skills studies to embed the child's real-world environment and measure generalization and maintenance of skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review identifies, as strengths of the compiled trials, incorporating the subject's real-world environment in the study protocol and including generalization and maintenance of the new skill as outcome measures. Embedding familiar settings "has the potential to reduce discomfort known to be common in children with ASD when faced with changes in their routine", diminishing anxiety as a confounding variable. Generalization measurements, the review argues, suggest "an intervention has sticking power and usefulness beyond the study environment itself." Designers of technology-assisted social skills interventions should therefore assess skills in alternative environments and over time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Outcome measurement should include generalization to alternative environments and people other than the researchers, plus maintenance over time.
#### Constraints
- The review notes small sample sizes and reliance on researcher or parent observation as measurement limits across the compiled studies.

### Target Learners
- School-age children with autism spectrum disorder

### Target Learning Goals
- Social skills such as emotion recognition, communication, and collaborative play
