# Guide students to build and calibrate their own smartphone DNI instruments using tracing paper, a simple collimator tube, and on-site or satellite reference data

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An instructional strategy in which instructors have students replicate the article's setup with their own devices: after feasibility was demonstrated with professional equipment, "instructors could guide students to prepare their own smartphones similarly to the experiment, using tracing paper and a simple collimator tube, to measure direct illuminance in normal incidence". Students then calibrate their instruments against on-site DNI data or satellite estimates, and in the process develop scientific-mathematical skills such as recording and processing experimental data, assessing data quality, and assigning uncertainty to simplified irradiance models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear-sky day for calibration, following the principles of professional ISO-standard calibrations
- Readily available materials plus an open location in clear-sky conditions for a couple of hours
#### Constraints
- Professional measurement and tracking equipment is not indispensable and is included only to demonstrate that the smartphone is a calibratable, reliable instrument

### Target Learners
- university physics students in instructional laboratories

### Target Learning Goals
- recording and processing of experimental data
- assigning uncertainty to simplified models for direct solar irradiance estimation
