# Strengthen attitudes alongside technical instruction to convert knowledge into sustained virtual simulation engagement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the strong mediating role of attitude, the article recommends that institutions go beyond technical instruction: "Strategies such as showcasing successful learning outcomes, integrating simulation into assessment systems, and highlighting its relevance to clinical competence may help strengthen positive attitudes". It also recommends blended teaching combining virtual simulation with classroom elements, and targeted programs for Clinical Medicine students, implemented during specialty courses or internships.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment to integrating virtual simulation into curricula and assessment systems
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that relying solely on virtual simulation may lead to a subpar learning experience because its process is relatively rigid; it recommends virtual-actual combination

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students, primarily Clinical Medicine majors and students taking the licensing examination

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained engagement in virtual simulation training
- clinical skills development
