# Design K-12 online and blended courseware that places content in contexts while reserving teacher activities for enriching contextual cues and decontextualization

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's design implications for online and blended learning environments: interactive courseware for K-12 learners should consider "placing learning content in to contexts, but still consider a human teacher's activities of enriching contextual cues and decontextualization". Providers should continue to give guidance through both computer support and teachers' face-to-face support, and developers should consider seamlessly connecting a series of single context settings to form multiple context settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Courseware must place learning content into contexts
- Teacher support must include enriching contextual cues and decontextualization activities
#### Constraints
- Findings come from 9th-grade vocational high school students studying a logic unit; the authors caution different results could be expected with older students or other domains

### Target Learners
- K-12 learners in online and blended settings

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual and procedural knowledge of abstract content such as logic

## Claims

- [Combined conceptual and procedural knowledge of logic was highest in the teacher-supported courseware groups, with single- and multiple-context courseware alone resembling teacher-only instruction](../claims/combined-knowledge-contextualization-teacher-support.md) [+W]
- [Prior knowledge and type of contextualized learning environment each moderately predicted logic learning, but environment type did not interact with critical thinking disposition](../claims/covariate-prior-knowledge-environment-ctd-logic.md) [+W]
- [Critical thinking disposition progress did not differ significantly among the four groups, and the teacher-only group gained the most](../claims/ctd-progress-no-group-difference-teacher-only-largest-gain.md) [~W]
- [On procedural knowledge of logic, the multiple-context courseware with teacher support outperformed teacher-only instruction, while the context-courseware groups performed similarly](../claims/procedural-knowledge-multiple-context-teacher-support.md) [+W]
- [Ninth-graders learning logic with contextualized courseware plus teacher support achieved higher conceptual knowledge than courseware-only or teacher-only groups](../claims/teacher-supported-contextualized-courseware-conceptual-logic.md) [+W]
