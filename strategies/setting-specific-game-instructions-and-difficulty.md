# Provide more direct instructions and adapt game difficulty and content to the setting and audience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
From player feedback, the authors recommend clearer guidance about the game's goal and controls, and tailoring versions to context: a downloadable home version that is somewhat challenging to keep players engaged, and an exhibition version where objectives are found quickly to avoid frustration. They write: "more direct instructions are needed so the player knows what they are supposed to do". They also propose modifying texts and graphics for children under 10, their most enthusiastic players.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback channels from players in each setting
- Separate builds or configurations for home and exhibition use
#### Constraints
- The most enthusiastic current players are children under 10, which may require reworking existing texts and graphics

### Target Learners
- general public at science fairs
- children under 10 years old
- home players downloading the game

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding what the game asks them to do
- sustained engagement with planet-formation content
