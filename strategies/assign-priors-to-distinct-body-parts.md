# Assign distinct body parts to distinct temporal statistics when learners must handle multiple event-timing distributions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that priors assigned to different body parts are learned independently while those sharing one body part are generalised, the article suggests exploiting body-part specificity in skilled activities such as batting. The authors suggest that "a batter may beat time using a foot when a fastball is pitched (i.e., short prior), whereas using a hand when a slowball is pitched (i.e., long prior)" to improve hit rates. Anatomically distant effectors yield the fastest separation of priors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two distinct temporal event statistics occurring in the task; distinct motor effectors available for responses associated with each statistic
#### Constraints
- Evidence is from laboratory coincidence timing with healthy adults; application to daily behaviours such as sports is proposed, not tested

### Target Learners
- adults performing skilled sensorimotor timing tasks, e.g., athletes

### Target Learning Goals
- concurrent learning and use of multiple temporal prior distributions for anticipatory timing

### Affordances
- Somatotopic Organisation Prior Learning
