# Teach ratio through varied contextual problem states solved with multiple strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's recommendations for developing proportional reasoning: teaching content should use "verbal contextual problem states" so proportional operations are not perceived as procedural, bringing daily-life problem states into the classroom; different problem types and different problem-solving strategies should be included; students should not be hurried in the transition to operational states; and students should see multiplicative and additive relations and their differences. In the study this took the form of twelve ratio problems solved over six weeks using unit ratio, equivalent fraction, cross-multiplication and change-multiplier strategies, with students sharing solutions on a smart board.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Problems representing different ratio problem types (Lamon, 1993) and time for students to explore understanding across multiple lessons
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a small quasi-experiment (n=16 per group) in one Central Anatolian secondary school without random selection

### Target Learners
- sixth-grade secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- proportional reasoning
- using multiple problem-solving strategies
- distinguishing multiplicative from additive relations
