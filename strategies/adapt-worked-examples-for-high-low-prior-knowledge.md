# Adapt worked-example sessions for high and low prior-knowledge students who did not benefit

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 8 studies (4 causal, 3 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 8 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the high and low prior-knowledge groups did not benefit significantly, the authors recommend redesigning the sessions for them. They suggest "maybe a smaller group session using the worked example worksheets with a focus on helping the students more would benefit the low prior mathematical knowledge group," and for high students "The addition of more challenging problems or tasks in SP session could be beneficial," or having high prior knowledge students serve as peer mentors to low prior knowledge students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Worked-example worksheets as the base material
- class assistants or peer mentors to support smaller-group formats
#### Constraints
- Both suggestions are proposals in the discussion, not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- college students with high or low prior mathematical knowledge in introductory mathematics

### Target Learning Goals
- improving the knowledge-regulation component
- college algebra success for struggling students
