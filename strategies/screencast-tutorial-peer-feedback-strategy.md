# Use student-created screencast tutorials with peer feedback to surface students' problem-solving values

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 4 studies (2 qualitative, 1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The classroom practice described had students select one of their assigned problems and create a screencast tutorial teaching how to solve it, with prompts left open regarding how tutorials should be created. After posting, the teacher prompted students to view several peers' work and provide feedback on quality and usefulness. Through viewing and commenting both online and in class, students developed norms for what constituted good screencasts, making their values about problem solving visible to researchers and teachers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need access to recording technology (iPads) and a shared posting site; the teacher prompts peer viewing and feedback
#### Constraints
- The article notes it is an open question whether screencasts improve student learning

### Target Learners
- high school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- explaining and communicating physics problem solving to peers
