# Provide adult support through group facilitators, mentors, and a school advocate for transitioning at-risk youth

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article identifies adult support as continuously present in dropout prevention intervention research and builds it into the intervention design. As the article states, "our intervention included adult support in the form of group facilitators, mentors and a school advocate." Graduate student interns working toward master's, Ph.D. or Ed.S. degrees provided mentoring, and the advocate was a school counselor serving as the designated point of contact in the home school system. The authors report that adult support through facilitators, mentors and advocates helps change school attitudes of at-risk youth transitioning back to traditional settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mentors interact with participants at least twice weekly through group sessions and individual counseling sessions
- A designated school counselor advocate housed at the home school
#### Constraints
- The authors note the treatment group was given the posttest assessment by facilitators and mentors they had developed a relationship with, which could be a reason for higher scores

### Target Learners
- at-risk high school youth returning from alternative school placements

### Target Learning Goals

- school engagement
- dropout prevention
