# Develop clear ethical AI guidelines specifying appropriate uses, framing AI as supplementary, limiting direct output copying, and promoting transparency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students proposed that ethical guidelines could mitigate AI guilt if they are clear, specific, and integrated into teaching. Per the article, "Key components of these guidelines should include: specifying appropriate uses of AI, encouraging AI as a supplementary tool, limiting the direct use of AI outputs, promoting honesty and transparency, and integrating educational support to foster a better understanding of AI tools." Educational institutions are advised to embed these into practice via talks and workshops that demystify AI.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Guidelines must be clear, specific, and well-integrated into the educational framework
- Educational institutions must provide support such as talks or workshops on ethical AI use
#### Constraints
- Derived from one qualitative study; the authors call for experimental studies testing the effectiveness of such interventions before claiming they work

### Target Learners
- secondary school students using generative AI tools

### Target Learning Goals
- responsible and ethical AI use; reduced guilt and anxiety about AI-assisted academic work
