# Dilute laboratory work across more sessions, plan each step, and avoid dead times when running labs with low-motivation vocational students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article distils organizational recommendations from the pilot's difficulties for running laboratory sessions in vocational contexts. Students "had not enough time for completing activities", so the authors conclude there is "the need to dilute the work in more parts, by planning less activities in each lab session". They also advise that "dead times during the experimentation must be avoid, because pupils can have a decreasing of attention", and that each step in the use of spaces and times must be carefully planned, tested and adapted to students' reactions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Enough scheduled lab sessions so that activities can be spread over more parts with fewer activities per session
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a pilot in which labs were carried out at the end of the school year over a period not well suited, with sessions delayed by management of activity outside the school

### Target Learners
- Upper secondary vocational school students, ages 14-15

### Target Learning Goals
- Measuring simple physical quantities, properties of matter, and elements of thermodynamics
