# Train teachers through workshops to design their own task-relevant active math lessons rather than handing out a fixed manual

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention's teacher training consisted of four workshops (two before the intervention, two follow-up workshops during the first four months) aiming to "provide teachers with the skills to implement task-relevant physical activity into the math teaching". The material focused on "developing the teacher's own abilities to design active math lessons" rather than handing out a complete manual, and teachers shared activities and advice via an internet-based intra-school communication forum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers attend a mandatory training course of four workshops covering implementation methods, activity design, and fidelity reporting
#### Constraints
- Compliance reporting was difficult: teacher reports had very low response rates for paper, e-mail and SMS methods, which the authors call a threat to internal validity

### Target Learners
- Elementary school teachers implementing active math lessons
- 1st-grade children (age ~7)

### Target Learning Goals
- Mathematics achievement through task-relevant physical activity integrated into math instruction

### Affordances
- Task Relevant Pa Embodied Cognition Framework
