# Deliver the initial CIED learning curve in metrics-based simulation to a proficiency benchmark before in vivo practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that the first part of the learning curve take place in 'a risk-free training environment' using metric-based (virtual reality) simulation, so that a predefined, objectively assessed performance level is quantitatively ensured before trainees proceed to real patients. Simulation examples include a porcine tissue model with surgical equipment for lead fixation and a full physics virtual reality simulator for lead implantation. Clinical supervisors then receive pretrained beginners whose performance level is known with quantitative assurance, optimising further clinical skill acquisition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Validated procedure metrics and a proficiency benchmark derived from experienced implanters' performance
- Simulation platforms (physical models or virtual reality) enabling metrics-based scoring
#### Constraints
- PBP 'is not meant to completely replace clinical in vivo training but aims to supplant a significant part of the initial learning curve on real patients'

### Target Learners
- Novice CIED implanters at any level of training

### Target Learning Goals
- Reaching a predefined proficiency level before operating on patients
- Reducing patient risk during the learning curve
