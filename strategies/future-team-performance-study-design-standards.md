# Design standards for future studies of surgical team performance in minimally invasive surgery

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The review's discussion sets out how future studies should be designed to overcome the heterogeneity it found: analyse multiple approaches (open, laparoscopic, robot-assisted) in relation to a single procedure, use multiple trained observers, preferably from audiovisual recordings, quantify interobserver reliability, use incident-based methodology with a predefined outcome set including causation and consequences of flow disruptions, and relate team performance to direct and indirect performance metrics. The stated purpose is to gain insight into team performance demands unique to minimally invasive surgery and to develop structured, evidence-based training programmes that enhance patient safety and procedural flow.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple trained observers with quantified interobserver reliability
- A predefined outcome set including causation and consequences of procedural flow disruptions
#### Constraints
- The review notes ethical considerations, hazards to team privacy and liability issues may limit use of audiovisual recording, and recording quality is a prerequisite

### Target Learners
- surgical teams in minimally invasive abdominal surgery

### Target Learning Goals
- non-technical skills and team coordination in the operating theatre
