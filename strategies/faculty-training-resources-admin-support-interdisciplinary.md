# Structured cross-disciplinary faculty training, leadership resource allocation, and dedicated administrative support to overcome interdisciplinary implementation barriers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A three-part implementation strategy for universities adopting the cultivation model's interdisciplinary components. The article states: "Recommended strategies to overcome these barriers include structured cross-disciplinary faculty training, enhanced resource allocation from institutional leadership, and establishing dedicated administrative support to facilitate effective interdisciplinary collaboration." It responds to barriers observed during initial pilot implementation, including limited faculty interdisciplinary teaching expertise, resource constraints and cross-departmental coordination challenges.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional leadership commitment to resource allocation
- Administrative structures capable of cross-departmental coordination
#### Constraints
- Barriers were identified during early pilot implementation at a single university level; the article notes limited faculty expertise and resource constraints are inherent in early phases

### Target Learners
- top-notch basic medicine students

### Target Learning Goals
- interdisciplinary collaboration and research capability
