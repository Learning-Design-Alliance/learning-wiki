# Reimagine campus physical spaces as part of institutional diversity, equity, and inclusion commitments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that universities committed to diversity, equity, and inclusion and to being an engine of economic mobility 'must change, in part, by reimagining physical spaces.' Because campuses currently operate as territorial, surveilled, and symbolic defensible spaces, changing the built environment is presented as one necessary lever alongside social and cultural change. The review frames spatial redesign as a concrete, actionable dimension of inclusion work in higher education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional commitment to diversity, equity, and inclusion; the article treats spatial change as part of broader institutional change, not a standalone fix.
#### Constraints
- The article states its new evidence is proof of concept, not definitive proof, and that future work must test whether territoriality, surveillance, and symbolism predict SES and racial gaps in belonging.

### Target Learners
- lower-SES students
- racially minoritized students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- sense of belonging at the university
- empowered use of campus public space

### Affordances
