# Design training scenarios featuring misbehaviours in varied contexts and provide data-driven feedback to strengthen professional vision

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teacher training program developers design scenarios in which the same misbehaviour occurs across different contexts (instructional situations, student backgrounds, seating positions), with detailed explanations of how to integrate contextual information when interpreting causes. It further recommends using the IVR-based PVAS's recorded data to give student teachers personalised performance reports comparing their responses to experienced educators, enabling expert teachers to discuss perception and interpretation with them and to track professional vision development longitudinally. The article states developers 'can design scenarios that feature misbehaviours occurring in various contexts.'

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment data capable of diagnosing perception and interpretation problems, and expert educators available to discuss performance reports with student teachers
#### Constraints
- The implementation case study did not experimentally validate the system's effectiveness; the sample was small and culturally specific to Taiwanese secondary settings

### Target Learners
- student teachers (novice teachers) in teacher education programs

### Target Learning Goals
- interpreting student misbehaviour by integrating contextual information; perceiving misbehaviour amid distracting classroom behaviour
