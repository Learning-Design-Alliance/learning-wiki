# Use qualitative methods and actively promote the research-teaching nexus rather than further quantitative verification

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that, given the difficulty of empirically verifying the research-teaching nexus with quantitative tools, "qualitative methodology may prove more useful to explore perceptions of the nexus", revealing faculty beliefs about its existence, nature, and evolution under increasing time constraints. They further suggest that if the nexus is held as a value of academia, researchers and administrators should explore ways of actively promoting it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to faculty willing to discuss their perceptions of the research-teaching nexus
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a direction for future work, not a tested intervention; the study's own indicators captured only recent (5–10 year) research performance

### Target Learners
- tenure and tenure-track faculty in the United States

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding faculty perceptions of the relationship between research and teaching
- holistic faculty evaluation
