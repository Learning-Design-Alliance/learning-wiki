# Digital Publishing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Digital publishing involves using online platforms and tools to share student writing and creations with a wider audience, fostering engagement and providing valuable feedback. Platforms range from class blogs and digital book creators to fanfiction communities and media-sharing sites. Because the work is received by real readers rather than only a grading teacher, students write for an [authentic audience](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md) and experience genuine reception, discussion, and impact.

## Design Implications

Publishing for real readers changes both motivation and revision behavior: when students know their work will be read by peers or the public, they attend more to clarity, audience, and quality [~M]. The pedagogical value depends less on the platform than on the cycle it enables — drafting, public reception, and revision informed by reader response [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to digital devices and internet connectivity; attention to equitable access
- Teacher guidance on platform selection, moderation, and online interaction norms
- Explicit instruction in copyright, fair use, and digital citizenship
- A structured feedback protocol so audience comments connect to writing goals ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) and [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) can structure this)

#### Constraints
- Unmoderated comment streams can deliver harsh or unhelpful responses; unstructured public feedback does not reliably improve writing and can suppress risk-taking [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [-M]
- Privacy and safety concerns require consent, pseudonyms, or closed communities for younger learners
- Platform novelty can substitute for substance — engagement gains may reflect the medium rather than improved writing [~W]
- Students with weak digital literacy or limited connectivity may be excluded or disadvantaged

#### Implementation Variability
- Fully public (blogs, fanfiction sites) vs. semi-public (class-only blogs, LMS galleries) vs. curated (school literary magazines, digital books)
- One-off showcase publishing vs. iterative publish–respond–revise cycles
- Text-only vs. multimedia (podcasts, video, digital books), which broadens participation for students who struggle with print

### Target Learners
- K–12 and postsecondary writing and media arts students, particularly adolescents whose motivation rises when work has real stakes and audience [Autonomy and authentic purpose support intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Reluctant writers, who often engage more when writing serves social connection and identity expression [~M]
- English learners, who benefit from authentic communicative purposes and exposure to varied reader communities [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Writing quality: audience awareness, revision, and craft [Writing strategy instruction combined with authentic purposes improves writing quality.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+M]
- Digital citizenship and multimodal composition
- Community participation: giving and receiving constructive critique

### Instructions
1. Establish purpose and audience — students identify who they are writing for and why ([Application](../elements/application.md))
2. Draft and peer-review using a feedback protocol focused on task and process
3. Revise and publish to the chosen platform ([Integration](../elements/integration.md))
4. Guide structured reading of audience responses; students annotate and discuss what readers noticed ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md), [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
5. Revise again based on reception, then reflect on how audience shaped the text ([Enhance Retention and Transfer](../elements/enhance-retention-and-transfer.md))

## Examples
- **[Storybird](https://storybird.com)** — students create and publish illustrated digital books for a community of readers.
- **[FanFiction.net](https://www.fanfiction.net)** and **[Archive of Our Own](https://archiveofourown.org)** — research on adolescent fanfiction communities shows young writers developing craft through public posting, reader reviews, and iterative revision.
- **Class blogs** (e.g., via Edublogs) — students publish posts and comment on peers' work under teacher moderation, often paired with international blogging exchanges such as the [Student Blogging Challenge](https://studentchallenge.edublogs.org).
- **[NaNoWriMo Young Writers Program](https://ywp.nanowrimo.org)** — students draft and "publish" novels with goal-setting and community support.
