# Use knowledge of the specific points and lengths of exposure where children are susceptible to contextual effects to structure interventions aimed at reducing learning inequities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review argues that identifying when and for how long children are susceptible to contextual effects is a prerequisite for effective intervention against learning disparities. It states that "a fundamental first step in structuring effective responses to promote learning equity is a comprehensive knowledge base detailing the specific points and lengths of exposure where children are susceptible to contextual effects." Interventions should target modifiable familial and neighborhood factors while attending to structural causes of inequity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal evidence identifying developmental timing and duration of exposure to modifiable factors
#### Constraints
- Attention must remain on systemic and structural issues as root sources of inequitable circumstances, not individuals, families, or subgroups

### Target Learners
- children in inequitable social and economic circumstances

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing learning disparities and supporting children to reach their learning potential
