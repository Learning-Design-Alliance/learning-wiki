# Improve ICU nurses' PED competence through systematic professional training, PED assessment tools, and measures promoting independent learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's management implications recommend building nurses' PED competence because KAP scores were low. It states improvement "may include, but is not limited to, the development of tools for assessing PED, systematic and professional training, and the development of multidisciplinary collaborative models." For the low attitude scores it suggests reward and punishment measures to promote independent learning, and for low practice frequency it asks department managers to increase PED knowledge and skills training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Department managers able to organise PED-related professional knowledge and skills training
- Measures such as reward and punishment to promote nurses' independent learning initiative
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a cross-sectional survey in some tertiary Grade A hospitals in Gansu Province, with possible regional and selection bias as the authors state

### Target Learners
- ICU nurses in tertiary care hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge of postextubation dysphagia incidence, assessment, and management
- Bedside swallowing assessment and rehabilitation training practices
