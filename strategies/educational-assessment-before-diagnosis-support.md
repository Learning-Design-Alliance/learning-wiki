# Base additional school adjustments and special support on educational assessments rather than psychiatric diagnosis, with support preceding diagnosis

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that Swedish schools identify and support students with mental health problems on the basis of educational assessment rather than medical diagnosis, stating that "it is educational assessments that should form the basis for additional adjustments or special support in school rather than a medical diagnosis". It argues special support and preventive measures should precede a psychiatric diagnosis, with student health services and schools as central actors. The study's diagnosis-specific findings inform which disorders need most support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Student health services and pedagogical staff able to conduct educational assessments and cooperate; the article notes Swedish schools currently lack clear guidelines and have poor staff cooperation and limited service access.
#### Constraints
- Recommendation is situated in the Swedish school and student health service context, where the article notes access to services is limited in most schools.

### Target Learners
- Compulsory-school students (age 15–16) with diagnosed or suspected mental disorders

### Target Learning Goals
- Preventing educational disadvantage associated with mental disorders among school-age children
