Project Options and Self-Grading¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 6 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 2 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 3 of 6 report an effect size

Description¶

This strategy combines two moves: offering learners a genuine choice among project formats (e.g., paper, presentation, website, exhibit) and involving them in building a criteria chart — a co-constructed rubric — for each project. Learners complete the project, grade their own work against the criteria, and write a short reflection justifying the grade they assigned. The combination converts assessment from something done to students into something done with them.

Design Implications¶

Choice supports autonomy, which is a core driver of intrinsic motivation Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation. [+S], while co-constructed criteria and self-assessment make success criteria transparent and build evaluative judgment Assessment for learning improves achievement. [+S]. The reflective justification is the load-bearing component: self-grading without a criterion-referenced rationale risks grade inflation and shallow compliance, whereas criterion-anchored self-assessment with feedback produces measurable learning gains [~M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Multiple project options that are genuinely equivalent in rigor and aligned to the same learning goals
  • A co-constructed criteria chart developed before work begins, not after — criteria defined only at grading time cannot guide production
  • Structured time for self-assessment against the criteria, plus a written justification for the assigned grade
  • Instructor moderation: teacher grades alongside students and reconciles large discrepancies through feedback or conference

Constraints¶

  • Untrained self-assessors systematically over- or under-rate their work; self-grading without calibration against exemplars inflates grades and yields little learning benefit [-M]
  • Choice without structure can overwhelm novices and shift attention from learning goals to format decisions [~M] — too many options or options with unequal cognitive demands undermine the effect
  • Students with limited prior experience of evaluative judgment may initially find self-grading threatening or performative, especially in high-stakes contexts; grading the self-assessment itself (for honesty and criterion use, not for the grade chosen) reduces this pressure
  • Co-constructing criteria takes class time; skipping it and handing out a teacher-made rubric removes most of the benefit

Implementation Variability¶

  • Criteria charts can be built whole-class, in small groups, or drafted by the teacher and negotiated with students — full co-construction is most powerful but slowest
  • Options can vary in modality (paper vs. podcast vs. prototype) while sharing a common criteria chart, or each option can carry its own chart
  • Self-grading can be formative (draft-stage, feeding revision) or summative (final grade, moderated by the teacher); formative use is lower-risk and builds the skill before summative stakes
  • Reflection can be a short written justification, a structured check-in conference, or a portfolio annotation

Target Learners¶

  • Effective across K-12, higher education, and adult learners Assessment for learning improves achievement. [+S]
  • Particularly valuable for adolescents and adults developing self-regulated learning, who benefit from practicing evaluative judgment on their own work [~M]
  • Novices need more scaffolding (exemplars, calibration practice against graded samples) before self-grading is trustworthy

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Self-regulation: monitoring, evaluating, and adjusting one's own work
  • Metacognition: articulating the reasoning behind quality judgments
  • Motivation and ownership: increasing engagement through autonomy and voice
  • Domain learning: deeper engagement with content when criteria make quality explicit

Instructions¶

  1. Define the learning goals and present 3–5 project options of equivalent rigor; consider a choice board to structure the options.
  2. Facilitate co-construction of a criteria chart: show exemplars at different quality levels, ask "what makes this strong?", and distill student language into 4–6 criteria.
  3. Have learners plan their project against the criteria before starting work.
  4. During production, schedule check-ins where students self-assess drafts against the chart and revise.
  5. On submission, students grade their own work against the criteria and write a reflective justification citing specific evidence from their project.
  6. Teacher grades independently, then reconciles discrepancies through feedback or a brief conference; treat large gaps as teaching moments about evaluative judgment.

Related Strategies¶

  • Academic Choice (planning, working, reflecting) — shares the autonomy-plus-reflection structure; this strategy applies it to summative projects
  • Rubrics and criteria charts — the assessment infrastructure this strategy depends on
  • Give learners control over how learning is measured via assessment choices and science-fair-type projects

Related Elements¶

  • Assess Performance — self-grading is a form of performance assessment turned inward
  • Provide Feedback — teacher moderation and discrepancy conferences supply the external feedback loop
  • Articulation — the reflective justification is an articulation task that makes reasoning explicit
  • Check-in — mid-project conferences keep self-assessment formative rather than end-loaded

Examples¶

  • Expeditionary Learning / EL Education (https://eleducation.org) — crew-level "quality work" protocols in which students co-construct criteria, critique drafts, and reflect on their work against standards.
  • Ron Berger's "Audience and Authenticity" projects (e.g., An Ethic of Excellence) — students revise work against peer- and teacher-negotiated criteria before public exhibition.
  • A high school history unit where students choose a research paper, podcast, or museum exhibit, all graded on a single co-built criteria chart; each student submits a self-grade plus a paragraph citing two pieces of evidence per criterion.
