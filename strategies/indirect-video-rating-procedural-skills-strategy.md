# Use video-based 'indirect' rating as a resource-sparing alternative to on-site expert rating of procedural skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article evaluates rating trainees' performances via video recordings instead of requiring an on-site expert, to avoid the presence of an expert during training. Results suggest "equivalence for ‘direct’ and ‘indirect’ ratings", so video rating can meaningfully substitute or supplement on-site rating for procedures like chest tube insertion, reducing the need for an expert to be physically present.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video recording of the training performance; independent expert raters viewing the recordings
#### Constraints
- The authors recommend averaging the scores of an on-site 'direct' rater and at least one 'indirect' rater to obtain the most accurate results, given remaining differences between raters

### Target Learners
- medical students
- surgical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- objective assessment of procedural and technical skills
