# Use simulation during device deployment to identify unexpected error-prone situations and involve caregivers in risk assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Beyond training, the article positions simulation as a risk-assessment tool during device implementation: it reveals which situations require dedicated training support and surfaces errors the expert team did not anticipate. The authors report that two errors "had not been expected, illustrating the need to involve caregivers in risk assessment and demonstrating suitability for use". They also state simulation can be useful for both providing a training model and identifying situations requiring dedicated training support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An evaluation grid built by a multi-disciplinary expert group after in-depth training on the new device
- A scenario reproducing the device's routine clinical tasks
#### Constraints
- Caregivers may be less rigorous about asepsis in simulation than in clinical practice, as the article notes they perceive less value in applying themselves to this parameter

### Target Learners
- ICU nurses
- clinical teams adopting new medical devices

### Target Learning Goals
- Identification of device use errors before clinical deployment
- Risk assessment for new device implementation
