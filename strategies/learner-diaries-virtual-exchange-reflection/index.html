Use learner diaries at key stages during virtual exchange to prompt reflection¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The EVALUATE study used four online learner diary entries at key stages during the exchange, with prompts "focussing on the telecollaborative experience and its relationship to the development of digital-pedagogical competences and intercultural competence". Diaries were completed after each task and before the post-test, capturing students' evolving reflections on intercultural encounters, technology use, and collaboration challenges.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Diary prompts must be open-ended and tied to specific stages of the exchange.

Constraints¶

  • Response rates declined across diary entries: 438 completed Diary 1, 374 Diary 2, 277 Diary 3, and 385 Diary 4.

Target Learners¶

  • Student teachers in initial teacher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reflection on intercultural and digital learning experiences

Claims¶

  • Web-based SRL training combined with a learning diary (Group TD) produced statistically significant pre-post gains in SRL knowledge, the SRL overall score and self-efficacy, but not in mathematics scores, among prospective university students in an online preparation course [+M]
  • A daily learning diary alone (Group D) did not produce statistically significant pre-post gains on any measured outcome in an online mathematics preparation course [-M]
  • Keeping a structured learning diary without further intervention did not improve university students' self-regulation [-M]
  • In a randomized trial of SRL interventions in an online mathematics preparation course, group and time interacted significantly on SRL knowledge, self-efficacy and the SRL overall score, with Group TDP (training + diary + peer feedback) showing the most prominent gains [+W]
