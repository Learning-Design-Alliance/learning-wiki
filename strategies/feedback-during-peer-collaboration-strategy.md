# Provide feedback when children collaborate without adult confirmation, since regression may be limited to no-feedback situations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's cautionary discussion recommends attending to feedback when arranging peer collaboration. In this study children received no confirmation or disconfirmation of predictions, and the author notes that "the regression found in this study may be limited to situations in which children are not provided with any type of feedback", citing related work in which children given feedback showed no regression. The implementable implication is that collaborative sessions should include some independent confirmation of agreed answers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Differences of opinion resolved solely on the verbal plane, without independent confirmation, are the risk condition
#### Constraints
- The regression found may be limited to no-feedback situations; the supporting evidence comes from this study and related work it cites

### Target Learners
- children aged 5 to 9 working on balance-beam problems

### Target Learning Goals
- rule-based reasoning about balance-beam problems
