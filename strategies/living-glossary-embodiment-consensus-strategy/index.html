Develop a community-maintained living glossary of embodiment terminology on an open-science platform¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors call for interdisciplinary working groups and a white-paper-style consensus process to develop "a living glossary (and, where appropriate, an open ontology) hosted on an open-science platform" such as a version-controlled repository like GitHub or OSF. The resource should use open contribution workflows, provide citable versioned releases, record synonyms, exclusions, and hierarchical relations in machine-readable form, and include epistemic provenance metadata, scope notes, and mapping fields for non-equivalent terms.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Governance should be explicitly inclusive, with contribution and review pathways involving scholars with expertise in non-Western and Indigenous traditions; updates should remain community-governed and review-based

Constraints¶

  • A consensus conference is useful primarily as a catalyst to establish initial structure, governance, and release standards, not as a one-off product

Target Learners¶

  • embodiment scholars across disciplines
  • newcomers to the field
  • AI tool developers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • cross-disciplinary evidence synthesis and faithful AI-assisted literature integration

Claims¶

  • Embodiment research lacks a shared vocabulary, with over 20 distinct labels for embodiment-related concepts appearing in the last decade [+W]
  • Loose use of movement-based learning language risks conflating evidence-based embodied learning with debunked neuromyths [+W]
  • Fragmented vocabularies bias AI-mediated literature retrieval toward the user's home discipline [+W]
  • Inconsistent embodiment labels cause functionally similar experiments to go uncited across disciplines, hindering meta-analysis [+W]
  • Educational ontologies are rarely used in practical e-learning platforms, mainly due to cost, complexity, integration, performance, and scalability problems, which ontology reuse can partially overcome [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Foster communities of practice for AI governance instead of rigid top-down policies
  • Attach a minimal terminology statement (term, operational definition, hypothesized pathway) to each study
