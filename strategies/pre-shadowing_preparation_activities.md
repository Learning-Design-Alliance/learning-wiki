# Pre-Shadowing Preparation Activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Pre-shadowing preparation is a set of activities completed before an educator shadows a student for a day. It typically includes: (1) writing focused learning goals for the shadow day; (2) selecting a student whose experience can illuminate those goals; (3) coordinating consent and logistics with the student, family, and staff; and (4) explicitly listing one's assumptions so the day can be approached with "beginner's eyes." Preparation converts an unstructured visit into a purposeful observation.

## Design Implications

Preparation works because it directs limited attention: a full school day contains far more sensory and social information than an observer can process, so advance goals function as an advance organizer that filters what gets noticed [Advance organizers improve learning.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]. Surfacing assumptions before observation reduces confirmation bias — observers who have articulated a prior belief are better positioned to notice disconfirming evidence rather than silently filtering it [Cognitive disequilibrium motivates conceptual change.](../claims/cognitive-disequilibrium-motivates-conceptual-change.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Approximately one hour of preparation time
- Templates or worksheets for goal setting and assumption listing
- Consent and coordination with the student, parents/guardians, and staff
- A debrief plan for after the shadow day ([Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md) or structured [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md))

#### Constraints
- Coordination can fail or stall; an unwilling student or family undermines the experience [-W]
- Overly narrow goals can tunnel vision the observer, causing them to miss unanticipated but important phenomena [~M]
- Assumption-questioning exercises done superficially (listing assumptions without genuinely holding them loosely) can paradoxically strengthen confirmation bias [~W]
- Ethical risk: shadowing a minor requires genuine informed consent, not just administrative clearance [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Goal focus varies: engagement, college and career readiness, experiences of English Learners, transitions between classes, or social dynamics
- Some programs (e.g., [Shadow a Student Challenge](https://shadowastudent.org)) pair the educator with a random student; others use deliberate selection to target a population of concern
- Preparation can be individual or done in a cohort with shared goal-setting and post-shadow [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)

### Target Learners
- Educators and school leaders participating in student shadowing to gain insight into student experience
- Especially valuable for administrators who have been out of the classroom long enough that their mental model of a student's day is outdated [Activation improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Teachers preparing to shadow in a different grade level, department, or school context

### Target Learning Goals
- Empathic understanding of the student experience ([Building Empathy](../principles/building-empathy.md))
- Perspective-taking and bias awareness
- Generating grounded, actionable insights for school improvement

### Instructions
1. **Set learning goals.** Write 1–3 focused questions or goals for the day (e.g., "How does an EL student experience unstructured time?") — [State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md)
2. **Select a student.** Choose a student whose schedule, grade level, and experiences can answer those goals; consider students whose perspective differs from your default assumptions
3. **Coordinate.** Obtain student assent and parent consent; brief the student's teachers and relevant staff so the day runs normally
4. **Question assumptions.** List what you currently believe about this student's experience and commit to testing each belief rather than confirming it — [Cognitive Conflict](../elements/cognitive-conflict.md)
5. **Plan the debrief.** Schedule immediate post-shadow [Individual Reflection](../elements/individual-reflection.md) while observations are fresh
