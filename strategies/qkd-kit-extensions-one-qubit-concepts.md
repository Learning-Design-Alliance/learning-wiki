# Extend the QKD analogy kit to broader one-qubit quantum concepts by re-labeling mounts and adding optical elements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend re-labeling the wave-plate mounts from QKD language to quantum-computing language and adding household or inexpensive optical elements to explore a wider range of quantum concepts with the same apparatus. They state: "By re-labeling the wave-plate mounts from the language of QKD (polarization states and bases) to the language of quantum computing (unitary operations and projection operators), other experiments are possible." Suggested extensions include the Elitzur-Vaidman bomb paradox, non-commutativity of Pauli operators, projective measurement effects, variable-retardance wave plates from plastic food wrap, calcite crystals, optically active solutions, and quantitative Malus' law exploration with the automated kits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The existing QKD kit apparatus with re-labelable wave-plate mounts
- Slightly birefringent household materials like plastic food wrap, or added elements like calcite crystals and optically active solutions, for the extended experiments
#### Constraints
- The kit at its core acts as an analogy for a one-qubit quantum device, so extensions are limited to one-qubit protocols and concepts

### Target Learners
- high-school students
- undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- unitary operations
- projective measurements
- non-commutativity of Pauli operators
- Elitzur-Vaidman bomb paradox
- Malus' law
