# Use alternative dissemination formats (blogs, plain-language summaries, graphical and video abstracts) to connect education research with practitioners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors conclude that alternative methods of sharing research, such as non-peer-reviewed publications and conference talks, "can be an effective method of connecting re-search with practitioners", and recommend the community build on these efforts. They point to plain language summaries being more effective than original abstracts at producing comprehension, feeling of understanding, and enjoyment, and to graphical abstracts, video abstracts, and Twitter threads gaining traction, while noting these are restricted to a limited number of journals and researchers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Writing style must adapt to a broadened audience including STEM educators, not only sub-field researchers
- Future work should integrate these dissemination methods into publication and award structures such as tenure and promotion
#### Constraints
- Alternative approaches are currently restricted to a limited number of journals and researchers

### Target Learners
- K-12 and university STEM educators
- DBER researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- accessing and applying education research findings in teaching practice
