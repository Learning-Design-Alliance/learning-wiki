# Use spaced repetition sessions to mitigate skill decay in high-acuity, low-occurrence procedural skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's pilot curriculum indicates that a didactics-simulation model with spaced repetition — training separated into discrete sessions with measurable intervals — can counter skill decay in rarely performed procedures. One spaced-repetition simulator session one month after a six-month washout raised mean scores 23% (95% CI 9–37%) and reduced decay from 41% to 19%, though scores did not return to baseline.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Criterion-based proficiency must first be established through simulation-based education before decay can be mitigated by spaced repetition
#### Constraints
- The article states further work is necessary to define optimal repetition intervals, and a single session did not fully reverse decay

### Target Learners
- Emergency physicians and other clinicians maintaining rarely used procedural skills

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of procedural and image-acquisition skills between training and clinical use
