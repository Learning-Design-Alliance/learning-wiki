Collect and evaluate long-term outcomes, strengthen measurement, and report study features consistently in early intervention research¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors' concluding recommendation is that the field needs "More research is needed with collection and evaluation of long-term outcomes, a focus on measurement, and consistency of primary study reporting." The descriptive synthesis revealed gaps including a notable lack of long-term measurement for children with etiologies other than autism or DLD, such as Down syndrome, and inconsistent reporting of race/ethnicity, home languages, and caregiver characteristics. The article also suggests researchers and clinicians might decide to maximize postintervention outcomes specifically for outcome categories that are more vulnerable to fade-out over time.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Long-term outcome assessments must occur at least 3 months after the end of intervention, the inclusion threshold this review used.

Constraints¶

  • The article states the time-to-follow-up analysis was limited to 20 studies and 129 effect sizes, with many outcome measures (n = 67) having a single long-term data point, so conclusions about durability require additional longitudinal research.

Target Learners¶

  • infants, toddlers, and preschoolers ages 0–5 with language impairments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • language and social communication outcomes, including prelinguistic skills and expressive and receptive language

Claims¶

  • For linguistic outcomes, long-term effects were larger for children with DLD (g = .36, marginal) than for autistic children (g = .01, nonsignificant) [+W]
  • Posttest effect sizes significantly predict long-term effect sizes for both prelinguistic and linguistic outcomes [+W]
  • Long-term intervention effects are larger for prelinguistic outcomes (g = .36) than for linguistic outcomes (g = .14, nonsignificant) [+W]
  • Time between intervention and follow-up did not significantly predict long-term effect sizes [+W]
