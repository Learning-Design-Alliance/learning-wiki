# Emotion Emojis

## Description
Students express how they are feeling by drawing or sharing an emoji. Volunteers then share their emoji and explain how it reflects their current feelings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Emojis (drawn or printed), time for individual reflection and whole-class sharing
#### Constraints
- Some students may not be familiar with emojis or may not feel comfortable expressing their feelings through them. It is important to provide a variety of emojis to choose from and to create a safe and supportive environment where students feel comfortable sharing.

### Target Learners

- All learner levels, especially K–12

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Express feelings in a non-verbal way, encourage self-awareness, promote sharing and empathy

### Affordances
- Provides a fun and engaging way for students to express their feelings, especially for those who may have difficulty articulating their emotions verbally. Normalizes talking about feelings and empowers students to view their emotions as assets.

### Personalization
- Students can choose to draw their own emojis or select from a pre-made sheet. Instructors can provide a variety of emojis to choose from, representing different emotions.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe student participation and engagement in the activity. Collect student feedback on the activity to assess its effectiveness.

## Impact
- The simple act of acknowledging our emotions helps calm the amygdala and reduce the intensity of the emotions we feel. By naming our emotions, we feel more in control. We are less likely to experience an emotional hijack when we name our feelings, and students can create space to make intentional choices about how to move forward.
