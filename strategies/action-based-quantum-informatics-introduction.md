# Use action-based, unplugged activities to introduce quantum information and quantum key distribution

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
For younger or less advanced students, the article recommends enactive approaches: role-playing games where students act as qubits and scientists to explain superposition and entanglement, a pen&paper experience of the BB84 protocol, and building and using the Qeygen analog machine. With the Qeygen machine, "students can exchange a key using the BB84 protocol, simulate an eavesdropper, and experience the possibilities and limitations of quantum key distribution".
