# Explicit Strategy Instruction

## Description
Explicitly teaching strategies involves providing learners with specific tools and techniques to solve problems and improve their learning. It includes identifying the strategies, demonstrating how to use them, and providing opportunities for practice and feedback. It goes beyond just knowing the strategies and ensures that learners understand how, when, and why to use them effectively. This approach can help learners become more self-directed and better equipped to handle complex tasks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires explicit instruction, modeling, and opportunities for practice and feedback. Educators need to be knowledgeable about effective strategies and able to guide learners in their use.
#### Constraints
- May be time-consuming and require significant planning. Some learners may struggle to apply strategies in new contexts.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Equip learners with specific strategies for problem-solving and learning, improve their ability to apply strategies effectively, and foster self-directed learning.

### Affordances
- Provides learners with concrete tools and techniques, improves their ability to monitor and regulate their learning, and increases their confidence in tackling challenges.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to different content areas and learning styles. Strategies can be tailored to individual learner needs and preferences.

## Examples
- An educator gives students a math problem that will make many of them feel stuck, and then guides them through the process of getting unstuck, helping them name their process and adding to a list of strategies. For instance, if a student says he wrote the information from the math problem down and points to a chart, the teacher will say: “Oh that’s interesting. You pulled the important information from the problem out and organized it into a chart.”
