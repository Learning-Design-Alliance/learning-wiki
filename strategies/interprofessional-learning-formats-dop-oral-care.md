# Interprofessional learning formats connecting dental students with nursing and geriatric care programmes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because oral hygiene routines, early detection of oral problems and access organisation are often delivered by nursing staff and caregivers rather than dentists, the article recommends that curricula create structured interfaces with nursing and geriatric care programmes. It states: "Interprofessional learning formats, such as joint simulation sessions, shared case discussions and outreach activities in long‐term care facilities, may strengthen communication, mutual understanding of roles and continuity of oral healthcare within the broader care network."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration with nursing, geriatric medicine, social care and allied health professionals
#### Constraints
- Such formats address care pathway gaps that cannot be addressed by dental training alone

### Target Learners
- Dental students and learners in nursing and geriatric care programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- Communication, mutual understanding of roles and continuity of oral healthcare within the care network
