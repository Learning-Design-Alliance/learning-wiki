# Use negative controls in registry-based studies to detect unobserved confounding

## Description
The article highlights "the use of negative controls in registry-based studies, where often a wide range of variables at different time points is available". Negative control analyses (and proximal causal inference) were deployed in supplementary sensitivity analyses to probe whether unmeasured confounding could explain the small estimated medication effect.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Registry data with a wide range of variables at different time points, allowing plausible negative control exposures or outcomes to be constructed
#### Constraints
- Unobserved confounding was detected only for the reading domain in this study's negative control analysis

### Target Learners
- children diagnosed with ADHD

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement on standardized national tests
