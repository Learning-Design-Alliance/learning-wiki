# Introduce measurement uncertainty through dispersion of repeated measurements plus a one-variable derivative propagation recipe

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A two-hour session at the beginning of the course introduces uncertainties through the dispersion of repeated measurements of a distance with a ruler, leaving systematic uncertainties aside. Students express results as a range in which the true value has great probability to be found, and a recipe relates the uncertainty on a dependent quantity to that on the independent variable through the absolute derivative of the function relating them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need not know partial derivatives; the recipe is restricted to quantities depending on one measurable variable
#### Constraints
- A rigorous treatment of uncertainties is beyond the scope of a first-year course, and generalization to multiple variables is not attempted

### Target Learners
- First-year university students without advanced mathematical background

### Target Learning Goals
- Grasping the uncertain nature of any measurement
- Propagating uncertainties in one-variable experiments
