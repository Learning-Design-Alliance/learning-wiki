# Favor instructive over restrictive parental mediation to reduce adolescents' cyberhate victimization risk

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that parents adopt instructive mediation — communicating regularly with children about online activity, encouraging them to discuss problems, and jointly establishing rules without intrusion — rather than restrictive control. The authors state: "Our results suggest that parents tend to use instructive mediation strategies over-restrictive – and perhaps even avoid restrictive mediation altogether." Regarding cyberhate, instructive mediation may include discussions of free speech versus hate speech and developing children's critical thinking about online information.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Parents communicate regularly with children about online behavior and jointly establish rules without being intrusive
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional, so temporal ordering of mediation and victimization cannot be established

### Target Learners
- Adolescents aged 12-18
- Parents of adolescent Internet users

### Target Learning Goals
- Managing cyberhate experiences
- Critical attitudes toward sharing private information online
- Distinguishing free speech from hate speech
