# Provide clear, unambiguous feedback to younger adolescents and train the ability to identify patterns in noisy information

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article derives recommendations for educational settings from its findings on younger adolescents' reversal learning. It states that younger adolescents particularly struggled to discern valid from invalid feedback, that "Clear, unambiguous feedback may be crucial for their learning", and that training the ability to identify patterns in noisy information could be a valuable educational focus for this age group. It further recommends calibrating task demands to children's and adolescents' developmental capacities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Task demands should be calibrated to children's and adolescents' developmental capacities, as the article recommends for both research and educational contexts.
#### Constraints
- The article notes improvements in reversal learning during early adolescence may be limited to certain tasks that are tailored in a specific way.

### Target Learners
- Children and younger adolescents (around 10 to 14 years)

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning stimulus-outcome associations from feedback
- Discerning valid from invalid feedback
- Executive flexibility
