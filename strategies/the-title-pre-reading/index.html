The Title (Pre-Reading)¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 5 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 2 associational, 1 causal), q2–q4 · 2 of 5 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Learners are shown only the title of an upcoming reading and asked to form small groups to pool what they already know about the topic and predict what the text will cover. Each group then shares its ideas with the whole class, and the facilitator records them for later comparison with the actual text. The activity activates relevant background knowledge, surfaces misconceptions, and gives learners a purpose for reading.

Design Implications¶

The strategy works because comprehension depends on connecting new text to existing knowledge structures; a brief activation activity before reading improves recall and inference-making from text Advance organizers improve learning and retention of meaningful material. [+M]. The group format adds value beyond individual brainstorming: hearing peers' associations exposes learners to knowledge they lack and to plausible misconceptions the facilitator can address before reading. The sharing phase should end with explicit framing — what the class knows, what it is unsure about — so learners read to resolve genuine questions rather than passively.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A title that is informative enough to cue relevant knowledge without giving away the text's argument
  • Small groups (3–5) with a recorder, and a visible whole-class record (board or shared doc) of pooled ideas
  • A brief post-reading return to the recorded predictions so learners compare them against the text (Class Discussion)

Constraints¶

  • A vague or overly clever title cues the wrong schema and wastes the activation opportunity [~M]
  • Learners with little topical knowledge contribute little and may encode peers' guesses as fact; facilitators should label speculation explicitly [-M]
  • Prior knowledge does not guarantee better outcomes — inaccurate or confidently held misconceptions activated before reading can interfere with comprehension Prior knowledge is not always related to better performance. [~M]
  • If the pooled ideas are never revisited after reading, the activity becomes an isolated warm-up with little durable effect

Implementation Variability¶

  • Add a prediction task: each group writes three statements the text will probably make, then verifies them while reading
  • Use a KWL-style record (Know / Want to know / Learned) to structure the pre- and post-reading comparison
  • For second-language learners, allow bilingual brainstorming so knowledge isn't limited by target-language vocabulary
  • Run it asynchronously in online courses using a shared document or discussion thread before the reading is released

Target Learners¶

  • Learners who have some topical knowledge but need help retrieving it before reading [+M]
  • Mixed-ability groups benefit most: pooling distributes knowledge that no single learner holds alone
  • Less effective for complete novices, who generate few associations; pair with a short advance organizer or image instead

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading comprehension: building a schema for the text before encountering it
  • Prediction and inference: forming hypotheses and checking them against evidence
  • Metacognitive monitoring: noticing what one does and does not know before reading

Instructions¶

  1. Display only the title of the reading; do not distribute the text yet.
  2. Form small groups and ask them to list everything they know or associate with the topic and predict the text's main points (Activation).
  3. Have each group share with the whole class while the facilitator records all ideas publicly (Class Discussion).
  4. Highlight disagreements and open questions; frame these as the purpose for reading.
  5. Learners read the text, then return to the record to confirm, revise, or reject the pooled ideas — asking them to explain the discrepancies strengthens conceptual learning Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding. [+M].

Related Strategies¶

  • Activating Prior Knowledge — the broader family; The Title is a minimal, text-anchored variant
  • Anticipation Guides — a structured pre-reading variant using agree/disagree statements instead of open brainstorming
  • KWL Charts — extends the activity across the full reading cycle with a "Learned" phase

Examples¶

  • Before reading an article titled "The History of Jazz Music," learners brainstorm in groups what they know about jazz's origins and cultural impact, then check their predictions against the article.
  • In a science course, the title "Why the Sky Is Blue" prompts groups to pool ideas about light and scattering; the facilitator surfaces the common misconception that the sky reflects the ocean before learners read.
