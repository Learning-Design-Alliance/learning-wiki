# Pace AI workshops to meet all students' needs, providing written extension tutorials for advanced students while keeping a low barrier to entry

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that future AI workshops "pay speciﬁc attention to students’ pacing needs, providing extension opportunities for advanced students, while still providing a low barrier to entry." In practice, the facilitators sped up the pace after teacher feedback and created Zoom breakout rooms for students falling behind, and provided written tutorials so advanced students could go ahead and add extensions such as new intents. Daily teacher debrief feedback (e.g., Zoom polls to assess progress, asking students to unmute and demonstrate their agents) was integrated into the workshops as they progressed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Daily teacher feedback channels to adjust pacing during the workshop
- Instructor support ratios sufficient for breakout-room catch-up support
#### Constraints
- Pacing feedback was mixed: some teachers and students appreciated the slow pace as helpful for learning, while others felt it reduced engagement

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in grades 6-12 with varied programming experience

### Target Learning Goals
- AI literacy and conversational AI concepts
- Programming skills through app and skill building
