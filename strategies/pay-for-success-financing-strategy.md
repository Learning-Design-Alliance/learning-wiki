# Use Pay for Success (Social Impact Bond) financing to scale an evidence-based prevention program

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Pay for Success financing loans funds from private and philanthropic investors to public jurisdictions to expand programs, with repayment contingent on independently evaluated outcomes generating cost savings. The article used it to expand CPC in Chicago Public Schools: Goldman Sachs, Northern Trust, and the Pritzker Family Foundation provided "$17 million in loans" to serve "an additional 2,600 children over the next four years". Economic evaluation determines program suitability and the magnitude of success payments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An independent evaluation must determine whether contract metrics are met before the government makes success payments to investors.
#### Constraints
- The article states the scope of evaluation is limited to contract metrics: no assessment of implementation fidelity is planned, and savings from child maltreatment and juvenile arrest outcomes were excluded because counties and the state declined to participate, so savings are likely underestimated.

### Target Learners
- Low-income children ages 3 to 9 in preschool through third grade

### Target Learning Goals
- School readiness for kindergarten
- Grade 3 literacy proficiency
- Prevention of special education placement
