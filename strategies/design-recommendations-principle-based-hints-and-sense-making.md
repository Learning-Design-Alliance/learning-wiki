# ITS developers should continue to provide principle-based and bottom-out hints and support sense making around them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on its theoretical analysis and review, the article recommends that ITS developers keep providing principle-based on-demand hints, since they "may support useful sense making, even if their effectiveness may vary by domain and by student". Bottom-out hints are also needed to help students get unstuck and offer opportunities for spontaneous self-explanation. The article further recommends supporting sense making explicitly, e.g., prompting self-explanations after errors, exploring self-explanation support for bottom-out hints, contingent tutoring, and hints whose interactivity matches learner characteristics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hints should abstractly characterize the problem-solving knowledge, state the action, its conditions, the justifying principle, and briefly how it applies
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness of hints may vary by domain and by student; hint writing is a balancing act against keeping hints short; no rigorous experimental test of replacing hints with self-explanation prompts exists

### Target Learners
- Students learning with step-based intelligent tutoring systems

### Target Learning Goals
- Domain-level problem-solving skill and conceptual understanding
