# Design future developmental observational-learning studies to manipulate social context, direct versus indirect observation, and active versus passive learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose three design recommendations for future developmental studies of observational learning: distinguishing "indirect" observational learning (observing others' choices and outcomes in absence of the other player) from direct observational learning in the player's presence; comparing active individual learning with purely passive observational learning without immediate trial-by-trial evaluation, which "allows for a more direct comparison of both learning processes based on a similar amount of information"; and addressing how task difficulty and intelligence relate to age differences, since children showed higher age-normed intelligence scores.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Future studies should measure or control intelligence, as children showed higher age-normed IQ scores than adults and intelligence may influence performance and neural activation
#### Constraints
- The current task design cannot fully separate stochasticity from exploration, and the additional third condition and faster timing of prior EEG paradigms may explain discrepant findings

### Target Learners
- children (8–10 years)
- young adults (18–20 years)

### Target Learning Goals
- observational reinforcement learning
- learning from others' choices and outcomes
