# Strengthen GBT residency training with dedicated curricula, entrustment assessments, and simulation phantoms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that GBT training programs establish comprehensive dedicated courses, a standardized assessment process aligned with training content, and simulation phantoms, because residents lack operational opportunities within limited rotation time. It reports that "93.94% (124/132) of residents believed that simulation phantoms should be used for training purposes" and 91.67% supported dedicated GBT courses. The strategy draws on competency-based medical education and entrustable professional activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A dedicated assessment process aligned with training content
- A comprehensive GBT curriculum special for training
- Simulation phantoms for practice where patient opportunities are limited
#### Constraints
- Based on self-reported survey data from Chinese training bases; the authors note small sample size and selection bias, and that only self-evaluation indicators were employed

### Target Learners
- radiation oncology residents in standardized residency training

### Target Learning Goals
- independent performance of gynecological brachytherapy procedures
- competence in target delineation, dose evaluation, and applicator placement
