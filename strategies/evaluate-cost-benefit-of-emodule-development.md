# Evaluate the cost–benefit of developing eModules and consider curating existing web resources or blended models

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because learning efficacy did not differ between the interactive eModule and the passive webpage, the article recommends that medical educators "evaluate if the costs of developing and utilizing eModules are justified". Alternatives include "curating and vetting already existing web-based learning resources", and the authors suggest the most successful approach for both efficacy and satisfaction "would be to take a blended model that mixes CAL with traditional neuroscience learning methods".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators must know the financial and human resources available and whether to target efficacy or student enjoyability
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a single small trial (32 students) that may have been underpowered to detect a small true difference in quiz scores

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- Medical educators designing curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical neuroscience and neuroanatomy learning
- Self-directed learning motivation
