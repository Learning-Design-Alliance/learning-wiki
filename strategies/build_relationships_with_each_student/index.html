Build Relationships with Each Student¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 7 studies (4 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 1 of 7 report an effect size

Description¶

Building relationships with each student means deliberately establishing trust, care, and mutual respect so that learners feel known, valued, and capable of success. In practice this involves showing interest in students' welfare, respecting their perspectives, communicating high expectations, knowing their academic and social needs, and recognizing their achievements. It is carried out through frequent low-stakes interactions — greetings, Check-Ins, personalized feedback, and informal conversations — sustained over time.

Design Implications¶

Teacher–student relationship quality is one of the most consistent correlates of engagement and achievement across K–12 and higher education Teacher-student relationship quality predicts engagement and achievement. [+S]. Relationships are not a substitute for good instruction; they function as the relational substrate that makes feedback, challenge, and productive struggle tolerable — students take intellectual risks from teachers they trust [~M]. Brief, structured relational practices (e.g., empathic discipline framing) can shift outcomes even when time is scarce Brief empathic discipline intervention reduces suspensions. [+M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Time and routines for individual contact: greetings, conferences, Check-Ins
  • Genuine consistency — trust accrues from repeated, reliable behavior, not one-off gestures
  • Knowledge of students' backgrounds, interests, and academic needs, gathered systematically rather than assumed
  • Pairing warmth with high expectations; warmth without challenge reads as low regard

Constraints¶

  • Relationships cannot compensate for absent or incoherent instruction; students detect warmth used as a management substitute [~M]
  • Effects weaken or reverse when students perceive teacher closeness as inconsistent or unfair, particularly for adolescents with prior negative school experiences [~M]
  • Requires sustained effort; gains erode when relational practices stop, and one teacher's effort is diluted by a school-wide climate of distrust [-W]
  • Performatively "caring" behaviors without follow-through can damage trust more than no gesture at all [-W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • 2×10 strategy: two minutes of non-academic conversation for ten consecutive days with a disengaged student
  • 5-Minute Writing Conferences — brief individual conferences that combine relationship-building with feedback on work
  • Belonging brief interventions: structured activities that frame adversity as common and temporary, improving outcomes for at-risk students Belonging interventions improve outcomes for marginalized students. [+S]
  • Autonomy-supportive teaching: offering choice and rationale, which students experience as respect Autonomy support fosters intrinsic motivation. [+S]

Target Learners¶

  • Students at risk of disengagement — those with challenging life circumstances, prior school failure, or low sense of belonging Belonging interventions improve outcomes for marginalized students. [+S]
  • Adolescents, for whom perceived teacher support strongly predicts engagement; effects are weaker when relationships are perceived as controlling rather than supportive [~M]
  • All learners benefit, but relationship-building is highest-leverage at transitions (new school, first-year, returning adults)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Affective and motivational outcomes: engagement, persistence, belonging
  • Behavioral outcomes: cooperation, attendance, reduced disciplinary incidents Brief empathic discipline intervention reduces suspensions. [+M]
  • Enabling conditions for academic goals: relationships make Feedback and rigorous Assessment more effective because students trust the source

Instructions¶

  1. Learn and use every student's name and one personal detail within the first week; greet students at the door.
  2. Run regular Check-Ins — brief structured prompts that surface how students are doing academically and personally.
  3. Hold short individual conferences (see 5-Minute Writing Conferences) that pair personal connection with feedback on actual work.
  4. Communicate high expectations explicitly: tell students they can succeed and explain what success requires.
  5. Use Active Listening in one-on-one interactions — reflect back what the student says before responding.
  6. Recognize achievement publicly and privately, calibrated to what each student values.
  7. When discipline is needed, frame it empathically — private, perspective-taking, oriented to the student's goals Brief empathic discipline intervention reduces suspensions. [+M].

Related Strategies¶

  • Active Listening — the conversational skill that makes individual interactions feel genuine rather than procedural
  • 5-Minute Writing Conferences — a time-efficient structure combining relationship and feedback
  • Activating Prior Knowledge — knowing students' backgrounds enables instruction that connects to them
  • Teachers should learn about students' lives and commitments outside school and build relationships, which students say increases respect, effort, and engagement
  • Personalize the learning environment and instructional process to foster belonging

Examples¶

  • 2×10 relationship strategy — a teacher spends two minutes of non-academic conversation with a targeted student for ten consecutive days; widely used in positive behavior support implementations.
  • Responsive Classroom — published K–8 approach in which daily morning meetings and teacher language protocols institutionalize relationship-building routines.
  • First-year seminar programs — many universities assign instructors to small cohorts with required one-on-one early-semester meetings, improving retention for first-generation students.
