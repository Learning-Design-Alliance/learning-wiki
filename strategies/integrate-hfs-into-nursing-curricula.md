# Integrate high-fidelity simulation-based learning into undergraduate nursing curricula for cardiac auscultation training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating simulation-based learning into nursing curricula to bridge the gap between theoretical knowledge and real-world clinical practice. The article reports that high-fidelity simulation "allows students to practice interactive clinical scenarios with high level of safe and risk-free" and that the study "highlights the value of integrating simulation-based learning into nursing curricula". The recommendation rests on the study's randomized comparison showing significantly higher post-test knowledge, skill, and overall scores for simulator-trained students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have completed the health assessment course and have clinical training experience, as the participants did
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single nursing faculty with 60 students in only 2 groups; post-test skills were not re-assessed, so long-term retention is unexamined
- The knowledge test lacked formal validity and reliability testing

### Target Learners
- Fourth-year and internship-level undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- Cardiac auscultation knowledge
- Cardiac auscultation physical assessment skills
