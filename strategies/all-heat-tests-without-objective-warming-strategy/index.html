Before concluding that suggestibility varies from test to test, try heat tests given entirely without objective warming of the coil¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The reviewer proposes a specific methodological variation for testing general suggestibility: administer the heat-illusion trials with the coil never actually heated. This would test whether reported warmth under no objective warming reflects a general disposition to accept suggestion. The reviewer writes that "this variation in method should be tried before it is finally concluded that the suggestibility of an observer varies from 'test to test."'

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A concealed switch arrangement allowing the experimenter to shunt current from the resistance coil without disturbing illumination from the lamps, as described in Scott's apparatus.

Constraints¶

  • The reviewer frames this as a variation that should be tried before a conclusion is drawn, not as a tested manipulation in the reviewed study.

Target Learners¶

  • adult college students in laboratory psychology experiments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • measuring individual suggestibility as a general faculty versus task-specific suggestibility

Claims¶

  • Suggestibility scores on an after-image test and a warmth-illusion test correlated insignificantly in the same group of college students [~W]
