# Facilitator-led pedagogical intervention pairing slow learners with proficient peers and giving feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a facilitator role, following Littlewood's conceptualisation of the teacher as "facilitator of learning" with sub-roles as classroom manager, consultant, adviser and co-communicator. Concrete moves include pairing inhibited slow learners with enthusiastic proficient learners, demonstrating tasks with volunteers at the start of each task, correcting ill-constructed sentences and mispronunciation, having students read aloud for pronunciation, and giving feedback after each activity so students feel they accomplished a challenging task.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A teacher willing to take on facilitator, consultant and co-communicator roles, and quality of interaction in groups, since Ellis (2003) is cited that it is not enough to simply put students into groups
#### Constraints
- The article reports some slow learners still felt inhibited to participate in class interaction despite the intervention

### Target Learners
- first-year engineering undergraduates in ESL classrooms, including slow learners

### Target Learning Goals
- spoken proficiency, confidence in oral presentation, and error tolerance

### Affordances
- Tblt Speaking Development Framework
