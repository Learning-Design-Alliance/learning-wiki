# Name Pronunciation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Pronouncing learners' names correctly is a low-cost, high-signal practice that builds trust and rapport from the first interaction. Mispronunciation — or repeated failure to attempt it — can produce feelings of anxiety, invisibility, shame, and resentment that contribute to social and educational disengagement [Names carry identity and mispronunciation is experienced as a microaggression that marginalizes students of color.](https://doi.org/10.1080/10665684.2012.719878) [+M]. The strategy is to elicit each learner's preferred name and pronunciation early, record it, and practice it until correct.

## Design Implications

Getting names right is a concrete enactment of belonging: it signals that the learner is seen as an individual, which is a precondition for engagement and persistence [Brief belonging-focused practices improve outcomes for at-risk students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+S]. The instructor's effort matters as much as accuracy — a visible good-faith attempt followed by correction is received very differently from casual or repeated mispronunciation.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Elicit each learner's preferred name and pronunciation directly (never assume from spelling), ideally in a [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) or first-week survey
- A system for recording pronunciations (roster notes, phonetic spellings, audio recordings, or tools such as NameCoach)
- Willingness to be corrected publicly and to self-correct without defensiveness

#### Constraints
- Some languages contain phonemes absent from the instructor's phonological inventory, making accurate pronunciation genuinely difficult; the effort itself, not perfection, carries the relational signal
- Repeated mispronunciation after correction damages rapport more than an honest first error [-M]
- In very large classes, individual mastery is impractical; substitutes (name tents, self-introduction norms, peer name use) partially but incompletely replace it [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- First-day introductions with learners pronouncing their own names for the class
- Phonetic annotations on rosters or LMS name fields; audio name recordings (e.g., NameCoach in Canvas)
- Learner-chosen "name in use" (nickname, anglicized form) — but only when chosen by the learner, never imposed

### Target Learners
- All learners, but especially those whose names are uncommon in the instructional context — multilingual learners, international students, and students of color, who report disproportionate rates of name-based microaggression [Names carry identity and mispronunciation is experienced as a microaggression that marginalizes students of color.](https://doi.org/10.1080/10665684.2012.719878) [+M]
- Learners at risk of disengagement, for whom belonging cues are most consequential [Brief belonging-focused practices improve outcomes for at-risk students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Affective and climate goals: trust, rapport, psychological safety
- Social-emotional learning: identity affirmation and respect for difference
- Prerequisite conditions for participation-oriented goals such as [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) and collaborative work

### Instructions
1. During the first session or via a pre-course survey, ask each learner to state their name as they say it, and note a phonetic rendering.
2. Practice each name aloud before the next meeting; use spaced self-testing (retrieval practice on names) rather than rereading the roster.
3. When you err, correct immediately, apologize briefly, and move on — do not ask the learner to accept a shortened or anglicized version for your convenience.
4. Normalize correction: tell learners explicitly that you want to be corrected and will not be offended.
5. Use names consistently in [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md), feedback, and discussion to consolidate recall.

## Examples
- **NameCoach** (https://www.namecoach.com) — learners record their own name audio; recordings appear in the LMS roster so instructors and peers can hear correct pronunciation before class.
- **First-week name tents** — a common practice in K-12 and large university courses: learners write their name (with phonetic hint) on a folded card, giving the instructor a visible prompt while learning.
- **Kohli & Solórzano's teacher-education cases** — documented examples of teachers anglicizing students' names ("Angel" → "Angel-uh" for Ángel) and the resulting disengagement, used in teacher preparation to build awareness.
