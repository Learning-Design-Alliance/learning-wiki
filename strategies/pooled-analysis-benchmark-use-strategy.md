# Use pooled analysis of repeated items for benchmark comparisons across cohorts, institutions, and benchmark assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend applying pooled item analysis in three situations: "1) detecting significant differences on tests that employ repeated items; 2) comparing performance at different institutions that use a same test; 3) reporting the performance of students on benchmark assessments, beyond the Progress Test." They present the study as a blueprint for evaluating programmatic changes in educational settings, with per-item comparison also useful to detect knowledge gaps even when the pooled analysis shows no difference.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to item-level performance data from repeated or shared test items
#### Constraints
- The authors state the method is not expected to completely replace other tools, and further studies should compare it with other statistical procedures

### Target Learners
- medical students
- undergraduate students in programmatic assessment settings

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating effectiveness of curricular change
- benchmark assessment reporting
