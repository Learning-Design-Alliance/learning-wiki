# Explicit Instruction: Argumentation with Evidence

## Description
This instructional strategy involves directly teaching learners how to construct arguments and support claims with evidence, particularly in online settings. This includes providing direct links to evidence and critically evaluating sources to form well-reasoned arguments. The approach emphasizes the importance of understanding and considering opposing viewpoints to build a comprehensive and persuasive case.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to online resources, familiarity with social media platforms, and direct instruction in argumentation techniques.
#### Constraints
- May require significant time investment to teach and practice argumentation skills. Some learners may struggle with critically evaluating sources or understanding opposing viewpoints.

### Target Learners

- Middle school, high school, and college students, as well as adult learners in professional development programs.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Develop skills in constructing evidence-based arguments, enhance critical evaluation of sources, promote understanding of diverse viewpoints, and improve online communication.

### Affordances
- Promotes critical thinking, improves digital literacy, and enhances communication skills. By requiring evidence-based arguments, it reduces reliance on personal biases and encourages rational discussion.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted to different subject areas and learning styles. Scaffolds can be provided to support learners with varying levels of argumentation skills.
