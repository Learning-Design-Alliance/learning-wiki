# Design problems that require modeling, interpretation, and evaluation rather than one-step algorithmic processing

## Description
The article recommends activities addressing the neglected steps of the math-in-science model: traditional instruction provides ready-made models, lets students do mathematical manipulations, and rarely asks them to interpret results or evaluate model adequacy. The author reports finding and developing problems that address these issues, such as a Lotka-Volterra problem asking which variable is predator or prey, what the parameters represent, and whether important effects have been omitted.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must recognize what is complex in a problem for a student, which the article says is currently difficult
#### Constraints
- The article notes physics education research is only beginning to improve these issues at the introductory level, and the problems persist throughout the physics major curriculum

### Target Learners
- introductory through advanced undergraduate physics students, including intermediate mathematical methods students

### Target Learning Goals
- modeling physical systems with mathematics, interpreting mathematical results physically, and evaluating model adequacy
