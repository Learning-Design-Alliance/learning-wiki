# Pair professional-degree postgraduate-style one-to-one supervisor teaching with mini-CEX to support trainees with weaker knowledge bases

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors suggest that the supervisor-dominated one-to-one teaching mode enjoyed by PDPG trainees contributes to their better mini-CEX outcomes: each PDPG trainee has a supervisor who is usually an associate professor or professor with extensive clinical experience, providing "plenty of opportunities for PDPG trainees to improve their clinical knowledge and skills with the guidance of an experienced senior doctor." Hospital managers and trainers can extend similar senior-doctor guidance to resident trainees to optimize mini-CEX educational impact.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supervisors should be senior doctors (associate professor or professor level) with extensive clinical experience.
#### Constraints
- This factor is a proposed, untested contributor in this retrospective study; the strategy targets trainees who lack such supervision by default.

### Target Learners
- Resident standardization training trainees without an assigned one-to-one supervisor

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical knowledge and skills development measured by workplace-based assessment
