# Incorporate typing into educational strategies to alleviate handwriting-related cognitive load and support word retention for students with dyslexia

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating typing into educational strategies for students with dyslexia, because the complex graphomotor demands of handwriting may hinder novel word acquisition. It states that "Incorporating typing into educational strategies could alleviate the cognitive load associated with handwriting and enhance word retention for these students." The recommendation rests on the study's finding that dyslexic readers learned spelling and nonword-image associations better through typing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners must have access to typing as a writing modality during word-learning activities
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence comes from a study of Italian middle school students with developmental dyslexia learning nonwords paired with images

### Target Learners
- middle school students with developmental dyslexia

### Target Learning Goals
- orthographic learning of novel written words
- semantic association of novel words with referents
