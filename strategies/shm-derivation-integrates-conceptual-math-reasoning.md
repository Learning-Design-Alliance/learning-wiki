# Use the SHM derivation to integrate conceptual and mathematical reasoning in the introductory course

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A concrete teaching recipe: after deriving the SHM solution via the chain rule and integration, instructors 'have the freedom to briefly talk about the subject of non-homogeneous ordinary differential equations, followed by a further exploration of the obtained solution for the oscillations of a mass attached to a spring from the physical point of view'. Along the way, instructors can discuss the conserved quantity, explore particular initial conditions, and introduce frequency via the units of k/m.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have encountered the chain rule, inverse trigonometric functions, and integration methods in their mathematics courses
#### Constraints
- Designed for the spring-mass SHM equation in a first calculus-based introductory physics course

### Target Learners
- university students in introductory calculus-based physics

### Target Learning Goals
- connect mathematical techniques to physical concepts
- understand oscillatory motion, energy conservation, and frequency
