# Let cadets choose their own publication venue after understanding the trade-offs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Rather than defaulting every project to peer review, the program discusses possible venues in the project document and in ongoing conversations, and "cadets themselves choose the venue for publication submission." Venues include hobbyist magazines, Department of Defense Research Reports through DTIC, online repositories such as arXiv and Nature Precedings, and mainstream or undergraduate research journals. This keeps the project confined to a single semester while giving cadets ownership of the dissemination trade-offs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A range of acceptable venues including grey literature options
- Faculty and administrative review and approval mechanisms for publications
#### Constraints
- Some groups initially aspire to peer review and settle for grey literature after rejections; undergraduate research journals proved unresponsive, providing no peer reviews after many months

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate freshmen conducting original research

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding scholarly communication and publication norms
- Scientific writing for different audiences
