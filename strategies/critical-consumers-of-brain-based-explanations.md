# Use review evidence to make teachers critical consumers of brain-based explanations, rather than dropping neuroscience directly into practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend that neuroscientific findings reach classrooms and clinical practice only through a collaborative effort, positioning reliable knowledge summaries as a base for teacher training. They caution that direct implications "cannot be merely 'dropped' in practice, yet require a collaborative effort between scientists and practitioners". The strategy is to equip practitioners with scientifically grounded knowledge so they can critically evaluate brain-based claims about numeracy learning and remediation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaborative effort between scientists and practitioners to translate findings into practice
#### Constraints
- Direct implications to classroom or clinical practice cannot be merely dropped in; the review states many causal questions about the underlying brain networks remain

### Target Learners
- teacher trainees and practising teachers
- clinical practitioners supporting children with mathematical difficulties

### Target Learning Goals
- critical evaluation of brain-based explanations of numerical and arithmetic learning
- informed design of mathematics instruction and remediation
