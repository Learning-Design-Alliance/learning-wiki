# Foster help relationships in informal peer interactions and connect academically less engaged students with highly engaged peers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, given the co-evolution of friend and help relationships, "it is particularly important to foster help relationships in the informal peer interactions and motivate academically less engaged individuals to create social connections with highly engaged peers". It further suggests that "coaching educators to target helpers or other influential peers as agents of change could also promote a positive peer social influence on adolescents' academic engagement".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators need to identify influential helpers or highly engaged peers within the classroom peer network
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that assembling distractive peers within the same setting could reinforce and worsen adolescents' distractive behaviors, so peer grouping must be handled carefully

### Target Learners
- early adolescents, especially academically less engaged students

### Target Learning Goals
- behavioral and emotional academic engagement
