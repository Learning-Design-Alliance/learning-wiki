# Use serious games with unlimited-time access for teaching assessment of distressing clinical content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend incorporating serious games into nursing curricula for content students find distressing, since the game lets students "access educational content without time limitation and experience unpleasant burn scenes in a game-like environment". They hope the method will be included in nursing lesson plans due to its advantages over traditional methods, and note students are eager to review content and correct mistakes because of the game's attractive and competitive environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- a serious game designed with burn specialists, nursing professors, health informatics specialists and a software engineering team
- scenarios based on real cases from the clinical department
#### Constraints
- the study was a basic evaluation of the game at one university with a small sample, and the game will be developed in future phases

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- burn patient assessment knowledge and skills
