# Use the home numeracy environment as a target for future experimental interventions supporting foundational number skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors position the home numeracy environment as a candidate lever for early intervention: while their findings are correlational, they "provide a foundation for future experimental studies assessing the impact of early interventions targeting the home numeracy environment of young children to enhance foundational number skills." Because all subcategories of practices, including informal ones, were associated with children's neural measures, interventions need not be restricted to formal instruction-like activities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Experimental (e.g., longitudinal or intervention) designs, since the supporting evidence is cross-sectional and correlational
#### Constraints
- Current evidence does not establish that changing home numeracy practices causes changes in skills or brain function

### Target Learners
- Preschool and kindergarten children (around 5 years of age) and their parents

### Target Learning Goals
- Foundational number skills and symbolic number processing

### Affordances
- Experience Dependent Tuning Numeracy
