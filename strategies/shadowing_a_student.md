# Shadowing a Student

## Description
Shadowing is a direct observation technique where an adult follows a student throughout their school day to gain real-time qualitative evidence of the student's experiences. The goal is to observe and note facts about the student’s learning, interactions, and daily experiences without interpretation, opinion, or judgment. This practice allows educators to uncover insights into a student's lived experiences, identify areas that need to be addressed, and understand the school environment from the student’s perspective.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A designated adult (educator, administrator, or other staff member), a student willing to be shadowed, a shadowing template for note-taking, and a scheduled school day for the observation. Requires staying objective and making low-inference observations.
#### Constraints
- Requires significant time commitment. Can be intrusive if not done sensitively. May be influenced by the observer's biases, despite efforts to remain objective. Effectiveness depends on the observer's ability to accurately record and interpret observations.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Educators, school leaders, product designers, and anyone seeking to understand student experiences and improve school environments, particularly focusing on diverse student populations.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Gain insights into student experiences, identify areas needing improvement, uncover truths about the school environment, understand the impact of race on teaching and learning, and inform actions to support students.

### Affordances
- Provides real-time, qualitative data about student experiences. Allows for direct observation of student interactions, relationships, and learning. Uncovers hidden biases and disparities. Facilitates empathy and understanding of diverse student needs.

### Personalization
- The focus of shadowing can be personalized based on specific learning goals, such as understanding the experiences of English learners, students of color, or students with specific needs. Educators can choose students with different profiles to broaden their understanding.

## Assessment Evidence
- Notes and observations recorded during the shadowing experience, summaries of findings, reflections on insights gained, and plans for action based on the shadowing data. Analysis of student interactions, teacher practices, and school environment.

## Impact
- Increased understanding of student experiences, identification of disparities and biases, improved instructional practices, and enhanced support for diverse student populations. Improved school culture and climate through data-driven decision-making.
