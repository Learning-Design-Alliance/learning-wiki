# Integrate emotional support and emotion-regulation components into coaching, mentoring and guided reflection during the teaching practicum

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that practicum support structures explicitly address emotions, since the evaluated coaching trainings did not. It suggests guided reflection sessions in which peers or cooperating teachers help student teachers "categorize emotional experiences and discuss possible regulation strategies, such as cognitive reappraisal", and that future coaching models incorporate components offering emotional support and coping strategies. Trust-building measures and a clear rationale for joint lesson planning should also be integrated into coach training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coaches or mentors trained in an approach such as Content-Focused Coaching; reflection sessions with peers or cooperating teachers
#### Constraints
- The training as implemented in this study did not explicitly target emotional outcomes or strategies for managing emotions

### Target Learners
- student teachers during an obligatory teaching practicum

### Target Learning Goals
- managing teaching-related emotions such as anxiety, anger and enjoyment
- developing self-efficacy and career-choice security
