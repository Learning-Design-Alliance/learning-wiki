# Integrate structured reflection sessions like GGR into undergraduate clinical clerkships, especially where bedside exposure is limited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating structured reflection sessions like GGR into undergraduate clinical teaching "especially in settings where bedside exposure is limited", to support diagnostic reasoning. GGR's group format promotes collaborative learning, hypothesis revision and metacognitive monitoring within time-constrained clinical environments, and the authors suggest it suits undergraduates at an early clinical reasoning stage.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained facilitators and 60-minute case-based sessions
#### Constraints
- Evidence is from a single public-sector teaching hospital with a 4-week intervention assessing only short-term effects

### Target Learners

### Target Learning Goals
