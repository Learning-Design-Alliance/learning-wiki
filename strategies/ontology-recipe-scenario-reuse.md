# Reuse abstract MI 'recipes' across scenarios so new virtual client cases require only modification of lower-level rules

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For scaling the virtual client platform beyond a single scenario, the authors propose incorporating "abstract rules or 'recipes' about the MI process into the software", where a recipe encapsulates a generalisation about MI session dynamics that holds regardless of the health behaviour in focus. Higher-level recipes are reused as they are, while lower-level rules defining explicit content are modified for each new scenario. The authors attribute to Bickmore et al the finding that this approach "allowed a second scenario to be developed in only 4% of the person hours" required for the first, while reusing 22% of the software code.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Software that enacts the higher-level recipe, building on integration of dialogue knowledge representation and management and ontology integration
#### Constraints
- The person-hour and code-reuse figures are attributed to Bickmore et al's prior work, not demonstrated in this pilot

### Target Learners
- Health professionals across disciplines and varied client groups, including those in rural and remote locations

### Target Learning Goals
- Enable MI training scenarios covering the range of client types, conditions and behaviours seen in real-world practice
