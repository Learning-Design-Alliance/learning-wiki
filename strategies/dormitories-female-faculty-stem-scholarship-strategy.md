# Invest in women's dormitories and safe housing, female faculty, targeted STEM scholarships and mentorship, and cultural legitimacy across fields

## Description
The article's forward-looking reform priorities, quoted directly: "Priorities include investing in women’s dormitories and safe housing, recruiting and retaining female faculty, creating targeted scholarships and mentorship pathways in technical STEM, and broadening the cultural legitimacy of women’s participation across all fields of study." Each priority maps to a barrier the data show: dormitory shortages limited rural women's access, cultural norms steered women away from technical fields, and evening programs excluded women on safety and acceptability grounds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained investment in housing, faculty recruitment, and financial support mechanisms
#### Constraints
- The strategy responds to conditions documented at Kabul University; the article notes structural barriers included dormitory shortages and cultural norms discouraging women from technical disciplines

### Target Learners
- Female students, particularly those from rural areas and those considering technical STEM fields

### Target Learning Goals
- Broadening women's participation across disciplines, especially engineering, ICT, computer science, and physics
