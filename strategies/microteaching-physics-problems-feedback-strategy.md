# Microteaching on introductory physics problems with multi-source feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A practice-teaching activity in which each GTA selects an introductory physics problem, prepares it without provided solutions, and facilitates it for peers acting as students in a 10-minute session. Sessions host a maximum of 10 GTAs; while a GTA facilitates, peers split into two groups, and the instructor and each peer group complete a feedback form, giving each GTA feedback from three different sources that feeds a debrief essay. The format changed in 2015 from topic-based lessons to problem facilitation after GTA feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- GTAs must independently solve and prepare their chosen problem beforehand
- Feedback forms from the instructor and two peer groups
- A Microteaching Debrief Essay written from the feedback
#### Constraints
- Each GTA attends only one session; emphasis that facilitators must engage the audience rather than lecture

### Target Learners
- first-time physics GTAs

### Target Learning Goals
- facilitating problem solving
- giving and receiving feedback
- engaging students through questioning
