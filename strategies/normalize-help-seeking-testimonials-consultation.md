# Normalize help-seeking through recorded testimonials, leadership messaging, and framing support as academic consultation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The paper recommends medical schools annually ask graduating students and faculty volunteers to record brief audio or video 'testimonials' of healthy help-seeking options they used, made available electronically to successive cohorts. This builds on findings that lecture-based formats were perceived as patronizing while testimonials were more receptive. It also recommends leadership messages citing that "approximately 40% of medical students annually seek out formal or informal supports", and framing referral as an academic consultation rather than an evaluation to detect deficits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Volunteers should give only generic references to stressors and focus on helpful options; brief outlines may assist volunteers to address known barriers such as confidentiality and career worries
#### Constraints
- A one-time lecture reiterating these messages is unlikely to change help-seeking behavior

### Target Learners
- medical students
- graduating students and faculty volunteers

### Target Learning Goals
- normalizing help-seeking
- reducing reluctance to self-identify as needing support
