# Provide differentiated digital-readiness support and usability-focused design for AI simulation systems in nursing education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the stronger load–flow association among less digitally ready students, the article recommends that nursing education institutions prioritize high-usability AI virtual simulation systems, integrate usability testing into procurement, and develop differentiated support for students with varying digital readiness. The authors state that "students with lower digital readiness may benefit from pre‐training orientation sessions, step‐by‐step guided tutorials, and additional technical support during initial system use". Instructional designers are also advised to use clear navigation, concise instructions, and minimal operational steps to limit extraneous load and support flow.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Usability testing and iterative design improvements integrated into procurement and implementation of educational technology platforms
#### Constraints
- Recommendations rest on cross-sectional associations; the article states longitudinal or experimental studies are needed to establish causal relationships

### Target Learners
- Nursing students with varying levels of digital readiness, especially lower-readiness students

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning engagement and flow during AI-supported virtual simulation practice
