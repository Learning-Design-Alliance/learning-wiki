# Position learners as bug designers for peers to increase engagement with bugs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's DbD approach positions learners as creators of intentional bugs for their peers rather than only solvers of bugs they stumble upon. The authors state they positioned learners this way, "potentially increasing their engagement with bugs by allowing them to be creators of bugs rather than just solvers", and that students decided what kind of bugs to select and make, drawing on their own and classmates' prior experiences. The strategy unfolds across planning, design approval, construction, exchange, solving, and reflection sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need prior experience with bugs from earlier projects; a teacher approves designs and encourages considerate bug selection solvable in a single class period; access to e-textiles materials (microcontrollers, sensors, actuators, conductive materials)
#### Constraints
- Studied in one high school e-textiles unit; some students expressed frustration when solving peer-created bugs, though afterwards reported feeling more comfortable and competent

### Target Learners
- high school novice computing students, most with no prior computing education

### Target Learning Goals
- debugging skill and confidence; growth mindset practices such as choosing challenges and comfort with failure
