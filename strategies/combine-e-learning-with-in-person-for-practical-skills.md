# Apply e-learning alongside in-person instruction as a complementary learning opportunity, especially for practical and clinical courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend using e-learning alongside in-person instruction rather than as a replacement, concluding that e-learning "should be applied alongside the in-person method as a complementary learning opportunity". The recommendation rests on the study's finding that practical course scores did not improve significantly under e-learning, while the authors state that in-person education is more effective for acquiring practical skills than virtual learning environments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- E-learning content and platforms capable of delivering theoretical course material online with embedded assessment modules
#### Constraints
- The article states e-learning alone "might not have the necessary efficiency in practical and clinical medical education"
- The authors frame e-learning as an optimal solution for maintaining learning processes in exceptional emergencies such as the COVID-19 pandemic and call for further research on its efficacy

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- theoretical knowledge acquisition
- practical and clinical skills acquisition
