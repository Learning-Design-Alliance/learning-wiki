Combine cognitive tasks and ecological teacher questionnaires when measuring children's inhibition for academic research¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends measuring inhibition with both experimental tasks and teacher ratings, because "both types of measures capture unique and important aspects of inhibition." Teacher ratings were chosen over parent ratings since teachers observe children's behavior in relation to academic tasks in the classroom. Combining settings was intended to capture inhibition skills across experimental and classroom contexts, though in this study the two measure types did not correlate.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Researchers should select teacher rather than parent ratings when the outcome is academic, because teachers observe behavior in relation to academic tasks

Constraints¶

  • In this sample the BRIEF inhibition subscale was not associated with the stroop measures, suggesting the two measure types assess different aspects of inhibition

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school children

Target Learning Goals¶

  • accurate assessment of cognitive inhibition as a cognitive determinant of arithmetic skill

Claims¶

  • Teacher-rated inhibition (BRIEF) does not correlate with stroop-based cognitive inhibition measures [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Combine standardized tests and naturalistic classroom observations (mixed methods) when assessing oral communicative competence
  • Evaluate AI literacy interventions with behavioral measures of LLM interaction in addition to self-reports
  • Future CB-PA research and practice should use standardized, ecologically valid outcome measures, objective activity parameters, and longitudinal designs
