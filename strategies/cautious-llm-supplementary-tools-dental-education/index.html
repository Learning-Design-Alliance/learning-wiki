Strategies

Use LLMs cautiously as supplementary learning tools in dental education, with output verification and professional supervision¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 theoretical), q1–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that, given pooled LLM accuracy of about 64%, dental educators may use LLMs as supplementary tools for "generating explanations, highlighting key concepts, and supporting formative self-testing" while correctness must be verified, especially in high-stakes domains. The strategy positions LLMs as education and structured knowledge support rather than autonomous clinical recommendation, with outputs interpreted critically under professional supervision.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Outputs must be interpreted critically and under professional supervision, with correctness verified by the user.

Constraints¶

  • The article states LLMs are not suitable for autonomous clinical decision-making and that observed accuracy should not be interpreted as evidence of safe independent clinical reasoning.

Target Learners¶

  • dental students
  • dental hygiene students
  • dental trainees preparing for licensing and specialty examinations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • dental knowledge assessment and examination preparation
  • formative self-testing
  • explanation of key dental concepts

Claims¶

  • Large language models achieve a pooled accuracy of 63.69% (95% CI 60.26%-67.06%) in answering dental examination questions [+M]
  • Between-study heterogeneity in LLM dental-question accuracy is very high (I² = 91.5%), with a prediction interval spanning 29.65% to 91.71% [~M]
  • Subgroup analyses show significant between-model differences, with Copilot (75.25%) and ChatGPT-4 (72.97%) achieving the highest pooled accuracies [~M]
  • Reviewed literature reports risks of overreliance, AI errors in complex tasks, and students' uncritical acceptance of AI-generated outputs [+M]
  • LLM output can be misleading or erroneous because a stochastic element in text generation sometimes selects less likely continuations, so it requires continuous supervision and critical evaluation by domain experts. [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use LLMs as supportive educational tools and complements, not replacements, in medical education
  • Frame LLM use in biomedical education around low‐stakes formative tasks with expert‐guided verification and rationale requests
  • Use current LLMs in the notary domain only as supervised support tools in preliminary drafting or educational contexts
  • Verify ChatGPT outputs against reliable resources and do not rely on it solely for high-stakes exam study
