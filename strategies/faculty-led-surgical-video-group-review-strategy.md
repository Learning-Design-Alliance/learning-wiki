# Use high-quality surgical videos, ideally in faculty-led group study, to compensate for lost operating room time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends viewing high-quality surgical videos to help make up for the significant loss of operating room time, whether or not simulation is available. It states that "higher points of learning can be achieved when done in a group study format, ideally led by faculty." Self-review is encouraged, but the authors caution that freely available video libraries vary widely in content and quality, particularly on platforms such as YouTube, and recommend staff oversight when viewing videos of questionable quality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to high-quality surgical video libraries
- Staff oversight and discussion when viewing videos of questionable quality
#### Constraints
- Video libraries vary widely in content and quality, with particular concern over freely available videos on YouTube
- Society libraries from the ACS and SAGES offer an alternative but are incomplete and in some cases expensive
- Hospital-based simulation access is limited because residents are discouraged from coming to the hospital unless on call

### Target Learners
- surgical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- operative and technical knowledge
- compensating for decreased operative case volume
