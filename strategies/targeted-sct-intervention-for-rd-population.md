# Develop targeted SCT interventions for reading disorder populations distinct from ADHD treatment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that SCT affects anxiety and academic performance in RD children with low ADHD-IN, the authors recommend interventions targeted at SCT itself rather than only at ADHD. They state: "Our findings suggest that intervention programs or therapeutics specifically targeted to improve SCT (not just ADHD) should be developed for the RD population." Existing evidence on SCT treatment comes from ADHD populations, so SCT-specific approaches for RD populations remain to be developed and tested.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions or therapeutics designed to reduce SCT symptoms specifically, evaluated in RD populations without comorbid ADHD.
#### Constraints
- Prior SCT treatment studies (organizational skills interventions, methylphenidate, lisdexamfetamine) were conducted exclusively among individuals with ADHD, so benefits in RD populations are unknown.

### Target Learners
- children with reading disorder and low levels of ADHD-Inattention symptoms

### Target Learning Goals
- improving academic performance and reducing anxiety by improving SCT symptoms
