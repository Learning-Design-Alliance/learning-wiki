# Ensure learners master lower task layers before allowing AI automation of those components

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The essay recommends that, following the logic of the three-layer model, "educators should ensure that learners have mastered the lower layers (or components) of a task before letting learners automate them using an AI, similarly to how calculators are introduced and used in schools". This sequencing is intended to prevent diminished ability and illusions of competence, since offloading the semantic layer (e.g., generating an entire essay from prompts) cannot substitute for engaging with content and acquiring one's own style through training and feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Precise analysis of learning tasks and objectives to determine skill levels at which different task parts can be offloaded.
#### Constraints
- The essay states more empirical research is needed to ascertain the effectiveness of this approach and its transferability to different subjects, content domains, and task types.

### Target Learners
- school and university learners using generative AI tools

### Target Learning Goals
- protecting skill acquisition and understanding while permitting AI support for already-mastered subskills
