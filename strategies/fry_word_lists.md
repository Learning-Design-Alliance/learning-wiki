# Fry Word Lists

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
The Fry word list contains 1,000 high-frequency words divided into ten levels, further split into groups of twenty-five words based on frequency and difficulty. These "instant words" aim to build reading fluency: students are encouraged to recognize the words by sight, practice them in context through phrase and sentence reading, and write short sentences containing Fry words. The list, developed by Edward Fry, is based on frequency analysis of materials commonly encountered in grades 3–9, and covers a large share of all running text — the first 100 words alone account for roughly half of most written English.

## Design Implications

The strategy rests on the premise that automatic word recognition frees attentional and working-memory resources for comprehension [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]. Because high-frequency words recur constantly, rapid recognition of even the first few hundred words yields disproportionate fluency gains. However, sight-word drill works best when embedded in connected text rather than taught as isolated flashcards, and grouping words into sets of twenty-five is an application of [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) that limits the load of each practice cycle [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Fry word lists (or a digital equivalent), leveled reading materials containing the target words
- A routine for repeated, distributed exposure — brief daily review outperforms massed drill [Distributed practice improves retention.](../claims/distributed-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S]
- Ongoing [Practice](../elements/practice.md) in reading words in isolation *and* in phrases/sentences, with [Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) on misreadings

#### Constraints
- Drill on isolated words without contextual reading produces word-calling that does not transfer to comprehension [-M] — fluency with a list is not the same as understanding text
- Many high-frequency words are irregularly spelled (e.g., *said*, *there*, *of*); pure phonics decoding fails on them, so they require whole-word teaching, while decodable words should still be connected to phonics instruction [~M]
- Rote memorization can displace phonemic awareness and decoding work for struggling readers if it becomes the sole instructional focus [-M]
- The list is frequency-based, not developmentally sequenced by phonetic complexity; some early-level words are harder to decode than later ones [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Flashcard drill, word walls, games (e.g., bingo, slap-the-word), timed one-minute reads, and phrase/sentence reading cards
- Digital implementations (e.g., [Epic!](https://www.getepic.com) leveled readers, [Reading A–Z](https://www.readinga-z.com) high-frequency word lessons) pair list practice with decodable text
- Individualized lists: teachers assess which words a student already knows and build a personal "working set" of 5–10 unknown words at a time

### Target Learners
- Early readers (K–2) building a sight vocabulary that supports fluent first-pass reading [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Struggling readers who lack automaticity and laboriously decode common words, overloading working memory during text reading
- English language learners, for whom high-frequency words provide the connective tissue of English sentences — though they may need explicit meaning support alongside recognition [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Sight-word automaticity and reading fluency
- Indirect support for comprehension by reducing decoding load [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+M]
- Early writing: Fry words supply most of the function words children need to compose sentences

### Instructions
1. **Assess baseline.** Have the student read the current level's 25-word list aloud; record known and unknown words.
2. **Introduce a small set.** Teach 5–10 unknown words explicitly — pronounce, spell, and discuss meaning — applying [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) to keep the set manageable.
3. **Practice in isolation.** Use flashcards, games, or timed reads for brief daily review, spacing review across days rather than massing it [Distributed practice improves retention.](../claims/distributed-practice-improves-retention.md) [+S].
4. **Practice in context.** Read phrases and short sentences built from the target words, then connected text at the student's level, so recognition is exercised where it matters.
5. **Provide feedback and write.** Correct misreadings immediately and have the student write original sentences using the words [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md).
6. **Track and advance.** Re-assess periodically; move to the next 25-word set when the student reads the current set instantly and accurately in context.

## Related Strategies
- Repeated reading of leveled text — the natural companion; Fry word drill builds the lexical base that repeated reading consolidates into fluency
- Phonics instruction — complementary; decodable high-frequency words should be linked to taught sound-spelling patterns rather than memorized as wholes

## Related Elements
- [Practice](../elements/practice.md) — distributed, repeated exposure is the core mechanism of sight-word learning
- [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) — the 25-word sets bound the size of each practice cycle
- [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) — immediate correction prevents mispronunciations from consolidating

## Examples
- **Classroom word walls:** A first-grade teacher posts the current set of 25 Fry words, reviews them daily with a two-minute chant, and requires students to use three wall words in their morning journal writing.
- **[Reading A–Z](https://www.readinga-z.com) high-frequency word books** — printable books that repeat a small set of Fry words in predictable sentence frames, pairing list practice with contextual reading.
- **[Sight Words.com](https://sightwords.com)** — free Fry-list flashcards, games, and phrase cards organized by level, with a recommended sequence of teach-practice-assess cycles.
