# Position AI simulation as a complement to—not a replacement for—traditional clinical placements, with faculty preparation and dedicated quiet spaces

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend treating AI-enhanced simulation as an augmenting strategy within clinical training. "In light of these challenges and limitations, AI-enhanced simulation should be viewed as a complementary strategy in clinical training rather than a substitute for traditional clinical experiences. Its value lies in augmenting, not replacing, real-world learning." Successful facilitation required educators proficient with the technology who could conduct prebriefs, monitor engagement, lead debriefings, and align simulation with outcomes; sound-absorbing spaces and reliable connectivity were also needed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty readiness covering both technology proficiency and instructional tasks such as prebriefs, monitoring, and debriefings
#### Constraints
- In resource-limited or high-use settings, network failures and background noise degraded session quality, suggesting possible multiple subscription services

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students
- nurse educators in placement-constrained programs

### Target Learning Goals
- therapeutic communication
- psychiatric assessment
- education continuity where placements are limited
