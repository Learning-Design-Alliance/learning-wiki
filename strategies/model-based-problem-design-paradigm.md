# Design problems via a model-based paradigm that induces qualitative, comparative reasoning instead of equation manipulation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes starting from a PER-based model of the knowledge elements, structures, and skills needed for expert-like problem solving, targeting deficits given students' current state, inferring cognitive processes that build those structures, and designing activities that engage those processes. Such problems can be "superficially similar to traditional problems" yet have "a drastically different and more beneficial effect". The worked example converts a ladder-friction calculation into a qualitative ranking task: "we could omit the numerical values and instead ask students whether the friction force on the base of the ladder increases, decreases, or stays the same as the man walks up the ladder". A further variant asks students to write a strategy without equations or select the most useful principle.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A model of the knowledge structures and skills required for expert-like problem solving, and knowledge of students' current understanding
#### Constraints
- The paradigm is presented as a design approach grounded in PER findings, not as an empirically tested intervention in this article

### Target Learners
- high school and university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual, qualitative, and comparative reasoning; strategic knowledge; structuring knowledge by key principles
