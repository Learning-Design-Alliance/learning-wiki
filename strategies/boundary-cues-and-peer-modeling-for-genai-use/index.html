Provide explicit instructional boundary cues, process-oriented assessment, and peer modeling of verification to frame responsible GenAI use¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

This strategy operationalizes the contextual-support construct the article identifies as the environmental antecedent of trust calibration and collaborative regulation. Participants described contextual support through "explicitinstructionalboundaries,assessmentexpectations,andpeer norms", such as instructors defining the permitted role of GenAI and classmates modeling comparison of AI answers against papers or class notes. Teachers can state permissible AI uses, design process-oriented assessments, and normalize verification practices so that passive copying becomes less viable.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructor guidance about permissible AI use, process-oriented assessment design, and visible peer norms for responsible practice

Constraints¶

  • The article reports contextual support as a perceived, self-reported condition in a cross-sectional design; it does not establish which specific instructional manipulations causally produce calibrated trust

Target Learners¶

  • university undergraduates across humanities, social sciences, business, and STEM disciplines

Target Learning Goals¶

  • task-appropriate reliance on GenAI
  • verification as a normalized academic behavior

Affordances¶

  • Genai Collaboration Process Model

Related Strategies¶

  • Renew writing assessment and integrity practice through AI-literacy instruction, process-focused assessment design, and contextual detector training
  • Use a six-question drafting and audit framework for GenAI assessment rules covering task rules, defaults, disclosure, contribution, verification, and hierarchy
  • Calibrated disclosure templates scaled to risk of the permitted use
  • Institutional recommendations: shift from detection to process-oriented assessment, transparent AI-use protocols, and revised integrity policies
  • Collaborative Human-AI Revision Design embedding prompt engineering across drafting, feedback, and rewriting phases
  • Universities combine responsible AI use guidelines, accessible technical support, AI literacy training, and assessment-embedded critical evaluation activities to address AI learning anxiety
  • Assessment design levers that make learning visible and reduce the payoff from generic generated text
  • Cultivate learners' critical AI literacy and integrity-focused group practices for GenAI-supported group work
  • Help GenAI users articulate their use as an efficiency-enhancing practice to reduce negative peer evaluations
