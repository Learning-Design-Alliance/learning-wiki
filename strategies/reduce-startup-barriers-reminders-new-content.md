# Lower the barriers to first use and stimulate adherence with new content, reminders, and explicit usage expectations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend making Web-based interventions as easy as possible to start using, because in their study considerable time elapsed between expressing interest and being able to use the application, and steps like checking email, clicking a link, and creating a profile "require effort and could thereby decrease the odds of using the intervention". Once participants become users, the application itself should stimulate adherence by regularly providing new content, including email or text-message reminders, and explicitly telling participants what is expected of them in terms of usage.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The application must be accessible the moment people are interested, without multi-step onboarding that requires effort
#### Constraints
- Recommendation is grounded in the authors' interpretation of their own trial's low usage (64% used at least once, median 1.0 log-on), not in a tested manipulation of these design features

### Target Learners
- adults with BMI 18.5–28.0 using Web-based preventive health interventions

### Target Learning Goals
- adoption and sustained use of Web-based behavior-change applications
- healthy dietary and physical activity behavior
