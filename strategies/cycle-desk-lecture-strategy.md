# Incorporate low-intensity cycle desks (RPE ≤2/10) into college lecture courses to reduce sedentary time without harming academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy places stationary cycle desks in a lecture classroom and instructs enrolled students to pedal at a very low rate of perceived exertion during class meetings. The article found "it appears feasible to incorporate low intensity exercise into a classroom lecture venue without negatively altering student academic performance," and participants "did not perceive the cycle desks as being disruptive during class."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cycle desks physically fit within the lecture classroom and are randomly placed to minimize seating-location effects
- Students receive verbal and visual training on using an RPE scale before the intervention
#### Constraints
- Tested with a small sample (21 completers) in one course at a small university, so results are not generalizable to all college students
- The article states results "were limited by sample size" and recommends larger, longitudinal studies in diverse classrooms

### Target Learners
- college students (19–24 years) enrolled in lecture courses

### Target Learning Goals
- maintain academic performance in lecture courses while reducing weekly sedentary behavior time
