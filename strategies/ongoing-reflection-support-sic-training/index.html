Provide ongoing processes for reflection and support as clinicians' serious illness communication skills develop¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that training go beyond one-off instruction: "The findings from this study point toward the need for development of ongoing processes for reflection and support for clinicians to explore their beliefs, experiences and challenges in SIC" as their skills grow. This responds to findings that physicians lacked full awareness of emotion regulation strategies even after SIC training, and that clinician-learners could benefit from exploring conscious and subconscious communication competencies.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Support and commitment across all levels of the care continuum, inclusive of clinicians, leadership, and organizational structures

Constraints¶

  • The review notes physicians' own emotions in SIC were variable and could conflict with their observed or expressed behavior even after training

Target Learners¶

  • clinician-learners in serious illness communication training
  • practicing clinicians using the SICP

Target Learning Goals¶

  • developing contextual reflective competence and emotion regulation in serious illness conversations

Claims¶

  • Confidence is required for serious illness conversations, with discomfort arising especially around discussing prognosis [+W]
  • Communication skills training programs improve clinician patient-centeredness, but training approaches are variable and long-term retention is untested [+W]
