# Adapt simulation session length and content to learner experience level, and emphasize maintenance training for low-frequency procedures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors adapt the same curriculum to learner background: sessions for experienced learners were shortened to 2 hours, the steps-listing activity and deliberate-practice stations were removed, and for groups more comfortable with US guidance the US portion was shortened while anatomic site and catheter size selection were emphasized. They argue "Frequent opportunities for simulation-based training are vital for low-frequency procedures," citing evidence that a gap of more than 2 weeks between simulation and patient care can be a barrier to skill transfer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Skilled facilitators who can tailor which activities to include; knowledge of learners' prior procedural experience
#### Constraints
- The 2-week transfer-barrier finding is cited from prior work (Mema and Harris), not tested in this article

### Target Learners
- novice and experienced pediatric procedural learners

### Target Learning Goals
- retention and transfer of procedural skill for low-frequency procedures
