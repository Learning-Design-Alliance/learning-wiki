# Future sedentary-behavior interventions should target both school and extra-school settings and vary break type, duration and intensity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the null cognitive findings, the authors recommend that future interventions not only reduce classroom sitting but simultaneously address the extra-school setting, and test different types of breaks (standing, light, moderate and vigorous exercise) with different durations and intervals. They suggest the behavioral change of replacing sitting with standing during classes may be insufficient without changes outside school or higher activity intensity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must extend beyond classroom time to the extra-school setting to plausibly affect cognitive outcomes
#### Constraints
- In the present study the intervention only reduced sedentary behavior at school and had no effect on daily physical activity measured by accelerometry

### Target Learners
- School-aged children and adolescents

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving cognitive function and academic achievement through reduced sedentary behavior
