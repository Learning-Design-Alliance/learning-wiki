# Three-step construction of lens ray diagrams from bundles of elemental images, with focal points redefined in terms of elemental images

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes constructing ray diagrams step by step: from any viewpoint on the lens draw a horizontal ray and a ray bundle containing the object; from the same viewpoint draw a focal ray and transfer the distances ui and li from the front focal plane into the back focal plane in reverse order; repeat for at least one more viewpoint and draw the lens image where the refracted ray bundles overlap completely. Focal points are redefined so they can be found experimentally with finite objects. The author states "Our method includes the conventional method as a special case."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rays previously established as connecting elemental images (Section 4)
- Experimentally determined front and back focal points based on the redefined definitions
#### Constraints
- The article states the method is only valid for paraxial rays and for a thin lens; geometric and chromatic aberrations and diffraction are neglected

### Target Learners
- 12th-grade high school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Predicting where elemental images compose a sharp image with convex and concave lenses

### Affordances
- Multi View Lens Imaging Elemental Images
