# Provide researcher involvement, teacher engagement and communication as key supports in school independent research projects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that "Researcher-involvement, uncommon in many other programmes, along with teacher engagement and communication are found to be key elements to success in independent research projects overall". Concretely this means staged researcher contact (kick-off workshop, in-school mentoring visit, comments on drafts, campus conference) plus ad hoc communication, with teachers encouraged to communicate early and often so the university can assist with struggles. The authors also suggest teachers facilitate students' group dynamics and consider peer mentoring from previous years' students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Researchers with expertise in the project areas available to mentor students and schools
- Teachers who communicate and engage with the programme and its expectations from the outset
#### Constraints
- Schools which do not solicit a researcher visit were much more likely to drop out, but the association is observational
- Peer mentoring is only viable for schools which complete one year in the first instance and has had mixed results at other schools

### Target Learners
- 14–18 year-old school students in state and independent schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustaining participation and success in open-ended independent research projects in physics

### Affordances
- Prise Independent Research Project Framework
