# Distributed Practice

## Description
Distributed practice involves practicing content in short sessions spaced out over time, contrasting with massed practice (cramming) where content is practiced in one long session. It leverages the principle that “Forgetting helps Remembering.” Time between practice sessions leads to some forgetting, requiring learners to actively reconstruct and fix what they struggled with, thus improving retention and understanding. The process starts after initial learning with pretty good accuracy, followed by spaced practice sessions with intervals depending on the time until assessment. For example, some suggest the length of time between sessions should be about 20% of the time between initial learning and when you will be assessed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires scheduling and adherence to spaced practice sessions. May initially require a mass practice session to achieve pretty good accuracy.
#### Constraints
- Learners may initially perceive it as less effective than easier techniques like re-reading, as it involves struggling to recreate information. The 'harder' activity feels like you are not learning more. The opposite is true.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Applicable to learners of all ages and skill levels. Especially beneficial for those preparing for exams, quizzes, or aiming for long-term retention of skills.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Enhance long-term memory and understanding of studied material. Improve accuracy and fluency in skill performance over extended periods.

### Affordances
- Leads to more accurate and longer-lasting retention, helps the brain work hard to recreate 'lost' information. Results in better understanding and memory compared to easier techniques like re-reading or highlighting.

### Personalization
- The number and length of distributed practice sessions can be adjusted based on the length of time until assessment and individual learning needs.

## Assessment Evidence
- Compare long-term retention rates between learners using distributed practice and massed practice. Track decreasing errors in successive distributed practice sessions.

## Impact
- Research shows distributed practice is twice as effective as massed practice for long-term memory, sometimes up to several years. It helps learners better remember and understand studied content.
