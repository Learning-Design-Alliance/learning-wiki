# Offer anonymous online parenting support to lower barriers to help-seeking for parents with mental illness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend preserving anonymity in online parenting support because it lowers barriers to seeking help for parents who feel shame or fear losing custody of their children. Satisfaction was expressed with "the anonymity of the course, the opportunity to participate without leaving home, and the fact that no child care was needed." The authors argue anonymity may also help lower the risk of child abuse, and note that a funding structure for online services that preserve anonymity is being proposed in the Netherlands.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A funding structure that accommodates anonymous participants, since anonymity does not fit health insurance companies' identification procedures
#### Constraints
- Facilitators need a protocol for suspected child abuse, which was being drawn up for approval by professional sectors and the Netherlands Health Care Inspectorate

### Target Learners
- parents with mental illness hesitant to seek help through identified services

### Target Learning Goals
- early access to parenting support before problems escalate
