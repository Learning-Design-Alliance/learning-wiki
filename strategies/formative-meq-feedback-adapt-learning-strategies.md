# Use a formative MEQ under exam conditions with immediate peer-review feedback so students can adapt their learning strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a formative exam (MEQ*) taken under examination conditions at the end of the first semester, corrected within 75 minutes via student peer review so that "students receive immediate feedback on their performance and can align their individual learning strategies". Because the MEQ* score predicts the summative MEQ-1, the feedback informs students of their current knowledge state in time to adjust preparation during the second semester.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The formative exam must be structured like the summative MEQ and adapted to the level of knowledge, with an answer horizon provided for peer-review correction.
- Scores must be verified by a qualified evaluator to check the points assigned by fellow students.
#### Constraints
- Some students do not specifically prepare for the formative exam since it is not part of the overall grade; the average score of about 57% suggests students do not yet sufficiently realize its purpose as feedback.
- Free-text formats require relatively high effort in formulating answer horizons and in correction, and reviewer discretion can reduce reliability.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in a problem-based learning curriculum

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- basic science knowledge (anatomy, physiology, biochemistry)
- exam preparation and self-regulated learning strategy adjustment
