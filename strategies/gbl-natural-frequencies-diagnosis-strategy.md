# Use short online game-based learning with natural frequencies and immediate feedback to teach diagnostic test interpretation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A specific implementable training recipe: have learners watch a short video on natural frequencies, then play a fast-paced online game requiring gestalt estimates of post-test probability with immediate natural-frequency feedback. The article reports the intervention "nearly doubled diagnostic accuracy in medical trainees and practicing clinicians and persisted for three months" and concludes GBL "is easily disseminated to medical training programs".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A publicly available online game with immediate feedback using natural frequency methods
- A short tutorial video explaining natural frequencies
- Approximately 45 minutes or less of learner time
#### Constraints
- The outcome scale of diagnostic accuracy has not been validated
- The sample was within a single medical system and may not be generalizable
- Participants were reimbursed, and attention may not be replicated with voluntary participation

### Target Learners
- medical students
- residents
- practicing clinicians

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnostic accuracy
- interpretation of test results
- estimating post-test probability
