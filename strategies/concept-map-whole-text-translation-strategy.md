# Teach whole-text translation by having students build concept maps instead of word-by-word translation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy replaces word-by-word translation training with the concept map tool, a web-based application in which concepts are enclosed in circles or boxes and linked by lines with linking words specifying relationships. Students use the maps to decide which words matter for understanding the whole text, then summarize the text into a paragraph with translation. In this study the second group was taught text translation by the concept tool method for a semester.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the concept map tool, which runs on desktop or laptop web browsers and is not meant for tablets and smartphones that rely on tapping
#### Constraints
- The tool is designed for desktop or laptop use, not tapping-based mobile devices, as the article states

### Target Learners
- English-as-a-foreign-language students learning translation from English to Arabic

### Target Learning Goals
- Whole-text comprehension and translation skills
- Distinguishing essential from accessory words when reading texts
