# Remove numerical mini-CEX scores from assessment forms and provide narrative feedback only

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends, as one of two consequences of its findings, eliminating numerical scores from workplace-based assessment forms because they carry little informational value. It states that "Narrative feedback is rather suitable to inform learners regarding a change in practice compared to 'tick boxes'". The strategy targets the scoring weakness the study documented, where practically all students receive scores in the upper range of the scale.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainers must provide narrative feedback in place of numerical ratings, so the form must include space for written comments.
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as one of two options, the alternative being to improve rating-scale design; it derives from a study in undergraduate clerkships at one medical faculty.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- Informing learners about needed changes in clinical practice via feedback
