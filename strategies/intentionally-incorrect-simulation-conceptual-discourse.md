# Intentionally incorrect physics simulations to provoke conceptual reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article uses deliberately incorrect orbital representations as a discourse prompt: an unrealistic orbit not in the equatorial plane, and an unlikely orbit that appears geostationary above a point on the northern hemisphere. Students were challenged to "explain and elaborate what is incorrect about this orbit," for instance the higher speed nearer Earth and lower speed further away, and to use Newton's Third Law with a free body diagram to argue why the force direction cannot sustain a circular orbit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A free body diagram showing equal and opposite forces on Earth and satellite supported students' Newton's Third Law reasoning in this context.
#### Constraints
- This approach is drawn from prior work on intentionally incorrect simulations cited by the authors; it was applied to geostationary orbit contexts in this article.

### Target Learners
- junior college physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- explaining why an orbit cannot be geostationary using force reasoning
