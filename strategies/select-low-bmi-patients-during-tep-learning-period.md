# Surgeons in the learning period can select lower-BMI hernia patients to overcome the learning curve more easily

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that trainees performing laparoscopic TEP hernioplasty account for patient BMI while still on the learning curve. It states that "Difficulty associated with BMI in the learning period can help young surgeons to select hernia patients in order to overcome the learning period easily." Because the BMI–time correlation was significant only early in the series, patient selection is framed as a temporary aid during the learning period rather than a permanent rule for experienced surgeons.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Applies to surgeons still within the TEP learning period, which the article's cited literature places at roughly 30–60 surgeries
#### Constraints
- Based on a single-surgeon series; the authors state further studies gathering young surgeons are needed to better evaluate the correlation

### Target Learners
- Surgical trainees learning laparoscopic TEP hernioplasty

### Target Learning Goals
- Achieving proficiency in the technically demanding TEP procedure and overcoming the learning period safely
