# Increase student preparation for preclass material via clear expectations, focused preparation tasks, and explicit links to in-class active learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose concrete instructor methods to raise the proportion of students who prepare preclass material in flipped or inverted designs, motivated by their finding that only 46% of randomized students attended and IC attendance was lower. They state: "Providing clear expectations to students, keeping the preparation tasks focused, and explicitly linking preparation activities to in-class active learning could be some key methods for instructors to increase the proportion of students who prepare for classes."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires instructors to define expectations and design focused preparation tasks connected to in-class activities
#### Constraints
- Proposed in a context where class attendance is not mandatory and adherence to preclass preparation could not be verified

### Target Learners

### Target Learning Goals
- preparation for case-study courses on antithrombotic drug use
