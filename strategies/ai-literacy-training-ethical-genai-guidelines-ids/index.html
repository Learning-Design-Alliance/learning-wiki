Provide AI literacy training, human-AI interaction opportunities, and ethical-use guidelines and policies for instructional designers¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that organizations and academic programs equip instructional designers to use GenAI effectively and ethically. It states that IDs "need to consciously reflect on their ID practices and design decisions with ethical considerations to avoid misuse of AI and harmful impacts on learners," and that organizations should provide relevant training or human-AI interaction opportunities to build AI literacy, awareness, acceptance, and trust. Administrators may support IDs in exploring GenAI by providing more incentives, and the authors call for guidelines and policies regarding the ethical use of GenAI.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Organizations and academic programs provide relevant training or human-AI interaction opportunities to equip IDs with AI literacy

Constraints¶

  • Creating guidelines is not an easy task, especially if the entities responsible for policy-making lack sufficient resources and understanding of AI

Target Learners¶

  • Instructional designers working in higher education, corporate, non-profit, and pk-12 sectors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Effective and ethical integration of GenAI tools into instructional design workflows and professional practice

Claims¶

  • IDs raised data security, privacy, authorship, ownership, and plagiarism concerns, noting a lack of guidelines and policies for ethical GenAI use [+W]
  • IDs expressed quality concerns over GenAI-generated content, including bias, inaccuracy, hallucinations, and lack of authenticity [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Institutions should adopt clear, prominently displayed AI policies and cultivate AI literacy for ethical, employability-oriented use
  • Develop student AI literacy and institutional GenAI policies, guidelines and safeguards for responsible integration
  • Institutions should respond to GenAI with clear governance frameworks and structured professional training programs for staff.
  • Develop clear institutional GenAI policies, communicate them, raise awareness, and promote digital literacy
