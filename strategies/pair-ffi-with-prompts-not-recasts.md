# Pair form-focused instruction with prompts that push learners to self-repair rather than recasts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
When delivering form-focused instruction, teachers should use corrective feedback in the form of prompts (which push learners to retrieve and repair their own output) rather than recasts. The study found that "FFI is more effective when combined with prompts than with recasts or no feedback," particularly for enabling learners to acquire rule-based representations of grammatical gender and proceduralize that knowledge. The advantage appeared mainly on written tasks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Form-focused instruction on predictable form-meaning patterns already in place
- Teachers able to implement prompts consistently during subject-matter lessons
#### Constraints
- Advantage shown mainly on written tasks and to a lesser degree oral tasks; strongest evidence concerns grammatical gender attribution in French immersion contexts

### Target Learners
- Fifth-grade (10–11-year-old) French immersion students

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate grammatical gender attribution and proceduralization of emerging L2 forms
