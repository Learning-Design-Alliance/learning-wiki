# Include a post-game debriefing period in which groups reflect on an evolutionary tree from the game through guided questions.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
After Phase 2 gameplay, VENOMventure participants took part in a debriefing period in which the facilitator showed an evolutionary tree from the game and asked group questions such as "What do you think the game was about?" and "What is happening at the splitting points?" The article states "The debriefing period is an opportunity for metacognition and reflection on learning that occurred during the game and is often viewed as a design element that supports learning". Participants could also ask the facilitator questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator available to lead the group debriefing with the game's evolutionary tree and prompt questions
#### Constraints
- Phase 1 participants did not have a separate debriefing period because the debriefing questions were already part of the Phase 1 interview

### Target Learners
- Families with youth ages 8 years and up in informal science settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Tree thinking: interpreting evolutionary trees, including time direction, shared ancestry, and trait change on branches
