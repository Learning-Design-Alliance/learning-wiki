# Embed explicit teaching practices in mathematics programs and sustain multi-year teacher experience with them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that an explicitly designed core program raised teachers' use of evidence-based practices, the authors "encourage curriculum developers to consider embedding explicit teaching practices within mathematics programs," specifically ensuring programs offer frequent, cognitively demanding practice opportunities such as mathematics verbalizations. They further recommend that teachers sustain implementation of explicit mathematics programs, because additional years of experience may enhance teachers' use of evidence-based practices, and they encourage researchers to investigate whether continued implementation produces greater student mathematics achievement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must offer frequent, cognitively demanding opportunities for students to practice, such as mathematics verbalizations, and teachers need sustained multi-year experience with the program.
#### Constraints
- The cross-sectional evidence for a second year of experience was nonsignificant, with moderate effect sizes only for demonstrations, group responses, and feedback and no evidence for individual responses.

### Target Learners
- Students with or at risk for mathematics learning disabilities in kindergarten core mathematics instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- Increased teacher delivery of overt models, student practice opportunities, and academic feedback during core mathematics instruction
