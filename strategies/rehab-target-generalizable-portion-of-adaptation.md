# Design gait rehabilitation to target the shared, generalizable portion of adaptation, since treadmill training produces patterns partially linked to the device

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that findings on partial generalization could inform customizable gait rehabilitation approaches aiming to induce asymmetric changes in joint-level kinematics. Because "training with devices like split-belt treadmills can produce new walking patterns that are partially linked to the device", some of the adapted pattern will only be expressed on the treadmill, reducing overground generalization. They suggest "development of new methods to increase credit assignment to the trainee" may improve transfer of adaptation-based treadmill training to real-world walking.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Understanding of how adaptation-based treadmill training generalizes in clinical populations, which the authors say needs further study
#### Constraints
- Findings may not apply to motor tasks different from split-belt walking adaptation; mechanisms likely differ for less automatic movements such as obstacle negotiation or staircase stepping

### Target Learners
- clinical populations with intact adaptation-based motor learning areas, e.g. cerebral stroke, cerebral palsy

### Target Learning Goals
- transfer of treadmill-based gait training gains to overground real-world walking

### Affordances
- Credit Assignment Two Forward Models Walking
