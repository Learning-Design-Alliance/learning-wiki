# Identify highly anxious adolescent girls and provide graduated coping supports, from lifestyle regulation to psychotherapy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings that girls were more anxious and had lower quality of life while anxiety did not predict achievement, the authors recommend targeted support for anxious adolescent girls. They advise that "it is important to identify adolescent girls with high levels of anxiety to improve their coping skills", starting with lifestyle measures and escalating to professional psychological interventions. The strategy is addressed to schools, parents, and clinicians supporting final-year students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Parents and school staff able to identify signs of students' anxiety early, as the article recommends training parents for early identification
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a two-point observational study of ninth-graders in one Latvian city; the article states it could not confirm causality

### Target Learners
- final-year ninth-grade adolescents, especially girls with high anxiety levels

### Target Learning Goals
- managing anxiety before high-stakes state examinations
- maintaining wellbeing across the final school year
