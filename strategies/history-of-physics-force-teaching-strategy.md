# Include aspects of the history of physics when teaching the force concept

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends drawing on the history of physics to support learning of the force concept. It notes that historical approaches can make students aware that Newton's laws today look quite different from what Newton originally formulated, which "may help them notice and make sense of the various conceptions of force they encounter in textbooks". The framework's categories are themselves motivated by the historical development of the concept, e.g. the momentum form of Newton's second law.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers or materials need access to historically grounded accounts of how the force concept developed, such as the distinctions between Newton's original momentum-based formulation and modern acceleration-based presentations
#### Constraints
- The article cites this as a suggestion by several authors (e.g. Coelho, 2010; Gauld, 2014) rather than as a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- upper secondary school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- noticing and making sense of the various conceptions of force encountered in textbooks
- understanding how physics developed as a human product
