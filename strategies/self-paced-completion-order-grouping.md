# Let self-paced completion order form skill-similar competing groups for practice games

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In the described lesson, grouping for the competitive game emerged naturally from the order in which students finished the previous activity, so that "students with similar mathematical skills were placed in the same group". The authors present this as an advantage of the individualized format, since such division is necessary especially during competition, and it required no separate sorting step by the teacher beyond supervision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A self-paced sequence of activities whose completion order reflects student skill, and a game application that supports multi-student competition.
#### Constraints
- The article describes this in a single 45-minute mathematics lesson with 27 students; it reports no test of the grouping's effect on learning.

### Target Learners
- students in a regular full classroom working individually on practice tasks

### Target Learning Goals
- practicing integer calculation and equation-solving skills through competition

## Claims

- [Students in the individualized tablet lesson progressed far enough to reach a competitive game activity that the frontal-instruction control group could not reach due to time constraints](../claims/individualized-pacing-reached-game-activity.md) [+M]
