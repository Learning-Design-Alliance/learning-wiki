# Incorporate spaced review, elaboration opportunities, dual coding, and frequent low-stakes assessments into medical curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "Curricula that incorporate spaced review of core knowledge, opportunities for elaboration through problem-solving and discussion, visual and verbal integration to support dual coding, and frequent low-stakes assessments that promote retrieval practice may support effective learning behaviors throughout medical training." This translates the study's strategy-performance associations into concrete curriculum design features, including low-stakes quizzes that support engagement and memory consolidation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curriculum structures that permit spaced review and repeated low-stakes assessment
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is cross-sectional and exploratory rather than causal

### Target Learners
- medical students across all academic years

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term retention of core knowledge
- active processing of information
