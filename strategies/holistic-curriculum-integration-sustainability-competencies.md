# Integrate all sustainability competencies holistically across dietetics curricula rather than emphasising isolated aspects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review concludes that "Education for dietitians and nutrition professionals should adopt a more comprehensive approach, integrating all sustainability competencies across the curriculum," arguing that insufficient coverage of all competencies within individual interventions can lead to gaps in students' overall competence development. It recommends teaching approaches address a broad range of competencies rather than emphasising isolated aspects, with holistic and coordinated integration across training programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained coordination and collaboration among faculty, curriculum planners and institutional leaders, plus time, institutional support and motivation
#### Constraints
- In case of limited time and capacity, the review notes specialisation may be appropriate, with students developing deep expertise in one or two competencies while building a foundation in others

### Target Learners
- Dietitians and nutrition professionals across undergraduate, master's and doctoral programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Comprehensive sustainability competency development, including underrepresented inter-personal, integration, intra-personal and futures-thinking competencies

### Affordances
- Redman Wiek Sustainability Competencies Framework
