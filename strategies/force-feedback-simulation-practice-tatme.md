# Repetitive practice of TaTME purse-string suturing in a safe simulation environment with qualitative and quantitative force feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, given observed force levels exceeding safety thresholds, trainees repeatedly practice the transanal purse-string suture on the instrumented box trainer outside the operating room. It states that "the repetitive practicing of this surgical technique in a safe simulation environment outside the OR is highly recommended" and that trainees may benefit from deliberate practice with qualitative and quantitative force feedback. The strategy uses the force and torque metrics identified in the study as objective feedback on instrument handling and tissue manipulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instrumented training setup capable of measuring port forces, torques, and tissue interaction forces
- Provision of qualitative and quantitative force feedback during training
#### Constraints
- The article notes the discriminating power of time and force parameters likely depends on the complexity of the assessed task

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees learning TaTME, particularly novices for the procedure

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing tissue-damaging forces during suturing
- improving instrument handling and collaboration
