# Include performance review and clinical mentoring meetings and follow-up training as complements to standard training and supportive supervision in iCCM quality improvement programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that iCCM programs pair initial clinical training with follow-up training (a half-day to one-day refresher visit within eight weeks, including observation of consultations and case scenarios) and PRCMM (two-day district-level meetings every six months combining register review with clinical practice and facilitator feedback). The authors state that "these interventions should be considered as complements to standard trainings and supportive supervision to improve the quality of iCCM services." Both interventions were significantly associated with correct management in their survey.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires trained facilitators, woreda iCCM facilitators or implementing partner NGO staff, access to iCCM patient registers, and health facility capacity for the clinical practice day of PRCMM
#### Constraints
- The evidence is observational without a comparison group, so causal conclusions are limited; the article notes these interventions are not always included in quality improvement programs

### Target Learners
- Health Extension Workers providing iCCM services at rural health posts

### Target Learning Goals
- Correct assessment, classification, treatment, and referral of childhood pneumonia, diarrhea, malaria, malnutrition, and measles according to iCCM clinical guidelines
