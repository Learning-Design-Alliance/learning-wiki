# Expect Excellence

## Description
Expect Excellence means communicating — through goals, feedback, task design, and daily interactions — that all students are capable of high-quality work, then providing the scaffolding to make that work achievable. In project-based learning (PBL) contexts, it counters the misconception that open-ended projects are unsuitable for struggling or marginalized learners by pairing ambitious goals with structured support.

## Design Implications

High expectations function through two mechanisms: they raise the goals students pursue, and they shape teacher behavior — more wait time, more substantive feedback, more re-engagement after failure [~M]. Goal-setting research shows that specific, difficult goals outperform easy or vague "do your best" goals, provided learners have the ability and commitment to pursue them [Specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance than vague or easy goals.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md) [+S]. Expectations also operate through self-efficacy: when students see credible evidence that peers like them succeed at demanding work, their persistence improves [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]. Crucially, high expectations without adequate scaffolding produce failure and disengagement rather than growth — the expectation must be paired with [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and contingent support [Contingent scaffolding improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Publicly communicated, specific quality criteria (rubrics, exemplars of excellent work) so "excellence" is observable rather than rhetorical
- Scaffolding that adapts to learner need — modeling, coaching, and fading — so demanding tasks remain attainable [Contingent scaffolding improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Feedback focused on task and process, not person, so students know how to close the gap to excellence [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- Revision cycles built into projects, signaling that first drafts are not final judgments of ability

#### Constraints
- High expectations without scaffolding widen achievement gaps: struggling learners experience repeated failure, lowering self-efficacy [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [-M]
- Teacher expectations can become self-fulfilling in the negative direction — low expectations produce differential treatment and depressed performance (Pygmalion effects) [~M]
- Praise that attributes success to innate ability ("you're so smart") undermines persistence after setbacks compared to process-focused framing [~M]
- Unrealistic expectations relative to available time and support produce burnout and disengagement rather than growth [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- Whole-culture level: school-wide norms, public exhibitions of high-quality student work, portfolios with revision histories
- Task level: tiered entry points into the same demanding project, so all students pursue the same standard through different supports
- Feedback level: critique protocols (e.g., gallery walks with "kind, specific, helpful" norms) that make excellence a shared, discussable standard

### Target Learners
- Students from marginalized or historically low-expectation backgrounds, for whom teacher expectation effects are strongest [~M]
- Learners with fragile self-efficacy who benefit from visible evidence that demanding work is achievable [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- All learners in open-ended PBL settings, where ambiguity makes quality criteria especially necessary

### Target Learning Goals
- Dispositional: persistence, ownership of quality, growth mindset
- Performance goals: producing work that meets authentic, high standards
- Self-regulation: internalizing quality criteria for self-assessment [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Define excellence concretely: publish rubrics and anchor exemplars so the standard is visible, not implied ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) can frame what excellent work looks like before students begin)
2. Set specific, challenging goals for each project rather than "do your best" framing [Specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance than vague or easy goals.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md) [+S]
3. Scaffold toward the standard: model expert work, then coach and progressively release responsibility as competence grows [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]
4. Give task- and process-level feedback on drafts, with structured opportunities to revise [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
5. Make excellence public: exhibit final work to authentic audiences ([Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md)) so the standard has real consequences

## Related Strategies
- **Establish a Culture of Revision** — normalizes drafts and critique, making high standards attainable rather than punitive
- **Critique Protocols (Kind, Specific, Helpful)** — structures peer feedback that operationalizes quality criteria
- **Public Exhibitions of Learning** — authentic audiences raise the stakes and credibility of excellence expectations

## Related Elements
- [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) — the ongoing support that makes demanding goals reachable
- [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) — rubrics and exemplars make the standard explicit and actionable
- [Scaffolding](../elements/scaffolding.md) — temporary support structures that must fade as competence grows

## Examples
- **Expeditionary Learning (EL Education)** — schools built around "Deeper Instruction" and public exhibitions of high-quality student work; crew structures pair high expectations with individualized support ([https://eleducation.org](https://eleducation.org))
- **High Tech High (San Diego)** — project-based school where all students exhibit polished work publicly, communicating that excellence is expected of everyone, not a select few ([https://www.hightechhigh.org](https://www.hightechhigh.org))
- **PBLWorks (Buck Institute) Gold Standard PBL** — lists "challenge and success" as a design principle: projects must be demanding yet scaffolded so all students can produce high-quality work ([https://www.pblworks.org](https://www.pblworks.org))
