# Prepare readers somatically and by belief-position before assigning challenging equity content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Adapting Resmaa Menakem's approach in My Grandmother's Hands, the authors address readers by what they believe about systemic oppression and warn that engaging the material may trigger trauma reflexes. They suggest readers allow bodily and emotional responses — reflexive constriction, defensive thoughts, a sudden shock of recognition or understanding — to move through them without holding on to them, then ask what the new knowledges commend us to do. This is a concrete reading-support recipe for difficult justice-related content.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The strategy requires acknowledging readers' positionality, since readers with multiple undervalued identities may find confirmation of their experiences while others find the material challenging
#### Constraints
- The authors note that for some readers engaging the material may trigger trauma reflexes and cause additional pain, and Menakem recommends such readers not read the book

### Target Learners
- physics community members reading equity-focused white papers
- readers with varied positionalities and privilege profiles

### Target Learning Goals
- processing challenging content about systemic oppression
- converting emotional response into action for justice
