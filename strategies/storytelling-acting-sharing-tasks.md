# Use storytelling and story-acting tasks for sharing reading experiences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the study, the article's most highly recommended extensive reading tasks are telling stories and acting the whole or part of the story. Sharing or reflecting reading experiences is described as "a real-life task which should be incorporated in reading instruction whenever possible", and RSA can be adapted to suit specific reading contexts with varying levels of students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time for multiple sharing rounds and play preparation
- Groups small enough for every member to retell her story
#### Constraints
- When time is limited, shorter readings such as short contemporary stories available on-line or in printed form are recommended

### Target Learners
- EFL students at varying levels

### Target Learning Goals
- speaking confidence
- listening comprehension
- summarizing and retelling

### Affordances
- Read Share Act Pattern
