Strategies

Offer a wide variety of optional form-focussed activities learners choose from according to individual needs, pre- or post-task¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because gaps between what learners want to communicate and what they can communicate differ for each learner, the authors recommend offering "a wide variety of FonF activities that they can choose from depending on their individual needs at a given-time (pre- or post-task)". Pre-task form focus may be most beneficial when students are not forced to do the activities but choose them on realising the task demands command of specific forms. This reinforces individualised post-task FonF based on each learner's needs.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learners become aware of gaps between intended and actual communicative ability
  • A database of contextualised activities on problematic structures adapted to CEFR levels

Constraints¶

  • Gains in accuracy from pre-task FonF were not shown systematically and none were statistically significant in this study

Target Learners¶

  • first-year university ESP students, particularly B1 learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • autonomous engagement with form-focussed practice; accuracy in written L2 production

Claims¶

  • Completing pre-task form-focussed micro-tasks produced no statistically significant differences in accuracy of targeted forms among B1 learners [~W]
  • Twelve of fifteen targeted forms appeared more frequently in productions of students who completed pre-tasks, but frequency differences were slight and non-significant [~W]
  • The virtual resource center was underexploited: under half of students visited it, mostly briefly, though users found it increasingly useful [~W]
