# Word-set exercises for contextualising vocabulary via imagined possible worlds

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Word-set exercises are open-ended puzzles in which learners infer the intended reader, topic and purpose of a letter from lists of presented words, imagining 'possible worlds' that contextualise the lexis and accessing relevant schemas and registers. The teacher elicits and challenges justifications for unusual choices while the class acts as a jury on their feasibility, generating oral practice with puzzle value. They develop awareness of the shifting meanings and style values words take in differing contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- They assume the declarative base, the provisional definition, has been fixed in place first, for example through completion of definition frames
#### Constraints
- There are no absolutely correct answers; the exercises depend heavily on teacher elicitation

### Target Learners
- orientation-year university students, e.g. engineering and business students at the University of Bahrain; adaptable to beginners via odd-one-out variants

### Target Learning Goals
- sociolinguistic awareness of register and context
- purposive reading strategies
- oral production through justifying choices
