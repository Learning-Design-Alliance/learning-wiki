# Deliver emergency simulation training at scale using 360-degree VR blended with in-person clinical placements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend 360-degree VR simulation as a scalable, cost-effective way to give large cohorts immersive emergency experience that clinical placements cannot guarantee. "Unlike in-person simulation, VR simulations can be delivered to 30 to 60 students simultaneously with just one or two facilitators." They position VR as blended learning alongside in-person placements, noting headsets (£300) cost markedly less than simulation suites (£100 k to 1 million).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- VR headsets and 360-degree filmed interactive scenarios; screening for kinetosis, hearing loss, or anxiety prospectively
#### Constraints
- The study did not compare VR to in-person simulation; VR scenarios are not a replacement for in-person simulation, which allows training in technical skills; a novelty effect may have influenced results in favour of VR

### Target Learners
- medical undergraduates
- large student cohorts in institutions with limited simulation facilities

### Target Learning Goals
- managing life-threatening emergencies
- situational awareness and remaining level-headed in dynamic scenarios
