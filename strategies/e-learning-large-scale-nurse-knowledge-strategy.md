# Use e-learning to improve knowledge in large groups of nurses as an alternative to labour-intensive traditional education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advocates e-learning as a scalable way to raise knowledge across large nursing workforces: "As an alternative to traditional education, which is relatively labour-intensive and time-consuming, e-learning can be useful when improving knowledge in a large group of nurses", citing flexibility in time management, pace of learning, and quality assurance. The study's own results — significant gains in all subgroups and a high participation rate — support wider introduction of delirium e-learning in hospitals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to computers with internet, email addresses of staff, and organizational supports such as introductory meetings and reminders
#### Constraints
- Barriers such as a limited level of computer literacy in users are associated with the use of e-learning; the study measured knowledge only shortly after completion, with no insight into knowledge retention

### Target Learners
- Hospital nursing staff, including nurses, nursing students and healthcare assistants

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical knowledge improvement (delirium recognition and care) at scale

## Claims

- [Baseline knowledge differed by age and education level, but pre-to-post change scores did not significantly differ between demographic subgroups in a single-group pre-post study of an e-learning course](../claims/baseline-demographic-differences-no-subgroup-effect-differences.md) [+M]
- [Nursing staff scored lowest at baseline on definition-of-delirium questions (74.3), and scores in this category rose by 11.3 from pre-test to post-test in one uncontrolled pre-post study](../claims/definition-category-lowest-baseline-highest-gain.md) [+M]
- [Baseline delirium knowledge of Dutch hospital nursing staff was relatively high (mean 79.3), contrary to other studies](../claims/dutch-nurses-baseline-delirium-knowledge-high.md) [~M]

- [Participation in the e-learning course was high, with 86.4% of invitees starting the baseline test and 95.4% of participants completing the final test](../claims/high-participation-rate-nursing-e-learning.md) [+M]
