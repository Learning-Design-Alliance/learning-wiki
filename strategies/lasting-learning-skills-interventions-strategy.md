# Provide learning-skills interventions with lasting impact, targeting relaxed and sociable learners and building metacognitive knowledge toward diligent or hard-working strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that medical educators develop interventions on learning skills for students showing unfavorable strategy patterns. They state that "Both, the relaxed and the sociable learners are likely to benefit from learning skill interventions" and that educators need to "impart sufficient metacognitive knowledge to adopt the hard-working if not the diligent strategy", showing students how to gain control over their learning processes to become proficient lifelong learners. The intervention must produce a lasting change in the pattern of learning strategy combinations, since early sociable habits were quickly abandoned without shifting toward more successful patterns.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions on learning skills must have a lasting impact on the pattern of the students' learning strategy combinations, not only short-term change
#### Constraints
- Supporting evidence comes from one monocentric cohort with a small sample (107 students) and covers only biology and physiology examinations, so benefit for other subjects is not established

### Target Learners
- Preclinical medical students, particularly those with relaxed or sociable learning patterns

### Target Learning Goals
- Adopting diligent or hard-working learning strategy combinations; gaining control over learning processes for lifelong learning

## Claims

- [Diligent and hard-working learners obtained significantly better biology and physiology examination scores than relaxed and sociable learners when grouped by learning preference](../claims/diligent-hard-working-higher-biology-physiology-scores.md) [+W]
