# Reduce the number of students per CBL session by adding classrooms, subgroups and teachers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because a majority of students (54.4%) identified the 20-participant session size as a barrier, the authors recommend shrinking session groups. The article states: "A reduction in the number of students in each session is recommended, and this can be achieved by increasing the number of classrooms, subgroups, and teachers." It also cites research suggesting smaller groups of 4 or 5 students can increase social presence, commitment and participation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Additional resources: more classrooms, subgroups and teachers to absorb smaller groups
#### Constraints
- The article notes there is no one ideal size for discussion groups; group-size decisions should weigh course content, learning objectives, pedagogical approach, assessment methods and instructor workload

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in clerkship-phase case-based learning

### Target Learning Goals
- Equal participation in CBL discussion
- Social presence and commitment in group learning
