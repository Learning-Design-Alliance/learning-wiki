# Suite of NAPLAN reforms: online testing, writing redesign, LBOTE investment, My School redesign, public assessment literacy, and broadened scope

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The report proposes five implementable reforms to reduce the hazards of high-stakes assessment while retaining its diagnostic value. These are "transitioning to NAPLAN's online testing, refining the written content and the 'My School' platform, enhancing public assessment literacy, bolstering investment in LBOTE education, and broadening NAPLAN's scope". Online computerised marking would return data to schools within days versus the five-month paper cycle; writing redesign would incorporate teacher formative assessments into the summative assessment; schools would lead workshops, seminars and simplified reports to build assessment literacy; and pilot tests in select regions would extend coverage to natural science, creativity, critical thinking and problem-solving.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools and educational authorities lead communication of NAPLAN's objectives and processes to parents and students before and after assessments
- Further research on construct validity is needed before online testing is fully relied upon
#### Constraints
- The report notes online testing brings challenges concerning construct validity, necessitating further research to ensure NAPLAN's validity

### Target Learners
- Australian students in Years 3, 5, 7, and 9
- LBOTE students including ESL and EFL learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Literacy and numeracy achievement
- Higher-order thinking skills including creativity, critical thinking and problem-solving
