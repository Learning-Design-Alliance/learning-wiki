# Tailor SEL assessment and intervention programmes to the cultural context of Arab communities in Israel

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that SEL work in Arabic-speaking communities account for collectivist values, religious identity, minority status and systemic inequalities. It states that "SEL assessment and intervention programmes must be tailored to the unique context of Arab communities in Israel" and that curricula should reflect "local cultural, linguistic and historical identities, for instance by incorporating community traditions, stories, native languages". Schools should engage parents and community members as partners in designing SEL initiatives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Engagement of parents and community members so local values and voices inform programme design and objectives
#### Constraints
- The article notes global, Western-based SEL models have only partial applicability in this community; evidence for the recommendation is the study's discrepancy findings plus cited cross-cultural frameworks, not a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- Arabic-speaking students in Arab communities in Israel

### Target Learning Goals
- Social–emotional skill development and accurate SEL assessment in a culturally attuned form

## Claims

- [Parents rate Arabic-speaking students' SEL skills higher than students themselves, and teachers rate them lowest (parents > students > teachers), regardless of grade level](../claims/sel-perception-discrepancy-parents-students-teachers.md) [+M]
- [In SEM, only teachers' SEL reports significantly predicted students' academic scores, with students' and parents' contributions non-significant](../claims/sem-only-teachers-sel-predicts-academic-scores.md) [+M]
