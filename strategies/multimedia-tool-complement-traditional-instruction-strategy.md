# Integrate a multimedia learning platform as a complement to traditional instruction, assigning curated playlists with embedded quizzes and flashcards for spaced repetition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study's implementation assigned Osmosis videos alongside, not instead of, didactic teaching: "playlists of assigned videos from Osmosis were recommended to be watched before the live sessions throughout the course along with multiple attempts to watch the videos, answer the embedded quiz questions or flashcards." Completion of assigned questions and flashcards earned 2% participation credit, and faculty monitored weekly engagement on dashboards. The authors frame this as using commercially available multimedia resources "for providing spaced repetition to achieve higher learning retention."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Same instructors and learning management system across groups to control confounders; platform access for students; alignment of assigned videos with covered course content after the material is taught.
#### Constraints
- The authors state effectiveness may depend on the specific multimedia tool, how it was implemented to provide spaced repetition, the subject matter, and learner characteristics; difficulty of subject matter and extent of contact with concepts should be considered when planning integration.

### Target Learners
- medical and podiatric medical students in preclinical and early clinical courses

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge retention
- concept understanding
- course performance
