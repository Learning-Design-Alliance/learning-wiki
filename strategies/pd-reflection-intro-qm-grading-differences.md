# Professional development leaders can ask TAs to reflect on differences in their grading of intro and QM solutions together with the PER recommendation on grading explication of the problem solving process

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that leaders of professional development courses use the intro-versus-QM grading activity as a reflection tool: "Leaders of professional development courses can ask TAs to reflect on any differences in their grading of the intro and QM solutions along with the PER recommendation". The authors state this reflection, combined with the recommendation about grading on explication of the problem solving process, may help TAs transition towards desirable grading practices in all courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- TAs grade both intro physics and QM student solutions within a professional development course so that differences in their criteria can surface for reflection
#### Constraints
- The activity was studied with first-year physics TAs in a mandatory, semester-long professional development course at a research university in the U.S.

### Target Learners
- first-year physics graduate teaching assistants

### Target Learning Goals
- reflective grading practices
- explication of the problem solving process in student solutions
- use of grading as formative assessment

## Claims

- [Two-thirds of the TAs stated they would grade QM and intro physics solutions using different criteria](../claims/two-thirds-tas-stated-different-criteria-intro-qm.md) [+W]
- [Forty percent of TAs would grade advanced students on explication of concepts but intro students on correct formulas, mathematical steps and the final answer](../claims/forty-percent-grade-concepts-qm-equations-intro.md) [+W]
- [Twenty-six percent of TAs expected explicit demonstration of understanding in QM because the subject is more complex, and another twenty-six percent because it is expected of advanced students](../claims/tas-expect-explicit-understanding-qm-complexity-advanced-expectations.md) [+W]
