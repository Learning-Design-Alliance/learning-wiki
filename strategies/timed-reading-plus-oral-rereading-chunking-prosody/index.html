Combine timed reading with repeated oral reading using chunked text and prosody modeling, holding comprehension at a 70% criterion¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The TROR treatment had students read each passage silently under time pressure, then reread it aloud twice: once chorally after the instructor modeled a passage marked with forward slashes separating 3–5 word phrases, and once to a partner mimicking the instructor's prosody, pace, and rhythm. Students aimed for at least six of eight comprehension questions correct and were told to read more carefully if below 70% or push faster if perfect. This combination produced significant rate and comprehension gains.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Individual stopwatches for timing accuracy
  • Passages matched to learners' vocabulary level (first 1,000-word level here)
  • Strategy training and two acclimation practice passages before treatment

Constraints¶

  • The article reports no statistically significant difference between this combined treatment and timed reading alone

Target Learners¶

  • lower-proficiency Japanese university EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • silent reading rate
  • reading comprehension
  • chunking and prosody

Affordances¶

  • Reading Fluency Automaticity Account

Claims¶

  • The TROR group (timed reading plus repeated oral reading with chunking and prosody) made statistically significant reading rate gains of about 13 to 27 swpm across four scoring methods [+W]
  • The TROR group showed significant comprehension gains on the average and last-minus-first scoring methods, but not on the extreme methods [+W]
