Use multi-day training protocols in human eyeblink conditioning rather than single-day-only designs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 8 claims (5 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends extending human eyeblink conditioning studies beyond single-session designs, arguing that "single-day studies with fewer than 100 trials may overlook the underlying mechanisms of cerebellar consolidation and the benefits of extended training across multiple days." Its own 3-day, 300-trial protocol demonstrated continued learning gains on days 2 and 3. Future studies are also encouraged to compare different training intervals, such as schedules spanning two weeks versus two months.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • At least one additional training session beyond day 1; the article's protocol used 100 trials per day across 3 non-consecutive days

Constraints¶

  • The article does not establish a learning asymptote or identify the optimal number of days; additional training days to establish an asymptote and a non-associative control condition are listed as beneficial future additions

  • young human adults

  • cerebellar associative motor learning (conditioned eyeblink response acquisition)

Claims¶

  • Multi-day embedded eyeblink conditioning improved blink amplitude and frequency on days 2 and 3 [+W]
  • CR onset latency variation tends to increase over days while participant sex shows no significant effect on either CR measure [~W]
  • CR percentage gains occur on both day 2 and day 3, with a larger gain on day 2 than day 3 [+W]
  • Human eyeblink conditioning learning continues across days until maximum CS-US associative strength is reached, where CRs saturate [+W]
  • CR percentage improves significantly with each additional day of multi-day embedded eyeblink conditioning training [+W]
  • Length of intervals between training days does not significantly affect CR percentage [~W]
  • Short CS-US intervals (150 and 250 ms) produce very few conditioned responses in humans [~W]
  • Training blocks significantly increase conditioned response percentage in delay eyeblink conditioning [+W]
