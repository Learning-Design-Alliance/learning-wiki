# Alternate winter and summer assessments over multiple years to control for novelty and season effects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
To go beyond short-term studies that "may suffer from effects of novelty and season", the protocol schedules six assessment waves (T0–T5) at approximately 6-month intervals, alternately in summer (July 2017, 2018, 2019) and winter (January/February 2018, 2019) over three academic years. The article argues this design "will not suffer from effects of novelty, and effects of season are taken into account" because students' enthusiasm may wane and children are more activity prone in summer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A study duration long enough (two-and-a-half years) to extend beyond the novelty period of the desks, with matched seasonal measurement windows
#### Constraints
- Requires a cohort young enough to remain in the same school for the duration; grade three was chosen as the oldest grade permitting a study of this length before curricular changes in grade six

### Target Learners
- primary school children aged 8–9

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term measurement of sedentary time and academic performance
