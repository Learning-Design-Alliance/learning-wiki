# Create inclusive, supportive, process-oriented sports environments that prioritize participation experience over competition for adolescents with high BMI

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators and parents build activity settings emphasizing "participation experience, cooperative interaction, and emotional development, rather than simply prioritizing competition and sports performance." This is grounded in the authors' argument that ridicule, competitive exclusion, or social rejection can turn physical activity into a new stressor that weakens or reverses its positive mental-health effects in this population.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- activity contexts free of ridicule, competitive exclusion, or social rejection
#### Constraints
- the study did not measure activity context (stigma vs. social support), so this mechanism was not directly tested

### Target Learners
- adolescents with high BMI vulnerable to weight stigma and peer exclusion

### Target Learning Goals
- mental health and well-being
- sustained participation in physical activity
