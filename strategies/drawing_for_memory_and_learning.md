# Drawing for Memory and Learning

## Description
Drawing is used as a learning and memory tool. Drawing forces individuals to process information visually, kinesthetically, and semantically, leading to better recall. Studies show that drawing concepts improves recall compared to writing notes verbatim. Drawing activates multiple modalities (visual, kinesthetic, and semantic), resulting in deeper processing and better retention of information.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Drawing materials (paper, pens, pencils, etc.). Concepts or information to be learned and drawn.
#### Constraints
- It may be misinterpreted that drawing is beneficial because it taps into a particular learning style. Instead, drawing taps into multiple modalities—visual, kinesthetic, and semantic—which is superior to tapping into only one.

### Target Learners

- Students of all ages and levels, including undergraduate students and adult learners.

### Target Learning Goals

- Enhance memory and recall, deepen understanding of concepts, and engage multiple cognitive processes.

### Affordances
- Active engagement, multi-modal processing (visual, kinesthetic, semantic), and improved recall compared to passive learning methods such as listening or reading. Effective encoding strategy that is reliable and replicable.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to various subjects and concepts. Students can create drawings based on their understanding and learning style. The benefit of drawing is not dependent on the students’ level of artistic talent.

## Assessment Evidence
- Assess memory and recall through tests or quizzes. Evaluate the depth of understanding reflected in the drawings. Compare recall rates between drawing and other methods like writing.

## Impact
- Increases recall by nearly double compared to writing. Confirmed to be a reliable, replicable means of boosting performance. It requires elaboration on the meaning of the term and translating the definition to a new form (a picture).
