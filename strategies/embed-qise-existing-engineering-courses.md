# Expand QISE access by embedding QISE concepts into existing engineering courses, investing at non-PhD-granting institutions, and offering interdisciplinary courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (3 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends four growth opportunities for engineering programs: "Embedding QISE concepts or modules into relevant existing courses within engineering disciplines", "Investing in the development of QISE programs and coursework at non-PhD-granting institutions", offering interdisciplinary courses for access across subdisciplines, and integrating QISE into mechanical engineering, the largest subdiscipline by degrees granted. Embedding minimizes added coursework in rigid engineering degree programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Engineering curricula have limited elective room, so integration should use existing relevant courses such as physical electronics, materials science, or semiconductors
#### Constraints
- Adding standalone QISE courses to all existing engineering programs may not always be feasible due to program rigidity

### Target Learners
- engineering undergraduate and graduate students, including students at two-year, four-year, and non-PhD-granting institutions

### Target Learning Goals
- quantum information science and engineering knowledge and workforce preparation
