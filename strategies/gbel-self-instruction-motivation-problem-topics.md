# Use game-based e-learning for self-instruction on topics where student motivation is a problem

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' recommendation from the trial: "Game-based E-learning should be taken seriously into account as an alternative instructional method on topics where student motivation might be a problem." They conclude GbEl "can be used as an effective teaching method for self-instruction", supported by the significantly higher test scores and desire for more material of this style in the game group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators should carefully balance benefits and costs prior to project start, since game development remains work-intensive
#### Constraints
- Evidence from prior systematic reviews was limited and inconsistent; the authors could not separate motivational effects mediated by longer training time from instructional efficacy

### Target Learners
- medical students in undergraduate training

### Target Learning Goals
- self-study of cognitive content, e.g. laboratory diagnostics
