# Include multiple observers in simulation scenarios for stressful procedures so trainees learn to manage audience-related stress

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that simulation training for neonatal endotracheal intubation deliberately include non-mandatory personnel as observers, because the trial showed observers raise trainee heart rate without lengthening intubation. As the conclusion states, "it is imperative that simulation scenarios be representative of real life clinical situations where multiple observers are often present." The authors suggest this may "better equip residents to deal with such stressors" and that educators might improve training by providing learners with resources in stress management.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation scenarios must include an audience of observers comparable to real clinical settings, since the presence of non-mandatory personnel as observers is stressful to trainees
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence comes from a mannequin-based task without distractors or simulated vital signs; the authors note results may have differed with an audience combined with distractors as in a real NICU

### Target Learners
- first-year pediatric and neonatology residents with no experience in neonatal intubation

### Target Learning Goals
- performing neonatal endotracheal intubation under stress
- managing stress during procedures performed before multiple observers
