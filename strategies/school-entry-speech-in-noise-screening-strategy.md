# Screen for speech-in-noise ability at school entry to identify children at risk of literacy delay

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend applying speech-in-noise screening at school entry, arguing that existing tools are not routinely used and that early identification could enable early intervention and individually appropriate teaching. They state that screening tools to flag speech-in-noise deficits "are not routinely applied at school entry, suggesting we are missing a key opportunity for early identification of children at risk of developing a literacy deficit". The strategy rests on the Grade 3-only association between speech-in-noise ability and literacy found in this study, and the authors call for further research on younger children to establish predictive value.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated speech-in-noise screening tool such as Sound Scouts, and further research establishing predictive value in children younger than those studied
#### Constraints
- The association between speech-in-noise and literacy was observed only in the younger (Grade 3) cohort, and the authors note screening for phonological awareness and decoding alone does not provide adequate insight into underlying mechanisms of individual learning delay

### Target Learners
- children at school entry and in the early primary years

### Target Learning Goals
- early identification of children at risk of reading and literacy delays

### Affordances
- Phonological Processing Account Speech In Noise Literacy
