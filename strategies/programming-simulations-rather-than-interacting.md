# Having students program simulations themselves rather than interact with pre-written ones

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article advocates that students engage in the actual creation of the simulation programs as opposed to simply interacting with pre-written simulations. The author argues that programming the simulations themselves motivates students to understand the mechanics of the PLA as they implement it. By programming the optimization function and incrementally changing parameters, students internalize why the action principle dictates light's behavior. The author notes that 'the latter method will motivate students to understand the mechanics of the PLA as they implement it themselves.'

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need familiarity with Python programming or willingness to learn it, as the simulations are written in Python.
#### Constraints
- The approach requires more time than simply interacting with pre-written simulations.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- understand the mechanics of the Principle of Least Action
- internalize optimization proofs for reflection and refraction
