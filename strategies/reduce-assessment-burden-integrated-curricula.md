# Reduce assessment burden in integrated spiral curricula by combining formative and summative assessments or using technology-enhanced assessment platforms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that future iterations of R-ISAM address the assessment intensity students and faculty reported. Students perceived the number of examinations as high and the cumulative workload as demanding, and faculty noted increased administrative workload from expanded practical examinations and OSPE stations. The authors state that "Future iterations of R-ISAM should consider strategies to reduce assessment burden, such as combining formative and summative assessments or using technology-enhanced assessment platforms".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional support for assessment redesign and technology-enhanced platforms
#### Constraints
- OSPE/OSCE remain valid tools but their implementation can be resource-intensive and administratively demanding

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students in integrated anatomy curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- Managing assessment workload while maintaining competency-based evaluation
