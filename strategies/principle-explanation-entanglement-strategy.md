# Teach quantum entanglement via principle explanation in analogy to the standard special relativity introduction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that physics educators introduce and resolve the mystery of quantum entanglement using the Mermin device, Figures 1-12, and Tables I-IV, "in precise analogy to the standard introduction of SR in introductory physics textbooks." Just as textbooks derive the light postulate from NPRF and time dilation and length contraction from the light postulate, educators can derive the Planck postulate from NPRF and 'average-only' conservation from the Planck postulate, giving first-year or gen ed students a complete, rigorous, accessible resolution of the trilemma.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should have covered the first two chapters on quantum physics in Serway & Jewett (Planck's radiation law, Schrödinger equation, photoelectric effect) before the entanglement unit
- The mathematical details are optional and may be shared with more advanced students at the instructor's discretion
#### Constraints
- The authors expect serious delay before widespread textbook adoption, citing the historical precedent of Maxwell's equations taking decades to reach the textbook stage

### Target Learners
- first-year physics students in the introductory sequence for scientists and engineers
- general education students

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptually introducing and resolving the mystery of quantum entanglement
- connecting quantum entanglement to quantum information science
