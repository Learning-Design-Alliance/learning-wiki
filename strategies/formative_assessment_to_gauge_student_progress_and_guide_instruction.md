# Formative Assessment to Gauge Student Progress and Guide Instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Formative assessment is the ongoing collection and interpretation of evidence about student learning *during* a learning sequence, used to adapt teaching and learning while there is still time to act. Unlike summative assessment, which certifies outcomes after instruction, formative assessment functions as feedback loops for both parties: students learn where they stand relative to the goal, and instructors learn where instruction is working and where it needs adjustment.

## Design Implications

Formative assessment is among the most consistently supported instructional practices, with effect sizes among the largest in educational research when it includes actionable feedback rather than mere measurement [Formative feedback improves achievement when it addresses task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. Its power depends on the *use* of the evidence, not its collection: assessment only becomes formative when the instructor adjusts instruction or the student adjusts learning behavior in response [Assessment functions formatively only when evidence is acted upon.](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) [+M]. Frequent low-stakes checks also strengthen self-regulation by prompting students to monitor their own progress against criteria [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Regularly scheduled low-stakes checks (quizzes, exit tickets, drafts, comprehension questions) aligned to explicit learning goals
- Success criteria students can understand and use to judge their own work ([Rubrics](../elements/rubrics.md) or annotated exemplars)
- A feedback channel that reaches students quickly enough to matter ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md))
- Instructor time or tooling to review evidence and adjust subsequent instruction

#### Constraints
- Feedback that arrives too late, or that reports only scores without guidance, produces little or no learning gain [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels, not at self level.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [-S]
- Grading everything formative can crowd out instruction and encourage grade-chasing rather than learning; keeping checks ungraded or lightly weighted preserves their function
- In large or asynchronous online courses, instructor response capacity is limited; automated checks scale but miss misconceptions that require human interpretation
- Frequent testing without corrective follow-up can entrench errors — high-confidence wrong answers persist unless addressed [High-confidence errors improve retention even when wrong.](../claims/high-confidence-errors-improve-retention.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Instructor-driven:** quizzes, drafts with comments, observation protocols, question-driven [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md)
- **Student-driven:** self-assessment against rubrics, peer review, reflection journals — builds evaluative judgment [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]
- **Technology-mediated:** embedded comprehension checks, adaptive quizzing ([Adaptive Difficulty](../elements/adaptive-difficulty.md)), weekly pulse surveys in LMS platforms
- **Cadence:** daily exit tickets vs. weekly checkpoints vs. milestone drafts; the right cadence depends on how quickly misconceptions compound in the subject

### Target Learners
- Struggling learners benefit most, because early detection prevents error accumulation — but only if feedback is specific and actionable [Feedback most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- Adult learners in online and blended environments, who lack informal in-person signals and need explicit progress evidence
- Novices, who cannot yet judge their own understanding accurately and rely on external checks [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Advanced learners need less frequent checking; over-assessment can feel like surveillance and reduce autonomy [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Skill and knowledge development: diagnosing gaps before they compound
- Self-regulated learning: building students' capacity to evaluate their own work against criteria
- Instructional calibration: giving instructors evidence to adjust pacing, grouping, and re-teaching decisions
- Metacognition: prompting students to reflect on what they know and don't know

### Instructions
1. Define the learning goal and success criteria students can see ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) or rubrics)
2. Collect evidence of current understanding through a low-stakes check ([Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md)) — quiz, exit ticket, draft, or discussion probe
3. Interpret the evidence against the goal: who is ready, who has a specific misconception, what should change tomorrow
4. Deliver feedback that names the gap and the next step ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)) [Feedback most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
5. Adjust instruction — re-teach, regroup, or accelerate — and give students a chance to act on the feedback ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
6. Re-check after the adjustment to close the loop ([Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md))

## Examples
- **Weekly draft feedback in a writing course:** students submit a draft section each week; instructor returns two process-level comments (one strength, one next step) rather than line edits; students revise before the final submission.
- **Exit tickets in a math lesson:** last five minutes, one problem targeting the day's concept; instructor sorts responses into "got it / partial / misconception" piles and plans the warm-up accordingly.
- **Asynchronous online course pulse checks:** a weekly two-question survey plus an auto-graded quiz; the instructor posts a short video addressing the most common error from the quiz, closing the loop for the whole cohort at once.
