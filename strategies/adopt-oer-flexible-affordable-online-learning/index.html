Adopt OER to make digital learning flexible and affordable for learners facing medical, financial, and daily life challenges¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends using open educational resources in online learning, arguing that the COVID-19 pandemic pushed institutions worldwide to shift instruction digital and that OER can "make learning more flexible and affordable for those who face medical, financial, and daily life challenges." It treats Hilton's (2016) synthesis as collective evidence supporting adoption and as guidance on how OER can be used internationally.

Claims¶

  • OER effectiveness in online courses is attributed to their technological affordances [+W]
  • Synthesized studies of OER in college courses found academic outcomes comparable to traditional textbooks and positive perceptions among students and instructors [+M]
  • Open textbooks appear to offer financial benefits to students without decreasing learning outcomes in higher education [+M]
  • In direct comparisons of OER and commercial textbooks, a strong majority of participants report OER are as good or better. [+M]
  • The COVID-19 pandemic pushed institutions globally to abruptly shift instruction digital [+W]
