# Spaced Practice

## Description
Spaced practice involves distributing learning sessions over time, contrasting with massed practice (cramming). This technique enhances long-term retention by requiring the brain to actively recall information, creating stronger neural connections. Educators can implement this by breaking large lessons into smaller sessions spread out over several days and use retrieval practice, beginning each session with opportunities for learners to recall prior lessons. Online tools can support spaced practice by allowing personalized study schedules, sending reminders, and incorporating online flashcards with mixed content. To optimize spaced practice, there can be a maximum time allowed for learners to spend in one session, timing them out and only allowing learners to re-engage once the desired time interval has passed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires planning and scheduling of learning sessions. Access to study materials and tools. Implementation can be facilitated by online platforms that automate spacing and reminders.
#### Constraints
- May require initial effort to set up schedules. Learners may resist the method due to its distributed nature. Requires consistent adherence to the schedule for optimal results.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track learner performance over time to measure retention. Compare results of spaced practice versus massed practice. Monitor learner engagement and adherence to study schedules.

## Impact
- Research consistently shows spaced practice is superior to massed practice for long-term retention. Retrieval practice paired with spaced practice further enhances memory. Savings in relearning can occur with spaced review sessions—i.e., individuals may not be able to recall every item correctly at the start of a review session (because of forgetting), but are quicker at relearning the information.
