Strategies

Strengthen vascular simulation research with modern validity frameworks, longer skill-retention follow-up, cost-effectiveness analysis, and more nontechnical-skills and cadaver models¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's forward-looking recommendations for integrating simulation into vascular curricula: prospective studies should use Messick's modern validity framework, include "a longer follow-up time of trainee skill retention and discussion of cost-effectiveness", and validate simulators "in large, well-designed randomized control trials with adequate follow-up time". It also calls for greater implementation of nontechnical-skills testing and of cadaver models alongside other modalities, and for novel high-fidelity simulators with greater sensory and haptic feedback for basic endovascular skills.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Future trials must include adequate follow-up time and larger samples, since many current trials had small sample sizes.
  • Training institutions and designers should implement cadaver models in combination with other simulation modalities.

Constraints¶

  • Open vascular simulators face greater resistance and cost than endovascular counterparts because a three-dimensional field is harder to replicate than a two-dimensional computer system; NTS implementation carries high costs of implementation and maintenance.

Target Learners¶

  • vascular surgery trainees
  • surgical trainees at any stage
  • medical students on vascular placements

Target Learning Goals¶

  • open and endovascular procedural skill proficiency
  • nontechnical skills such as teamwork and communication
  • skill retention over time

Affordances¶

  • Messick Validity Loe Simulator Evaluation

Claims¶

  • Only seven studies of vascular surgery simulation achieved a level of effectiveness greater than 3/5, with the majority scoring LoE 1-2/5 [+W]
  • Nontechnical-skills simulation of ruptured AAA with the Resusci Anne Simulator directly improved patient outcomes, reducing door-to-occlusion time, door-to-needle time, and 30-day mortality (LoE 4/5) [+W]
  • High-fidelity simulation methods (simulators, 3D models, cadaver-based practice) are associated with superior Acquisition retention, though some studies report contradictory findings [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Develop and validate a reusable, cost-effective, high-fidelity training platform and build homogeneous assessment tools for aneurysm clipping simulation
  • Prioritize larger multi-center RCTs with standardized AR systems, uniform metrics, and long-term follow-up when evaluating AR surgical training
  • Future research should use standardized validated outcome measures, longitudinal follow-up designs, and methods linking learner gains to patient-relevant outcomes
  • Future research should include long-term RCTs with adequate sample sizes, standardized outcome measures, and cost-effectiveness analyses
