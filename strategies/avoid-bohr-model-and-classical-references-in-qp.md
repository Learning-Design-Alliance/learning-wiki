# Avoid classical-physics framing and the Bohr atom model when introducing quantum concepts such as electron orbits

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on the reviewed literature (Ireson 1999, Greson 1999) and the study's own questionnaire data, the article recommends that instruction avoid linking quantum concepts to classical physics and skip the Bohr model when introducing the hydrogen atom. The author argues that "if instructors do not introduce Bohr's model of atom students won't be confused about the orbits of electrons and do not fall for the incorrect statement", a recommendation the faculty member Albert also supported.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must select instructional sequences and textbook treatments that omit or defer Bohr-model orbit descriptions when introducing quantum phenomena
