# Use scenario-based high-fidelity ALS simulation with video-review debriefing and realistic scenario elements in undergraduate medical education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementation strategy is a scenario-based high-fidelity simulation in which teams of three students manage a scripted cardiac arrest case, followed by video-review debriefing. Students highlighted the realism: "The presence of the patient's relative inside increased the realism," and one noted that watching the recording showed their actual compression pace differed from what they believed. The authors report the training was subsequently made a mandatory curriculum component.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulation center with high-fidelity simulators and video recording capability
- Scenarios developed with emergency medicine specialists, national ALS instructors, and paramedics
- CPR feedback wristbands for objective compression measurement
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted at a single center with final-year medical students who had completed the ALS course and anesthesiology internship

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- advanced life support technical skills
- teamwork and leadership in resuscitation
- accurate self-evaluation
