# Use training-level group contrasts (novice vs mid-level; proficient vs expert) as the core construct-validation design for simulation-based skills assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (7 for, 2 mixed) · 5 studies (3 design, 2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's validation strategy compares performance across cohorts defined by training status and self-reported surgical and simulation exposure, testing whether scores increase monotonically with expected skill level. The design pairs two contrasts: novice (PGY-1) vs mid-level (PGY-3) residents as the primary outcome, and ABOG-certified proficient surgeons vs fellowship-trained experts as the principal secondary outcome. Standardized orientation, blinded proctors, and central review of video and specimens support score comparability across sites. This strategy operationalizes construct validity as the ability of the assessment to distinguish groups expected to differ in the measured skill.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cohorts must be defined both by level of training and self-described exposure to surgery and simulation, with a qualification survey to screen participants
- Sample size of at least 30 evaluable participants per cohort to meet normal-distribution assumptions under the central limit theorem
#### Constraints
- Expert-novice comparisons have been argued to add little to a validity argument (Cook et al, cited in the article), and selection bias can arise when higher-level trainees come from simulation-rich programs

### Target Learners
- Surgical trainees across training levels (residents, certified specialists, fellowship-trained experts)

### Target Learning Goals
- Establishing construct validity evidence for simulation-based technical skills assessments
