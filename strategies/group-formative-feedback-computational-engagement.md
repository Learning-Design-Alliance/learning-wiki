# Use group work with written formative feedback to keep every student engaged in computational work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy pairs group-of-four computational work with tutor interactions and written formative feedback that names what each student does well and what they can improve. The article reports that the group focus coupled with tutor interactions helped mitigate the major challenge of keeping each student engaged with the work, avoiding a single VPython person controlling the computer. Feedback in the first week often tells students who are driving the work too much to step back, or hesitant students to take more responsibility, and early computational projects signal that every student is expected to learn computation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors who observe group work and deliver individualized written formative feedback
- Computational projects placed early in the semester to signal expectations
- Rewarding engagement, sharing ideas, and debating through scores
#### Constraints
- The authors note they have not completely solved the engagement problem, and not every student performs the same tasks since there is only one computer for four students

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students in groups of four

### Target Learning Goals
- computational modeling
- collaborative problem-solving
- shared responsibility for learning
