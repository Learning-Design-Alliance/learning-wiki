# Repairing Harm

## Description
Repairing harm is a restorative practice where, instead of simply being sent off to a punishment, a student who has misbehaved is tasked with figuring out how to repair whatever damage was caused by their behavior, and then to actually carry out that repair with everyone who was impacted by their actions. First, the student meets with an adult to identify the harm caused and all the stakeholders impacted. Next, the student considers how the behavior affected others, developing empathy. Finally, the student creates and carries out a plan to repair the harm with every stakeholder, which may involve an apology or more extensive actions. This process is an alternative to traditional punishment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An administrator or responsible adult to facilitate the process, a private conference space, and a willingness from the student to engage in self-reflection and take responsibility for their actions.
#### Constraints
- May take more time than traditional punishment and requires a genuine effort from the student to understand and repair the harm caused. Some students may deny the behavior, requiring careful handling by the adult facilitator.

### Target Learning Goals

### Affordances
- Helps students understand the ripple effect of their actions, develop empathy skills, and take responsibility for their behavior. It is more effective in opening up a child's eyes to how their behavior made others feel and offers a less traumatic approach compared to traditional punishment.

### Personalization
- Adaptations can be made based on the severity of the harm and the needs of the stakeholders involved.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the student's engagement in the process, the quality of the repair plan, and the impact of the repair on the stakeholders involved.

## Impact
- Restorative justice practices like repairing harm can lead to deeper and longer-lasting regret in students, stemming from a place of empathy. It is a more effective way to open up a child's eyes to how their behavior made others feel, and it is less traumatic than traditional punishment.
