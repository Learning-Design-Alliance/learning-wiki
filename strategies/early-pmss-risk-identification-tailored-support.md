# Use PMSS scores early in medical education to identify students at risk of poor academic performance and offer tailored support programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend assessing perceived Medical School stress with the PMSS relatively early, because scores predict later exam performance and high-stress students "still have sufficient time to learn and practice stress-management techniques". Tailored support programs may prevent at-risk students from entering a vicious circle of high stress and poor performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stress must be measured early enough — the study notes measuring more than 1 year before the M1 exam leaves time for intervention
#### Constraints
- The predictive value of the PMSS for academic performance needs confirmation in further studies; larger studies are needed to control for more confounders; interventional effects on performance are not yet shown

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- early identification of students at risk of poor academic performance
- preventing the stress-performance vicious circle
