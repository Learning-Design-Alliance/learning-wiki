# Pairing with Positive Role Models

## Description
Pairing learners with peers who demonstrate strong relationship skills involves intentionally matching learners who may face challenges in social interactions with those who exhibit positive behaviors such as effective communication, empathy, and conflict resolution. This strategy allows the learner to observe and learn from their peer's behavior in real-time, fostering their own skills in building and maintaining positive relationships. Positive role models can demonstrate appropriate social cues, model effective problem-solving during conflicts, and encourage inclusive behavior, thereby influencing the learner's social development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Careful selection of role models who exhibit positive social skills and are willing to provide support. Structured activities or projects that encourage interaction and collaboration between the pair. Regular monitoring and feedback to ensure the pairing is beneficial and effective.
#### Constraints
- The effectiveness of the pairing depends on the compatibility of the learners and the willingness of the role model to provide support. There is a risk that the learner may become overly reliant on the role model or that the pairing may not address underlying issues contributing to social challenges.

- Improve social skills, foster positive relationships, provide role models for effective communication and conflict resolution

### Affordances
- Provides opportunities for observation and imitation of positive behaviors, fosters social learning through interaction with peers, and offers a supportive environment for developing social skills. This can lead to increased confidence and improved social competence.

### Personalization
- The pairing can be tailored to the specific needs and interests of the learner, ensuring a compatible match. The role model can provide individualized support and guidance, addressing the learner's unique challenges and fostering their specific strengths.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe changes in the learner's social interactions, communication skills, and conflict resolution abilities. Gather feedback from the learner, the role model, and other educators or caregivers. Monitor the frequency and quality of positive interactions between the learner and their peers.

## Impact
- Research indicates that peer-mediated interventions, including pairing with positive role models, can effectively improve social skills and reduce social isolation among learners with social challenges. Positive role models can provide support, encouragement, and guidance, leading to improved social outcomes and increased participation in social activities.

## Examples
- A learner who struggles with initiating conversations is paired with a peer who is outgoing and friendly. The peer models how to start conversations, asks open-ended questions, and actively listens to the learner's responses. Over time, the learner becomes more confident in initiating conversations and building relationships with others.
