# Reflective Journaling

## Description
Reflective journaling is a technique used to encourage reflective learning by highlighting students’ thoughts and perceptions about course content. It's a learning exercise where students express their understanding, reflections, response, or analysis of an event, experience, or concept in writing, encompassing all aspects of their thoughts and emotions. This form of writing increases attention and concentration during class time and plays a major role in the transformative learning process by allowing students to critically reflect on their beliefs and consciously make and implement plans that bring about improved ways of redefining those beliefs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a journal (physical or digital), writing utensils, and dedicated time for journaling. Instructors need to provide prompts and feedback, and create a supportive learning environment.
#### Constraints
- Reflective journals should not be a simple summary of course material or a learning log. Focus should be on reactions to what has been read and reflections and thoughts about the material.

### Target Learning Goals

### Affordances
- Provides a channel of inner communication that connects beliefs, feelings, and actions. Creates effective learning conditions that result in self-discovery. Helps students develop their knowledge and understanding of course content.

### Personalization
- Offer a range of prompts to address diverse interests and experiences. Allow learners to focus on aspects of the course content that are most relevant to their personal and professional goals.

## Assessment Evidence
- Analyze journal entries to assess learners' understanding of concepts, critical thinking skills, and personal reflections. Track changes in learners' thinking over time. Evaluate the depth and quality of learners' responses to prompts.

## Impact
- Promotes transformative learning by helping individuals make meaning of their own experiences in order to develop as learners and leaders. Through this practice, they develop into reflective practitioners, understanding more about why things are done rather than how.

## Examples
- Possible questions for a reflective journal include: What was the most interesting thing I read for this lesson? What were three main things I learned from this lesson? What did I previously think was true, but now know to be wrong? What did we not cover that I expected we should?
