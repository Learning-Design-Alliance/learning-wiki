# Intensify preclinical training, add early clinical exposure, and use simulation (problem-based learning, high-fidelity and virtual reality simulators) to prepare students for endodontic emergencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends reducing the gap between preclinical preparation and clinical performance by intensifying preclinical training, incorporating early clinical exposure, and using pedagogical approaches such as problem-based learning, high-fidelity physical simulators, and virtual reality simulators. It argues these could "reinforc[e] students' preparation for clinical practice" and improve confidence before students face stressful emergency situations. Communication training and simulation with challenging patient scenarios are also recommended.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curriculum time and resources for additional preclinical training, simulation equipment, and communication training
#### Constraints
- Recommendations rest on the authors' interpretation and cited studies, not on interventions tested in this cross-sectional study

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students in clinical training (fourth to sixth year)

### Target Learning Goals
- confidence in managing endodontic emergencies
- communication skills with difficult patients
- technical procedures such as anaesthesia and drainage
