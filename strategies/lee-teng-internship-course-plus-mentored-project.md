# Lee Teng undergraduate internships combine a USPAS fundamentals course with an eight-week mentored research project

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The Lee Teng Undergraduate Internships, instituted in FY 2008 by Argonne, Fermilab and the USPAS, select ten to twelve students per year who have just completed their junior year. All interns receive a scholarship to take the USPAS course “Fundamentals of Accelerator Physics,” then complete an eight-week research project at ANL or FNAL under a mentor's supervision. The paper states mentors remain available to guide the student through graduate school application or senior thesis preparation, and that participating in a summer research internship substantially raises the chances of receiving a government-sponsored fellowship.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Selection of students who have just completed their junior year (or sophomore year for exceptionally talented students)
- Mentor matching at each laboratory by the selection committee
#### Constraints
- The paper reports ten to twelve students selected per year (ten per year per a footnote), a small-cohort program

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics and engineering students entering their senior year

### Target Learning Goals
- fundamentals of accelerator physics
- hands-on research experience
- preparation for graduate study
