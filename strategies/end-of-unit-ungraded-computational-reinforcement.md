# Position computational activities as ungraded, end-of-unit reinforcement of already-learned concepts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The teacher scheduled coding activities as "culminating activities to studying a concept," placed after students had spent several days learning the concept, and used them to reinforce and apply ideas rather than introduce new ones. He chose not to grade them, reasoning that "It's okay to not have a grade assigned to every activity in your class," so the activity "can be its own reward." He also emphasized that many different solutions exist to the coding projects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should already have spent several days learning the concept before the computational activity is introduced
#### Constraints
- The teacher had not used computation to develop new concepts, noting "I think you probably could. I just haven't done that."

### Target Learners
- high school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- reinforce conceptual understanding of physics
- exercise creativity in a computational environment
