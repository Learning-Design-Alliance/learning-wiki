# Match the implementation pathway to institutional ambition: proctor-guided broad training for hub centers versus small-team complexity escalation for technical stewardship

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends two implementable pathways: "Centers aiming to function as a regional/national training hub may benefit from more restrictive case selection with structured proctor guidance, while centers pursuing technical stewardship may prioritize limiting team size in order to escalate complexity faster." The choice must be ethically weighed against local caseload, regional experience, and the responsibilities of surgeons to be trained. Both options are feasible within local center constraints.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured proctor guidance and restrictive case selection for the training-hub pathway; limiting team size for the complexity-escalation pathway
#### Constraints
- The decision must be ethically weighed according to the local caseload, regional experience and availability and responsibilities of the surgeons to be trained

### Target Learners
- Console surgeons in training at high-volume HPB centers
- Tableside surgeons and residents gaining robotic exposure

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe expansion of the number of surgeons operating robotically
- Escalation of procedural complexity
