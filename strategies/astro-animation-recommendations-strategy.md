# Design recommendations for art-science collaboration courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors present recommendations for institutions initiating art-science programs, noting their relative importance depends on whether art and science are equal components or science serves as a point of entry for art research. Recommendations include that "Learning goals and measurement must be defined clearly", with science learning measured through weekly quizzes and a final test and animation learning measured through rubrics covering creative voice, pertinence of response, production quality and collaboration efficiency. Further recommendations cover early communication setup, sharing the how-it's-made process, hands-on science demonstrations, and use of NASA open educational resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Communication between students and scientists established from the first day of class
- Clearly defined learning goals and measurement instruments for both science and animation components
#### Constraints
- Recommendations' relative importance differs depending on whether a program has art and science as equal components or science is a point of entry for art research

### Target Learners
- undergraduate art students
- educational institutions initiating art-science programs

### Target Learning Goals
- science learning through artistic practice
- interdisciplinary art-science collaboration skills
