# Replace station rotations with shorter weekly small-group observatory sessions and invest in teaching-assistant training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 2 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the logistical obstacles encountered in the first implementation, the authors recommend abandoning three-station rotations in favor of scheduling smaller groups in the observatory for shorter periods: "Instead of using rotations through three stations, we plan to schedule smaller groups to be in the observatory for shorter periods of time." They also state that more effort should be placed in training teaching assistants who run the night laboratories, that labs requiring students to quickly learn small-telescope operation will be modified or eliminated, and that weekly rather than biweekly attendance reduces variation in clear weather across sections.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teaching assistants working with students in smaller groups
- Weekly scheduling to reduce variation in clear weather per section
#### Constraints
- Non-majors could not realistically learn to operate the small telescopes quickly
- Night-time staffing demands and student time burden were significant obstacles in the rotation model

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate non-science majors in introductory astronomy

### Target Learning Goals
- Retaining the hands-on activities shown to significantly improve student learning while reducing waiting time and student stress
