Combine quick response research approaches with meta-aggregative review methodology to capture educator experiences of rapid digital shifts and extend the TPD-for-OBL framework¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 review, 1 design, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends a research strategy for crisis contexts: apply Quarantelli's (2002) quick response research approach, as Mackey et al. (2012) did to capture educator experiences in a post-earthquake shift to blended learning in New Zealand, combined with the Philipsen et al. meta-aggregative methodology. This would "obtain the important perspectives of teachers and professional development personnel regarding their rapid shift to digital" and could extend the TPD for OBL framework for the after-COVID era.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to educators and professional development personnel experiencing a rapid shift to digital teaching, to capture their perspectives

Constraints¶

  • The approach is proposed for crisis or disaster contexts of mandated rapid shifts, per the New Zealand post-earthquake precedent

Target Learners¶

  • in-service teachers
  • professional development personnel

Target Learning Goals¶

  • preparing teachers to design and deliver online and blended learning instruction

Claims¶

  • The COVID-19 mandated shift to digital teaching created learning and performance contexts so different that the Philipsen et al. TPD-for-OBL framework's applicability may be limited [+W]
  • The Philipsen et al. framework of TPD components for online and blended learning is biased toward higher education settings [+W]
  • Prior TPD research focused on teacher impact, leaving student-centered evaluation of TPD for OBL unexamined [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Future research should focus on methods for disrupting educational inequity in online and blended learning
  • Apply the review's findings to prepare K-12 teachers for online and blended learning
  • Use the pandemic disruption as an opportunity to record data on shifting to online student-centered learning
