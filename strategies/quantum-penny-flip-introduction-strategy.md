# Use the Quantum Penny Flip game as the introduction to gate-based quantum computing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes teaching gate-based quantum computing through the Quantum Penny Flip game rather than through established algorithms: the game "can be used for an introduction to gate-based quantum computing" and to "easily and convincingly demonstrate a form of quantum supremacy". Learners program the game from a small set of gates (identity, Pauli X, Hadamard) on a single qubit in a browser-based environment, encountering superposition, measurement and quantum advantage along the way.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a cloud-based quantum computing platform or its graphical programming environment; only the simplest basics of Dirac's bra-ket formalism are needed.
#### Constraints
- Earlier computer-game implementations of the game require software to be installed, which the article calls a hurdle for use in teaching.

### Target Learners
- students in introductory courses on quantum physics
- high school students interested in the topic

### Target Learning Goals
- introduction to gate-based quantum computing
- programming quantum gates on a real quantum computer
