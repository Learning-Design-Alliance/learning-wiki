# Integrate AI-driven scaffolding with teacher-led offloading and refine feedback granularity to prevent over-scaffolding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on the observed differentiated trajectories and the time-lag effect in lower-performing cohorts, the authors recommend that future models integrate AI-driven scaffolding with teacher-led offloading to mitigate the Matthew Effect, and that feedback granularity be refined to prevent over-scaffolding. This positions human-machine collaboration as the practical pathway: the AI supplies personalized, timely feedback while teachers retain final review and strategic guidance. The recommendation extends the study's finding that adaptive feedback produced divergent developmental trajectories across proficiency cohorts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- AI feedback must adhere to predefined rubrics and be paired with final teacher review of student revisions.
#### Constraints
- High-density feedback may induce cognitive overload, as suggested by the observed time-lag effect in lower-performing cohorts.

### Target Learners
- Elementary students in a cognitive transition phase (grade 6)

### Target Learning Goals
- Critical thinking development through personalized writing feedback
