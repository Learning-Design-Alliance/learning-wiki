# Make female genital schistosomiasis part of the midwifery curriculum and run regional health campaigns to raise awareness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that Ghanaian midwifery training deliberately cover FGS, because pre-service midwives will staff primary care points where FGS could be suspected and referred. The conclusions state "it is recommended that FGS be made part of the curriculum for the midwifery programme so that there could be increased awareness", and the discussion urges training institutions to make deliberate efforts to highlight FGS. It further recommends that the Volta Regional Health Directorate organize health campaigns through school activities and mass media to improve knowledge of urogenital schistosomiasis in endemic areas.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Health care training institutions must make deliberate efforts to highlight FGS in midwifery training
- Curriculum review by the training institutions and accreditation bodies
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted only in the Volta Region, and among pre-service rather than in-service health workers, which the authors state hinders generalizability to the in-service group

### Target Learners
- final-year pre-service midwifery students in schistosoma-endemic regions

### Target Learning Goals
- awareness and knowledge of FGS transmission, symptoms, complications, treatment and prevention for primary reproductive health care

## Claims

- [Only 23.3% of final-year midwifery students in the Volta Region of Ghana had heard of female genital schistosomiasis, compared with 64% for urinary schistosomiasis](../claims/fgs-awareness-23-percent-volta-midwifery-students.md) [+W]
- [Among FGS-aware students, 91% wrongly selected antibiotics as FGS treatment while only 52.3% chose praziquantel](../claims/fgs-mistaken-antibiotic-treatment.md) [+W]
- [Over half (54.6%) of FGS-aware students incorrectly believed FGS can be transmitted through unprotected sexual intercourse](../claims/fgs-sexual-transmission-misconception.md) [+W]
- [FGS-aware students showed relatively high recognition of genital symptoms: 95.4% genital itching/burning, 90.9% bloody vaginal discharge, 81.8% pelvic pain](../claims/fgs-symptom-recognition-high-among-aware.md) [+W]
- [School/workshops were the commonest reported source of information on both FGS (59.1%) and urinary schistosomiasis (54.9%)](../claims/school-workshops-commonest-information-source.md) [+W]
