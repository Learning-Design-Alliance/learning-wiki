# Include dedicated CWM skill development alongside general medical communication training in physiotherapy curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that OSCE-based MC training improved general communication but not movement-based communication, the authors recommend that physiotherapy training programs add CWM skill development. They state that "the inclusion of CWM skill development should be considered in PT training programs" and that future studies should explore effective ways to teach CWM skills, since no standardized program currently exists for teaching communication with movement. The recommendation rests on the observed gaps in physical contact, moving around patients, and explaining test results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must include practice with physical contact and movement around patients, which the OSCE-based MC training in this study did not include
#### Constraints
- The article does not identify an effective CWM teaching method; it only establishes that general MC training alone is insufficient

### Target Learners
- Physiotherapy students prior to clinical practice

### Target Learning Goals
- Physical contact with patients
- Moving around patients
- Explaining test results in understandable terms
