# Engage student groups in unique multiweek projects so that comparing approaches with peers shapes nuanced views of experimentation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors propose a design mechanism in which lab courses assign distinct projects to different groups at the same time. They report that "students notice experiment- speciﬁc approaches to experimentation," and suggest that comparing and contrasting one's own approaches with those of others could combine firsthand and secondhand experiences through which students develop nuanced ideas about experimental physics as context-dependent combinations of performances. This strategy is offered as a plausible mechanism for future study, not a tested manipulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Groups must work on distinct projects concurrently so that experiment-specific approaches differ across groups and can be noticed and compared
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a proposed mechanism for future work; the impact of working on different projects simultaneously on students' views was not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students in lab courses

### Target Learning Goals
- developing nuanced views of experimental physics as comprising context-dependent combinations of performances
