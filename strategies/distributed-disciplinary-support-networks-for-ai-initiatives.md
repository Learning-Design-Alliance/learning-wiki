# Institutional AI initiatives should incorporate discussion, reflection, and distributed discipline-specific support networks rather than centralized information provision

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that institutional structures "may make most impact if, rather than simply providing information, resources or estab- lishing guidelines, they instead incorporate opportunities for discussion and reflection on the issues at stake, as well as low-stakes opportunities for situated disciplinary use of AI." It notes faculty in the sample relied most on department colleagues as information sources, so "models that lean into distributed supportnetworksleveragingdiscipline-specificknowledge rather than centralized efforts may prove more successful."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Initiatives must incorporate opportunities for discussion, reflection, and low-stakes situated disciplinary use of AI
#### Constraints
- The article states institutional initiatives showed weak association with AI use at this stage of adoption, and relationships may change as adoption becomes more widespread and formally supported

### Target Learners
- STEM faculty in departments of physics, chemistry, and astronomy in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Faculty adoption and situated disciplinary use of AI tools
