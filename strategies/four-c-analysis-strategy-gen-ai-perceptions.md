# Use the Four C Model to analyze student perceptions of gen-AI and creativity in physics courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates an analysis strategy: collect student narratives about gen-AI and creativity, then code them against the Four C Model, expanding the definitions where gen-AI introduces new elements. The authors state their aim is to “catalyze further studies and inquiries on the effects and dynamics of gen-AI and creativity in computational physics,” building on Marrone et al.'s call for exploring the model in other education contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to student narrative data and an iterative coding process using the Four C definitions as a codebook
#### Constraints
- With only six participants the authors state results may not be generalizable to larger populations

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics majors

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding how students perceive and use gen-AI in creative learning processes

### Affordances
- Four C Model Expanded Gen Ai Definitions

## Claims

- [A minority of students saw gen-AI as capable of creativity, with one student humanizing AI as having prior experiences](../claims/student-humanizes-gen-ai-creative.md) [+W]
