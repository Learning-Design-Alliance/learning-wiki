# Embed relationship-building supports in school structures, teacher preparation, and policy signals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article argues that because sociocultural contexts shape teachers' beliefs, supports for TSR-building should be embedded across those contexts: school leaders and peers can model and signal that relationship-building is the norm; preparation and professional development programs should devote time to developing teachers' capacity to build relationships; and policies should avoid signals that relationship-building is merely "extra credit." The strategy follows directly from the framework's placement of sociocultural contexts as the foundation of teachers' motivational beliefs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School leaders and professional communities must communicate that building TSRs is an expected norm
- Teacher preparation and PD programs must allocate time to relationship-building capacity, which the article says many currently fail to do
#### Constraints
- Teachers have little control over externally regulated school procedures such as class size, which the article notes affects TSR interactions and perceptions

### Target Learners
- K-12 teachers
- Preservice teachers
- School leaders

### Target Learning Goals
- Building and maintaining positive teacher-student relationships across diverse classroom contexts
