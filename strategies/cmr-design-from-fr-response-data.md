# Build multiple-choice distractors from coded free-response student answers, with partial credit for partially correct reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's development recipe: compile a priori codes from the FR rubric, apply and expand them across multiple semesters of FR responses, and turn the most common justifications into reasoning elements students may select. As the authors put it, "Reasoning elements were taken from common codes identiﬁed in the student responses to the FR version," and students "can receive partial credit for selecting some, but not all, of the necessary elements." This preserves insight into partially correct reasoning while enabling fast, objective grading.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires pre-existing free-response student data from which to derive distractors and reasoning elements
- Requires a nuanced grading rubric reflecting different levels of student understanding to maintain agreement with FR scores
#### Constraints
- The authors state an important limitation may be the format's reliance on pre-existing data from a FR version of the item or test
- Not all questions lend themselves to the format; some would require a prohibitively large number of reasoning elements
- Viability with less experienced students, such as introductory students or non-majors, has not yet been established

### Target Learners
- upper-division physics majors

### Target Learning Goals
- assessing conceptual understanding of upper-division electrostatics at scale
