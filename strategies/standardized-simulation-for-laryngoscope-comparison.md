# Use standardized simulated patients for comparative video-laryngoscope studies to inform technique selection and training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend standardized simulated patients as the setting for comparing video laryngoscopes, stating this approach "has been proven to be a reliable alternative to clinical trials." Such comparisons, they argue, reveal the strengths and weaknesses of devices with different structures during normal intubation and thereby provide evidence for clinical intubation technique selection and training. In this article the strategy is instantiated by the 29-novice manikin study with blinded video-based outcome assessment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A manikin that closely mimics the human airway with realistic oral cavity, pharynx, larynx, and trachea
- Blinded independent observers analyzing recorded procedure videos
#### Constraints
- Conclusions from normal-airway simulation cannot be generalized to beginners dealing with difficult airway intubation
- Manikin studies cannot fully assess participants' mental stress or cognitive deficiencies

### Target Learners
- Novice intubators in anesthesia and emergency airway training

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquiring safe and effective video-laryngoscope intubation technique
