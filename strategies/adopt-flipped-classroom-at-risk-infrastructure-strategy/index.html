Adopt flipped classroom instruction, with particular attention to at-risk students, supported by institutional infrastructure and teacher video-design training¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's recommendations section advises instructors to "consider using the flipped classroom as a strategy especially for at -risk students in need of learning help," and calls on the university to invest in the infrastructure required for flipped classrooms, sponsor related research and conferences, and provide training sessions for in-service teachers in designing videos. It also recommends further research on the type of self-regulated learning students use and on building a theoretical framework for new technology use.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • University infrastructure for flipped classrooms and assistant tools for faculty
  • Training sessions for in-service teachers on utilizing and designing videos

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on a single quasi-experiment in one calculus course at one university with male students only

Target Learners¶

  • university preparatory-year students, especially at-risk students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving student achievement in mathematics courses

Claims¶

  • Flipped classroom instruction produced significantly higher calculus achievement than traditional instruction in a quasi-experiment at University of Tabuk [+M]
  • Prior flipped-classroom studies report mixed achievement results, with some finding gains and others no significant differences [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Adopt flipped classroom in preventive medicine courses according to institutional resources, with teacher information-technology training
  • Design flipped-classroom videos with sound-image synchronization, short durations, full-body instructor visibility, and opportunities for immediate questions
  • Promote the flipped classroom in low-resource settings using free digital tools, peer instruction, and group discussions.
  • Design flipped classroom instruction according to subject type, knowledge type, and students' independent learning ability
  • Staff development should make teaching principles explicit through articulation, reflection and evaluation, supported by institutional incentives and training
  • Keep online lecture videos under twenty minutes and segment by topic with speed control
