# Use activities and case studies with the nature of science as a recurring theme, rather than a single first-day activity or lecturing about it

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends shaping science attitudes through classroom activities and case studies woven through the term. The author writes that "Activities and case studies are more likely to inﬂuence attitudes than simply lecturing about the nature of science , and making the nature of science a recurring theme is likely to have more impact than a single ﬁrst-day activity". Implemented activities include Ob-scertainers black-box hypothesis testing in groups, the Kelvin-Helmholtz versus geological-age solar-system case study, a random-walk balloon demonstration of photon diffusion, and class polling through the Shapley-Curtis debate.
