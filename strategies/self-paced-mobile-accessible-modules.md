# Deliver training as self-paced, mobile-accessible modules at learners' convenience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends a screen-based delivery model to upskill a workforce it describes as "busy and often time poor": simulations viewed on computers, tablets or smartphones offering "repetitive practice and feedback for both individual and group use, that are available when and where they are needed assuming internet and other infrastructure is accessible." The modules allow self-paced learning and easily re-entering modules at users' convenience, with no limit on access frequency, and were designed to take account of variable education and literacy levels and of staff for whom English may not be the primary language.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Internet and other infrastructure accessible; modules must support mobile technologies for greater convenience of access
#### Constraints
- Uptake depends on staff availability and motivation; the study uses weekly reminders, emails and in-person attendance at staff meetings where uptake is low

### Target Learners
- Residential aged care workforce, including personal care attendants and nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- Complete palliative dementia care training despite time constraints and varying literacy levels
