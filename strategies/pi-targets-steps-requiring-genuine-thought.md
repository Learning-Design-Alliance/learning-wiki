# Identify PI question targets by finding steps in a solution that require genuine thought rather than routine procedure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
To develop PI questions for a quantitative problem, the instructor reviews their own solution and flags every step that involved more than plugging in a number, algebraic manipulation, or executing a well-known algorithm. The author writes: "every time you had to really think about what to do next – you have identified a step with which many students will likely struggle. These are the steps for which you should develop PI questions." Each flagged decision point becomes one or more PI questions that scaffold the in-class solution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The instructor must first identify which quantitative problems students should solve in class
- Prior teaching experience or a review of one's own solution path is used to locate difficulty points
#### Constraints
- Examples in the paper are limited to the upper-division astronomy context, though the author states the guidelines apply from introductory to graduate level

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate astronomy students

### Target Learning Goals
- quantitative problem-solving
- constructing mathematical representations of physical situations
