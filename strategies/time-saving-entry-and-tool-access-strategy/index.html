Open post-qualification courses by demonstrating time-saving AI uses and secure free access to AI tools for teachers¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends leveraging teachers' most common motive for AI use in lesson preparation and follow-up — saving time (n = 63) — as an opening argument, and having post-qualification specifically address how AI can save time in everyday teaching practice. Because lack of free access to AI tools was the most common reason for not using AI for preparation and follow-up (n = 21), responsible departments should make tools available to ease teachers' first steps.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Responsible departments must provide free access to AI tools

Constraints¶

  • Self-selection bias may have influenced the reported proportion of teachers using AI

Target Learners¶

  • in-service secondary science educators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • efficient use of AI for lesson preparation and follow-up

Claims¶

  • Nearly all educators most want AI to free up time in existing workflows and return capacity to overburdened staff [+W]
  • Educators benefit from AI chatbots through time-saving administrative assistance and improved pedagogy [+W]
  • Participants reported that AdaPT reduced perceived preparation workload and aligned with their natural lesson adaptation workflows [+W]
