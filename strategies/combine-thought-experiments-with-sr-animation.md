# Combine thought experiments with computer-generated animation, and sequence visualizations from simple to complex, when teaching Special Relativity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The author argues that thought experiments alone are insufficient for students who struggle with the common-sense, 4-D space-time, and simultaneity breakpoints, and proposes "combining thought experiments with real, visual representations of SR by means of computer generated animation". Visualizations should be clear and free of clutter, present one conceptual difficulty at a time, cycle through β to show how speed changes appearances, and build on each other to provide the contrasting schemes Vergnaud stresses. Animations should be paired with familiar visuals such as Minkowski diagrams and include limiting behavior so students see continuity with Newtonian physics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Visualizations must be pedagogically linked to situations and schemes as presented in the didactic sequence
- Animations must be simple and uncluttered so the new common sense is unobstructed
#### Constraints
- The article's animations omit Doppler shifting and aberration for simplicity, and use a fixed snapshot-style camera; effects such as a moving camera and projection onto a u-v plane are left for future work

### Target Learners
- secondary school students
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of Lorentz contraction, optical distortion, and simultaneity in Special Relativity
