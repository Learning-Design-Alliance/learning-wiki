# Integrating intervention training on indirect metacognitive strategies (self-planning, self-monitoring, self-evaluating) into English lessons to manage vocabulary learning.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy involves integrating intervention training on metacognitive strategies into learners' current English lessons to strengthen their ability to use English properly (Nguyen & Gu, 2013). Teachers could encourage students to make their learning schedule and design learning tasks and materials, encouraging use of 'self -planning, self -monitoring, and self-evaluating, to manage or regulate their learning both inside and outside the classroom'. Overall, training should help shift the role from teachers to the learners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers could encourage students to make their learning schedule and design learning tasks and materials.
#### Constraints
- The study has not provided any empirical effects of incorporating indirect strategies into English lessons; future studies on this issue are warranted.

### Target Learners
- low-proficiency EFL vocational-college students

### Target Learning Goals
- vocabulary self-regulation
- depth of vocabulary knowledge

## Claims

- [Direct and indirect vocabulary learning strategies correlate significantly and positively with each other (r=0.75).](../claims/direct-indirect-strategies-correlated.md) [+W]
- [Low-proficiency Chinese EFL students use direct vocabulary learning strategies more frequently than indirect strategies (p<0.001).](../claims/efl-direct-strategies-more-frequent-than-indirect.md) [+W]
- [Indirect vocabulary learning strategies correlate more highly with both depth (r=0.78) and breadth (r=0.66) of vocabulary knowledge than direct strategies (r=0.58, r=0.51).](../claims/indirect-strategies-stronger-correlation-with-vocabulary-knowledge.md) [+W]
- [Strategy use correlates significantly and positively with both breadth (r=0.65) and depth (r=0.77) of vocabulary knowledge, with depth showing the higher correlation.](../claims/strategy-use-correlates-with-depth-more-than-breadth.md) [+W]
