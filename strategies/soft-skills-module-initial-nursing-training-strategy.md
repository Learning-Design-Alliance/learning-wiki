# Include a soft skills module and the flipped classroom in the initial training of nursing and health technology students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating soft skills development into initial training programmes for nursing and health technology students, including a dedicated soft skills module and use of the flipped classroom. It cites a quasi-experimental study in a Moroccan training institute showing the flipped classroom "is capable of developing students' SS such as adaptation, decision-making, lifelong learning, stress management, communication, and teamwork". Because those skills appear among the participants' top 10, the authors judge employing the flipped classroom in initial training could be beneficial.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training programmes must make room for a soft skills module alongside basic technical skills, and the flipped classroom requires sharing documentation with students before the session through digital platforms.
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence the article cites is a single quasi-experimental study at one Moroccan training institute, reported by the authors rather than tested in this survey.

### Target Learners
- Nursing and health technology students in initial training

### Target Learning Goals
- Development of soft skills including adaptation, decision-making, lifelong learning, stress management, communication, and teamwork
