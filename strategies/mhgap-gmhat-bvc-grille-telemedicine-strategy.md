# Use effective assessment tools and telemedicine to support GP identification of mental disorders in developing countries

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that strategies to overcome GPs' diagnostic and evaluation challenges lie in "utilizing effective tools, such as the mhGAP, GMHAT/PC, BVC, Grille’s assessment tool, and telemedicine." The mhGAP provides process paths based on symptoms for non-psychiatrists; GMHAT/PC detected mental disorders with 81% sensitivity and 92% specificity; BVC functions better than single non-structured clinical judgments; telemedicine supports record sharing and evaluation across services.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training so GPs learn how to choose suitable assessment tools and correct misunderstandings about screening scales
#### Constraints
- The article notes no evidence that screening with the GHQ helps GPs perform efficient diagnoses, and screening scales may increase the risk of overdiagnosing patients

### Target Learners
- General practitioners in developing countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Identifying, diagnosing, and systematically evaluating mental disorders in outpatient primary care
