# Workshop leaders can guide faculty to reflect on facilitation moves and use video or case study examples to promote discussion of group interaction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that if a workshop leader centrally pursues the goal of teaching faculty to notice problematic student interactions and allocates time to unpacking the task, experiences like the observed one could be generative. They suggest leaders "guide faculty to reflect on what facilitation moves supported or inhibited collaboration in their group" and how students' experiences might differ. Video or case study examples could provide alternative scenarios, including narrowing in on gender dynamics that are otherwise difficult to discuss.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The workshop leader must be able to allocate time to unpacking the task
#### Constraints
- Faculty will rarely find themselves taking on student roles in school-like environments

### Target Learners
- new physics and astronomy faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- noticing and addressing problematic student interactions such as unequal participation and lack of attentiveness to others' ideas

- [Faculty in the focal group focused more on “doing school” — interpreting rules and pleasing authority — than on doing science](../claims/faculty-doing-school-not-doing-science.md) [+W]
