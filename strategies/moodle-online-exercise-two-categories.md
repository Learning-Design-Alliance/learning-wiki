# Use two categories of automatically graded online exercises — algebraic exercises with random numbers and fixed-option exercises — and grade final results rather than formula knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article sorts its Moodle exercises into two categories: "algebraic exercises with random numbers", where students enter a final numerical result including units, and "exercises with fixed options", such as multiple-choice and drag-and-drop, suited to conceptual questions. Because students may use a cheat sheet and WolframAlpha, the authors argue there is no need to grade formula recall, and Moodle's random short-answer matching allows non-algebraic randomized questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Moodle question types with automatic grading, and exercise design time of about one to two days per week for developing new questions and creating sketches and animations
#### Constraints
- Algebraic multiple-choice questions with random numbers can reveal the correct option's position or let an approximately matching wrong answer be graded as wrong, requiring manual checks of generated number rows

### Target Learners
- first- and second-semester engineering and STEM students completing weekly non-mandatory online exercises

### Target Learning Goals
- self-testing of algebraic and conceptual physics knowledge through repeated, automatically graded practice
