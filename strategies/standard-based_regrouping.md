# Standard-Based Regrouping

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Standard-based regrouping forms temporary, homogeneous groups based on learners' demonstrated mastery of *specific standards* rather than general ability. Learners are assessed formatively — through pre-assessments, warm-up questions, and exit tickets — to identify which standards they have not yet mastered; learners from multiple classes or sections can be pooled into groups targeted at a single standard. Groups are fluid: they dissolve and reform as mastery data change, so placement reflects current skill on a defined objective, not a fixed label.

## Design Implications

Standard-based regrouping operationalizes [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md): grouping decisions are driven by evidence of current mastery, and instruction in each group can be precisely targeted at the gap [Formative assessment practices improve achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Because groups are standard-specific and temporary, it avoids many of the documented harms of permanent, general-ability tracking while retaining the efficiency of teaching a narrower range of readiness in each group [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Valid, quick [formative assessment](../elements/assess-performance.md) instruments aligned to specific standards (pre-assessments, exit tickets)
- Flexible scheduling or timetabling that allows pooling learners across classes
- Instructional materials and tasks aligned to each target standard, at varied difficulty
- A data system or routine for tracking mastery and regrouping on a regular cycle

#### Constraints
- If groups become stable across the year, the approach collapses into conventional ability tracking, which depresses outcomes for lower-group learners [Ability grouping effects depend on group stability and flexibility.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [~M]
- Frequent regrouping without clear communication can stigmatize learners placed in "remedial" groups [-M]
- Requires substantial teacher time for assessment and data analysis; under-resourced implementations produce stale groupings that mis-target instruction [-M]
- Pooling across classes depends on aligned pacing calendars; misaligned curricula make regrouping incoherent [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Within-class regrouping** — temporary groups during a single lesson block, reformed weekly based on exit tickets
- **Cross-class regrouping ("regrouping for reading/math")** — a grade level reorganizes learners across classrooms for one subject period
- **School-wide WIN (What-I-Need) time** — a daily intervention block where all learners regroup by current standard need
- **Learner self-selection** — learners choose which standard group to attend after seeing their own data, supporting [autonomy](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Struggling learners, who receive targeted instruction at their actual entry point instead of instruction pitched beyond them [Formative assessment practices improve achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]
- Advanced learners, who are regrouped toward extension rather than being held back by whole-class pacing [+M]
- All learners benefit when groups are genuinely fluid; fixed placement benefits only already-advantaged learners [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Closing specific skill gaps against defined standards (procedural and conceptual)
- Mastery of prerequisite knowledge needed for subsequent units
- Efficient whole-grade coverage of standards with differentiated [practice](../elements/practice.md)

### Instructions
1. **Assess** — administer a short pre-assessment or [exit ticket](../elements/check-in.md) aligned to the target standard; score against mastery criteria ([Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md))
2. **Group** — form temporary groups of learners sharing the same standard deficit; release masters into extension or peer-tutoring roles
3. **Target instruction** — deliver focused [direct instruction](../elements/direct-instruction.md) on the specific gap, followed by guided [practice](../elements/practice.md)
4. **Feedback and re-assess** — provide immediate, task-specific [feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) and re-assess to confirm mastery ([Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md))
5. **Regroup** — dissolve and reform groups based on new data; move mastered learners out and new learners in
6. **Apply** — return learners to mixed groups for [application](../elements/application.md) tasks that integrate the standard with others

## Examples
- **School-wide WIN time** — many U.S. MTSS implementations (e.g., described by the [RTI Action Network](http://www.rtinetwork.org)) run a daily 25–30 minute block in which all learners regroup across classrooms by current standard need, then return to homerooms.
- **Reading regrouping in Success for All** — the [Success for All](https://www.successforall.org) program regroups learners across grade levels into reading groups by assessed reading level, re-assessing every eight weeks; this cross-class regrouping is a core design feature.
- **Zearn / Eureka Math small groups** — teachers use digital lesson-level exit-ticket data to form next-day small groups targeted at specific standards, with the platform's reports driving the grouping ([zearn.org](https://www.zearn.org)).
