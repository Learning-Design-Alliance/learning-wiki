# Add the HiNTS bedside oculomotor examination for vertigo patients and implement it in clinical routine through simulation training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 4 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
To support clinical decision-making for dizziness and vertigo patients, the authors plan to adopt the three-step bedside oculomotor examination HiNTS (Head impulse, Nystagmus, Test of Skew), which they describe as "a useful tool discriminating central and peripheral causes of vertigo." They state: "We plan to add HiNTS for this patient group with implementation in the clinical routine through simulation training," using the same simulation infrastructure to embed the new routine.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Systematic HiNTS testing has not been part of the center's clinical routine and must be introduced through training
#### Constraints
- Targets patients presenting with dizziness and vertigo specifically

### Target Learners
- stroke physicians
- stroke treatment team members

### Target Learning Goals
- discriminate central and peripheral causes of vertigo
- support clinical decision-making in acute vertigo
