# Deliver a three-day simulation workshop on normal vaginal birth before formal clinical education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a three-day workshop in which students practice cephalic birth on a pelvic and vaginal birth moulage before entering hospital maternity placements. Day one combines lecture, slides, and videos on vaginal and pelvic examination and cephalic birth, followed by instructor demonstration on the moulage; students then "practice normal vaginal delivery on the moulage" individually, with formative assessment of at least three simulated births under supervision and constructive feedback. The strategy precedes, rather than replaces, formal clinical education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trained instructor with midwifery expertise and access to a pelvic and vaginal birth moulage for individual student practice
#### Constraints
- The article states the practice environment "could not provide a large group for individual training"
- Simulation is described as "an expensive training method, especially when used to teach skills"

### Target Learners
- undergraduate midwifery students before formal clinical education

### Target Learning Goals
- performing an uncomplicated cephalic (normal vaginal) birth, including vaginal examination, head control, perineum protection, umbilical cord checking, and skin-to-skin contact
