# Stop and Jot

## Description
Stop and Jot is a processing activity that provides structured opportunities for learners to pause and respond in writing to key questions during a lesson. This technique involves designating a “stop box” on learners' notes where they jot down their thoughts, summaries, or answers to instructor-posed questions. It promotes retention, comprehension, and active engagement by giving learners time to process and make sense of the material before moving forward. The strategy serves as both a check for understanding and a study tool, highlighting essential information for future review.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires minimal materials: paper, pens/pencils, and prepared questions or prompts. Effective implementation requires instructors to provide sufficient processing time and facilitate sharing of responses.
#### Constraints
- May be less effective for learners who struggle with writing or prefer alternative processing methods. Some learners may require additional support to formulate clear and concise responses.

### Affordances
- Supports active recall and critical thinking by prompting learners to reflect on key concepts. Enhances note-taking skills by providing a structured format. Allows instructors to quickly assess understanding and address misunderstandings. Encourages personal relevance by allowing learners to make connections to previously learned material.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to various learning styles through variations like Jot-Pair-Share, Quick Jot, and Stop and Fill. Templates and prompts can be customized to fit the topic of study. Digital versions can be implemented using collaborative documents or online note-taking tools.

## Assessment Evidence
- Review the content and depth of student jots to assess comprehension and critical thinking. Observe the level of engagement during sharing and discussion. Analyze the connections made between new and previously learned material.

## Impact
- Increased retention: Research shows that activities like Stop and Jot, which encourage learners to pause and reflect, aid in memory and retention of new information. Enhanced comprehension: By providing structured processing time, learners are better able to make sense of the material and identify key ideas.
