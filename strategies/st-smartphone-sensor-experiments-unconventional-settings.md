# Use built-in mobile-device sensors for science experiments across unconventional settings such as amusement parks, gyms, parks and means of transport

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article enumerates a repertoire of sensor-based activities spanning astronomy, optics, sound, mechanics, magnetism and atmospheric science, explicitly extending experimentation beyond the traditional laboratory — for example the acceleration and rotation sensors 'para hacer diversas experiencias en un parque de atracciones'. Further listed uses include the camera with a diffraction grating for spectra and fluorescence, microphone and speaker for sound studies, pressure sensor and GPS for atmospheric properties, elevator and drone vertical velocities, and the microphone input turning the device into a small oscilloscope. Most cited references are recent, within about four or five years.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- a mobile device carrying the relevant built-in sensors and a program or application that allows registering the measured values
#### Constraints
- manufacturers do not include sensors such as the accelerometer or GPS for educational but for commercial purposes, so application programming and experiment design are described as a delicate matter

### Target Learners
- students in physics and experimental science courses
- workshop and talk participants learning what their devices can measure

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental measurement and data analysis across mechanics, optics, sound, magnetism and atmospheric physics topics
