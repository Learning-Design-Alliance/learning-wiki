Expand loan forgiveness, high-retention preparation pathways, and compensation supports to reduce teacher loan burdens and strengthen recruitment and retention¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report recommends five federal, state, and local policy approaches: expanding loan forgiveness and service scholarship programs; expanding affordable high-retention preparation pathways such as residency programs; raising teachers' salaries; bolstering net compensation through tax credits and housing subsidies; and underwriting costs of high-need, advanced credentials. It argues that because "over 90% of outstanding student loans being administered or held by the U.S. Department of Education, the federal government is in a particularly strong position" to act. These recommendations follow directly from the report's findings on debt prevalence, subgroup disparities, and stress.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Federal, state, and local governments and education agencies with authority over loan programs, preparation funding, and compensation policy

Constraints¶

  • The report notes existing forgiveness program design and administration present hurdles, such as PSLF payment-counter resets on consolidation and concurrent TLF/PSLF service credit being prohibited

Target Learners¶

  • Prospective teacher candidates
  • Current full-time public school teachers, especially beginning, special education, and Black teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Recruiting and retaining a well-prepared, stable, and diverse teacher workforce to advance student learning and development

Related Strategies¶

  • Build high-retention, supportive pathways into teaching for candidates of color
  • Offer targeted service scholarships, loan forgiveness, and 1-year postbaccalaureate residency programs to rapidly expand the supply of well-prepared teachers in shortage fields and locations
  • Establish high-retention preparation pathways—residencies, Grow Your Own models, service scholarships, and mentoring/induction—to stem turnover in high-need subjects and schools
  • Deploy a portfolio of research-based strategies — service scholarships, residencies, Grow Your Own programs, mentoring and induction, and compensation reform — to strengthen the teacher pipeline
