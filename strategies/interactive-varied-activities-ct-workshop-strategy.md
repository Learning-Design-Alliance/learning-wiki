# Teach critical thinking through varied interactive activities — role plays, case scenarios, essays, and discussions — within a short foundation workshop

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors attribute the course's success to its "thoughtfully structured competencies, which were conveyed in an engaging and interactive style, featuring abundant illustrations and problem-based queries." Sessions used videos, role plays, text reading and analysis, essay writing, matching quizzes, case scenario discussions, and PowerPoint presentations, and the authors state the diverse activities maintained engagement and prevented monotony. Participants' own feedback echoed this: 44.3% named the activities as the best part and 53.8% said the interactive nature made the program enjoyable.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Committed, trained CT instructors and well-defined competencies; the article states effectiveness relies on curriculum quality and educator competence
#### Constraints
- Participants suggested more clinical scenarios (51.4% of respondents to Q11) and noted that changing groups for each activity was not required

### Target Learners
- First-year postgraduate medical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- Critical thinking knowledge and application in clinical decision-making
