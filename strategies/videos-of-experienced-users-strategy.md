# Supplement computer-based mathematics courses with videos of experienced users working problems, and use over-qualified teachers, because optimized methods embed subconscious knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article argues that methodological evolution driven by heavy use is often invisible to its users, so optimized methods cannot be taught explicitly; students start by imitation and develop full skill through heavy use. It follows that "computer-based courses must be supplemented at least by videos of experienced users working problems in order to be fully successful." The author further concludes that "effective teaching of subconscious knowledge requires teachers who are over-qualiﬁed in the subject matter," and that expertise in teaching by itself has no benefit for this purpose. This strategy targets transmission of subconscious, use-optimized technique.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers must themselves have enough heavy use of the methods to develop automatic use of optimized versions
#### Constraints
- The author states expertise in teaching by itself has no benefit for teaching subconscious knowledge

### Target Learners
- students in computer-based mathematics courses
- students preparing for technical careers

### Target Learning Goals
- developing automatic use of optimized mathematical methods
- acquiring subconscious procedural knowledge of expert technique
