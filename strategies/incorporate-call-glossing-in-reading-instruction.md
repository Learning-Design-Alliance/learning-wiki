# Incorporate CALL-based glossing technology into EFL reading instruction and materials

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers and material designers use computer-assisted glossing in reading lessons: teachers "may rely on CALL to enhance the learning experience for language learners", and material designers "can prepare appropriate CALL programs which may promote learning" and "provide texts with different kinds of glossing". The recommendation extends to integrating the findings into institutional syllabuses where reading is a key objective, and the authors suggest new technologies may raise learner motivation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners need computer access during reading activities; the study's participants needed computer literacy to work with the software in class
#### Constraints
- The study's own limitations include limited duration, only two passages, shared rather than individual computers, and uncontrolled environmental variables such as noise, temperature, light, time of day, and seating arrangements

### Target Learners
- EFL learners at upper-intermediate proficiency
- teachers and materials designers in EFL contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- reading comprehension in a foreign language
