# Use automated large-scale video analysis of classroom behavior to overcome small-sample limits of classroom attention research

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The dissertation recommends automated assessment as a route to large-scale classroom research on attention-related behavior. It notes that classroom studies "often have rather small sample sizes limited to specific grades, age groups, and school subjects, resulting in a lack of generalizability of results", and that automated detection significantly reduces the cost of data collection, enabling analysis of large-scale data. Automated annotation of behaviors such as hand-raising demonstrates this potential.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Computer-vision pipelines capable of processing authentic classroom video recordings
#### Constraints
- Human observers, and similarly automated approaches, have difficulty detecting internal cognitive states such as mind wandering from observable behavioral cues

### Target Learners
- students in classroom instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- assessment of behavioral engagement and attention-related behaviors at scale
