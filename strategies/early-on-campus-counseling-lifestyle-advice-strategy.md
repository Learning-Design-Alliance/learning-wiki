# Use on-campus health and counseling services to identify and improve students' unhealthy lifestyle behaviors early in university study

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that universities direct attention to their health sciences and counseling divisions to determine and improve students' unhealthy lifestyle behaviors, since students with impaired mental health who maintain healthier lifestyles may not show increased risk of poor academic performance. They write it "is might optimal for students to lessen their risk of poor academic performance at an early stage by seeking advice from on‐campus counselors."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An on-campus Health Sciences and Counseling division able to identify students' unhealthy lifestyle behaviors and provide counselor advice
#### Constraints
- The buffering role of healthy lifestyle behaviors was observed in a single Japanese university cohort, and the low-risk-group finding may reflect lower statistical power; the GPA <2.0 cut-off is based on one university's regulation and might not apply elsewhere

### Target Learners
- first-year university undergraduates

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining academic performance (avoiding GPA <2.0) through healthy lifestyle behavior management
