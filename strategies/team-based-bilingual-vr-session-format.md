# Team-based, bilingual VR training session format combining orientation, remote-facilitated simulation, and debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's session recipe consists of four components: an introduction and orientation, a virtual environment orientation module, the simulation scenario with a remote instructor joining via the virtual environment, and a debriefing and feedback session. Sessions used "a group-based training approach with enhanced nursing representation, allowing nurses and allied health professionals to train alongside physicians", with approximately 25% of training delivered in English with Vietnamese translation and 75% in Vietnamese.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A bilingual facilitator (here a certified NRP instructor) to provide translation and technical coordination; an onsite team member for translation and troubleshooting; remote instructor with technical expertise in the VR platform
#### Constraints
- Real-time human translation was necessary because the VR system lacked built-in multilingual capabilities, adding logistical complexity; unstable internet connections occasionally disrupted the immersive experience, resolved by using individual mobile hotspots and physical cable connections

### Target Learners
- interdisciplinary neonatal care teams (physicians, nurses, allied health professionals) in resource-constrained international settings

### Target Learning Goals
- neonatal resuscitation teamwork, role clarity, collaborative decision-making, and communication skills
