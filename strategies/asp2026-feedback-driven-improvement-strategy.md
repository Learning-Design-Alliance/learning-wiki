# Use student group feedback and anonymous surveys at event close to collect actionable improvement items for future editions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
ASP2026 collected participant feedback through two approaches at the end of the event: in-person students were randomly arranged into eleven discussion groups presenting collective feedback, and an anonymous survey used predefined questions. "Going forward, improvements will be implemented to address feedback from the ASP2026 participants, as presented in Section 6, to the extent feasible." The groups' improvement suggestions included expanding hands-on activities, optimizing lecture range and depth, testing logistics earlier, adding practical sessions and group projects, extending the program with field trips and scientific entrepreneurship courses, and improving interactions of online participants.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A dedicated feedback session near the event's end and an anonymous survey with predefined questions
#### Constraints
- Improvements are implemented only "to the extent feasible" per the article

### Target Learners
- African university students
- High school teachers
- Online and in-person event participants

### Target Learning Goals
- Continuous improvement of intensive physics school programs based on participant feedback
