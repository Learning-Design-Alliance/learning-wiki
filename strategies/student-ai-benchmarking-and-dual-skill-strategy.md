# Students should learn to solve problems both with and without AI tools, and carefully benchmark AI solutions against their own understanding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A student-facing recommendation set: learn to solve problems both without AI tools (a foundation for higher-level thinking) and with them, use benchmarked AI tools as tutors, and verify AI outputs because they can be confidently incorrect. The article advises that "it can be a good exercise to carefully compare ChatGPT solutions with your own and figure out which solution is correct and why."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must benchmark any AI tool against their own understanding and previously solved problems
#### Constraints
- ChatGPT solutions can look believable but contain errors, and the models sometimes defend incorrect answers

### Target Learners
- undergraduate engineering students
- students with access to generative AI tools

### Target Learning Goals
- independent problem-solving skill
- critical evaluation of AI-generated solutions
