Implement formal peer tutor training programs under faculty supervision within simulation curricula¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends formal tutor training programs embedded in undergraduate simulation curricula, combining peer tutoring with expert faculty supervision. Nine of the 11 included studies provided tutor training, focused on INACSL best-practice standards and online modules on adult education, simulation frameworks, feedback, and time management. Training is presented as addressing the identified lack of pedagogical preparation among peer tutors and supporting sustainability.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Peer tutors should be selected outstanding students from advanced courses who are trained to fulfill their role, since lack of prior experience was a reported challenge requiring specific training before assuming the tutoring role.

Constraints¶

  • The review notes concerns about sustainability given time required, reliance on volunteer participation, and the additional time investment needed for tutor training.

Target Learners¶

  • Health sciences undergraduate students acting as peer tutors

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Pedagogical and teaching competencies
  • Technical and non-technical clinical skills

Claims¶

  • Reviewed peer-tutoring simulation studies mostly reached only Kirkpatrick Level 2, with no assessment of behavior change or clinical-practice results [~W]
  • Peer tutoring benefits extend to the tutors themselves, including confidence, autonomy, leadership, teamwork, and empowerment [+W]
  • Peer tutoring contributes to psychological safety and reduced anxiety in demanding simulation learning environments [+W]
  • Peer tutoring in clinical simulation is associated with gains in non-technical skills including self-efficacy, self-confidence, self-perception of learning, and reflective abilities [+W]
  • Peer tutoring in clinical simulation is associated with improved technical skills such as laparoscopic tasks, lumbar puncture, and cardiovascular examination [+W]
