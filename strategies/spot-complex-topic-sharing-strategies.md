# Strategies for sharing complex technical topics with young audiences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
SPOT gives presenters a six-point recipe for making difficult topics such as black holes and gravitational waves accessible: tailor to audience age, build background for the big picture, tell stories through relevant discoveries rather than reciting facts, make analogies to common experiences, incorporate audience activity, and use local technologies. The article instructs presenters to "Make analogies and comparisons to common experiences, such as what size the Earth would be if Jupiter were the size of a beach ball." and to incorporate activity, "such as having the students in the audience stand up and act out moons orbiting planets." These actions, the article states, keep the audience more focused than a lecture-style presentation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Presenters trained in the guidance and comfortable adjusting to available technologies on site
#### Constraints
- Some topics remain difficult for adults as well as children, so tailoring to audience age is required

### Target Learners
- K-12 students and general public audiences

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding of complex technical topics such as black holes and gravitational waves
