# Embed brief HIIT-style vigorous activity bursts into regular PE lessons as a scalable school physical activity approach

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (3 for, 4 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a pragmatic, scalable intervention format: PE teachers incorporate two elements of additional VPA into lessons, consisting of "4 min of VPA as part of an active 10-min warm-up, and three 2-min (VPA) infusions per hour of PE", with VPA defined as activity raising the heart rate to 71-85% of maximum heart rate. Teachers were invited to create their own warm-ups and infusions meeting the required intensity and duration. The article reports schools said they would recommend the intervention to promote PA.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- PE teacher training (the trial used a 2-h online or face-to-face session plus website videos and top-up support visits)
#### Constraints
- The article reports time constraints, lesson disruption, lack of space with class size, seasonal variation and declining engagement due to repetitiveness were important determinants of poor adherence
- The article notes the intervention proved incompatible with lesson objectives and timetables in some schools, and flexibility in intervention delivery may be required

### Target Learners
- secondary school pupils in PE lessons (Year 8, aged 12-13)

### Target Learning Goals
- increasing vigorous physical activity during the school day
