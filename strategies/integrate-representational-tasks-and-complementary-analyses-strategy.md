# Integrate multiple representational tasks with discussion and collaborative work into chemistry teacher education courses, and analyze both correct and incorrect classifications when evaluating instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that science teacher educators "might consider using multiple representational tasks combined with discussions and collaborative work to offer preservice chemistry teachers opportunities for learning the particular content", both to build scientific conceptions and to model strategies for future teaching. It also offers a methodological recommendation: rather than using a single lens, "the use of complementary analyses could provide more comprehensive information about students learning" because statistical analysis of correct classifications alone made the effect appear extremely positive.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities and discussions emphasizing subject matter knowledge about fundamental concepts integrated into teacher education courses
#### Constraints
- The more accurate picture emerged only when inappropriate classifications were analyzed thoroughly, since correct-count analyses alone overstated the effect

### Target Learners
- preservice chemistry teachers
- science teacher educators

### Target Learning Goals
- scientific conceptual understanding of fundamental chemistry concepts
- awareness of one's own inadequate or nonscientific conceptions
