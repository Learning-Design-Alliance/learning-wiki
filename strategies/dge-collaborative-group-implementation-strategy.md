# Implement DGE activities within collaborative group learning with instructor facilitation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends running dynamic geometry activities in small groups with an instructor circulating to guide discussion, because the DGE alone does not produce learning. Students "discussed in group to solve the given problems", the instructor visited groups and answered questions, and group progress was displayed publicly so students could check and revise their answers. Students themselves perceived that the group discussion helped them solve the DGE problems, an explanation the article connects to Roschelle and Teasley's notion of constructing shared conceptual space.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor intervention during implementation
- Groups of three to four students sharing one internet-connected laptop
#### Constraints
- Internet connection trouble and unequal device ownership can hinder implementation

### Target Learners
- undergraduate mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- constructing understanding of the formal definition of limit
