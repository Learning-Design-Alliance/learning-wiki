# Rethink physics assessment for the LLM era: protect summative assessment authenticity in unsupervised settings and exploit LLMs for formative support of self-evaluation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that tested LLMs solve advanced Olympiad physics problems at a level exceeding top-performing students, the article recommends rethinking assessment practice in physics education. For summative assessment, unsupervised formats are compromised because LLMs "can solve advanced physics problems at a level exceeding that of top -performing students, making it difficult to ensure the authenticity of student work". For formative assessment, the article sees potential "where LLMs can support students in evaluating their own problem solutions". The strategy is to redesign summative settings toward supervised or LLM-resistant formats while deliberately integrating LLMs as evaluative feedback partners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators need awareness of current LLM problem-solving capabilities to judge which assessment formats remain authentic.
#### Constraints
- The findings are based on six text-based mechanics problems from the German Physics Olympiad scored against official schemes; the article notes efforts to design innovative problems do not guarantee solutions are inaccessible via web search or training data.

### Target Learners
- secondary and tertiary physics students
- physics teachers and assessment designers

### Target Learning Goals
- authentic assessment of physics problem-solving ability
- student self-evaluation of problem solutions
