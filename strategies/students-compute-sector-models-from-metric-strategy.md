# Have students compute and construct sector models themselves from a given metric to study geodesics independently

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
As an extension beyond using provided models, the article recommends letting participants calculate sector models themselves, introduced via the sphere example and then applied to the black hole equatorial plane. The article states that sector models realize their full potential "when they are computed and constructed by the participants themselves", because this enables them to study other curved spaces, e.g. determining geodesics for a given metric, replacing setting up and solving the geodesic equation with model construction and graphic geodesic construction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participants must first acquire the concept of a metric as a function returning the distance between two nearby points
- Three-step procedure: subdivide into elements of area, compute edge lengths from the metric, construct flat sectors
#### Constraints
- The simplified edge-length calculation introduces approximation errors (up to 0.13% for the sphere example, 5.4% at the innermost radial edge of the black hole model)

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in courses introducing basic concepts of general relativity
- pre-service physics teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- acquire skills for studying the geometry of a surface when given the metric
- obtain graphically what a standard university course obtains by calculation
