# Have students feel the ground's upward support while standing and walking to embody Newton's third law

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article invites students to feel the floor pushing up on them while shifting weight between feet, and to feel the ground pushing them forward when walking and backward when slowing, framing locomotion as mutual interaction with Earth rather than one-way action. The article says through such activities 'students can start to feel the reality of mutual interaction over the illusion of one-way action'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Guided prompts directing attention to simultaneous upward support or friction-driven forward push
#### Constraints
- Presented as experiential invitations, not measured interventions

### Target Learners
- high-school physics students
- first-year university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Newton's third law as mutual interaction in everyday movement
