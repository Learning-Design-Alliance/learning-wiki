# Teachers iteratively refine LLM-simulated student ideas with classroom context before instructional use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that teachers treat LLM output as a starting point for co-development rather than finished material: through iterative prompting, "teachers can correct the output and clarify classroom contexts, including prior instruction, learning objectives, discussion norms, and students' existing knowledge." Teachers may refine learning objectives and re-prompt the LLM, generating new facilitation ideas. This positions teachers as collaborators and innovators who participate in creating, evaluating, and refining GenAI-enabled learning activities, and it requires teacher oversight to align simulated ideas with the lesson's knowledge scope.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher oversight and idea co-development to align LLM-generated ideas with the targeted lessons' knowledge scope
- Professional development introducing teachers to responsive teaching vision and guidance on critiquing AI simulations
#### Constraints
- Some LLM-generated prompts were overly leading, redirecting conversations to AI-chosen topics
- LLM ideas may use advanced or unrealistic vocabulary unnatural for students

### Target Learners
- in-service science teachers
- pre-service teacher education novices

### Target Learning Goals
- anticipating student ideas
- planning responsive instructional moves

### Affordances
- Responsive Teaching Framework Nguyen Cao
