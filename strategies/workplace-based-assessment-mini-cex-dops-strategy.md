# Use workplace-based assessment formats such as Mini-CEX and DOPS for intermittent assessment of clinical skills with little advance effort

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 4 studies (3 associational, 1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
As an alternative to OSCE-style examinations, the article points to workplace-based formats: "Alternatives are other formats such as the Mini-CEX and others (CEC, DOPS) that can take place directly at the \"workplace\"". These formats offer possibilities to assess clinical skills intermittently with comparatively little à priori effort, fitting the ward settings where final-year students spend their training. The article notes a trade-off: more effort is required to instruct and train examiners correctly for these formats.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examiners must be instructed and trained correctly for these formats
#### Constraints
- More effort is required to instruct and train examiners correctly for these formats than for OSCE-style examinations

### Target Learners
- final-year undergraduate medical students on clinical wards

### Target Learning Goals
- intermittent formative assessment of practical clinical skills at the workplace
