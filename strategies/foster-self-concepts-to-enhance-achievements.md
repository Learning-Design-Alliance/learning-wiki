# Teachers can foster students' achievements across subjects by fostering self-concepts and supporting awareness of self-concept increases

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's practical implication is that achievement gains can be pursued through students' self-perceptions, because self-concept levels and changes predicted subsequent achievements within and, in some cases, between subjects. The Educational Impact statement concludes that "teachers can help students enhance their achievements in different subjects via impacting on their self-perceptions (i.e., fostering students' self-concepts and supporting students to become aware of increases in their self-concepts)." This rests on the temporal comparison finding that self-concept increases were followed by achievement increases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal awareness of students' subject-specific self-concepts so that increases can be supported and made salient to students
#### Constraints
- The within-subject self-concept change effects were small (β = .17 to .20 in Study 1) and did not hold for all subjects or all studies, so impacts on achievement should be expected to be modest

### Target Learners
- School students in math and verbal subjects, Grades 4 to 11

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving math and verbal achievement via academic self-concept enhancement

### Affordances
- Reciprocal 2I E Model
