# Incorporate combined pre-task and on-line planning (POLP) as a fluency-enhancing option

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, where large-scale strategic investment is impractical, "individual teachers can incorporate mixed planning condition of POLP as an alternative fluency-enhancing option". Teachers should allot sufficient time for pre-task and on-line planning before speaking activities and teach learners to use that time constructively. Curriculum developers are likewise advised to "give priority to both types of task planning" because planning helps students direct attention and exploit resources efficiently.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient time allotted for both pre-task and on-line planning
- Instruction in how to use planning time constructively
- Qualified teachers able to apply task-supported training and evaluate oral performance reliably
#### Constraints
- The POLP-over-OLP advantage was significant only in that pairwise contrast; POLP did not differ significantly from PTP

### Target Learners
- Iranian TEFL university sophomores
- EFL learners in exposure-restricted contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- oral fluency
- effective task planning
