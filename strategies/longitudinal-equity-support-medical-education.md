# Adopt longitudinal equity support spanning pre-application through graduation, with multidimensional disadvantage measures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that equity policies address financial and psychosocial pressures continuously rather than treating access and progression separately. They state that "Equity policies must therefore adopt a longitudinal focus, supporting students from pre-application through graduation rather than treating access and progression as separate challenges." Recommended interventions include financial support to reduce material hardship, wellbeing services, structured mentorship, learning environments that value socioeconomic diversity, and outreach, mentoring, and representative role models for geographically disadvantaged students. They also recommend employing multidimensional measures of disadvantage rather than single proxies such as postcode or parental occupation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutions and policymakers would need to implement integrated and sustained interventions, including financial support, wellbeing services, structured mentorship, and inclusive learning environments.
#### Constraints
- The authors note that widening participation initiatives may be insufficient on their own if they do not also address ongoing financial and psychosocial pressures extending from pre-admission through training.

### Target Learners
- applicants and students from socioeconomically disadvantaged backgrounds in medical education

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable access to and progression through medical education
