# Extend game-based financial literacy instruction to students with substantial debt and low-to-moderate income adults

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extending their successful classroom experience beyond campus to groups who need financial knowledge most. They suggest school administrators target students with substantial student loans, and that off campus a good starting group is people seeking tax assistance from the IRS Volunteer Income Tax Assistance (VITA) program, since financial literacy is especially important for these groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Free, accessible games such as those used in the course
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on one small case study plus similarity to Tufano et al. (2010), a study of 84 low-income adults across six locations in the US

### Target Learners
- college students with substantial student loans
- low-to-moderate income adults seeking VITA tax assistance

### Target Learning Goals
- financial literacy
- informed financial decision-making
