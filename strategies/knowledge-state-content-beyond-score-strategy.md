# Use knowledge-state content beyond the placement score to adjust curricula and advise students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that placement programs and instructors examine which specific items a student's knowledge state contains, not just its percentage score. If an item is basic for a course but incoming students lack it, "the precalculus curriculum can be adjusted to place additional emphasis on the item," and advisors can use item-level states to enable "detailed conversations with students regarding the likelihood of their success in various courses." This treats the knowledge state as richer diagnostic information than a 20-30 question exam score.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A knowledge-space-based adaptive assessment producing per-item knowledge states for each student
- Outcome data (final grades) linked to initial assessment states
#### Constraints
- The authors caution against generalizing until corroborating results are found from other institutions; the significant items reported are specific to the student body and courses at the University of Illinois

### Target Learners
- incoming university students in placement-governed and related mathematics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- improving course placement effectiveness and prerequisite preparedness in the college algebra through calculus sequence
