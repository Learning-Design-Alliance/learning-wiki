# Triangulate curriculum impact with multiple instruments plus longitudinal tracking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article evaluates a curriculum intervention by combining a concept inventory probing conceptual mastery against misconception distractors, a card-sorting task probing expert-like organization of knowledge, a content-focused MCAT-style posttest against historical norms, and longitudinal tracking into upper-level courses. The authors state that "The data triangulation of three instruments, followed by early longitudinal tracking data, supports that the ICB textbook's focus on inquiry... increased learning." Because each instrument has different strengths and noise, triangulation guards against overreading any single measure.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to validated instruments appropriate to the course span (here a broad concept inventory and a card-sorting task)
- A historical or concurrent comparison baseline, here 15 years of MAT scores and ACT normalization
#### Constraints
- The authors note it may be more difficult to measure the benefits of courses focused on scientific practices with present methods, and effects may take time to appear

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate STEM students in yearlong course sequences

### Target Learning Goals
- Detecting gains in conceptual expertise, content knowledge, and long-term performance
