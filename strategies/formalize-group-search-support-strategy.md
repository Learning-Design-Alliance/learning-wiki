# Departments should formalize support for the research group search via individual development plans, structured networking, and rotation-style research exposure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend departments better facilitate the group search to improve retention, particularly for traditionally underrepresented students, and overall satisfaction. Proposed steps include a physics-specific individual development plan (IDP) tool that should "explicitly emphasize exploring research options and joining a research group," more structured opportunities to meet faculty and older graduate students, and more formalized ways for students to experience working in different research groups, such as a rotation system, since research seminars are "beneficial but clearly insufficient" for informing students about available research.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Departmental commitment to structuring the first-year group search process, including explicit guidance on steps to join a group and when those activities should take place
#### Constraints
- The authors note students may be unable to try out different research groups during the first year without making that year even more stressful, as one interviewee described

### Target Learners
- first- and second-year physics PhD students
- traditionally underrepresented graduate students in physics

### Target Learning Goals
- navigating the research group and advisor search
- making informed decisions about research topics and advisors
