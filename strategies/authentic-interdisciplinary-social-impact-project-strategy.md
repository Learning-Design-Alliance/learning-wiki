# Use authentic interdisciplinary learning projects with real social impact to develop students’ empathetic understanding before professional practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building empathetic understanding through authentic, socially impactful collaborative projects during university study rather than waiting for professional practice. "Through providing students with an authentic learning experience which has obvious social impact and currency, students were enabled to reflect on their existing perceptions and to gain new insights into the concerns, preferences, and perspectives of individuals who access and provide refuge services." The strategy pairs knowledge-sharing from clinically placed students with design briefs grounded in stakeholder contact, and the authors state the project provides a model for future interdisciplinary and social impact subjects.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaborative meetings with stakeholders from the service sector, fieldwork opportunities, and mixed-discipline teams working on real scenarios
#### Constraints
- Results were limited by a small sample from one university; whether empathy changes are sustained over time or influence professional practice has not been determined

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and master’s students in health and design disciplines

### Target Learning Goals
- empathetic understanding of service users
- user-focused design responses to social problems
