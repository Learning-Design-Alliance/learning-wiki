# Future motivation–achievement research should explore behavioural mediation pathways, broaden motivation operationalisation, and use appropriate longitudinal modelling

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their null mediation finding and unilateral result, the authors recommend three directions for future work: "further exploration of behavioural mediation pathways, a broad operationalization of motivation, and the application of appropriate modelling strategies to investigate the motivation‐achievement reciprocity." This follows from their observation that most reciprocity studies operationalise motivation as self-reported beliefs (e.g., academic self-concept) and neglect the behavioural route from motivation to achievement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies should measure effort objectively rather than only by self-report, since subjective and objective effort measures have shown different relations to motivation and achievement
#### Constraints
- The recommendation arises from a single one-hour experiment with a short-timeframe intrinsic-motivation measure; the authors present it as underscoring the need for further exploration rather than as a settled conclusion

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students
- school and university learners generally

### Target Learning Goals
- academic motivation
- academic achievement
- effort investment in learning
