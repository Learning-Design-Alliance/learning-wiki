# Countries with low chlamydia and STI testing in general practice need nationally agreed guidance, nationally agreed STI training within the GP residency curriculum, and ongoing education support and finances in general practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's forward-looking recommendation for raising STI testing provision in general practice. It states that countries with low testing "will probably need nationally agreed guidance, nationally agreed STI training within the GP residency curriculum, and much on-going education support and finances within the general practice setting". The recommendation rests on the four-country comparison showing testing countries had these elements in place.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Nationally agreed guidance and nationally agreed STI training within the GP residency curriculum, plus ongoing education support and finances within the general practice setting
#### Constraints
- The article notes it only assessed a small number of countries and is not able to generalise the results widely

### Target Learners
- General practitioners and primary health care staff

### Target Learning Goals
- STI and chlamydia testing, management and care provision in general practice
