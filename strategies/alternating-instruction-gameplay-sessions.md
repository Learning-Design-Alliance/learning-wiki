# Alternate short instructions with guided gameplay sessions replacing lectures in a university physics course

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the intervention, traditional lectures were replaced by sessions in which students received "short instructions to the game and the content" and were then "challenged to perform concrete tasks in the game", with instructions and playing alternating throughout the two-hour session under guidance from the lecturer and teaching assistants. Content questions were chosen to represent content required to master the games. This structure is the pilot whose outcomes the authors report.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Games must map to course content, with content questions chosen carefully to represent the content required to master the game
- Guidance by lecturer and teaching assistants during gameplay
#### Constraints
- Evaluated as a small-scale pilot in one second-year quantum mechanics course; the authors state more data is needed to support or dismiss their hypotheses

### Target Learners
- second-year university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- quantum mechanics concepts such as eigenstates, cooling, and tunneling

## Claims

- [In one uncontrolled pretest-posttest study, a second-year university course had an average normalized gain of 0.20 from pretest to posttest after gamified quantum mechanics gameplay sessions](../claims/gamified-quantum-course-average-gain-0-20.md) [+W]
- [Students who reported playing the game was fun to a large extent had a significantly lower gain (0.13) than the average student (0.20)](../claims/high-fun-students-lower-gain.md) [~W]
- [Students with the top grade in Mechanics and Thermodynamics showed a high gain (0.28), and students reporting much better understanding trended above average (0.24, not significant)](../claims/high-prior-graders-high-gain-gamified.md) [~W]
- [Students with the lowest pretest scores showed the largest gains, with binned gains decreasing from 0.33 (0–3 correct) to 0.18 (4–6) to 0.05 (7–9)](../claims/lowest-pretest-scorers-largest-gains.md) [~W]
- [97 per cent of students reported that coupling gameplay to authentic quantum research gave them a higher or much higher motivation for learning](../claims/research-games-boosted-motivation-97-percent.md) [+W]
