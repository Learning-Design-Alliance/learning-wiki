# Integrate high-fidelity body donor models with low-fidelity simulators across the training progression

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends combining high-fidelity body donor models with low-fidelity simulations so that trainees develop both foundational and advanced skills: "The integration of both high-fidelity body donor models and low-fidelity simulations can offer a comprehensive training experience". It grounds this in cognitive load theory, arguing low-fidelity models reduce intrinsic and extraneous load for early-stage learners, while body donor models provide anatomical realism for advanced practice. The review cites evidence that general surgery trainees preferred low-cost models for initial learning of hernia repair while favouring body donor models for overall training experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to both low-fidelity simulators and body donor models within a training programme
- Sequencing that matches model fidelity to trainee stage, with low-fidelity models for novices
#### Constraints
- Body donor models are more expensive than lower-fidelity alternatives, though costs can be mitigated through planned coordinated use across multiple specialties

### Target Learners
- novice surgical trainees
- postgraduate surgical residents

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational laparoscopic skills
- advanced laparoscopic procedures
