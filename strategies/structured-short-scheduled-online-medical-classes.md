# Structure online medical classes under one governing authority, with a pre-announced timetable, short topic-focused sessions, and sufficient doubt-clearing time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's Table 1 and discussion recommend concrete fixes for the problems its survey documented: arranging classes under one governing authority, proper software and basic training, well-structured content catering to the target audience, and "Sufficient time for interaction to clear doubts should be there." The discussion adds that each webinar must "have a strict short time limit, and be structured like a regular classroom lecture rather than a panel discussion," with a predesigned timetable announced well ahead so residents can plan duty slots.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Classes must be structured and designed to meet the curricula of both undergraduate and postgraduate students
- A predesigned timetable announced well ahead of the classes
- Basic software training before starting classes
#### Constraints
- Developed from a survey of Indian medical respondents during COVID-19 lockdown; the authors note online modes are not yet integrated in the Indian teaching curriculum and adoption has to be gradual to avoid technological shock

### Target Learners
- medical undergraduates
- medical postgraduates
- teaching faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- medical knowledge refinement
- doubt resolution and conceptual understanding
