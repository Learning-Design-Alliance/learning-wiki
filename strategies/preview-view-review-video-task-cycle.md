# Teach video-based listening lessons through a three-phase preview–view–review task cycle

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article operationalizes Willis's task cycle as a three-step lesson routine for video listening. Step 1 preview uses "warm-up questions, brainstormed relevant vocabulary, and conducted other preview activities"; Step 2 plays the clip for general comprehension, replays for details, and uses the pause button as needed; Step 3 checks comprehension with yes/no, wh-, and inferential questions. It was applied across all ten experimental sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video clips accompanied by corresponding tasks and activities (e.g., true-false, multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank)
#### Constraints
- Applied in the study to pre-intermediate EFL learners in ten 60-minute sessions

### Target Learners
- pre-intermediate EFL learners

### Target Learning Goals
- listening comprehension of authentic language
