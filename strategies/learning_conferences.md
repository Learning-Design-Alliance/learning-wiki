# Learning Conferences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A learning conference is a short, scheduled one-on-one conversation between teacher and learner focused on the learner's current work, goals, and next steps. The teacher listens more than talks, asks diagnostic questions, offers targeted feedback, and negotiates a concrete next action. Conferences are a staple of writing workshop models (Calkins, Atwell) and reading instruction, but the format generalizes to any domain where individual work can be reviewed in dialogue.

## Design Implications

Conferences concentrate the teacher's diagnostic attention on a single learner, allowing feedback to be pitched at the task and process level rather than the self level — the feedback levels associated with the largest learning gains [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. Because the teacher can probe understanding and adjust support in real time, the conference functions as contingent scaffolding: hints are calibrated to what the learner actually shows they know [Scaffolding that responds to the learner's current understanding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]. Conferences also externalize goal-setting and self-evaluation, building the self-monitoring habits that support self-regulated learning [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation of learning.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A predictable, short time structure (typically 3–10 minutes) with the rest of the class engaged in independent work
- A record-keeping system (conference notes, goal logs) so each conversation builds on the last
- Teacher skill in open-ended probing and [active listening](active-listening.md) — the learner, not the teacher, should do most of the talking
- A concrete artifact of the learner's work to anchor the conversation

#### Constraints
- Time cost is severe: at 5 minutes per learner, a teacher of 150 students needs over 12 hours of class time for one full cycle, so coverage depends on peer structures or rotating schedules [-M]
- Poorly run conferences drift into teacher monologue, which produces compliance rather than self-regulation and can depress autonomy [Autonomy support fosters intrinsic motivation; controlling feedback undermines it.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~M]
- Feedback delivered publicly or overheard can threaten self-efficacy; conferences must be private to be safe for discussing errors [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Without written records, conferences produce no durable trace and goals are forgotten between meetings

#### Implementation Variability
- **Writing conferences** (writing workshop): teacher confers with a few writers per session while the class writes independently
- **5-minute conferences**: tightly scripted check-in-goal-feedback-next-step format for whole-class coverage ([5-Minute Writing Conferences](5-minute_writing_conferences.md))
- **Peer conferences**: structured partner protocols extend coverage once learners are trained in feedback norms
- **Advisory/progress conferences**: longer meetings (10–20 min) reviewing portfolios and goals across subjects, common in competency-based schools

### Target Learners
- Developing writers and readers who benefit from individualized, process-level feedback [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process rather than the person.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- Learners with weak self-regulation, for whom externalized goal-setting and monitoring build internal habits [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation of learning.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Struggling or disengaged learners, for whom individual attention and achievable next steps support persistence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M]
- Less necessary for advanced learners who already self-monitor, unless used for challenge and extension

### Target Learning Goals
- Process and strategy goals: improving *how* learners draft, revise, solve, or study
- Self-regulation: goal-setting, monitoring, and reflection on one's own learning
- Metacognitive awareness: articulating what one did and why
- Motivation and relationship-building: establishing that the teacher knows and values the learner's individual work

### Instructions
1. **Prepare**: review the learner's current work and your notes from the previous conference; identify one focus.
2. **Open with the learner's talk**: ask an open question ("Tell me about what you're working on") and listen — resist correcting immediately ([active listening](active-listening.md)).
3. **Research**: probe with diagnostic questions to locate where the learner's understanding or process breaks down ([coaching](../elements/coaching.md)).
4. **Teach one thing**: give a single, process-level piece of feedback or a brief strategy demonstration — not a list ([feedback](../elements/feedback.md)).
5. **Negotiate a next step**: agree on one concrete, achievable action the learner will take before the next conference.
6. **Record**: log the focus, teaching point, and next step so the next conference continues the thread ([check-in](../elements/check-in.md) records serve this function).
7. **Follow up**: begin the next conference by checking the agreed next step, closing the feedback loop ([assessment for learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md)).

## Examples
- **Teachers College Reading & Writing Project (Calkins' Units of Study)** — the canonical writing workshop structure: 10-minute minilesson, independent writing, and the teacher conferring one-on-one with 3–4 students per session using a research-decide-teach sequence. https://www.tc.columbia.edu/tcrwp/
- **Summit Public Schools / Basecamp** — weekly one-on-one mentor conferences in which students review their personalized learning plans and set goals, pairing conferences with competency-based progress tracking. https://summitps.org
- **Conferencing in EL Education (Expeditionary Learning) crews** — regular advisory conferences over student portfolios, culminating in student-led conferences with families. https://eleducation.org
