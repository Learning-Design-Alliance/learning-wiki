# Implement CBL successfully by ensuring pre-class preparation, authentic progressive cases, dual-competence facilitators, adequate time, and process-plus-outcome assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review enumerates crucial considerations for maximizing CBL's potential: "ensuring student preparation through baseline knowledge provision and orientation to active learning norms via pre-class materials and formative assessments, designing authentic clinically relevant cases that unfold progressively to mirror real-world reasoning and align with curricular objectives, equipping instructors with dual competence in content mastery and facilitation." It further recommends allocating adequate time for preparation and discussion, developing assessments that evaluate both process and outcomes using standardized rubrics, and addressing student resistance through clear goal communication and gradual exposure to interactive formats.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Student preparation via pre-class materials and formative assessments
- Instructors with dual competence in content mastery and facilitation
- Adequate time allocation for pre-class preparation and in-class discussion
- Assessments aligned with CBL goals using standardized rubrics
#### Constraints
- CBL's effectiveness is context-dependent and may be compromised by students lacking foundational knowledge, poorly designed cases, untrained facilitators, insufficient time, misaligned assessment, or student resistance to active learning

### Target Learners
- pharmacy undergraduate students
- pharmacy interns
- post-graduation and continuing education students

### Target Learning Goals
- applied clinical skills
- problem-solving
- collaboration
- critical thinking
