# Combine a 45-minute lecture with paired, hands-on simulation tasks guided by structured written instructions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The study's intervention format pairs a 45-minute lecture tailored to each condition with an active session in self-selected pairs, where students complete condition-specific tasks, submit a joint report and an individual report, and follow "written instructions for active learning, which provided step-by-step guidance". In the high-tech arm, students chose one of 3D printing, virtual reality, or EMG activities, modifying an assistive-device model, simulating a VR therapy session, or analyzing muscle activation patterns, with reflection questions and documentation criteria built into the instructions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Self-selected pairs within their designated group; structured written instructions outlining task objectives, tools, procedure, reflection questions, and documentation criteria.
#### Constraints
- Studied with second-year OT students in a single assistive technologies course; reports accounted for 100% of the course grade, which the authors note may bias self-report responses.

### Target Learners
- second-year occupational therapy students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- technological problem-solving confidence
- entrepreneurial traits
- reduced technophobia
