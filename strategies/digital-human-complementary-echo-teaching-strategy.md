# Use digital-human simulation as a complementary tool alongside traditional teaching for echocardiography education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "while the digital human provides a more engaging and interactive means of teaching echocardiography, it is not intended to replace traditional teaching methods entirely. Rather, it should be used as a complementary tool to enhance the learning experience of medical students." This strategy positions simulation of sectional anatomy as an adjunct that enhances engagement and spatial understanding while retaining the foundational benefits of lecture-based instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a digital human device and appropriately trained instructors with extensive echo-teaching experience
#### Constraints
- High device cost may limit feasibility in resource-limited settings
- Cannot simulate pathological conditions, limiting scope to normal anatomy

### Target Learners
- medical students learning echocardiography

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of cardiac sectional anatomy and probe positioning for echocardiography
