# Before disseminating an e-learning program, condense already-known content, keep statistically grounded explanations, and offer a face-to-face alternative

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors derive dissemination improvements from their item-level and satisfaction results: content participants already knew should be reduced, while "the content to be included in the program needs to be more concise" and explanations "supported by statistical data" depicting the actual situation of abuse cases should be retained. They also suggest offering both formats: "it may be useful to create both e-learning and face-to-face educational programs in the future so that participants can choose based on their preferences."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Item-level test data identifying which content is already known and which needs correction before dissemination.
#### Constraints
- The authors note the too-long rating was similar to a previous e-learning program of comparable duration, which they read as an appropriate level.

### Target Learners
- obstetric midwives and nurses
- professionals in continuing nursing and midwifery education

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of knowledge for handling high-risk abuse cases
- improved completion and fit to work schedules
