# Introduce systematic dementia-specific education or training for all health care staff, including support staff

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that health services aim for a high proportion of staff who have undergone dementia-specific education, potentially making it "a mandatory component of both employment criteria and staff development programs". Because projected growth means staff from all levels will contact patients with dementia, education must target all staff, not only clinical groups. The authors frame this as one piece of an overall plan to improve care delivery.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Education or training would need to target all staff who would potentially have contact with patients with dementia
#### Constraints
- The article notes knowledge alone does not necessarily translate into change in care, and that high quality care is not solely dependent on broad education about dementia

### Target Learners
- nurses
- medical staff
- allied health staff
- support staff (administrative, cleaning, patient transport, security)

### Target Learning Goals
- dementia knowledge
- quality of dementia care
