# Structure digital learning material non-linearly and add worksheets to force active thinking and written scientific explanations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structuring the learning material in another way than the linear trajectory used in their tablet learning path, suggesting that more structure in the learning material, and even an extra worksheet on paper, might have forced students to think more actively and to express scientific explanations. Students in the study followed one linear trajectory throughout the lesson and did not take notes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learning material should include structural elements such as worksheets or note-taking prompts that require active written responses
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is based on the authors' retrospective analysis of their own linear learning path design rather than on a tested alternative

### Target Learners
- secondary school students working with digital learning paths

### Target Learning Goals
- active engagement with science content
- expressing scientific explanations
