# Schedule remedial training at intervals scaled to procedure complexity, frequency, and consequence severity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because reviewed studies show a predominant decrease in skills over time, the review recommends that "Retention studies should aid in establishing an optimal interval for remedial training in order to maintain the originally learned skills", achieved by sequentially testing residents at intervals after initial training to determine when skills decay produces subpar performance. The review states the time point "would be variable, influenced by the procedure's complexity, performance frequency and the severity of the consequences from subpar performance".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sequential testing of residents at intervals after initial training to determine when decay results in subpar performance
#### Constraints
- The optimal interval is variable, influenced by procedure complexity, performance frequency, and severity of consequences; short retention intervals may fail to capture decay while long intervals risk confounding

### Target Learners
- Residents who have completed initial procedural CBE/ML training

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintenance of originally acquired procedural skills over time
