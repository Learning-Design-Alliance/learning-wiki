# Think-Pair-Share (Turn & Talk)

## Description
Think-Pair-Share is a cooperative learning strategy designed to encourage individual participation and articulate communication. It involves three distinct steps: Think (students think independently about a question or topic), Pair (students discuss their thoughts in pairs), and Share (pairs share their ideas with a larger group). This technique promotes critical thinking, active listening, and the development of well-formed responses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a well-defined question or topic, clear instructions for each step, and a designated time frame for individual thinking, pair discussion, and whole-class sharing. Requires instructor to actively monitor and facilitate discussions.
#### Constraints
- If not carefully structured, dominant students may overshadow quieter peers. Time constraints can limit the depth of discussion. Questions must be designed to promote meaningful sharing and avoid simple, single-answer responses.

### Target Learners

- Applicable across all grade levels and class sizes, from elementary to adult learners. Adaptable for various subject areas and learning environments.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote active participation, critical thinking, and articulate communication; enhance understanding through peer interaction; provide a safe space for students to develop and refine their ideas before sharing with the whole class.

### Affordances
- Increases student engagement and participation, provides structured thinking time, allows for peer support and collaboration, and boosts confidence in sharing ideas with the whole class. Encourages students to take ownership of their learning and negotiate meanings.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted by providing different types of questions or prompts, varying the time allotted for each step, and modifying the sharing format. Pairings can be strategically assigned based on learners' needs and communication preferences.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the level of student participation and engagement during each step. Assess the quality and depth of student responses during pair and whole-class discussions. Monitor the types of questions students ask and the connections they make between ideas.

## Impact
- Research shows that Think-Pair-Share enhances learning by providing opportunities for students to elaborate on ideas through talk. It increases personal communication necessary for internal processing and retention. Students take ownership of learning and negotiate meanings, leading to positive changes in self-esteem and higher-level thinking skills.
