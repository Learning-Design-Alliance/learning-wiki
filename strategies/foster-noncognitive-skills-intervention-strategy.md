# Foster non-cognitive skills as an avenue for educational intervention, particularly in later school years

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's concluding recommendation is that supporting motivation, self-regulation and education-specific attitudes may improve academic outcomes. It states that "Fostering non-cognitive skills might provide a successful avenue for educational interventions", grounding this in its finding that non-cognitive genetic and phenotypic associations with achievement strengthen across compulsory education. The strategy is framed as an avenue suggested by the developmental pattern, not as a tested intervention in this study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Non-cognitive skills (motivation, self-regulation, education-specific attitudes) must be measurable and targetable in the educational setting
#### Constraints
- The article tested genetic and phenotypic associations, not an intervention; the recommendation is the authors' interpretive conclusion

### Target Learners
- school-age children and adolescents in compulsory education (ages 7–16)

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement
- academic motivation and engagement
- self-regulation
