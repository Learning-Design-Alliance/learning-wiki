# Integrate online patient simulations into course delivery and learning management platforms to raise uptake and acceptability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends positioning online patient simulations within the curriculum rather than offering them as optional extras. In this trial, cohort two students were recruited "through the faculty online learning management platforms (e.g. Moodle), advertisements on social media, faculty newsletters, and lecture 'shout outs'" and eCREST was integrated into a relevant module, yielding greater uptake (22% vs 13%), higher retention and higher acceptability than cohort one. The authors conclude it is feasible to run a summative effectiveness trial if the tool is appropriately positioned in the curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to faculty online learning management platforms for advertising and delivery
- Coordination with module timing; cohort two were invited prior to final examinations, which the authors suggest made the tool useful for revision
#### Constraints
- Uptake may vary across schools with different curricular approaches; the problem-based learning school had the lowest uptake (11%), possibly because students already had more exposure to patient cases
- Volunteer recruitment risks selection bias

### Target Learners
- Final-year undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning skills training
