# Cap the Kepler's 2nd law module with a computational exercise predicting the timing and location of Mars' next opposition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
After teaching Kepler's 2nd law through the historical development culminating in Kepler's equation, the instructor assigns a computational homework in which students predict the timing and location of Mars' next opposition given the orbital elements and the current positions of Mars and the Sun. The article reports it was tested in both traditional and flipped classroom environments, with the 2020 online class sharing a python jupyter notebook via simultaneous video conferencing while freely debating. The author states the method is thus in line with active learning and constructivist learning theory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be able to manipulate Kepler's equation quantitatively, which requires computational techniques because the equation is transcendental
#### Constraints
- The article notes the transcendental nature of Kepler's equation makes this best suited to graduate curricula where computational techniques are routinely applied

### Target Learners
- junior and senior physics/astronomy undergraduates
- first-year astronomy PhD students

### Target Learning Goals
- apply Kepler's 2nd law to predict a planet's position in its orbit
- connect the law to real observational prediction
