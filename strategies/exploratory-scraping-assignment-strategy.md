# Use an exploratory scraping assignment rather than a worksheet to teach AI data-collection practices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors designed the dataset exercise as open exploration: students choose among scraping tools, handle issues such as CAPTCHA, pagination, and dynamic content loading, and decide how to balance quantity and quality in light of copyright concerns. The article states the assignment "was not intended as a worksheet exercise but rather was created as an exploratory assignment for students to research and determine the best choices for fulfilling the needs of program development". Discussion questions address tool trade-offs, data-collection challenges, and ethical and legal considerations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An instructor-assigned theme per group and deliverables including a working script, dataset, and report
#### Constraints
- The article reports implementation focused on scraping mechanics rather than copyright data in fall 2024

### Target Learners
- junior-level computer science students

### Target Learning Goals
- selecting and adapting web scraping tools
- recognizing limitations and benefits of scraping tools
- considering ethical and legal constraints on data collection
