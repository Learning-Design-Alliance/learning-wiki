Use broad-based tier-two behavioral interventions as a vehicle to begin services before formal ASD diagnosis¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that children whose behavioral problems prompt referral for generic intervention can participate in broad-based tier-two programs such as First Step without waiting for diagnostic confirmation, since "The First Step intervention at the very least appears to provide a vehicle to begin services prior to formal diagnosis". It further suggests a tiered model in which non-responders to a generic tier-two intervention receive additional screening to select those who might benefit from disorder-specific augmentations. This strategy operationalizes the authors' translational-research argument for expanding access to evidence-based treatment for young children at risk for ASD.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A validated broad-based tier-two intervention deliverable in school settings
  • A behavioral screening and referral pathway that identifies children with elevated externalizing behavior

Constraints¶

  • The article states First Step is not a comprehensive or specific treatment for ASD; children with known ASD require comprehensive intervention addressing all core symptoms
  • The screening targeted externalizing disorders, so the sample likely overrepresented externalizers and underrepresented children with internalizing behaviors or limited language

Target Learners¶

  • preschool children with challenging behavior who are at risk for ASD but not yet diagnosed

Target Learning Goals¶

  • early access to behavioral intervention prior to formal diagnosis
  • prosocial academic behaviors and school success skills

Claims¶

  • Preschool First Step was implemented with high fidelity and was feasible and socially acceptable for children at risk for ASD and their teachers and parents [+W]
  • Parents of children at risk for ASD reported significantly greater reductions in autism symptoms and gains in empathy after First Step, with medium-to-large but non-significant effects on communication and cooperation [+W]
  • Preschool First Step reduced teacher-reported ASD symptoms and problem behaviors in children at risk for ASD, with Hedges' g from -0.65 to -1.12 [+W]
  • In a responder analysis, 38% of intervention versus 7% of control children recovered or improved on parent-reported autism symptoms, a non-significant difference [+W]
