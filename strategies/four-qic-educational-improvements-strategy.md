# Improve QIC education by shifting focus to project and change management skills, knowledge transfer, adapting principles to context, and inter-project reflexive learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on sense-making meetings with five experts, the article proposes four improvements to the educational program of quality improvement collaboratives: "1) shift the focus to project- and change management skills; 2) focus more on knowledge transfer to colleagues; 3) teach participants to adapt the taught principles to their own situations; and 4) foster intra-project reflexive learning to translate gained insights to other projects (inter-project learning)." Concrete inter-project tools include writing reviews discussed with other teams, reflective meetings to share lessons learned, and brainstorming meetings on potential problems and solutions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- QIC faculty must understand what a team wants to do in its own organization and what it needs for this, per the suggested team-centered educational approach
#### Constraints
- Attendees considered change and project management skills hard to teach because they are personal competences developed over time; not all need to be taught in a QIC but must be available in participating teams

### Target Learners
- healthcare professionals in hospital quality improvement project teams

### Target Learning Goals
- project and change management skills
- communication and knowledge-transfer skills
- reflexive skills for adapting improvement principles to local contexts
