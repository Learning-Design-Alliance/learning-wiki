# Use role-play and hands-on activities with models and analogies to teach Einsteinian physics to young children

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 review, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Einstein-First team uses role-play to teach concepts of Einsteinian physics because it helps students look at occurrences of the universe from different perspectives. Role plays cement modern terminology such as photon, atom, and curved space, and allow shy students to participate behind a mask. Hands-on activities using models and analogies — such as Nerf guns for photons and spacetime simulators for curved space — make abstract concepts tangible for young learners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Presenters need training in Einsteinian physics content and age-appropriate pedagogy
#### Constraints
- Some content was above the understanding of the class; presenters spoke too long at one time causing some children to lose focus

### Target Learners
- Primary school students aged 7-8

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning concepts and language of Einsteinian physics
