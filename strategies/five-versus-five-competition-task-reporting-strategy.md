# Implement a five-versus-five head-to-head group competition with task-reporting debriefings to raise robot-simulator training efficiency without extra equipment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's implementable recipe: divide trainees into subgroups, then into two five-member red-versus-blue teams that train head-to-head on simulators with partially visible opponent progress, announce winning rules after grouping, allow participation at any time, and hold frequent intragroup task reports guided by peer evaluation. The authors report that "frequent intragroup task reports can improve the scoring efficiency" and recommend the mechanism "in large-scale training conditions or training institutions with lower training levels".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two identical simulators and an automatic scoring system providing an overall score and seven sub-standards
- A standardized operation video and a trainer to highlight key parameters such as workspace range
#### Constraints
- The authors note simulator programs "cannot cover all the skill requirements required for robotic surgery" and "cannot completely replace actual machine practices"
- Evidence comes from fourth- and fifth-year undergraduate volunteers; the authors call for extension to residents, experts, and professors

### Target Learners
- robotic-naive undergraduate medical students
- novice trainees in large-scale robot training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- robotic surgical operational skills
- satisfactory operational work habits
- training efficiency under limited equipment and instructors
