# Target modifiable factors — early intervention for struggling students, substance-use prevention, satisfaction and self-perception support, and study-habit coaching — to improve medical student performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's discussion and conclusion recommend a set of interventions aimed at the modifiable factors its regression identified. It states "targeted early intervention programs are crucial to support struggling students and prevent academic setbacks", calls for "robust prevention and cessation initiatives" for psychoactive substances, and urges universities to "implement peer-mentoring and academic support programs to guide students facing academic difficulties, as well as stress-management and resilience-building workshops". It also recommends strengthening psychological support services, integrating stress-management education into the curriculum, promoting time-management skills and efficient daily revision strategies, and regular DREEM-based evaluation of the educational environment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional capacity for support services, prevention policies, peer-mentoring programs and curriculum-integrated stress-management education
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is a cross-sectional study, so the authors note causal inferences are precluded and call for longitudinal studies to evaluate intervention effectiveness

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students, particularly struggling students and international students facing adaptation challenges

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance, student well-being, academic self-perception, study habits and substance-use prevention
