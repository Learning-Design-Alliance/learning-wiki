# Joint basic-science and clinical faculty debriefing using Socratic facilitation to immerse basic science content in the clinical case

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Each debrief pairs a basic science faculty member (Ph.D. in immunology/microbiology) with a board-certified allergist/immunologist physician, attempting to elicit "the critical, shared teaching approach to integrative learning". All faculty use a Socratic approach, engaging students in dialogue on normal immunologic development and signaling pathways, correlating pathway aberrations with specific immunodeficiencies (XLA, CVID, DiGeorge, SCID, Bare Lymphocyte Syndromes, Hyper IgM), interpreting diagnostic tests, and comparing diseases on basic science and clinical grounds. Debriefs last approximately 25-30 minutes after a 15-minute case.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty training: a required six-hour course on advocacy and inquiry debrief techniques plus a one-hour case-specific faculty development meeting before each session
- Standardized faculty guide with goals, objectives and discussion prompts
- Three faculty pairs kept constant across both simulation days

### Target Learners
- Second-year preclinical medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Consolidating basic immunology concepts and applying them to clinical disease presentation and differential diagnosis
