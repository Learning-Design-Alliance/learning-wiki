# Use high-fidelity simulation with standardized clinical scenarios to evaluate and train emergency guideline adherence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q3`–`q4` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article demonstrates using high-fidelity simulation with a programmable mannequin and a standardized clinical scenario to provide a standardized clinical environment for detailed investigation of clinical practice and guideline implementation. All participants performed an identical simulated status epilepticus scenario individually, with performances frame-in-frame video/audio recorded and coded second-by-second by two independent observers. Training in simulation centers represents a valuable additional key component to increase the quality of emergency management.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A programmable high-fidelity mannequin capable of simulating diverse physiological and pathological states
- Comprehensive emergency medications and equipment available
- An instructed and pre-trained nurse acting strictly command driven
- Frame-in-frame video/audio recording with independent observer coding
#### Constraints
- The simulator setting may have only limited transferability and generalizability
- Real-world research is needed to confirm impact on patient outcomes

### Target Learners
- resident physicians in various medical fields

### Target Learning Goals
- emergency management of status epilepticus
- guideline adherence in time-critical clinical scenarios
