# Cascade (peer) training model for affordable scale-up of RDT skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Trained health workers transfer RDT skills to colleagues at their own facilities, multiplying a central training event without repeated central sessions. The study observed ten peer-trained workers performing comparably, and concludes "Future training programmes should systematically evaluate the utility and cost-effectiveness of the cascade training model in the implementation of parasite-based malaria diagnosis." The authors note peer training may help maintain use amid high staff turnover and that over half of training costs were transport and per-diems.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Initial central training of at least one worker per facility who can then train colleagues
#### Constraints
- In this study the cascade finding was incidental with not enough data to compare it with one-day first-hand training

### Target Learners
- lower-level health workers at peripheral facilities

### Target Learning Goals
- performing and interpreting malaria RDTs in routine fever case management
