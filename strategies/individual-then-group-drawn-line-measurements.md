# Structure drawn-conductor laboratory work as individual uniformity measurements followed by paired or grouped R-versus-l measurements aggregated into a joint plot

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a concrete collaboration structure for the series-circuit exercise: "all students can individually draw lines with different pencils or papers and perform uniformity measurements", then "perform in pairs or groups" the R versus l measurements and "aggregate them in a joint plot for discussion and conclusions". Students using the same paper and pencil combination then discuss differences introduced by different drawers, leading to assessments of error margins and of the meaningfulness of comparing results across conductors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each student needs pencils, matte paper and access to a digital ohmmeter; group aggregation requires a shared plot for discussion.
#### Constraints
- The article notes drawn conductors are sensitive to drawing pressure, so all results compared should follow the same drawing protocol by the same person, or differences must be discussed explicitly.

### Target Learners
- high school students
- early undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- systematic experimentation
- collaborative data aggregation and discussion of measurement error
