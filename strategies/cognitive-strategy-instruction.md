# Cognitive Strategy Instruction

## Description
Cognitive strategy instruction (CSI) makes the hidden mental procedures of skilled performers — summarizing, predicting, decomposing problems, monitoring comprehension — explicit and teachable. The instructor names the strategy, models its use ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)), guides practice, and gradually transfers responsibility to learners until they deploy the strategy independently. The goal is not content mastery alone but durable, transferable procedures learners own.

## Design Implications

CSI works because strategies reduce the working-memory and search burden of complex tasks, letting learners allocate effort to understanding rather than figuring out *how* to proceed [Cognitive load management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md). Effects are strongest when instruction is explicit and sequenced — model, guided practice, independent practice — rather than when strategies are merely named or suggested [Palincsar & Brown's reciprocal teaching combined modeling, guided practice, and fading to produce large comprehension gains.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]. Teaching the *when and why* of a strategy (conditional knowledge), not just its steps, is what produces transfer to new tasks [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-specified strategy with named, teachable steps (e.g., reciprocal teaching's predict–question–clarify–summarize cycle)
- [Modeling](../elements/think-aloud.md) that makes expert reasoning visible, not just the strategy's outcome
- Guided [practice](../elements/practice.md) with [coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and corrective feedback before independent use
- Deliberate [fading](../elements/fading.md) of support so learners take ownership [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+S]
- Explicit discussion of when, where, and why to use the strategy

#### Constraints
- Strategies taught as rote sequences without conditional knowledge are applied indiscriminately or abandoned once instruction ends [-M]
- Ineffective when the strategy is redundant with what learners already do — stronger readers gain little from comprehension-strategy training [Expertise reversal: guidance that helps novices can burden those with more prior knowledge.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Overloading novices with multiple new strategies at once exceeds working memory and produces shallow acquisition of all of them [-M]
- Requires sustained practice over weeks; brief one-off "strategy lessons" show negligible effects [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Reciprocal teaching** — learners take turns leading the strategy dialogue in small groups, adding peer modeling
- **Self-regulated strategy development (SRSD)** — pairs strategy steps with goal-setting and self-monitoring for writing [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- **Embedded vs. standalone** — strategies can be taught within subject-matter lessons or as separate lessons then applied; embedded instruction improves transfer
- **Prompt cards and checklists** — externalize strategy steps during the guided phase, then fade

### Target Learners
- Novices and struggling learners who lack effective procedures for the task [Strategy instruction produces its largest gains for lower-achieving students.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+S]
- Learners with adequate foundational knowledge but poor task approach — CSI cannot substitute for missing prerequisite skills [-M]
- Less valuable for advanced learners who already use effective strategies [Expertise reversal: guidance that helps novices can burden those with more prior knowledge.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency in complex tasks: reading comprehension, writing, mathematical problem solving
- Metacognitive development: planning, monitoring, and evaluating one's own cognition
- Transfer: applying learned procedures to unfamiliar tasks when conditional knowledge is taught

### Instructions
1. **Assess current approach** — identify what learners already do and where their procedures break down.
2. **Name and justify the strategy** — state the steps and explain when and why it helps.
3. **Model with a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)** — the instructor performs the task while verbalizing decisions, monitoring, and self-correction.
4. **Guide [practice](../elements/practice.md)** — learners execute the strategy with prompts, checklists, and [coaching](../elements/coaching.md); feedback should target the process, not just the answer [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
5. **Fade support** — remove prompts and hand over the dialogue as competence grows [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+S].
6. **Promote maintenance and transfer** — have learners apply the strategy across varied tasks and self-monitor continued use.

## Related Strategies
- [Reciprocal teaching](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — the canonical CSI program for reading comprehension
- [Self-regulated learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) — CSI is the cognitive half; SRL adds motivational and monitoring components

## Examples
- **Reciprocal Teaching** (Palincsar & Brown, 1984) — small reading groups rotate the roles of predictor, questioner, clarifier, and summarizer; the teacher models each role first, then fades. Produced 30+ percentile-point comprehension gains with seventh graders.
- **SRSD for writing** (Graham & Harris) — students learn genre-specific planning strategies (e.g., POW + TREE for opinion essays) alongside goal-setting and self-instruction; widely validated for students with writing difficulties.
- **Cognitively Guided Instruction** ([CGI for math](../patterns/cognitively-guided-instruction-cgi-for-math.md)) — teachers elicit and formalize children's informal problem-solving strategies, then build instruction around them.
