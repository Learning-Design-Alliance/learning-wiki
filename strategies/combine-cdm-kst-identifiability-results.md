# Use the CDM–KST link to combine identifiability results across both research camps when developing diagnostic assessment models

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends exploiting the formal link between cognitive diagnostic models and knowledge space theory so that identifiability results from both traditions can be pooled. It presents this integration as demonstrating "the benefits that arise from integrating the two theoretical perspectives", since combining results from both camps yields a more complete identifiability picture than either alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Practitioners must be able to translate models between the CDM and KST formalisms using the equivalences established in the article
#### Constraints
- The strategy rests on the specific model equivalences proved (MS-DINA and CBLIM), not on arbitrary pairs of models

### Target Learners
- Researchers and assessment developers building diagnostic measurement models

### Target Learning Goals
