# Sloppy jotting: unstructured, error-filled brainstorming to separate idea generation from surface polishing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The sloppy jotting technique is "a deliberate practice of unstructured, error -filled brainstorming" in which students "give themselves permission to write badly," producing rapid unfiltered drafts full of typos and half-formed ideas without stopping to edit. The article reports students discovered thoughts they did not know they had, and that the unpolished drafts create psychological distance facilitating evaluation of ideas independent of presentation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must produce rapid, unfiltered text without stopping to edit or structure their thoughts
#### Constraints
- Many students initially resist, having been trained that good writing means error-free prose from the first draft

### Target Learners
- higher education students writing drafts

### Target Learning Goals
- prioritizing substantive exploration over formal correctness in early drafts
- building inhibitory control over the fluency-equals-quality instinct

### Affordances
- Form Substance Discrimination Construct
