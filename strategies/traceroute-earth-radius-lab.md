# A traceroute-based lab in which students estimate the Earth's radius from Internet packet round-trip times across transoceanic cables

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Students run traceroute from an internet-enabled computer to a distant hub, read round-trip times per hop, and identify the time jump at a transoceanic cable crossing. Using d=vt with light speed in glass about 2/3 of vacuum, they convert the delay to cable distance, then use ratio and proportion with a globe and string to estimate the Earth's radius. The authors report it is 'an inexpensive and quick way to involve introductory physics students in a hands-on use of scientific notation and to teach them about systematics in data.'

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An internet-enabled computer with the traceroute utility, a classroom globe, string, and identification of the geographic nodes on either side of a transoceanic cable
#### Constraints
- The most difficult part is determining the geographic location of the nodes on either side of the transoceanic cable, since machine names are sometimes non-descript or not given

### Target Learners
- introductory general physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- hands-on use of scientific notation
- applying d=vt and elementary geometry
- understanding systematics and bias in data
- basic facts about the Internet and geography
