# Interview graduate student researchers and faculty about lab practices as a first step in setting learning goals for content-specific lab courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends grounding learning-goal development for content-specific courses in interviews of graduate student researchers, since "proficiency in electronics depends largely on specific content knowledge" and content-specific courses parallel the hands-on experience of graduate researchers. The authors suggest instructors "encourage the use of the Internet, written documentation, and experts outside the classroom (as a proxy for company technicians) to learn electronics," and that the classification scheme "could be modified and extended to other areas of lab work besides electronics, for example optics."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to graduate student researchers across relevant sub-disciplines; semi-structured interviews with coding and inter-rater reliability checks
#### Constraints
- The scheme was developed in one physics department at CU Boulder and validated only with that sample (22 students, 9 faculty)
- The authors state they begin by focusing on academic applications as a proxy for other research sectors

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- developing electronics skills aligned with research-lab practice
