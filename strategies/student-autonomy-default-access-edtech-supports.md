# Empower student autonomy by making supports student-facing and increasing default access, which can raise engagement and reduce barriers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report recommends having student-facing options available so students can choose whichever feature they need in the moment, and increasing access to and encouraging use of features such as audio support. District data showed all students, including those with greater needs, used features at similar rates, and feature use was associated with students attempting questions even on difficult articles, essentially eliminating the effect of article difficulty. Teachers commented that the features enable greater independence, including support during small-group instruction when the teacher cannot help.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Features must be available on the student-facing platform as choices; defaults matter, as the audio opt-out default increased availability and use.
#### Constraints
- The report notes more research is needed to determine how features affect learning; the engagement relationship is correlational.

### Target Learners
- K-12 students with varying strengths and challenges, including below-grade readers, English language learners, and students with disabilities

### Target Learning Goals
- Engagement with reading assignments
- Independent reading strategy use
