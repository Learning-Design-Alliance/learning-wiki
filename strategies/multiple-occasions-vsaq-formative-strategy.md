# Run at least two formative VSAQ occasions rather than lengthening a single occasion, and have teachers review self-marked answers after each session

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing "multiple occasions with at least two formative VSAQ examinations" because adding occasions raises the Phi-coefficient more efficiently than adding items: two occasions of ten items reach Phi = 0.71 while a single occasion needs twenty-five items. Because a ten-item ISF-VSAQ takes students roughly 30 to 60 minutes, extending items risks lengthening exams and cognitive overload. Teachers should validate unexpected self-marked answers after every session and feed common errors back before the summative exam.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An electronic platform exporting answers for efficient teacher review
- Clear guides and examples before each session
- Time for teachers to review unexpected answers after every session
#### Constraints
- Studied only in cardiovascular pharmacology with pre-clinical students at one institution; the optimal number of sessions for maximizing knowledge retention needs further research

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- pre-clinical third-year students

### Target Learning Goals
- reliable formative assessment of applied pharmacology knowledge
- timely self-feedback on knowledge gaps
