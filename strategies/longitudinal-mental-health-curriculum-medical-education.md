# Integrate mandatory, longitudinal mental health literacy, self-care and reflective practice curricula throughout medical education rather than as isolated end-of-programme options.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that institutions integrate mandatory, longitudinal teaching in mental health literacy, self-care and reflective practice throughout the curriculum, rather than as isolated end-of-programme options, to normalise help-seeking and equip students with effective coping tools. The authors further recommend that institutions ensure confidential, easily accessible counselling and psychological support independent from assessment structures, along with clear guidance on rational psychotropic use (particularly benzodiazepines) in collaboration with healthcare services. Proactive screening should target at-risk students, including those reporting no coping strategies, no physical activity, marked functional impairment or multiple psychotropic medications.
