# Use dialogue and shared interpretation discussion to develop reading and writing competences and metalinguistic consciousness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that pupils share thoughts about a text and discuss interpretation-related questions, because dialogue is extremely beneficial when developing reading and writing competences. Sharing thoughts lets pupils consciously observe their own reading processes, and discussing interpretive questions develops critical reading skills reaching metalinguistic consciousness. In the study, group work gave all children the opportunity to participate, and even less competent children actively expressed opinions and contributed to writing stories.
