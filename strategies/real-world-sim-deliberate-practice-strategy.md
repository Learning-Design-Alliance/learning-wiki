# Use simulated real-world tasks as a platform for deliberate practice and risk-perception training with cognitive-state monitoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend extending their approach beyond measurement: "real-world tasks can be used as a platform for deliberate practice and improved risk perception, where performance and brain activity can be targeted to improve decision making process especially in hazardous conditions." They further suggest that monitoring brain activity such as in bilateral caudate could track cognitive states and task engagement, valuable for training recruits, and note portable neuroimaging methods (fNIRS, EEG) being developed for simulator-based training and re-certification settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated simulated real-world task that can be paired with performance and brain-activity measurement
- Portable neuroimaging or cognitive-state monitoring systems, which the article notes are still under development
#### Constraints
- The article's own fMRI monitoring required a scanner bore whose lighting and technical conditions defeated eye tracking; portable application remains prospective

### Target Learners
- pilot trainees and recruits, especially in military and simulator-based training settings

### Target Learning Goals
- safe landing decisions in hazardous weather; improved risk perception
