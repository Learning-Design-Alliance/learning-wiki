Focus collaborative diagnosis on initially unshared information through a cycle of eliciting and sharing¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because group members tend to share too little relevant information and shared information is discussed more often and earlier than unshared information, the article recommends that collaborators "focus more heavily on initially unshared information" in collaborative diagnosis, since this strategy also leads to more accurate diagnoses. The article concludes that "A cycle of eliciting and sharing information may consequently be beneficial"; simulation settings can scaffold such strategies during training.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Collaborators must possess distributed or partially unshared information for the strategy to pay off; support may be needed when collaborators lack collaboration experience

Constraints¶

  • The article frames this as an expectation drawn from prior research (e.g., hidden-profile findings) rather than a directly manipulated result in this study

Target Learners¶

  • pre-service and practising teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • accurate collaborative diagnosis of pupils' skills

Claims¶

  • Pre-service teachers' collaborative diagnostic accuracy does not per se exceed individual diagnostic accuracy, but collaboration support can significantly enhance the added value of collaborative diagnosis [+W]
  • Increased information sharing appears to be the reason collaboration support enhances the added value of collaborative diagnosis [+W]
