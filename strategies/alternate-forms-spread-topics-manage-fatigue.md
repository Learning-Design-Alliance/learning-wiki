# Alternate instructional formats and spread topics apart to manage fatigue in intensive programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
In a month-long intensive program, the authors managed mental and physical fatigue by switching between lectures, hands-on activities, inquiries, and field trips, and by scheduling less intensive, student-driven lectures among content-driven ones. They report that the interwoven 2007 schedule worked better than isolating one course in one week: "The schedu ling of “Stars and Cells” was less fatiguing, and learning beneﬁted from spreading ideas apart."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple course components in different formats that can be interleaved across the schedule; awareness of program-wide constraints such as field trips and meal times.
#### Constraints
- Field trips are partial mental breaks but physically draining; students dislike missing meal times with friends from other clusters, so scheduling must coordinate with the larger program.

### Target Learners
- high-school students in residential intensive programs

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained engagement and retention across a multi-week course
