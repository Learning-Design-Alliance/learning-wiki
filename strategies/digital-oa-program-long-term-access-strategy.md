# Offer continuous digital self-management programs to support long-term adherence where face-to-face programs are inaccessible

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend offering digital self-management programs as a way to deliver first-line OA education and exercise to patients whom traditional programs fail to reach. Based on the registry cohort's positive results at 24 and 48 weeks, they argue "digital interventions may represent a viable alternative for patients without access to or not interested in participating in traditional face to face programmes" and that such programs "may also complement traditional programmes to enhance long-term adherence to treatment". This is a discussion-level recommendation, not a tested comparison.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A digital program delivering education and exercise with human supervision (chat or telephone access to a physiotherapist) is needed for this strategy to work as described.
#### Constraints
- The article states further investigation into specific components of digital interventions outside of exercise is warranted; the recommendation rests on an uncontrolled registry cohort, so causal effects are not established.

### Target Learners
- Adults with hip or knee osteoarthritis without access to or not interested in face-to-face programs

### Target Learning Goals
- OA self-management knowledge
- long-term adherence to exercise and education
