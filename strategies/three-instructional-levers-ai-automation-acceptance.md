# Three theory-grounded instructional levers for AI-automation tooling: instruction-sequencing scaffolds, self-efficacy supports, and trust-calibration interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes three concrete instructional levers grounded in trust-in-automation theory and human–AI interaction research: "instruction -sequencing scaffolds for effort-expectancy friction", "self -efficacy supports for the gap between recognizing usefulness and feeling capable", and "explicit reliability demonstrations that turn aggregate output-quality endorsement into calibrated trust". The levers follow from the mixed-methods integration, particularly the Output Quality divergence, and place verification behaviour rather than acceptance attitude at the center of pedagogical design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured opportunities for students to encounter, recognize, and respond to automation failure, not merely opportunities to observe success
#### Constraints
- The article states evaluation of acceptance should not be reduced to a single immediately-post-instruction survey; longer-horizon, behaviourally anchored measurement is required to capture whether favourable initial attitudes mature into appropriately calibrated reliance

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate computer engineering students with basic-to-intermediate Python competence and daily use of general-purpose AI tools but no prior AI-development experience

### Target Learning Goals
- Acceptance of and appropriately calibrated reliance on AI-automation tooling, including verification of outputs produced by opaque language-model agents
