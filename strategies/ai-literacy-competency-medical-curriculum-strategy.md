# Frame LLM integration as an assessable AI literacy competency with differentiated integrity policies and reasoning-focused assessment redesign

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends a three-part practice response to its synthesis: treat LLM use as "a learnable competency rather than a prohibited tool or an unregulated resource", incorporate AI literacy—"the ability to critically appraise, verify, and appropriately weight LLM-generated content"—as an explicit assessable outcome, replace recall-heavy multiple-choice with formats evaluating clinical reasoning and uncertainty management, and adopt institution-specific integrity policies distinguishing constructive from dishonest use, since blanket prohibition is likely ineffective.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formal incorporation of AI literacy as an explicit and assessable learning outcome within competency-based medical education frameworks.
#### Constraints
- Blanket prohibition policies without enforcement capacity risk institutional credibility deficits; assessments relying predominantly on factual recall are particularly vulnerable to LLM completion.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students (preclinical and clinical years)
- Medical school faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- AI literacy (critical appraisal, verification, and appropriate weighting of LLM-generated content)
- Clinical reasoning and applied judgement
