# Use gesturing and motor-imagery supports as candidate interventions for SES-related spatial thinking gaps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that higher parental education was associated with higher premotor activity during mental rotation, the authors propose supports for motor imagery, such as gesturing, as a possible intervention. They note that "gesturing interventions can be beneficial in improving lower-SES childrens' spatial thinking and subsequent mathematics performance in preschoolers," citing prior work. The strategy targets the premotor/motor-imagery pathway the study linked to SES and STEM achievement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Future research must first confirm whether SES differences in premotor activity reflect strategy differences such as motor imagery or active manipulation.
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a future direction; the current study did not test gesturing interventions, and its cross-sectional design cannot infer causality.

### Target Learners
- high school students from lower-SES backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- spatial thinking
- STEM achievement
