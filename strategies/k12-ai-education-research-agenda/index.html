Define a shared empirical research agenda grounding K-12 AI education designs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The commentary's central recommendation is that the field move beyond design work to systematic empirical study of how children learn AI. As it states, "it is time now to define a shared, more basic research agenda to ground our emerging frameworks, curricula, and systems on more solid empirical ground." Proposed strands include longitudinal studies of AI conceptual understanding and beliefs, collaborations with developmental psychologists and math education researchers, and grade-band analyses of the Five Big Ideas to identify what is and is not developmentally appropriate.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instruments for measuring AI attitudes and knowledge must evolve, be tested across systems and curricula, and be adapted for different grade levels

Constraints¶

  • Evidence for many current design choices is still emerging; demand for curricula has outpaced the field's empirical research capacity

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 learners, including currently underserved K-2

Target Learning Goals¶

  • AI conceptual understanding, beliefs, and ethics

Claims¶

  • Elementary students hold limited views of the difference between programmed behavior and AI-produced behavior [+W]
  • An ethics-first middle school curriculum (DAILy) led nearly all participants to express more nuanced views of AI [+W]
  • Student questionnaire responses suggest K-12 learners can learn advanced AI concepts [+W]
  • Middle school students in a dance/creativity AI workshop reported low self-efficacy beliefs about AI [+W]
  • Short-duration TPACK-based professional development improved teachers' ML knowledge and interest (ML4STEM) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Advance AI-motivation research through theory-driven, pedagogically explicit, longitudinal designs covering broader motivational constructs
  • Collaborative cross-cultural research agenda for validating AI-enabled pedagogical accompaniment
  • Develop AI literacy frameworks that build metacognitive awareness distinguishing system-assisted performance from independent competence
