Use the Meeting-House Debate strategy to instruct critical thinking skills in EFL reading classes¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The Meeting-House Debate strategy is a structured classroom debate format in which each side gives its opening argument, the rest of the class questions the debaters or offers comments, a teacher acts as a moderator ensuring each team receives questions equally, and finally each side gives its final argument. In this study it was implemented with debater teams of five members, twelve "debriefers" who asked questions and gave critical opinions, debate etiquette instruction, brainstormed and voted-upon controversial topics, and sessions held twice a week for one and a half months. The article concludes that improving reading skills through this debate technique was superior to the lecturing strategy.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Debater teams, "debriefers" responsible for questioning and voting, debate etiquette training, and controversial topics chosen by brainstorming and voting

Constraints¶

  • Treatment sessions lasted one and a half months with 11th graders; the authors note the limitation of treatment duration and the delimitation of assessing just 11th graders

Target Learners¶

  • Intermediate EFL high school students (11th graders)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading comprehension
  • Critical thinking skills

Claims¶

  • Instructing critical thinking through debate significantly improves EFL learners' critical thinking skills compared to lecturing [+W]

  • Gender is not a significant factor in EFL learners' critical thinking skill gains from debate instruction [+W]
