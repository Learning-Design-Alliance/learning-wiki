# Design peer assessment in PtBL with rater training, collective group ratings, non-anonymous paper-based scoring, and qualitative comments to support reliability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing peer assessment in PtBL with specific design features that its own study used and its discussion links to reliability: raters were "familiarized with every item on the rubrics" with illustrative scenarios, ratings were given collectively by each student group, assessments were paper-based and non-anonymous, and qualitative comments accompanied scores. The discussion also recommends rater training on implicit biases, anonymity and randomization where favoritism is a risk, involving students in rubric development, and regular assessor meetings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires rater familiarization and training before assessment, a validated rubric, and a scoring format (paper-based, with qualitative comments) as described
#### Constraints
- The article states peer assessment "cannot replace teacher evaluations" and that further improvements may be needed before summative use; bias remains a student concern

### Target Learners
- pre-clinical medical students in group-based project courses

### Target Learning Goals
- reliable and valid summative contribution of peer assessment; student engagement and feedback quality
