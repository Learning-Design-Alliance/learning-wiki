Five design principles for integrating music into EMI instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

From its synthesis, the review proposes five design principles for EMI practitioners: "Alignment: Choose music that connects thematically or linguistically with disciplinary content"; Brevity (keep tasks short, ≤3 minutes, to respect content delivery); Scaffolding (structure pre-, while-, and post-activity phases); Recurrence (brief, regular activities for cumulative gains); and Learner Agency (allow choice within curated options). The review states these principles serve EMI's dual objectives of disciplinary knowledge acquisition and language development and can be adapted flexibly across fields.

Claims¶

  • Digital music platforms were consistently linked to positive learner perceptions and motivation, with effectiveness contingent on teacher mediation [+W]
  • Direct EMI evidence for music-mediated pedagogy is scarce, concentrated in arts disciplines, and lacks controlled measures [+W]
  • Across the synthesized corpus, 34 studies reported affective gains while 17 found statistically significant listening or fluency improvements and 21 reported no fluency outcomes [+W]
  • A six-week lyric gap-fill program improved listening comprehension scores and self-reported engagement among Korean university students [+W]
  • Background music with lyrics impaired reading comprehension and listening performance, especially under high linguistic load [-W]
  • Rhythm-shadowing training produced measurable improvements in mean length of run and pause ratio as fluency indicators [+W]
