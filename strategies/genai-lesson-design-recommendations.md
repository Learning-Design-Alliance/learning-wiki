# Design GenAI lesson-planning tools around teacher-specific alignment, integrated multimedia student materials, bite-sized activities, and sustained professional development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors close with five actionable recommendations: systems should learn and adapt to teachers' individual preferences while balancing surveillance concerns; integrated GenAI programs should "generate a variety of multimedia student materials to go with lesson plans – including worksheets, PowerPoints, handouts and templates, and mathematical visuals" with built-in teacher collaboration; suggestions should be shorter, bite-sized chunks involving one or two active learning approaches at a time; research should address time efficiency; and the field should address big-picture concerns through teacher education on how GenAI works, its affordances, limitations, and ethical implications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coupling activity generation with sustained teacher professional development covering mental models of LLMs, dangers of bias and hallucinations, prompt engineering, implementation training, and an ongoing community of practice
#### Constraints
- Specificity to teachers' preferences needs to be balanced with issues of surveillance of teachers using GenAI

### Target Learners
- Mathematics teachers, especially those new to integrating generative learning

### Target Learning Goals
- Increasing the prevalence of evidence-based generative learning activities in mathematics instruction
