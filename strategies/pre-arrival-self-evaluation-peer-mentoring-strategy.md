# Offer pre-arrival materials and self-evaluation paired with developmental exercises, plus peer mentoring, to foster adaptive traits and social support before students arrive on campus

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on the PHABS-PSS framework, the review recommends that practitioners ensure students have or are developing a social support structure on arrival, and foster key adaptive traits and skills such as "developing positive coping strategies, conscientiousness, and positive attitudes towards help seeking". Because evidence for curriculum-embedded interventions is limited, interventions should be timed before students arrive: universities could offer materials for incoming students to review during the gap between accepting an offer and course start, revisited during induction. Self-evaluation exercises should identify strengths and areas for development but must be paired with developmental exercises to avoid fixed mindsets, and peer mentoring from more senior students can leverage social support and support acclimation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Self-evaluation exercises must be paired with developmental exercises to ensure students do not develop fixed mindsets about their strengths
#### Constraints
- Given limited evidence for curriculum-embedded interventions (Upsher et al., 2022), the authors favour pre-arrival timing; institutional differences should be acknowledged when assessing even person-centred interventions

### Target Learners
- incoming traditional first-year university students, during the period between accepting an offer and arriving on campus

### Target Learning Goals
- psychosocial transition success: social support building, positive coping, conscientiousness, positive help-seeking attitudes, and acclimation to university

### Affordances
- Phabs Pss Framework
