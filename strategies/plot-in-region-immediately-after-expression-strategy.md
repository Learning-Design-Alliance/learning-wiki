# Ask students to plot the function in each region immediately after writing its expression, using coordinate axes with irrelevant regions shaded out

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article found that asking students to plot the electric field in each region immediately after finding an expression for it impacted students positively, making them "more likely to find the correct expressions and more likely to be consistent in plotting the expressions they f ound". Students received coordinate axes with the irrelevant regions shaded out, which the authors hypothesize focused attention on relevant information. Students in Groups 1 and 2 obtained no extra points for these intermediate steps, so the benefit came from the scaffold itself, not grading incentives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must first write the expression for the electric field in each region before plotting it in that region
- Coordinate axes with irrelevant regions shaded out are provided to isolate the region at hand
#### Constraints
- The added instruction to additionally evaluate the field at beginning, mid and end points of each region before graphing had an adverse effect and should not be combined with this scaffold, per the article's findings

### Target Learners
- calculus-based introductory physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- writing mathematical expressions for the electric field
- graphing the electric field consistently with those expressions
