# Deploy chatbots through university-provided messaging services with institutional support, without requiring teachers to join student chat groups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article derives recommendations for successful chatbot deployment in higher education from the survey responses. Since most teachers prefer institution-provided solutions and do not want to participate in student chat groups, the authors argue chatbots can "open the door for deployment of chatbots without the intervention of the professor, simply tapping university provided services". Teachers also want institutional support, official tools and maintenance by technical staff.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tools should be approved or provided by the university or educational centre and maintained by the centre's technical staff rather than the teacher.
#### Constraints
- Forcing teachers to create and participate in chat groups with students would create a certain amount of resistance, per the survey responses.

### Target Learners
- Higher education and vocational tertiary education teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- Supporting student communication, tutoring logistics and course organization via chatbots and messaging platforms
