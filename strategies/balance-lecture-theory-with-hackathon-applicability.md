# When running course-integrated hackathons, balance the quantity of lecture theory against its applicability to hackathon tasks and provide ample feedback sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A design strategy for educators following the hackathon-integrated course approach. The authors recommend weighing "the balance between the quantity of theory provided and its applicability in the hackathon tasks" and providing ample feedback sessions where educators discuss the lectures and their relation to the hackathon tasks. They further recommend that interventions account for likely low team familiarity in online settings and that more interesting real-life use-cases, possibly with industry collaboration, be used to boost student interest and participation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators prepare lecture materials and feedback sessions aligned with the hackathon tasks
#### Constraints
- Derived from one specific online cybersecurity course; the authors state it is not possible to generalize their findings beyond that context

### Target Learners
- university students in online courses using hackathons

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding lecture concepts well enough to complete hackathon tasks
- maintaining engagement and collaboration despite low team familiarity

## Claims

- [All teams rated the lecture intervention above average, with high and low grade teams rating it higher (M = 4.00) than middle grade teams (M = 3.50), and students complained of excessive, sometimes confusing lecture content](../claims/lecture-intervention-perception-grade-groups.md) [+W]
- [All teams rated the feedback intervention above average, and students preferred real-time online feedback sessions over written feedback](../claims/feedback-intervention-online-sessions-preferred.md) [+W]
