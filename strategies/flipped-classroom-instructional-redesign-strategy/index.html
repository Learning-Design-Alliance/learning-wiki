Instructors redesigning higher education courses should implement flipped classrooms as an effective pedagogical practice while attending to subject-specific and pre-class-engagement conditions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's conclusion recommends that "Instructors and other educational leaders in higher education institutions can pursue instruction redesigns and educational supports to implement ﬂipped classrooms as an eﬀective pedagogical practice". It also proposes "repeated use of ﬂipped classrooms and related, modiﬁed strategies on a trial-and-error basis", because effectiveness depends on students' effective use of pre-class time. Future research is urged to explore relationships between various forms of flipped classrooms and outcomes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must actually use the available pre-class time effectively, which the article states effectiveness depends on.

Constraints¶

  • The mixed results across departments and subjects show various instructional forms and strategies are factors determining effectiveness; STEM subjects such as chemistry, engineering, mathematics, and physics showed smaller effects.

Claims¶

  • Flipped classrooms in higher education show a medium overall positive effect on student outcomes compared with traditional lecture-based classrooms [+W]
  • Flipped classrooms benefit students in STEM subjects such as chemistry, engineering, mathematics, and physics less than students in other subjects [+W]
  • Flipped classroom effects are larger on affective and interpersonal outcomes than on cognitive outcomes [~W]
