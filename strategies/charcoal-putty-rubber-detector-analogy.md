# Charcoal and putty-rubber drawing exercise as an analogy for particle-detector interaction making the invisible visible

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students lay a charcoal ground across paper and use putty rubbers to draw by removing charcoal, creating white marks on a dark surface. The article states that "In this context it represents the particle interaction with the substance of the detector through which the particle traces become visible." The exercise encourages consideration of the whole visual field and overlapping trails to convey three-dimensional particle motion projected into two dimensions, followed by a critique linking artistic choices to real cloud and bubble chamber event features.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Charcoal, putty rubbers, and paper, with a critique session in which artistic choices are discussed in relation to the scientific context
#### Constraints
- The charcoal ground can serve as a Higgs field reference only depending on the scientific level of the students involved

### Target Learners
- Key Stage 4 students aged 14 to 16 in the British education system

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding that knowledge of particles is acquired through experiments in which particle interactions in detector material make traces visible
