# Deploy self-directed CBVT as a remote, faculty-free route for teaching basic procedural skills when in-person instruction is impractical

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends self-directed, incremental and sequential video training as a remote-learning model requiring "little to no direct faculty involvement" and no skills lab. The authors argue it "could fill part of the void if hospital-based training becomes unsafe or impractical", citing COVID-19 disruptions to rotations, and state the model "may be able to be modified or expanded to teach other skills or more complex skills". Videos are being re-formatted for widespread dissemination.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recorded stepwise tutorial videos hosted on a platform students can access anytime and any place
#### Constraints
- Long-term retention of skills is unknown
- Transfer from the table-top model to clinical settings is untested
- The stepwise design was not compared against other training paradigms

### Target Learners
- medical students
- junior surgical trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- basic procedural skills acquired outside the operating room
- remote self-paced skills practice

## Claims

- [Students engaged consistently with the gated CBVT videos, rating them 4.2/5 on average and the elective 4.3–4.6/5 on organization, value, and reusable resources, with 98% recommending it](../claims/cbvt-high-usage-and-course-satisfaction.md) [+W]
- [Novice medical students' product quality, technique (GRS), and speed rose significantly from pre-test to post-test across all six assessed knot-tying and suturing skills after a self-directed sequential CBVT course, in one uncontrolled pre-post study](../claims/cbvt-improves-pqs-grs-speed-six-skills.md) [+W]
- [Students' self-ratings of ability across 11 knot-tying and suturing skills rise from 1.4 to 3.9 (scale 1–5) after the CBVT elective (P < 0.01)](../claims/cbvt-self-ratings-rise-all-skills.md) [+W]
- [Students' written-exam knowledge of surgical instruments, materials, and techniques rises from 54% to 87% after the CBVT elective (P < 0.01)](../claims/cbvt-written-exam-54-to-87-percent.md) [+W]
- [Tying against tension showed smaller score increases than the other knot-tying skills from pre-test to post-test in one group after the CBVT course](../claims/tying-on-tension-most-difficult-cbvt-skill.md) [~W]
