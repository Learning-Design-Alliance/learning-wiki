# Use Kahoot continuously after each thematic block and collaborative TikTok video projects alongside traditional teaching in medical courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a concrete implementation: Kahoot quizzes of 10–15 questions at the end of each thematic block with immediate answer display and teacher-led discussion of distractors, plus group TikTok videos (3–4 students, ≤10 min) summarizing assigned topics with rubric-based evaluation of "creativity, content, ability to diffuse, reliability of scientific content, and compliance with time". Participation counted 10% of the final mark each. The authors recommend continuous rather than occasional use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A question bank categorized by difficulty from previous exams to keep the final exam comparable across cohorts
- A previously explained evaluation rubric and professor moderation for the video activity
- Student access to mobile devices and the institution's Moodle platform
#### Constraints
- Participation was voluntary, and self-selection may inflate apparent benefits
- Evidence comes from one subject at one institution; the authors state more studies are needed to extrapolate and generalize

### Target Learners
- third-year undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding indications and side effects of radiotherapy
- consolidation and assessment of course knowledge
- creative handling and communication of scientific content
