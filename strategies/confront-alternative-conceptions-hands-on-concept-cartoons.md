# Confront students directly with their alternative conceptions using student-centred, hands-on experiences or concept cartoons in real-world contexts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q4` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
As effective ways to promote conceptual change, the article recommends "student-centred, hands-on experiences or concept cartoons which confront students directly with their alternative conceptions". It adds that providing further support in a real-world context would be promising, because students' conceptions are context- and even situation-specific. This strategy targets student-tailored interventions for conceptual change.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must be student-tailored and directly confront learners with their own alternative conceptions
#### Constraints
- Students' conceptions are context- and situation-specific, so support in one context may not transfer

### Target Learners
- students holding alternative conceptions of photosynthesis and plant nutrition

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual change from alternative to scientific conceptions of tree assimilation
