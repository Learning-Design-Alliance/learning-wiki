# Design anticipation training around how the importance of cues evolves over the unfolding action

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors state their findings have implications for designing training programs to enhance perceptual-cognitive skill, arguing that "the importance of anticipation cues evolve over time". Training should therefore progress through the temporal phases of an opponent action, exposing learners to the cues available at each phase, and should account for which cues (such as hip-region information for direction) support which judgements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to representative film of opponent actions that can be temporally and spatially edited to isolate cue availability at different phases.
#### Constraints
- Deception and disguise of movements were not controlled in the test film, which the authors note influences anticipation accuracy; findings are from soccer penalty-kick anticipation with male goalkeepers.

### Target Learners
- soccer goalkeepers
- team-sport athletes developing anticipation skill

### Target Learning Goals
- anticipating opponent action outcomes from early postural cues
