# For single-examiner examinations, consider video-recording with subsequent review and re-scoring of flagged examiners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Where only one examiner is present per station and statistical bias detection fails, the authors suggest "full video-recording, with other examiners subsequently reviewing cases and re-scoring them" as a non-statistical possibility, or reviewing only tapes of examiners flagged as marginal by preliminary indices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Extensive examiner time for reviewing and re-scoring recorded cases
#### Constraints
- Would presumably cost examiner time equivalent to having two examiners at a station
- How a preliminary flagging analysis would work is not clear

### Target Learners
- candidates in clinical examinations with a single examiner per station

### Target Learning Goals
- detecting and deterring examiner bias where co-examiner comparison is impossible
