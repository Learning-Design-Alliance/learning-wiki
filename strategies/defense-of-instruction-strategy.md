# Defense of Instruction: coaches require teachers to articulate and defend instructional decisions with student data

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Defense of Instruction is the metacognitive practice at the center of every MAST activity, in which "teachers were asked to articulate their specific decisions and the reasons they made them". Teachers state class goals and objectives and their progress toward them, base self-evaluations on objective student achievement data, and answer coach questions that challenge them to refine goals and explore alternative methods. Delivered as a cognitive apprenticeship, coaches model their own decision-making aloud, then scaffold teachers until the questions become the teachers' own reflective practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- coaches trained to articulate their own thought processes and ask probing questions
- teachers collect objective student achievement and performance data to ground their self-evaluations
#### Constraints
- The defense process was initially unfamiliar to many teachers and required personalized coaching and scaffolding

### Target Learners
- in-service secondary mathematics and science teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- metacognitive self-monitoring, data-driven reflection, and intentional instructional decision-making
