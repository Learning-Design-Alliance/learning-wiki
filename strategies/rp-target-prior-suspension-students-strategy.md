# Target restorative practices implementation support toward students with a prior suspension history

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the RP intervention's clearest experimental effect was on students with a prior suspension, the article supports concentrating restorative relationship-building and problem-solving efforts on this high-risk subgroup. The authors write that "a prior suspension is one of the strongest predictors of a future suspension" and that the reduction for previously suspended students "represents the breaking of the cycle of suspensions". Speculative mechanisms include improved relationship-building through SEL curricula and circles, shifted adult mindsets attending to unmet needs, and increased student engagement in SEL skill-building such as restorative conferences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires the Whole School RP Project infrastructure: trained RP coordinators, weekly community-building circles, SEL curricula, and staff and administrator buy-in
#### Constraints
- The finding emerged after only one year of a two-year design truncated by the Covid-19 pandemic, was not preregistered, and the article does not know whether RP changed student behavior or staff response

### Target Learners
- Students with a prior history of out-of-school suspension
- K-12 students in urban public schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Breaking the cycle of repeated suspensions
- Reducing exclusionary discipline for high-risk students
- Dismantling the school-to-prison pipeline
