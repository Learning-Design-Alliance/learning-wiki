# Establish systematic audit-and-feedback systems with personalized coaching to evaluate and improve ICU assessment quality

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that healthcare organizations "establish systematic audit‐and‐feedback systems to evaluate assessment quality and provide personalized coaching for inconsistencies". In this study, the audit component paired every audited bedside nurse's ICDSC assessment with an independent blinded expert nurse evaluation, and the feedback component was delivered as "immediate corrective feedback and reinforced proper techniques" during one-on-one bedside mentoring. Educational interventions should be "implemented regularly and reinforced periodically to maintain nurse competencies".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trained expert nurse available to conduct blinded audit assessments and deliver personalized bedside coaching
- Electronic medical record access to extract bedside ratings for comparison
- A predefined randomization scheme to ensure sampling fairness across shifts, units and timeframes
#### Constraints
- The effect of the educational intervention was assessed only shortly after its completion; long-term sustainability remains uncertain
- Some nurses may have completed more assessments than others, creating a potential bias that cannot be fully excluded

### Target Learners
- Intensive care unit nurses
- Healthcare organizations and nurse educators

### Target Learning Goals
- Reliable clinical assessment practice and sustained nurse competency in delirium screening
