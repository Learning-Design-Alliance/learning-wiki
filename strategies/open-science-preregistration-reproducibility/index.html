Adopt open science practices—data and code sharing, pre-registration, and Registered Reports—to improve reproducibility and replicability¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article identifies reproducible research as a recurrent workshop issue with specific challenges for developmental cognitive neuroscience. It states "replicability and transparency can be improved by open science initiatives such as making data and code available to other researchers" and by pre-registered studies, in which the set-up and analysis plan are determined before data collection. Registered Reports conduct peer review mainly before data collection, so publication does not depend on obtained results, allowing unbiased publication of results.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Pre-registered reports should state the set-up and analysis plan before data collection, with post hoc adjustments explicitly stated
  • Large and representative samples to improve reproducibility

Constraints¶

  • Registered Reports are not the preferred format for purely exploratory research that is not hypothesis-driven

Target Learners¶

  • Not applicable—research-practice strategy benefiting studies of children and adolescents

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reliable knowledge about developmental and learning dynamics
