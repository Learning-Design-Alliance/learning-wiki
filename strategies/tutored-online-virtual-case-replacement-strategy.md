# Replace interrupted bedside apprenticeship with tutored online virtual patient sessions combining tutor interaction and independent practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
When in-hospital training is impossible, split students into small groups (18-23) and run two-hour tutored sessions on a virtual patient platform, each divided into a 30-min introduction, two independent 20-min practice sessions on the virtual patient, and a 50-min debriefing with the class connected via an online meeting platform. The article reports this format was appreciated by 93% of students and prevented training interruption.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors with adequate previous experience in conventional medical training, given advance access to the simulated cases
- Small groups to warrant the best modality of online training and allow enough interactivity with tutors
#### Constraints
- Reported for sixth-year students facing graduation deadlines during COVID-19 hospital bans; no learning-outcome measures were assessed, and randomized prospective studies are stated to be necessary

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining clinical training continuity when hospital placement is unavailable
