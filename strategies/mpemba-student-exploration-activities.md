# Eight open student exploration activities investigating freezing of water with varied containers, covers, hardness, stirring and freezers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes a set of eight student investigation activities on cooling and freezing of water, arguing that because "the phenomenon has no definitive explanation, it is an appropriate candidate to students' experimentation" and the activities "don't need any special equipment". The activities vary initial temperature, covering of containers, container shape, water hardness, stirring during cooling, freezer type, distilled-water supercooling, and methods to verify stronger flowing in hot water.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A freezer or homemade ice/salt cooling mixture
- Thermometers for temperature measurement
#### Constraints
- The phenomenon does not occur regularly, so results from similar initial conditions may differ and require discussion

### Target Learners
- secondary school students
- student teams in school projects

### Target Learning Goals
- experimental skills
- observation and careful description of measurement conditions
- discussion of differing results
