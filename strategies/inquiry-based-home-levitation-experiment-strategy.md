# Inquiry-based presentation of a home-performable levitation experiment with tunable depth of understanding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes presenting the salt-stratification cork experiment through an inquiry path: "the phenomenology is ﬁrst demonstrated to the students by a facilitator", after which students perform the experiment autonomously and are then lectured on the physical principles and analysis methods. Three driving puzzles are posed: the source of the mechanical work raising the cork, the origin of the restoring force, and the interaction between two corks. The level of deepening can be tuned to students' knowledge, making it suitable from elementary school to undergraduate levels.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Everyday materials: a weighed-down wine bottle cork with a steel marble, a jug, coarse kitchen salt, tap water; video tracking with the open-source Tracker software (and Fiji), feasible even with a smartphone
#### Constraints
- The experiment is particularly suitable for undergraduate students, who can appreciate all its implications; repeated measurements perturb the system, making long series of repeated measurements not useful

### Target Learners
- elementary school students
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- buoyancy and hydrostatic equilibrium
- density stratification
- oscillations and wave phenomena
- inquiry scientific reasoning
