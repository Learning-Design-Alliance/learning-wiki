# Implement the OD in routine OSCEs with a prior formative transition phase and dedicated examiner training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking implementation recommendation: examiners reached consensus that the OD "could serve as an effective summative tool for assessment of medical students' CR ability if implementation included a prior transitional formative phase to enable both students and examiners to become familiar with the methodology". The discussion further highlights delivering OD-specific training to develop assessor expertise and more consistent scoring, and plans to pilot-test the OD in a formative OSCE to evaluate prompt use and reliability maintenance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examiner training clarifying the task's aims, standardisation during OD discussions, and consistent rubric scoring
#### Constraints
- The way examiners ask questions may influence candidate performance, a concern especially if examiners increasingly adopt their own questioning instead of the prompts

### Target Learners
- final-year undergraduate medical students
- OSCE examiners

### Target Learning Goals
- valid and reliable assessment of clinical reasoning in summative OSCEs
