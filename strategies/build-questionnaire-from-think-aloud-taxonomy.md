# Construct task-specific strategy questionnaires directly from a think-aloud coding taxonomy, item by item

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends building questionnaires so that each item corresponds to one coding category of a think-aloud taxonomy for the same task, phrased in learners' own vocabulary from real protocol fragments, and referring explicitly to the just-finished task. The author reports this yielded "a correlation ( r=.51) between the questionnaire and the think-aloud protocols", higher than regularly reported, and cautiously concludes such questionnaires "might become reasonably adequate alternatives for the labor-intensive think-aloud method."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated think-aloud coding taxonomy for the target task; items explicitly referring to the specific task and text
#### Constraints
- The article states both instruments "should probably be continuously used in mixed-method designs for theoretical considerations", and the correlation was only marginally significant in a small sample

### Target Learners
- secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring learners' learning strategies and metacognitive activities
