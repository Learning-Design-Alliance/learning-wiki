# Role play-based assessment of therapist competence with simulated patients

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends assessing the skill component of therapist competence via standardised role plays: the trainee acts as therapist with a simulated patient enacting prepared clinical scenarios drawn from a battery chosen to represent the treatment's core strategies and procedures and patients of varying difficulty. This contrasts with the therapy quality method, which the article says is not suited to testing skill because sampling the treatment's main strategies across patients of varying difficulty is rarely feasible post-training and almost impossible pre-training. The closest approximation cited is Sholomskas and Carroll's (2006) protocol with recordings rated by blind raters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios must be representative of the treatment's core strategies and procedures and involve patients of varying difficulty; scores and competence thresholds must be validated against treatment manuals, expert views, therapists of known ability, and patient outcome
#### Constraints
- The major difficulty lies in creating the scenarios and devising a reliable way of rating performance; the method has not yet been used for this purpose

### Target Learners
- Trainee therapists being evaluated before, during and after training

### Target Learning Goals
- Applying treatment knowledge in clinical practice; demonstrating skill at implementing a specific treatment
