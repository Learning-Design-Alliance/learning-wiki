Use a scaffolded sequence of collaborative improv story-telling and story-writing games transitioning to individual writing to develop writing fluency¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes a specific instructional sequence: students first play collaborative oral improv story-telling games framed by "Yes, and..." and a simple story structure (establish character, relationship, objective and where; create a problem and solve it), then transition to collaborative written narratives using the same structure, and finally compose individual narratives. The sequence is designed to move students from collaborative speech to individual writing, honoring the developmental connection between oral and written language.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A facilitator familiar with short-form improv games and their rules, particularly "Yes, and..."
  • A sequence progressing from oral story-telling games to collaborative story-writing games to individual story-writing

Constraints¶

  • The article's studies used short exposure durations (570 and 180 minutes), and the author notes it is not known if longer exposure would continue to produce growth

Target Learners¶

  • High school students, including regular education and special education populations

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Increase writing fluency (word and sentence usage in journal writing)

Claims¶

  • Approximately 90% of improv instructional time was collaborative, yet students transferred gains to individual writing [+W]
  • Female students showed greater improvement than male students across all categories in both studies [~W]
  • Students with special needs and lower-performing students showed the largest gains in writing fluency after the improv sequence [+W]
  • A sequence of collaborative improv games increased word and sentence usage in high school students' journal writing [+W]
  • After improv exposure, growth in sentence usage exceeded growth in word usage, suggesting 'Yes, and...' was internalized as a writing prompt [+W]
  • Short improv exposure (under 10 hours) produced gains consistent with meta-analytic findings that moderate drama instruction durations are most effective [+W]
