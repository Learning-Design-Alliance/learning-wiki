# Implement CBL with institutional faculty support, clinician facilitators, authentic multimodal cases, preparation incentives and a safe group environment while avoiding behaviour-only assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review offers healthcare educators an implementation strategy: 'Fostering student trust in learning through access to clinician facilitators and authentic cases leads to improved learning outcomes.' It further states that 'Recognition of faculty and student efforts to contribute to CBL is essential for successful implementation' and that 'The facilitation of a safe and secure group learning environment is required for students to meaningfully engage with CBL.' Assessment should not focus solely on behaviour, and teamwork should be fostered to avoid negative group dynamics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional investment in faculty time, training and recognition of contribution
- Facilitators with clinical experience, or non-clinician facilitators with dedicated training and regular meetings to clarify learning objectives
- Preparation resources and advance notice for students
#### Constraints
- Where assessment focuses solely on behaviours and teamwork is not fostered, negative group dynamics may result
- Findings derive from a realist synthesis of 25 studies, largely in-person CBL (n=24) in early-year medical programmes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate health professions students

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance, clinical reasoning, communication skills, participation and teamwork
