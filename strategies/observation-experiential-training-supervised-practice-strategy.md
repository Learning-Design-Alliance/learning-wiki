# Develop novice patient educators through observation of experienced educators, experiential training (roleplay and rehearsal), and supervised practice with constructive critical reflection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Participants recommended that novices gain secondary experience by observing expert educators and rehearsing educational sessions through roleplaying with colleagues as surrogate patients or artificial case scenarios, then conducting education under supervision with constructive critical reflection. Observation was said to increase awareness of "effective communication skills such as using appropriate language". Supervision should prepare novices to prioritize and adjust education to individual patients' needs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A novice educator willing to rehearse and perform under supervision
- Access to experienced educators to observe and to provide feedback
#### Constraints
- Videotaping one's own teaching was considered potentially threatening or uncomfortable for some
- Presence of an experienced educator could result in a passive role for the novice

### Target Learners
- Novice health professional patient educators in cardiac care

### Target Learning Goals
- Patient education and communication skills for coronary heart disease patients
