# Teach teachers six specific autonomy-supportive instructional behaviors via a workshop-plus-practice-plus-peer-discussion program

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The autonomy-supportive intervention program (ASIP) trained PE teachers in six instructional behaviors: "take the students' perspective", vitalize students' autonomy during instruction, "provide explanatory rationales for teacher requests", "use non-pressuring and invitational language", "display patience", and "acknowledge and accept students' expressions of negative affect as okay". Delivery combined a 3-hour morning workshop, a 3-hour afternoon workshop with professionally-created videos of actors modeling each behavior plus scaffolding and corrective feedback, and a 2-hour peer group discussion one month into the semester.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need modeling videos, practice with mentoring and corrective feedback, and a follow-up peer discussion to develop the skill to enact each behavior in their own classroom
#### Constraints
- The intervention targeted autonomy support specifically rather than competence- or relatedness-supportive strategies, and was implemented in Korean secondary PE classes

### Target Learners
- secondary school PE teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- increasing teacher autonomy support to raise student engagement and reduce disengagement
