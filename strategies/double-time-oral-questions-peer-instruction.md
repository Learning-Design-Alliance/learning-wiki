# Allocate at least double the session time and favor oral questions and peer instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
From their implementation experience the authors recommend that a gaming activity of this type needs at least double the hours actually available, to let students become familiar with the games, strengthen the teacher–class bond, and give more space to discussion and peer instruction. They also suggest asking questions orally rather than in writing within an already underway project, to create a climate of active discussion, and reducing the written questions in future offerings. They plan longer time windows or several game sessions in future school visits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longer time windows or the activity divided into several game sessions
#### Constraints
- Two 55-minute school hours shrank further due to room changes and setup; the computer room barely held a class, causing lapses in concentration

### Target Learners
- high-school classes in computer-room settings

### Target Learning Goals
- familiarity with complex-systems dynamics through guided play and discussion
