# Use a short emotional stimulus video before BLS training as a cost-efficient alternative to announced practical assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Before a standardised BLS course, show a roughly 2-minute video depicting a cardiac arrest death and a survivor interview, containing no BLS instruction. The article reports this "cost-efficient and time-efficient alternative" produced performance comparable to an announced practical assessment, and states emotional stimuli "offer the potential of being widely applied" in layperson and professional training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A standardised BLS course follows the stimulus, using the 4-stage approach with equal training time and tutor support across groups
#### Constraints
- The study cannot provide an extensive overview of the influence of emotional activation on learning clinical skills; further research on the detailed psychological effects is stated as necessary

### Target Learners
- first-year medical students
- laypersons learning BLS
- professionals in refresher courses

### Target Learning Goals
- practical CPR skills: adequate compression depth and rate within ERC guidelines
