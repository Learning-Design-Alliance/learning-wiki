# Use a Sierra Leonean demonstrator and consider role-modelling effects when designing training videos for low-resource surgical training programmes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends attending to who demonstrates skills in instructional materials: the training video's operator was "a Sierra Leonean surgeon speaking in English", and the authors suggest his unmasked features "may have led to a role‐modelling effect on the trainees' learning". They call for further assessment of this effect, noting implications for training programmes in low-resource settings that use expatriate trainers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional video with a demonstrator whose identity is visible to trainees
- Further assessment of the role-modelling effect, which the authors state is needed
#### Constraints
- The role-modelling interpretation is an author hypothesis, not a tested result in this study

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees in low-resource settings
- associate clinicians in task-sharing programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of surgical technical skills from instructional video materials
