# Investigate repeated VR exposure to support students without frequent video game experience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation is to test whether repeated exposure to VR can improve learning scores and reduce gender differences for students who do not play video games frequently. They pose this as "a natural question is whether repeated exposure to VR can improve scores and ameliorate gender differences" for infrequent gamers, building on their finding that VR's advantage concentrated among regular video game players and that a slight gender bias against women appeared in VR effectiveness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Repeated VR sessions beyond the single treatment used in the preliminary study
#### Constraints
- The underlying findings are preliminary; the authors note the chances of false positives at the 0.05 level are non-negligible because many correlations were sought

### Target Learners
- students who do not report frequent video game use

### Target Learning Goals
- improving understanding of 3D electrostatics concepts and ameliorating gender differences in gains from VR treatments
