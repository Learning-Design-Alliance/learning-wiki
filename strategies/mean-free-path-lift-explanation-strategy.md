# Use the mean-free-path asymmetry approach to explain air acceleration above a wing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The thesis proposes explaining why air speeds up above a wing through the asymmetry of a particle's mean free path in the wind shadow above the airfoil: a particle above the airfoil is more likely to collide with particles from the left or above than from below, so it accelerates rightward and downward. The author reports making "more positive experiences with the mean free path approach" when students struggled to explain this acceleration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students reasoning about particle collisions rather than viscosity, Coanda effect, or boundary friction
#### Constraints
- Drawn from the author's own 10-year teaching experience rather than a controlled study; the approach also lets students derive that drastic changes in curvature or angle of attack lead to stall

### Target Learners
- students learning aerodynamic lift concepts

### Target Learning Goals
- explaining air acceleration above a wing
- understanding stall at high angles of attack
