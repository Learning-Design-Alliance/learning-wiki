# Prioritize coverage of competencies on non-modifiable causes of obesity to expand obesity education while working against weight stigma

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 unmarked) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that supervised practice programs hoping to expand obesity education and decrease weight stigma prioritize competencies related to non-modifiable causes of obesity, since these currently receive little coverage. The authors write that "improving coverage of these competencies related to non-modifiable causes of obesity may be a place to start", grounding this in attribution-theory-based stigma-reduction evidence. Related recommendations include prioritizing obesity training given finite curriculum room and encouraging practitioners working in obesity to serve as preceptors, with telehealth potentially easing distance barriers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs use the 2017 interprofessional Provider Competencies as a guide for developing obesity education
- Willing preceptors or telehealth-supported observation of RDN patient visits to address rotation-site shortages
#### Constraints
- There is no objective way to rank the importance of obesity-related competencies, so priority-setting under limited curriculum time is difficult per the article
- The 2017 competencies' approach is contested by some directors favoring weight-neutral approaches

### Target Learners
- Entry-level registered dietitian nutritionists in supervised practice programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Obesity prevention and management competencies
- Non-judgmental, non-stigmatizing obesity care
