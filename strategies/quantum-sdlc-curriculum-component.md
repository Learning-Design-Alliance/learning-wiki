# Introduce quantum software engineering and the hybrid classical-quantum SDLC alongside programming frameworks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
As the second step of the approach, the article recommends that instructors introduce a variety of quantum programming languages and frameworks, then "delve deeper into the quantum software engineering and quantum software development lifecycle" the authors explored in prior work. Various frameworks, different lifecycle proposals, and the basic principles of Quantum Software Engineering should be discussed during lectures, since the SDLC of hybrid classical-quantum applications is "a comprehensive and multi-dimensional process" that needs to be explored by instructors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors should introduce the three suggested frameworks (Qiskit, PennyLane, Ocean) plus optionally Microsoft QDK with Q#, Intel Quantum SDK, and Cirq before covering the lifecycle material
#### Constraints
- The lifecycle content draws on the authors' separate survey of quantum software engineering and the SDLC rather than being fully specified in this article

### Target Learners
- M.Sc./Ph.D. students in quantum computer programming and quantum software engineering courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding quantum software engineering principles
- Understanding the software development lifecycle of hybrid classical-quantum applications
