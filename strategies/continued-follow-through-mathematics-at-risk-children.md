# Provide continued follow-through interventions in mathematics for at-risk children after early-intervention effects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's forward-looking recommendation is that early-intervention effects should not be expected to persist without continued support: "The fact that at least some effects last for African-American children 3 years after the end of all interventions support the need for continued follow through interventions in mathematics for at-risk children." The authors argue against interpreting preschool gains' fade-out as a reason to defund preschool, instead treating treatment effects as relative to the subsequent educational experiences children receive. The strategy is to plan and implement follow-through treatment (as TRIAD did in kindergarten and first grade) rather than expecting short-term early interventions to last indefinitely.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Follow-through treatment must be designed and delivered in the years after the initial intervention, as the TRIAD Follow-Through component was in kindergarten and first grade
#### Constraints
- The article notes fade-out interpretations often call for decreased funding and attention to preschool, which the authors reject as mistakenly treating initial effects as independent of future school contexts

### Target Learners
- at-risk children in the elementary school years, especially African-American children

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained mathematics achievement after early intervention
