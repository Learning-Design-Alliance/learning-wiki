# Provide sufficient workplace time to address national guidelines and develop competences, treating this as a supervisor's task

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that elderly care workplaces allocate sufficient time for workers to familiarize themselves with national guidelines and policies, and that competence development be treated as a supervisor's responsibility. It states: "sufficient time to address the guidelines should be provided at workplace and competences developed, which can be seen as a supervisor's task", because with knowledge of the guidelines workers can evaluate and change their working practices. The recommendation rests on the finding that addressing the Act for Elderly Care and the memory policy was associated with higher self-rated QoC.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Workers must know the guidelines before they can evaluate and change working practices, as the article states the guidelines are not useful if workers do not know them
#### Constraints
- The study is cross-sectional, so no cause-and-effect relationship could be determined; associations between guidelines, competences and QoC were weak

### Target Learners
- elderly care workers (practical nurses, registered nurses, supervisors) in institutionalized and non-institutionalized care

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of national elderly care guidelines and policies
- professional competence in areas such as memory-disorder support and falls prevention
