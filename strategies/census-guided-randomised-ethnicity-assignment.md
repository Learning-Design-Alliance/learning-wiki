# Randomise ethnicity assignment across exam questions using local census demographics and physician review to avoid cultural clues

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a concrete procedure for adding ethnicity to exam questions without distorting answers. The demographic composition of London's 2021 Census guided how many questions represented each ethnic group, and "Ethnicities were then randomly assigned to questions to reduce the risk of bias and minimise the introduction of 'cultural clues'". Four physicians reviewed every question to ensure adding ethnicity did not alter the correct answer, repeating randomisation where it did.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to local census demographic data for the assessment population.
- Physician review of each modified item to confirm the correct answer is unchanged.
#### Constraints
- Five of 200 questions (e.g. research methods questions) were deemed unsuitable for ethnicity inclusion and excluded from analysis.

### Target Learners
- final-year undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- authentic formative assessment reflecting diverse patient populations
