# Run a sandwich-case clinical teaching session with opening questions, intragroup discussion, group reporting, teacher summary, feedback, and follow-up questions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a structured clinical teaching session layered on top of conventional breast surgery teaching. It opens by introducing the learning content and randomly grouping interns around a provocative topic question, poses guiding questions, has each intern participate in intragroup discussion where students "boldly expresses academic views and seeks common ground while reserving differences," then moves through group reporting, teacher summary, student feedback, and answering questions with post-class assignments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A lead teacher to design topic questions, summarize, answer doubts, and assign follow-up literature review homework
- Space for interns to sit in randomly formed discussion groups
#### Constraints
- The authors state the method "has higher requirements on teachers and requires a larger learning space for discussion" and "takes slightly longer time than the traditional method" because students adapted slowly to active learning

### Target Learners
- clinical interns in breast surgery rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- independent thinking and problem solving
- communication and cooperation skills
- connecting theoretical knowledge with clinical practice

## Claims

- [Breast surgery interns taught with combined sandwich-case teaching scored higher on theoretical, practical, and comprehensive assessments than interns taught with traditional teaching](../claims/sandwich-case-teaching-higher-assessment-scores-breast-surgery.md) [+W]
