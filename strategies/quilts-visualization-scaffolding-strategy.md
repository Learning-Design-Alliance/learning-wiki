# Develop scaffolded quantum learning tools (QuILTs and peer-instruction) that exploit prior knowledge and computer-based visualization

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' design strategy for advanced quantum mechanics instruction is to build Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs) and peer-instruction tools that account for students' prior knowledge. They write that they are "taking into account these issues and students' prior knowledge to develop Quantum Interactive Learning Tutorials (QuILTs)". The tools "employ computer-based visualization tools" so students can use visual representations of concepts such as the wave function to develop intuition about quantum phenomena.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction must account for students' prior knowledge and skills, exploiting their prior knowledge of probability and mathematical skills, and build on it via scaffolding
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as work in development rather than a tested intervention in this text

### Target Learners
- physics graduate students
- advanced undergraduate students in quantum mechanics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- developing intuition about quantum phenomena
- qualitative understanding of the wave function
- bridging quantitative and conceptual aspects of quantum mechanics
