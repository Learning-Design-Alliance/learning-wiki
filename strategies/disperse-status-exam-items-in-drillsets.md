# Disperse independent status-exam questions within drillsets to measure general mathematics ability during drill practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors embedded an 18-question status exam inside every secondary school drillset so that learning on items outside the drilled material could be measured: "with each requested drill item, the student will receive a status exam question with some positive probability." This turns routine drill allocations into an independent measurement stream, enabling analysis of whether practising mathematics raises general ability on unseen problems rather than only the drilled methods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Drill items generated in large numbers with random numbers to avoid direct repetition
- A grade based on a weighted average of recent answers so students can self-monitor
- Care in interpreting repeated sightings, dropouts and abuse
#### Constraints
- Status exam questions may be seen multiple times by the same student across drillsets
- Some students give up before completing all drillsets
- Abusers answering every question correctly bias estimates downwards

### Target Learners
- secondary school students practising mathematics in self-learning drill systems

### Target Learning Goals
- general mathematics ability on unseen problems, beyond item-specific drill mastery
