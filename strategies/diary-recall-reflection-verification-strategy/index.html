Diary recording with recall, reflection and delayed verification as a self-directed learning strategy¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The learner keeps a daily journal of communication problems and dubious utterances, then recalls and verifies them with native speakers when the chance arises, as when he memorized "things are eased out now" from a phone call and consulted other native speakers afterwards. Recording and reflecting daily "have provided me with a strong impetus for identifying and overcoming gaps in my communicative competence". The strategy converts tolerated inaccuracies into noticed gaps and later negative input.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to native speakers for later verification of stored dubious utterances.

Constraints¶

  • Suited to advanced adult learners in an authentic target-language environment; the article frames it for self-directed learning situations.

Target Learners¶

  • adult advanced second language learners
  • self-directed learners

  • identifying and overcoming gaps in communicative competence
  • noticing and correcting linguistic and sociolinguistic errors
