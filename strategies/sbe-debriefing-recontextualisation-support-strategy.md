# Design SBE with strong debriefing and explicit support for recontextualising knowledge across sites, and enact shared values collaboratively between educators and clinical nurses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that SBE build in, assess and use teaching strategies to develop students' reworking of knowledge as they transition between learning sites — academia, clinical placements, SBE, groupwork and assessor relationships. It states that "giving strong emphasis to debriefing sessions and supporting students to recontextualise knowledge gained in either university or placement settings will tend to decrease the theory‐practice split". At system level, espoused shared values on the curriculum between nurse education and practice need to be enacted collaboratively.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- SBE must remain pedagogically, not technologically, led, with philosophical paradigms or pedagogical models matching the method to desired learning outcomes
#### Constraints
- The article notes SBE is not a pedagogy but a method of instruction, and that opportunities in SBE are not evidence that students rework knowledge in practice

### Target Learners
- undergraduate student nurses in professional preparation programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- recontextualising knowledge across learning sites
- reducing the theory-practice split
- reducing anxiety about placements
