Deliver a critical thinking module through three sequenced instructional strategies ending in applied reflection¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes a module delivered as three instructional strategies: a context-free introduction to critical thinking focusing; a mixed method teaching the thinking tools while students' own context-free problems are discussed against the tools for feedback; and a third strategy engaging students in "a challenging cognitive situation" by applying critical thinking in teaching and learning. Reflection is used when students construct and apply their own critical thinking to identified cognitive situations. This sequence moved students from tool instruction to situated application in health talks.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must already have theory of health education and clinical exposure, as the year-2 participants had, before the module treatment

Constraints¶

  • The article reports the impact only for year-2 nursing students in one private medical college in Northern Peninsular Malaysia

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate nursing students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • applying critical thinking tools to deliver patient-centred health education

Claims¶

  • A critical thinking module produced significantly higher post-test achievement in health education than control among Malaysian nursing students [+W]
  • Student nurses applied the seven CoRT critical thinking tools when delivering health talks to patients [+W]
  • Students reported their thinking had improved after induction to the critical thinking module [+W]
  • Experiment and control groups did not differ significantly on pre-test scores before the critical thinking treatment [+W]
  • No significant interaction of treatment and pre-test on participants' achievement in the critical thinking module study [+W]
  • Early introduction of a scientific video appeared to constrain students' representations, which they applied without adjusting for new contexts [~W]
