# Application day: students discuss several prepared published applications of each technique, choosing the discussion order

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In each module the final lecture day is devoted to examining how the technique was used in publications. The instructor searches journal collections across disciplines, summarizes the research, and pulls out graphs, methods, and results for discussion: "I always prepared several articles (minimum four, usually five, maximum seven) for each course topic, with about equal depth on each, and let the students choose the order we considered them for discussion." Students walk through the analyses, ask why procedures were performed, and suggest improvements, learning to critique statistical work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor time to prepare several summarized articles per topic at roughly equal depth
- Articles drawn from different disciplines relevant to student interests
#### Constraints
- The application lecture day took a lot of time to prepare, and it was a challenge to summarize enough background for the application to interest students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate statistics students without prior introductory statistics coursework

### Target Learning Goals
- recognizing appropriate and inappropriate applications of techniques and critiquing statistical work in published research

## Claims

- [Most students reported the per-module application day examining published articles was beneficial, while a minority did not find it helpful](../claims/application-day-beneficial-majority.md) [+W]
