# Peer review of AI-assisted writing with hallucination-flagging to build awareness of fabricated content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 9 studies (4 qualitative, 3 review, 1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 9 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Midway through the semester, students write a 2-4 page ACS-style mini review on how AI could support research in their subfield; each paper is reviewed by a classmate using a structured rubric and an AI-generated-text detection tool. "Peer reviewers were asked to comment only on flagged sections where hallucinations appear" (e.g., fabricated references, incorrect numbers and facts). No penalty is given for likely AI-generated content; the activity is designed to build awareness of hallucinated content and foster ethical writing practices. Students reported that seeing peers' approaches helped them reflect on responsible AI use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured peer-review rubric and an AI-generated-text detection tool
#### Constraints
- No penalty is given if some content can likely be AI-generated; the goal is awareness, not policing

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate chemistry students using AI tools in scientific writing

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluate AI-generated content critically and practice responsible AI use in scientific communication
