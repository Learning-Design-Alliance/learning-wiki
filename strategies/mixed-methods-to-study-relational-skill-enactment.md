# Use mixed-methods approaches, including ethnography and assessor interviews, to study how relational skills are enacted and valued in practice assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
To build on the finding that written assessment documentation underrepresents relational skills, the authors recommend future research combining methods that reach beyond documentation. They suggest "integrating ethnography or interviews with practice assessors would allow exploration of implicit biases, contextual influences, and the 'how' of relational skill enactment", and propose comparative studies across institutions to distinguish broader trends from local practices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to practice assessors and assessment settings for ethnographic observation or interviewing, beyond documentary data alone
#### Constraints
- The single-site design of the current study limits generalisability to national or global contexts, per the authors

### Target Learners
- student nurses on clinical practice placements

### Target Learning Goals
- relational skill enactment in clinical practice
- fair and holistic competency assessment
