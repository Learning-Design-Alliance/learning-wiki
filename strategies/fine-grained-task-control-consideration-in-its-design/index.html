Consider students' perceived control over fine-grained task facets (technology, pedagogy, content) when designing ITS learning experiences¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that topic-related self-efficacy showed no relation to task emotions in the ITS, the article recommends that designers attend to momentary, task-level control rather than only general competence beliefs. The authors state this "calls for more fine-grained considerations of students’ perceived control over the various facets of the task", which "may include technology-related features (i.e., navigational properties, amount of accessible resources), pedagogy-related features (degree of structuration of the learning support, clarity of learning goals), and content-related features (i.e., causal maps, clarity of the knowledge content)". This strategy applies to designing open-ended ITS learning experiences.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The ITS task facets whose control learners perceive must be identifiable, including navigational properties, learning support structure, and knowledge content clarity

Constraints¶

  • The article notes ITS ill-defined goals are known to place high demands on cognition and self-regulation, so perceived topic-level control may show weaker relations with emotions than task demands and coping ability

Target Learners¶

  • Secondary school students using intelligent tutoring systems

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Supporting positive emotional experience (enjoyment, low boredom) during autonomous digital science learning

  • Topic-related interest, but not importance, utility, or self-efficacy, predicted task-related enjoyment while secondary students learned with an ITS [+W]
