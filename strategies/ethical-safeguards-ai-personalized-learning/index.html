Address core ethical challenges—data privacy, algorithmic fairness, and transparency—when deploying AI-driven personalized learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 associational), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review identifies three central ethical challenges for AI-driven personalized learning: data privacy and security (requiring "robust data protection, anonymization, and clear consent protocols"), algorithmic fairness and bias (mitigated through "regular bias audits, diverse datasets, and fairness-aware algorithms"), and transparency and explainability (explainable AI models and transparent data practices to ensure accountability and foster trust). It argues addressing these requires technical safeguards, robust governance, and a human-centered approach so innovation benefits all learners equitably.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Robust data protection, anonymization, and clear consent protocols
  • Regular bias audits, diverse datasets, and fairness-aware algorithms
  • Explainable AI models and transparent data practices

Constraints¶

  • Many AI systems operate as black boxes, making it difficult for educators and students to understand or challenge decisions

Target Learners¶

  • All learners in AI-driven personalized learning systems, with attention to marginalized groups

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Equitable benefit from AI-driven personalized learning

Claims¶

  • Educator- and institutional-level challenges include curricular misalignment, need for teacher mediation, unequal access, privacy concerns, academic integrity risks, and algorithmic bias [+M]
  • Review reports ethical concerns of educational AI including algorithmic bias and exploitation of personal data [+W]
  • Institutional trust, data-collection concern, and comfort with instructor data use are the key factors in students' decisions to consent to learning analytics [~W]
