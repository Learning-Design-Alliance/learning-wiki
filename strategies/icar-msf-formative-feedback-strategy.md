# Use the ICAR within a multi-source feedback process to provide formative feedback to residents on collaborator competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating the modified ICAR into a multi-source feedback process as formative assessment: raters from multiple professions complete the rubric during a rotation, and residents receive a summary package of quantitative scores and qualitative comments. The authors conclude the approach "could be a feasible and reliable assessment approach to providing formative feedback to post-graduate medical residents on collaborator competencies". Qualitative comments let learners see how their collaborative behaviour affected the team and reflect on it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Raters from at least two professional groups must complete multiple assessments per resident during an observed rotation; residents receive a feedback summary to review and reflect on
#### Constraints
- Best suited to formative rather than summative use; the study did not recruit patient raters and was limited to a single institution

### Target Learners
- post-graduate medical residents on clinical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- interprofessional collaboration and team functioning

- [The modified 17-item ICAR showed high internal consistency overall (α = .981) and across all six domains (α = .881–.963)](../claims/icar-high-internal-consistency.md) [+W]

- [Using the modified ICAR in an MSF process significantly reduced missing assessment data compared with daily assessments (8.8% vs 13.1%)](../claims/icar-msf-reduced-missing-data.md) [+W]
- [Raters who interacted with residents less than once per shift scored Collaboration-domain items lower, with a significant interaction-frequency-by-item effect](../claims/interaction-frequency-item-effect-icar.md) [~W]
