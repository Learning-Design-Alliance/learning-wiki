# Sustain trainer motivation through financial incentives, chosen partners, institutional recognition and dedicated scheduling time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' discussion recommends organizational measures to sustain trainers' motivation to deliver PFA-O sessions: financial compensation, freedom to choose and coordinate with one's training partner, institutional recognition, and time dedicated to training in schedules. They note that "the ability to choose one's partner emerged as a major motivating factor in our study (mentioned by 35.71% of respondents)", a point they say is probably neglected too often by coordinators who organize sessions by trainer availability alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training coordination must permit trainers to choose their partner and coordinate with them before the session
#### Constraints
- Findings come from volunteer military caregivers in a well-resourced institutional setting and may not generalize to other populations

### Target Learners
- PFA-O trainers (nurses, psychologists, psychiatrists) and the training coordinators who organize their sessions

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained trainer motivation and continued delivery of introductory PFA-O simulation sessions
