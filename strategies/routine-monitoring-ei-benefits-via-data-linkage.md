# Use updated administrative data linkages for routine monitoring of early intervention program benefits as service contexts change

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that jurisdictions maintain and update linked administrative data systems to track whether EI benefits persist as programs and populations evolve. It states that "Updated data linkages can enable routine monitoring of the benefits of EI programming as contexts change," noting EI programs have evolved over two decades and that universal prekindergarten and 3-year-old programs now provide other forms of early enrichment. This strategy treats population-level data linkage as an ongoing evaluation infrastructure rather than a one-time study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Linked public health and educational administrative data systems; probabilistic data linkage capacity
#### Constraints
- EI programs and the urban populations accessing them have changed over the last two decades; universal prekindergarten programs provide other enrichment forms that complicate attribution

### Target Learners
- Children with developmental delays or disabilities in large urban public school systems

### Target Learning Goals
- Monitoring long-term academic outcomes of early intervention programming
