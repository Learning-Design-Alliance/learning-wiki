Implement simulation-based education with targeted interventions, high-intensity low-dose high-frequency training, and strong integration and support¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (1 for, 3 mixed, 3 against) · 2 studies (2 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's implications for practice identify three shared features of effective and feasible SBE programs: "targeted interventions that address specific, high-impact clinical areas with clearly defined, measurable outcomes"; "high-intensity training, such as low-dose, high-frequency models, to ensure skill acquisition and retention"; and "strong integration and support, including leadership engagement and strategies to address and overcome real-world barriers to implementation". Practitioners should enable success through well-structured, context-sensitive strategies rather than broad claims.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Leadership engagement and strategies to overcome real-world implementation barriers
  • Clearly defined, measurable outcomes tied to high-impact clinical areas

Constraints¶

  • The review cautions the impact of SBE on patient outcomes is not guaranteed and is influenced by multiple factors

Target Learners¶

  • healthcare providers and trainees in pediatric and neonatal critical care settings

  • technical and nontechnical resuscitation and procedural skills with retention

Claims¶

  • Frequent brief on-site Helping Babies Breathe simulation training was associated with a significant decrease in 24-hour neonatal mortality in a Tanzanian before–after study [+W]
  • All included simulation-based research studies were judged to have high or critical risk of bias, and only 16 of 2481 screened studies met Level 4 inclusion criteria [-W]
  • The review suggests current observational evidence may overestimate simulation's impact, since more rigorous cluster-RCTs showed only time-limited mortality reductions or nonsignificant results [-W]
  • In 18% (3/16) of included simulation studies, positive clinical trends did not reach statistical significance [-W]
  • Simulation-based education studies in pediatric and neonatal critical care more consistently report system-level process improvements (Level 4a) than significant direct patient-level outcome improvements (Level 4b) [~W]
  • Single-center simulation studies were more likely to demonstrate significant patient-outcome improvements than multicenter studies [~W]
  • Contextual resource constraints in low- and middle-income settings may limit implementation of structured, feedback-intensive clinical teaching models [~W]
