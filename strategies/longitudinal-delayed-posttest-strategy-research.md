# Use longitudinal designs with delayed posttests when evaluating communication strategy instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' central methodological recommendation is that strategy-training studies extend the instructional period and add delayed posttests, so declarative knowledge of strategies can automatize into procedural knowledge. They state: "we wish to reiterate our recommendation that future studies take a more longitudinal approach," noting that a delayed posttest would show whether learners can produce set phrases extemporaneously, and that prior studies (Lam, Naughton, Nakatani) ran longer than this three-week study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional period long enough for repeated practice; data collection delayed relative to treatment
#### Constraints
- If the study is delayed so learners acclimate to a co-constructed setting, care is needed to minimize teacher effect, e.g., using instructors other than the learners' own

### Target Learners
- first-year university EFL students

### Target Learning Goals
- proceduralization of communication strategy use in discussion
