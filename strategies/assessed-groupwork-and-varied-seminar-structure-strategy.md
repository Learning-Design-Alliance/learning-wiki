# Structure future flipped courses with assessed group-work elements and varied feedback sessions instead of mandating attendance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on lessons learned, the authors recommend keeping learning activities non-mandatory but restructuring them: an assessed element at the end of group-work contributing to the final exam could be implemented, and students could anonymously assess other group-members' contributions. They also propose increasing variation in feedback sessions, for example "facilitator-led discussions and poster-presentations", and breaking sessions into different periods of the day or extending them over a week, citing research that learning is better achieved when spaced out over time in smaller chunks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educator time to design assessed group-work elements and varied session formats
- Digital infrastructure for shared documents and potentially interactive or social-media activities
#### Constraints
- The authors state educators with little previous experience or training in active learning may struggle to find an optimal level of activity

### Target Learners
- Higher education students in professional health programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- Pre-class preparation and accountability
- Sustained engagement across long seminars
- Higher-order learning through varied feedback formats
