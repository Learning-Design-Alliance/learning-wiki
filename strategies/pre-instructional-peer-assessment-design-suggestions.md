# Four practical suggestions for designing and implementing technology-enhanced pre-instructional peer assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on the findings, the author proposes a set of suggestions for designing and implementing technology-enhanced pre-instructional peer assessment: 'a training should be provided to students so that they can provide and manage feedback as well as take action upon it effectively'; discussions with students about assessment criteria, possibly inviting students to develop the criteria; monitoring students' attitude toward the grading strategy to judge its suitability; and using the assignment's content and context as a link to the main body of the lesson.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor time to provide feedback training, discuss assessment criteria, and monitor student attitudes toward the grading strategy
#### Constraints
- The suggestions derive from an exploratory descriptive study in one Statistical Methods course; the author notes future studies with a larger sample and a longer period are needed to verify the evidence

### Target Learners
- higher education students
- prospective mathematics teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- preparing students for new content acquisition
- providing and acting upon peer feedback effectively
