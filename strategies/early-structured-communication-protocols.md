# Introduce structured communication protocols (I-PASS, SBAR) early as foundational patient-safety skills rather than advanced competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study identifies the lower frequency of references to safe communication protocols (n = 42) as an opportunity to "intentionally reinforce this area in undergraduate training." The authors recommend "positioning them as foundational skills to be introduced early" to "foster a stronger culture of patient safety," citing evidence that structured tools such as I-PASS and SBAR reduce failures in information transfer.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty training in structured communication tools
#### Constraints
- Their integration has often been limited and considered an advanced competency

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe communication protocols for patient safety and information transfer
