# Integrate a sensory meditation and a contemplative videography into undergraduate physics courses to draw students' attention to their embodied experience of physical phenomena

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article presents two classroom practices: a guided somatic sensory meditation in which students 'direct their attention int o and through their bodies, gradually expanding their awareness into the surrounding space,' and a contemplative videography in which students select one classical physics concept, film its manifestations for a week, and compile a short film. Both are followed by short written reflections graded only on completion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students complete at least one practice within an existing physics course and submit a one-to-two-page written reflection afterwards
#### Constraints
- The practices were studied in calculus-based classical mechanics and electrodynamics courses aimed primarily at physical sciences, engineering, and mathematics majors, all taught by the first author

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in calculus-based mechanics and electrodynamics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting formal physics principles to personal, embodied experience
- heightened sensory awareness and observation of physical phenomena
