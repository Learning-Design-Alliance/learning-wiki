# Brief in-school self-affirmation writing exercises administered before major assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (4 for, 2 mixed, 3 against) · 4 studies (4 causal), `q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
Students receive a personalized list of things that could be important to them (e.g., Friends and Family, Music, Religion), select the two or three most important, and write a brief essay explaining why those things matter to them. Exercises take about 15-20 minutes, are distributed by classroom teachers, completed quietly and independently during the school day, and are timed to precede major assessments. Comparison students write about how unimportant things might matter to someone else.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Personalized copies with the student's name on a cover sheet to maintain the fidelity of random assignment
- Teachers not informed of the condition to which their students were assigned
#### Constraints
- The district-wide scale-up found no overall average effect on state test scores and no closing of the Black-White gap, so effectiveness on high-stakes tests is not supported by this replication

### Target Learners
- seventh-grade students in a public school district

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing stereotype threat and closing racial and gender achievement gaps
