# Design interventions at teacher and test levels to reduce content bias in math tests

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because excluding money, food, and social-interaction content from tests is "neither desirable nor feasible", and monetary understanding is a crucial learning goal, the authors propose "two possible levels for interventions: a) teacher level (e.g., giving teacherstools to provide additional guidance to students during test taking) and b) test level (e.g., removal of speciﬁcc o ntent that may be particularly triggering, or adding speci ﬁc instructions when using content that can produce bias)". These interventions aim to make math tests fairer and enhance social equality in education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Developing and applying bias-reducing interventions; the authors note simply excluding triggering content is neither desirable nor feasible given monitoring-study practice and the educational value of money content.
#### Constraints
- The article states the interventions are a proposed promising avenue; their effectiveness has not been tested in this study.

### Target Learners
- low-SES elementary and middle-school students taking standardized math tests

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate assessment of mathematics ability regardless of socioeconomic background
