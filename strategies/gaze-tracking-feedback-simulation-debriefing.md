# Use gaze tracking as a feedback tool to alert students and instructors to key moments in simulation learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends leveraging the eye-mind link so that "simulation-based learning environments could integrate gaze tracking as a feedback tool to assess attention, cognitive workload, or uncertainty in decision-making". Gaze cues could guide students to moments where they might employ self-regulation strategies, either as guides to post-action debriefing or as on-line interventions applied while students are in-situ. The guided reflection used in this study, in which students reviewed and segmented their own gaze footage, was designed as a complementary activity grounded in evidence-based debriefing practices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mobile eye-tracking equipment and a reflective review process in which students view their own gaze footage
#### Constraints
- The article notes the study could not collect objective instructor ratings without interfering with the simulation process, and recommends testing links between gaze and more objective performance measures in future research

### Target Learners
- nursing students in clinical simulation education

### Target Learning Goals
- self-regulation
- reflection on performance
- self-efficacy
- situational awareness
