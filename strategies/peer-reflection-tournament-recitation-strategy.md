# Run recitations as a timed peer-reflection tournament in which teams select and defend the best homework solution

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's PR recitation format had randomly generated teams of three to six students compare their homework solutions each week to identify the "best" solution, with winning students advancing through rounds to three finalists whose solutions were critiqued by a TA/UTA panel before a class-wide clicker vote. Teams whose member advanced earned bonus course credit, with consolation prize winners earning "one third of the course credit that was awarded to the winner". Each of three competitions was timed to roughly 15 minutes within a fifty-minute recitation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Two UTAs plus a TA per recitation; randomly assigned teams regenerated weekly by a computer program; coordinated quiz problems for the comparison group; bonus-credit reward system
#### Constraints
- No recitation quiz was given to the PR group due to time constraints; recitation scoring differed between groups, so groups were curved separately

### Target Learners
- introductory algebra-based physics students in recitation sections

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating and adopting effective problem solving strategies such as drawing diagrams, defining symbols consistently, and justifying physics principles before writing equations
