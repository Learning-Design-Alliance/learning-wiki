# Send SMS exercises in the afternoon or evening, two or three per week

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A scheduling strategy for SMS-based learning support derived from students' stated timing preferences. The article reports that "future messages should be sent between one and eight p.m." and that students "all preferred receiving them in the afternoon or in the evening", with two or three messages per week considered a good quantity. This keeps messages from arriving during busy class-time hours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners' timetables should be surveyed before fixing a sending window
#### Constraints
- Based on preferences of a small group of 13 university students; the authors had expected students to resist academic messages outside faculty hours

### Target Learners
- university students with busy timetables

### Target Learning Goals
- regular distributed practice via mobile messages
