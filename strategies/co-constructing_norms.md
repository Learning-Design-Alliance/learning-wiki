# Co Constructing Norms

## Description
Co constructing norms is a strategy in which the instructor facilitates a structured discussion through which learners propose, refine, and agree upon the norms that will govern their shared work — covering behavior, participation, discussion etiquette, and how the group handles disagreement. The resulting agreements are documented, revisited, and enforced (or renegotiated) by the community rather than imposed unilaterally.

## Design Implications

Co constructed norms increase learner ownership and buy-in, which supports engagement and willingness to take intellectual risks [Autonomy-supportive environments strengthen intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]. The process also functions as a belonging intervention: learners who help author the community's rules are more likely to experience the environment as theirs, which is especially consequential for students from groups at risk of feeling marginal [Belonging interventions improve outcomes for at-risk students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. Norms must be specific and behavioral — "build on others' ideas before disagreeing" — rather than generic values ("be respectful"), or they cannot be enacted or monitored.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured protocol for generating and consolidating proposals (e.g., think-pair-share, sticky-note brainstorm, small-group drafting) so all voices contribute, not just the most confident ([Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md))
- Instructor willingness to be bound by the norms, including norms that constrain instructor behavior
- A visible, persistent artifact (poster, syllabus addendum, shared document) that norms can be referenced against
- Periodic revisiting — norms decay if never reactivated ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md))

#### Constraints
- Co construction without instructor framing produces vague or unworkable norms; facilitation must push toward specificity and testability [-W]
- If norms are never referenced or enforced after the initial session, the exercise reads as performative and can undermine trust [-M]
- Time cost is real: a meaningful norming session takes 30–60 minutes, which competes with content coverage in tightly scheduled courses [~W]
- In large or short-duration settings (single-session workshops, lecture courses), full co construction is often impractical; abbreviated versions (proposing discussion agreements for one activity) are more realistic

#### Implementation Variability
- **Full syllabus co construction** — learners help set policies on deadlines, device use, and participation (used in democratic and open education settings)
- **Discussion agreements** — norms scoped to seminar or [Collaborative Learning](../patterns/collaborative-learning.md) talk moves (e.g., "challenge ideas, not people")
- **Team charters** — norms co constructed within small groups for project work, including how the group handles non-contribution
- **Renegotiation checkpoints** — norms revisited at midterm or after group conflicts, treating them as living agreements

### Target Learners
- K–12 classrooms establishing community at the start of a year, where ownership effects are strongest [Autonomy-supportive environments strengthen intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Adult and professional cohorts where learners bring relevant experience about what makes collaboration work
- Mixed or diverse cohorts where explicit norms reduce ambiguity about participation expectations [Belonging interventions improve outcomes for at-risk students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Less suited to very young learners without scaffolding for abstract behavioral language, or to one-off sessions with no ongoing community to govern

### Target Learning Goals
- Community and climate: establishing psychological safety for discussion, critique, and risk-taking
- Self-regulation: giving learners shared language for monitoring their own and peers' behavior
- Collaborative competence: making the implicit rules of productive group work explicit and negotiable

### Instructions
1. **Frame the purpose.** Explain why norms exist and that the community will author them together; the instructor retains veto power for safety and equity but shares genuine authority over the rest.
2. **Generate individually or in pairs.** Learners propose norms from experience — "what helps you learn in a group? what shuts you down?" — before whole-group sharing, to widen participation.
3. **Consolidate and sharpen.** Facilitate merging duplicates and rewriting vague proposals into specific, observable statements; test each with "what would this look like in practice?"
4. **Document visibly.** Record the agreed norms in a persistent artifact all members can reference.
5. **Rehearse and reference.** Apply the norms immediately in a first activity; when a norm is violated, refer to the artifact rather than issuing ad hoc corrections.
6. **Revisit and revise.** Schedule checkpoints (e.g., midterm) where the community reviews whether norms are working and amends them — modeling that agreements are living commitments.

## Examples
- **Responsive Classroom (Center for Responsive Schools)** — the "Hopes and Dreams → Classroom Rules" sequence, in which K–8 students' goals drive teacher-facilitated co construction of rules ([https://www.responsivereads.com](https://www.responsivereads.com))
- **PBLWorks (Buck Institute) project classrooms** — co constructed "norms for collaboration" posted and referenced during team project work ([https://www.pblworks.org](https://www.pblworks.org))
- **University seminar norming** — first-week discussion agreements ("disagree with ideas, cite evidence, step up/step back") revisited at midterm in writing-intensive courses
