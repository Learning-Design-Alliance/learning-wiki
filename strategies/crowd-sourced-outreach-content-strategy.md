# Crowd-sourcing content from a whole research group distributes workload and sustains an outreach account over time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The strategy distributes content creation across many group members rather than concentrating it in a communications office. The article states that crowd-sourcing "helps distribute the workload more evenly among the whole CMS group", enabling the small organizing team to keep up its scientific duties while volunteers contribute recent results or "life at the lab" content. This approach is also credited with the project's long-term sustainability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Volunteers from within the group willing to contribute content, and a small organizing team that finds volunteers, collaborates on production, maintains the schedule, and manages the account
#### Constraints
- The three organizing team members work full time in scientific activities, so the strategy depends on outreach remaining a manageable side commitment

### Target Learners
- early career researchers in large research groups
- the science-interested general public

### Target Learning Goals
- science communication skills for contributing scientists
- public awareness of incremental day-to-day research
