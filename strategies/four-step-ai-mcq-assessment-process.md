# Four-step process for applying AI models to MCQ assessment in a new medical specialty

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a transferable workflow for using LLMs to evaluate MCQ quality in disciplines beyond orthopedics. It recommends that "validated, specialty-specific MCQs should be gathered from the target discipline", then entered into selected AI models with responses systematically recorded and evaluated for accuracy, followed by independent expert assessment of LLM answers with consensus resolution of discrepancies, and finally refinement of poorly performing or ambiguous items for clarity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Validated, specialty-specific MCQs from the target discipline
- At least two faculty experts independently assessing LLM-generated answers with consensus review of discrepancies
#### Constraints
- The authors state reevaluation by teachers or assessors remains essential to ensure responses are clinically relevant and summative assessment results accurate

### Target Learners
- Medical students in orthopedic rotations
- Medical educators and assessors

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate and reliable assessment of medical knowledge and clinical reasoning via MCQs
