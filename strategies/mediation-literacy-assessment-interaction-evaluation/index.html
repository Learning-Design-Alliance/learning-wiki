Teach mediation literacy and redesign assessment and evaluation to measure interaction, not only satisfaction¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Beyond generic AI literacy, the article argues "Education-abroad students need a further capability: recognising what part of an encounter AI is performing, whether the task is administrative or educational, how much interpretive agency is being transferred". Assessment should examine the interpretive process — comparison of initial and revised interpretations, analysis of AI-generated cultural explanations, evidence gathered through direct interaction — with disclosure of how mediation occurred. Programme evaluation should record whether AI use changed the frequency and quality of host-community interaction rather than treating satisfaction as the principal measure.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Pre-departure orientation should ask students to classify realistic scenarios by friction type and mediation level; programmes should publish human escalation routes for safety, compliance and distress matters

Constraints¶

  • AI should not be treated as authoritative for visa or immigration compliance, medical emergencies, personal safety, discrimination reporting, legal obligations or acute psychological distress

Target Learners¶

  • education-abroad students
  • faculty and cultural mentors
  • programme evaluators

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mediation literacy
  • reflective judgement
  • tolerance for ambiguity
  • host-community interaction quality

Related Strategies¶

  • Prepare-participate-reflect sequence with a mediation log for AI use in education abroad
  • Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection
