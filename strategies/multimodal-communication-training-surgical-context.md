# Combine lectures, role-playing, simulations, peer feedback, and briefing/debriefing tailored to the surgical context to improve communication skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article identifies a combination of teaching methods as effective for communication skills training in surgery: "lectures, role-playing, simulations, peer feedback, and briefing and debriefing tailored to surgical context." Survey respondents' own preferences aligned with this multimodal approach, with 73% advocating for involvement of professionals specifically trained in communication, 34.9% suggesting role-playing exercises with real-world scenarios, 24.9% webinars, and 19.6% methodical lessons on communication.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Feedback from instructors and simulated patients in a safe setting
- Involvement of professionals specifically trained in communication (endorsed by 73% of respondents)
#### Constraints
- Tailoring these strategies to Italian surgery schools would require institutional support, national curriculum integration, and faculty training in communication pedagogy

### Target Learners
- surgical residents
- early-career surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- communication skills for difficult conversations
- emotional competence in patient interactions
