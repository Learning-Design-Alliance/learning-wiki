# Incorporate self-directed, encounter-driven topics and online sources into traditional didactic schedules under educator direction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that residency programs blend self-learning activities with traditional didactics. It states that "self-learning activities, under educator direction, may be ideal for covering topics that the residents themselves find important or that relate to their recent patient encounters," and that educators should "consider incorporating the use of online sources into our traditional educational environments." This follows from the survey findings that 80% of residents choose topics from recent clinical encounters and prefer podcasts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educator direction to guide topic selection and critical appraisal of resources
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that a delicate balance exists between learner-preferred modalities and the modalities seasoned educators believe most important, and that more research is needed to identify the optimal balance

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical knowledge acquisition relevant to recent patient encounters
- Critical appraisal of online educational resources
