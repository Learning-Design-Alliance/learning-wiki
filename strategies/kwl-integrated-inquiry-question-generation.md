# Embed the KWL strategy across inquiry phases to guide question generation and question-quality monitoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy integrates the Know–Want–Learn chart into the phases of inquiry-based learning so that questioning is scaffolded throughout the lesson. As the article states, "Teachers use the KWL strategy in instruction to guide students in analyzing and reasoning to generate questions, as well as monitoring and evaluating the quality of their questions". At orientation, students review prior knowledge (K) and generate specific, investigable questions (W) from teacher-provided everyday contexts; they then investigate by expanding questions through a cycle of question, answer, follow-up question, and re-question, and record what they learned (L) at conclusion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher guidance to help students conceptualize vague curiosity into specific, investigable scientific questions
- Initial question contexts drawn from scientific concepts close to students’ everyday experiences
#### Constraints
- The strategy was implemented within problem-based inquiry lessons following regular lessons on the same content

### Target Learners
- Primary school students in science classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- Generating investigable scientific questions and monitoring and evaluating question quality
