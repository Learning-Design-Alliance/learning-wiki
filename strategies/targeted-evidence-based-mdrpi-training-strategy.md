# Provide targeted, evidence‐based MDRPI training programmes plus periodic knowledge tests and feedback to correct misinformation and raise nurses' knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that nurses receive education on MDRPI and that organisations run "updated, evidence‐based training programmes that emphasise correct dressing selection and safe device fixation techniques in line with current clinical guidelines." Because 82% of participating nurses reported no prior MDRPI training and several items revealed misinformation (e.g., only 19.4% correctly answered that devices should not be tightly fixed), the strategy targets both knowledge gaps and wrong beliefs. The authors add that "periodic knowledge tests and feedback studies" should evaluate education's effectiveness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training content should align with the international clinical practice guideline from NPIAP, EPUAP, and PPPIA, which the article calls a recognised standard for prevention and treatment.
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted in a single hospital and most participants had not received prior training, which the authors state reduces generalisability of the findings motivating this strategy.

### Target Learners
- Practising nurses in surgical, internal, and intensive care clinics

### Target Learning Goals
- Correcting misinformation and improving knowledge of MDRPI prevention, dressing selection, device fixation, skin inspection, risk assessment, and staging
