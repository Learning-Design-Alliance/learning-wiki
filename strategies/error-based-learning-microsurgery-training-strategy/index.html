Use error-based learning and standardized error classification to accelerate microsurgical skill acquisition¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends structuring microsurgical training around systematic identification, measurement, and correction of errors, using standardized error classification across all operative phases. It states: "Standardized error classification may enhance training programs, accelerating the acquisition of microsurgical skills along the learning curve and improving clinical outcomes." It also calls for a comprehensive, large-scale scoring system covering every phase from preparation to final evaluation.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Error types must be clearly defined and quantified; assessment tools such as ALI or MARS10 should be used to structure feedback

Constraints¶

  • The review notes current errors are highly heterogeneous, which made developing standardized rubrics challenging; a non-validated risk of bias tool was used

Target Learners¶

  • microsurgery trainees
  • surgical residents entering microsurgical practice

Target Learning Goals¶

  • acquisition of microsurgical anastomosis skills through error recognition and correction

Claims¶

  • Backwall catches are the most frequent needle-passage error, described in 21 of the reviewed articles [+W]
  • Back-wall stitches, uneven lumens, and excessive suture tension are consistently associated with reduced anastomotic patency [+W]
  • Intra-operative errors are the most frequently reported microsurgical errors across studies [+W]
  • Pre-operative errors stem largely from insufficient preparation, including setup issues and cognitive distractions that affect accuracy [+W]
  • Severe torsion may reduce patency, but limited torsion (≤180°) often shows no clear early patency penalty, while excessive tension is more consistently associated with failure [+W]
