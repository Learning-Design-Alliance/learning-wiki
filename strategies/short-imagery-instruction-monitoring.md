# Use a short visual-imagery strategy instruction to strengthen diagnostic recollection-based cues before paired-associate learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's training consisted of a standardized, brief instruction in visual imagery: the administrator explained "the advantages of memorizing word pairs as one interconnected image", demonstrated with two drawings, and gave practice with feedback on ten word pairs. Because recollection-based cues are highly diagnostic of later memory performance, strengthening their accessibility via imagery improves both recall and the quality of monitoring judgments (JOLs and CJs), especially in children and older adults.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A short standardized instruction with worked examples and practice pairs with feedback on imagery quality
#### Constraints
- Effects on monitoring were limited in younger adults, who already use imagery spontaneously; the article notes results of a longer intervention might exceed these findings and that effects were tested on a recognition task with concrete noun pairs

### Target Learners
- elementary school children
- adolescents
- younger adults
- older adults

### Target Learning Goals
- paired-associate memory
- metacognitive monitoring accuracy and differentiation
