Diagnose students' misconceptions with pre-instruction tests before teaching basic concepts¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 associational), q2–q4 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Before teaching basic concepts, administer a success test and concept test to list common misconceptions, then prepare teaching materials and concept tests based on those misconceptions. The article states "Students' previous knowledge about the concepts and their misconceptions about these are needed to be defined before teaching basic concepts."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Use pre-instruction tests to elicit and categorize misconceptions.

Constraints¶

  • Misconception lists were specific to air pressure in this 9th-grade geography context.

Target Learners¶

  • 9th grade high school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Conceptual understanding in geography

Claims¶

  • Misconceptions can lead to other misconceptions as students interpret new concepts [+W]
  • Conceptual change instruction reduced specific air pressure misconceptions more than traditional instruction [+W]
  • Traditional instruction was associated with increases in some air pressure misconceptions [+W]
  • On distractor-driven science items, instruction can first strengthen students' attraction to an alternative conception before that preference declines [~M]
  • Instruction that confronts science misconceptions (conceptual-conflict interventions, refutation text) produces conceptual change more often than standard exposition [+M]
