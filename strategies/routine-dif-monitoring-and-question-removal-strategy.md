# Make routine DIF analysis part of high-stakes assessment governance: identify, review, withdraw and publicise questions with notable DIF

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that providers of high-stakes assessments make DIF evaluation one of a range of routine fairness measures. Routine analysis will identify the apparently rare cases of notable DIF and "promote fairness at the borderline of pass/fail decisions". Flagged questions should be reviewed by blinded expert panels, withdrawn from the question bank where notable, and publicised amongst question writers, so that connecting psychometric analyses with question-writing expertise promotes awareness of bias and builds a body of knowledge around questions most likely to produce bias.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to candidate background characteristics and item-level response data, DIF software (e.g. the difR or lordif R packages), and a panel of clinician assessors for blinded review of flagged questions.
#### Constraints
- The article states the causes of DIF were unexplained and whether they are false positives or due to some yet unstudied factor remains open; replications on novel datasets are required, and the method was only one of a family of DIF procedures.

### Target Learners
- postgraduate medical examination candidates

### Target Learning Goals
- fairness of high-stakes summative assessment decisions
