# Present papers in journal club via a four-highlight scaffold centered on physical meaning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For presenting a paper, the guide prescribes showing the title and authorship first (papers are identified by first author and year), then explaining four highlights: the big-picture problem, the data and analysis, the physical meaning of the results, and how the results fit the larger picture. Presenters are told to focus on physical meaning, circle back to the original purpose, present non-linearly if needed, and always know where to find answers to audience questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A completed reading of the paper; awareness that the audience spans subfields
#### Constraints
- The guide frames this as rough scaffolding from which presenters develop their own style, and notes presenters are not expected to know every detail

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students presenting in journal clubs and seminars

### Target Learning Goals
- communicating scientific results to peers
- connecting presentation content to the paper's original motivation
