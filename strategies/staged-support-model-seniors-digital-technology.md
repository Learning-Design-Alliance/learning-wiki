# Introduce seniors to digital reminder technology through a short hands-on course, a written manual, and weekly individual phone support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's support model for introducing seniors to new digital technology combines a short introduction course where participants try features at their own pace with a research assistant present, a written manual covering the core functions, and weekly phone calls from the same research assistant targeting the user's needs, questions, and potential difficulties. Participants reported the calls provided reassurance and emotional support, which the article notes has earlier been highlighted as important for learning and using technology. This model was described as sufficient for delivering instructions and tips.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A research assistant or support person available for a 30–90 minute introduction course and recurring weekly phone calls over the use period.
#### Constraints
- The article states findings come from active seniors recruited through a pensioners' organization and may not generalize to seniors with age-related or cognitive-related difficulties using technology.

### Target Learners
- Community-dwelling seniors learning new everyday technology

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning to use digital calendar and reminder functions independently
