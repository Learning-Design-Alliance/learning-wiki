# Include attitude-building elements in future skills-oriented educational interventions to strengthen their effect on student attitudes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' forward-looking recommendation: because the skills-oriented CST improved attitudes only slightly while knowledge and skills improved impressively, future skills-oriented educational interventions should add explicit attitude-building elements. The article states that "including attitude-building elements may enhance the effect of the geriatric CST on medical students' attitudes" and proposes studies testing whether such elements change attitudes better than the CST used here.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A skills-oriented educational intervention as the base format
- Evaluation designs able to detect attitude change (the authors suggest larger experimental groups and longer time frames)
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is untested in this study; the article only suggests future studies determine whether attitude-building elements work better than the CST evaluated here

### Target Learners
- Medical students in early clinical training

### Target Learning Goals
- Attitudes toward elderly patients
