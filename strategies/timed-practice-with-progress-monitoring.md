# Timed Practice With Progress Monitoring

## Description
Timed practice with progress monitoring pairs brief, repeated, time-limited practice trials on a well-defined skill (e.g., math fact retrieval, decoding, typing) with systematic charting of performance — typically rate correct and errors per minute — across sessions. The timing converts accuracy into a fluency measure, and the visible record of growth serves both instructional decision-making and learner motivation.

## Design Implications

Timed practice targets the shift from accurate to *fluent* performance: automatic execution frees working memory for higher-level processing [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]. Progress monitoring supplies the formative-evaluation loop that makes practice adaptive rather than repetitive — data on rate of improvement tells instructors when to raise goals, change methods, or move on [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Visible growth records also leverage self-regulated learning: learners who track their own performance against explicit goals show stronger persistence and strategy use.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A skill with a countable, repeatable response unit (facts, words, keystrokes, problems)
- An accuracy baseline established *before* timing — timed practice on unlearned material produces errors and frustration
- A simple, consistent recording format (chart, graph, dashboard) updated every session
- Short sessions (1–10 minutes) with immediate feedback on errors ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) with corrective feedback, not just scoring)

#### Constraints
- Timed practice before accuracy is established increases errors and math anxiety, particularly for learners with learning disabilities [~M] — the skill must be accurate first, fluent second
- Emphasis on speed can degrade strategy use: learners under time pressure abandon deliberate reasoning and guess [~S]
- Progress monitoring without instructional adjustment adds measurement burden with no learning benefit — data must drive decisions [Fuchs & Fuchs formative evaluation effects depend on feedback-informed instructional change.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [~M]
- Poorly calibrated time limits (too tight) shift attention from the skill to the clock, adding extraneous load [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Precision Teaching / fluency-based instruction**: rate-based aims (e.g., digits correct per minute) with celeration charts
- **Curriculum-Based Measurement (CBM)**: brief standardized probes administered weekly, common in special education and RTI/MTSS
- **App-based dashboards**: platforms like [XtraMath](https://xtramath.org) and [TypingClub](https://www.typingclub.com) automate timing, scoring, and progress graphs
- **Self-monitoring variants**: learners chart their own scores, which strengthens self-regulation even when the instructor does not use the data

### Target Learners
- Learners who are accurate but slow — the strategy's core use case for building automaticity [+S]
- Struggling learners in tiered intervention systems, where progress data identifies non-responders early [+M]
- Less appropriate for novices still acquiring the skill, or for complex, open-ended tasks where speed is not the bottleneck [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Automaticity of foundational skills (math facts, decoding, keyboarding, sight vocabulary)
- Retention and endurance: fluent performance maintained over time and under distraction
- Self-regulated learning: goal-setting and self-evaluation against one's own performance data

### Instructions
1. Establish accuracy with untimed [practice](../elements/assessment.md) and corrective feedback before introducing timing.
2. Set a specific fluency aim (e.g., 40 digits correct per minute) based on peer norms or criterion tasks.
3. Run a short timed probe (1–3 minutes); score rate correct and errors immediately.
4. Have the learner chart the result and compare against their aim and previous scores.
5. Give immediate corrective feedback on errors, then brief targeted re-practice of missed items.
6. Review the chart weekly: if celeration is flat, change the instructional method, not just the goal; if the aim is met, fade timing and move to application.

## Examples
- **[XtraMath](https://xtramath.org)** — daily few-minute timed math-fact fluency sessions with automatic progress graphs for teachers and families.
- **Precision Teaching in special education** — rate-based fluency aims (e.g., words read correct per minute) charted on standard celeration charts, with instructional changes triggered by flat learning curves.
- **[Read Naturally](https://www.readnaturally.com)** — repeated timed reading of passages with graphs of words-correct-per-minute, combining timing, feedback, and self-monitored progress.
- **CBM in MTSS** — weekly curriculum-based measurement probes in reading and math used to decide whether Tier 2 interventions are working.
