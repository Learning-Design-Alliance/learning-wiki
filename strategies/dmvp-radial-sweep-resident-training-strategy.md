# Teach residents a standardized disc-macula-vessel-periphery image-interpretation sequence augmented with repeated radial sweeps, peripheral scanning, and fixation-efficiency exercises

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that residents be taught a standardized viewing technique emulating expert analysis: the established disc-macula-vessel-periphery (DMVP) sequence, which attendings in the study adhered to, augmented with "multiple outward sweeps through each quadrant, ensuring a thorough peripheral examination." Training programs could also add "timed diagnostic challenges or simulations that reward accurate, rapid assessments" to promote fixation efficiency, and use eye-tracking technology to give residents feedback on their gaze patterns.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires trainees reading retinal diagnostic imaging as part of ophthalmology training, such as during fluorescein conferences
#### Constraints
- The authors note visual search strategies alone do not substitute for extensive clinical experience, and longitudinal research is needed to validate the approach

### Target Learners
- ophthalmology residents (PGY2–PGY4)
- retina fellows

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate and efficient interpretation of retinal diagnostic imaging
- development of expert-like visual search strategies
