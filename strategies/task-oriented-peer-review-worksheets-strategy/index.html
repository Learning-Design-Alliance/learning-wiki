Use task-oriented peer review worksheets with repeated explanation of the feedback role¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes peer review worksheets that are "specifically task-oriented, asking peer reviewers for written responses on both the worksheet and the copy of their author's essay," administered at each writing stage (thesis statement, outline, introduction, conclusion, drafts). The main goal was not to evaluate peers but to provide constructive feedback helping classmates improve their final essays. The authors stress that an extended and repeated explanation of the peer review process is crucial, especially where the tool is scarcely used.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Repeated explanation of the peer review process and of the reviewer's role
  • Reviewer feedback must not affect the author's grade, while reviewer scores reflect feedback quality

Constraints¶

  • In the Ecuadorian environment the tool is scarcely used at school or university level, so students need extensive orientation

Target Learners¶

  • university EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving argumentative essay quality through constructive peer feedback

Affordances¶

  • Sociocultural Peer Review Efl Framing

Claims¶

  • EFL students who used peer review report high acceptance of collaborative work, with 80% preferring group work and enjoying the process [+W]
  • Students perceive peer review as improving composition quality, with ideas and structure benefiting most [+W]
  • Learners report peer review fostered critical thinking in an EFL academic-writing course, with mixed answers on identifying proper academic writing [+W]
  • More than 70% of participants reported peer review improved multiple aspects of their writing, and the vast majority approved of the method [+W]
