# Use a sandbox tutorial letting students freely explore equipment, medications and the virtual patient before scripted scenarios

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The programmers revamped the original tutorial—which had been designed to blind students to scenario details—into a "sandbox" tutorial in which students freely use and interact with all available equipment, medications, and the virtual patient outside of a scripted scenario. The authors explain: "we introduced a “sandbox” tutorial in which students were free to use and interact with all of the available equipment, medications, and virtual patient outside of a scripted scenario to gain contextual understanding of how the scenario functioned."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Virtual environment containing interactable equipment, medications and a virtual patient, separate from the scripted scenario
#### Constraints
- The original tutorial's blinding of scenario details was sacrificed for contextual exploration, a change the authors made deliberately for student training rather than prior-research purposes

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical and nursing students new to VR clinical scenarios

### Target Learning Goals
- familiarity with equipment locations and medication availability in the simulated emergency room
