Make language learners aware of interest as an affective factor so they can apply it in self-study reading¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Beyond material design, the article addresses learners directly: it suggests that learners be made aware of the impact of interest on vocabulary acquisition through reading and act on this awareness in their own self-studies. It states that "language learners should also be aware of the significant impact of interest as an affective factor in vocabulary acquisition through reading activities and can act based on the findings of this research especially in their self-studies."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Learners engaged in reading-based vocabulary study, including self-study, who can select or reflect on the topics they read

Constraints¶

  • The article's own comparison found no vocabulary gain advantage for the favored topic in this Iranian context, so the strategy's expected benefit is not demonstrated by the study

Target Learners¶

  • Intermediate EFL learners aged 15-25 in language institutes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Second language vocabulary retention through reading activities

Claims¶

  • Learners did not gain more vocabulary from favored-context (sport) passages than from disfavored-context (geography) passages; the favored-context hypothesis was rejected [-M]
  • The authors interpret the findings as stressing the role of interest and interest-driven motivation in L2 vocabulary acquisition [+W]
  • In a survey of 41 institute students, sport was the most liked reading topic and geography among the most disliked [~M]
