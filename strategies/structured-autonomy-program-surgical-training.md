# Implement structured autonomy programs with competency-based progression for independent resident operating

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a specific implementable program: a six-year residency allowing independent operating from PGY-4 onward, with eligibility requiring national exam completion, consistent intraoperative evaluations, and a dedicated surgical principles course. Each case was reviewed preoperatively with an attending, patients were informed a resident might perform the operation, and postoperative performance was reviewed in departmental feedback sessions. An attending remained available on call during resident-led cases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Attending availability on call during resident-led cases
- Preoperative review of each case and plan with an attending
- Patient informed consent disclosing that a resident might perform the operation
- Departmental feedback sessions for postoperative quality review
#### Constraints
- The retrospective study design could not randomize case allocation, which likely reflected attending discretion based on fracture complexity and patient factors

### Target Learners
- senior orthopedic residents (PGY-4+)

### Target Learning Goals
- progressive operative autonomy
- safe independent performance of complex trauma procedures
