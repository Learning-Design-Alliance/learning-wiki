# Shift study time from input (restudying) to output (self-testing and generation)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 theoretical), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The chapter closes with a concrete study recipe for learners: "try to spend less time on the input side and more time on the output side, such as summarizing what you have read from memory or getting together with friends and asking each other questions." Any activity requiring retrieval or generation—rather than re-presenting material—makes learning more durable and flexible. The authors also urge teachers and parents to consider how to introduce desirable difficulties into instruction, and students to abandon the tape-recorder model of memory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities must require learners to retrieve or generate information, rather than just re-presenting information to themselves
#### Constraints
- Difficulties become undesirable if the learner lacks the background knowledge or skills to respond to them successfully

### Target Learners
- students managing their own study
- teachers and parents designing instruction

### Target Learning Goals
- durable and flexible retention of studied material
- learning how to learn
