# Contextualise international digital-health competency frameworks for India and deliver them through modularised blended learning for working professionals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends adapting imported competency frameworks to local conditions rather than adopting them wholesale, stating that "multiple frameworks, including the Saudi Health Informatics Competency Framework, TIGER, Management Competency Assessment Programme (MCAP) and HITCOMP, need to be contextualised for the Indian context." It further argues that curricula for working professionals should be modularised for blended delivery, since a traditional classroom-based approach may not satisfy working professionals seeking flexible learning options. Institutionalisation through capacity-building at universities and national institutions is proposed to foster a digitally competent health workforce.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Localised frameworks that take worker capacities and structural inequities into account
- modularised curricula combining online and in-person instruction
#### Constraints
- India's fragmented digital infrastructure, variety in healthcare delivery and differences in digital literacy; poor rural digital infrastructure and disparate regulatory frameworks limit direct adoption of WHO and Canadian frameworks

### Target Learners
- working hospital administrators
- health informatics professionals in India
- allied health professionals

### Target Learning Goals
- digital health competencies
- health informatics competency development
- management competencies for digital health services
