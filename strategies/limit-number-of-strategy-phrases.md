# Limit the number of target phrases taught in communication strategy training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that future communication strategy instruction restrict how many formulaic phrases learners must study, because teaching many phrases in a short treatment may overload learners. They note "It could be argued that 17 phrases were overwhelming for the learners to study and produce in a short period of time and thus had an adverse effect on their conversation," and recommend that "future studies can prioritize limiting the number of phrases to be studied."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors select or cap a small set of formulaic phrases realizing the target strategies before treatment
#### Constraints
- Recommendation is speculative; the article did not manipulate phrase count, and the 17-phrase overload is an argument, not a tested result

### Target Learners
- first-year university EFL students

### Target Learning Goals
- oral communication strategies in small-group discussion
