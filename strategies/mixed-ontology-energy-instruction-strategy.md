# In interdisciplinary physics courses for biology students, teach energy with a mixed substance/location ontology using potential energy diagrams and bar charts that can represent negative energy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper recommends that when chemical energy is a central piece of the energy picture, instruction should make negative energy accessible from the beginning by combining substance and location metaphors rather than relying on the substance ontology alone. As the authors put it, "negative energy needs to be accessible from the beginning," and their professor transcript models fluent movement between both metaphors. The location metaphor enables energies both above and below zero, which the substance metaphor struggles to represent.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Chemical bond energy must be a central component of the course's treatment of energy, as in interdisciplinary physics-for-biology courses
#### Constraints
- The authors' cost-benefit analysis is specific to their interdisciplinary context; other contexts (K-12 teacher professional development, general Modeling Instruction courses) have led other authors to argue for the primary use of the substance ontology

### Target Learners
- undergraduate biology students in introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding chemical bond energy as electric potential energy
- reasoning about negative potential energy and conservation of energy
