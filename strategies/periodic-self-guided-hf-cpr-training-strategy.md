# Implement periodic self-guided CPR training using high-fidelity manikins in every medical student's training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that after initial instruction, students should undertake recurring self-guided practice on high-fidelity CPR manikins to maintain resuscitation skills over time. They state that "a periodic self-guided training, which can be achieved using high-fidelity CPR-manikins appears to be a promising way to reach the aim of maintaining persistent sufficient CPR skills". The recommendation rests on their finding that some intervention advantages persisted while the primary-endpoint difference did not, and that guideline requirements are hard to achieve even immediately after intensive training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to high-fidelity CPR manikins for self-guided practice sessions
#### Constraints
- The authors note the best possible learning strategy should include "a significant emphasis on long-term skill retention", since guideline requirements are hard to achieve even directly after intensive CPR training

### Target Learners
- fourth-year medical students
- medical personnel learning advanced adult CPR

### Target Learning Goals
- initiating guideline-conform CPR
- maintaining persistent sufficient CPR skills including minimal interruptions and adequate compressions
