# Teaching the Principle of Least Action through three interactive simulations of light behavior

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article introduces three numerical simulations to help undergraduate students understand the Principle of Least Action in optics: light reflecting in equal angles, light refracting through different mediums, and light moving between two points in the least time. The simulations are designed to be experiential and interactive, allowing students to change parameters and observe real-time graphs. The author states that 'simulations have the potential to catalyze discovery and engagement in the physics classroom in a way that no proof or textbook can.'

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need access to Python and the VPython 3D graphics module to run or program the simulations.
#### Constraints
- The simulations are designed for Optics or Advanced Mechanics courses dealing with the Action principle, reflection, refraction, or the Brachistochrone problem.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- understand the Principle of Least Action in Optics
- understand reflection, refraction, and the Brachistochrone problem
