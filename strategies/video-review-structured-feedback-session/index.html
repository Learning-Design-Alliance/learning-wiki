Structured post-procedure feedback sessions using video review with a senior surgeon¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

For live surgery, the review describes feedback facilitated by review of a videotape recording of the operation and a 60 min structured feedback session, during which technical deficiencies and possible errors were covered and instructions for improvement offered. This approach was associated with reduced procedure time and improved economy of movement and error scores in live cholecystectomy. The authors note this manner of provision may be resource intensive, so cost- and time-effectiveness must be considered.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Videotape recording of the operation
  • Structured feedback session with a senior surgeon covering technical deficiencies and possible errors

Constraints¶

  • Resource intensive; cost- and time-effectiveness must be considered

Target Learners¶

  • surgical trainees performing live procedures

Target Learning Goals¶

  • intraoperative technical skill improvement
  • error reduction

Claims¶

  • VBC outperforms controls whether delivered in single or multiple sessions and in simulated or live surgery [+W]
  • VBC session length of over 20 minutes is likely optimal, with shorter sessions more often failing to show significant benefit [+W]
  • Video review sessions impose substantial time costs, with review sessions lasting roughly 30–90 minutes, and monetary costs are rarely reported [~W]
  • Feedback based on intraoperative surgical performance improves surgical performance across live and simulated procedures [+W]
