# Teach partition functions by enumerating system states directly, bypassing the single-particle partition function

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends a teaching approach in which students compute partition functions by directly enumerating the states available to the whole system, rather than via the single-particle PF q, which is not relevant for quantum statistics. The authors state the advantage is that "students by ignoring the single-particle PF understand states available to the system itself" and that "the PF correctly counts the available states and provides an interpretation to the PF". This makes the origin of exchange degeneracy and Pauli exclusion visible.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A computational environment (the article uses open source Python) to enumerate and sum over system states
#### Constraints
- The approach is demonstrated only on small N-particle, n-level systems

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- interpreting the partition function as a count of available states
- understanding Fermi-Bose statistics and indistinguishability
