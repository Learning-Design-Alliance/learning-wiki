Future ISS research and evaluation should include detailed demographic and contextual data, develop valid data collection tools, and identify optimal frequency and length of simulations¶

Strategy · All strategies

Description¶

The review's conclusions set out research and evaluation priorities for in-situ simulation. It recommends that studies capture demographic and contextual detail to support transferability across care settings and teams and to assess confounding, that valid and reliable data collection tools be developed to capture team and individual performance complexity in real settings, and that research identify the optimal frequency and length of ISS. The review states "In-situ simulation studies need to include detailed demographic and contextual data to consider transferability across care settings and teams and to assess possible confounding factors."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Valid and reliable data collection tools should be developed to capture the complexity of team and individual performance in real settings

Constraints¶

  • There is currently a lack of consensus for implementing and evaluating in-situ simulation, per the review's conclusions

Target Learners¶

  • healthcare practitioners in patient care settings
  • multidisciplinary clinical teams

Target Learning Goals¶

  • improving evaluation of ISS interventions
  • identifying optimal ISS frequency and length
  • assessing transferability across settings

Claims¶

  • Regular ISS over longer periods was reported for complex practices and patient outcomes, while one to three sessions were reported effective for more easily defined or isolated tasks [+W]
