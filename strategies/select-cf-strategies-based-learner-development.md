# Select written corrective feedback strategies based on learners' level of development and language analytic ability.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For the effect of written corrective feedback to be maximized, it is essential for teachers to pay more attention to individual differences and select CF strategies based on learners' level of development. Analytically weak learners can benefit from DMC to the same extent as analytically strong learners because metalinguistic information can make up for the lack of ability in inducing linguistic rules. In contrast, analytically strong learners benefit more from DOC than analytically weak learners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers assess learners' level of development and language analytic ability before selecting CF strategies
#### Constraints
- The strategy is grounded in the study's finding that language analytic ability mediated the effectiveness of DOC rather than that of DMC

### Target Learners
- low-intermediate EFL learners with varying language analytic ability

### Target Learning Goals
- mastering uses of English articles efficiently
