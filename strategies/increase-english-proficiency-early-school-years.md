# Increase English proficiency — the language of instruction — during or before the early school years to improve attainment and behavioral functioning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
From the finding that low English proficiency at school entry predicts poorer attainment and more difficulties in both EAL and monolingual children, the authors recommend building proficiency in the main language of instruction early. They state that "increasing proficiency in English, or the main language of instruction, during the early school years or prior to school entry will improve social, emotional, and behavioral functioning and academic performance." They also urge supporting families to continue rich first-language interactions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions delivered during the early school years or prior to school entry, targeting the main language of instruction
#### Constraints
- The authors note it may not be practical for clinicians and educators to offer direct instruction in other languages, particularly where over 300 languages are represented in schools; they call for randomized controlled trials to evaluate such interventions

### Target Learners
- Children with low proficiency in the language of instruction at school entry, including children with EAL

### Target Learning Goals
- English language proficiency
- Academic attainment
- Social, emotional, and behavioral functioning
