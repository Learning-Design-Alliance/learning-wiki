# Adopt a tiered curriculum for urban-rural interactive classrooms: interactive experiential courses for lower grades, reasoning-focused theoretical courses for higher grades

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends structuring the interactive-classroom curriculum by grade level because the value of interactive, atmosphere-driven teaching declines as students mature. The authors state that "the curriculum for these classrooms should adopt a tiered structure," with lower grades receiving interactive, hands-on, experiential courses and higher grades shifting to theoretical courses demanding logical reasoning, critical thinking, and computational skills. This follows from the finding that gamified, highly interactive formats show declining efficiency and evaluation in senior grades.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Volunteer teachers trained in course design that matches grade-level cognitive development
- Awareness of students' grade level when selecting course content and format
#### Constraints
- Derived from a study set in Chinese rural Hope Primary Schools; the authors note the cultural context may restrict broader applicability

### Target Learners
- Rural primary school students in grades 1–6 attending urban-rural interactive classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining learning efficiency and positive teaching evaluation across grade levels in remote interactive instruction
