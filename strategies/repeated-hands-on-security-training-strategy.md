# Improve security training efficacy through more hands-on, repeated, and systematically exposed training rather than one-off awareness videos

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their null and counterintuitive results, the authors recommend moving beyond awareness-style video training. They note Hansche (2001) classifies their intervention as mere security awareness and advocates "more hands-on approaches for security education" involving active working sessions, though such approaches are not always feasible for large institutions. They also suggest testing different training programs and gauging the potency of systematic and repeated exposure, since frequent exposure may encourage behavioral modification.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must go beyond awareness-level video modules to active, involved working sessions to change actual behavior
#### Constraints
- Involved, hands-on training approaches are not always feasible, especially for a large public institution with thousands of staff and faculty and over thirty thousand students

### Target Learners
- undergraduate business students using personal mobile devices

### Target Learning Goals
- adopting secure mobile device practices

### Affordances
- Motivated Avoidance Security Complacency
