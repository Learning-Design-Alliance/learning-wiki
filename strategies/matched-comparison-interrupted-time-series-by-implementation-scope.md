# Use interrupted time-series designs with matched comparison groups, adapting the level of analysis to each site's scope of implementation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
To estimate quasi-experimental effects of proficiency-based reforms, the evaluators "use what amounts to an interrupted time-series design with a matched comparison group," varying the level of analysis by site: district-level pre/post trends with propensity-matched schools (Site A), school-by-grade comparisons within district or state (Site B), and student-level comparisons within the same schools, subjects, grades, and years (Site C).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Matched comparison groups constructed on prior achievement and demographics; robust standard errors reflecting nesting of observations within schools
#### Constraints
- Aggregated-data approaches risk confounding from changes in student composition; classroom-level comparisons cannot fully separate teacher from curriculum effects

### Target Learners
- high school students in proficiency-based pilot programs

### Target Learning Goals
- mathematics and reading/ELA achievement on accountability and benchmark assessments
