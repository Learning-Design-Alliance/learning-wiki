# Use a halogen work lamp at about 42 cm as an artificial Sun for indoor panel measurements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
When weather prevents outdoor measurements, the authors use a 500 W halogen work light as an artificial Sun equivalent. Measured with a laser power meter, a lamp placed about 42 cm from the panel glass (48 cm from the filament) provides incident light roughly equivalent to standard 1 kW/m² solar insolation, allowing the maximum power point activities indoors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 500 W halogen work lamp, a laser power meter or similar to verify placement distance, and attention to ventilation
#### Constraints
- The halogen spectrum is quite yellow, ≈2700 K, much cooler than sunlight; the article notes panel efficiency may read lower if the cells absorb red photons less efficiently, and the authors have not investigated the spectral absorption of their cells

### Target Learners
- elementary education undergraduates

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring panel power output and maximum power point under controlled illumination
