# Build space test proficiency through simulators first, then shadowing by seasoned operators during high-tempo activities (the backseat IP model)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors argue a future Space Test Pilot School would have an outsized role in developing space test training that gives operators valuable experience without actually risking the vehicle. "This training can be done first by using simulators, and later by allowing operators-in-training to be shadowed by seasoned operators during high-tempo activities." The seasoned operator can then jump in if things start going awry, in a backseat IP model.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A future Space Test Pilot School or equivalent training institution
- Seasoned operators available to shadow trainees during high-tempo activities
#### Constraints
- A space operator cannot depend on recurring objectives to train for, since each vehicle has a unique personality that can change over mission life

### Target Learners
- operators-in-training for space test
- junior space operators

### Target Learning Goals
- operating spacecraft under stress
- remaining calm and diagnostic in demanding unknown situations
