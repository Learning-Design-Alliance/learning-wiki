# Co-Creating Classroom Norms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Co-creating classroom norms is a strategy in which students participate in generating the expectations for how the class will operate — how members treat each other, engage in discussion, handle disagreement, and pursue academic work. Rather than receiving a teacher-imposed rules list, students propose, refine, and commit to norms, typically through structured discussion followed by public documentation and periodic revisiting.

## Design Implications

Co-created norms build ownership and buy-in: students follow agreements they helped author more reliably than rules imposed on them, and the process itself signals that their voice matters in the learning community [~M]. The practice supports a sense of belonging and shared responsibility, conditions associated with improved engagement and outcomes [Belonging interventions improve student outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. It also functions as an autonomy-supportive practice, which fosters intrinsic motivation [Autonomy support increases intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]. The mechanism is partly social-contractual: publicly negotiated norms give peers standing to hold one another accountable without framing every correction as teacher authority.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A structured process (brainstorm → cluster → refine → ratify) so the conversation produces a short, usable set of norms rather than a wish list
- Teacher facilitation that treats student proposals seriously while steering toward norms that protect learning and safety
- Public, visible documentation (poster, shared document) that norms can be referenced against
- A routine for revisiting norms — especially after violations or at natural breakpoints (new term, new project)

#### Constraints
- Co-creation without genuine teacher influence produces norms students perceive as fake, undermining trust [-M] — if the outcome is predetermined, the ritual reads as theater
- Norms generated purely by students may omit protections for marginalized students or fail to address academic behaviors; the teacher must add and justify missing items [~M]
- One-off creation at the start of the year without revisiting leads to rapid decay; norms must be reactivated through [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) and referenced during conflict
- Time-consuming; in short courses, a lighter-weight ratification of a proposed draft may be more efficient [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Full co-creation:** students generate norms from scratch (works well in advisory, seminar, and project-based settings)
- **Draft-and-ratify:** teacher proposes a small set; students amend, question, and commit (efficient for shorter courses)
- **Restorative variant:** norms are framed as community agreements with explicit repair processes, not rules with penalties
- **Online variant:** norms for discussion forums and video sessions (camera norms, response etiquette) co-created in the first synchronous session

### Target Learners
- K–12 students, where the process doubles as instruction in self-regulation and community membership [~M]
- Adult and higher-education learners, where co-created agreements support candid discussion of contested material [~W]
- Particularly valuable for students whose prior school experiences have been punitive or exclusionary; shared authorship rebuilds trust [Belonging interventions improve student outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Social-emotional: perspective-taking, shared responsibility, conflict resolution
- Metacognitive/self-regulated learning: internalizing standards for one's own conduct and work quality
- Community building: establishing the relational foundation required for [Collaborative Learning](../patterns/collaborative-learning.md) and discussion-based formats

### Instructions
1. **Open with purpose.** Ask students what they need from classmates to learn well; ground the conversation in shared goals rather than rules ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) or quick-write).
2. **Brainstorm and cluster.** Collect proposals, group them into themes (respect, participation, honesty, effort).
3. **Refine to a short list.** Push for 4–6 positively framed, observable norms ("We build on each other's ideas") rather than prohibitions.
4. **Add what's missing.** Teacher names any protections or academic expectations students omitted and explains why.
5. **Ratify publicly.** Every member (including the teacher) explicitly commits; post the norms visibly.
6. **Revisit and repair.** Reference norms during [Discussion Groups](../patterns/discussion-group.md) and conflict; hold periodic norm check-ins to revise as the community matures.

## Examples
- **Responsive Classroom (Center for Responsive Schools)** — the "Hopes and Dreams → Classroom Rules" protocol, in which rule creation flows from students' stated learning goals ([https://www.responsiveschools.org](https://www.responsiveschools.org))
- **Restorative Practices implementations** (e.g., Oakland Unified School District's whole-school model) use community agreements co-authored by students as the foundation for restorative circles ([https://www.ousd.org/restorativejustice](https://www.ousd.org/restorativejustice))
- **PBLWorks (Buck Institute) project-based learning** — co-created "norms for collaboration" anchor team contracts in every project cycle ([https://www.pblworks.org](https://www.pblworks.org))
