Use jigsaw, role-play, note-taking, joint projects, and game-based activities to embed cooperative learning elements in pronunciation instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies

Description¶

The article describes concrete techniques for delivering phonetic and phonological instruction through CL structures: "Techniques such as jigsaw puzzle, role-play, note-taking, and joint project were utili zed to facilitate learning th rough cooperative skills among group members." Groups of three or four were formed by random and heterogeneity principles, given team names and logos, and ended sessions with technology applications such as a PowerPoint Jeopardy game. A researcher-created Bingo game required each member to discriminate target sounds (e.g., ban versus van) for individual accountability and positive interdependence.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Groups formed on random and heterogeneity principles
  • Team-building and social-interaction activities prior to instruction
  • Lessons carefully prepared to entail most CL elements

Constraints¶

  • CL was used as a teaching and learning format, not an assessment tool; grades depended mostly on individual performance

Target Learners¶

  • Adolescent and high-school English language learners in ESL pronunciation classes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accurate pronunciation of target English consonants
  • phonetic discrimination of minimal pairs
  • social skills through group work

Claims¶

  • Phonetic and phonological instruction delivered in a cooperative learning setting significantly improved ELLs' pronunciation of target English sounds over six weeks [+W]
  • Cooperative learning and conventional instruction produced no statistically significant difference in pronunciation improvement among Spanish-speaking ELLs in a six-week quasi-experiment [-W]
  • Phonetic and phonological instruction delivered in a conventional setting also significantly improved ELLs' pronunciation of target English sounds [+W]
