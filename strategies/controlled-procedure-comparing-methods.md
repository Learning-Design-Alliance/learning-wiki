# Controlled procedure for comparing conflicting teaching methods: equal-ability groups differing only in the factor under test, with uniform conditions and delayed testings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article sketches a controlled procedure for answering method questions such as how to divide time between oral and written arithmetic. Pupils are divided so that "about equal capabilities exist on each side"; groups agree on differing apportionments of recitation time; after a fixed number of days on a topic such as simple interest by the 6 per cent method, all groups are tested under absolute uniformity of time and directions, with tests printed and administered by one person equally strange or well known to all children. Testings are repeated after about a month and at the end of a year to compare retention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All factors save the one being tested must be equalized, including amount of home work, individual coaching, handling of written work and use of incentives
- Tests and directions should be printed, and one person should give all the tests while teachers, principals and others refrain absolutely from participating
- Thorough testings at the close of teaching, after about a month, and at perhaps the end of a year
#### Constraints
- The article notes that even with a very large number of systems tested there is always an element of doubt, hence the controlled procedure is desirable before final conclusions are reached

### Target Learners
- elementary school pupils divided into groups of about equal arithmetical ability

### Target Learning Goals
- determining the relative effectiveness of conflicting arithmetic teaching methods
