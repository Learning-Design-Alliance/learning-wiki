# Reduce surgical learning in trials through pre-trial technique standardisation training and balancing randomisation by surgeon

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes design features used in TOPS to minimise learning: all surgeons undertook a formal programme including direct technique instruction in the operating theatre from the technique's developer, written instruction, video demonstrations, an illustrated seminar and a discussion session, and were endorsed as competent before participation. Operating surgeon was also incorporated as a stratification factor in the randomisation process. The article reports these measures reduced but did not fully eradicate learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the technique developer for instruction and endorsement; ability to stratify or minimise randomisation within surgeon
#### Constraints
- Defining minimum operation-count prerequisites was not possible in TOPS because required experience levels were unrealistic for many surgeons in participating countries; not all aspects of palatal repair are amenable to standardisation

### Target Learners
- surgeons participating in surgical trials

### Target Learning Goals
- standardised delivery of a surgical technique across trial surgeons
