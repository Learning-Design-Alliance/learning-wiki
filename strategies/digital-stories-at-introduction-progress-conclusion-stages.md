# Use teacher-developed digital stories at introduction, progress, and conclusion stages of mathematics lessons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes placing teacher-developed digital stories at different lesson stages: in introduction activities to attract attention and arouse curiosity, in progress activities to bring main concepts and principles with review, association and exemplification, and in conclusion activities for summarizing, integration, evaluation, feedback and remotivation. In the study, 11 stories were applied by the mathematics teacher across four weeks of instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stories developed by teachers based on the content of the related course
- Scenarios suitable for the grade level and learning objectives
#### Constraints
- The teacher reported not having time to prepare stories and needing expert support; stories were only usable when readily available

### Target Learners
- secondary school mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- learning abstract mathematics content by associating it with daily life
