# Design faculty evaluation forms to encourage standard descriptors for both genders and provide implicit bias training for trainees

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the discussion the authors propose interventions to prevent or mitigate implicit bias in narrative evaluation: implicit bias training for physician trainees and designing faculty evaluation forms to encourage the use of standard descriptors for both genders. This responds to their finding that, although numeric ratings were equal, the descriptors used differed by faculty gender and may be differentially valued by promotion committees.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Narrative evaluations must be part of the assessment system, since the authors note written comments may carry greater importance given the limited spread of numeric ratings
#### Constraints
- The article states further study is warranted on how the different descriptors are associated with performance appraisal and promotion; the interventions themselves were not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- Physician trainees (medical students, residents, fellows)
- Academic medical centers using narrative evaluations for promotion

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing implicit gender bias in evaluative language about faculty
