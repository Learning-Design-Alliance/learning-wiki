# Incorporate LLM-driven peer question-asking into VR learning environments, especially for complex subjects

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing LLM-driven virtual classrooms in which AI peer learners ask questions after instructional segments. In more complex subjects, the authors state, "LLM-driven peer interactions can effectively direct attention toward key instructional elements and increase cognitive engagement", while in less complex subjects the benefits come through engagement and verbal processing. The strategy operationalizes peer questions as instructional signals in immersive settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An LLM-driven virtual classroom with a teacher avatar presenting instructional slides and structured question opportunities after each slide
#### Constraints
- Topic complexity was not manipulated as an independent variable, so the complexity-dependent effects are exploratory
- Technical issues were reported primarily related to the speech-to-text functionality

### Target Learners
- adult learners in higher education or specialized training

### Target Learning Goals
- sustained attention to instructional content
- engagement and participation in virtual lectures
