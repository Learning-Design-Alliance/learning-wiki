# Use game modifications — varied noise levels, adversarial noise, measurement noise, limited resources, and stabiliser size — to deepen exploration of error correction trade-offs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article lists modifications educators can layer onto the base game. "Exploration of noise levels" lets players change the Noise Value or dice; "adversarial noise levels" leave the Noise Value choice to the Noise alone, possibly kept secret; a "measurement noise" phase adds swapping after marbles reach the Receiver's bag; a "limited resources" points budget prompts the Receiver to optimise information gain; and a "stabiliser size" variant lets the Receiver question groups of two, four, or six Messengers instead of only pairs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The limited resources modification requires setting up a points budget where asking one question to a pair costs a negative point
- The stabiliser size variant needs enough players to form larger parity check groups
#### Constraints
- Adding more Noise players is not recommended for fewer than 6 players
- With larger groups, Messengers must count the parity of their pieces of information rather than whether all agree — a confusion that arises going from two to four players

### Target Learners
- children aged 7 and above playing the parity check or quantum code versions

### Target Learning Goals
- explore the interplay between information redundancy and noise levels
- understand how stabiliser size affects error localisation
