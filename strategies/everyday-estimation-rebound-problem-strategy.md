# Use everyday estimation problems, such as the rebounding tennis ball on a basketball, to test physical reasoning without exact calculation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The collection includes estimation tasks grounded in everyday scenarios rather than abstract symbols. Problem 2.1 asks: "A tennis ball sits atop a basketball as the two are dropped without initial speed from height 1 m. Estimate the height the tennis ball will rebound." The printed solution reasons frame by frame and yields a rebound height of 9 m, while explicitly acknowledging real-world corrections: air resistance and energy losses mean the actual height is less. This strategy assesses order-of-magnitude physical reasoning and model assumptions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be able to choose reference frames and make idealizing approximations (negligible mass ratio, elastic bounce)
#### Constraints
- The solution flags that the estimate overstates the real height due to air resistance and impact energy losses

### Target Learners
- first-year university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- estimation and order-of-magnitude reasoning
- choice of reference frame in collision problems
