# Apply retrieval practice (quizzes, peer teaching, flipped classroom) and monitor detail loss to balance gist and episodic memory

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review frames retrieval as an active process initiating reconsolidation that further modifies and integrates a memory. Iterative associative retrieval yields neural signatures of both mPFC generalization and parietal episodic specificity, which "is probably a reason why retrieval practice is so effective in educational situations." Box 1 recommends spacing study, repeating optimally through retrieval on separate days, keeping a list of important details and studying them separately, and incorporating regular checks to spot false memories and misconceptions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Retrieval should be iterative and associative so both generalization and episodic specificity are engaged
#### Constraints
- Episodic specificity induction also enhances false memory formation (sensitivity to DRM critical lures), so further research is needed before applying such methods educationally

### Target Learners
- students in middle and high school classes
- adult learners

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term retention
- strengthening memories through elaboration and integration
- detecting misconceptions
