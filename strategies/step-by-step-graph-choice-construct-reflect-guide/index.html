Step-by-step guide for teaching graph type choice, construction, and critical reflection¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review reports that Angra and Gardner (2016) developed, based on a qualitative analysis, a step-by-step guide for teaching students how to choose a graph type, construct the graph after planning each step, and finally critically reflect on the graph choice. According to the review, "Based on these steps, teachers can adapt their instructions in relation to students' answers, and students can develop a clear process for graphing." This strategy supports explicit graphing instruction, which the review links to developed graphing skills.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers adapt their instructions in relation to students' answers during the graphing process.

Constraints¶

  • The guide was developed from a qualitative analysis of novices and experts constructing graphical representations based on tables.

Target Learners¶

  • Students learning to construct graphs from numerical data

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Choosing an appropriate graph type for a task
  • Constructing graphs accurately
  • Critically reflecting on graph choices

Claims¶

  • Eleven of the included studies reported positive effects of graphing or graphing instruction, and none reported negative effects [+W]
  • Explicit graphing instruction developed graphing skills while all intervention conditions facilitated conceptual knowledge acquisition in one undergraduate comparison [+W]
  • Graphing difficulties cluster into graph construction, variable ordering, and data translation categories, with construction difficulties most frequent [~W]
  • Graphing instruction in K-12 STEM education is beneficial for student learning, improving both graph construction and graph interpretation skills [+W]
  • Manual graphing practice based on experimental data outperformed provision of computer-constructed graphs in one comparison study [+W]
  • Students experience difficulties during graphing both during graph construction and during the interpretation and usage of data [~W]
