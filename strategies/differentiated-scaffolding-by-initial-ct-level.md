# Differentiate CT scaffolding by initial CT level: open-ended challenges for moderate-CT, constraint-based tasks for high-CT, structured pathways for low-CT learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Based on the Optimal Development Zone finding, the authors recommend differentiated scaffolding in CT education: "moderate-CT students benefit from open-ended challenges; high-CT students need constraint-based tasks; low-CT students require structured pathways with agent modification tasks." This follows from the non-linear relationship between initial CT level and gains, in which both high-CT and low-CT peers showed constrained gains. It treats initial CT level as a grouping variable for tailoring task design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment of students' initial CT levels (e.g., an objective baseline such as the Bebras Challenge used for grouping in this study)
#### Constraints
- Grounded in a five-day short-term intervention; the authors state longitudinal studies are needed to assess persistence and transfer

### Target Learners
- pre-high school students at high, moderate, and low initial computational thinking levels

### Target Learning Goals
- computational thinking development through AI agent creation
