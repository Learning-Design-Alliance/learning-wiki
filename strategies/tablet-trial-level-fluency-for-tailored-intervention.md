# Use brief tablet-based trial-level fluency assessment to pinpoint specific arithmetic weaknesses for tailored intervention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes using its tablet-based assessment, which records trial-level accuracy and reaction times across problem types, operations, and problem sizes, to move beyond global fluency scores. Whereas existing chained-fluency assessments "only identify a general weakness", the trial-level data "can help pinpoint speci ﬁc aspect(s) of arithmetic in which a learner might need additional support", opening the door to personalized teaching strategies and tailored interventions. The assessment captured effects after just 3 minutes with no manual scoring, making it suitable for contexts lacking time or resources for traditional testing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires tablet devices with custom assessment software administered in classroom group settings.
- Requires the fixed task order with a preceding warm-up task to ensure learners are accustomed to touch responses.
#### Constraints
- The data cannot fully explain the cognitive mechanisms underlying arithmetic fluency without specifically asking participants how they solved a given problem.
- Results come from one geographic region and participants missing demographic information were excluded, which the authors state may limit generalizability.

### Target Learners
- Elementary through middle school students (3rd-7th grade in the study)

### Target Learning Goals
- Single-digit arithmetic fluency, including identifying whether a learner struggles with fact retrieval versus procedural operations
