# Integrate video-based simulations into traditional MCQ examinations to assess non-verbal cue recognition and diagnostic competence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend adding video-based simulations to text-based MCQ examinations so that assessments capture real-time perceptual and interpretive skills alongside declarative knowledge. They argue that "GM-ITE MCQs primarily evaluate declarative knowledge and structured reasoning, whereas the clinical simulation video captures real-time perceptual and interpretive skills," so the video format adds value by assessing competencies not fully captured by text. They advocate broader adoption of video simulations in medical education, including for diagnostic-skill preparation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Professionally produced scenarios with accurate medical content and informed-consenting actors
- Rater training or calibration protocols for consistent evaluation of free-text and non-verbal cue responses
#### Constraints
- The authors call for ongoing evaluation of the reliability and validity of video-based assessments
- Cross-sectional design cannot establish that video-based assessment causally improves competence

### Target Learners
- resident physicians in general medicine training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnostic competence
- recognition and interpretation of non-verbal clinical cues
- integration of verbal and non-verbal patient information
