# Use inherently dual optical illusions in lessons, presentations and books to explain counter-intuitive quantum concepts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article invites teachers to exploit optical illusions as sensorial aids for quantum concepts: bi-stable drawings for superposition, collapse and entanglement; a flipping card for complementarity; spinning illusions for spin; hybrid and animated illusions for measurement and spin superposition. The teacher should present the correspondences explicitly and stop spectators from forcing the analogy where it breaks, for example by forbidding mirror views of the card's rear face.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must make clear that repeating a measurement on a real quantum state yields the same result, unlike repeated viewing of a bi-stable drawing
#### Constraints
- Each illusion has stated limits of applicability, and the author says it is beyond the work's scope to analyze the adequacy of every known illusion

### Target Learners
- high school students
- general population

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding quantum superposition, complementarity, entanglement, teleportation and spin
