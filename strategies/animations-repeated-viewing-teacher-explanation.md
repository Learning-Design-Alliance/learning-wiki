# Use computer animations with repeated viewing and teacher explanation to improve attitudes toward science

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a specific animation implementation: animations were played once without commentary, then twice more with students expressing opinions and the teacher providing explanations. This repeated-viewing-with-discussion approach produced the most positive attitude development toward science among the three methods studied. The authors recommend employing active learning methods and techniques for other subjects and units as well.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Animations relevant to unit activities obtained from a curated source
- Teacher provides explanations during repeated viewings
- Students are asked to express opinions about animations
#### Constraints
- Animations were less effective than Jigsaw II for academic achievement and knowledge retention

### Target Learners
- seventh-grade middle school students

### Target Learning Goals
- positive attitudes toward science lessons
