# Use four embodied strategies — gestures, manipulation, body metaphors, and reconstructive eye movements — to ground animation movements in learners' bodies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes four strategies for making learning from animations more effective by involving human action: letting learners follow depicted movements with gestures or observe gestures embedded in the animation, letting learners manipulate the movements through interaction, embodying movements with body metaphors such as human characteristics on moving elements, and stimulating learners to reconstruct the perceptual processing of movements at test, for example via a static picture as a spatial marker. The authors state "we have suggested four different strategies to enhance learning from dynamic visualizations" and that these strategies ground the animation's movements in learners' bodily experiences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Gestures and manipulations should be related to the movements depicted in the animation to aid retention and comprehension
- Animations must be designed to allow different forms of interaction for the manipulation strategy
#### Constraints
- The strategies have not yet been tested within the domain of instructional animations
- For the gesture strategy, learners can only follow one or two movements at a time, posing a problem for animations with several simultaneous movements
- Following movements is a rather passive strategy that does not automatically induce constructive mental processes, and may be inferior for learners with prior knowledge
- Research evidence on the utility of such linkage for educational practice is really just emerging

### Target Learners
- learners studying mechanical or technical processes via dynamic visualizations

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the movements of dynamic systems depicted in animations
