# Leverage teachers' existing positive attitudes toward the profession to engage and maintain their interest in professional development activities that receive no compensation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that programs build on baseline teacher motivation and job satisfaction when asking teachers to invest effort in new techniques and content. Because teachers "are eager to help children learn", these positive attitudes "can serve to engage and maintain teachers' interest in learning new techniques and contents", which matters particularly where no monetary or in-kind compensation is provided for time spent in professional development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Baseline teacher motivation and job satisfaction must be assessed so programs can build on existing positive attitudes
#### Constraints
- Applies in contexts like OPEQ where no monetary or in-kind compensation is provided for time spent in professional development activities

### Target Learners
- elementary school teachers in low-income, conflict-affected settings

### Target Learning Goals
- teacher engagement in professional development and adoption of new techniques and content
