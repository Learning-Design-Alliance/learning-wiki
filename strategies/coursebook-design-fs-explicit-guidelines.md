# Integrate guidelines for explicit teaching of formulaic sequences into coursebook teacher manuals and embed formulaic-language exercises systematically across lessons with spaced repetition and retrieval.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that "coursebook designers may consider integrating guidelines for explicit teaching of these items into teacher manuals" since degree of salience in coursebook input or activities showed no effect on learning. It further suggests that "exercises that teach formulaic language should be embedded into coursebooks" with target items "repeated and retrieved at least eight times in spaced intervals," and that sequences be spread systematically across several lessons rather than occurring in one or a few exercises in isolation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Target items should be repeated and retrieved at least eight times in spaced intervals
- Sequences should spread systematically across several lessons instead of occurring in one or a few exercises in isolation
#### Constraints
- Based on findings from a 6-week EFL program with low-proficiency learners; the article was truncated in the source text

### Target Learners
- Low-proficiency EFL university students

### Target Learning Goals
- Receptive knowledge of form and meaning of formulaic sequences
