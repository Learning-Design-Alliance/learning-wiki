Graphic Organizers as Thinking Tools¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 18 studies (8 causal, 5 quant-synthesis, 4 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 5 of 18 report an effect size

Description¶

Students interact with content by organizing chunks of it into graphic organizers — concept maps, matrices, Venn diagrams, flowcharts, hierarchical taxonomies — in groups, pairs, or individually, then comparing their results. The organizer is not a handout to fill in passively; it is a thinking tool that forces learners to select, relate, and structure ideas, making their mental models visible and discussable. Comparing organizers across learners surfaces alternative interpretations and drives productive discussion about similarities and differences in the content.

Design Implications¶

Graphic organizers work because they externalize relational structure: spatial arrangement and links encode how ideas connect, supporting dual coding and reducing the working-memory burden of holding relationships in mind Chunking reduces working memory load. [+S]. Meta-analytic evidence shows knowledge-map activities outperform reading or list-based study conditions on average Nesbit & Adesope meta-analysis of concept/knowledge mapping. [+M], with the strongest effects when learners construct the map themselves rather than study a completed one Learner-generated organizers outperform studying provided ones. [+M]. The organizer type should match the target structure — hierarchies for taxonomies, matrices for comparisons, causal chains for processes — and the task should require justification of placements, not just completion.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Content with genuine relational structure worth mapping; organizers add little to isolated facts
  • Organizer type matched to the structure of the content (hierarchy, matrix, cycle, cause–effect)
  • A comparison or discussion phase where learners defend and revise their organizations
  • Templates or scaffolds for novices (Advance Organizers can seed the frame before content is encountered)

Constraints¶

  • Studying a pre-completed organizer is substantially less effective than constructing one Learner-generated organizers outperform studying provided ones. [-M] — fill-in-the-blank organizers can become low-level transcription tasks
  • Poorly matched organizer types force content into an alien structure, distorting rather than clarifying relationships [~M]
  • For learners with high prior knowledge, organizer scaffolds can be redundant and even depress performance Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases. [~M]
  • Construction takes time; the overhead can exceed benefits for simple or short content [-W]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Individual → pair → whole class: learners draft alone, merge with a partner, and reconcile differences as a class — the comparison phase is where misconceptions surface
  • Progressive fading: provide a fully structured organizer first, then partial frames, then a blank canvas as competence grows
  • Error-based variants: give learners a deliberately flawed organizer to diagnose and repair, which builds discrimination Erroneous examples build conceptual knowledge. [+W]
  • Digital tools (concept-mapping software) allow rapid reorganization and link labeling, supporting iterative revision

Target Learners¶

  • Novices who lack a schema for the content and benefit from an external relational structure Nesbit & Adesope meta-analysis of concept/knowledge mapping. [+M]
  • Learners who benefit from dual-channel presentation of verbal and spatial information Dual coding improves recall. [+M]
  • Less beneficial for experts, for whom the imposed structure adds little Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases. [~M]

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Relational knowledge: understanding how concepts connect, not just what they are
  • Comparative analysis: identifying similarities and differences across categories or cases
  • Knowledge restructuring: revising prior misconceptions by reorganizing ideas Activation of prior knowledge improves learning. [+M]
  • Metacognitive awareness: making one's own organizational assumptions visible and debatable

Instructions¶

  1. Select an organizer type that matches the content's structure (hierarchy, matrix, Venn, flowchart, concept map with labeled links).
  2. Optionally prime the frame with an Advance Organizer so learners know what kind of structure to look for.
  3. Have learners construct the organizer individually or in pairs, requiring them to Annotate links and justify placements rather than only position nodes.
  4. Use Peer Collaboration to compare organizers; focus discussion on why items were placed differently.
  5. Revise and consolidate, connecting the final structure to the Application task that follows.

Related Strategies¶

  • Activating Prior Knowledge — organizers can serve as the elicitation vehicle, surfacing existing structure before new content reshapes it
  • Concept Mapping — the specific, research-dense form of this strategy using labeled propositional links
  • Student-generated mapping exercise

Related Elements¶

  • Advance Organizers — a provided frame given before content, priming the structure learners will fill in
  • Annotating — link labels and margin notes turn a spatial layout into explicit propositions
  • Analogies — an organizer can map an unfamiliar structure onto a familiar one
  • Peer Collaboration — the comparison phase that converts individual organization into discussion

Examples¶

  • A foreign language class uses a hierarchical organizer to classify food vocabulary into categories (meats, fruits, vegetables), then compares trees in pairs to negotiate ambiguous items (e.g., tomato). Resource: Cult of Pedagogy, "Graphic Organizers".
  • A biology course uses IHMC CmapTools for students to build labeled concept maps of the circulatory system before and after instruction, making knowledge restructuring visible.
  • A history class uses a comparison matrix across three revolutions (causes, key actors, outcomes), then defends cell entries with evidence from primary sources.
