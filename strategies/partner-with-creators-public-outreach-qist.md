# Partner with professional content creators and engage public venues to disseminate accurate QIST explanations widely

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy has educators leverage established public-facing channels to correct QIST misinformation. Educator C, who makes YouTube videos explaining concepts like teleportation, advises hooking onto people who disseminate professionally, noting "if you can insert your message into that pre-established large following, that's how you reach the largest number of people at the minimum amount of effort on your part". Educator B similarly recommends public lectures and science centers where people can ask questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to professional content creators or public venues such as science centers willing to collaborate on QIST communication
#### Constraints
- The educators' suggestions come from interviews about their own practice, not from a test of the strategy's effectiveness

### Target Learners
- general public and early learners including pre-college students

### Target Learning Goals
- correct public understanding of QIST concepts such as teleportation and quantum key distribution
- managing expectations about what quantum technologies can do
