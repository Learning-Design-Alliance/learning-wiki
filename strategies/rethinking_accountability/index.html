Rethinking Accountability¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 7 studies (3 quant-synthesis, 3 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q4 · 3 of 7 report an effect size

Description¶

Rethinking accountability means recognizing that students did not choose disruptive circumstances (a pandemic, family crisis, displacement, or institutional disruption) and should not be unjustly penalized for the challenges those circumstances create. Instructors balance generosity with high standards: they modify assignments, offer flexibility in deadlines and grading policies, use alternative assessments, and prioritize maintaining connection and hope over strict procedural enforcement. The goal is to keep accountability meaningful — students still demonstrate learning — while decoupling it from penalties for factors unrelated to learning.

Design Implications¶

Fair assessment requires that grades reflect achievement of learning goals rather than compliance, access, or circumstance; when grading conflates the two, it loses both validity and motivational power Formative assessment should provide feedback about the gap between current and desired performance, not merely record a score. [+S]. Flexibility that preserves clear expectations supports autonomy and competence, both of which fuel intrinsic motivation and persistence Autonomy-supportive conditions strengthen intrinsic motivation. [+S]. Perceived fairness and attainable success also sustain self-efficacy, which predicts whether students persist through difficulty Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence. [+M].

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Open, frequent communication channels with students (Check-Ins) so instructors know who is struggling and why
  • Grading policies that allow revision, retakes, and extensions without ad hoc negotiation for every case
  • Alternative assessment formats that can substitute when standard conditions (proctoring, timed exams, reliable internet) are unavailable (Assessment, Accommodations)
  • Feedback that focuses on the task and the process of improvement rather than the person Feedback is most effective at task and process levels. [+S]

Constraints¶

  • Blanket leniency without standards erodes the information value of grades and can reduce student effort [-M] — unconditional grade inflation removes the feedback signal students need to calibrate their learning
  • Individualized accommodations at scale consume substantial instructor time and can be applied inconsistently, creating new fairness complaints [~M]
  • Institutional requirements (registrar policies, accreditation, prerequisite sequencing) may limit how far grading can be adjusted [-W]
  • Flexibility offered only reactively (after failure) benefits students who already know how to ask; proactive design is needed to avoid widening equity gaps [~M]

Implementation Variability¶

  • Revision-based grading: allow resubmission of major work until proficiency is demonstrated, as in standards-based and Competency-Based Assessment approaches
  • Grace-period policies: universal late-work windows or dropped-lowest-score schemes that avoid case-by-case negotiation
  • Alternative demonstration: substitute portfolios, oral assessments, or Case Studies when standard formats are inaccessible
  • Effort-separate grading: report behavior and participation separately from achievement so grades carry a single, clear meaning

Target Learners¶

  • Students facing disrupted access, caregiving responsibilities, or crisis conditions, for whom rigid deadlines convert circumstance into academic penalty
  • Students whose self-efficacy is fragile; early punitive failure predicts disengagement and withdrawal Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence. [+M]
  • High-achieving students still benefit from clear standards; pure leniency serves them poorly [~W] — they need accurate feedback to know what mastery requires

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Maintaining engagement and persistence through disruption
  • Accurate self-assessment: grades that reflect actual learning support calibration
  • Well-being and hope as preconditions for sustained learning effort

Instructions¶

  1. Audit current assessments for penalties unrelated to learning (timed conditions, single-attempt policies, attendance points) and remove or restructure them (Assessment).
  2. Publish a universal flexibility policy (extensions, retakes, revision cycles) so students do not need to disclose hardship to receive it.
  3. Establish regular Check-Ins to identify struggling students early and adjust expectations individually.
  4. Replace deficit-focused grading feedback with improvement-oriented feedback tied to specific criteria Feedback is most effective at task and process levels. [+S].
  5. Offer alternative assessment pathways (Accommodations) that assess the same learning goal by different means.
  6. Preserve at least one clearly held standard per course so accountability retains meaning; communicate the reasoning behind both the flexibility and the standard.

Related Strategies¶

  • Grading for Equity — a systematic framework (Feldman, 2019) operationalizing many of these practices: minimum grading, revision opportunities, and separating behavior from achievement
  • Standards-Based Grading — grades report proficiency on explicit goals, making revision-based accountability coherent
  • Trauma-Informed Teaching — supplies the relational foundation (predictability, choice, connection) that rethought accountability depends on
  • Give learners control over how learning is measured via assessment choices and science-fair-type projects
  • Develop flexible, alternative accountability frameworks tailored to schools' missions rather than relying on uniform measures such as the SAT

Related Elements¶

  • Assessment — the structure being rethought; alternative formats live here
  • Accommodations — the mechanism for individualized adjustment
  • Check-Ins — how instructors learn which students need which flexibility
  • Assess Performance — where revised standards and retake policies are enacted

Examples¶

  • Pandemic-era policies (2020): Many universities adopted universal pass/fail or extended withdrawal options; instructors who paired these with structured check-ins and revised assignments reported better sustained engagement than those relying on blanket leniency alone.
  • Revision-based grading in writing courses: Elbow's contract grading and portfolio-based assessment let students revise until standards are met, decoupling the grade from first-attempt performance while keeping the standard intact.
  • Khan Academy's mastery system (https://www.khanacademy.org) — students retake exercises until mastery is demonstrated; accountability is preserved (mastery must be shown) but failure is not terminal.
