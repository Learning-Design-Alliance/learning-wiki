Address teacher beliefs in professional development for ICT integration¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because beliefs significantly predict classroom computer use, the article states that "the dominant role of beliefs as a mediating factor in adopting educational innovations has major implications for professional teacher development". Professional development for ICT integration should therefore engage teachers' educational beliefs rather than only building technical skills. The article also suggests extending research to teachers' attributions and perceptions about how ICT might support their teaching.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Professional development provision that engages teachers' educational beliefs, not only technical ICT training

Constraints¶

  • The article does not test an intervention; this implication follows from the correlational findings, which cannot establish that changing beliefs changes computer use

Target Learners¶

  • primary school teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • integrating computers into classroom practice

Claims¶

  • Teacher beliefs explain variance in computer use comparable to technology-related variables [+W]
  • Constructivist teacher beliefs positively predict primary teachers' classroom use of computers [+W]
  • Traditional teacher beliefs negatively predict primary teachers' classroom use of computers [+W]
