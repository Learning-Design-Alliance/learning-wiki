# Use active learning strategies and style-aware lesson planning to raise low critical thinking scores

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the surveyed nursing students' critical thinking scores were low (mean 6.75 ± 2.16 against a scale midpoint of 15.98), the authors recommend strategies to improve critical thinking, including "frequent use of individual and group active learning strategies," tests targeting high levels of the cognitive domain, problem analysis and discussions, and promoting self-directed learning. They also suggest instructors consider the dominant learning style of each class in lesson planning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors who assess students' dominant learning styles and have the capacity to prepare higher-cognitive-domain tests and probing questions
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a low observed mean score in one Iranian nursing program; the article states current education methods in Iran may not effectively strengthen critical thinking and should be revised
