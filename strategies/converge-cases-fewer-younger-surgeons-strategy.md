# Converge complex-procedure cases toward fewer, younger surgeons with adequate volume within a short training period

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that higher annual volume and younger starting age shorten proficiency gain curves, the authors recommend establishing systematic training programs and concentrating case volume: "cases should be converged towards fewer and possibly younger surgeons, providing them with adequate volume within a shorter period of time." They note collaboration between hospitals or centralization to high-volume hospitals may be needed to supply sufficient procedural volume during limited training periods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adequate procedural volume per trainee within a limited period, potentially requiring inter-hospital collaboration or centralization
#### Constraints
- Study covered only open esophagectomies; applicability to minimally invasive esophagectomy requires future study; the study does not demonstrate the optimal annual volume or surgeon age

### Target Learners
- surgeons beginning independent practice in complex cancer surgery

### Target Learning Goals
- shortening the proficiency gain period to minimize learning-associated mortality and morbidity
