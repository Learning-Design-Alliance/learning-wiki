# Set explicit rules of the game for estimation: no lookups, no calculator, powers of 10, trusted assumptions, and stated physical principles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Introduced early in the class, these rules require students to develop their own numerical estimation skills without looking up data, do all calculations to one or two significant figure accuracy using powers of 10 notation, "explicitly state your underlying assumptions" grounded in plausible personal experience, and "specify the physical principles you are using". The author reports that doing this approximate math helps students "own" numbers and feel comfortable proposing, manipulating, and interpreting them.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must understand why the rules are used and believe they will lose credit for not following them
- Instructors must be explicit about why estimation is being learned and what value it has
#### Constraints
- With ubiquitous search engines available, the no-lookup rule is described by the author as "a hard principle to enforce"; many students do not trust themselves to do one-digit math without a calculator and are uncomfortable with powers of 10 notation

### Target Learners
- introductory college physics students, including life-science majors

### Target Learning Goals
- quantitative estimation
- sense of scale
- comfort with approximate arithmetic
