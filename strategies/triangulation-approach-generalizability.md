# Triangulate internal-validity findings with interaction analyses, propensity-score generalizability indices, and adapted extensions to establish external validity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report proposes a three-part methodological strategy for establishing that an experimental finding generalizes when probability sampling of schools is impractical: (1) test interactions between treatment effects and policy-relevant contextual variables within the sample; (2) use propensity scores and post-stratification to quantify sample-population match and estimate a population average treatment effect; and (3) examine adapted implementations in new settings. The authors conclude that "triangulation among several approaches to examining generalizability can be a reasonable substitute for the impractical technique of probability sampling," and urge the field to balance attention to internal and external validity.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A well-defined inference population with school-level covariate data available for the full population, such as a state longitudinal data system
#### Constraints
- Propensity methods are limited to matching variables available for the whole population and may omit factors that moderate effects, such as technology infrastructure; extension studies without local control groups support only cautious causal claims

### Target Learners
- Education researchers and school leaders evaluating whether research findings apply to their populations

### Target Learning Goals
- Establishing external validity and generalizability of educational intervention effects
