Address resource constraints and design quality when implementing virtual laboratories in low- and middle-income contexts¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 review), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review notes that two-thirds of included studies were conducted in high-income countries, so findings may not cover challenges facing low- and middle-income contexts. It states that "limited internet access, lack of digital tools, lower digital literacy levels, and inadequate technical support can significantly affect the feasibility and effectiveness of virtual laboratories," and calls for "further research on adaptable and affordable virtual laboratory models matched to resource-constrained environments."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Adequate internet access, digital tools, digital literacy, and technical support for feasible implementation

Constraints¶

  • Findings may not completely cover the challenges and limitations facing institutions in low- and middle-income contexts

Target Learners¶

  • Healthcare students in resource-constrained institutions

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Basic medical sciences laboratory education

Claims¶

  • Contextual resource constraints in low- and middle-income settings may limit implementation of structured, feedback-intensive clinical teaching models [+W]
  • Evidence on clinical teaching models is concentrated in high-income settings: most included studies were conducted in the United States and none originated from Africa [+W]
  • Cost, equipment availability, maintenance and faculty training are reported implementation barriers for laparoscopic simulation, especially in resource-constrained settings [+W]
