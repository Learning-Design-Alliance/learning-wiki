# Deliver smartphone-based, scenario-based PFA review modules and peer case-sharing via WeChat to sustain skills after initial training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Facing the observed decline in skills at the 2-month follow-up, the authors recommend "adopting smartphone‐based strategies that fit into nurses' daily routines". Instructors could use WeChat to deliver short, scenario-based review modules reinforcing the PFA core actions (Look, Listen, Link) during brief work breaks, and participants could join mobile group chats to share real-world cases and experiences. The article notes such e-learning strategies have proven effective for enhancing competence and that mobile devices offer a practical way to collect long-term follow-up data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a widely used mobile platform such as WeChat
- Short scenario-based review modules aligned with PFA core actions
#### Constraints
- The article recommends this strategy to address skill decline; it did not test it in this study
- Face-to-face and online approaches still require verification of cost-effectiveness between them

### Target Learners
- Registered community nurses in community health service centers

### Target Learning Goals
- Long-term retention of PFA knowledge and skills
- Reinforcement of PFA core actions (Look, Listen, Link)
