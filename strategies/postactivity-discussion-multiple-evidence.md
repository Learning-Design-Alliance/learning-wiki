# Postactivity discussion resolves conflicting evidence across data types and connects student phylograms to scientific consensus

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 causal), `q3`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size

## Description
A structured end-of-session discussion drives home the main points: sequences were selected essentially at random, students can verify species identities with BLAST searches, and conflicting evidence across activities is examined. The article notes that although three protein-sequence incidents suggested gorilla matched humans and chimps, "the chromosome density maps argue that the gorilla is more closely related to orangutans than to humans or chimps," demonstrating the need for more and longer sequences and multiple overlapping methods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Time reserved after the activities for discussion
- Optionally, Internet access for BLAST and phylogram searches
#### Constraints
- Chromosome maps cannot easily be searched bioinformatically; an Internet search for chromosome maps is suggested instead

### Target Learners
- introductory biology students

### Target Learning Goals
- weight of evidence in scientific reasoning
- interpreting phylograms and cladograms
- connecting exercises to current scientific consensus
