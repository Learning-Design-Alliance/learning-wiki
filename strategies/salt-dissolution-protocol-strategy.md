# Salt-dissolution experiment protocol with groups measuring temperature throughout and free choice of salt quantity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
An implementable classroom recipe: split students into small groups, give each a kit (large bowl, beaker with 500 mL of room-temperature water, about 100 g of kitchen salt NaCl, thermometer, stirring rod or spoon), have students measure water temperature after thermal equilibrium, dissolve the salt while stirring gently, and "takes notes of what is happening: is the temperature changing? Does all the salt dissolve in the water?" Students may "experiment with the quantity of salt that they like".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Thermometer with minimum range required: -50 °C to +50 °C
- Gentle stirring — the guide warns that stirring too quickly could raise the temperature and alter the results by introducing an external source of energy
- 500 mL of water measured with a beaker or a scale
#### Constraints
- The guide warns stirring too quickly "could raise the temperature of the system and alter the results of the experiment by introducing an external source of energy"

### Target Learners
- GCSE students
- A-level students

### Target Learning Goals
- Observe the energy associated with bonds
- Explain macroscopic temperature changes via bond creation and breaking
