# Model constructive evaluation by having the teacher openly improve through students' suggestions, so students learn that timely, constructive evaluation benefits both parts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A concrete tutoring activity: the educator starts with himself, recognizing his own mistakes and improving through student suggestions, thereby showing students that "timely and constructive evaluations can be of beneﬁt for both parts". The author argues that framing evaluation around recognizing and learning from mistakes secures student agreement and collaboration, and gives students the satisfaction of teaching their teachers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The teacher must be willing to improve through the suggestions of the students and to start with himself, which the author calls hard but necessary
