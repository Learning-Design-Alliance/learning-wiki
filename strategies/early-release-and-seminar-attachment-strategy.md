# Release videos early in the course and attach them to mandatory seminars with group homework

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because engagement declined as the term progressed and seminar-linked videos were viewed far more, the article's design implication is to publish pre-class videos early relative to the final exam and pair them with sessions carrying compulsory attendance and group tasks. The regression found seminars (+14.2% viewing, −15.5% non-access) and earlier release (+5.9% viewing) as independent factors. The authors caution that "Other unmeasured classroom dynamics need to be considered for further studies".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A course structure that can schedule compulsory seminar sessions with mandatory group homework tied to specific video topics
#### Constraints
- Causal direction is not established in this observational study; uncontrolled factors such as motivation and perceived relevance may contribute
- Contradictory findings exist for postgraduate populations, who engaged more with later-released content

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Voluntary pre-class video engagement and completion of embedded self-assessment questions
