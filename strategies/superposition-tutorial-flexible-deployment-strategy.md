# Use the superposition tutorial flexibly as in-class/recitation work, think-pair-share or clicker questions, or homework after prior instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends several concrete ways to incorporate the tutorial: in class or recitation in part or full, converted into think-pair-share or clicker questions after Gauss's law instruction, or assigned as homework followed by brief class discussion. The authors note "The tutorial could be used in part or in full either in class or in recitation" and that it is designed as a supplement, not a first introduction. Instructors may also select matching pre-/post-test questions when using only part of the tutorial.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students should have had prior lecture-based instruction in the relevant concepts, since the tutorial is designed as a supplement, not the first tool to introduce the concepts.
#### Constraints
- If only part of the tutorial is used, instructors should select the pre-test and post-test questions corresponding to the material in those sections.

### Target Learners
- first-year calculus-based introductory physics students, mainly engineering majors

### Target Learning Goals
- applying the superposition principle together with Gauss's law to find net electric fields for spherical, cylindrical and planar charge distributions

## Claims

- [Students who used a guided-inquiry tutorial on superposition with Gauss's law significantly outperformed comparison students on post-test questions (p < 0.001)](../claims/superposition-gauss-law-tutorial-improves-post-test.md) [+M]
- [After traditional instruction, only about 35% of students found the electric field direction at point A and only 14% at point B on a pre-test combining a line charge and charged cylinder](../claims/pretest-direction-line-cylinder-low-success.md) [+W]
- [Even after the tutorial, only 60% of tutorial students found the field direction at point A inside one of two adjacent charged cylinders, which the authors call extremely difficult](../claims/adjacent-cylinders-point-a-extremely-difficult.md) [+W]
- [A common student difficulty is adding the magnitudes of electric field contributions without considering direction when combining Gauss's law with superposition](../claims/adding-magnitudes-ignoring-direction-difficulty.md) [+W]
- [Students often measure distance in field formulas from the surface of a charged sphere or cylinder rather than from its center or axis](../claims/distance-from-surface-difficulty.md) [+W]
- [Students incorrectly claim that a non-conducting charged object shields its interior from the electric field due to external charges](../claims/nonconductor-shielding-misconception.md) [+W]
