Strategies

Have students set individual conversational goals and use student-friendly task cards with sentence stems during collaborative activities¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes an implementable recipe for student-led collaborative work: before each activity, students determine their individual conversational goals (for example, contributing more to the conversation or giving others the opportunity to speak), the teacher opens each lesson by having students share these goals, and students receive task cards written at their language level with pictures showing task expectations. The authors report that students used the sentence stems and materials frequently when speaking, and that students held one another accountable by redirecting peers to stay on task. The strategy is drawn from the treatment as actually implemented in the study.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Directions must be written at the language level of the students with pictures to help with task expectations
  • Students must determine their individual conversational goals prior to beginning the activity

Constraints¶

  • The article notes that although students used the sentence stems and materials frequently when speaking, the depth of math discussion did not follow

Target Learners¶

  • elementary-aged English Language Learners
  • first-grade students

  • student-led collaboration

Claims¶

  • Student engagement increased in the IC-JPA treatment group as disengaged behaviors declined over the intervention while remaining constant in the comparison group [+W]
  • In the IC-JPA group, teacher-delivered redirections decreased while student-to-student redirections increased, indicating growing student self-autonomy [+W]
  • IC-JPA instruction did not produce a statistically significant difference in correct academic language usage compared to traditional instruction [-W]
  • First-grade ELL students taught through traditional small-group instruction significantly outperformed IC-JPA-taught students on math achievement when controlling for initial ability [-W]
