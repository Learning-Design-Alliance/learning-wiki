Schools should set clear smartphone usage rules with technical safeguards and familiarise parents with smartphones' pedagogical affordances to reduce resistance¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that schools prepare to "deal with parental resistance and to address parental concerns" on two fronts. For social risks, "schools should use a firewall or filtering program to restrict harmful content" and BYOD students may use a guest-type Wi-Fi network with limited access. For pedagogical resistance, "it is important to familiarize parents with the pedagogical affordances of using smartphones in the classroom", since parents lacking belief in pedagogical potential resist most severely.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • School capacity for classroom management and technical filtering infrastructure; communication channels to parents

Constraints¶

  • Grounded in a self-selected sample of Israeli parents; the article notes further studies are needed

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary, middle and high school students in BYOD programmes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reducing parental resistance to smartphone-supported learning

Claims¶

  • Parenting style is associated with resistance level: authoritarian parents show the highest active resistance while most authoritative and permissive parents do not resist [+W]
  • Pedagogical resistance is rated lowest of the four resistance factors at every parental resistance level, while social and economic factors are rated highest [+W]
  • Pedagogical and social resistance factors positively predict actual parental resistance, while an authoritative parenting style is a negative predictor [+W]
  • About two-thirds of surveyed parents resist educational smartphone use in schools, and more than half of them actively resist [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Schools should consider parents' socioeconomic status, provide necessary information, and support training for effective technology-integrated partnerships
