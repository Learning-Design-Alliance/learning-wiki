# Use finger-based manipulative methods with active student involvement to teach multiplication facts to deaf learners

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends hands-on, student-centred approaches for deaf learners, arguing that "method of manipulative “hands on” and an active involvement of students in the teaching and learning activities is necessary to help deaf students in learning multiplication facts." MFTM operationalises this by having students use their own fingers as physical calculation materials, combining visual perception, physical movement and active involvement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need knowledge of pedagogy, content and math assessment
- Sign language total communication as medium of instruction
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single quasi-experimental study with the researcher delivering MFTM

### Target Learners
- Deaf primary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning and mastering multiplication facts
