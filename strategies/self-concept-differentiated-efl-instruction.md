# Differentiate English instruction by learners' academic self-concept level to optimize emotional pathways

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's discussion recommends tailoring English instruction to students' English academic self-concept. For higher self-concept learners, "educators should focus on helping them transform positive emotions into cognitive resources to foster autonomous learning", e.g., project-based tasks, timely constructive feedback, and reflection opportunities such as learning diaries or peer assessments. For lower self-concept learners, "instructional priorities may include cultivating interest and self-efficacy in English learning as well as mitigating the risk of learned helplessness". Anxiety-reduction techniques and an error-tolerant classroom climate are also suggested.

### Target Learners
