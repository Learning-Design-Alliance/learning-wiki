# Conduct spiritual inquiry rather than spiritual assessment in PAT preparation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using the term "inquiry" rather than "assessment" for spiritual exploration in PAT preparation, to reaffirm the participant-led process of care and de-link assessment from presumed outcomes. Chaplains conduct a spiritual and religious history including attention to spiritual background, beliefs, core emotional motivations, perceptions and needs. The article warns that "Continuing to neglect these aspects of histories poses potential safety risks."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Use of evidence-based spiritual screening and assessment tools such as the FICA Spiritual History Tool or the PC-6 Model
- Learning whether a participant remembers a previous challenging psychedelic experience or holds religious prohibitions about psychedelics
#### Constraints
- The language of inquiry communicates the value of accompaniment rather than outcome-focused treatment planning

### Target Learners
- Chaplains
- Spiritual care providers in clinical settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding participants' spiritual backgrounds and needs before dosing sessions
- Identifying spiritual assets and risks for engaging PAT
