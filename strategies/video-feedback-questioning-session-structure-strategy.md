# Run video-feedback sessions in which a supervisor guides analysis of the player's own successful and unsuccessful decisions through open questioning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy combines video-feedback with supervisor-led questioning as an explicit learning strategy producing verbalisable tactical knowledge. Each session analyses 6 of the player's own actions (3 successful, 3 unsuccessful, random order) through a fixed sequence: watching the point twice with contextual details, self-analysis, then combined player-expert analysis of decision context, possible solutions, selected response, execution, result, and alternatives. The supervisor "guide[s] the analysis of the game situation through open questioning, but not intervening directly or giving answers".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An expert supervisor trained to direct attention through open questioning without giving answers
- Video recordings of the athlete's own competition matches, applied within 24 hours after competition
#### Constraints
- Showing videos to athletes without directed attention has limited results because recorded events happen too fast and there is a lot of available information; the authors recommend the approach especially at lower performance levels

### Target Learners
- Intermediate youth tennis players in formative stages

### Target Learning Goals
- Tactical knowledge and quality of decision-making during real game play
