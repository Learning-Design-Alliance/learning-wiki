# Five recommendations for scientists venturing into science communication with teachers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on a decade of disseminating retrieval practice research, the author offers five recommendations for scientists seeking to engage in science communication: share colleagues' work if hesitant to share your own; "Approach science communication like an experiment or pilot test"; aim to be credible and relatable by pairing data with stories, since "people learn through stories"; get input from someone outside your expertise; and offer talks or Q&As to local schools and colleges. The recommendations are experience-based, not empirically tested.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Written or low-cost formats the article highlights: forms of communication with which scientists already have experience, not requiring financial resources, and provided free of charge
#### Constraints
- The author states science communication can become time consuming and there is no perfect design or procedure; an active online presence or large following is not needed

### Target Learners
- K–12 teachers
- higher education faculty

### Target Learning Goals
