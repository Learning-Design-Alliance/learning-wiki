Strategies

Schedule within-group and between-group discussion sessions on epistemological beliefs in science lessons¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend making epistemological discussion sessions a regular feature of science teaching, since group discussion and student-teacher interaction appeared to drive belief change. Their fourth recommendation states that "within and between groups discussions conducted as part of TAM and EETAM are effective in development of epistemological beliefs. Therefore, this type of discussions sessions should find more place in science lessons." Students defended written arguments (claims, warrants, qualifiers, rebuttals) against other groups.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Heterogeneously grouped students working in groups during lessons
  • Activity sheets on which students write claims, warrants, qualifiers or rebuttals

Constraints¶

  • Attributed effect rests on the study's comparison and on cited literature, not on an isolated manipulation of discussion format

Target Learners¶

  • 7th grade science students working in small groups

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Epistemological beliefs; argumentation skills

Affordances¶

  • Eetam Model

Claims¶

  • Post-test ANOVA on epistemological beliefs showed a statistically significant overall difference among the three groups (F(2,95)=3.593, p=.031) [+W]
  • EETAM produced significantly higher epistemological belief post-test scores than the comparison group, while TAM did not differ from either the comparison group or EETAM [+W]
  • Interviews found epistemological belief change themes only in the EETAM group (certain knowledge, omniscient authority, innate ability), with no epistemological findings in the TAM group [+W]
  • The authors interpret the six-week intervention length as insufficient for a significant TAM-versus-EETAM difference in epistemological beliefs [~W]
  • Think-pair-share instruction produced more positive epistemological beliefs about physics and physics learning than conventional teacher-centered teaching, including on the PSG, SM/E and CU sub-scales [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Collaborative Learning
  • Embed explicit reflection on epistemic beliefs within constructivist teaching practice so beliefs evolve through enacted epistemic cognition
