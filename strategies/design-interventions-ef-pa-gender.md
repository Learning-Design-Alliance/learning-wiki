# Consider executive functions, physical activity and gender when designing interventions to improve student academic achievement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that intervention design target the student variables its models identified as contributing to achievement: supporting executive functions, promoting physical activity, and accounting for gender patterns. It states this information "should be considered when designing interventions to improve student academic achievement", and that determining which variables contribute to achievement will help improve the design of intervention strategies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should be informed by the variables the regression models identified as contributing to achievement: EF, PA and gender
#### Constraints
- The article notes much of the variance in achievement remains unexplained in the tested models and future studies should include additional student, family and educational variables

### Target Learners
- adolescent secondary school students in Compulsory Secondary Education

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement in Language, Mathematics, Geography and History, English and Physical Education
