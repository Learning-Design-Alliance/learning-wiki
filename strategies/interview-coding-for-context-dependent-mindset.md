# Use interview-based coding for context-dependent variation in mindset beliefs rather than relying only on Likert-scale surveys

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes studying mindset in college physics through a "novel methodology by which researchers can look for context-dependent variation in mindset beliefs", which "examines student reflections about challenge in interview data". Interview questions are designed to elicit mindset-related talk (e.g., about feeling proud, hard moments, and what it means to be good at science), drawing on the context-dependent epistemological resources framework. This moves beyond the mindset literature's survey methodologies, which treat beliefs about intelligence as somewhat stable and binary.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interview questions designed around the context-dependent epistemological resources theoretical framework
- Coding scheme development processes for both challenge and response to challenge
#### Constraints
- Developed with students who had some fear of physics but were willing to work hard, in transformed classrooms with optional project-based components, which the authors hypothesized was most likely to show variation in mindset-related dialogue

### Target Learners
- undergraduate life sciences students in transformed introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- studying mindset-related variation in student descriptions of challenge

## Claims

- [In the landscape of college physics challenges, mindset beliefs cannot always be considered the dominant factor in how students respond to challenge](../claims/mindset-beliefs-not-always-dominant.md) [+W]
