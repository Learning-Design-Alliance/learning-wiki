Design pharmacist competency-training programs combining didactic teaching with interactive, patient-centered approaches and communication programs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 8 claims (5 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on its synthesis, the review recommends that healthcare administrators and educators design competency-based training programs that "include the most effective elements of implementation strategies and delivery arrangements interventions, such as combining didactic teaching with interactive, patient-centered approaches, and communication programs." It further encourages collaboration between professionals and technology-based interventions to make programs more successful and scalable, and suggests policymakers develop guidelines supporting ongoing professional development.

Constraints¶

  • Evidence derives predominantly from high-income country settings; the review notes generalizability may be restricted

  • pharmacists in community and hospital settings

Claims¶

  • The review attributes implementation-strategies effectiveness to experiential, interactive, and patient-centered learning methods alongside traditional lectures [+W]
  • Across the 25-year span of reviewed studies, educational approaches shifted from content-based to competency-based learning [+W]
  • Implementation-strategies interventions consistently improved pharmacists' knowledge, skills, or attitudes in chronic disease management across six RCTs [+M]
  • Combined implementation-strategies–delivery-arrangements interventions improved knowledge and skills in two of three RCTs, with percentage score differences from 4.5% to 30% [~M]
  • A combined implementation-strategies–delivery-arrangements intervention on pharmacogenetics and medication therapy management showed no significant knowledge improvement [-M]
  • Two implementation-strategies interventions showed mixed effectiveness, with gains immediately after intervention but not at follow-up [~M]
  • Communication skills training programs improve clinician patient-centeredness, but training approaches are variable and long-term retention is untested [+M]
  • Innovative andragogical strategies — simulation, narrative medicine, reflective writing — enhance empathy, compassion, and communication skills in medical students [+M]
