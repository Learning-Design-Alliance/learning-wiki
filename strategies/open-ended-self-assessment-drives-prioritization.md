# Use open-ended self-assessment with graded recall to drive content prioritization

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the Quiz Mode, students recall knowledge to answer open-ended questions, reveal the answer, and grade the quality of recall on a 4-point likert scale, yielding a Perception of knowledge score. The system computes "a weighted average of the last three sessions FlashcardPerception of knowledge" per Flashcard and preferentially selects the hardest questions when prior ratings exist, so students can "review the Flashcards for the questions with the lowest Perception of knowledge" and tailor future study sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Each Flashcard must contain open-ended questions
- Students must self-assess recall repeatedly for the weighted average to be informative
#### Constraints
- The article warns grading "is, however, subject to affective factors" and users "may feel inclined to overrate their Perception of knowledge"
- It remains unknown whether self-reported evaluations correlate with exam grades

### Target Learners
- medical students in self-directed study

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge recall, self-assessment, and identifying materials with lesser retention
