# In-class Study Skills intervention: a short small-group discussion on spaced studying and self-testing, with pre-test writing prompts and reminders

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The Study Skills (SS) intervention introduced two study skills through a 20–30 minute structured small-group discussion led by a research team member: "spaced studying" and "retrieval practice", the latter including "self-testing, such as doing practice problems, taking practice quizzes or tests, and making flashcards". Discussions were structured around what students already did to study and where they could change behaviors, to make the techniques personally relevant. Before exams, students wrote about what kinds of problems they expected on the test and strategies to solve them, and received short reminders throughout the term. The article reports this simple, cost-free classroom technique was associated with better grades for highly math-anxious students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a structured 20–30 minute in-class small-group discussion led by a facilitator who redirects off-topic or incorrect responses
- Requires follow-up reminders and pre-test writing activities for effects to continue, as the article states students may require continued support
#### Constraints
- Effects were demonstrated for highly math-anxious students relative to the ER comparison group; no no-contact/waitlist control group was included in the same cohort

### Target Learners
- High school math students, especially those high in math anxiety

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving math grade performance; reducing avoidant study behaviors; building effective study habits (spaced studying, self-testing)
