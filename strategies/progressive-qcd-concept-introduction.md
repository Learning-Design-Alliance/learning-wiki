# Introduce physics concepts progressively, one new rule per level, saving complicating concepts like spin for later levels

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A concrete recipe used in Quantum 3: start with one central concept and add rules gradually. As printed, "The game starts by introducing the central quantum number of the game: color. More concepts are presented to the player, and they learn new rules at each new level." Flavor follows quickly; antiparticles and spin appear at higher levels, where "the player should be looking for more of a challenge." Spin is simplified to up or down arrows so it does not break the trio rule players have learned.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The core gameplay must stay stable so new concepts do not break what the player has learned
#### Constraints
- Spin had to be simplified to up or down arrows to fit the game's rules and intuition

### Target Learners
- K-12 students, especially young girls

### Target Learning Goals
- Learning QCD rules incrementally: color, flavor, antiparticles, spin
