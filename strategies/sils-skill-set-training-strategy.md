# Teach a defined SILS skill set: crossed instruments, ambidextrous dissection, left-handed endoloop application, and angled-scope navigation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that SILS training should target a specific set of skills that distinguish single-incision from multiport surgery. It states that "SILS skills should include an ability to work with crossed instruments, ambidextrous dissection, left-handed endoloop application, and angled-scope navigation". These skills map onto the steps residents took the most cases to master, such as dissection and endoloop ligation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Residents should already be well versed in conventional laparoscopic appendectomy before learning SILS
#### Constraints
- The article limits SILS applicability in its institution to children over 4 years of age because SILS instruments may be unwieldy for small infants

### Target Learners
- general surgery residents (PGY III)

### Target Learning Goals
- single-incision laparoscopic surgery skills including instrument crossing, ambidextrous dissection, endoloop application, and angled-scope navigation
