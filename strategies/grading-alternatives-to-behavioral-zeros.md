# Offer instructors grading alternatives that eliminate behavioral zeros

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors who wish to eliminate the use of behavioral zeros have several options: adopting standards-based grading, which "may be challenging for large lecture courses, it is possible"; minimum grading, "the practice of never assigning grades lower than 50% (which is still failing)"; or using a GPA-like 4.0 scale to minimize the impact of behavioral zeros. These practices respond to the finding that zeros punish behavior rather than measure understanding and unequally impact student groups.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must be willing to change their grading scale or scheme; standards-based grading may be challenging to implement in large lecture courses
#### Constraints
- The article does not empirically test these alternatives; it presents them as options for instructors concerned with increasing grading equity

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large introductory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- grades that represent knowledge and skill rather than test-taking behavior
