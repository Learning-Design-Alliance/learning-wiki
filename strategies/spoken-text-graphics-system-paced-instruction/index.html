Use audio-visual (spoken text with graphics) presentation for system-paced, high element interactivity instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), q2–q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The meta-analysis supports presenting textual information through an auditory mode while graphical information is presented visually, particularly when materials are system-paced and high in element interactivity. The author notes the strong observed effect under system-paced conditions "must be weighed against the additional cost of developing audio-visual instructional materials", and points to inexpensive implementation routes such as tape-recorded instructions and recorded comments in PowerPoint slideshows or Camstudio files stored on websites.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructional materials high in element interactivity (e.g. science, mathematics with multiple interacting elements)
  • System-paced presentation, where the pace is set by the timing of the audio-visual materials

Constraints¶

  • The effect may disappear or reverse under self-paced conditions
  • Learner expertise must be considered, as effects reversed as expertise increased in Kalyuga et al. (2000)
  • Development of audio-visual materials carries additional cost that must be weighed against learning gains

Target Learners¶

  • novice students in primary, secondary, technical and adult education studying complex materials

Target Learning Goals¶

  • learning from graphics-based instructional materials accompanied by explanatory text

Affordances¶

  • Cognitive Load Theory Modality Account

Claims¶

  • Presentation pacing moderates the modality effect: strong under system-paced conditions, reversed under self-paced conditions [+M]
  • Graphics with spoken text outperform graphics with printed text for learning (overall modality effect, d = 0.72) [+M]
  • Within-subjects designs also show a moderate-to-large modality effect (d = 0.73) [+M]
  • Presenting words as spoken narration rather than on-screen text alongside graphics improves learning [+M]
  • Modality effect: presenting explanatory text in spoken rather than written form with visual information increases effective working memory capacity and learning [+M]
  • Modality effect sizes differ across fields of study, with social science effects negative and science effects largest [~M]
  • The modality effect is particularly strong for students learning through virtual reality media [~M]
