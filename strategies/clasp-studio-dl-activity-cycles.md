# Run introductory physics in studio-type discussion/laboratory sections where small student groups work through activity cycles of model-building, application, and whole-class discussion.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
CLASP's core instructional structure is studio-type discussion/laboratory (DL) sections of 25-35 students that meet 140 minutes twice weekly, where "students work together to understand a model and practice applying it before engaging in whole-class discussions." Lecture is limited to one 80-minute session per week and only reviews or previews DL topics; the DLs are "the main driver of the curriculum." The course features an explicit focus on understanding models (words, graphs, equations) and applying them to real-world physical, chemical, and biological situations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- DL sections taught by faculty or graduate TAs; exams and quizzes during lecture determine most of the grade; lecture does not introduce material not covered in DLs
#### Constraints
- At NU only the A course was CLASP-based; implementing CLASP required curricular reordering so content coverage in A and B was unchanged

### Target Learners
- bioscience and STEM undergraduates in introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- model-based qualitative and quantitative analysis of real-world situations
- active sense-making in physics
