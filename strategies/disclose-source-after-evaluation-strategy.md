# Train pre-service teachers' AI-related evaluative skills by comparing AI-generated and human feedback on the same artefact with sources disclosed only after initial evaluation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends programmes train pre-service teachers to evaluate feedback against explicit quality criteria rather than relying on source heuristics. It proposes that "Learners could compare AI-generated and human feedback on the same artefact, with the sources disclosed only after an initial evaluation," making source-related biases observable, combined with structured reflection and iterative feedback cycles. This responds to the finding that source beliefs shaped perceptions but not uptake.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires explicit quality criteria of the kind used in the study's coding manual, and structured reflection on reasons for trusting or rejecting a source
#### Constraints
- The article notes information alone may not suffice, citing evidence that explaining how an LLM produces output did not change teachers' evaluations of LLM-labelled feedback

### Target Learners
- pre-service teachers in teacher education programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- AI-related evaluative skills for assessing AI-generated feedback
- critical evaluation of feedback sources
