# Use the 1-year lag school-level science pretest as the covariate when planning science CRTs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
When planning a cluster randomized trial with science outcomes, the article recommends using the previous year's school-level science scores as the covariate. This covariate set has high explanatory power at the level of randomization and is practical: school-level scores "are likely available via a website or very quickly from a school administrator," whereas individual student scores are more time-consuming and costly to obtain. The article demonstrates the strategy in two worked MDES applications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the previous year's school-level science test scores for the schools in the planned study
- A two- or three-level CRT design with randomization at the school or district level
#### Constraints
- A student-level science pretest is typically unavailable because science is not tested annually in most states; where a 1-year lag student-level science pretest exists it explains more variance

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in science impact studies

### Target Learning Goals
- science achievement
