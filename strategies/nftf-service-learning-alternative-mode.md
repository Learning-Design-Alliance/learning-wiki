# Adopt nFTF service learning as a viable alternative mode that can extend service to remote or mobility-constrained populations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests that nFTF service learning "may be an alternative mode of service learning that can be used in the future, even after the pandemic." It "allows students to serve people living in remote villages without traveling as long as they can access the Internet" and "allows students to serve people with disabilities who have difficulty in commuting from their home to the service site." The findings support the feasibility and effectiveness of this mode.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Internet access for students and service recipients
- professional teacher training for online delivery
- adjusting teaching content and activities for effective online delivery
- providing adequate support from teachers and teaching assistants
- preparing contingency plans for technical problems
#### Constraints
- nFTF service learning evokes new challenges such as low effectiveness of communication and unsatisfactory online curriculum

### Target Learners
- university students

### Target Learning Goals
- service leadership qualities
- positive youth development competencies
- life satisfaction
