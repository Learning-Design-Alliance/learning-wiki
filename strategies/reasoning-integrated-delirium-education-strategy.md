# Delirium education for ICU nurses should integrate clinical reasoning training — case-based practice, simulation and reflective thinking — rather than lecture-style specialty training alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the mediating role of clinical reasoning competency, the authors recommend that delirium training "should not be limited to traditional approaches, such as speciality training and fixed theoretical knowledge, but should develop implementable innovative teaching strategies to exercise clinical reasoning competency", naming clinical case-based practices and brainstorming as priorities. They also recommend simulation-based reasoning training in low-risk environments and case vignettes to test staged training effectiveness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured, planned and mutually responsive teaching of theory and clinical reasoning practice to bridge the gap between theory and practice
- Assessment of nurses' self-efficacy levels with reasonable tools to tailor interventions
#### Constraints
- Recommended on the basis of a cross-sectional study; the article notes current group-based specialty training in China is "less practical and interactive"

### Target Learners
- ICU nurses, particularly junior nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical reasoning competency
- Delirium recognition and management
- Self-efficacy for delirium care

### Affordances
- Imb Skills Model Delirium Care Framework
