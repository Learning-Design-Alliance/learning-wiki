Administer intercultural sensitivity development training by integrating it with the scientific research methods course¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that intercultural sensitivity development training be delivered by integrating it with the gains of the scientific research methods course, arguing that because "the scientific research methods course is present in all graduate programs, it can be argued that it would be an effective way to provide students with intercultural sensitivity, too." The integration used research-review activities on different cultures mapped onto course outcomes such as problem statements, variables, document review, interviews, surveys, and validity and reliability discussion.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Course outcomes to which culture-focused activities can be mapped (e.g., interview and survey outcomes)
  • Use of the teaching via research-review strategy in both integrated and standard delivery, as done in the study

Constraints¶

  • The study is limited to activities that can be integrated with the scientific research methods course outcomes and to the emotional dimension of intercultural competence

Target Learners¶

  • second-year pre-service English teachers
  • graduate students taking scientific research methods

Target Learning Goals¶

  • intercultural sensitivity
  • scientific attitudes and behaviors such as open-mindedness and viewing events without prejudice

Affordances¶

  • Fogarty Nested Integration Model

Claims¶

  • In a quasi-experiment with pretest-posttest and control group, intercultural sensitivity development activities integrated with a scientific research methods course produced a significantly greater pretest-posttest change in intercultural sensitivity than the standard course [+W]
  • After the integrated activities, experimental-group students shifted from agreeing to disagreeing with items such as getting upset easily with people from different cultures and thinking their culture is better than other cultures [+W]
  • Descriptively, the experimental group's posttest intercultural sensitivity mean (99.20) was higher than the control group's (89.27), with both groups starting at moderate levels [+W]
  • At the beginning of the semester, experimental and control groups did not differ significantly in intercultural sensitivity scale scores [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use experiential intercultural training methods such as simulations, role-playing and critical incidents to encounter perceptual and value differences
  • Intercultural programming to enhance socio-emotional engagement for probation students
