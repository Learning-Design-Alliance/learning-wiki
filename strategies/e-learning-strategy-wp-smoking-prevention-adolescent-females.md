# Use e-learning-based interventions as an educational strategy for reducing water pipe smoking in adolescent females

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends e-learning-based interventions for water pipe smoking prevention in adolescent females, concluding that "The use of e-learning-based interventions is an educational strategy for reducing WP smoking in adolescent females." The authors emphasize cost-effectiveness and feasibility, noting e-learning "overcomes some of the barriers to face-to-face education and provides more flexibility for learning" by allowing access to training sessions at any time and place.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An e-learning management system platform with personal participant pages, uploaded videos and booklet materials, and session-gating questions
#### Constraints
- The evidence comes from female adolescents in schools with high WP prevalence in western Iran; the authors also recommend policymakers reduce WP access for those under 18

### Target Learners
- adolescent females

### Target Learning Goals
- prevention of water pipe smoking
