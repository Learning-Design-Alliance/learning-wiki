# Use a mobile inflatable planetarium plus a portable telescope as a first contact with science for students who cannot visit fixed facilities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy brings a mobile inflatable planetarium, which the article says allows showing "in minutes, all the movements of the stars during a full night", directly into schools, followed where conditions permit by an observational night with a portable telescope. It is aimed at teachers who need to attract young students to science and at students for whom travel to a fixed planetarium or large telescope is difficult.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mobile inflatable planetarium and a portable telescope, plus volunteers to help execute the visits, as acknowledged by the author
#### Constraints
- The observational night requires a good, dark night without clouds or artificial lights, which limited the project to only one such night

### Target Learners
- school students of all ages, from basic levels to high school, in small-town Brazil

### Target Learning Goals
- first contact with science and observational astronomy, including stars, constellations, planets and relative positions in the sky
