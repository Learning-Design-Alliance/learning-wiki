# Use a hybrid analytical-computational strategy based on superposition to let students explore magnetic fields of realistic systems at all points in space

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends combining purely mathematical and physical methods with computational tools so that students can explore complex current-carrying systems beyond textbook exercises. The authors state that this combination 'encourages students to use computational tools and to create their own systems', which they describe as motivating mainly for engineering students, and that it 'contributes to the scientific training of future physicists and engineers' facing situations beyond those usually proposed in textbooks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators must design and implement computational resources of easy access, which the authors note requires more time to integrate the strategy
#### Constraints
- The methodology applies to systems that can be expressed in terms of n wires

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics and engineering students in electromagnetism courses

### Target Learning Goals
- calculate and visualize magnetic fields of realistic current-carrying systems at all points in space
- develop computational and analytical skills in electromagnetism
