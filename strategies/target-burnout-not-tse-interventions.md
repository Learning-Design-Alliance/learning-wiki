# Focus teacher interventions on preventing and ameliorating burnout symptoms rather than raising TSE levels

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because burnout dimensions consistently predicted future TSE while TSE did not consistently predict future burnout, the article recommends that interventions aiming to support teacher retention and well-being target burnout. It states that "strategies may need to focus on preventing and/or ameliorating the burnout symptoms rather than raising TSE levels," warning that deploying strategies to increase TSE may have minimal effects. Suggested multi-level approaches include cognitive emotion regulation strategies for teachers, school-level factors such as collegial leadership and preparedness, and professional knowledge and classroom management training in teacher education.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should address factors associated with burnout, especially when teachers are showing or beginning to show symptoms of burnout
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on longitudinal evidence from a Croatian teacher sample; the authors call for replication in other cultures

### Target Learners
- pre-service and in-service teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- teacher well-being, burnout prevention, teacher retention
