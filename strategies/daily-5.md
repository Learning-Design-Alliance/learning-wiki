# Daily 5

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Daily 5 (Boushey & Moser, 2006) is a structure for organizing literacy blocks in elementary classrooms. Students cycle through five independent tasks — Read to Self, Read to Someone, Listen to Reading, Work on Writing, and Word Work — while the teacher confers with individuals or leads small guided-reading groups. The framework pairs these task rotations with explicit training in [independent working behaviors](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) and student [choice](../elements/choice-boards.md) about which tasks to do and in what order.

## Design Implications

Daily 5 converts the literacy block from whole-class seatwork into structured independent practice, maximizing time-on-task with text while freeing the teacher for differentiated instruction. Its core mechanism is behavioral and motivational: short, explicitly modeled routines build the stamina and self-management that sustained independent reading requires [~M], and student choice within a fixed task set supports autonomy and engagement [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]. The volume of reading it generates matters: time spent actually reading is one of the strongest predictors of reading growth, particularly for developing readers [~S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit, repeated modeling and practice of each task's routines before students work independently (a [demonstration](../elements/think-aloud.md)-based launch phase, typically 10 days per task)
- A sufficient classroom library with books matched to students' independent reading levels
- A predictable schedule with visual tracking of task choices ([choice boards](../elements/choice-boards.md) or check-in charts)
- Short teacher-led small-group or conferring blocks that run concurrently with rotations

#### Constraints
- Effectiveness collapses without the upfront routine training; rotations launched before behaviors are internalized produce off-task time rather than practice [-M]
- Independent reading at the wrong difficulty level yields little growth — text must sit within students' instructional or independent range [~S]
- The fixed five-task menu can crowd out teacher-directed phonics for students who need systematic code instruction; Word Work must be deliberately aligned to a scope and sequence, not left as generic spelling practice [-M]
- Rotations consume substantial instructional minutes in transitions; in short blocks, fewer, longer rotations outperform five brief ones [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Daily 3 / Daily 2**: subsets of the five tasks, common in upper grades where writing and reading dominate
- **Math Daily 3**: the same rotation structure applied to mathematics (Math by Myself, Math with Someone, Math Writing)
- **CAFE integration**: pairing rotations with the CAFE conferring system (Comprehension, Accuracy, Fluency, Expand vocabulary) for goal-setting during teacher conferences
- **Fixed vs. free rotation**: teacher-assigned order for students needing structure; free choice for students with established stamina

### Target Learners
- K–5 students, especially emergent and developing readers who need high volumes of accessible text [~S]
- Students developing [self-regulation](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md); the stamina-building launch sequence explicitly trains sustained attention [~M]
- Multilingual learners benefit from Listen to Reading and Read to Someone, which add oral language models [+W]
- Less suitable as-is for students with significant decoding gaps, who need more teacher-led explicit code instruction than the rotation model provides [-M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading fluency and stamina through daily sustained reading practice
- Word recognition and spelling through structured Word Work [Automatic word recognition frees resources for comprehension.](../claims/automatic-word-recognition-frees-resources-for-comprehension.md) [+S]
- Writing fluency through frequent low-stakes composing
- Self-regulated learning behaviors: goal-setting, independence, and task management

### Instructions
1. **Launch Read to Self first.** Model correct and incorrect behaviors ([demonstration](../elements/think-aloud.md)), build stamina in 3-minute increments, and chart progress daily until students sustain 15–20 minutes.
2. **Introduce remaining tasks one at a time**, each with its own stamina-building cycle, before running combined rotations.
3. **Establish choice structures.** Use a [check-in](../elements/check-in.md) chart or [choice boards](../elements/choice-boards.md) so students select task order while the teacher sets minimums (e.g., Read to Self daily).
4. **Run small-group instruction concurrently.** Pull guided-reading or strategy groups during rotations; keep groups short and targeted.
5. **Confere individually.** Set one goal per student (CAFE or equivalent), teach to it, and record progress for [assessment for learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S].
6. **Align Word Work and Work on Writing** to your phonics and writing scope and sequence so independent tasks reinforce, rather than diverge from, taught content.
