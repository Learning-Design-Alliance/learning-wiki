Use weekly formative quizzes during EFL listening instruction to support achievement and reduce anxiety¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teachers embed frequent low-stakes testing in listening courses rather than relying on a single summative exam. It reports that teachers using instructional formative assessment "report that their students retain more information, understand concepts more rapidly and are more enthusiastic about what they are learning", and its own experiment assigned one quiz per week across a two-month course. The strategy is presented as building confidence and relieving anxiety through continual positive feedback.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Quizzes administered weekly during the term
  • Same instructors and listening materials across groups
  • Feedback given while learning is occurring

Constraints¶

  • Evidence comes from one quasi-experimental study with 60 learners in a single Iranian language institute; the article reports no effect size

Target Learners¶

  • Iranian university-age EFL learners (ages 19-25) in language institutes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Listening efficacy
  • Anxiety reduction
  • Achievement on final listening tests

Affordances¶

  • Formative Assessment Continuous Monitoring Feedback

Claims¶

  • The experimental group's mean listening score rose from 51.56 on the pre-test to 69.86 on the post-test [+W]
  • Formative assessment during listening instruction produced a significantly higher post-test score than traditional instruction in Iranian EFL learners [+W]
  • The formative-assessment group improved significantly from pre-test to post-test while the control group did not [+W]
