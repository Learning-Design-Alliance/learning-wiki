Use active, collaborative problem-solving methods with concrete teaching aids rather than lecture-only instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), q1–q2 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teachers replace teacher-centred presentation with active methods in which students face problems, think about solutions, and solve them with the teacher's help, using concrete aids. In the study's active condition, "teaching aids such as coloured balls and clothing were used", and students built bracelets from counters to grasp circular versus gyrate permutation before returning to the formula. The article states "it is suggested that teachers use active and collaborative methods, in order to solve maths problems."

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need virtuosity and command over the lesson, which the article says the success of this method depends on

Constraints¶

  • The active group's advantage was demonstrated only for second-grade high school students learning combinational mathematics in this quasi-experiment

Target Learners¶

  • second-grade high school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • creative thinking in mathematical problem solving
  • combinational mathematics concepts

Claims¶

  • Three mathematics teacher educators all foregrounded engagement-focused teaching-and-learning activities, variously hands-on problem-solving and group work, collaboration, and student demonstrations with class discussion [+W]
  • Active training improved problem-solving performance, while traditional and heuristic methods did not differ from each other [+W]
