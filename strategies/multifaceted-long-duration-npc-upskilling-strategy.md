# Use a multifaceted long-duration training package combining classroom, mentorship, on-the-job support, leadership and audit for up-skilling non-physician clinicians

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose their combined training model as a way to build clinical service improvement capacity in low-resource settings. The package mixes "1-week lecture courses, emergency simulations, one-to-one clinical on-the-job training and leadership training" over 30 months, plus audit projects, with emphasis on "problem solving through leadership, audit and service improvement". Trainees used leadership skills to change team dynamics and resource use, and the authors suggest "this multifaceted educational approach to change clinical practice is a powerful and novel way of reducing the burden of maternal and neonatal mortality in sub-Saharan Africa".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supported specialist obstetricians available to work alongside trainees in their districts
- Accreditation to raise the status and quality of the educational experience
#### Constraints
- Impact on national maternal and neonatal mortality rates may be difficult to see from a small cohort; mortality effects await trial results

### Target Learners
- Non-physician clinicians in low-resource settings in sub-Saharan Africa

### Target Learning Goals
- Emergency obstetric and neonatal clinical skills
- Leadership and clinical service improvement
