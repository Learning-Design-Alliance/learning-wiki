# Incorporate empathic nonverbal communication training into medical education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that clinicians should be trained in the nonverbal behaviors that communicate empathy, such as eye contact, open posture, touch, and concerned facial expression, because these behaviors increased patient perceptions of empathy, warmth, and competence in its experiment. It states: "incorporate empathic nonverbal training into medical education", noting that empathy training meta-analytically can be successful and that one program emphasizing nonverbal behavior increased patient ratings of clinician empathy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must target the specific nonverbal behaviors the literature implicates in communicating empathy, as the article's empathic condition did
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence comes from an online analog design using still photographs rather than video or live encounters, and the six behaviors were clustered so individual contributions are unknown

### Target Learners
- medical students
- resident physicians
- clinicians

### Target Learning Goals
- nonverbal communication of empathy
- patient-clinician rapport
- patient perceptions of warmth and competence
