# Integrate clip uploading into hands-on teaching sessions to address low portfolio completion

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
After observing that only one participant completed the image portfolio, the authors changed the program so that "uploading of scans as a standard practice during hands-on teaching sessions" was included to address this barrier. The strategy embeds portfolio-building into supervised bedside sessions rather than leaving it as an independent voluntary task, so clips are captured and uploaded while the learner is already scanning with an expert present.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Hands-on teaching sessions with bedside access to patients
- An upload-capable ultrasound device and secure server for anonymized clips
#### Constraints
- The article attributes low portfolio completion to time required to practice, a knowledge gap in how to upload clips, and lack of incentive, and has not reported outcomes of this change

### Target Learners
- Hospitalist faculty in a longitudinal POCUS program

### Target Learning Goals
- Image acquisition skill maintenance
- Portfolio completion meeting SHM national standards

## Claims

- [Passing the one-year hands-on test correlated with number of clip uploads (r = 0.85), attendance at hands-on teaching sessions (r = 0.7), and attendance at monthly conferences (r = 0.5)](../claims/iscan-clip-uploads-hands-on-attendance-correlate-success.md) [+M]
