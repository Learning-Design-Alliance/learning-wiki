Increase Multiple Intelligence Theory-based activities in elementary school mathematics curricula¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Based on their finding that MI-based teaching produced higher achievement on geometric objects, the authors recommend "that the number of activities prepared via the theory of multiple intelligence in elementary school mathematics curricula be increased". They also recommend establishing learning environments based on the theory that "enable individualization of students and realization of active learning". The recommendation is grounded in their own experimental comparison favoring the MI curriculum.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Activities must be prepared via the theory of multiple intelligence within elementary mathematics curricula

Constraints¶

  • Evidence base is a single 60-student quasi-experiment on one geometry unit in second grade

Target Learners¶

  • elementary school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • geometry knowledge
  • mathematics achievement

Claims¶

  • Within the experimental group, achievement scores rose significantly from pre-test to post-test after Multiple Intelligence Theory-based instruction [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Develop lesson plans covering all learning objectives across various intelligences
  • Assign intelligence-matched art activities to student groups identified by an MI inventory
  • Plan multiple intelligence implementations for longer periods, since effects grow with implementation duration
