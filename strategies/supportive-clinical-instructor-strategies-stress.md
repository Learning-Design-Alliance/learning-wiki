# Supportive clinical-instructor strategies to reduce midwifery students' stress

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that instructor-related factors caused the most student stress, the authors recommend that clinical instructors provide support through open communication and that school managers act on instructor capacity. They state that "the use of experienced instructors, the development of communication skills of the instructors, increasing coping skills of the students and the creation of a supportive environment may be helpful." Instructors are also advised to increase students' awareness of stressors and avoid public admonition, which the article links to damaged self-confidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- School managers willing to assess student stress during clinical learning and to employ experienced instructors and improve instructors' communication skills
#### Constraints
- Recommended for clinical learning environments in cultures similar to the Iranian setting described; the underlying study is a small single-university cross-sectional survey

### Target Learners
- undergraduate midwifery students in clinical placements

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing stress in clinical learning
- supporting professional skill development in maternity settings
