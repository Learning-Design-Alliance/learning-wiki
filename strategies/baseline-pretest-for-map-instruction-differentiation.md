# Use a baseline 50-states pretest to inform differentiated geography instruction, and do not rely on CMT spatial-reasoning scores for placement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends giving students a fill-in-the-blank baseline test on the 50 states before direct map instruction, because baseline scores proved a strong predictor of performance on the computer-based games. It explicitly advises that "the implementation of baseline tests throughout my curriculum is recommended," while CMT math scores "are not recommended for application in the future" for geography tasks. The strategy supports matching game difficulty to varied map-literacy skill levels.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A baseline examination on the 50 states administered before instruction, as the article's fill-in-the-blank map test was
#### Constraints
- The article states CMT math spatial-reasoning scores did not translate well to the geography tasks performed in this study

### Target Learners
- middle school social studies students, including students with disabilities

### Target Learning Goals
- knowledge of the names, shapes, and locations of the 50 U.S. states
