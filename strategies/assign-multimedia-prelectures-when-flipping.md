# Flip the classroom by assigning pre-recorded multimedia lectures rather than textbook readings as the pre-class material

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors moving lectures outside the classroom assign pre-recorded multimedia lectures instead of textbook readings, because readings are a less efficient way for novices to meet concepts for the first time. It argues that replacing chalk-and-talk with assigned readings "results in a less efficient tradeoff for the student" and suggests instructors "move the traditional lecture outside the classroom by assigning students pre-recorded multimedia lectures that better replicate the chalk-and-talk experience". This prepares students for more effective active learning in class.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multimedia pre-lectures must be short, visually intensive modules covering the key concepts before class
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that results "do not suggest that instructors should stop using textbooks", which serve as references and convey real-world applications; the recommendation is stated for introductory courses

### Target Learners
- students in introductory economics courses, particularly those with no prior economics exposure

### Target Learning Goals
- pre-class preparation and first exposure to key concepts in flipped classrooms
