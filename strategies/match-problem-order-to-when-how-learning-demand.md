# Match problem order to the dominant learning demand: blocked orders for how-learning domains, interleaved orders for when-learning domains

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's design recommendation is to choose problem order according to which learning component the domain demands most. In domains where the how-part of skills is the harder learning problem, the authors advise starting with more blocked orders; where rigorous when-learning is required, they advise interleaved-ordering problems. This follows from their simulation finding that interleaving supplies more negative-feedback opportunities that sharpen skill preconditions, while blocking concentrates same-type positive examples that support acquiring operator sequences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of whether the target domain's learning challenge lies mainly in when-part (skill application conditions) or how-part (operator sequence) acquisition
#### Constraints
- Recommendation is grounded in a simulation study with SimStudent; the authors state future studies on human subjects are needed to validate the hypothesis
- Results were obtained in well-defined domains with explicitly represented problem content and a known set of solution rules

### Target Learners
- Students learning math and science problem-solving skills, such as equation solving, stoichiometry, and fraction addition

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition of both when to apply a skill and how to apply a skill in problem solving
