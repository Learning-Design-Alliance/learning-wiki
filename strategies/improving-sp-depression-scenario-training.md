# Improving SP depression-scenario training through countertransference discussion, SP self-evaluation, and video supplements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that the depression/suicidal tendencies roleplay was rated worst, the authors recommend bringing "the topic of countertransference into the communication training", collecting SP evaluation data from a larger teacher sample, interviewing SPs about playing a depressive patient, interviewing students about their feelings, and supplementing students' lack of clinical experience with "appropriate video clips of real patients and a corresponding discussion forum". They also suggest analysing the role scripts and using Stanislawski's Method.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Further research and evaluation data from other locations, since there is very little literature on SP roleplay evaluation in psychiatry
#### Constraints
- The recommendations respond to a small-effect finding (d = .202/.225) whose cause the authors say is not yet established; the mixed model was not calculated for teachers because of sample size

### Target Learners
- fourth-year medical students
- standardized patient actors
- course instructors

### Target Learning Goals
- communication skills in psychiatric exploration
- dealing with feelings toward depressive patients
