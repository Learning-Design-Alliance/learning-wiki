# Require students to arrive with their own reasoning, expose it to AI challenge, and account for what changed

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A design recommendation from the article's discussion: merely asking students to use AI critically is insufficient instructional design. Instead, the article argues, "An assignment must require students to arrive with their own reasoning, expose that reasoning to challenge, and account for what changed." Otherwise a polished answer or quick verification remains a sufficient response. This strategy operationalizes the NLTB's three stages at the assignment level and makes Path 1 and Path 2 responses non-compliant by design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assignments with staged structure: independent reasoning documented before AI contact, dialogic challenge protocols, and a reflective synthesis deliverable
#### Constraints
- The article notes this rests on pilot evidence; a formal comparison of scaffolded and unscaffolded conditions is the immediate priority for testing the design claim

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students using generative AI in coursework

### Target Learning Goals
- higher-order thinking
- critical evaluation of AI output
