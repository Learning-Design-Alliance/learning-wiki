# Gradual introduction of board-game-based learning to support inexperienced teachers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a set of good practices for introducing modern board-game-based learning in classrooms, because teachers without previous experience of games reported stress and insecurity at the start of the programme. The recommended practices are: "preparing the students by introducing the games with videos of how to play them; ii) reading the instructions in groups; iii) resolving doubts with teachers in the classroom; iv) giving both teachers and students advance notice of what game will be played the next day." Future studies will require a more gradual introduction of the approach.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers receive advance preparation, including a programme manual and how-to-play videos
#### Constraints
- Teachers without any previous experience of games reported a degree of stress and insecurity at the start of the programme

### Target Learners
- primary school students in first to sixth grade

### Target Learning Goals
- executive functions
- academic skills
