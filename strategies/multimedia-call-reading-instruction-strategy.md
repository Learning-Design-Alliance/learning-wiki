# Use multimedia CALL software with pictures, audio and dictionaries to support L2 reading instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that foreign language syllabus designers, curriculum planners and language instructors incorporate computer-assisted language learning into reading instruction. The treatment combined picture-supported texts, read-aloud audio, one-click dictionary lookup and motion pictures, and the article states the findings "carry important implications for foreign language syllabus designers, curriculum planners, and language instructors".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to multimedia reading software and training time for learners to become familiar with it, as in the study's dedicated training session
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one quasi-experiment with intermediate female learners in an Iranian EFL context; the article does not claim generalization beyond this setting

### Target Learners
- Intermediate EFL learners in classroom or institute settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension development in a foreign language
