# Use simulation-mannequin training for the initial stages of TEE training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend simulation-based TEE training for beginners acquiring psychomotor skills and echo-anatomy knowledge, concluding that "Simulation based TEE training provides more effective training than other teaching methods" and is "especially suitable for the initial stages of TEE training." In the trial this meant a 2-hour theoretical tutorial followed by two 1-hour practical sessions with a 1:1 instructor-to-student ratio on the mannequin, which outperformed e-learning and operating-room teaching on both exams.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- One-on-one instructor-led sessions on a mannequin-based TEE simulator
- A preceding theoretical tutorial covering anatomy, physics, and the 11 basic views
#### Constraints
- Hands-on bedside practice remains necessary, since image acquisition skills can only be gained at the bedside and require repetitive exposure
- Operating-room teaching is constrained by limited cases, faculty availability, and patient-safety risk for beginners

### Target Learners
- Anesthesia and intensive care residents beginning TEE training

### Target Learning Goals
- Psychomotor skills and knowledge of echo-anatomy for the basic perioperative TEE examination
