# Combine a theoretical empathy session with an immersive aging simulation suit experience in long-term care staff training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adding an experiential aging simulation component to didactic empathy training for long-term care professionals. The session combined one hour of theoretical education on empathy and aging with a one-hour standardized GERT suit activity performing daily nursing home tasks. The authors state these findings "support the use of immersive simulation as a complementary strategy to traditional didactic approaches in empathy training for long-term care professionals."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trained researcher delivers a standardized theoretical session on aging, functional limitations, and empathy alongside the supervised suit activity
#### Constraints
- Findings are limited to short-term, self-reported measures; no behavioral or patient outcome data were collected; durability remains uncertain

### Target Learners
- healthcare professionals in long-term care (nurses, nursing assistants, therapists, doctors, psychologists)

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical empathy, particularly perspective taking and compassionate care toward older adults
