# Enhance simulation-based methods with detailed visual materials and real-life examples to teach complex observational skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
A design recommendation arising from residual skill gaps: 'simulation-based methods should be further enhanced, particularly through the inclusion of detailed visual materials and real-life examples, to effectively teach more complex observational skills'. The article ties this to visual assessment items that stayed inadequate after training, and its limitations section also suggests repetitive training at regular intervals, digital tools for reviewing materials, and community-based programs so women can reinforce and disseminate skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation sessions must add detailed visual materials and real-life examples targeting complex observational skills.
#### Constraints
- Aimed at observational skills that remained inadequate for 61% of intervention participants on checklist item 3 even after training.

### Target Learners
- Refugee women learning breast self-examination
- Participants with low health literacy

### Target Learning Goals
- Complex visual assessment skills in breast self-examination
- Retention and reinforcement of learned skills over time
