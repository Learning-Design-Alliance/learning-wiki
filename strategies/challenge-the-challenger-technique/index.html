Challenge the challenger: students create group quiz questions and challenge the opposing group to answer them¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A teaching technique, common in corporate workshops and adapted here for adult students, in which students "take up the challenge to create group quiz questions and then challenge course mates from the opposing team to answer those questions". Lecturers mediate responses, refine questions throughout, and act as moderators structuring the information and network system. It was implemented in tutorial classes a week after students were told of the activity.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students work in groups with lecturer facilitation; the self-access learning concept with group-member cooperation is channelled to the group

Constraints¶

  • Evaluated only for understanding of the four variable measurements; the authors suggest next testing effectiveness for application and problem solving

Target Learners¶

  • higher education communication undergraduates

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding of nominal, ordinal, interval and ratio variable measurements in quantitative research

Claims¶

  • Both 'group quiz' and 'challenge the challenger' interventions significantly improved students' subjective-section understanding of variable measurements [+W]
  • 'Challenge the challenger' was more effective than 'group quiz' for the subjective section, but not for the objective section [+W]
  • Students gave positive feedback on both non-conventional activities, reporting greater interest, understanding and proactivity [+W]
