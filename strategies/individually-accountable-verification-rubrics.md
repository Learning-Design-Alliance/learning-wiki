# Make output verification an individually accountable practice within team assignments via rubric requirements

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Because test-based verification collapsed in team settings and never fully recovered, the article recommends that "Assessment rubrics that explicitly require evidence of output verification, such as attached test logs, documented debugging processes, or reflection on what was checked and how, would make this practice visible and accountable in ways that current team assignments do not." This addresses the diffusion-of-responsibility mechanism the authors propose.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Rubrics must specify acceptable evidence of verification, such as test logs, documented debugging processes, or reflection on what was checked.
#### Constraints
- The article notes the direction of self-report bias, if present, would tend to attenuate observed differences, suggesting the true verification shift may be larger.

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate computing students working on team-based projects

### Target Learning Goals
- Rigorous verification of AI-generated code, especially test-based verification
