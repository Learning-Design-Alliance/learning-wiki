# Sequence tutor style within PBL: begin facilitative, shift to non-facilitative as students gain PBL experience

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend modifying the tutor style during PBL training rather than fixing one style throughout. In their conclusion they state that "Initially, the facilitative style may be more suitable, but the non-facilitative tutoring style should be introduced when students have gained sufficient knowledge in PBL", which they believe would be of optimal long-term benefit to students and tutors. This matches student statements wishing for a facilitative tutor for early cases who then "becomes increasingly non-facilitative".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tutors able to perform both styles reliably, supported by Train-the-Teacher preparation
#### Constraints
- Concluded within the limitations of a hybrid dental curriculum; the non-facilitative style was reported as not appropriate for PBL beginners

### Target Learners
- undergraduate dental students new to PBL

### Target Learning Goals
- self-directed learning and clinical reasoning in PBL
