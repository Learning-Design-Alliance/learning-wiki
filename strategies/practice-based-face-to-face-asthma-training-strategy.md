# Strengthen primary-care asthma management through practice-based face-to-face training with pulmonologist involvement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends expanding post-graduation asthma education and building practice-based training programs for primary care physicians. It states that "it is necessary to increase post-graduation asthma education programs, identify barriers to guideline reading habits, and develop guidelines for primary care physicians," and that strengthening asthma management through "a practice-based training program, with the involvement of pulmonology specialists" is promising. It also proposes awareness education on SABA overuse, investigating barriers to asthma control test use, and multidisciplinary collaboration, training, and feedback mechanisms integrated with pulmonologists.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training should be face-to-face and practice-based, the format preferred by 64.9% of surveyed physicians
- Involvement of pulmonology specialists to support and train primary care physicians
#### Constraints
- The study is cross-sectional and self-reported, so it does not establish that such training improves clinical outcomes; the article states the design does not permit cause-and-effect conclusions

### Target Learners
- Family physicians and general practitioners in primary care
- Family medicine resident physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- Asthma diagnosis, treatment, and follow-up according to GINA recommendations
- Guideline adherence and appropriate use of asthma control assessment tools
