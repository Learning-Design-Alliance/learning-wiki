# School and community sports programs that maintain healthy weight may support children's academic performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors recommend that schools and community settings promote weight optimization, writing that "a program that aims to maintain healthy weight in schools and/or community sports club settings may contribute to an enhanced academic performance." The recommendation rests on their 2-year longitudinal finding that favorable weight change (weight loss in children with overweight/obesity, weight gain in children with underweight) was associated with improved grade points, and the authors add that "Dissemination of this study's ﬁndings is particularly important for educators and policymakers."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must aim to move children toward a healthy weight status (weight loss for overweight/obesity, weight gain for underweight) rather than change weight in a single direction for all children
#### Constraints
- The supporting evidence is an observational longitudinal association, not an intervention trial; mechanisms remain unclear, and low BMI may reflect lean build or slower maturation, which the study could not disentangle

### Target Learners
- Japanese junior high school students aged 12–13 years

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance across five subjects (Japanese, Mathematics, Social studies, Sciences, English)
