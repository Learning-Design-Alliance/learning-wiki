# Use low-cost finger tracking on a suturing simulator for independent, expert-free training and objective self-assessment of open suturing skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a simulation setting combining a basic open suturing model with finger motion tracking so trainees can practise and assess skills without an expert observer. Because the SurgTrac application "can be downloaded to any smartphone or tablet", the setup is described as accessible in virtually every country and usable at home or work, supporting self-directed continuous training and skill maintenance when clinical exposure is inconsistent.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mobile device running the SurgTrac application, simple materials such as gloves and balloon-tube tags, and a suturing simulator
#### Constraints
- The parameters shown valid assess technical aspects only and cannot be extrapolated to quality aspects such as firmness of the knot or distance between sutures; a pass-fail cut-off still needs to be established

### Target Learners
- medical students
- surgical residents
- doctors maintaining surgical skills

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition and maintenance of basic open suturing skills
- independent objective self-assessment
