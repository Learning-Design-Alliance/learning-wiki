# Peer Feedback Protocols

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Peer feedback protocols are structured routines — such as gallery walks, two-stars-and-a-wish, critical friends protocols, or rubric-guided peer review — that govern how learners exchange feedback on one another's work. The protocol specifies who reviews what, against which criteria, in what sequence, and with what language, converting the informal act of "giving feedback" into a teachable, repeatable procedure.

## Design Implications

Peer feedback works when it functions as [assessment for learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md): feedback that names the gap between current and target performance and suggests how to close it improves achievement [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Structured protocols matter because unstructured peer comments tend to be vague ("good job") or purely evaluative; explicit criteria and sentence frames push feedback toward specificity and actionability. Receiving structured peer feedback produces learning gains comparable to instructor feedback in many contexts [Peer feedback can match teacher feedback in improving writing quality.](../claims/collaborative-writing-improves-text-quality.md) [+M], and the act of *giving* feedback — critiquing work against criteria — is often as beneficial as receiving it, because it forces evaluative judgment [Peer assessment interventions improve academic performance.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear success criteria or a rubric learners have been taught to apply ([Assessment](../elements/assessment.md))
- A staged protocol (e.g., describe → question → suggest) with sentence stems or prompts
- Training and rehearsal in giving feedback before it counts — feedback quality improves with explicit instruction in critique
- Time and structure for authors to act on the feedback, not just receive it

#### Constraints
- Feedback quality degrades when reviewers lack domain knowledge; novices may endorse flawed work or misidentify problems [~M] — pairing peer review with instructor moderation or exemplar calibration reduces this
- Unstructured or purely social peer exchange produces negligible gains and can entrench errors [-M]
- If feedback is graded or tied to social standing, reviewers inflate ratings and authors dismiss criticism [-M]; anonymity or low-stakes framing mitigates this
- Protocols applied ritually, without follow-up revision time, become compliance theater with no learning benefit [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Calibrated review:** learners rate sample artifacts and compare their judgments to expert ratings before reviewing peers (used by [Peergrade](https://www.peergrade.io) and [Calibrated Peer Review](https://calibratedpeerreview.org))
- **Reciprocal pairs vs. rotating groups:** stable pairs build trust; rotation widens exposure to varied work
- **Written vs. oral protocols:** oral formats (critical friends tuning protocols) suit complex, in-progress work; written formats suit drafts and asynchronous settings
- **Anonymous vs. attributed:** anonymity increases candor; attribution builds accountability and dialogue

### Target Learners
- Secondary and higher-education learners with enough metacognitive maturity to critique work against criteria [~M]
- Developing writers and designers, who benefit from seeing how peers interpreted the same task [Peer feedback can match teacher feedback in improving writing quality.](../claims/collaborative-writing-improves-text-quality.md) [+M]
- Less effective for very young learners without scaffolded sentence frames and teacher moderation [-W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Quality improvement of drafts and products (writing, design, code, arguments)
- Evaluative judgment: internalizing criteria so learners can assess their own work [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+M]
- Communication and [argumentation](../elements/argumentation.md) skills through justified critique

### Instructions
1. Establish success criteria with learners using a rubric or exemplars; model applying the criteria to an anonymous sample work.
2. Train the protocol: rehearse sentence stems ("I noticed…", "Have you considered…", "A stronger version would…") on low-stakes samples ([Articulation](../elements/articulation.md)).
3. Pair or group learners and run the staged review — describe the work, ask questions, offer suggestions — with each reviewer producing specific, criterion-referenced comments.
4. Give authors structured time to interpret and act on feedback through revision; treat revision, not the feedback itself, as the deliverable.
5. Debrief the feedback quality itself: which comments were actionable and why, building evaluative judgment over successive rounds.

## Examples
- **Critical Friends Groups tuning protocols** ([National School Reform Faculty](https://www.nsrfharmony.org)) — staged presentation, clarifying questions, and warm/cool feedback on work in progress.
- **Two Stars and a Wish** — primary-classroom protocol: two specific strengths plus one suggestion, constraining young reviewers to balanced, concrete comments.
- **Calibrated Peer Review (CPR)** ([https://calibratedpeerreview.org](https://calibratedpeerreview.org)) — learners calibrate against expert-scored samples before reviewing peers, addressing the novice-reviewer constraint.
- **Peergrade / PeerStudio** — platforms that anonymize submissions, distribute reviews, and flag low-quality feedback for instructor moderation.
