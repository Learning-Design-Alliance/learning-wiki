# TAs should proactively initiate brief interactions with many students during lab work periods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' recommendation for TA professional development: because TA-initiated interaction frequency correlated with engagement, which in turn correlated with learning, "TAs should proactively engage with many students". Brief check-ins may suffice, since interaction length did not affect engagement in this course; short exchanges may open the door for student questions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- TAs act as the sole instructors with full control of their lab sections and circulate during the student working period
#### Constraints
- The supporting correlations are observational, not causal, and draw on a single observed lab session in one inquiry-based course

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate physics students in large-enrollment lab sections

### Target Learning Goals
- staying on task during inquiry-based lab work
- scientific and data analysis skills
