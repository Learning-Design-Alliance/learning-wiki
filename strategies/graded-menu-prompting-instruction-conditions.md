# Graded menu of four instructional conditions for teaching learning-oriented prompting, chosen by available time and desired durability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article offers instructors a graded set of four options occupying ascending ICAP engagement levels: a two-sentence reminder (under one minute), scenario-based worked examples (about three minutes), component selection (about 11 minutes median), and select-then-write with automated GPT-4o feedback (about 37 minutes median). The authors state "Instructors can thus choose primarily by available time and desired durability", since gains did not reliably depend on measured learner characteristics and voluntary use was high in every condition. Each option's gains, time cost, and component coverage are quantified.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Condition 4 requires an automated feedback system validating prompt components against pre-specified acceptance criteria
- Conditions 2–4 require scenario materials isomorphic in structure to assessment tasks
#### Constraints
- The gradient reflects joint effects of engagement, duration, and content exposure, not a pure engagement manipulation; no direct manipulation check of cognitive engagement was collected

### Target Learners
- first-year undergraduate introductory programming students

### Target Learning Goals
- learning-oriented prompting with GenAI
