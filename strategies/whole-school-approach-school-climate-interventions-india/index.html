Whole-school approach with school-based intervention packages to improve school climate in Indian secondary schools¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review concludes that school-based intervention packages are needed to improve school climate and that "The whole school approach could be a better strategy for intervention." Its recommendations include enhancing teacher-student supportive relationships, strengthening peer group support through extracurricular activities, organizing regular parent-teacher meetings, reducing class sizes in government schools, and training school counselors on the significance of school climate. The only identified Indian intervention program (SEHER) showed that an encouraging school climate significantly reduces symptoms of depression, bullying victimization, and perpetration of violence.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Multilayered approaches involving all stakeholders of the school community—teachers, students, parents, administrators, staff, and community leaders

Constraints¶

  • The review notes schools, educational authorities, and teacher-training programs have neglected cultivating a positive school climate, and scarcity of school-based intervention programs exists in India

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school adolescents in India
  • government and private school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • emotional health
  • academic achievement
  • reduced bullying and violence

Affordances¶

  • School Climate Multidimensional Collective Experience

Claims¶

  • Academic achievement and school climate differ significantly between government and private schools in India, with private school students performing better [+W]
  • Substantial proportions of Indian adolescents report peer pressure, bullying at school, and emotional distress from teacher-student interaction [+W]
  • School climate correlates positively with academic achievement in included Indian studies, with reported correlations of 0.637 and 0.55 and higher grades in strong learning climates [+W]
  • Positive school climate characteristics are significantly associated with reduced depression and stress symptoms, improved self-esteem, and better academic performance among Indian secondary school adolescents [+W]
  • Open, autonomous, familiar, and controlled school climate types significantly affect adolescent psychological hardiness dimensions, with gender differences favoring male students [+W]
  • School environment is positively associated with educational aspiration among Indian secondary students (r = .346), motivating participation in school activities [+W]
