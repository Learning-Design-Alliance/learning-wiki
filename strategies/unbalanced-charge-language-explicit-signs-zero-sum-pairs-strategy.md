# Three instructional moves for teaching charge as a signed quantity: use “amount of unbalanced charge” language, make signs explicit, and teach zero-sum pairs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article offers three suggestions for promoting student learning of charge. First, instructors should "make use of the phrase 'amount of unbalanced charge' as a way to help students interpret net charge in the intended manner," since "net charge" alone may cue number-line interpretation. Second, instructors should specify "+5 µC" rather than simply "5 µC," establishing a habit of actively seeking meaning in the sign. Third, they suggest "explicit instruction on the quantification of opposites as zero-sum pairs (i.e., pairs of numbers which sum to zero)" so students can conceptualize integers as complements rather than positions on a number line.
