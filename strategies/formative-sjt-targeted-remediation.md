# Use mid-course SJT performance, especially around key clinical transitions, to identify students for early targeted remediation and formative feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends educators use insights from mid-course SJT performance to flag students who may benefit from additional support, allowing early targeted remediation. It suggests using SJTs formatively rather than summatively so feedback encourages reflection and strengthens commitment to professional development, and conceptualises formative SJTs with feedback as a form of scenario-based learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- access to SJT scores administered during training
- a remediation or support pathway for flagged students
#### Constraints
- The article notes it remains to be established whether students flagged by low SJT scores can be successfully remediated, and emerging evidence on structured remediation programmes is preliminary

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students, particularly those transitioning to clinical placements

### Target Learning Goals
- professionalism
- professional identity formation
