# Montessori classroom conversion as an early childhood intervention for refugee settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that organizations working with refugee populations consider Montessori education as an effective early childhood intervention. The authors hope the study "encourages non-government organizations, government agencies and international agencies working with refugee populations to consider Montessori education as an effective early childhood intervention to promote school readiness and lay the foundation for future success." The strategy involves converting existing classrooms to child-directed learning with Montessori materials, freeing teacher time for one-on-one attention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher training in the Montessori method and specifically designed Montessori materials
#### Constraints
- The authors caution that much more work must be done before knowing whether implementing the Montessori method actually does a better job of meeting the social, emotional and academic needs of refugee students

### Target Learners
- Displaced and refugee children in early childhood education

### Target Learning Goals
- School readiness, positive child development, and academic achievement
