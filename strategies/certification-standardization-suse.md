# Implement certification systems to standardize student ultrasound education and quality-assure minimum competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review proposes certification as an external quality-assurance mechanism for student ultrasound education: it sets a benchmark of fundamental ultrasound expertise, acts as a gatekeeper for safe practice, and can align curricular learning objectives with required qualifications, thereby standardizing teaching. The authors argue certification "can function as an incentive for local universities to adapt their curricula to align with the required standards", and suggest re-accreditation examinations to test and reinforce skill retention into residency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A valid, objective assessment as the prerequisite for certification
- Definition of core competencies and standardized criteria for recognizing student ultrasound tutors
#### Constraints
- Planning, implementation, and ongoing awarding would require significant financial and personal resources and could increase bureaucracy; certification programs may impose extra costs on participants

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- volunteer student ultrasound tutors

### Target Learning Goals
- fundamental ultrasound competence maintained through graduation and into residency
