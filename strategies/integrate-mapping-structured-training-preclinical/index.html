Integrate mind and concept maps into preclinical curricula with structured training, weekly review use, and collaborative mapping¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that educators actively incorporate mapping tools into preclinical lesson plans, especially for courses requiring synthesis of complex information, and "provide structured guidance on how to create and use them". Suggested implementations include incorporating mind maps into weekly review sessions or pre-exam study plans, replacing conventional essay questions with concept-map creation on tests, and encouraging collaborative mapping in small groups. The review also recommends structured training sessions, noting students often need multiple practice sessions before becoming proficient at developing useful concept maps.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • structured guidance on how to create and use the maps
  • training sessions to enhance effectiveness
  • multiple practice sessions before students become proficient

Constraints¶

  • making insightful and thorough maps takes a lot of time, adding to academic burden; students find it impossible to map every topic given the vast number of subjects

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate preclinical medical students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • knowledge retention
  • comprehension
  • critical thinking
  • organizational skills
  • self-directed learning

Claims¶

  • Online testable concept maps improve pathology quiz scores with moderate-to-large effect sizes (Cohen's d = 0.7–0.8) [+W]
  • Concept maps suit deep hierarchical integration tasks while mind maps suit organization and rapid recall, with mean score gains of 5–15% [~W]
  • Two RCTs found no significant performance difference for mapping interventions, yet students consistently preferred the methods [~W]
  • Mind maps and concept maps significantly enhance knowledge retention, recall, and assessment performance in four of six RCTs among preclinical medical students [+W]
  • Mind mapping significantly improves knowledge retention in neuroanatomy learning versus didactic lecture (Teli et al., 2020) [+W]
