Adopt FCCL in clinical medicine teaching except in Western surgical education, with rigorous RCT designs and expanded disciplines¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends administering FCCL in clinical medicine teaching, with careful selection regarding surgical education in Western countries. It further recommends expanding FCCL application across disciplines such as "obstetrics, gynecology, pediatrics, and others," adopting rigorous experimental designs including "randomization through random number tables and blind assessment of exam scores," and evaluating student and faculty satisfaction through feedback surveys.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructor preparation of learning materials including instructional videos and case designs
  • Support for teachers adapting to the new pedagogical approach

Constraints¶

  • Careful selection needed for surgical education in Western countries
  • Results should be interpreted critically and applied contextually rather than adopted rigidly

Target Learners¶

  • Clinical medical students and residents

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Theoretical knowledge
  • Clinical analytical skills

Claims¶

  • Chinese students in FCCL groups outperform LBL peers in both theoretical scores and clinical analysis skills [+W]
  • FCCL outperforms LBL on clinical analysis skills with a large pooled effect (Cohen's d = 1.53) [+W]
  • FCCL outperforms LBL on theoretical scores with a moderate pooled effect (Cohen's d = 0.60) [+W]
  • Western students in FCCL groups outperform LBL peers in clinical analysis skills but not in theoretical scores [~W]
  • Clinical analysis skills effect size is unstable and sensitive to two studies, dropping to d = 0.50 after their exclusion [~W]
