# Rubrics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Rubrics are evaluation tools consisting of a set of criteria, a fixed scale, and descriptors that distinguish the differences in the levels of the scale. They are used to judge the degree of understanding, proficiency levels of skills, the quality of products or performances, and learner growth from one level to the next. Three types are common: holistic (an overall impression), analytic (distinct elements judged independently), and developmental (growth along a proficiency continuum).

## Design Implications

Rubrics make quality criteria explicit *before* performance, converting assessment from a private judgment into a shared, learnable standard. Their strongest documented effect is on self-regulated learning: when students use rubrics to self-assess, they internalize the criteria and monitor their own work against them [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]. The descriptors function as advance organizers for quality — learners know what "good" looks like before they begin, which directs attention to the features that matter [Assessment for learning.](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear learning objectives; criteria must be stated in observable, learner-comprehensible terms ([State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md))
- A rating scale with descriptors that genuinely distinguish adjacent levels — vague adjectives ("good," "adequate") undermine the tool
- Explicit instruction in how to interpret and apply the rubric; students do not use rubrics well without modeling and practice [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Time to create or select the rubric and to score against it ([Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md))

#### Constraints
- Generic rubrics produce generic feedback; if descriptors do not name task-specific features, learners cannot act on them [Assessment for learning.](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) [-M]
- Holistic rubrics rarely give enough detail for learners to identify areas for improvement; analytic rubrics are preferable when the goal is feedback rather than grading speed
- Rubrics used only as grading instruments — never shown to or used by students — forfeit nearly all of their learning benefit [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [~M]
- Overly detailed analytic rubrics can fragment attention on surface features (counts, checkboxes) at the expense of holistic quality judgment

#### Implementation Variability
- **Analytic vs. holistic:** analytic for feedback and self-assessment; holistic for efficient scoring of large volumes
- **Single-point rubrics:** list only the "proficient" descriptor, with space for notes above/below — reduces the checklist mentality of multi-level scales
- **Co-constructed rubrics:** involving students in drafting criteria deepens ownership of the standards [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- **Developmental rubrics:** describe growth across a continuum, suited to portfolio and competency-based contexts
- **Early-childhood adaptation:** symbols, faces, or charts replace text descriptors

### Target Learners
- All levels, particularly K–12 and higher education; adaptable across subjects and skill domains
- Developing self-regulators benefit most — rubrics give them concrete criteria for self-monitoring [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Novices, who otherwise lack a model of what quality performance looks like
- Less useful for learners who already hold well-calibrated internal standards; for them the rubric adds little beyond what they already do

### Target Learning Goals
- Quality judgment: understanding what distinguishes strong from weak performance
- Self-assessment and self-regulation: comparing one's own work to explicit criteria [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Goal-setting: using descriptors as proximal targets for revision [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]
- Feedback literacy: interpreting and acting on criterion-referenced feedback

### Instructions
1. Derive criteria directly from the learning objectives ([State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md))
2. Write descriptors that name observable, task-specific features at each scale level; avoid comparative or vague language
3. Share the rubric *before* the task and model its use on a sample ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md))
4. Have students self- or peer-assess a draft against the rubric before submission ([Self-Assessment](../elements/self-assessment.md))
5. Score with the rubric and attach criterion-referenced comments ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md), [Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md))
6. Require revision targeting the lowest-scored criteria, closing the feedback loop

## Related Strategies
- **Self-assessment with rubrics** — the primary mechanism by which rubrics improve self-regulation rather than merely documenting grades
- **Criterion-referenced feedback** — rubric descriptors supply the language for specific, actionable feedback
- **Portfolio assessment** — developmental rubrics track growth across artifacts over time

## Related Elements
- [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) — rubric descriptors make feedback specific and criterion-referenced rather than evaluative
- [Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md) — the scoring function; rubrics standardize judgment across raters and tasks
- [State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md) — criteria are only meaningful when traceable to stated objectives
- [Self-Assessment](../elements/self-assessment.md) — the learner-facing use that drives the self-regulation benefits

## Examples
- **[Exemplars](https://www.exemplars.com)** — standards-aligned performance-task rubrics for mathematics and science, with annotated student work at each level so learners can see what the descriptors mean
- **[New Tech Network school-wide rubrics](https://newtechnetwork.org)** — shared analytic rubrics for collaboration, communication, and agency used across all courses, so criteria accumulate meaning across a student's schooling
- **Single-point rubric in writing workshop** — teacher lists only the proficient descriptor for thesis, evidence, and organization; students annotate their draft above (exceeded) and below (needs work) each row, then revise
