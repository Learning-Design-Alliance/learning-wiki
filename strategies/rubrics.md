# Rubrics

## Description
Rubrics are evaluation tools consisting of a set of criteria, a fixed scale, and descriptors that distinguish the differences in the levels of the scale. They are used to judge the degree of understanding, proficiency levels of skills, the quality of products or performances, and learner growth from one level to the next. Three types of rubrics are commonly used in schools: holistic (provides an overall impression), analytic (divides performance into distinct elements and judges each independently), and developmental (describes growth along a proficiency continuum).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires clear learning objectives, well-defined criteria aligned to standards, and a rating scale with detailed descriptors. Time is needed for teachers to create or select appropriate rubrics, teach students how to use them, and provide feedback based on rubric scores.
#### Constraints
- If not constructed carefully, rubrics may be too generic and not provide specific enough feedback. Holistic rubrics may not offer enough detail for learners to understand areas for improvement. Students need explicit instruction on how to interpret and use rubrics effectively.

### Affordances
- Provides clear expectations, supports self-assessment, offers specific feedback, and aids in monitoring progress. Benefits teachers by specifying qualities of successful performance and offering insights into instructional adjustments. Supports a shared understanding of learning targets between educators and students.

### Personalization
- Rubrics can be customized with different scales (e.g., 4-point, 7-point), criteria, and descriptors to align with specific learning objectives and standards. For younger learners, symbols, charts, or faces can be incorporated for easier comprehension.
