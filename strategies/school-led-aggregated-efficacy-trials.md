# School-led aggregated efficacy trials with independent expert oversight

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The report concludes that "It is feasible for schools to conduct evaluations of their own planned interventions, under favourable circumstances, and with some advice and oversight from independent expert evaluators". Three school clusters that individually applied to the EEF were aggregated into one trial; each school ran its own small trial, made evaluation decisions such as randomising pupils, and Durham University evaluators advised on research process, randomisation and testing, then aggregated the results.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Expert advice and oversight from independent evaluators
- Workshops training school leads in RCT design, randomisation, and test analysis
- Cluster leads to manage interventions and communication within each cluster
#### Constraints
- The main area of concern was allowing schools to do the randomisation, which produced pre-test imbalance and possible selection bias
- Participating school leaders were already enthusiastic about the programme, which may limit generalisability to less enthusiastic schools

### Target Learners
- Pupils in school-led intervention programmes being evaluated locally

### Target Learning Goals
- Building school capacity to generate evidence about their own interventions
