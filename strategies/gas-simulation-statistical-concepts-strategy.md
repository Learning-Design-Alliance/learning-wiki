# Use an interactive gas simulation to demonstrate statistical thermodynamics concepts without formal formulae

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends supporting secondary-school explanation of thermodynamics with an applet in which students observe molecular collisions, heating/cooling, piston pressing and Brownian motion directly. The authors write that the applet explains these features "without the need of formal mathematical formula", since the Maxwell-Boltzmann derivation goes beyond the secondary curriculum. Teachers can run the simulation step by step and display histograms and energy plots in real time.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to the freely available web simulation; teacher selection of the appropriate mode (velocity distribution, heating/cooling, pressing, Brownian motion) for the topic being explained
#### Constraints
- The gas modelled is two-dimensional and dilute, so displayed relations use two-dimensional factors and ideal-gas relations are only approximations

### Target Learners
- secondary school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting temperature, energy and molecular velocity; understanding the random character of molecular velocities and the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution
