Baseline recipe for NLP studies in learning analytics: datasets of at least 1,000 instances, Random Forest with interpretable feature sets, and BERT/LSTM evaluation¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 5 studies (3 design, 1 review, 1 associational), q2 · 0 of 5 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review distills its technical synthesis into a recommended baseline configuration for future NLP work in learning analytics. It advises "using a dataset comprising a minimum of 1,000 instances", "combining the Random Forest algorithm with TF-IDF, Structural Features, LIWC, and Coh-Metrix as baseline models", and "evaluating word embeddings through BERT and LSTM classifiers". This gives researchers a comparable starting point grounded in what the field actually reports.

Claims¶

  • Random forest algorithms yielded the best classification performance in K–8 MMLA studies comparing multiple machine learning models [+M]
  • On the TU/e CS overlap dataset, supervised NLP classifiers (Random Forest, XGBoost) outperform thresholding and zero-shot LLM baselines at detecting course overlap [+M]
  • A random forest classified flagged versus non-flagged word problems with the best accuracy of five tested models (AUC = 0.75) [+W]
  • A support vector machine classifier outperformed decision tree and random forest models in predicting cognitive engagement levels of online discussion posts [-W]
  • An LSTM-based strategy temporal analytics framework outperforms random forest, SVM, and majority-class baselines for stealth assessment predictive accuracy [+W]
  • Academic word list count, word count, and Flesch-Kincaid grade level are the most important features for predicting cognitive engagement in discussion posts [+W]
