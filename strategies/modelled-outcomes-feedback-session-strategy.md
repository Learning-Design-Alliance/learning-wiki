# Use co-designed modelling feedback sessions to let participants reflect on their choices and reconsider preferences in light of revealed trade-offs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
After running the model on participants' choices, present the modelling outcomes back to the participants in a dedicated session so they can see how their preferences influence system design. The article reports this enabled a meaningful, fact-based dialogue, provided opportunities to reassess choices, deepened understanding of renewable electricity systems and trade-offs, and addressed misconceptions spread through social media. The authors frame such sessions as part of empowering underrepresented groups and fostering procedural justice and inclusive solutions through effective communication, empowerment, and collaborative decision-making.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Modelling results that directly reflect participants' questionnaire choices
- A facilitated discussion session at a participating institution
- Willingness to present trade-offs, including costly or surprising outcomes
#### Constraints
- Interpretation of workshop results may introduce bias; participants in other regions may hold different preferences, so local considerations should complement national focus

### Target Learners
- Young people aged 15-16
- Underrepresented community groups engaging with energy planning

### Target Learning Goals
- Understanding trade-offs and the necessity of compromises in energy system design
- Correcting misconceptions about renewable energy technologies and costs
