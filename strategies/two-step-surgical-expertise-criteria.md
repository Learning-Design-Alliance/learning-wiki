# Two-step process for defining surgical expertise in future expertise-based trials: choose a learning-curve proxy, then assess surgeons against predefined criteria

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The review proposes that expertise criteria for a specific intervention be developed in two steps: "1) decide on the proxy to be used for the learning curve, and 2) assess eligible surgeons by comparing their performance to the previously defined expertise criteria." Suitable proxies include number of years in practice, number of previous cases performed, annual caseload, and training, with a consensus requirement for reaching the learning-curve plateau. Surgeons near the cutoff may increase expertise and be re-evaluated. Performance measures, if used, should be adjusted for preoperative risk because expert surgeons may operate on higher-risk patients.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Investigators should review previously reported learning-curve estimates for the intervention or similar interventions, or conduct a survey of experts to estimate required experience when prior reports are not robust.
#### Constraints
- Criteria can be lenient for pragmatic trials or stricter for explanatory trials; centres ideally must have expertise in both interventions, and a feasibility survey can identify eligible centres and providers.

### Target Learners
- surgeons being credentialed as expert operators for clinical trials

### Target Learning Goals
- demonstrating procedural expertise at the learning-curve plateau for a defined intervention
