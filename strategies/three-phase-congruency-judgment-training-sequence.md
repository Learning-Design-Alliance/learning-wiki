# Three-phase fading-support training sequence from congruent exposure to randomized congruency judgment with feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 quant-synthesis), `q3`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Both training programs used an identical three-phase sequence built around matched and mismatched question-answer pairs. Phase one exposes only congruent pairs while children identify focus position and type; phase two adds incongruent pairs "assigned to odd and even number trials respectively", with children pressing Y or N to judge congruency; phase three randomizes trial order and adds feedback. This graduated structure moves from supported exposure to active judgment with feedback, delivered either without social interaction (Eprime) or through interaction with the Furhat robot. Each program comprised two sessions of 1 to 1.5 hours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Six target sentences with subject-verb-object structure in various focus conditions, audio stimuli with matched and mismatched question-answer pairs, and a response mechanism (buttons or Y/N keys) for congruency judgments
#### Constraints
- Materials were limited to six sentences split across two sessions; the study notes sentences were limited to five-syllable subject-verb-object structures

### Target Learners
- Cantonese-speaking autistic children

### Target Learning Goals
- Mapping acoustic cues to information structure categories (broad, narrow, contrastive focus) in perception and production
