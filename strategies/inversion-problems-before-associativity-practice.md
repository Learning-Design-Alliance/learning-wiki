# Use brief inversion-problem activities before associativity instruction to increase shortcut use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests that brief activities in which learners solve 'a + b – b' inversion problems before encountering 'a + b – c' associativity problems can increase their willingness to use associativity shortcuts. In all three studies, learners "who first solved inversion problems (e.g. 'a + b – b') were more likely than controls to then use associativity on 'a + b – c' problems." The activity is brief (about 4 minutes 30 seconds of paper-based problem solving) and does not explicitly prescribe strategies, which the authors argue may better preserve strategy flexibility than explicit demonstrations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners should solve 'a + b – b' inversion problems (not 'a + b – a' or two-term problems) as the pre-activity, since only that format was expected to activate all three mechanisms
#### Constraints
- The effect was demonstrated on self-reported shortcut use on a single test problem; it did not significantly increase problems solved or attempted on a timed problem-solving measure, and the effect may depend on how conducive the test problems are to shortcuts

### Target Learners
- adult undergraduates in mathematics-related courses

### Target Learning Goals
- willingness to apply associativity shortcuts on three-term arithmetic problems
- easing the transition to algebra

### Affordances
- Three Mechanisms Inversion Promotes Associativity
