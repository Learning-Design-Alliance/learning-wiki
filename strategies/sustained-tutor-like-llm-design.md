# Design LLM deployments as sustained, tutor-like, scaffolded activities rather than one-off answer-provision tools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 mixed) · 8 studies (2 review, 2 associational, 2 theoretical, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q3` · 2 of 8 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Across all three purposes, the article's moderator analyses point to the same design lesson: implementation design, not simply access, determines outcomes. Programs that "positioned LLMs as active tutors, and that sustained their use over eight weeks or longer, achieved the largest and most reliable gains", while unguided or answer-provision use can harm long-term learning. The article recommends designs that keep learners initiating, explaining, and revising, such as tutor-like systems that hold back the final answer and ask for justification, plan–revise cycles, and reflective journals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sustained intervention duration (eight weeks or longer for the largest gains)
- a tutor or partner pedagogical role rather than a passive tool role
- interactional scaffolding such as reflective prompts or structured peer exchange for socialisation goals
#### Constraints
- Subjectification gains were strongest in small-scale programs (1–50 participants) and attenuated sharply in cohorts larger than 100, so scalability requires redesigning roles, time, and assessment
- benefits are larger when LLMs are embedded in structured, guided workflows; unguided use can produce neutral or negative longer-term outcomes

### Target Learners
- K-12 and higher education students

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance and skills
- collaboration and participation
- autonomy and self-regulation

### Affordances
- Biesta Tripartite Educational Purposes
