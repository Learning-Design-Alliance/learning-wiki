# Multi-level screen-use intervention strategy: daily recreational threshold, segmented learning schedules, eye-care rules, and postural and environmental settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the dose–response findings, the review recommends that "interventions targeting screen-related health risks among university students should adhere to systematic and multi-level principles." Behavioral interventions include establishing "a safe daily threshold for recreational screen use (e.g., ≤ 2.5 h)", implementing "segmented learning schedules with regular rest breaks (recommended brief rest every 25–30 min)", reducing media multitasking, and ensuring adequate physical activity. Physiological protection includes promoting the "20–20-20" eye care rule (every 20 min, look at an object 20 feet away for 20 s), optimizing posture and visual environments (monitor approximately 15° below eye level, viewing distance ≥ 91 cm for computers and ≥ 40 cm for smartphones), and blue light mitigation and circadian rhythm regulation to improve sleep quality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional and individual capacity to structure daily schedules, learning environments, and device-use habits around the recommended thresholds and rest intervals
#### Constraints
- The ≤ 2.5 h threshold is a safety boundary derived from where dose–response risks rise sharply, not a demonstrated optimum; the physical activity association was sensitive to individual studies and should be interpreted with caution

### Target Learners
- university students
- students in screen-intensive higher-education programs

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining academic performance
- reducing digital eye strain during study
- protecting sleep quality and musculoskeletal health

### Affordances
- Screen Exposure Three Pathway Mechanism Framework
