# Explicitly differentiate discipline-specific from transferable skills in assignment reflection to broaden students' employability skill awareness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because only 7.5% of students reported growth in problem-solving despite likely using it, the authors recommend making transferable skill development explicit. As printed: "Future iterations of this assignment should explicitly discuss and differentiate between discipline‐specific and transferable skills and include a reflection question surrounding students' use of these two categories of skills to help broaden students' perspectives of employability skills". The strategy targets the tendency for transferable skills to remain implicit while discipline-specific skills are highlighted.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A reflection component in the assessment that prompts students to identify both skill categories
#### Constraints
- Grounded in self-reported perceptions from a single course offering without a control group

### Target Learners
- undergraduate STEM students

### Target Learning Goals
- awareness of transferable employability skill development, including problem-solving
