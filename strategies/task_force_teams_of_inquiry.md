# Task Force Teams of Inquiry

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Task force teams of inquiry organize learners into small, mission-driven teams, each of which selects a topic from a class-generated brainstorm list. The team's mission is to explore, research, and present on that topic, typically culminating in a public product such as a report, presentation, or recommendation. The teacher frames the inquiry, connects it to relevant standards, and facilitates rather than directs the investigation.

## Design Implications

The strategy combines two well-supported mechanisms: cooperative goal structures, which raise achievement and motivation relative to individualistic or competitive structures [Johnson & Johnson's meta-analytic review of cooperative learning.](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520903035994) [+S], and inquiry-oriented tasks, which support deeper conceptual understanding when scaffolded [Inquiry-based approaches improve conceptual understanding relative to traditional instruction.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654312458075) [+M]. Topic choice from a brainstorm list leverages learner autonomy, which supports intrinsic motivation [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]. However, the inquiry component requires substantial guidance: minimally guided team inquiry produces weaker outcomes than scaffolded inquiry, particularly for novices [Minimally guided instruction is less effective than guided instruction for novices.](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1) [-S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A brainstorm list of genuinely researchable topics, generated with learner input
- Clear team guidelines: roles, timelines, deliverables, and quality criteria shared in advance
- Structured collaboration supports — assigned roles, [check-ins](../elements/check-in.md), and milestone deadlines — so that responsibility is distributed rather than absorbed by one or two members
- Access to research resources and instruction in how to evaluate and synthesize them
- A culminating product with an authentic audience where possible [Authentic audiences improve student work.](../claims/authentic-audiences-improve-student-work.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Unguided team inquiry degrades into unfocused searching for novices; teams need embedded scaffolds, checkpoints, and expert feedback [Minimally guided instruction is less effective than guided instruction for novices.](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1) [-S]
- Free-riding and status hierarchies undermine learning unless roles and individual accountability are designed in; group work without individual accountability can reduce achievement for capable members [Cooperative learning requires both positive interdependence and individual accountability.](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520903035994) [~S]
- Teams formed solely by friendship tend to replicate existing social hierarchies and exclude marginalized learners [~W]
- Large teams (more than 4–5 members) dilute participation and increase coordination costs [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Team formation: self-selected, teacher-assigned, or randomly formed — teacher assignment with attention to heterogeneity is often safest for equity
- Duration: single-week mini-inquiries to semester-long projects
- Structure: fully open inquiry, guided inquiry with teacher-provided questions, or structured [case-based-learning](../elements/case-based-learning.md) variants
- Jigsaw adaptation: each member researches a subtopic and teaches it to teammates, enforcing interdependence

### Target Learners
- Upper elementary through adult learners; younger learners need heavier scaffolding of both research skills and teamwork
- Learners with basic research and self-regulation skills benefit most; novices need guided inquiry structures rather than open exploration [Minimally guided instruction is less effective than guided instruction for novices.](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_1) [~S]
- Learners who struggle socially may need explicit collaboration instruction and role support rather than exemption from team work

### Target Learning Goals
- Inquiry and research skills: question formulation, source evaluation, synthesis
- Collaborative competence: shared planning, negotiation, and accountability [Cooperative goal structures raise achievement and motivation.](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520903035994) [+S]
- Communication: presenting findings to authentic audiences
- Deep conceptual understanding of the investigated topic [Inquiry-based approaches improve conceptual understanding relative to traditional instruction.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654312458075) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Facilitate a class brainstorm of researchable topics; curate the list against standards and feasibility.
2. Form teams of 3–5, balancing self-selection with teacher judgment about composition and heterogeneity.
3. Have each team select a topic and draft a focused inquiry question; require teacher approval of the question before research begins.
4. Assign rotating roles (coordinator, researcher, recorder, presenter) and set milestone [check-ins](../elements/check-in.md) with deliverables.
5. Teach or review research and source-evaluation skills before teams begin; provide curated starting resources for novices.
6. Structure interim peer feedback using [assessment](../elements/assessment.md) criteria shared in advance [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S].
7. Schedule culminating presentations to an audience beyond the team; include individual reflections and peer evaluations to secure individual accountability.

## Examples
- **Project Citizen** (Center for Civic Education, https://www.civic-ed.org) — class teams identify a community problem, research policy alternatives, and present a portfolio and hearing before civic panels.
- **eCYBERMISSION** (Army Educational Outreach Program, https://www.ecybermission.com) — grades 6–9 teams select a community problem, investigate it with STEM methods, and submit findings to a national competition.
- A middle-school science unit where teams each select one human-impact topic from a brainstormed list (water quality, invasive species, urban heat), research it over three weeks with weekly teacher check-ins, and present recommendations to a local council member.
