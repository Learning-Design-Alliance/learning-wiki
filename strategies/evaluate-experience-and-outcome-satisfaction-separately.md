# Evaluate satisfaction with the learning experience separately from satisfaction with the learning outcome

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because learners may equate a pleasant experience with effective learning, the article recommends that short-term evaluation "augment any testing of the satisfaction of the learner with their experience of the learning process by also checking (and possibly contrasting) their degree of understanding of, and satisfaction with, the learning outcome." All four experience-outcome combinations are possible, so evaluators should measure both and, in the longer term, track future learning choices the way a delayed post-test tracks retained gains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Evaluation instruments must capture both experience satisfaction and outcome satisfaction, and longer-term follow-up of future learning choices
#### Constraints
- Meta-affective and meta-cognitive mentoring must be sensitive to learners equating pleasant experience with effective outcome, or challenging experience with ineffective outcome

### Target Learners
- learners using AIED systems whose experience satisfaction may diverge from their learning outcome

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate self-assessment of learning
- meta-affective and meta-cognitive awareness
