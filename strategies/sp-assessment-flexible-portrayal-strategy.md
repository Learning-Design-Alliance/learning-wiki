# Involve standardized patients in assessment and allow flexible portrayals, with training that standardizes responses to unsatisfactory, intermediate, and good examinee behavior

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Participants suggested involving standardized patients as additional assessors because "assessments by SPs could be complementary to those of physician raters because they perceive other dimensions of communication". They also proposed allowing greater flexibility in SPs' role-plays, with training that standardizes reactions to different quality levels of examinee communication.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- SP training must adapt to the increased complexity of portraying pre-determined emotional hints at a pre-defined level of intensity.
- Feasibility of SP assessment depends on the time interval between stations; a short interval such as 2 minutes might prevent thorough evaluation.
