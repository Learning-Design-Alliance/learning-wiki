Open research directions for learning companions: domain portability, multiple companions, group models, and natural dialogue beyond menus¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article outlines open research questions for learning companions in 2015: generality and portability "to new domains, instructional situations, and paradigms"; how to "deploy, utilize, and coordinate multiple learning companions" in an intelligent CSCL; tracking both a student model and a "group model"; additional feedback paths such as attentiveness indicators; agents for open learning environments and MOOCs; and "more natural and comprehensive dialogues that move beyond the menu-based approaches of the past." These directions guide future learning companion design.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Addressing social isolation, dropout, and self-regulation among members of large online learning communities

Constraints¶

  • The complexity and cost of porting a learning companion to a new instructional situation is high

Target Learners¶

  • learners in MOOCs and online learning communities
  • learners in intelligent CSCL environments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustained engagement and self-regulation at scale
  • effective collaborative learning support

Claims¶

  • Deliberative discussion, detectable through dialogue acts, is characterized by social cooperation and openness to persuasion and should lead to better learning outcomes [+W]
  • Dialogue acts can distinguish balanced, supportive collaborative learning groups from unbalanced, unsupportive ones [+W]
  • Instructional roles of group members can be deduced from machine-inferable factors, and presence or absence of roles indicates learning effectiveness [+W]
  • Machine learning, including neural nets, can detect localized instances of good or poor collaboration during collaborative learning [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Pedagogical agent interventions to improve collaborative learning: keep conversation coherent, encourage deliberative discussion, and monitor peer needs
  • Future student-simulation work should extend beyond turn-level math dialogue and address history-selection and faithfulness gaps
  • Pursue open AIED-in-MOOCs research directions: sensing-based affective modeling for embodied adaptive learning and MOOC data visualization for stakeholder feedback
