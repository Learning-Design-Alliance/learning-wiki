# Use up-to-date SDT/AGT motivational constructs in targeted, element-level interventions rather than curriculum-wide comparisons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that research on motivation in PBL stop treating PBL as a monolithic sum-total environment and instead study specific elements — problems, tutors, group dynamics — with contemporary motivational measures. It argues that "targeting the research to specific elements and moments of PBL can break down some of the complexity of the psychological aspects of PBL", and that future work should "use up-to-date motivational constructs in more targeted interventions". This follows from the finding that curriculum-level comparisons yielded no conclusive results on intrinsic motivation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies must employ contemporary SDT and AGT constructs with their later theoretical nuances, differentiating motivation subtypes rather than using coarse composites
#### Constraints
- The article notes one combination of PBL elements might yield greater motivation while another might not, even within environments both claiming to be PBL

### Target Learners
- medical, STEM and higher-education students in PBL programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- designing and researching motivating PBL environments
