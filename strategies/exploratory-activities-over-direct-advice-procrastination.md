# Use exploratory activities rather than telling students what to do when addressing procrastination

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's closing recommendation is that interventions letting students explore themselves through activities are more useful than direct instruction. It names the Socratic method and guided discovery, metaphors, and therapeutic card activities as techniques considered effective in this research and important for further work on managing academic procrastination. It also suggests integrating such programs into university curricula as electives or credit courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Activities must let students explore their own thoughts and behaviors rather than simply being told what to do
#### Constraints
- Recommendation is based on the authors' interpretation of this single quasi-experimental study

### Target Learners
- university students

### Target Learning Goals
- managing academic procrastination behaviors
