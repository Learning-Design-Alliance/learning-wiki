# Use an OSS worn by a standardised patient in communication teaching to raise awareness of patients with obesity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (4 for, 3 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
A teaching strategy in which a standardised patient wears an obesity simulation suit during a simulated doctor-patient encounter so that students experience a realistic consultation with a patient with obesity and reflect on potential prejudices. The authors state: "we strongly believe that integrating an OSS into the routine undergraduate medical teaching context is a valuable tool. It can raise medical students’ awareness for communication encounters with patients with obesity." They recommend adding class time to discuss reservations towards people with obesity professionally.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An SP programme with trained standardised patients able to bear the physical strain of the suit
- Small-group sessions with experienced teachers moderating and questionnaires completed afterwards
#### Constraints
- Reduction of bias by using an OSS was beyond the scope of this study and would need to be examined in a pre-post test setting
- Further research should include intervention studies; long-term assessments of anti-fat attitudes are needed

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- weight management and obesity-related communication
- reducing anti-fat prejudices
- empathy with patients with obesity
