# Build student buy-in deliberately: explain the method on day one and connect early quizzes to worksheets

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
A specific recipe for securing student buy-in in a large active classroom where students are accustomed to passive anonymity. The authors spend the first day explaining how and why the flipped classroom works rather than covering content, and use "an early ﬁrst quiz" as "an eﬀective means to engage students in the learning process, by pointing out connections between the exam and the worksheets." Buy-in requires constant instructor attention throughout the term, including class-wide discussion of worksheet philosophy and mechanisms for distributing answers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Constant work and attention on the instructor's part throughout the course
- Students must see worksheets as integral to their quiz and exam success
#### Constraints
- The authors report this from implementation experience; buy-in is described as essential but requiring ongoing effort, not as a one-time fix

### Target Learners
- undergraduates in large research-university lecture courses resistant to active learning

### Target Learning Goals
- engagement with student-driven worksheet work in place of lecturing
