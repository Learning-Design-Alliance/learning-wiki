# Federal strategy to expand access to integrated, inclusive ECE through child care funding, universal preschool, Head Start alignment, and inclusive special education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 4 studies (3 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The whitepaper's first recommendation area directs the federal government to expand affordable child care, incentivize universal preschool for 3- and 4-year-olds, align Head Start with state preschool, and support inclusive special education. It proposes that subsidies cover all families under 150% of state median income "such that no family pays more than 7% of their income," and that federal formula funds reach all children at or below a share of state median income with a reasonable state match. It cites DC's blended Head Start-district preschool model, which more than doubled children receiving Head Start services, and birth-mandate states that align IDEA Part C and Part B eligibility.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient federal funding commensurate with the true cost of high-quality programs, including multiyear contracts or grants and enrollment-based rather than attendance-based financing
#### Constraints
- Blending Head Start and state preschool funding is currently difficult due to differences in federal and state standards and reporting requirements; the federal government has never fulfilled IDEA's 40% per-pupil funding promise

### Target Learners
- children birth through age 5
- children from low-income families
- children with disabilities

### Target Learning Goals
- access to high-quality early learning experiences
- socioeconomic, racial, and linguistic integration
- continuity of care and seamless transitions
