Colleges of education should employ origami instruction to promote preservice teachers’ spatial ability and geometric knowledge of shape and space¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the quasi-experimental findings, the authors recommend that, as done in Turkey and Israel, colleges of education in Ghana and elsewhere employ origami instruction to promote preservice teachers’ spatial ability and geometric knowledge regarding shape and space. They note that "materials for origami instructions are easy to obtain" and that the principles and steps guiding its use are readily available and adaptable from the internet, so even novice teachers could employ the origami approach and become proficient in its classroom use.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to origami paper materials and folding-step guidance, which the authors state are easy to obtain and adaptable from the internet

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on a single quasi-experiment with intact classes; the authors note validity threats such as controlling extraneous variables and switching treatment conditions, and suggest future studies switch groups and extend treatment duration

Target Learners¶

  • preservice teachers at colleges of education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • spatial ability
  • geometric knowledge for teaching shape and space

Claims¶

  • When pretests are used as covariates, origami instruction produces statistically significant gains in spatial orientation and geometric knowledge but not in spatial visualization [+W]
  • Origami instruction yields statistically significant posttest gains in geometric knowledge for teaching shape and space relative to conventional instruction [+W]
  • Origami and conventional instruction groups showed no preexisting differences on spatial orientation, spatial visualization, or geometric knowledge before treatment [+W]
  • Origami instruction yields statistically significant posttest differences in spatial orientation relative to conventional instruction [+W]
  • Origami instruction shows no statistically significant posttest advantage over conventional instruction in spatial visualization, despite descriptive gains [+W]
  • Before the intervention, the control and experimental groups were statistically equivalent in geometric thinking [+W]
