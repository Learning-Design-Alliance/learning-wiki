# Use a piston-in-tube heating exercise with an experimentally verifiable oscillatory solution in thermodynamics courses and laboratories

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A concrete implementable exercise: a vessel and tube filled with an ideal gas, closed by a frictionless piston under a constant external force, with two problems — prescribing a cubic-in-time piston motion to derive the required heating rate, and an adiabatic release solved numerically. The article notes 'The solution found in problem B can be experimentally observed with an Assmann-Rüchardt apparatus as modified by Schuﬂe', and that the apparatus is used to measure the ratio of specific heats from the oscillation period.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students with a knowledge of differential equations, or a re-expressed version avoiding direct mention of differential equations
- Access to an Assmann-Rüchardt-type apparatus for the laboratory verification
#### Constraints
- The article notes the printed L value is an effective distance idealizing the real set-up, where the real tube is between one and two metres long

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students
- laboratory course students

### Target Learning Goals
- applying thermodynamic laws to time-dependent processes
- connecting thermodynamic theory with measurable oscillation phenomena

### Affordances
- Time Dependent Classical Thermodynamics Formulation
