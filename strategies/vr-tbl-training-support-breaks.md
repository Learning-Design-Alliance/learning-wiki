# Provide VR user support, adjustable display settings, and built-in breaks when running virtual TBL sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A delivery strategy for VR-based TBL in which instructors resolve technical problems during the session and future applications build in usability safeguards. The article recommends that "Future VR applications in medical education may need to incorporate adjustable display settings and built‐in breaks to mitigate these issues." In the pilot, a faculty member resolved a student's echo and login problems, after which the student engaged fully, and one student who initially struggled with the VR interface engaged effectively after receiving support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors or faculty members available within the session to troubleshoot echo, login, and interface problems
- Brief MHW and VR headset training provided to all students before immersion
#### Constraints
- Technical issues still occurred despite prior training, so the strategy mitigates rather than prevents barriers
- The article frames adjustable display settings and breaks as future needs, not as tested interventions in this pilot

### Target Learners
- Medical students using VR headsets for virtual team-based learning

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining uninterrupted engagement and participation in virtual TBL sessions
