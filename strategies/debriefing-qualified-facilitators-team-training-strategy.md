# Include debriefing sessions run by qualified facilitators in team training to stimulate reflective learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that emergency team training programs, which often emphasize hands-on technical skills only, should add structured reflective learning. They write that "team training should include debriefing sessions run by qualified facilitators to stimulate reflective learning." In the studied training, critical exploration by local faculty in a confidential and safe environment challenged trainees' habits and beliefs and was perceived as crucial for changing practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Qualified facilitators to run the debriefing sessions
- A safe environment so multi-professional team members can critically reflect and challenge their own habits and beliefs
#### Constraints
- The authors note further research is needed to explore the optimal educational design of simulation training on obstetric emergencies like PPH

### Target Learners
- multi-professional maternity staff in low- and high-resource settings

### Target Learning Goals
- reflective learning
- teamwork in obstetric emergencies
- transfer of training to clinical practice
