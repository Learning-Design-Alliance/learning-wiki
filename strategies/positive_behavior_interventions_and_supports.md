# Positive Behavior Interventions and Supports

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Positive Behavior Interventions and Supports (PBIS) is a multi-tiered framework for establishing the social culture and behavioral supports needed for all students to succeed. Rather than reacting to misbehavior with punishment, schools explicitly teach behavioral expectations, acknowledge students who meet them, and escalate support through three tiers: universal expectations for all (Tier 1), targeted small-group supports (Tier 2), and intensive individualized interventions (Tier 3). Implementation is data-driven, with teams using office discipline referral (ODR) and attendance data to identify students needing additional support.

## Design Implications

PBIS treats behavior as a learnable skill set rather than a character trait, applying the logic of explicit instruction and reinforcement to social conduct [Behavioral expectations taught and reinforced schoolwide reduce office discipline referrals.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S]. Its effectiveness depends on implementation fidelity — schools that partially adopt the framework (e.g., posting expectations without teaching or reinforcing them) see substantially weaker outcomes [~S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A small team with administrator support that meets regularly to review behavioral data
- 3–5 positively stated, schoolwide expectations (e.g., "Be respectful, be responsible, be safe") defined with behavioral examples for each setting
- Explicit teaching of expectations at the start of the year and re-teaching after breaks, using [Modeling](../elements/modeling.md) and [Practice](../elements/practice.md) of specific behaviors
- A continuum of acknowledgment (e.g., token systems, praise) with a reinforcement ratio weighted toward positive contacts
- Consistent, data-informed consequences that are instructional rather than exclusionary

#### Constraints
- Weak implementation fidelity erodes effects; schools implementing fewer than 80% of critical features show diminished reductions in problem behavior [-S]
- Tier 1 alone is insufficient for students with chronic, intensive behavioral needs — withholding Tier 2/3 supports on the assumption that "good Tier 1 fixes everything" delays needed intervention [-M]
- Token reinforcement systems can lose effectiveness if acknowledgment becomes decoupled from specific, labeled feedback about which expectation was met [~M]
- Exclusionary discipline (suspension) used alongside PBIS undermines the framework; punitive responses disproportionately harm students with disabilities and students of color [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- Tier 2 options include Check-In/Check-Out (CICO), mentoring, and social skills groups; CICO is the most widely validated and easiest to scale
- Tier 3 typically uses functional behavioral assessment (FBA) leading to individualized behavior intervention plans
- Variants include schoolwide PBIS, classroom-level adaptations, and integration with academic multi-tiered systems of support (MTSS)

### Target Learners
- All students benefit from Tier 1 universal expectations, but the largest gains accrue to students at risk for behavioral difficulty [~S]
- Students with emotional or behavioral disorders require Tier 3 individualized function-based support; universal supports alone do not meet their needs [-M]
- Empathic, relationship-based discipline messaging specifically reduces suspension rates for middle school students of color [Brief empathic discipline interventions reduce suspensions among middle school students.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural knowledge of behavioral expectations in specific settings
- Self-regulation and social-emotional skills at Tiers 2 and 3
- School climate and engagement outcomes that indirectly support academic achievement [Belonging and school climate interventions improve student outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Establish a leadership team and secure staff buy-in; commit to using ODR and attendance data for decision-making.
2. Define 3–5 positively stated schoolwide expectations and operationalize them with examples and non-examples for each setting ([Non-Examples](../elements/non-examples.md)).
3. Teach expectations directly at the start of the year through [Modeling](../elements/modeling.md), role-play ([Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md)), and supervised practice in natural settings.
4. Implement a schoolwide acknowledgment system with specific, labeled praise tied to expectations ([Reinforcement](../elements/reinforcement.md)).
5. Screen ODR data at least monthly; route students with repeated referrals to Tier 2 supports such as Check-In/Check-Out ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md)).
6. For students unresponsive to Tier 2, conduct an FBA and build an individualized Tier 3 plan with [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) for the adults implementing it.
7. Review implementation fidelity (e.g., using the TFI — Tiered Fidelity Inventory) twice yearly and adjust.

## Examples
- **[Center on PBIS](https://www.pbis.org)** — the national technical assistance center providing implementation guides, fidelity measures (TFI, SAS), and state implementation networks across the U.S.
- **Maryland PBIS Partnership** — a statewide implementation spanning 800+ schools; Bradshaw et al.'s randomized trials of Maryland PBIS showed reduced suspensions and improved school climate.
- **Check-In/Check-Out (CICO)** — a Tier 2 intervention in which a mentor greets the student each morning, reviews goals, and provides point-based feedback each period, with a brief end-of-day check-out.
