# Design lab activities so students must rely on their notebooks in ways that emulate authentic documentation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that lab activities explicitly incorporate student documentation, because researchers 'emphasized the importance of having authentic use of notebooks' for students to learn documentation skills. Activities should be designed so that students rely on one another's documented work, are multi-staged enough to require synthesizing multiple measurements, provide minimal scaffolding so students record the process themselves, and span enough time that memory is insufficient. Without such design, students are unlikely to develop the skill regardless of framing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lab activities must necessitate use of lab notebooks, commensurate with how scientific documentation is performed, or students are not likely to develop the skill.
- The lab instructor should incorporate consideration for authentic context, audience, and timescale when designing activities.
#### Constraints
- Structured short activities (at most a couple weeks per activity) address context and audience but not timescale, per the article's own stated limitation.
- The authors present broad ideas intended to be adapted, given variation in course design, equipment, class size, and institution type.

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics laboratory students

### Target Learning Goals
- scientific documentation skills
- relying on notebooks to make sense of experiments over time

### Affordances
- Context Audience Timescale Principles
