# Support adherence to home exercise programmes with supervised feedback, reminders, monitoring, and time-efficient, low-complexity exercise design

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy packages the care-provider behaviours and programme-design features the study found patients experience as adherence-supporting: providers should "provides proper feedback and gives reminders during the supervised execution of exercises", explain the clinical condition convincingly, monitor adherence and progress, and prescribe exercises that are quick to perform, easy to begin, and fit into daily routine. It applies to physiotherapy and similar home-based exercise prescriptions for chronic pain patients.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Care providers must observe patient performance during instruction and give feedback and corrections
- Written or printed instructions should be provided as reminders
- Providers should regularly ask about adherence and health progress
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a qualitative focus-group study of patients with chronic neck or low back pain in one Spanish region, interviewed 1-3 months after treatment, so recall bias and limited generalisability are stated weaknesses

### Target Learners
- adult patients with chronic neck or low back pain prescribed home-based exercises

### Target Learning Goals
- adherence to prescribed home exercise programmes
