# Prohibit first-day computer use and student lectures to preserve problem solving and interaction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Two implementable rules the authors used to reinvigorate PBL: banning computers during the first session, because "students would search keywords to solve the case, bypassing the problem solving and reasoning exercise of PBL," and prohibiting student 'lectures,' PowerPoint, and extensive handouts on day two, requiring talking points be conveyed verbally or on whiteboards. Facilitators were instructed to note students who gave lectures instead of fostering discussion and to evaluate students on contributions to group problem solving.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty orientation making evaluation expectations explicit so students do not perceive assessment as based on individual presentation quality
#### Constraints
- Fidelity was imperfect: some faculty who had facilitated PBL for years had difficulty transitioning to the new model

### Target Learners
- medical students in small-group PBL tutorials

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning and problem solving
- interactive discussion rather than passive listening
