# Evaluate VRS curricula under mandatory, protected training time before recommending voluntary implementation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because voluntary uptake of the VRS curriculum was poor, the authors argue future implementation should be tested with mandated simulator training tied to live operating. They state that if such a program is recommended again, "assessment of the program in the setting of mandatory VRS training prior to live operating would be required", and that trainees suggested assessments and defined expectations may increase uptake.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Protected, rostered simulation time within the training week
#### Constraints
- The article states the benefits of the LapSim cannot be accurately assessed if training is not accessed; external motivators such as live operating rewards were already trialled with limited success

### Target Learners
- Novice and experienced gynaecology trainees (pre-vocational residents to senior registrars)

### Target Learning Goals
- Laparoscopic surgical skill development and transfer to live operating
