Develop standardized, validated SBAR training interventions and monitor fidelity within simulation programs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 review), q2–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review's forward-looking recommendations are that "standardized and validated interventions for SBAR training should be researched for effectiveness during nursing education," and that preparing "a method to check and monitor fidelity to SBAR in a simulation program that reproduces the clinical situation is necessary." It also proposes research comparing simulation methodologies such as web-based, high fidelity, and virtual simulations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Fidelity monitoring methods embedded in simulation programs that reproduce the clinical situation

Constraints¶

  • Current programs lack fidelity surveillance, and publication bias may hide studies reporting low fidelity or no clarity improvement

Target Learners¶

  • nursing students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • communication clarity
  • communication fidelity to SBAR

Claims¶

  • Fidelity to SBAR moderates communication clarity improvements: classroom studies show higher fidelity and larger gains than clinical-setting studies [+W]
  • Effects of SBAR-based simulation on communication ability, critical thinking, confidence, learning self-efficacy, and attitude toward patient safety are divergent across studies [+W]
  • The evidence base on SBAR-based simulation for nursing students comprises 12 studies with 886 participants, most rated weak in methodological quality [+W]
  • SBAR-based simulation programs produce significant improvements in communication clarity for nursing students in six of twelve reviewed studies [+W]
  • Structured communication tools such as SBAR incorporated into simulation are associated with improved team coordination and information transfer [+W]
