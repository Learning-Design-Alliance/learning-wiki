# Student/TA one-on-one conferences as a mentoring and student-voice strategy in collegiate science courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article implements student/TA conferences as a means of forming mentoring partnerships and giving students a voice. "The student/TA conferences consisted of 15-minute informal conversations between a student and t he TA," intended to help the TA learn about the whole student and give participants an outlet to be heard and respected. Questions served only as starting points rather than strict guidelines, and the TA followed students' guidance, including when some preferred to use the time for course tutoring.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor willingness to hold informal one-on-one meetings with students
- A structure where students are not penalized for what they share
#### Constraints
- Students were reluctant to voice course opinions, possibly due to the classroom power structure
- Rapport connections did not persist forward without a follow-up plan

### Target Learners
- college students in large introductory science courses

### Target Learning Goals
- student-instructor rapport
- student empowerment and voice
- early identification of struggling students
