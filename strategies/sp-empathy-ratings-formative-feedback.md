# Use simulated patient and examiner empathy ratings as formative feedback in communication skills teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend using empathy ratings as formative feedback rather than summative scores at this stage, since perspective taking and empathy are often explicitly stated in medical school learning outcomes. They suggest "Simulated patients’ feedback can easily be included in communication skills laboratory sessions, and communication skill workshops", and that the ratings "can be used in video feedback sessions or in certain types of problem based learning sessions". The correlation with overall examination performance gives encouragement to further research on summative use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ratings are used formatively at this stage; the authors state more research is needed to test whether the measures are robust enough for summative assessment
#### Constraints
- The study was carried out in one medical school and further replication work would be advisable
- Assessors may have their own attributions based on potentially unrelated factors, such as ethnicity, so adequate training and screening of raters is needed to ensure no discrimination takes place

### Target Learners
- final-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- empathy in patient interactions
- communication skills
