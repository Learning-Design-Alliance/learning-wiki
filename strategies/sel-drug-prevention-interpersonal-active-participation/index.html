Drug-prevention-focused SEL programs should develop intra- and interpersonal and affective competencies with active student participation and interaction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 7 studies (4 quant-synthesis, 2 review, 1 causal), q2–q4 · 4 of 7 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Drawing on the Tobler et al. (2000) meta-analysis of 207 school-based drug prevention programs, the article recommends that programs focus on developing students' interpersonal and affective competencies as well as knowledge about drugs, delivered interactively. The article reports that programs "who had an interactive delivery method, were the most effective", and in its discussion reiterates that interventions developing competencies "such as conflict -resolution, coping with stress and emotional regulation" with "active student participation and interaction, were the most successful in decreasing drug use".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Interactive delivery method and active student participation; focus on interpersonal and affective competencies plus drug knowledge

Constraints¶

  • The effectiveness evidence is attributed to the Tobler et al. (2000) meta-analysis of programs conducted 1978-1998 in Grades 6-12 with random assignment, not to the SET study itself

Target Learners¶

  • adolescents in Grades 6-12 in universal school-based programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reduced drug use via interpersonal, affective and self-regulation competencies

Claims¶

  • Peer programs, including cooperative learning strategies, were concluded to be the single most effective school-based approach for reducing youth alcohol and drug use [+W]
  • Environmentally focused interventions appear to be particularly effective for reducing delinquency and drug use [+W]
  • SEL programs improve social emotional skills [+M]
  • Social-emotional learning benefits persist at follow-up [+M]
