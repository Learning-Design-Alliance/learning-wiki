# Student Led Conferences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Student led conferences (SLCs) replace the traditional teacher-parent conference with a meeting in which the student presents a portfolio of work, evaluates their own progress against criteria, and sets goals for the next period, with the teacher acting as facilitator and families as audience and questioners. Preparation typically spans weeks: students select work samples, complete structured [reflection](../elements/reflection.md) templates, rehearse their presentation, and practice responding to questions. The format converts assessment reporting from something done *to* students into something done *by* them.

## Design Implications

SLCs operationalize [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) by making students active evaluators of their own work, which builds the self-monitoring skills central to [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) [+M]. The act of preparing and delivering an explanation of one's learning is a form of learning-by-teaching: articulating progress to an authentic audience deepens the presenter's own understanding and metacognitive awareness [Learning by teaching improves the tutor's own learning.](../claims/learning-by-teaching-improves-tutor-learning.md) [+M]. Because students must justify judgments against criteria, SLCs also strengthen feedback literacy — students learn to interpret and act on [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) rather than merely receive it [Feedback is most effective at the task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A [portfolio](../elements/portfolio.md) or curated collection of work samples spanning the reporting period
- Explicit success criteria and rubrics students can use to self-assess ([Rubrics](../elements/rubrics.md))
- Structured preparation time: work selection, written reflection, goal-setting, and rehearsal
- Teacher pre-conference coaching so students can articulate evidence, not just enthusiasm
- Family orientation — families need guidance on their role (asking questions, giving process-focused praise) since the format is unfamiliar to most

#### Constraints
- Without genuine student ownership of work selection and evaluation, SLCs degrade into teacher-scripted performances [-M] — students reading a teacher-prepared script learn little about self-assessment
- Families who expect traditional grade reporting may experience the format as evasive if achievement data is not presented alongside student narratives [~M]; hybrid formats that include a brief teacher data summary mitigate this
- Students with strong communication anxiety or limited language proficiency may find the public presentation format counterproductive without accommodations ([Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md)) [-W]
- Requires significant instructional time for preparation; schools that bolt SLCs onto existing schedules without reducing other demands see shallow preparation [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Portfolio defense model** — students present curated evidence against rubrics; common in project-based schools such as [Expeditionary Learning / EL Education](https://eleducation.org) network schools
- **Hybrid model** — student presentation followed by a three-way conversation with teacher and family
- **Whole-class workshop model** — multiple students present simultaneously in stations, with families rotating; used where conference time is limited
- **Digital portfolios** — platforms such as [Seesaw](https://web.seesaw.me) and [Google Sites](https://sites.google.com) host ongoing work collections that feed the conference

### Target Learners
- Most effective for upper elementary through secondary students, who have the metacognitive capacity for criterion-based self-evaluation [+W]
- Particularly valuable for students developing autonomy and ownership of learning; giving students authentic responsibility for presenting their progress supports intrinsic motivation [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+M]
- Requires scaffolding for younger students or those new to self-assessment; simplified reflection templates and rehearsal are essential, not optional

### Target Learning Goals
- Metacognition and self-regulation: evaluating one's own work against criteria and setting goals
- Communication and academic discourse: explaining and defending learning to an authentic audience
- Ownership and motivation: shifting locus of control for learning toward the student

### Instructions
1. **Establish criteria early.** Ensure every piece of work in the portfolio was completed against explicit rubrics students have already used for [self-assessment](../elements/self-assessment.md).
2. **Curate work samples.** Students select 3–5 artifacts demonstrating growth, struggle, and achievement — selection with justification matters more than volume.
3. **Reflect in writing.** Students complete structured reflection prompts linking each artifact to goals and criteria, using [Reflection](../elements/reflection.md) templates.
4. **Set goals.** Students draft specific, measurable next-step goals with teacher feedback during [check-ins](../elements/check-in.md).
5. **Rehearse.** Students practice the presentation with peers, receiving process-level feedback [Feedback is most effective at the task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
6. **Hold the conference.** Student presents; teacher facilitates and adds data the student may have missed; families ask prepared questions.
7. **Follow up.** Goals from the conference feed into subsequent [goal-setting](../elements/goal-setting.md) cycles so the conference is a checkpoint, not a terminus.

## Examples
- **EL Education (Expeditionary Learning) network schools** — passage presentations and student-led family conferences are a signature practice, with students defending portfolios before panels ([eleducation.org](https://eleducation.org))
- **High Tech High (San Diego, CA)** — students present digital portfolios of project work at student-led exhibitions and conferences each semester ([hightechhigh.org](https://www.hightechhigh.org))
- **Seesaw student-led conferences** — many elementary schools use Seesaw's student-created digital journals as the artifact base, with students walking families through selected entries
