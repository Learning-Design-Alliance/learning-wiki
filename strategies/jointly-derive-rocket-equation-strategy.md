# Derive the rocket equation jointly with learners, depending on their mathematical background

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
For classes with sufficient mathematical preparation, the handout suggests deriving the Tsiolkowski rocket equation together with the learners rather than presenting it ready-made. The background chapter develops the equation twice over — from Newton's second law and from momentum conservation — first for a force-free single-stage rocket and then for vertical ascent against gravity, so the joint derivation can follow either or both routes. This positions the equation as something students construct and understand rather than a formula to plug into.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must have the stated mathematics prerequisites (differential calculus) and mechanics fundamentals before attempting the derivation
#### Constraints
- The derivation in the material relies on simplifying assumptions, e.g. constant exhaust velocity w and constant mass flow µ, which the text notes do not hold in reality

### Target Learners
- upper-secondary students (grades 10–13) with differential calculus background

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding the derivation of the rocket equation
- connecting Newtonian mechanics and momentum conservation to rocket flight
