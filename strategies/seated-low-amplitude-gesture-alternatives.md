# Offer seated and low-amplitude gesture alternatives so embodied learning systems remain accessible to all participants

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends building accessibility into movement-based learning systems by providing alternatives for participants who cannot or prefer not to perform full-body movements. In both the study and the child-participation section, the authors note that "Seated and low-amplitude gesture alternatives were available to any participant who required them." The system is also designed with tolerance for variation to ensure accessibility for users with limited mobility or space. This strategy keeps embodied interaction usable in constrained everyday settings without excluding learners.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The interaction system must support alternative gesture amplitudes, e.g. seated or low-amplitude movements
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as an availability for any participant who required them, within a webcam-only, small-space system

### Target Learners
- users with limited mobility or space
- children aged 5-16

### Target Learning Goals
- participation in embodied data literacy practice
