Teach speech acts explicitly with attention to cross-cultural differences in EFL programs¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors recommend that teachers help students learn appropriate techniques through comprehensive teaching methodologies as an indispensable part of EFL teaching programs, examining communicative teaching in which "speech acts are explicitly taught with due attention to the cross-cultural differences" between Persian and English. They argue implicit teaching leads learners astray in choosing proper replies, and conclude that some form of metapragmatic instruction — deductive, inductive, explicit or implicit — is essential.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instruction that makes the processes of pragmatic decision-making explicit to learners

Constraints¶

  • Evidence base is one quasi-experiment on the request speech act with intermediate female learners at institute level in Iran

Target Learners¶

  • Iranian intermediate EFL learners at institutes, schools, and universities

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sociolinguistic conventions of language use and cross-cultural understanding

Claims¶

  • Explicit metapragmatic instruction on requests raises request speech act awareness of intermediate EFL learners relative to regular institute instruction [+M]
  • The authors interpret their results as showing that explicit metapragmatic instruction provides input enhancement that facilitates interlanguage pragmatic development [+W]
  • Before instruction, the experimental and control groups did not differ significantly in request speech act use [+W]
