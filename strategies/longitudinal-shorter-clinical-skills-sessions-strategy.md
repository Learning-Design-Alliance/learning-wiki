# Divide lengthy clinical-skills workshops into a longitudinal series of shorter sessions to ameliorate fatigue and enable spaced repetition

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Based on student and facilitator feedback that productivity declined near the end of the 3-hour session, the authors recommend "dividing the session into a longitudinal series of shorter sessions over time," which would ameliorate end-of-session fatigue and "allow for spaced repetition of the concepts." They note spaced learning has been shown to increase long-term retention and could also address the decline in student confidence at the 4-month mark, with case difficulty increased progressively and facilitator guidance decreased as students gain competence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple sessions spanning the preclerkship clinical skills course and clerkships
- Progressively increasing case or concept difficulty over the clerkship period
#### Constraints
- The authors state this recommendation is prospective; their own session was a single 3-hour workshop and the spaced version was not implemented or tested

### Target Learners
- Medical students in transition-to-clerkship or clinical skills curricula

### Target Learning Goals
- Retain focused physical exam and clinical reasoning skills over time
