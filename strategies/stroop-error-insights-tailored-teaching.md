# Use error rates from simple-rule tests such as the Stroop Test to identify students' conscientiousness and concentration weaknesses and address them through tailored teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (1 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators use error rates from the Stroop Test — or "any other test where subjects need to follow a relatively simple rule" — as indicative of conscientiousness, the way students approach an assigned task, and their ability to handle it, and then address revealed weaknesses "through tailored teaching approaches, such as individualized activities".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The test's potential to measure manifested conscientiousness and the ability to concentrate on an assigned, relatively short, one-off task should be studied further before such use.
#### Constraints
- The error–grade relationship on which this recommendation rests had, per the article, not been studied before the present study.

### Target Learners
- university students in teacher education programs
- adult learners

### Target Learning Goals
- fostering conscientiousness and concentration
- improving academic performance
