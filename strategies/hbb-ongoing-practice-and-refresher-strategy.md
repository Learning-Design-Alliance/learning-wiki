# Sustain resuscitation skills with daily practice, equipment checks, supportive supervision, and periodic refresher training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because skills decayed more than knowledge after initial training, the article recommends ongoing skills practice and monitoring, more frequent retesting, and refresher trainings. The study's monitoring program included "daily BMV practice sessions when BAs reported for work", daily equipment checks, regular observation of deliveries, debriefing after every resuscitation, death audits, monthly quality-assurance visits, and biweekly calls reviewing every resuscitated newborn. A half-day refresher course at roughly six months restored OSCE B pass rates to 99%.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facility master trainers to conduct monitoring activities and refresher sessions
- Logbooks and standard report formats to record practice compliance and resuscitation events
#### Constraints
- Quantitative practice data were not recorded; physicians were described as less compliant with daily BMV practice than nurses
- The article notes no evidence-based, effective strategy for maintaining resuscitation knowledge and skills has been identified and existing evidence is of low quality

### Target Learners
- Facility birth attendants who attend deliveries regularly

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of neonatal resuscitation skills over time

### Affordances
- Helping Babies Breathe Training Program
