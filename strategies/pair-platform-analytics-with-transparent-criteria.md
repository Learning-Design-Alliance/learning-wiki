# Pair convenient platform analytics with transparent, education-specific evaluation criteria rather than trusting either alone

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends treating evaluation itself as a first-class research object: reporting what a metric measures and "pairing convenient platform analytics with transparent, education-specific criteria rather than trusting either alone." Because platform scores index student performance while rubrics index agent teaching behavior, the two answer different questions and should complement rather than replace each other. A pragmatic division of labor follows: automated scoring is trusted most for dimensions where it is demonstrably calibrated, while human judgment is reserved for contested, subjective dimensions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A transparent, multidimensional rubric validated against human expert judgment
- Human calibration of automated scoring, especially for subjective or affective dimensions
#### Constraints
- Automated and expert judgments aligned well on cognitive-process dimensions but poorly on more subjective, affective ones

### Target Learners
- medical students
- educators adopting AI teaching agents

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate evaluation of AI tutor teaching quality
- agent selection and refinement decisions
