# Use mentor discussions and participation consequences, and avoid or reframe choice, to increase serious test-taking in low-stakes assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article closes with explicit recommendations for curriculum planners to increase serious test-taking behavior in low-stakes assessments: "Let low performers discuss their results with a mentor to show that faculty cares", "Have consequences for not participating to show that the low stakes test is an important part of the curriculum", avoid giving choice of place and time, and if choice must be given, "integrate the BPT into the assessment system rather than into the evaluation system". Each recommendation rests on the corresponding regression finding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Applies to low-stakes progress testing contexts where test-taking effort is measured and institutional strategies can be implemented
#### Constraints
- The study is quasi-experimental; schools self-selected strategies, so causal statements are improper
- Generalization is limited because schools self-selected into the progress test consortium

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- serious test-taking effort in low-stakes formative and large-scale assessment
