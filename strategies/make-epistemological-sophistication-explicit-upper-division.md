# Make epistemological issues of mathematical use explicit in upper-division physics instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (8 for) · 4 studies (3 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors suggest that many apparent difficulties with math at the journeyman level are actually difficulties with juggling and integrating different mathematical lenses, and that these issues typically develop tacitly as part of the hidden curriculum. They propose that "making these issues of epistemological sophistication explicit may be productive in creating upper division instruction that is effective for a larger fraction our physics majors." Instructors should surface questions of what knowledge counts as relevant and what counts as proof in a given problem context.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must recognize epistemological framing issues rather than attributing student struggles to lacking mathematical skill
#### Constraints
- The article states experts may take epistemic tool integration for granted and may not realize the nature of the difficulty their students are having

### Target Learning Goals
