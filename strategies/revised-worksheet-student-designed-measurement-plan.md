# Use a revised laboratory worksheet that guides students to decide measurement duration and number, discuss uncertainty sources, and preestimate uncertainty ranges before taking data

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author expanded the experiment's objectives so that, besides finding attenuation coefficients, "the students are engaged partly to the design of the experiment to realize in practice the importance of measurement uncertainty". The revised worksheet inserts questions guiding students to decide the duration and number of measurements, discuss the main sources of uncertainty and how to reduce it, and preestimate the uncertainty range using the derived relation for representative cases, then use those results to justify measurements or choose absorber thicknesses giving the most distinct measurements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The worksheet is accompanied by personal instructions, and requires the background and source counting rates so uncertainty can be determined at the beginning of the experimental process.
#### Constraints
- First findings come from a single-semester test with 18 students (6 groups of 3); the author reports only preliminary results.

### Target Learners
- non-physics-major undergraduate students in an introductory physics laboratory

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding measurement uncertainty
- connecting uncertainty to measurement interpretation and experimental design
