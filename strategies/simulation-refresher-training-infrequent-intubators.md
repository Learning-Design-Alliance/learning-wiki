# Simulation-based refresher training and proactive skills maintenance for physicians who infrequently perform intubation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advocates that "simulation training has become widely available, we advocate for this modality as a platform for active maintenance and advancement of procedural skills", particularly for providers below the three-performed or five-supervised annual intubation thresholds. In the authors' department, attendings who performed poorly were required to complete a department-funded airway refresher course plus simulation-center training and repeat assessment, and scores "dramatically improved" on re-assessment. This initiative grew into an ongoing simulation-based faculty skills advancement curriculum.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to simulation facilities and protected time for refresher training
- Assessment scores to identify providers below the proficiency cut score
#### Constraints
- Simulation is not real life and requires a suspension of disbelief; task trainers may not accurately replicate human anatomy
- Assessment on a simulator must be authentic to imply similar performance with real patients

### Target Learners
- Attending emergency physicians, especially pediatric EM and other low-frequency intubators

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintenance of endotracheal intubation proficiency over the practitioner's career
