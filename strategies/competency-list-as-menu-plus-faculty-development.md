# Treat the consensus competency and practice list as a menu of options and invest in faculty development while prioritization and evaluation research proceed

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that, pending prioritization studies, "educators and others may choose to view this list of competencies and practices as a menu of options," and that the widespread lack of awareness regarding health literacy suggests "faculty development will be an essential part of efforts to improve the health literacy competencies of students of the health professions." They also call for developing and validating evaluation tools and conducting comparative outcome studies to help prioritize the lists.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Development and validation of tools to evaluate the various health literacy competencies and practices
#### Constraints
- The lists are too long and unprioritized to be of optimal value to educators, administrators, and policymakers in their current state

### Target Learners
- health professions educators and faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- improving health literacy training and competencies of health professions students
