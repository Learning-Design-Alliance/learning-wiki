# Offer overdose-resuscitation simulation in community settings such as shelters to increase accessibility for people who use opioids

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend expanding simulation-based education beyond academic settings, noting low-technology and in-situ simulation may increase accessibility and portability. They state participants "recommended that simulation be offered in shelters and other settings in which overdoses occur frequently", framing increased accessibility as a potential paradigm shift toward community-based simulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Simulation protocols that participants find acceptable and realistic; community collaboration in design, as done with community members with lived experience
#### Constraints
- Currently, simulation-based education is primarily reserved for healthcare learners and is not widely available to non-clinicians

### Target Learners
- People who use opioids and their friends and family likely to witness overdose

### Target Learning Goals
- Bystander overdose response, naloxone administration, EMS activation, CPR
