# Implementation strategies for establishing a centralized tutor training program: central coordination, stakeholder inclusion, and evaluation of attained skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article distills design and implementation experiences into recommendations for comparable programs. A "central coordination point has proven itself valuable for planning, realization and administration", with a half-time (50% minimum) academically qualified position recommended. Further recommendations include including all responsible people from the start in curricular development to avoid resistance, networking individual departments to use existing school resources, holding regular group meetings such as supervisions to maintain tutor commitment, and evaluating the learning process through portfolios and supervisor feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A central coordination point, recommended as a half-time position (50% minimum) filled by an academically qualified person
#### Constraints
- Centralization of tutor training may take weight off individual departments but means a diminishment in the autonomy of decentralized structures

### Target Learners
- medical student peer tutors
- university faculty and teaching coordinators

### Target Learning Goals
- sustainable acquisition of teaching competencies
- quality assurance and professionalism in medical teaching
