# Match the feedback format to the educational goal: short immediate direct-observation feedback for busy clinics, delayed video-based feedback for professionalism, communication and clinical reasoning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends applying the two formative OSCE feedback formats differently according to the goals of the teaching session. For "daily clinical supervision in busy clinics, short, immediate feedback following direct observation focusing on 'single-loop' reflection appears to be the most suitable format given time constraints", facilitated by the Mini-Clinical Evaluation Exercise. Delayed, video-based feedback is recommended for longer, less frequent sessions addressing professionalism, communication, and clinical reasoning, where prior self-assessment, analysis and discussion of performance, and questioning of frames of reference may increase self-awareness and support pedagogical diagnosis and remediation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video-based format requires a videotaped encounter, student self-review beforehand, and a longer (40-min) delayed feedback session one week later
#### Constraints
- Direct observation format is limited by short and rigid feedback time; video-based format demands more time and less frequent scheduling

### Target Learners
- preclinical medical students preparing for clerkships
- trainees in daily clinical supervision

### Target Learning Goals
- communication skills
- professionalism
- clinical reasoning
- physical examination
- history taking

### Affordances
- Single Double Loop Learning Feedback Formats
