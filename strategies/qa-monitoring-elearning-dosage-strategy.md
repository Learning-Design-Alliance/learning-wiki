# Control e-learning dosage and engagement through Q&A monitoring and participant sharing of questions, thoughts, and experiences

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article identifies dosage control as a limitation of e-learning interventions: educators cannot lead learners through training sessions as in face-to-face settings. The El-Poems study addresses this "by asking the participants about the content and monitoring the amount of their attention to the program through the question & answer method (Q&A)", and participants are requested to share questions, thoughts, and experiences about proper posture, MP, and the intervention to compensate for the lack of group discussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Online sessions must include structured Q&A opportunities and prompts for participants to record and share their thoughts and experiences
#### Constraints
- The article states group discussion is a very important method for the success of such interventions and its absence remains a limitation of e-learning delivery

### Target Learners
- Adolescent music students in online health education programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustained engagement with and attention to postural education content
