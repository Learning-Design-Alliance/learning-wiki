# Evaluate short-term teaching innovations with longitudinal, skills-based assessments and delayed post-tests rather than a single written test

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 review), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their pilot's limitations, the authors recommend more comprehensive and longitudinal assessment of educational interventions. They specifically name objective structured clinical examinations to evaluate practical skills, validated instruments to measure clinical reasoning, and delayed post-tests at 3 or 6 months to assess knowledge retention and transfer to clinical practice, since a 20-question written test may not capture the full breadth of knowledge acquisition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment instruments covering practical skills and clinical reasoning, not only foundational knowledge
- Follow-up testing at delayed time points (e.g., 3 or 6 months)
#### Constraints
- Recommended in the context of a single-session (approximately 4 h) internship teaching intervention

### Target Learners
- medical interns in clinical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- practical clinical skills
- clinical reasoning
- knowledge retention and transfer
