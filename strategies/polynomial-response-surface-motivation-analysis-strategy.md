# Use polynomial regression with response surface analysis to test congruence and incongruence of two predictors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that researchers test linear and curvilinear (quadratic) relations jointly, rather than assuming linear relations, when examining how two predictors combine to predict an outcome. Centering scores around the scale midpoint (not the mean), comparing first-order, interaction, and full polynomial models via F-change tests, then bootstrapping slopes and curvatures along the lines of congruence and incongruence lets researchers locate "at what levels of autonomous motivation or controlled motivation, a motivational correlate will be even higher (or lower)". The approach also guards against inflated Type I error when testing interactions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Both predictors measured on comparable scales and centered around their scale midpoints before model fitting; bootstrap resampling to obtain confidence intervals for slopes and curvatures
#### Constraints
- The article notes that different combinations of linear and nonlinear relations can yield similar response-surface patterns, so no accurate prediction of the strength of individual relations is possible from the surface alone; only two of approximately five hundred SDT studies had used this approach

### Target Learners
- Researchers studying motivation-outcome relations in school populations

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate modeling of how autonomous and controlled motivation jointly predict academic achievement
