Model ultrasound simulation training on aviation: routine, mandatory, ongoing assessment for all grades¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review proposes that further investigation and deployment of ultrasound simulation "should follow models closer to pilot training, were training, and ongoing assessment are routine, mandatory, and completed by all grades." In commercial aviation, initial training and recurrent simulator assessment occur every 6 months, with mandatory emergency sessions creating immersive experiences; the article argues medical education should adopt this routine, universal, recurring structure rather than treating simulation as an optional extra.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Simulator sessions embedded as routine and mandatory across all grades of trainee
  • Recurring assessment intervals analogous to recurrent pilot simulator checks

Constraints¶

  • None of the simulators described to date have addressed the clinical context, communication, or clinical management that full-motion flight simulators address for aviation

Target Learners¶

  • doctors and allied health professionals training in obstetric ultrasound at all grades

Target Learning Goals¶

  • sustained obstetric ultrasound competence
  • ongoing competency assessment

Affordances¶

  • Simulation Waypoint Semiautonomous Practice

Claims¶

  • Mandated faculty participation achieved the highest simulator engagement (90%), and simulator-based assessment challenged a department's beliefs about resident preparedness [+M]
  • Meta-analysis finds a favorable effect of high-fidelity ultrasound simulation training on accuracy of fetal biometry in obstetric ultrasound [+M]
  • All four meta-analyzed studies reported positive effects of simulator training on novice performance, in CRL and FL measurement regardless of simulator model [+M]
