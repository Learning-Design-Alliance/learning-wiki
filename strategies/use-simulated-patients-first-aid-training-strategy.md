# Use simulated patients during first aid training for laypeople to improve long-term knowledge retention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that involving simulated patients in layperson first aid training supports the use of this method: "These results support the use of simulated patients during first aid training." The mechanism the authors propose is that the simulated patient influences the hardwiring of new information in memory, possibly by inducing a mild level of stress that has been suggested to enhance learning, or by adding to the diversity of educational approaches. The strategy is implementable within existing certification trainings by scheduling the simulated patient during specific practical-exercise topics.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulated patient actor professionally involved in patient simulation
- Standardized training content and professional first aid trainers
- A group discussion after first aid is provided to the simulated patient
#### Constraints
- No immediate improvement in knowledge, self-efficacy, skills, or satisfaction was demonstrated; the benefit was limited to 1-year knowledge retention
- Allocation was by availability of simulated patients rather than completely random, and 1-year retention data were collected partly via online survey

### Target Learners
- laypeople in occupational first aid certification training

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of first aid knowledge over 1 year
