# Integrate simulation courses and structured sub-step assistance, including an Education Team-Time-Out, into emergency surgical training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 unmarked) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose improving emergency surgical training by combining in-clinic sub-step assistance with simulation-based courses, and suggest mandatory integration of (simulation) courses into the training regulations analogous to US surgical curricula. They write that "Even in an emergency, it may be possible to assist with sub-steps in order to achieve better training" and that a short Education Team-Time-Out can define the assistant's skills before elective and emergency operations. Course formats like the ACT! course are seen as one building block in longitudinal competence acquisition.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Employer support for course participation, including exemption and cost absorption, since employers in Germany are not yet paid for the further training of doctors
#### Constraints
- Course formats can only represent one building block in the longitudinal acquisition of competence, per the authors
- The article notes the financing question of mandatory simulation courses remains open

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees (assistant doctors) in general and visceral surgery
- training assistants and specialists supervising emergency operations

### Target Learning Goals
- emergency surgical operative competence
- management of abdominal trauma interventions
- assistance with operative sub-steps
