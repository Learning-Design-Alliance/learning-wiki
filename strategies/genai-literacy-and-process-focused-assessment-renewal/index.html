Renew writing assessment and integrity practice through AI-literacy instruction, process-focused assessment design, and contextual detector training¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

Rather than relying on detectors as gatekeeping mechanisms, the article recommends that institutions focus on integrating explicit instruction on responsible AI use, redesigning assessments to focus on process, reflection, and transparency, developing policies that distinguish acceptable AI assistance from deceptive practices, and providing faculty training on interpreting detector outputs cautiously and contextually. Embedding GenAI literacy and ethical awareness into curriculum and practice is presented as a way to cultivate informed, responsible learners while mitigating the limitations of detection technologies.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional commitment to curriculum and policy renewal
  • Faculty development on detector output interpretation

Constraints¶

  • The article frames these as responses to detector limitations; over-policing through detection can damage trust and place unfair scrutiny on certain linguistic backgrounds

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students
  • EFL learners
  • university faculty

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Responsible and ethical GenAI use in academic writing
  • transparent authorship practices

Related Strategies¶

  • Adapt non-invigilated written assessments and their marking criteria in response to Gen-AI susceptibility
  • Institutional recommendations: shift from detection to process-oriented assessment, transparent AI-use protocols, and revised integrity policies
  • Provide explicit instructional boundary cues, process-oriented assessment, and peer modeling of verification to frame responsible GenAI use
  • Cultivate learners' critical AI literacy and integrity-focused group practices for GenAI-supported group work
  • Frame LLM integration as an assessable AI literacy competency with differentiated integrity policies and reasoning-focused assessment redesign
