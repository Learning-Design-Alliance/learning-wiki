# Package existing physics electives into named emphasis programs (business, astronomy, computational physics, optics) to attract majors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The department re-packaged existing courses and electives into named emphasis programs that make specializations and career paths explicit to students, parents and teachers. The article argues that 'Physics now needs to be marketed as does any other product, and the department has to have the products to suggest and then deliver to students.' Emphasis areas were chosen deliberately to match department expertise and job markets.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Department expertise in the chosen emphasis areas
- Enough elective courses to form coherent packages
#### Constraints
- Electives already existed at many departments but, from the student, parent, teacher point of view, 'the existence of these electives and what they can do is often lost'

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physics majors and prospective majors

### Target Learning Goals
- Career awareness and degree completion in physics
