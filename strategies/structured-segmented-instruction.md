# Structured/Segmented Instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Structured instruction involves systematically teaching information that has been chunked into manageable pieces. Complex skills or large bodies of information are analyzed into component parts, which are taught in a planned sequence; after the parts are mastered, they are recombined so learners perceive the process or concept as a whole. The strategy pairs segmentation (ordering) with chunking (sizing) — the instructor controls both what is presented at once and in what order.

## Design Implications

Structured/segmented instruction works because working memory is severely limited for novel material; presenting content in coherent, sequenced segments prevents overload and allows schemas to consolidate between segments [Chunking reduces working memory load by grouping information into fewer, more meaningful units.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Segmentation is most effective when each segment ends with a summary or practice activity that consolidates the part before the next begins [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S]. The critical design decision is segment size: segments must be large enough to be meaningful and small enough to be processed — and they should shrink as expertise grows [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task or content analysis that identifies genuine component parts and their dependencies, not arbitrary divisions
- A deliberate sequence — simple to complex, part to whole, or prerequisite-first — with explicit links between segments ([Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) help learners see where each part fits)
- Consolidation activity at the end of each segment ([Practice](../elements/practice.md), [Self-Explanation](../elements/self-explanation.md), or summary)
- A final integration phase that reassembles the parts into the whole task or concept

#### Constraints
- Segmentation that fragments meaningful structures harms learning: isolating parts that only make sense together (e.g., phonemes without words, grammar rules without discourse) forces learners to do the integrating themselves [-M]
- Over-segmentation creates passive, step-following behavior; learners may master parts without ever building an integrated schema [-M]
- Ineffective for learners with substantial prior knowledge, who benefit from whole-task presentation and find part-task sequencing redundant [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [-M]
- Perceived as rigid or slow by learners who could handle larger steps; requires significant instructor preparation time

#### Implementation Variability
- **Part-task practice**: components drilled in isolation before integration — appropriate only for absolute novices on complex recurrent skill components [Part-task practice reduces cognitive load for absolute novices during initial skill acquisition.](../claims/part-task-practice-reduces-load-for-novices.md) [+M]
- **Whole-task with simplifying assumptions**: teach the full task in simplified form first, then add complexity across segments — preferable for non-recurrent skills where parts lack meaning in isolation
- **Example–problem pairs within segments**: alternate worked examples with practice problems inside each segment [Example–problem pairs reduce extraneous load relative to problem solving alone.](../claims/example-problem-sequences-reduce-cognitive-load.md) [+M]
- **Learner-controlled segmentation**: in multimedia and e-learning, letting learners advance segments at their own pace improves outcomes compared with continuous presentation [Segmentation and learner pacing improve learning from multimedia instruction.](../claims/media-combinations-affect-recall-and-retention.md) [~M]

### Target Learners
- Novices with little prior knowledge, for whom unsegmented whole-task presentation overwhelms working memory [Chunking reduces working memory load by grouping information into fewer, more meaningful units.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Learners with learning disabilities or limited literacy, who benefit from explicit structure and small instructional steps
- Adult basic-skills learners returning to education, who need a clear, organized framework
- Less suitable for advanced learners, who perform worse under part-task sequencing than under whole-task conditions [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [-M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural skill acquisition: complex recurrent skills decomposable into consistent steps
- Schema construction for complex content: building an organized mental model piece by piece
- Retention of structured bodies of information, especially when segments are revisited over time [Spaced revisiting of segments improves long-term retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. **Analyze the task**: identify component skills and knowledge, and their prerequisite relationships; decide which parts can carry meaning in isolation and which must be taught in context.
2. **State objectives** for each segment so learners know what mastery of the part looks like ([State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md)).
3. **Sequence the segments**: order from prerequisite to dependent, simple to complex; plan the integration point from the start.
4. **Teach each segment explicitly** with modeling and worked examples ([Direct Instruction](../elements/direct-instruction.md), [Provide Guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md)).
5. **Consolidate each segment** with practice and feedback before moving on ([Practice](../elements/practice.md), [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)) [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
6. **Integrate**: recombine the parts in a whole task or synthesis activity so learners experience the concept or process as a coherent whole.

## Patterns That Use This Strategy
- [Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) — scripted, sequenced delivery of decomposed content
- [4C/ID](../patterns/4cid-four-component-instructional-design.md) — part-task practice for recurrent skills embedded in whole-task sequences
- [Gagné's 9 Events](../patterns/gagnés-9-events-of-instruction.md) — "present the content" and "provide learning guidance" events operationalize segmentation

## Examples
- **Decoding instruction**: breaking a decoding strategy into steps (identify vowel patterns, segment sounds, blend), teaching each step individually, then combining them to decode whole words; prefixes and suffixes taught separately before combining them in morphological analysis.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — math content organized into short segmented videos and exercises within a mastery-sequenced structure; learners cannot advance until each segment's exercises are completed.
- **[Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com)** — language content segmented into small skills sequenced by prerequisite relationships, with spaced review of earlier segments [Spaced revisiting of segments improves long-term retention.](../claims/spaced-repetition-improves-retention.md) [+S].
- **Driver education programs** — classroom rules, then simulation, then controlled practice of component maneuvers, then integrated on-road driving.
