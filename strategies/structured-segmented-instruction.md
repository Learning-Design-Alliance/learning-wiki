# Structured/Segmented Instruction

## Description
Structured instruction involves systematically teaching information that has been chunked into manageable pieces. Complex skills or large bodies of information are broken into parts, which are taught systematically according to a planned sequence. Each task is analyzed and broken into its component parts, and then after teaching the parts systematically, bring them back together so learners are aware of the process or concept as a whole.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires instructors to analyze tasks and break them into component parts. Requires careful planning and sequencing of instruction.
#### Constraints
- May be perceived as overly structured or rigid by some learners. Requires significant planning and preparation time for instructors.

- Reduces cognitive load by breaking information into smaller, more manageable chunks [Chunking reduces working memory load by grouping information into fewer, more meaningful units.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M], consistent with part-task practice for absolute novices during initial skill acquisition [Part-task practice reduces cognitive load for absolute novices during initial skill acquisition.](../claims/part-task-practice-reduces-load-for-novices.md) [+M]. ### Personalization
- Can be adapted to individual learner needs by adjusting the size of the chunks and providing varied examples and practice opportunities. Sequencing can be tailored to support learners with different levels of prior knowledge.
