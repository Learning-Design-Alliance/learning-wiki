# Use feedback after standardized patient encounters and flexible delivery modes to improve future clinic-based STI training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' implications for future training recommend: using unannounced SPs as a training method rather than only for data collection, with SPs giving clinicians "a handout about STI tasks" at disclosure or a training coordinator providing constructive feedback after the encounter; using paper-based and computer-based materials to train new staff and shift workers who cannot attend scheduled sessions; adding basic computer tutorials before computer-based training; providing the computer voiceover in multiple languages; and pairing training with 'syndrome packets' of supplies as tested by Colvin et al.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clinic routines that allow SP disclosure at the end of encounters, where a handout or coordinator feedback can be delivered
#### Constraints
- The article frames these as future recommendations from a pilot study; the SP-feedback idea was not tested in this pilot

### Target Learners
- doctors and nurses at primary healthcare clinics, including new staff and shift workers

### Target Learning Goals
- application of revised STI syndromic management guidelines in clinical encounters
