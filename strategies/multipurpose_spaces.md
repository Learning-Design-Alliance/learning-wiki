# Multipurpose Spaces

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
A multipurpose space is a physical learning environment designed for rapid reconfiguration — movable furniture, writable surfaces, accessible storage, and flexible power/technology — so the same room can host presentation, [class discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md), small-group collaboration, and independent work within a single session. The design goal is that the environment's affordances match the pedagogy of the moment rather than forcing activity to fit a fixed layout.

## Design Implications

The physical environment functions as a channel of [instruction](../principles/clear-structure-presentation.md): layout signals expected interaction patterns, and mismatch between arrangement and activity imposes friction that competes with learning goals. Evidence on active learning shows that pedagogy matters more than furniture per se, but reconfigurable space removes a practical barrier to implementing [Active Learning](../principles/active-learning.md) [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]. Environmental features that attract attention without supporting the task (busy displays, competing noise) measurably reduce learning, especially for young learners [Fisher, Godwin, & Seltman (2014) found highly decorated classrooms reduced attention and learning in young children.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [~M] — so flexibility must be paired with the ability to *reduce* stimulation, not just add it.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lightweight, mobile furniture (casters, nesting tables, stackable chairs) that one instructor can rearrange in under a few minutes
- Writable surfaces distributed around the room so group work is visible and shareable
- Reliable wireless connectivity and power access at multiple points, so technology does not anchor the room to one configuration
- Storage that lets non-current materials leave the visual field [Fisher, Godwin, & Seltman (2014) found highly decorated classrooms reduced attention and learning in young children.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [~M]
- Acoustic treatment sufficient that simultaneous small-group activity remains intelligible ([Acoustics and Noise Management](acoustics_and_noise_management.md))

#### Constraints
- Frequent reconfiguration consumes instructional time and adds transition costs; if switching takes more than a few minutes, teachers default to the static layout [-M]
- Open, highly flexible layouts can increase ambient noise and visual distraction, harming attention and off-task behavior, particularly for younger learners and students with attention difficulties [-M]
- Flexibility without teacher training yields little benefit: instructors tend to teach in the arrangement they know regardless of what the room permits [~M]
- Poorly zoned multipurpose rooms (no acoustic or visual separation) make simultaneous activities interfere with one another [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Rolling/flexible furniture only** — lowest cost; works within existing rooms
- **Zoned rooms** — fixed zones for different activity types (quiet corner, collaboration tables, presentation area) trading full flexibility for lower transition cost
- **Learning studios / SCALE-UP-style rooms** — round tables for 6–9 students with shared displays, designed specifically for [collaborative learning](../patterns/collaborative-learning.md)
- **Makerspaces and shared commons** — multipurpose design extended across a facility rather than a single room

### Target Learners
- Students in discussion- and project-based courses where activity type changes within a session [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]
- Young learners benefit from *low-stimulation* flexible design, but are most harmed by visually cluttered or noisy flexible spaces [Fisher, Godwin, & Seltman (2014) found highly decorated classrooms reduced attention and learning in young children.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [-M]
- Students with attention or sensory-processing challenges, for whom the ability to move to a lower-stimulation zone is a functional [accommodation](../elements/accommodations.md)

### Target Learning Goals
- Collaborative skills: peer discussion, group problem-solving, shared artifact creation
- Higher-order thinking in [active learning](../principles/active-learning.md) formats: application, analysis, argumentation
- Self-regulated work: zones supporting independent focus alongside social modes

### Instructions
1. Inventory the activity types the course actually uses (presentation, discussion, group work, individual work) and map each to a layout.
2. Select furniture and storage so every mapped layout is achievable in under five minutes by the instructor alone.
3. Establish reconfiguration routines with students so transitions are fast and orderly ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) routines can anchor transitions).
4. Zone the room acoustically and visually so simultaneous activities do not interfere.
5. Keep displays and decorations minimal and task-relevant; rotate rather than accumulate [Fisher, Godwin, & Seltman (2014) found highly decorated classrooms reduced attention and learning in young children.](../claims/relevancy-of-emphasis-directs-attention.md) [~M]
6. Pair the space with pedagogy: a flexible room used for lecture-only delivers no benefit [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S]

## Examples
- **SCALE-UP (North Carolina State University)** — purpose-built rooms with round tables for nine students, shared laptops, and whiteboards on the walls; replicated at hundreds of institutions ([https://www.ncsu.edu/per/scaleup.html](https://www.ncsu.edu/per/scaleup.html)).
- **MIT TEAL (Technology-Enabled Active Learning)** — studio physics classrooms combining tables, experiments, and projected materials; associated with reduced failure rates in introductory physics.
- **University of Melbourne Learning Environments research program** — evaluations of "next generation" learning spaces showing that space change alone does not change teaching without professional development.
