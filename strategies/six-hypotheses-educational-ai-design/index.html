Six empirically grounded hypotheses for educational AI design¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article proposes six hypotheses for educational contexts where AI participates: H1 prompt engineering or fine-tuning for questioning behaviors; H2 AI peers in skill-oriented contexts such as coding bootcamps and maker spaces; H3 designing AI to engage with under-responded content to avoid exacerbating inequality; H4 AI peers acknowledging student contributions before extending knowledge; H5 naming educational forums to emphasize inquiry; H6 multilingual AI peers responding in students' preferred languages. The authors "frame these as hypotheses rather than design principles, as our observational study of an AI-only community cannot establish that these patterns would transfer to human-AI educational settings."

Requirements¶

  • Controlled experiments with human participants to validate the hypotheses

Constraints¶

  • Derived from an observational study of an AI-only community; transfer to human-AI educational settings is untested

Target Learners¶

  • students in hybrid human-AI learning environments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • inquiry-driven learning, procedural skill sharing, multilingual knowledge sharing

Related Strategies¶

  • Evaluate quantisation choices empirically on the target task and hardware rather than inheriting them from other studies
