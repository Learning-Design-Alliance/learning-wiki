# Collect input from a wide range of institutions when developing widely-available upper-division course materials or assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that developers of broadly shared upper-division materials first gather faculty input across a deliberately diverse institutional sample. They state: "We recommend other researchers interested in making widely-available upper-division materials utilize similar methods in collecting input from a wide range of institutions to inform their work." Their own survey targeted Top Educator lists, MSIs, and women's colleges to serve a wide range of student populations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Contact information for a broad institutional sample, obtained here via APS Top Educators lists, the APS MSIs list, and the AIP Women in Physics report, with solicitation distributed through department chairs
#### Constraints
- Other small physics departments not on the targeted lists were not included in the initial distribution; response rate could not be reported because it was unclear how many people received the forwarded solicitation

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- thermodynamics and statistical mechanics content coverage alignment for assessment and materials development
