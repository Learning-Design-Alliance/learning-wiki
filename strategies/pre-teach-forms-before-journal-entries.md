# Pre-teach target language forms deductively before journal entries incorporate them

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In the intensive, syllabus-driven approach to focus on form, the teacher pre-teaches selected vocabulary, grammatical forms, or language functions in class, then assigns a journal entry requiring students to use those forms. For example, after lessons on comparative adjectives, students write entries comparing two people, places, or things. The author links this to DeKeyser's claim that holding declarative knowledge in mind during communicative tasks builds procedural knowledge that becomes automatized with practice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher selects and pre-teaches the target forms in class lessons before the journal assignment
#### Constraints
- The intensive approach takes time and is limited in the scope of errors that can be addressed

### Target Learners
- adult ESL students

### Target Learning Goals
- using targeted vocabulary, grammatical forms, and language functions in authentic written communication
