# Instructors nudge students into more productive frames to resolve framing-based impasses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (3 qualitative), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors use the framework diagnostically during group problem solving: noticing when students are stuck because of unproductive framing, then tipping or nudging them into a different frame. The authors write: "Using this framework may help instructors notice when students are stuck because of unpro- ductive framing, and give them tools to nudge students into a more productive frame." Instructors can also nudge students toward additional resources to resolve content errors. This strategy treats framing, not content knowledge, as the lever for intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor ability to observe group discourse and identify students’ current frame during problem-solving sessions
#### Constraints
- Some students, unlike Eric, may not self-reflect and get un-stuck on their own, so nudges may be needed; the framework was developed in one quantum mechanics course

### Target Learners
- Upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Getting unstuck during problem solving
- Coordinating math and physics reasoning
