# Use computer-based scaffolding within problem-centered instruction to support concept-level as well as application-level outcomes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends integrating computer-based scaffolding into problem-centered learning because scaffolding counteracts the purported weaknesses of problem-centered instructional models in helping learners achieve strong concept-level learning outcomes. Teachers often face a quandary between engaging students in authentic problem solving and ensuring performance on standardized tests emphasizing declarative knowledge; scaffolding within problem-centered instruction produced strong pre–post gains of at least 0.74 across concept-, principles-, and application-level assessment, with the strongest outcomes at the concept level.
