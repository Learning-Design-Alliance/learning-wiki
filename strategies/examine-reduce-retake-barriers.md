# Examine and reduce barriers to participation before implementing optional exam retakes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that instructors implementing optional exam retakes actively examine and reduce barriers to participation, since unequal participation drove unequal benefits. The abstract states that "barriers to participation must be examined and reduced for retakes to reduce disparities in scores". Barriers identified include retakes scheduled outside of class time, time constraints from jobs and caregiving, and test anxiety in high-stakes settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors must collect data on who participates and why, such as post-exam surveys about reasons for retaking or not retaking
#### Constraints
- Even when retakes are offered during class time, preparation outside of class remains a barrier for students with limited time
- Test anxiety remains a barrier: about a quarter of non-retakers indicated they did not retake because of test anxiety

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in large introductory courses, particularly students of color, socioeconomically disadvantaged students, and students who work

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable participation in optional assessment opportunities
- reduced disparities in exam scores

## Claims

- [Students who scored higher on first attempts were less likely to retake exams, and participation differed by social identity and by whether retakes were offered outside of class time](../claims/retake-participation-demographic-differences.md) [+W]
