# Before or After-School Physical Activity Programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Before- or after-school physical activity programs provide structured opportunities for movement outside instructional hours — morning exercise or "wake-up" sessions, intramural sports, walking clubs, or open recreational use of school facilities. They are typically delivered by teachers, coaches, or trained staff, and range from free-play supervision to sequenced skill instruction. The strategy positions physical activity as a complement to, rather than a replacement for, classroom learning time.

## Design Implications

Physical activity is associated with modest improvements in attention, executive function, and on-task behavior in children, with the strongest evidence for acute aerobic exercise and its short-term cognitive effects [Hillman et al. review exercise effects on brain and cognition.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2298) [+M]. Systematic reviews find that school-based physical activity does not harm academic performance and may benefit it, particularly in mathematics and reading when programs are sustained [Rasberry et al. systematic review of physical activity and academic performance.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amepre.2011.01.025) [+M]; effects on achievement itself are small and inconsistent [Donnelly et al. systematic review of physical activity and academic achievement.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ypmed.2016.01.022) [~M]. After-school programs produce broader social-emotional and behavioral gains when they are structured and sequenced rather than purely custodial [Durlak et al. meta-analysis of after-school programs.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2010.01564.x) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A consistent schedule and safe, accessible space (gym, field, playground) with equipment appropriate to the age group
- Trained staff who can deliver structured activity, not just supervise ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md))
- A mix of structured skill work and student choice; offering activity options supports autonomy and sustained participation ([Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md))
- Attendance and participation tracking to evaluate reach, since the students who most need activity are often least likely to attend voluntarily

#### Constraints
- Attendance is self-selected: students who are already active or academically engaged disproportionately enroll, limiting equity impact [-M]
- Effects on academic achievement are small and inconsistent; programs should not be justified primarily on achievement gains [Donnelly et al. systematic review of physical activity and academic achievement.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ypmed.2016.01.022) [~M]
- Unstructured "supervision-only" programs show weaker personal and social skill outcomes than SAFE programs (Sequential, Active, Focused, Explicit) [Durlak et al. meta-analysis of after-school programs.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2010.01564.x) [-S]
- Before-school sessions compete with transportation logistics and sleep; early start times can reduce total sleep in adolescents, offsetting cognitive benefits [-W]
- Benefits of acute exercise on attention are short-lived; a morning session does not carry into afternoon classes [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Morning movement breaks** (e.g., 10–20 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity before first period) targeting acute attention benefits
- **Intramural sports leagues** emphasizing participation over selection, avoiding cuts that exclude low-skill students
- **Open facility access** (gym or field open for recreational use) with minimal staffing — cheaper but less structured
- **Integrated programs** pairing activity with academic support or enrichment, which show the strongest social-emotional outcomes [Durlak et al. meta-analysis of after-school programs.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2010.01564.x) [+S]

### Target Learners
- Elementary and middle school students, who show the most consistent attention and behavior benefits from regular physical activity [Hillman et al. review exercise effects on brain and cognition.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2298) [+M]
- Students with high energy or attention regulation difficulties, for whom structured activity before class can improve on-task behavior [~M]
- Disadvantaged students who lack access to community sports — but only if barriers (fees, transport, skill-based selection) are actively removed [-M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Self-regulation: building routines for managing energy, attention, and behavior ([Self-Monitoring](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M])
- Social skills: cooperation, teamwork, and conflict resolution through structured group activity [Durlak et al. meta-analysis of after-school programs.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2010.01564.x) [+S]
- Physical competence and lifelong activity habits, with downstream effects on wellbeing and persistence ([Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+M])

### Instructions
1. Audit needs and barriers: survey students and families on interest, transport, and cost barriers; identify which populations the program currently fails to reach.
2. Set a consistent schedule (e.g., 3–5 mornings or afternoons per week) and secure space, equipment, and trained staff ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md)).
3. Design sessions with a predictable structure — warm-up, skill or game focus, cool-down — so students know what to expect ([Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md)).
4. Build in student choice among activities each session to support autonomy and sustained attendance ([Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md)).
5. Use brief [Check-Ins](../principles/check-ins.md) to monitor wellbeing, enjoyment, and barriers to participation.
6. Sequence skill development across weeks rather than repeating free play; explicit, progressive instruction drives skill gains ([Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md)).
7. Evaluate participation, fitness, behavior, and (secondarily) academic indicators each term; adjust scheduling and outreach to close equity gaps.

## Examples
- **[BOKS](https://www.bokskids.org)** — a before-school physical activity program offering structured 12-week curricula of running, games, and skill work, active in thousands of schools across North America.
- **[CATCH Kids Club](https://catchinfo.org)** — an after-school program combining physical activity with nutrition education, evaluated in multiple trials.
- **Intramural morning leagues** — many districts open gyms 30–45 minutes before first bell for drop-in basketball, running clubs, or dance, staffed by PE teachers on a rotation.
