Design service scholarships that cover a substantial portion of preparation costs, with stable multiyear funding and linked data systems¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The report's policy recommendations for service scholarship programs: benchmark funding so the grant "covers a substantial portion of candidates' preparation costs"; ensure stable, multiyear funding and consistent administration so preparation programs can promote and implement the program; support implementation in the field including timely disbursement and engagement of local education agencies in hiring; apply lessons learned to new supports such as student teaching stipends; and strengthen data collection through inter-agency data sharing.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Sustained funding levels sufficient to meet candidate demand at an award level that substantially covers preparation costs, plus collaboration with interest holders and clear communication to implementers

Constraints¶

  • The report notes $50 million for 2025–26 may be insufficient for nearly 7,500 annual recipients at the $10,000 maximum award, and uptake differs substantially across preparation programs, from 0% to full uptake

Target Learners¶

  • State policymakers
  • Educator preparation programs
  • Aspiring educators with financial need

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Strengthening the educator pipeline by recruiting, preparing, and retaining fully prepared teachers in high-need schools and subjects

Related Strategies¶

  • Build high-retention, supportive pathways into teaching for candidates of color
  • Offer targeted service scholarships, loan forgiveness, and 1-year postbaccalaureate residency programs to rapidly expand the supply of well-prepared teachers in shortage fields and locations
  • Deploy service scholarships, loan forgiveness, and teacher residencies to staff high-need fields and schools
  • Establish high-retention preparation pathways—residencies, Grow Your Own models, service scholarships, and mentoring/induction—to stem turnover in high-need subjects and schools
  • Sustain and expand residency pathways through maintained state grant funding, financial aid, apprenticeship structures, and technical assistance
  • Build a diverse teacher pipeline through Grow Your Own grants, teacher academies, scholarships, MSI investment, and residency pathways
  • State policy levers for sustaining yearlong, stipended clinical preparation: require or incentivize full-year stipended clinical experience, fund partnerships, and reduce testing fees
  • Use state completer survey data within accreditation to flag struggling programs and expand subsidized high-quality clinical pathways for underserved candidates
  • Deploy a portfolio of research-based strategies — service scholarships, residencies, Grow Your Own programs, mentoring and induction, and compensation reform — to strengthen the teacher pipeline
