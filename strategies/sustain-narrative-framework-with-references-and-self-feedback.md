# Sustain framework use with rotation-start references, embedded evaluation prompts, and ongoing self-feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend several low-cost mechanisms to maintain and extend workshop gains for busy faculty: distributing the framework as a reference at the start of each rotation and in the evaluation form itself, having faculty apply the utility rubric to their own evaluations as ongoing formative self-feedback, adding small-group discussion of mock learner videos or a mock CCC approach, and building bias-flagging components into learning management software. These follow directly from the authors' stated next steps for continued improvement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs must incorporate the framework and rubric into routine evaluation workflows or learning management software
#### Constraints
- Any software implementation that flags biased language "would require careful assessment and validation"; whether subsequent interactive sessions are needed versus a reference alone is an open question the article states.

### Target Learners
- Faculty supervisors of internal medicine residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintained quality and utility of narrative evaluations over time
