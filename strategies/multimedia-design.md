# Multimedia Design

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Multimedia design is the deliberate arrangement of words (spoken or printed) and pictures (static or dynamic) in instructional materials. It is carried out by applying empirically derived principles — segmenting, signaling, coherence, modality, redundancy, and personalization — to videos, slides, animations, e-learning modules, and illustrated texts, so that the presentation aligns with how working memory processes visual and auditory channels.

## Design Implications

Multimedia design is grounded in Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning: learners process visual and auditory information through separate channels of limited working-memory capacity, so design choices determine whether capacity is spent on essential processing or wasted on extraneous load [Cognitive overload degrades learning outcomes.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S]. The strongest-supported moves are removing decorative but irrelevant material [Irrelevant seductive details hurt learning.](../claims/coherence-principle-irrelevant-material-hurts-learning.md) [+S], breaking continuous media into learner-paced segments, and highlighting essential content with cues. Combining words with relevant images reliably outperforms words alone [Pairing words with relevant images improves learning over words alone.](../claims/multimedia-words-plus-images-beat-words-alone.md) [+S], consistent with [Dual Coding Theory](../theories/dual-coding-theory.md).

### Context
#### Requirements
- Analysis of which content is essential versus incidental before production
- Graphics that are structurally relevant (explain how something works), not decorative
- Narration and on-screen text managed deliberately — narration for graphics, text for standalone words
- Segmentation controls (pause, next) or pre-segmented chunks for complex material

#### Constraints
- Redundant on-screen text read aloud verbatim from narration hurts learning [Redundant on-screen text with narration hurts learning.](../claims/redundancy-principle-on-screen-text-hurts-learning.md) [~S] — the effect reverses when learners are non-native speakers or the text is short
- Over-signaling (cuing everything) eliminates the benefit of signaling [~M]
- Learner-paced segmentation helps novices but can be unnecessary overhead for experts [~M]
- Decorative graphics and background music consume capacity without aiding learning [Irrelevant seductive details hurt learning.](../claims/coherence-principle-irrelevant-material-hurts-learning.md) [-S]

#### Implementation Variability
- Video: segment into short clips with embedded questions or [Practice](../elements/practice.md) prompts at segment boundaries
- Slides: one idea per slide; graphics + spoken explanation rather than text-dense slides
- Animation: prefer static sequences with [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) when the content is not inherently dynamic [~M]
- Interactive e-learning: combine segmentation with learner control over pacing and replay

### Target Learners
- Novices and low-prior-knowledge learners, who lack schemas to compensate for extraneous load [Cognitive overload degrades learning outcomes.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S]
- Non-native speakers, for whom redundant printed text can support rather than hinder [~M]
- Less beneficial for experts, who suffer the expertise-reversal effect when guidance is excessive

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding of systems, processes, and causal models (how something works)
- Retention of structured verbal and visual information
- Transfer to novel problems when graphics depict underlying structure rather than surface features

### Instructions
1. Identify the essential content and select or create graphics that explain it structurally, not decoratively.
2. Apply [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) — segment continuous media into meaningful, learner-paced parts.
3. Add signaling (arrows, color, highlighting) to direct attention to essential elements.
4. Narrate graphics with spoken words rather than duplicating them as on-screen text; manage [Attention](../elements/attention.md) by keeping each screen focused on one idea.
5. Pilot with representative learners and check for overload using [Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md) or embedded questions.

## Examples
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — narrated, segmented problem-solving videos with minimal on-screen text, consistent with modality and coherence principles.
- **[PhET Interactive Simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu)** — University of Colorado simulations pairing dynamic graphics with brief, learner-controlled text prompts.
- **[Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com)** — pairs images with audio and short text, segmenting lessons into small units with immediate practice.
