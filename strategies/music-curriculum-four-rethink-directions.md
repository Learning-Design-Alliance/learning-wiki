# Rethink the music curriculum around DAW literacy, AI literacy, rights and economics knowledge, and defended ensemble practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes that music curricula need reconsidering, not merely amendment at the margins, and enumerates four directions: production and DAW literacy as a standard component at all levels; AI literacy for musicians as a practical and critical capacity to distinguish using AI as a shortcut versus a creative catalyst; critical and contextual knowledge of rights, economics, and the social role of music; and foregrounding ensemble and performance precisely because they are most resistant to AI substitution.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Curricula must move beyond adding an AI module or reactively banning AI tools, requiring more fundamental rethinking of what music education is for
#### Constraints
- The article warns ensemble and performance dimensions risk progressive marginalization under economic pressure and the convenience of digital tools

### Target Learners
- music students at all levels of education

### Target Learning Goals
- digital music production competence
- critical AI literacy
- understanding of music rights and economics
- embodied ensemble performance
