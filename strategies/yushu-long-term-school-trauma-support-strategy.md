# Launch long-term school-based trauma support: teacher training in emotional-state recognition, arts-based mindfulness group activities, and rotating class cadre roles

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q1`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends sustained intervention programs for school-aged disaster survivors, built around teachers as the adults who spend the most time with adolescents. It proposes teacher training "regarding the ability to recognize adolescents' emotional state and relevant coping methods", arts-based mindfulness group activities to enhance collective cohesion, and rotating class cadre roles to reinforce responsibility, aiming to help adolescents restructure their mental health.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integrated efforts from the family, school, community, and society, as in a collaborative trauma systems therapy (TST) approach
#### Constraints
- The article states interventions for post-traumatic stress are most effective when carried out 2–6 months after traumatic events, while this study addresses the long-term epidemiological perspective

### Target Learners
- Adolescent survivors of natural disasters
- Tibetan middle school students in disaster-affected regions

### Target Learning Goals
- School adaptation and adjustment
- Mental health restructuring after traumatic events
