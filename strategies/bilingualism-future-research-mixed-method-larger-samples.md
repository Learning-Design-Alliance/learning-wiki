# Future bilingualism research should combine mixed-method designs, multiple data collection tools and analysis methods, and larger samples

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The review recommends that future studies of bilingualism combine qualitative and quantitative approaches, because bilingualism is a multifaceted phenomenon with many interrelated dependent and independent variables. It states researchers "should use mixed -method, qualitative and quantitative data collection tools, and multiple data analysis methods together in order to achieve more effective results", and should conduct research with larger sample groups to generalize findings and reveal undetermined dependent variables.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Studies must address bilingualism, bilingual teaching, bilingual education or related topics with clear aims, methods, tools, analysis and participant numbers, as in the review's checklist.
#### Constraints
- The review notes mixed-method was used in only N: 4 of the 58 sample studies and no study fell in the 251-300 participant range.

### Target Learners
- bilingual and language-minority learners

### Target Learning Goals
- language skills
- teaching/learning processes
- working memory
- cognitive skills
