# Use e-learning as continuing medical education for providers who have difficulty attending face-to-face training sessions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends its e-learning approach as continuing medical education: "Given the effective results obtained from administering educational intervention regarding the clinical management of substance-dependent pregnant women during labor and post-partum using e-learning, it is recommended that this intervention be used as a valuable method for improving the knowledge and clinical skill performance of groups that have some difficulty attending at face-to-face training sessions." The design let midwives access content at any hour, easing attendance barriers such as coordinating work shifts and fatigue after work.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An accredited LMS with content meeting content production standards
- A technical-assistance contact for participants using the system
#### Constraints
- Short-term (one-month) retention assessment only; results may not generalize to midwives working in health centers

### Target Learners
- Midwives and health providers working with substance-dependent pregnant women

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge and clinical skill performance in perinatal and postpartum management of substance use
