# Use the customizable robot platform for varied scientific exercises, e.g. a forensic physics investigation of a robotic car crash

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose extending the platform beyond the light-following exercise: because the robot is simple and customizable, it can serve exercises in many fields of science, not necessarily related to robotics. As a concrete forward-looking suggestion they offer 'A more physics based experience could be to set up a robotic car crash and engage students in a forensic physics experience', where students could determine initial speeds, directions, or maneuvers of the artificial car drivers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Customizable robotic hardware; the authors offer support for programming and assembly and invite teacher feedback
#### Constraints
- The crash scenario is proposed, not tested in this article

### Target Learners
- students in general science introduction courses
- hardware-oriented lecture students
- physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- scientific reasoning
- applying physics reasoning to reconstructed events
