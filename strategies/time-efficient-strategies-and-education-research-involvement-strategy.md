# Support very active researchers with time-efficient active learning strategies and involve instructors in education research or funded instructional development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article reports that only very high levels of research activity correlate with more lecturing, and suggests those instructors "may need to be supported in implementing active learning strategies that do not increase, or even decrease, time required." It also notes that "instructors who engage in education research or funded education work are more likely to use active learning," so "external and institutional grants for instructional development appear to be potentially valuable strategies for improving instruction."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires grant programs or institutional funding for instructional development
- Requires selecting or designing active learning strategies that are time-efficient for research-active instructors
#### Constraints
- The article states the majority of research-engaged instructors are not engaged to an extent that impacts their use of active learning, so the support targets the very-active minority

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students in introductory STEM courses taught by research-active instructors

### Target Learning Goals
- Increased use of active learning instructional strategies among research-active instructors
