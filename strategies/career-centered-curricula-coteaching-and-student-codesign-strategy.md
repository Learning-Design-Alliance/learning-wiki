# Recommend career-centered IT curricula, cross-university co-teaching, and co-creating syllabi with students to improve IT learning outcomes and employability

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the census findings, the authors make three recommendations for student benefit: creating career-centered IT curricula, co-teaching involving different universities, and co-creating syllabi with students. They observe that the design of most SP-degrees and MA-degree majors "primarily centers around the technology rather than its related careers or applications", noting no offering university offers an IT-school FinTech SP-degree or major despite FinTech's growth, and recommend IT schools offer degrees centering around an IT career such as FinTech.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- For career-centered curricula, IT schools would offer SP-degrees and MA-degree majors centering around an IT career rather than a single supporting technology
#### Constraints
- The authors were not aware of any formal co-teaching arrangement involving instructors from two different universities, nor of any offering university involving students in co-designing an IT syllabus
- Co-designing syllabi with students requires extra effort for collaboration and a different mindset compared with traditional methods

### Target Learners
- IT bachelor's and master's students in Australian universities

### Target Learning Goals
- IT learning outcomes and graduate employability
