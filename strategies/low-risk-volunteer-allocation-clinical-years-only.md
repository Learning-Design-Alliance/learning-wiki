# Allocate student volunteers to low-risk prevention activities and limit clinical-setting volunteering to well-prepared clinical-year students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes a tiered deployment strategy for medical student volunteers during infectious outbreaks. It suggests it "may be plausible to focus the allocation of these volunteering students to activities relating to early levels of prevention with minimum risk of exposure, including health education and promotion, contact tracing and triages, as well as peer supports for isolating patients and HCWs", while clinical-setting opportunities "should be strictly limited to clinical-year students, provided that they are well-prepared and protected".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Proper trainings and adequate personal protective equipment before deployment
- Voluntary participation satisfying students' personal requirements
#### Constraints
- Implementation of such preparations does not necessarily imply students become a reserve for healthcare workers during an infectious outbreak

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical student volunteers during public health emergencies

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe contribution to pandemic response
- Collaborative patient care experience
