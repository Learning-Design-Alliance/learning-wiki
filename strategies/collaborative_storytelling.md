# Collaborative Storytelling

## Description
Learners work in pairs to create a story by responding to guiding questions. First, individuals think silently about their response to a question. Then, they share their answers with their partner and decide together which one to write down. The process repeats through a series of questions, and the pairs then write a simple version of their story with illustrations, practice telling it, and present it to another small group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Guiding questions, large easel paper, writing utensils, time for thinking, sharing, and writing. Can be adapted for digital platforms.
#### Constraints
- May require modeling and scaffolding, especially for introductory activities. Some students may dominate the collaborative process if not monitored effectively.

### Target Learners

- Elementary through high school students, particularly beneficial for English language learners.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Develop narrative writing skills, encourage active participation, enhance collaboration, foster creativity and imagination

### Affordances
- Increases engagement and active participation by decreasing group size, provides structured support for narrative writing, and allows for individual thinking time before collaboration. Reduces anxiety and increases self-confidence and motivation, especially for English language learners.

### Personalization
- Teachers can adjust the guiding questions to suit the grade level and learning objectives. The number of questions can be modified based on the time available and the learners' needs. Modeling responses to each question can be provided for introductory activities.
