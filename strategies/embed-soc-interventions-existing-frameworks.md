# Embed resilience and coherence-strengthening interventions into existing curricular structures rather than standalone courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that interventions to strengthen students' sense of coherence be integrated into existing educational structures rather than added as separate mandatory offerings. It states that "future interventions should be seamlessly integrated into existing frameworks rather than introduced as mandatory standalone courses", giving the examples of embedding resilience training and motivational workshops into orientation programs and incorporating peer-mentoring and stress-management discussions into routine clinical debriefing sessions. The aim is to promote coherence, coping capacity, and academic success across diverse student populations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Existing curricular structures such as student orientation programs and routine clinical debriefing sessions into which interventions can be embedded
#### Constraints
- The cross-sectional correlational design did not test any intervention; the recommendation is not supported by an experimental manipulation in this study

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate medical students
- Undergraduate nursing students

### Target Learning Goals
- Psychological resilience and coping with academic stress
- Academic achievement (GPA)
