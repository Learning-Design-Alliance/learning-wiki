# Replace live animal and cadaver robotic surgery training with validated synthetic organ models, enabled by scalable mass production

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends transitioning robotic surgical education to validated digital and synthetic organ models in place of live animals and cadavers, because animals and cadavers are expensive, scarce, ethically contested, and not validated for robotic training. To achieve this it states that "Scalable engineering methods for mass production of the high-fidelity models are needed to increase the sample size of future validation studies and then to incorporate models into global surgical training curricula". New models should provide a full procedural platform with adjacent organ and bony anatomy, and novel bioengineering methods should reduce production time, cost and labour.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scalable engineering methods for mass production of high-fidelity models
- Novel bioengineering methods reducing the time, cost and labour of production
- Full procedural platforms with relevant adjacent organ and bony anatomic structures
#### Constraints
- Synthetic model development is not as mature outside urology; gynaecology, general and colorectal surgery need more validated models
- Further validation studies exploring concurrent and predictive validity of synthetic models are required

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees in robotic surgery education programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- robotic surgery competency prior to live surgery
