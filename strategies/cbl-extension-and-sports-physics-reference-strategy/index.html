Extend the CBL model to other subjects and study programs, and develop sports-specific systematic physics reference material for the examination phase¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors' recommendations section proposes three follow-up actions: conduct further research "with more extensive participant" to examine the consistency of the CBL model beyond the two East Java universities; implement the CBL model "to other subjects, even students of other study programs"; and develop physics reference material that is systematic in physics topics yet specific to sport science students, needed when students are in the examining phase (Phase 3) of the model.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • The proposed reference must present physics topics systematically based on the science of physics while being specific to sport science students.

Constraints¶

  • The study was carried out only in two state universities in East Java, which the authors name as the reason further research is needed.

Target Learners¶

  • sport science undergraduate students
  • students of other study programs

Target Learning Goals¶

  • analytical thinking skills
  • physics learning in sport context

Claims¶

  • Paired t-tests show a statistically significant pretest-to-posttest increase in analytical thinking skills in all four classes at α = 0.05 [+W]
  • The significant improvement replicated across two universities, State University of Malang and State University of Surabaya [+W]
  • A Context Based Learning physics model improves sport science students' analytical thinking skills with a high-category N-gain of 0.78 [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Extend interactive workbook development to other grades and domains and train teachers in its use
  • Re-arrange and extend the web-assisted flipped-classroom activity materials to other science subjects and disciplines
