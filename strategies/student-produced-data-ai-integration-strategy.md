# Integrate AI into core curricula by using data that students already produce in subject activities to teach data-related AI competencies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy treats educational activities as data sources for AI systems, so that core-subject activities double as AI learning experiences. The article states that "Educational activities often produce data, and AI systems often require data", and participants used this to generate integrated curricula: paper airplane physics data trained a flight-prediction ML model, ESL pronunciation recordings trained a classification model in MIT App Inventor, and vocabulary images trained a Teachable Machine classifier. Future curricula can use such data to teach Data Literacy, Learning from Data, and Critically Interpreting Data, or to train ML models.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- AI learning tools that accept student-generated data (e.g., Machine Learning for Kids, Google Teachable Machine, MIT App Inventor) must be available in the classroom
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a small group of teachers integrating AI with non-technical subjects; the article says applying and extending general implications beyond this context should be done with caution

### Target Learners
- K-12 students in non-computing core subjects, including students with learning disabilities and ESL students

### Target Learning Goals
- AI literacy competencies such as Data Literacy, Learning from Data, and Critically Interpreting Data
- core subject content in physics, ESL, and literacy
