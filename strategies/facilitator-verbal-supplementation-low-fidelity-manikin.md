# Facilitator verbally supplies clinical findings that a low-fidelity manikin cannot display

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
When using a low-cost manikin with limited functionalities, the facilitator compensates for missing physical cues by narrating clinical data during the scenario. The article states "In the LC group, the facilitator communicated clinical data that could not be transmitted by the manikin (cyanosis, apnea, thoraco-abdominal asynchrony, auscultation characteristics)." The monitor display of physiological parameters and the capnogram further compensate for absent visual cues. This keeps scenario progression strictly identical across simulator types.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator with sufficient knowledge of the scenario to provide additional information about the patient's condition
#### Constraints
- Once apnea occurs, the only simulator difference is the appearance or not of cyanosis, which the monitor's SpO2 display compensates for

### Target Learners
- physicians in simulation-based continuing medical education

### Target Learning Goals
- recognition and management of pediatric airway emergencies
