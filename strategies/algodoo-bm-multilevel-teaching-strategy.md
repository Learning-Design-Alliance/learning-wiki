# Use Algodoo Brownian-motion animations across school levels: illustrate random motion at high school, teach data-treatment procedures and statistical-physics concepts at undergraduate level

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends deploying the Brownian-motion animations at several educational levels. At high school level "the animations of the BM can be used to illustrate the random motion of a particle suspended on a ﬂuid"; at undergraduate level teachers can elaborate scripts from the Algodoo data so students obtain physical quantities from time series as in laboratory experiments, and it serves as an introduction to statistical physics, normal distribution and the Gaussian curve. At higher levels students learn scientific procedures of model formulation, data collection and treatment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to Algodoo and spreadsheet software; teacher-prepared scripts guiding students through the time-series data treatment
#### Constraints
- The simulated parameters are arbitrary and do not represent any specific material system; the article notes the animations do not reach the random-walk state before about 0.8 s

### Target Learners
- high school students
- undergraduate physics students
- beginning science researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding Brownian motion
- data collection and treatment procedures
- statistical physics and normal distribution concepts
