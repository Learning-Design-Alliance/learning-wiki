# Use LLMs as supplements for theoretical learning while preserving hands-on training for practical skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that OT educators integrate AI tools to reinforce foundational biomedical knowledge while keeping experiential, educator-guided instruction for visual-spatial interpretation and embodied clinical judgment. The authors state "Educators should therefore use LLMs as supplements for theoretical learning while preserving hands-on training for practical skills," grounded in the finding that models pass on text but decline on visual items.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educator-guided approaches for reflective and experiential competence development must remain central, since LLMs remain limited in visual interpretation and contextual reasoning
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on a single year of the JNEOT and a Japanese-language exam; caution is required when generalizing

### Target Learners
- occupational therapy students

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational biomedical knowledge
- clinical reasoning
