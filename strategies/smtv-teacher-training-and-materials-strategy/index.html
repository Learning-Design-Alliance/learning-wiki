Train teachers and develop materials for the structural method of vocabulary teaching¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (2 quant-synthesis, 1 causal), q2–q3 · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that Teacher Training Institutions conduct workshops focused on "the morphological analyses of the words for teaching vocabulary", that secondary English teachers be trained to design graded learning activities providing oral practice under SMTV, and that textbook boards write English textbooks on the basis of SMTV with balanced stress on the four basic skills. Libraries are to be strengthened with books on the method, and further research is recommended controlling attitude, background, intelligence and gender.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher training workshops and willing, trained teachers; institutional support from textbook boards and libraries

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation rests on a single small-scale experiment in one boys' model school in Islamabad

Target Learners¶

  • secondary-level English learners, high and low achievers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English vocabulary learning and oral language practice

Affordances¶

  • Structural Method Teaching Vocabulary

Claims¶

  • High, average and low achievers taught with SMTV each significantly outperformed their DMTV counterparts on the posttest [+W]
  • High, average and low achievers taught with SMTV each significantly outperformed their DMTV counterparts on the retention test [+W]
  • Ninth-grade students taught vocabulary with the structural method significantly outperformed students taught with the definitional method on the posttest [+W]
  • The SMTV group significantly outperformed the DMTV group on the delayed retention test [+W]
  • Morphological instruction improves word-level literacy outcomes (decoding, spelling, vocabulary, morphological knowledge) but shows no reliable effect on reading comprehension [+M]
