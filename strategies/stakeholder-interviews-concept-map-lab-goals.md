# Operationalize lab learning goals through stakeholder interviews, prioritization, and concept mapping

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article outlines a concrete procedure for deriving a course's learning goals. It begins with "one key question: What do you wish students entering the advanced lab or your research group were already able to do?" An unstructured discussion was used deliberately to capture a diverse set of possible goals not influenced by the interviewer. The authors compiled the resulting list, categorized it into themes, prioritized by frequency, built a concept map to order dependencies, and vetted the final set with a faculty committee.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Solicit input from departmental faculty and stakeholders through one-on-one meetings and focus groups
- Discuss the categorized and ordered list with a faculty committee before finalizing
#### Constraints
- The procedure is presented as one example of how to use the AAPT recommendations, not a validated method; the interview key question deliberately focused on skills related to physics research

### Target Learners
- introductory physics lab students at research universities

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying and prioritizing lab learning goals aligned with departmental needs
