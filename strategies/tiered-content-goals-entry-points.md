# Tiered content goals provide multiple entry points for students with varied background knowledge

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
When a wide range of student background knowledge caused some students to be bored, the facilitators used tiered goals: teams demonstrating mastery of the first-tier goal (the readout process) were guided toward second-tier goals such as modeling bad pixels, color and read noise. The article states "The idea was to have various entry points so that all students could participate."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators prepared with tiered content goals in advance
- Formative monitoring to judge when a team has mastered a first-tier goal
#### Constraints
- Stated in the context of a large range of students' background knowledge; some students got bored since they had been exposed to the material before

### Target Learners
- students with a large range of background knowledge in the same classroom

### Target Learning Goals
- CCD readout process
- CCD noise sources and artifacts
