Strategies

Teach new medical vocabulary by writing the word in a circle on the board with its collocations around it, followed by comprehension questions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's experimental treatment procedure: the highlighted new medical word is written in a circle in the center of the board and its collocations are written around it, after which comprehension questions on the passage are asked. The article reports this made "the task more tangible" and produced significantly better retention than synonym, antonym, definition and translation techniques over five sessions.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Reading passages containing highlighted new medical vocabulary, such as those in the SAMT medicine course book
  • Sessions in which new words are presented with their collocations rather than in isolation

Constraints¶

  • Evaluated only with intermediate-level female medical students in an Iranian EFL context over a five-week period

Target Learners¶

  • EFL medical and paramedical university students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Retention of technical medical vocabulary
  • Appropriate use of medical collocations

Claims¶

  • The collocation group's mean score rose from 8.2 to 17.7 while the control group's rose from 8.7 to 11.1 [+W]
  • Teaching medical vocabulary through collocations produced higher posttest scores than traditional techniques in Iranian EFL medical students [+W]
  • Traditional vocabulary techniques alone produced no statistically significant pretest-to-posttest gain in the control group [+W]
