Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (1 review, 1 associational, 1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that effective institutional governance extend beyond prohibition, detection, or general guidance toward establishing practical conditions that support AI literacy, learner agency, privacy protection, critical evaluation, and avoidance of overdependence. Institutions act as an enabling and governing layer through curriculum and assessment structures, policies, infrastructure, professional development, procurement, data-protection arrangements, and accountability mechanisms. The authors argue that without adequate institutional support, responsibility shifts unevenly onto individual instructors and students.

Claims¶

  • Institutions have expanded student access to generative AI tools faster than they have developed policies, guidance, and shared learning goals for their use [+W]
  • AI outcomes in higher education are shaped by leadership capacity, infrastructure readiness, and governance structures rather than functioning independently [+W]
  • Among the 50 most innovative U.S. universities, instructor-set syllabus-level governance is the dominant GenAI convergence, with 33 of 50 providing syllabus or course-policy guidance [~W]
  • Existing generative AI guidance in higher education focuses on policy and academic integrity rather than learning mechanisms [+W]
