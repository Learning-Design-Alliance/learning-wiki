Institutional governance should establish practical conditions supporting AI literacy, learner agency, and critical evaluation rather than relying on prohibition and detection¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The article recommends that effective institutional governance extend beyond prohibition, detection, or general guidance toward establishing practical conditions that support AI literacy, learner agency, privacy protection, critical evaluation, and avoidance of overdependence. Institutions act as an enabling and governing layer through curriculum and assessment structures, policies, infrastructure, professional development, procurement, data-protection arrangements, and accountability mechanisms. The authors argue that without adequate institutional support, responsibility shifts unevenly onto individual instructors and students.

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Institutional arrangements must shape which forms of GenAI-mediated practice are recognized as legitimate and how responsibilities are distributed

Constraints¶

  • Student GenAI use extends beyond institutionally managed environments, limiting regulation through approved platforms alone

Target Learners¶

  • higher education students
  • instructors
  • institutional leaders

Target Learning Goals¶

  • responsible GenAI use
  • critical evaluation of AI outputs

Related Strategies¶

  • Graduate AI literacy training should promote purposeful, task-oriented, critically supervised GenAI use rather than targeting overall use levels
  • Institutions should respond to generative AI in education with guided critical engagement rather than prohibition
  • Address generative AI in higher education through assessment redesign and proportional reflective practices rather than surveillance or punitive controls
  • Design task-sensitive institutional AI policies rather than blanket permitted/prohibited distinctions
  • Establish comprehensive institutional AI governance frameworks including task forces, roadmaps, and transparent policies
  • Four strategic recommendations for supporting AI literacy across settings
  • Build ethical AI integration on four coordinated levels: faculty professional development, ethics curricula, clear institutional guidelines, and interdisciplinary updating
  • Ground AI integration in higher education on core principles of human agency, academic freedom, transparency, ethics, inclusivity, and critical thinking
  • Build institutional AI literacy and transparent risk-communication governance for agentic AI browsers
  • Teach mediation literacy and redesign assessment and evaluation to measure interaction, not only satisfaction
