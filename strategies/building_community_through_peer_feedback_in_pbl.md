# Building Community Through Peer Feedback in PBL

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Building community through peer feedback involves giving students opportunities to frequently share work-in-progress, exchange structured feedback, and revise. In project-based learning (PBL), critique cycles are positioned not as an assessment add-on but as a community practice: students depend on one another to improve their work, witness each other's growth over multiple drafts, and develop the norms of respectful, specific critique that make collaboration possible. The strategy treats feedback as a social act that simultaneously improves the artifact and builds the relationships and sense of belonging that sustain a project community.

## Design Implications

Peer feedback improves achievement when it is structured and criterion-referenced rather than left to spontaneous comment exchange [Peer assessment benefits learning when structured with clear criteria.](../claims/peer-assessment-structured-criteria-improve-learning.md) [+S]. Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and the process rather than the person [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]. The community-building effect depends on reciprocity and repeated interaction: students must both give and receive feedback across multiple cycles, so that helping becomes a visible, valued norm. Structured critique also builds the psychological safety that group work requires; cooperative structures work best when individual accountability and group reward are both present [Cooperative learning with group rewards and individual accountability improves outcomes.](../claims/cooperative-learning-group-rewards-and-individual-accountability.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Public or semi-public sharing of drafts at regular checkpoints ([Submission](../elements/submission.md), [Peer Interaction](../elements/peer-interaction.md))
- Explicit feedback protocols and sentence stems so critique is specific, kind, and helpful ([Feedback](../elements/feedback.md), [Review](../elements/review.md))
- Time and expectation for revision, so feedback visibly matters ([Revision](../elements/revision.md))
- Teacher modeling of critique language early in the cycle, with gradual release of responsibility to students [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- Unstructured "comment on each other's work" sessions produce vague praise and social comparison that can damage rather than build community [-M]
- Feedback quality suffers when students lack the domain knowledge to judge work; novices often rate surface features and miss substantive problems [~M]
- Public critique without established norms can expose low performers and threaten belonging, particularly for anxious or marginalized students [~M]
- If drafts are never revised, students learn that feedback is performative, undermining both trust and effort [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Gallery critique** (whole-class, e.g., High Tech High's protocol): work displayed publicly; students write warm/cool feedback on sticky notes
- **Critical friends** (pairs or trios): author presents, peers question and suggest, author records next steps
- **Speed feedback / tuning protocols** (e.g., NSRF protocols): timed rotation through multiple drafts with structured forms
- **Digital peer review** (e.g., Peergrade, Peerceptiv, Eli Review): anonymized or attributed exchange with rubric-anchored comments at scale

### Target Learners
- All students in a project community, but the belonging and reciprocity benefits are strongest for students at risk of disengagement, for whom visible contribution to peers builds standing [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Developing writers and creators, who benefit from authentic audiences and multiple revision cycles [+M]
- Less effective as a community builder in short, one-shot projects where relationships have no time to form through repeated exchange [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Quality improvement of project artifacts through iterative revision
- Social-emotional goals: giving and receiving critique, perspective-taking, and collaborative norms
- Metacognitive goals: evaluating work against criteria and planning revisions [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. **Establish norms and model critique.** Demonstrate kind, specific, helpful feedback on a sample draft before students critique each other ([Coaching](../elements/coaching.md), [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md)).
2. **Set a checkpoint with a draft deadline.** Students submit work-in-progress ([Submission](../elements/submission.md)) — drafts, not polished work, signal that feedback is expected and safe.
3. **Run structured peer review.** Pair or group students with a protocol and criteria ([Peer Interaction](../elements/peer-interaction.md), [Review](../elements/review.md), [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md)); require both a strength and a specific suggestion.
4. **Require revision and re-share.** Students act on feedback and show what changed ([Revision](../elements/revision.md)), making growth visible to the community.
5. **Repeat across the project.** Multiple cycles turn critique into a classroom routine and let students witness each other's trajectories.
6. **Close the loop publicly.** In final presentations, students credit peers whose feedback shaped the work, reinforcing reciprocity as a community norm.

## Examples
- **[Expeditionary Learning / EL Education](https://eleducation.org)** — EL schools build "cultures of critique" using Ron Berger's *An Ethic of Excellence* model: multiple drafts, public critique sessions, and "kind, specific, helpful" norms, with students revising work (e.g., scientific illustrations, essays) across many feedback rounds.
- **[High Tech High](https://www.hightechhigh.org)** — PBL charter schools in San Diego use gallery critique and critical-friends protocols at project checkpoints, with peer feedback shaping work toward public exhibitions.
- **[Peergrade](https://www.peergrade.io)** (now part of [FeedbackFruits](https://feedbackfruits.com)) — platforms that structure rubric-anchored peer review in online courses, enabling feedback exchange at scale in large enrollments.
