# Adapt practice activities to learners' age, cultural background, and aptitudes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that teachers adapt practice to their learners, since "Not all forms of practice will work equally well under all circumstances". Children may accept mechanical drills more willingly but benefit little from them, while games increase children's persistence; cultural and school-culture norms shape tolerance of repetitiveness, memorization, and negative feedback. The article states "adaptation to a given group of learners as a function of age or cultural background is both feasible and indispensable", and draws on Robinson's hypotheses linking activity types to specific aptitudes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need sensitivity to how practice activities can be adapted to their learners
#### Constraints
- Adaptation to individual aptitude constellations is hardly possible in the classroom and may wait for more advanced computer-assisted instruction

### Target Learners
- children, adolescents, and adults learning a second or foreign language across cultural contexts

### Target Learning Goals
- effective engagement with practice activities matched to learner characteristics
