# Balance quantum-specific and general STEM content in new quantum programs, offer broad quantum courses for non-specialist roles, and engage business education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 associational), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors derive three recommendations from their survey data for universities developing quantum education. Because few job roles require many quantum skills, educators "should consider the balance between quantum-speciﬁc courses and more general STEM courses" in master's programs. For jobs with few or no important quantum skills, universities should develop one or two broad quantum courses, and for business-related jobs they recommend engaging with campus business education leaders.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Universities developing new quantum master's programs or courses for non-quantum-specialist students
#### Constraints
- The authors caution the QIST industry is still young with many unknowns, so programs should also prepare students for traditional career paths

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate students preparing for quantum-industry careers
- business majors seeking quantum-industry roles

### Target Learning Goals
- quantum-related job skills aligned with industry hiring needs
- broad quantum literacy for non-specialist roles
