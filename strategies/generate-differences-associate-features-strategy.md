# Teach students to generate differences between related concepts and associate each feature with its concept name before assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests differential-associative processing could be used instructionally: "it would be interesting to examine whether differential -associative processing can be used as an instructional method where a teacher might ask students to generate differences between related concepts." In the study, students read paired definitions aloud, identified two differences per pair, then responded to stated features with the correct concept label.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Definitions of related concepts presented in pairs
- Time for students to generate differences without hints
#### Constraints
- Tested only in laboratory one-on-one administration with pairs of definitions from Introductory Psychology textbooks

### Target Learners
- university students in introductory psychology courses

### Target Learning Goals
- learning definitions of semantically related concepts for multiple-choice assessment
