# Pair structured feedback on assessed skills with demonstration of the correct execution, since self-assessment accuracy is difficult to train

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
After the formative examination, students should receive structured feedback that both gives a good idea of their performance and shows the correct execution of the skill assessed. The article reports that it was postulated that "video feedback would be sufficient to improve self-assessment", but a cited study achieved improvement only retrospectively when the student's video was juxtaposed with a video demonstrating correct manoeuvres. Criticism must be communicated meaningfully, as responsiveness to feedback is modulated by expectations and attitudes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured and qualitative feedback after the examination
- Showing the correct execution of the skill assessed
#### Constraints
- Responsiveness to feedback is modulated by expectations and attitudes
- The ability to correctly self-evaluate is difficult to teach or train

### Target Learners
- final-year undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- accurate self-assessment of practical clinical skills
- correct performance of physical examination and basic clinical procedures
