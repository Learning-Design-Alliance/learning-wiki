# Incorporate spaced learning into surgical residency training programs for cosmetic suturing skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the trial's finding that spaced training significantly enhanced both short-term acquisition and three-month retention of cosmetic suturing skills, the authors "propose the inclusion of the concept of spaced learning in residency training programs and surgical training courses". The concrete recipe is 40 minutes of one-on-one Peyton's 4-step instruction followed by a 20-minute active break, repeated three times, replacing an equal amount of concentrated continuous training. The authors note the concept could extend to other surgical skills training domains.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained instructors and protected training time divided into spaced cycles with rest intervals
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness demonstrated in simulated operating rooms; transferability to real-life contexts is not assessed, and the optimal spacing duration remains unknown

### Target Learners
- Surgical residents
- Residency training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquisition and long-term retention of cosmetic suturing and other surgical procedural skills
