# Enhance student self-esteem — via recreational reading, group activities, and reduced screen time — as a lever for academic performance in rural developing contexts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 3 studies (2 associational, 1 review), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that improving students' self-esteem is a route to better academic outcomes in rural China and other developing contexts, since self-esteem was robustly correlated with math achievement. It identifies behavioral correlates that could be targeted: "more recreational reading, and more involvement in group activities are all positively related to students' self-esteem", while more screen time and maternal migration were risk factors. The authors frame this as a direction for intervention design rather than a tested manipulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should target the social-environmental and behavioral factors the article found associated with self-esteem, such as recreational reading and group activities
#### Constraints
- The study is cross-sectional and non-experimental; the article states its results do not establish a causal relationship and that more empirical research is needed on which intervention designs are most effective
- The article states results may not apply outside less developed areas of rural China

### Target Learners
- Early adolescents (grades 4–8) in rural, low-income areas of developing countries

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic performance in mathematics
- Psychosocial development (self-esteem)
