# Conduct systematic ablation studies comparing levels of interface scaffolding and tailoring to identify when scaffolding approaches are effective

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The commentary recommends that "more systematic ablation studies should be conducted to understand their comparative effectiveness, not only in terms of learning outcomes, but also considering student acceptance and satisfaction", and that researchers should "strive to identify precise guidelines for when different levels of scaffolding can be suitable". It notes the original study only compared the fully adaptive SE-Coach against simple untailored prompts at every step.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Comparisons should span both the level of interface scaffolding (simple prompts vs. interface tools) and how to scaffold (every step vs. student-adaptive)
#### Constraints
- The original Conati and VanLehn (2000) study compared only one combination, so conclusions about other combinations are not yet supported

### Target Learners
- learners using intelligent learning environments for metacognitive support

### Target Learning Goals
- metacognitive skills such as self-explanation
