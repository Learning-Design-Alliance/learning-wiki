# Simulation Learning

## Description
Simulation learning engages learners in active learning experiences where they learn from each other, not just from the instructor. Simulations are simplified versions of reality that allow learners to evaluate actions and carry them out consistently, mirroring real-life situations without the same consequences. This fosters autonomy, social interaction, and problem-solving skills. Effective simulations include preparation, active student participation, and post-simulation debriefing to connect the experience to course goals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires intensive pre-simulation lesson preparation, including aligning the simulation with course goals, reviewing supporting materials, and conducting a trial run. Active student participation is crucial, with each student assuming a role. Sufficient time must be allocated for post-simulation debriefing to ensure students reflect on and discuss what they learned.
#### Constraints
- Simulations can be resource-intensive to prepare. Students may get carried away or not be adequately prepared, requiring instructors to redirect them. Some students may not prefer experiential learning. Simulations may not always align perfectly with course goals if not carefully designed.

### Target Learners

- K–12, higher education, and professional development programs across various disciplines, including business, social sciences, healthcare, and education

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Challenge misconceptions, promote critical thinking, encourage self-directed learning, foster deeper understanding of subject matter, develop empathy, and emphasize abstract concepts over factual information

### Affordances
- Enhances higher-order learning and problem-solving skills by providing hands-on experience. Allows for safe exploration of consequences without real-world risks. Promotes engagement and motivation through active participation. Fosters empathy and understanding of complex systems. Increases long-term retention of information by connecting theory to practice.

### Personalization
- Simulations can be adapted to suit different learning objectives, subject areas, and learner levels. Complexity and roles can be adjusted. Simulations can be designed for face-to-face or online environments. Can be integrated with other pedagogies, such as cooperative learning.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe student engagement and participation during the simulation. Assess the depth of reflection and discussion during the debriefing. Evaluate student understanding of course goals and alignment with the simulation. Gather feedback on the simulation's effectiveness from students.

## Impact
- Research shows simulations promote critical thinking, self-directed learning, and deeper understanding of subject matter. Effective at emphasizing abstract concepts and engendering empathy. Experiential learning enhances competence as practitioners. (References: McClintock, 2000; Kreber, 2001; Hakeem, 2001; Rocha, 2000; Jessup, 2001).
