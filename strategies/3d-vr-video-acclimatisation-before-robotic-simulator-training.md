# Use short 3D VR procedural-video acclimatisation before introductory robotic simulator training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 2 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy is to have novices watch a 20-minute procedural video (robotic anterior resection) on a VR headset displaying stereoscopic console footage before their first simulator exercises. The authors state these initial findings support "the possible integration of 3D VR video prior to standard robotic surgery training protocols", while cautioning that larger trials in clinical settings are needed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a VR headset playing stereoscopic video extracted from the robotic console
- Novice trainees without prior robotic console experience
#### Constraints
- Evidence is from a small feasibility RCT in a simulation environment; authors state larger sample sizes and clinical-setting outcome data are needed
- Mild transient cybersickness occurred in 21.4% of the 3D VR group

### Target Learners
- Novice robotic surgeons
- Surgical trainees and medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Robotic simulator performance
- Familiarisation with the 3D console view
