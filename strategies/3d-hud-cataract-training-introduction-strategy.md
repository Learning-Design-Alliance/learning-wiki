# Introduce 3D heads-up display systems in cataract training programs to ease the learning curve of phacoemulsification for novice surgeons.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 8 claims (6 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends introducing 3D heads-up display systems into cataract training programs because novice surgeons scored higher and operated faster on 3D while safety outcomes remained comparable. The authors state that "Introducing 3D in cataract training programs might ease the learning curve associated with phacoemulsification." They also report that "The skills grasped on the 3D system were transferrable to the TM system."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Surgeons must train on both systems — in the study each surgeon performed alternate sessions on TM and 3D — so skills transfer back to the traditional microscope.
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a single private-practice centre with 20 novice surgeons and routine grade 2 nuclear sclerosis cataracts with well-dilated pupils.

### Target Learners
- novice ophthalmic surgeons with fewer than 10 prior cataract surgeries

### Target Learning Goals
- acquiring surgical competence in phacoemulsification cataract surgery
