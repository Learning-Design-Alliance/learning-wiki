# Train faculty in a blended learning format and build a multidisciplinary development team

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends faculty training on how to develop and implement blended learning and on their changed role in a student-centered approach, as "a good starting point for building a development team". It recommends "familiarizing the faculty with the new approach by teaching them in a longitudinal, blended learning format including follow-up sessions", so educators build on their own experience as learners, and using the same platforms, tools, frameworks, and settings used for teaching students. Development of pilot implementations serves as an example for faculty. The team should ideally include a subject matter expert, an instructional designer and e-learning specialist, technical support, and an expert on face-to-face teaching, in collaboration with assessment and evaluation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty training using the same platforms, tools, frameworks, and settings used for student teaching
- A development team with different perspectives and skills, including subject matter, instructional design, technical support and face-to-face teaching expertise
#### Constraints
- The authors admit assembling the ideal multidisciplinary team might be difficult to achieve and suggests building it in the long term

### Target Learners
- Healthcare educators and faculty in healthcare education

### Target Learning Goals
- Faculty competence in designing and implementing blended learning
