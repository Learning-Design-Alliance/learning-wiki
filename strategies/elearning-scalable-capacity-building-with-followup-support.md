# Use high-quality interactive eLearning as a scalable capacity-building tool for health professionals, supplemented with follow-up practical training, supervision and mentoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article concludes that high-quality interactive eLearning can scale up training of health professionals where specialist trainers are scarce: "We consider the eLearning course to be a scalable tool to train high volumes of in-service and pre-service health professionals with the potential to advance the Sustainable Development Goals." Because the study provided no follow-up support, the authors advise that practical training, supervision and mentoring be added when the course is used in national programmes. The course can serve as a primer or refresher, convenient and free of cost to the user.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Introduction of the course through existing structures within national programmes or local research teams
- Follow-up practical training, supervision and mentoring, which the authors advise be included when the course is used in national programmes
#### Constraints
- Not all health facility staff took the course; it may be more difficult for isolated learners to change clinical practices
- Ghana was over-represented among the three countries in the evaluation

### Target Learners
- In-service and pre-service health professionals managing paediatric malnutrition

### Target Learning Goals
- Scaling up operational capacity to diagnose and manage severe acute malnutrition
- Improving quality of clinical care and survival of children with SAM
