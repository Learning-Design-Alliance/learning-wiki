# Use a strategy for imagining the past built on two sets of guiding questions applied to primary source documents

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The author outlines an instructional strategy, developed directly out of Collingwood's theory, in which students inquire into a past topic using primary source documents. "At its heart, the strategy is based on the development of two sets of questions that guide student imagining when examining primary source documents." One set contains inquiry-specific probing questions to mine information, determine creator bias, and seek corroboration; the other contains general re-enactment and interpolation questions (e.g., what the author was thinking, what was left out) reusable across inquiries. Students record responses with supporting quotations as evidence for their imagined picture of the past.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A guiding inquiry question related to the period, topic, theme, concept, or event being studied
- Primary source documents located and collected, directly related to the topic or event
- Students keep track of sources for copied quotations, learning appropriate referencing and citation styles
#### Constraints
- The degree of sophistication of the interrogative questions is dependent on the age and developmental level of the students; younger learners may respond to only one or two questions and draw pictures or create collages captioned with a source quotation

### Target Learners
- K-12 social studies and history students, with adaptations by age and developmental level

### Target Learning Goals
- Imagining and reconstructing the past from primary sources
- Evaluating source bias and corroborating evidence
- Supporting interpretations with cited evidence
