# Offer students a range of professor-suggested research topics to choose from when selecting their own proves frustrating

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors suggest that when students feel frustrated selecting a research topic, faculty can provide a menu of candidate topics while still honoring student choice. They write that "providing them with some options to choose and letting them choose the most intriguing one among them could be a possible solution as they are still allowed to pursue what they are interested in." This adapts the PBL autonomy feature to novice researchers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Professors prepare a set of candidate research topics aligned with their expertise
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a possible solution to a student suggestion, not a tested intervention

### Target Learners
- second-year premedical undergraduate students new to research

### Target Learning Goals
- selecting a research question
- sustaining student autonomy and interest
