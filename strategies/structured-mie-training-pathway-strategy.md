# Structured MIE training pathway: simulator training, progression through bariatric and minimally invasive gastrectomy, supervised case progression, and risk-based patient selection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The institution's training program moves advanced upper-GI surgeons through a fixed sequence — "prior training with a laparoscopic trainer/simulator, followed by bariatric surgery, minimally invasive gastrectomy (MIC), and then minimally invasive esophagectomy (MIE)" — with all procedures performed jointly by trainer and trainee plus an upper-GI fellow. Early on the trainee performs sub-steps; later selected cases are done under supervision. Patient selection during the learning period favors lower-complexity cases (normal BMI, tumor < T2, low ASA, no cardiac disease or diabetes), with risk adjustment used in learning-curve analysis to handle the resulting selection bias.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trainees must be advanced upper-GI surgeons with prior experience in gastric and bariatric surgery
- All procedures performed jointly with an experienced trainer plus an upper-GI fellow
- Thorough patient selection and quality control during the learning period
#### Constraints
- Patient selection during training introduces a selection bias the authors state required risk adjustment
- The sequence does not vary for trainees, as printed

### Target Learners
- advanced upper gastrointestinal surgeons in subspecialty training

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical autonomy in minimally invasive esophagectomy
- maintaining perioperative outcome standards during training
