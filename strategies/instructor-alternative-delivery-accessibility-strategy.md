# Instructors and staff should anticipate inaccessible webpages, offer alternative content delivery, and press administration for accessibility

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because physics webpages are largely inaccessible, the article advises instructors and staff who create or edit course webpages to "explore alternative ways of delivering content to students with disabilities to ensure that all students have equal access to physics curricula and other learning materials", to respond readily when students request information directly, and to push university administration to proactively create accessible content, since institutions reportedly change only under outside pressure such as lawsuits. Physics departments are also advised to build pages from simple web elements.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors and staff must be aware of the technological hurdles students with disabilities face and be prepared to provide requested information individually
#### Constraints
- The article reports institutions will not make accessibility changes unless there are outside pressures, such as lawsuits, citing McGough (2016)

### Target Learners
- current and prospective physics students with disabilities

### Target Learning Goals
- equal access to physics curricula, course webpages, and program information
