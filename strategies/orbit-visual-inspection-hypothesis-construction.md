# Use visual orbit inspection to construct hypotheses that complement statistical analysis of longitudinal educational data

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend using visual inspection of orbits as a first step before statistical modelling: orbits reveal interesting transitions at population and individual level, and these visual insights feed hypothesis construction for subsequent statistical tests. As they conclude, "Visual inspection of orbits can help in hypothesis construction in statistical analysis", and the comparison to the GEE model shows "visual results present initial insights to help and complement statistical analysis".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Binary longitudinal data with consistent 1/0 coding of answers
- A visual orbit plot of each subject's state sequence over the observation period
#### Constraints
- The article notes results may differ for students from a different course (e.g. Engineering), since its data came from food technology and analytical chemistry students
