# Design exercises that ask students to match or beat the performance of a published paper, and seed proposed solutions with deliberate poor choices for students to improve.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The author's promising approach is to start with a literature review, find papers applying a method to data of interest, and propose exercises that "match, or improve on, the performance of a published paper", which appeals to students because it communicates they are doing quality work. Proposed solutions are deliberately riddled with poor choices such as unnormalized data or accuracy on imbalanced data, because improving the code "helps students retain a stronger memory of these potential pitfalls".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to data and introductory problem-solving guidelines from the paper's authors, which the author requests for several exercises in her book.
#### Constraints
- Finding relevant problems and datasets is described as the greatest challenge; introductory datasets are too simple and advanced ones too complex.

### Target Learners
- Advanced undergraduate and early graduate physics and astronomy students

### Target Learning Goals
- Research-grade ML pipeline construction and awareness of common pitfalls
