# Hold intentional faculty meetings about testing and grading philosophy to prevent a hidden curriculum from developing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that faculty groups discuss their assessment and grading philosophies up front rather than assuming a shared understanding. When annotation data were shared at the end-of-course meeting, discussion revealed a spectrum of philosophies about the purpose of assessment, and the authors conclude that "intentional meetings among faculty members on this issue may help to avoid the development of a hidden curriculum."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mechanism for sharing item analysis and annotation data with the whole faculty group, such as an end-of-course meeting
#### Constraints
- The article notes that in research-intensive environments with autonomous faculty members there are few opportunities to meet as a whole group to discuss the curriculum or review examination questions

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in courses taught by multiple faculty members

### Target Learning Goals
- alignment between learning objectives, learning experiences, and evaluation
