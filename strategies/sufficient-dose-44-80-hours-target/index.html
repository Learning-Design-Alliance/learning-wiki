Provide a sufficient dose of 44-80 hours of intensive instruction as a general target for at-risk reading interventions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), q1–q3 · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Drawing from tutoring programs such as Reading Recovery and Success for All, the authors used "the recommended margin of 44-80 hours of instruction as a general target" in designing their reading intervention. Their tutored students received an average of 47.33 hours (SD = 4.58) of one-on-one tutoring, which falls within this recommended range said to substantially reduce the incidence of reading failure by accelerating at-risk students' reading proficiency to average levels.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Instructional time must be sufficient to accelerate at-risk students' reading proficiency to average levels of performance

Constraints¶

  • The amount of instructional time needed to help close the achievement gap varies a great deal depending on student needs

Target Learners¶

  • At-risk early grade readers needing supplemental reading assistance

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accelerating reading proficiency to average levels of performance

Claims¶

  • At-risk first graders who received 47 hours of one-on-one tutoring read significantly more proficiently than matched at-risk non-tutored peers [+W]
  • At-risk tutored students gained nearly 20 NCE points while non-tutored at-risk peers declined from fall to spring [+W]
  • The authors judge insufficient instructional intensity (dosage) as the primary factor behind the null result of learning strategies instruction in adult education [+W]
  • An achievement gap remained between tutored at-risk students and typically developing peers despite significant tutoring gains [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Foster a school-university collaborative relationship to support identification, assessment access, and communication
  • Run an after-school literacy tutoring program pairing an existing after-school site, a structured scripted curriculum, and service-learning college student tutors
  • Target recovery resources to low-performing students via tutoring and high-quality enrichment
