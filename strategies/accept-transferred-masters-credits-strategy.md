# Encourage Ph.D. programs to accept transferred master's course credits and develop graduate-program improvement guides to shorten time-to-Ph.D.

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper recommends that Ph.D. programs be more accepting of credits transferred from master's programs, since "Reducing the amount of coursework that must be redone can shorten the total time in graduate school" and lower tuition and opportunity costs. To support this, it proposes either a subject-oriented accreditation system or, preferably, extending the APS/AAPT Effective Practices for Physics Programs (EP3) guide — offering self-assessment steps and evidence-based strategies — to graduate programs, with support for smaller programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Master's programs would need to provide preparation comparable to the first years of a Ph.D. program for credit transfer to work as intended
#### Constraints
- APS and AAPT independently concluded undergraduate physics accreditation was not the appropriate course, motivating the EP3 guide approach instead
- Any accreditation system would need flexibility for departments varying in student/faculty size and fields, plus support for programs with limited resources

### Target Learners
- physics and astronomy graduate students
- students from lower-income and underrepresented backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- timely completion of the Ph.D.
- reduced redundant coursework and financial burden
