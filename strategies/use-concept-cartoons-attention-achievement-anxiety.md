# Teachers use concept cartoons to draw attention, raise participation and achievement, and reduce mathematics anxiety

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the findings, the article recommends that "Teachers may make use of concept cartoons to draw students' attention to the lesson, and to ensure their active participation". Because the study found cartoons positively affected self-efficacy, and prior work links high self-efficacy to higher mathematics achievement, it suggests using them to raise achievement and attitudes. It also proposes cartoons as "an effective tool in elimination of mathematics anxiety", following Hackett and Betz's claim that low self-efficacy is the source of that anxiety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cartoons integrated into regular lesson activities with group discussion
#### Constraints
- Recommendation grounded in one quasi-experimental study with 94 students in a single school

### Target Learners
- 7th grade mathematics students

### Target Learning Goals
- increased attention and participation
- higher mathematics self-efficacy and achievement
- reduced mathematics anxiety
