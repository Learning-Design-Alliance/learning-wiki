Implement location-based game design as a long-term cross-subject project with the teacher acting as facilitator¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes a concrete implementation recipe: over 12 weeks, external professionals led four workshop sessions in which students brainstormed the theme, defined educational goals, wrote the narrative, spotted locations and GPS coordinates, wrote location-based interviews, and produced audio-visual and encyclopaedia content, with the classroom teacher supporting between workshops. The teacher reported that "creating location-based game was very easy to implement into our school curricula, especially of these subjects: ICT, English, Czech, Geography and according to chosen topic also History."

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teacher availability for a long-term project: the teacher needs to supply students by additional materials, mentor and motivate them during the work
  • External technical support or authoring tools, since the NGO managed all the technical tasks and implemented students-made content

Constraints¶

  • Designing a location-based game is not only a long term project but desires a deeper involvement
  • Students didn't stay focused all the time, so the teacher had to help them find motivation

Target Learners¶

  • lower secondary students aged 14-15

Target Learning Goals¶

  • subject-matter knowledge (local history)
  • 21st century soft skills such as collaboration and responsibility

Claims¶

  • Student designers reported gaining soft skills such as teamwork, text comprehension and map reading from the game-design project [+W]
  • Learning by designing an educational location-based game produced greater knowledge acquisition than learning by playing one, with significant effects on five of nine knowledge questions [+M]
  • No significant difference between designers and players in subjectively perceived interest in the game topic [~W]
  • Group membership showed weak negative Pearson correlations with knowledge score and perceived interest [~W]
