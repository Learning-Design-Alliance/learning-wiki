# Run new-nurse assessment as paired role reversal: nurses alternate between being assessed and playing standardized patients, then debrief

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy replaces paper-based assessment with role reversal combined with standardized patient simulation. Nurses study a patient's medical records and nursing needs, then simulate not only disease symptoms but emotional states such as fear and anxiety. They are split into two groups — one under assessment, one playing standardized patients — and after a round of tests "the two groups were switched", followed by a debrief in which assessors encourage nurses to express feelings about the experience. Scoring combines the teacher's score with an SPS score covering actions and sentiments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Nurses must first be guided in how to properly simulate standardized patients, including understanding patients' medical records and nursing needs
- Assessment must combine the teacher's conventional score with the SPS examiners' score of nurses' actions and sentiments
#### Constraints
- Evaluated only in one hospital setting with newly recruited nurses; the article states the study still needs to be applied to clinical work

### Target Learners
- newly recruited graduate nurses in hospital pre-employment training

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical nursing competence
- patient-centred communication and humanistic care
- handling of standardized patient scenarios including emotional states
