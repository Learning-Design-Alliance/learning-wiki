# When the diagnosis is self-evident, use focused who/when/where/what/why/how discussion questions instead of a diagnosis commitment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The DEFT Diagnosis step allows a specific Discussion point when the diagnosis is evident. The article recommends addressing 'alternative relevant discussion points' using focused questions about who is at risk, when (temporal associations), where (geographic distribution), what therapy, why (underlying cause), and how (mechanism or complications).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clinical case in which the diagnosis is evident or already established
#### Constraints
- Applies only when the diagnosis is self-evident; otherwise the step asks for a unifying or differential diagnosis commitment

### Target Learners
- clinical-year medical students
- residents/registrars
- fellows

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnostic reasoning
- understanding disease mechanisms and complications
