# Use ChatGPT-based virtual patient chatbots as a supplementary platform for bedside history-taking practice and international case sharing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 design, 1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Deploy an LLM-based virtual patient chatbot as an additional exercise alongside conventional bedside teaching: students practice history-taking with virtual patients at any time and location, teachers curate cases through a non-technical interface, and partner institutions exchange local cases (e.g., dengue fever in Singapore, nasopharyngeal carcinoma in Hong Kong) so students see diseases not prevalent locally. The authors state the chatbot "will be a feasible supplementary teaching tool to conventional bedside teaching".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- De-identified patient data processed before launch
- Face-to-face bedside teaching remains central; the chatbot supplements, not replaces it
#### Constraints
- Privacy safeguards required: patient names replaced, one general student account so no student records are kept, and rare-case sharing needs extra anonymity precautions
- Case availability across institutions varies with policies and legal constraints

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical history-taking skills
- exposure to non-local and rare diseases
- clinical reasoning
