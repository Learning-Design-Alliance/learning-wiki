# Co-construct Orrery sequences with teachers across subjects and extend toward interdisciplinary STEAM approaches

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The authors report that "Different sequences have been co-constructed by teachers and researchers" at the sites where Human Orreries were installed, involving physics, mathematics and technology teachers. Their forward-looking recommendation is to motivate more classes and teachers from subjects other than mathematics and physics, such as art, technology, music, languages and physical education, and to design sequences fitting the STEAM approach, used as experimental situations for the enaction framework complemented with Activity Theory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Human Orreries installed and used thereafter by different teachers in schools, science centres and public places
- Collaboration between teachers and researchers to co-construct sequences
