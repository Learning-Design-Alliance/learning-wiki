# Reduce surgical workload when returning to operating through preoperative planning, familiar settings and distraction control

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends concrete workload-reduction practices for surgeons returning to non-essential operating when capability may not be as high as previously. Careful planning in the days before the procedure includes patient and procedure selection, allocating complex procedures more time, operating in the regular theatre with familiar teams, and sending a pre-planned equipment and implant shopping list to reduce day-of decision making. On the day, meticulous team briefs and checklists are advised, with hierarchical gradients actively lowered so any team member can challenge another without fear of repercussion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Preoperative planning should be thorough so fewer intraoperative decisions are required, potentially as a written plan with each step visible in theatre
#### Constraints
- Recommended in the specific context of returning to operating after the COVID-19 peak when capability may be reduced

### Target Learners
- surgeons returning to non-urgent operating
- theatre teams

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing intraoperative cognitive and physical workload
- preventing workload exceeding capability
