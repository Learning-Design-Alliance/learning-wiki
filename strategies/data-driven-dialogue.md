# Data Driven Dialogue

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Data Driven Dialogue is a facilitation protocol for collaborative analysis of student data (assessment results, attendance, work samples) by educator teams. Developed by Lipton and Wellman, it deliberately separates phases of exploration: groups first *activate* prior knowledge and predict what the data will show, then *observe* the data with strictly descriptive language, and only then *infer* causes and commit to actions. The structure prevents the common failure mode of leaping from a glance at data to an unfounded intervention.

## Design Implications

The protocol works because it imposes cognitive discipline on a task — interpreting data — where confirmation bias and premature closure are strong. Requiring predictions before seeing results creates a [cognitive disequilibrium](../principles/cognitive-disequilibrium.md) when expectations are violated, which motivates genuine analysis rather than rationalization [~M]. Separating observation ("what do I see?") from inference ("what might it mean?") counters the tendency to treat data as confirmation of existing beliefs, and descriptive-first norms reduce the social threat that makes data conversations defensive rather than inquiry-oriented [~W].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator (or trained team lead) who enforces phase boundaries and norms
- A concrete, shared data display — chart, table, or graph — accessible to all participants
- Time protected for the full sequence; compressing the predict/observe phases collapses the protocol into ordinary staff discussion
- Norms that distinguish descriptive statements from causal claims

#### Constraints
- Degrades into ritual compliance when teams have no authority to act on what they find — dialogue without decision rights produces cynicism [-W]
- Weak or poorly displayed data invites inference from noise; small-sample classroom data can mislead teams into overreacting [-M]
- Facilitator skill is critical: a facilitator who allows premature inference or blame-talk undermines the entire structure [-M]
- Requires repeated cycles; one-off data dialogues rarely change instructional practice [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Full three-phase protocol** (predict → observe → infer) for major assessment cycles
- **Abbreviated "data snapshot"** — a 20-minute observation-only round for interim checks
- **Data walls** — visual displays that anchor ongoing dialogue across meetings
- Adapted for student use: learners can run the same predict/observe/infer sequence on their own assessment data, turning the protocol into an [assessment for learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) practice [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]

### Target Learners
- Educator teams (PLC members, grade-level or department groups) analyzing common assessment data
- School leadership teams using the [Data Wise improvement process](../processes/data-wise-improvement-process.md)
- Students, in adapted form, reflecting on their own performance data [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Data literacy: reading and interpreting assessment displays accurately
- Collaborative inquiry: building shared, evidence-based understanding of learner needs
- Instructional decision-making: connecting data patterns to teaching adjustments [Assessment for learning improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]

### Instructions
1. **Predict.** Before showing the data, the facilitator asks teams to record predictions and assumptions ("What do we expect to see? What are we worried about?") — this activates prior knowledge and surfaces biases [Activation improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
2. **Observe.** Reveal the data; teams generate only descriptive observations ("63% of students missed item 4"), no explanations or blame.
3. **Infer.** Teams generate multiple hypotheses for observed patterns, deliberately seeking alternative explanations before settling on one.
4. **Act.** Connect inferences to instructional changes, assign follow-ups, and schedule a check-in on the next data cycle.
