# Focus principal-education presentations on inappropriate school counselor activities, since principals already understand appropriate activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that presenters be concise and spend less time covering appropriate activities, since principals already rate those highly, and instead place "more emphasis can be placed on educating principals regarding inappropriate activities." This follows from the finding that appropriate activities showed no significant changes because they were already rated as appropriate before the presentation, while several inappropriate activities showed significant decreases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prior knowledge of which activities principals already rate as appropriate versus inappropriate
#### Constraints
- Based on a future-principal sample that may not generalize to practicing principals

### Target Learners
- Future school principals
- Practicing school principals

### Target Learning Goals
- Recognizing inappropriate school counselor activities such as scheduling-registration, discipline, and administrative duties
