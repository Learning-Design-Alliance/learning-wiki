# Combine a briefing with a simulation scenario and individual expert feedback when training staff on a new medical device

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (2 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that, when training on medical devices, teams combine a verbal briefing with hands-on simulation and individual feedback on errors. The authors state it is "useful, specifically when training on medical devices, to include simulation training with expert feedback whenever possible, so that caregivers can really practice what has been explained to them". In their study, nurses practised BP measurement and arterial sampling on a manikin with fake blood, received individual feedback on their errors, and named the information they would pass to a colleague.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulation scenario and evaluation grid designed by a multi-disciplinary team trained in depth on the new device
- A dedicated evaluator and trained simulation nurses to run the session and give individual feedback
#### Constraints
- The study was mono-centric, in three surgical ICUs of one hospital, with nurses recruited on a voluntary basis

### Target Learners
- ICU nurses

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe and correct use of a new invasive medical device
- Reduction of device use errors
