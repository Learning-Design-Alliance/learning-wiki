# Reduce workloads and chunk instruction into smaller pieces to support diminished capacity and executive functioning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Under Reason, the brief advises educators to "Reduce workloads for yourself and your students," noting that with stress and change, "students' physical and mental capacities to handle an academic workload are diminished." Teachers should slim down normal lessons, for example reducing required reading or response length. They should also ensure instruction and materials are "chunked" so students focus on one new learning task at a time, give one or two instructions at a time, and allow plenty of time for processing and responding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Caregiver support to follow chunking and routines at home; schedules provided as customizable examples
#### Constraints
- The brief states effective and meaningful lessons may not happen immediately or all at once while families adjust to a new normal

### Target Learners
- K-12 distance learning students, especially those facing adversity or trauma

### Target Learning Goals
- Executive functioning and self-management
- academic engagement under stress
