# Use small-group task-based learning with case scenarios and hands-on laboratory procedures to teach integration in practical classes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing laboratory classes so students "work in small groups of 3–4 students to complete specific tasks". Tasks include brief case scenarios requiring students to interpret biochemical and microbiological test results, describe pathological changes, match findings across disciplines, and perform a matching laboratory procedure, followed by tutor discussion and feedback. The authors state these cognitive skills are consistent with the pedagogy of PBL and reinforce the curriculum design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks aligned with the learning outcomes of the laboratory class and the theme of the week
- Tutors from the contributing disciplines allocated and briefed about their roles
#### Constraints
- The strategy was implemented in a hybrid PBL preclinical curriculum organized around parallel body systems

### Target Learners
- preclinical medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- interpreting laboratory results
- refining hypotheses using evidence
- integrating basic-science knowledge with clinical applications
