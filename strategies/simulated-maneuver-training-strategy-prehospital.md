# Use crisis management training with simulated earthquake maneuvers to increase prehospital emergency personnel performance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's own recommendation for practice: crisis management training for emergency personnel should be delivered through simulated maneuvers rather than lecture alone. The conclusion states "it can be suggested that crisis management training in earthquake events be used with the simulated maneuver method to increase the performance of emergency medical personnel," and the discussion argues "purely theoretical training may not be effective in real situations."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Trained specialists should deliver the training so that "incorrect information and behaviors are not provided to emergency medical medical personnel" is avoided
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one trial with questionnaire-based assessment rather than field performance measures; long-term durability beyond 6 months is untested

### Target Learners
- Prehospital emergency and incident management center personnel

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving crisis management performance and preparedness for earthquake and disaster response

- [Prehospital emergency personnel who received crisis management training with earthquake simulation scored significantly higher on crisis management after the intervention than controls (Cohen's d = 1.2)](../claims/earthquake-simulation-crisis-training-raises-crisis-management-scores.md) [+M]
