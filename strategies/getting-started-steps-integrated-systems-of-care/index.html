Five getting-started steps for building an integrated, school-based system of care¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The guide prescribes five steps for LEAs beginning whole child work: examine the current system via needs assessment and asset mapping; identify a leadership team with key partners; identify long-term transformational goals and starting points; plan for sustainability given limited-time funds; and build a formal coalition for collective impact. It defines collective impact as involving "a centralized infrastructure, a dedicated staff, and a structured process that leads to a common agenda, shared measurement, continuous communication, and mutually reinforcing activities among all participants." Partners should be engaged as co-leaders committed to the full vision.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A leadership team that oversees implementation and monitors progress over time, with partners invited as co-leaders

Constraints¶

  • Many state and federal funding sources for these efforts are limited-time funds

Target Learners¶

  • Students and families in local education agency communities, prioritizing unmet needs beyond typical public education domains

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Coordinated, comprehensive whole child supports that improve health, well-being, and learning

Related Strategies¶

  • Discovery-phase data collection: surveys, listening sessions, and project inventories to map the current system
  • Assess team readiness across leadership, facilitation, communication, shared understanding, and capacity before starting
  • Use asset mapping to identify and celebrate existing strengths in the school and community
  • Use the tools as facilitated conversation starters that produce artifacts, not as a checklist exercise
  • Implement SEL systemically as a public health approach with shared vision, adult SEL, aligned programs, and continuous improvement
