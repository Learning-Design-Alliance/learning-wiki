# Build the chart from scratch at each event so participants actively construct it and gain a hands-on sense of nuclear scales

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q1` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article names the participatory rebuild as the project's key design choice: "the chart is built from scratch at each event, such that the participants actively engage in the construction and thereby get a hands-on sense of the scales involved". Rather than presenting a static display, every session has the audience lay out plates and stack towers, making the learners themselves produce the artefact through which the physics is then discussed.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Printed or tablet-based plate-layout instruction sheets, with neutron and proton numbers and layer counts, so participants can build their assigned region without expert guidance
- A stock of colour-coded LEGO bricks matching the layout colours
#### Constraints
- Construction time "have ranged from 1/2 hour to 4 hours depending on the type of event", so session planning must fit the event format

### Target Learners
- school students
- secondary school teachers
- public visitors at outreach events

### Target Learning Goals
- hands-on sense of the scales of nuclear energy and isotope abundance
- familiarity with the organisation of the nuclide chart
