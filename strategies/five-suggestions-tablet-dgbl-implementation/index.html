Five implementation suggestions for tablet-based DGBL in preschools¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the review findings, the author proposes five suggestions for practitioners: "Select self-adaptive games with extensible content and adjustable difficulty levels", evaluate interaction design to avoid cognitive overload, adopt long duration (more than 10 weeks) with low daily dosage (less than 20 min a day), use tablet games and hands-on activities complementarily, and provide sufficient pre-service training and sustained in-service support for teachers. These translate the four-level factor analysis into implementable practice.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Requires access to self-adaptive game content, appropriate devices, and teacher professional development structures

Constraints¶

  • The daily-dosage suggestion aligns with American Academy of Pediatrics guidance limiting screen use for children aged 2-5 to about one hour per day of high-quality media

Target Learners¶

  • preschool children aged 3 to 6 years, including children from low-SES families

Target Learning Goals¶

  • early mathematics skills including numeracy, shapes, spatial abilities, and measurement

Claims¶

  • Self-adaptive game design with multiple difficulty levels is positively associated with intervention effectiveness [+W]
  • Using tablet games as a supplement or as a replacement for routine math activities may have equivalent effectiveness [+W]
  • Tablet-based DGBL shows strong efficacy for children from low-SES families [+W]
  • Most tablet-based DGBL interventions in preschool settings improve young children's mathematical performance [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use regulated, supervised interactive tablet games as a low-cost stimulation support for early childhood educators
