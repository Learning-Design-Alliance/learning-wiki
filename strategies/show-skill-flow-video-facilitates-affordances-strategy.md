# Design instructional interfaces for physical-skill learning so demonstrations show the full flow of the skill and link seeing with hearing, rather than presenting static staged diagrams

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the sewing context, the anthropomorphic condition presented "a video of a human describing how to perform each relevant stitch" while the presenter simultaneously performed the stitch with needle and fabric, facilitating cognitive and sensory affordances. The contrasting condition used static diagrams with text, which the authors say lost the aspect of flow in performing a stitch. The recommended design activity is therefore to present instruction through demonstrations showing the continuous flow of the skill, aligning with Hartson's cognitive and sensory affordances, so learners can observe transitions between sub-stages.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructional content must be identical across presentation modes to avoid biasing comparisons; a demonstration medium must adequately show the skill being performed
#### Constraints
- The authors note a future experiment should reverse the pairing—an anthropomorphic condition that violates affordances and a non-anthropomorphic one that facilitates them—to clarify whether affordances matter more than anthropomorphic presence itself

### Target Learners
- adult novices learning hands-on practical skills such as sewing

### Target Learning Goals
- acquiring manual procedural skills
- following multi-stage physical procedures

### Affordances
- Hartson Four Affordances User Interfaces

## Claims

- [Facilitated cognitive and sensory affordances—seeing a stitch performed in full flow rather than interpreting static diagrams—explain the anthropomorphic condition's effectiveness and more positive perceptions](../claims/affordance-facilitation-explains-anthropomorphic-advantage.md) [+W]
- [Anthropomorphic video-based sewing instruction that facilitates affordances produced significantly more correct stitches and fewer incorrect stitches than non-anthropomorphic diagram-and-text instruction for both easy and difficult stitch tasks](../claims/anthropomorphic-sewing-feedback-more-correct-fewer-incorrect-stitches.md) [+M]
- [When cognitive and sensory affordances are not facilitated, errors and task completion time increase significantly, particularly for more difficult tasks, and user perceptions become more negative](../claims/unfacilitated-affordances-increase-errors-time-negative-perceptions.md) [+M]
- [Prior anthropomorphic-interface studies show inconsistent findings, with some favouring anthropomorphic feedback and others not, motivating the affordance-based explanation](../claims/inconsistent-anthropomorphic-interface-findings-literature.md) [~W]
