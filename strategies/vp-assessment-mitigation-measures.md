# Mitigation measures when using menu-based virtual patients as an alternative to standardized patients in assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (3 for, 4 mixed) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends concrete measures for practitioners who use VPs as an alternative to SPs in performance-based assessment, because VPs "could lead to lower diagnostic accuracy scores than SPs, which could, in turn, negatively affect students' grades". Suggested measures include motivating students with more interactive environments such as natural language processing or automated elaborated feedback, extending assessment time with menu-based VPs to raise the quantity of evidence generation, and careful training and screening of SP actors to prevent additional support where SPs are used.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment designers must monitor and manipulate intrinsic and extraneous cognitive load systematically, since both correlated negatively with diagnostic accuracy
#### Constraints
- The measures address menu-based VPs in history-taking assessment; the study could not rule out that SP actors provided additional support, so the direction of any performance bias remains open

### Target Learners
- medical students in years 3-6 of a 6-year program

### Target Learning Goals
- history-taking and diagnostic competence in dyspnea cases
