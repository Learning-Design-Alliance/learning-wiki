# Prioritize early fine-motor-skill interventions for socioeconomically disadvantaged populations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on finding that low handwriting performance clusters in lower-SES schools and that kinematic and pressure components are most affected, the authors urge "early interventions that focus on developing fine motor skills, particularly in socioeconomically disadvantaged populations". They suggest digital tools such as Dynamilis, which provides embedded activities tailored via a recommendation algorithm, could be evaluated for this purpose.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions should target the kinematic and pressure components identified as most affected by socioeconomic background
#### Constraints
- The cross-sectional design limits causal conclusions, and the authors call for longitudinal studies to assess intervention effectiveness

### Target Learners
- socioeconomically disadvantaged early-primary students

### Target Learning Goals
- handwriting fluency and fine motor skill development
