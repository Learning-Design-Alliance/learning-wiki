# Promote perceived parental acceptance and prevent peer rejection in pre-adolescence to support long-term educational attainment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends intervening in pre-adolescence through both home and school relationships: "promoting perceived parental acceptance and preventing peer rejection in pre-adolescence are promising for helping adolescents to reach higher levels of education in the long run." Programs should give parents tools to create an accepting environment at home, and schools should gain insight into adolescents' social status and promote a favorable peer climate, for example through anti-bullying interventions, which "may prevent negative peer experiences and foster academic achievement."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should target pre-adolescence, since the long-term effects of parental acceptance and peer rejection were measured from age 11.
- Schools need insight into adolescents' social status at school to foster a favorable peer climate.
#### Constraints
- The study is observational, so the recommendations rest on longitudinal associations rather than tested causal effects of interventions.

### Target Learners
- Pre-adolescents (around age 11) in primary education

### Target Learning Goals
- Long-term educational attainment; academic development and achievement
