# Repeatedly expose medical students to high-fidelity simulation to build psychological stability before clinical encounters

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that medical students be repeatedly exposed to high-fidelity simulation education so they gain "a sense of psychological stability" and can "competently deliver medical treatment in a clinical setting". Sessions of 10-15 minutes with Laerdal SimMan, prebriefing, and faculty feedback were used, with students rotating through pulmonary and gastrointestinal disease scenarios in small groups of two to three.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to high-fidelity patient simulators and faculty supervision, as used in the study's clerkship program
#### Constraints
- The study analyzed only two simulation experiences, which were not conducted equally for all groups, so effects of longer repeated exposure remain open

### Target Learners
- fifth-year medical students beginning clinical practice

### Target Learning Goals
- initial assessment, diagnosis, and treatment planning; effective teamwork and communication with the patient
