# Provide structured training in peer teaching and feedback before PAL activities, and run PAL as structured, well-organised, monitored activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends preparing students for PAL with training covering the benefits of PAL, the specific skill being taught or learned, and practice giving constructive written and/or verbal feedback, plus open discussion of expectations. It concludes that PAL activities “must be structured, well organised and monitored to ensure students’ learning experience is optimised”. In the pilots, training ran 1 to 4 hours face to face.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training in peer teaching and feedback prior to PAL activity
- Organisational structures so PAL sessions run smoothly, including clarifying peer learners' expectations
#### Constraints
- Where there is a less collaborative and cooperative culture amongst students, skill attainment may be limited

### Target Learners
- health professions students in classroom and clinical settings

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical examination, consultation and communication skills
- peer teaching and feedback skills
- critical writing, referencing and case presentation skills
