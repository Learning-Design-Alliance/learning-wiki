# Integrate trained school children (peers) and medical students as BLS assessors to reduce personnel requirements at school events

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends including trained school children and medical students alongside professionals as assessors of school children's BLS skills. Peers received two three-hour training sessions including the 4-step-approach and checklist practice; all assessors were trained with frame-of-reference training. This reduced the personnel requirement of professional assessors while maintaining high reliability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Peers must be trained beforehand in two three-hour sessions by experienced instructors, and all assessors must receive frame-of-reference training with the checklist up to 2 weeks in advance
#### Constraints
- Peers rated some items more strictly than professionals, so pass-rates depend on assessor mix

### Target Learners
- school children grades 7 to 10
- medical students in their final year of study

### Target Learning Goals
- reliable assessment and feedback of hands-on BLS skills
