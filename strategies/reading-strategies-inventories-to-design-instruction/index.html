Use reading strategies inventories to identify which strategies learners currently use before designing instruction¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that teachers help learners identify their reading strategies by using reading strategies inventories. Such inventories inform teachers and learners which reading strategies learners currently use, and having that information assists teachers in designing reading strategies instruction that focuses on strategies that are new to the learners rather than re-teaching familiar ones.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers need an inventory instrument, such as the Reading Strategies Questionnaire used in the study, administered before instruction

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • Secondary and tertiary learners reading in a second language

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Reading strategy awareness
  • reading comprehension

Claims¶

  • Explicit instruction in reading strategies was essential to bring about increased use of reading strategies by experimental-group learners [+W]
  • Grade 11 ESL learners who received explicit reading strategy instruction achieved statistically and practically significantly higher reading comprehension scores than control-group learners [+W]
  • Perceived reading strategy use positively affected reading comprehension among Grade 11 ESL learners [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Incorporate explicit direct instruction of metacognitive reading strategies into the reading curriculum
