# Design XR interfaces for young learners with embodied tasks, direct manipulation, and layered spatial scaffolds

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review distills evidence-based design recommendations for elementary XR STEM applications. Tasks should leverage embodied learning principles, requiring movement and physical manipulation to engage elementary students while supporting abstract concept formation. Interaction modalities should favor direct manipulation (grabbing, moving, rotating virtual objects with natural hand gestures) over abstract symbolic controls, with concise voice prompts supplementing gesture-based interaction. Because spatial ability varies across young learners, interfaces should provide layered scaffolds—visual guides, simplified representations, step-by-step procedures—enabling struggling students to access content while advanced learners explore complex spatial relationships.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Immediate feedback tied directly to actions to reinforce cause-effect relationships supporting procedural learning
- Analytics interfaces prioritizing teacher-facing interpretable indicators rather than opaque algorithmic scores
#### Constraints
- Documented individual differences in spatial ability mean a single interface will not fit all learners; technical expertise required for content creation means most teachers cannot develop or customize XR experiences

### Target Learners
- Elementary students ages 5-12 with varying spatial reasoning abilities

### Target Learning Goals
- Abstract concept visualization and embodied STEM learning in AR/MR/VR environments
