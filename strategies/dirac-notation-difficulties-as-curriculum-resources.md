# Use documented student difficulties with Dirac-notation operator representations as resources for developing curricula and pedagogies that integrate mathematical and conceptual aspects of the QM formalism

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 13 claims (13 for) · 6 studies (4 qualitative, 2 theoretical), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 6 report an effect size · 13 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend treating the documented difficulties as input for instructional design: helping advanced students become facile with different representations "requires that students integrate mathematical and conceptual aspects of the QM formalism involving Dirac notation". The strategy targets upper-level undergraduate and graduate QM courses, using the catalogued errors (incorrect orthogonality and normalization conditions, dropped delta functions, failure to interpret bracket expressions) to guide curricula and pedagogies for representational transformation skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instruction should target students enrolled in upper-level undergraduate and first-year graduate quantum mechanics courses who have received traditional instruction in the relevant concepts

- 

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students
- first-year graduate quantum mechanics students

### Target Learning Goals
- transform expressions involving quantum operators corresponding to observables between Dirac, position and momentum representations
- develop conceptual understanding of what Dirac-notation expressions mean

## Claims

- [Students' common difficulty is not knowing that a Dirac-notation bracket expression such as ⟨x|p̂|p′⟩ is conceptually equivalent to the operator acting on the state in that representation, even when they know the pieces separately](../claims/bracket-expression-not-recognized-as-operator-acting-on-state.md) [+W]
- [Graduate students were more facile than undergraduates at transforming ⟨x|p̂|p′⟩ and ⟨p|p̂|p′⟩, but interviews indicate this facility was largely due to memorized expressions rather than conceptual understanding](../claims/graduate-students-memorized-procedures-not-conceptual-understanding.md) [+W]
- [Only 23% of upper-level undergraduates answered a multiple-choice question correctly about the momentum operator acting on a momentum eigenstate in Dirac notation after traditional instruction](../claims/ug-23-percent-correct-momentum-operator-dirac.md) [+W]
- [Undergraduates struggled with a generic operator acting on a generic quantum state: 35% identified the correct position-representation form on a multiple-choice question and only 6% wrote it on the open-ended question](../claims/ug-generic-operator-generic-state-difficulty.md) [+W]
- [Only 27% of undergraduates correctly wrote ⟨p|p̂|p′⟩, and 8% invoked an incorrect normalization condition, often confusing the Kronecker delta with the Dirac delta function](../claims/ug-incorrect-normalization-kronecker-dirac-confusion.md) [+W]
- [Only 15% of undergraduates could write ⟨x|p̂|p′⟩ without Dirac notation, and some invoked an incorrect orthogonality condition between position and momentum eigenstates](../claims/ug-incorrect-orthogonality-position-momentum.md) [+W]
- [Upper-level students show inconsistent reasoning about the position-space wave function across consecutive questions](../claims/qm-inconsistent-reasoning-position-representation.md) [+W]
- [On the operator question, 54% answered correctly but 41% treated statements (1) and (2) inconsistently despite answering question 1 correctly](../claims/dirac-question4-operator-inconsistency.md) [+W]
- [Difficulties with Dirac notation persisted in advanced students, and regression on problem 1 suggests some had memorized procedures rather than mastered the notation](../claims/dirac-notation-memorized-procedures.md) [+W]
- [Only 41% answered the quantum measurement probability question correctly, with difficulty translating probability densities between representations](../claims/dirac-question5-measurement-probability-difficulty.md) [+W]
- [Graduate students' identification of ψ(x)=ψ(x) is context-dependent: 90% affirmed it in one question while 41% contradicted it on a consecutive question](../claims/qm-graduate-inconsistent-wavefunction-identification.md) [+W]
- [Only 45% of upper-level undergraduate students selected the correct answer on a multiple-choice question about the identity operator and expectation value expression in Dirac notation](../claims/only-45-percent-correct-q1-expectation-value.md) [+W]
