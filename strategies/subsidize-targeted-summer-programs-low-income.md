# Prefer subsidizing targeted summer programs or low-income families' program tuition over modified year-round school calendars

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
In its policy discussion, the article recommends that "it may be preferable to address inequities in parental involvement either by subsidizing targeted (or voluntary) summer programs or simply subsidizing the summer-program tuitions and fees of children in low-income households, schools, or neighborhoods". This is preferred to year-round calendars, which may harm high-achieving students' social and emotional development. Access alone is insufficient: parents must encourage and prioritize attendance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Parents must encourage and prioritize children's attendance, as access alone will not work
#### Constraints
- Modified year-round calendars might negatively impact the social and emotional development of high-achieving students

### Target Learners
- Low-income, low-SES school-aged children

### Target Learning Goals
- Reducing summer learning loss and closing SES achievement gaps
