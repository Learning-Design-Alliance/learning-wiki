# Drawing for Emergent Writing

## Description
Drawing activities support emergent writing by allowing unstructured drawing time and using drawings in literacy activities. Children plan, brainstorm, and develop new ideas through drawing, which serves as a pre-writing activity. Drawing a picture enables children to tell a story and express ideas through illustrations, which later serve as a plan for writing. Teachers can then have students 'write' about their picture to the extent of their abilities, read their words (with the teacher writing the correct forms), and celebrate their work to reinforce that pictures represent words and ideas, and that they are writers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Paper, crayons, or drawing tools. Time for drawing, discussion, and writing. Teacher guidance to facilitate the connection between pictures and writing.
#### Constraints
- If not facilitated properly, students may not make the connection between drawing and writing. Requires teacher guidance to steer stories back toward what is drawn.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Elementary-aged children, particularly pre-writers and emergent writers.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Develop foundational writing and reading skills, unlock imagination, build confidence, and understand the relationship between pictures, words, and ideas.

### Affordances
- Drawing provides a visual and kinesthetic way to express ideas, making abstract concepts more concrete. It fosters creativity, planning skills, and the understanding that pictures represent words and ideas.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted to various skill levels. Students can draw based on their own interests and abilities, and write to the extent of their capabilities. Teachers can provide prompts or questions to guide students.
