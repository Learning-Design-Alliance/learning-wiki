# Design curricula for long-term retention using spaced practice with expanding intervals, variable practice, and frequent comprehensive testing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review distills the retention literature into four key design issues: "make systematic use of distributed or spaced practice", an expanding ratio of intervals between sessions, as much variable practice as possible, and frequent testing, aiming at overlearning and accessibility of knowledge regardless of retrieval context. Concrete recommendations include brief appropriately spaced sessions, avoiding prolonged massed cramming, grading advanced courses to retroactively reinforce earlier knowledge, and cumulative end-of-course examinations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge to be retained must be worthwhile enough that faculty choose to reinforce it in successive courses rather than de-emphasize it
#### Constraints
- The review notes prolonged (massed) cramming is what is detrimental, while repeated short bouts of intensive study and short drill episodes might be beneficial; practical considerations may limit the feasibility of post-graduation refresher training

### Target Learners
- medical students
- students in formal academic courses

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term retention of factual and conceptual basic science knowledge
