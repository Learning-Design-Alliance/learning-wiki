# Address implementation costs and design unknowns before running school incentive schemes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q4` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The evaluation's conclusions direct schools and researchers to plan for the operational and financial costs of incentive schemes and to resolve open design questions. Schools found it difficult to organise and pay for events before knowing how many pupils would meet targets, and should consider the cost of monitoring and feeding back about effort. The report recommends that "Further research should explore the level of incentive required to induce pupil effort, and the long-term impact of such schemes," including possible adverse effects of incentivising only one subject or pupil group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Budget for the cost of incentives and of monitoring and feedback on pupil effort
- Resolve event logistics such as deposits before attendance numbers are known
#### Constraints
- The intervention tested incentives additional to existing school schemes, so it cannot test the effectiveness of small schemes schools already offered

### Target Learners
- Year 11 pupils in secondary schools

### Target Learning Goals
- Increasing pupil effort and GCSE attainment through incentive schemes
