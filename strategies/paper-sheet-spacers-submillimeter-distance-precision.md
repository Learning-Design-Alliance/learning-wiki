# Use counted sheets of paper as precision spacers to vary earbud-to-sensor distance below ruler resolution

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
This strategy replaces fine distance control with a stack of book pages: students measure the thickness of a large counted stack of pages (e.g., from "Page 1" to at least "Page 1000") and compute a per-sheet conversion factor, so that inserting 10, 20 or 100 sheets steps the earbud away from the phone screen with sub-0.1 mm resolution. The manual explains that a ruler's millimeter markings are too coarse, but "The thickness of a single sheet of paper can get us our desired measurement precision of less than 0.1 mm." Sheets are added in increasing increments (10 up to 80, then 20 up to 200, then 100).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A thick physical book with at least 1,000 pages (about 1-1/2 inches) so the per-sheet thickness can be measured precisely; a millimeter ruler
- Students must count sheets of paper, not page numbers, halving and rounding up the page count
#### Constraints
- If the phone's magnetometer shuts off at high fields, a minimum number of spacer sheets must be kept between earbud and screen

### Target Learners
- introductory undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring magnetic field dependence on axial distance with high precision
- experimental design and measurement-precision reasoning
