# Three-step procedure for adding linking changes to scientific texts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
In the discussion the authors propose a general procedure to improve the linkage between text information and the reader's previous knowledge: "(a)toidentifythetextualmacropropositions", "(b) to analyzeif possiblereadersmayhavetheknowledgetheymightneedtodeeplyunderstand thetextualmacropropositions, orif itisnecessarytoaddsomecontentideasintothetext", and "(c) to examine if the textual information... can be broughtclosertothereadersbyintroducingimagery,orbyusinglinguisticresources". In the study this was implemented by adding missing premises and familiar imagery (e.g., solar system analogies) to a physics text.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coherence textual changes should be implemented together with linking changes, since linking changes alone did not improve problem solving in the experiment
- Writers should make explicit only information unlikely to be easily activated, to avoid the text becoming tedious and boring
#### Constraints
- The authors state there are no clear general procedures for improving integration: these depend greatly on the reader's background and text content, and methods adequate for fourth graders or history texts may not suit high-school science

### Target Learners
- high-school science students reading unfamiliar scientific texts
- tenth-grade students

### Target Learning Goals
- deep level understanding of scientific text enabling problem solving
