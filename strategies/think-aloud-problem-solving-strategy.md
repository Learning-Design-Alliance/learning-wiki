# Use think-aloud protocols by instructors and students to make problem-solving processes visible and scaffold monitoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Instructors model problem-solving with a think-aloud protocol, describing actions, alternative pathways, and the pros and cons of paths not taken, and record think-aloud solutions with smartpens or tablets. Students likewise record think-aloud homework so instructors can give process feedback. The article argues that "Solvers who articulate their decision-making as they solve problems, are able to solve them more effectively than those who don't", provided the verbalization focuses on decision-making rather than merely talking out actions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Class time and assignments devoted to practicing think-alouds, with early feedback solely on the quality of verbalization, sometimes using non-STEM content
#### Constraints
- Most students are not particularly proficient at performing think-aloud solutions at the beginning of a course; simply talking out one's actions is not as effective as focusing on decision-making

### Target Learners
- lower-division undergraduate STEM students

### Target Learning Goals
- problem-solving process skills
- metacognitive monitoring
