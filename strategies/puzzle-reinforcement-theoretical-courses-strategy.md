# Use interactive, student-centered reinforcement techniques such as puzzles in theoretical nursing courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the trial results, the authors recommend that "interactive, student-centered teaching techniques, such as puzzles, be used more widely in faculties, especially in theoretical courses", so that participation increases, learning becomes more engaging, and information retention is supported. They further suggest integrating puzzles and other game-based learning tools into educational materials, and employing a range of techniques such as simulation, scenario-based learning, and flipped classrooms alongside traditional teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Puzzle materials aligned with learning objectives and reviewed for content accuracy
- Instructor monitoring, feedback, and individual discussion of errors
#### Constraints
- Findings come from a single-university sample of first-year students and should be interpreted within the study's methodological limitations

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students in theoretical courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Increased course participation
- Information retention
- Motivation toward instructional materials
