# Use immersive VR virtual patient scenarios to give pre-clinical and multicultural students safe, repeatable clinical communication practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (8 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends immersive VR simulation as a strategy for settings where “hands-on clinical engagement” is limited by institutional constraints, patient availability or language proficiency gaps. VR can “mitigate these barriers by providing structured, linguistically and culturally tailored scenarios that allow learners to experiment without fear of judgment or error,” supporting deliberate practice with repeated exposure, reflection and refinement. The authors suggest future course iterations should “expand scenario diversity and instructional time, guided by student feedback and ongoing evaluation.”

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenario design tailored to learners’ linguistic and cultural context
- Feedback and debriefing structures accompanying the simulation
#### Constraints
- Appropriate for pilot-stage integration; the authors call for larger controlled trials, objective outcome measures and longitudinal evaluation before wider claims

### Target Learners
- Pre-clinical medical students
- International students in multicultural medical programmes
- Digital-native (Gen Alpha) higher-education learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe practice of clinical communication before real patient contact
- Preparedness for rare or high-risk clinical events
- Intercultural sensitivity and communication
