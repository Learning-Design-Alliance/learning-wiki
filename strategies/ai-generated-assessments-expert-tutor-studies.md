# Use dynamically generated AI assessments to enable future studies of expert tutors in natural contexts

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future tutoring-efficacy studies use large language models to dynamically generate multiple-choice assessments, because controlling content across conditions with fixed tests effectively forces expert tutors into a curriculum script. The article argues that "If effective learning assessments can be dynamically generated by AI, then future studies could study both human tutors who were experts in the domain but not in tutoring and expert human tutors in their natural contexts, across topics." This would allow studying expert tutors without handicapping their dynamic instruction.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- AI-generated questions with quality and psychometric properties comparable to human-authored questions on the same topic
#### Constraints
- The article cites recent work showing LLM-generated questions have comparable quality to human-authored ones, but this was not tested in the present study

### Target Learners
- K-12 and higher-education students in tutoring studies

### Target Learning Goals
- measuring learning gains from dynamic, adaptive tutoring without constraining tutor instruction
