# Include student solutions in explicit instruction that are actually typical for the taught sample

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors' second design recommendation is that "the explicit instruction phase should include student solutions that really are typical." Only an alignment between the products of the problem-solving phase and the subsequent explicit instruction allows refuting misconceptions and integrating newly introduced knowledge components. In this study, the refuted solutions (evidence only, averaging) were typical in general Bayesian-reasoning research but rarely generated by this sample, so instruction missed students' actual errors such as missing normalization.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of the solution attempts learners actually generate during the problem-solving phase, e.g., from prior studies or piloting
#### Constraints
- Solutions typical in the general literature on a topic may not be typical for a specific sample or representation

### Target Learners
- undergraduate teacher students learning Bayesian reasoning

### Target Learning Goals
- integrating newly introduced knowledge components with learners' existing and erroneous ideas
