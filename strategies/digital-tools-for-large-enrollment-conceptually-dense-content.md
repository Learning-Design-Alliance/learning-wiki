# Integrate interactive digital tools into large-enrollment courses to teach conceptually demanding content where lab access is limited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating technology-enhanced, simulation-based approaches into cytogenetics education "particularly in contexts where traditional laboratory access is limited", and suggest the approach "offers a flexible model that may be extended to other areas of medical and health sciences education requiring visual interpretation and diagnostic reasoning". They also call for future research on long-term outcomes and objective performance measures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Asynchronous delivery through a virtual learning platform
- Structured online activities aligned with curricular learning outcomes
#### Constraints
- Findings rest on self-reported perceptions; the authors recommend controlled or randomized designs to strengthen the evidence base

### Target Learners
- large cohorts of undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- complex scientific domain knowledge
- diagnostic reasoning
