# Use conceptual meta-analysis by interdisciplinary teams to generate discipline-specific learning goals for literacy instruction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes a design methodology in which interdisciplinary and professionally diverse teams conduct a conceptual meta-analysis of extant theoretical and empirical work in each discipline. Teams consist of university-based researchers, curriculum and professional development designers, disciplinary experts, and collaborating classroom teachers. Each team carries out a literature search and critical review, meets regularly over months, and consults with disciplinary experts to resolve conundrums. The output is discipline-specific clusters of knowledge types organized into core construct categories that define learning goals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teams must include expertise in the disciplinary content area, the teaching of that discipline, and research on text-based learning in that discipline
#### Constraints
- The methodology is for the creation of learning goals of instruction, not for the creation of learning environments as in design-based research

### Target Learners
- Adolescent students in grades 6-12

### Target Learning Goals
- Defining what students need to know for disciplinary literacy
