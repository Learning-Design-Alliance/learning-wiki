Adopt appropriate mathematical games in junior secondary mathematics teaching¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that games significantly enhanced achievement in algebraic expressions, the authors recommend that junior secondary mathematics teachers adopt appropriate mathematical games, that textbook authors embed games in textbooks, and that the curriculum be reviewed to incorporate games. They write: "Mathematics teachers at the junior secondary school level should adopt appropriate mathematical games in the teaching of mathematics concepts", citing the facilitating and retentive abilities of games. The conclusion adds that games should be used "in conjunction with other appropriately selected teaching methods".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Games must be appropriate to the mathematical concepts being taught

Constraints¶

  • Evidence base is a single quasi-experimental study in one Nigerian local government area

Target Learners¶

  • Junior secondary school students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Algebraic expressions
  • Mathematics concepts generally

Claims¶

  • Junior secondary students taught algebraic expressions with card games and algebra tic-tacmatics achieved significantly higher adjusted post-test scores than students taught by conventional lecture method [+M]
  • Male students taught algebraic expressions with games achieved significantly higher adjusted scores than female students [~M]
  • The effect of games-based instruction on algebraic expressions achievement interacted significantly with gender, favoring male students [~M]
  • Both male and female students gained from games-based instruction, though the gain was higher for males [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Adopt cooperative TGT as an alternative mathematics teaching approach, supported by teacher training and ministry guidelines
