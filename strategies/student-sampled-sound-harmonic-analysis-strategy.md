# Have students sample sounds from their own instruments, voices, and resonant objects and analyze them spectrally in Audacity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The paper recommends steering the activity toward students' own sound sources: students record tones from their musical instruments, simple resonant objects like a wine glass or cymbal, or a volunteered sung tone, then use Plot Spectrum or the spectrogram mode to perform harmonic analysis. Extensions include demonstrating beats with two frequency sources offset by 1-3 Hz, comparing voice and instrument timbres, and showing spectral signatures of coins and brass cartridge cases.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Audacity installed on a computer with a built-in microphone; sound sources such as instruments, voices, or resonant objects
#### Constraints
- Obtaining the Fourier series of Bessel functions from a drum head is described as tricky, requiring skill in tuning drum heads for even tension

### Target Learners
- students in introductory or advanced courses introducing Fourier series and analysis

### Target Learning Goals
- connecting Fourier series terms to audible and visible frequency components of real sounds
