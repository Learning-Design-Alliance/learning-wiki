# Structure VRS sessions as group scenario drills followed by peer discussion and instructor-led debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's VRS session recipe combines collaborative drills with structured reflection: the lesson "began with an introduction to VR system operation (5-10 minutes), followed by group VRS scenario drills (30-40 minutes)", after which each student executed their part, received system feedback, discussed the session with group members, and completed a 10-minute instructor-led debriefing with questions and reflections before repeating the drills. This sequence is intended to help students consolidate learning and transform it into meaningful learning outcomes.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- an instructor-led debriefing session after the drills
- prerecorded VR videos and system-operation instructions before class
- technical support staff available during the session
#### Constraints
- with large control-group classes the debriefing was postponed a week; the authors recommend future work examine debriefing timing effects

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing students in infection control training

### Target Learning Goals
- consolidating infection control skills and knowledge through feedback and reflection
