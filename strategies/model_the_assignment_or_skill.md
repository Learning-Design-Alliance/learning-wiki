# Model the Assignment or Skill

## Description
Teachers demonstrate how to perform a task or skill by verbalizing their thought processes while completing it. This helps learners understand the strategies involved and supports auditory processing. For example, a teacher might model annotating a text or writing a paragraph, sharing their internal dialogue and decision-making process.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a teacher or experienced learner to demonstrate the task while verbalizing their thoughts. Edtech tools can enhance this strategy by providing interactive models and recording features.
#### Constraints
- Learners may passively observe the model without actively engaging in the thinking process. Models need to be clear and well-articulated to be effective.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Applicable to all learner levels, especially beneficial for those who benefit from auditory learning and explicit demonstrations.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Demonstrate effective strategies, increase awareness of thought processes, support auditory processing, and provide a model for learners to follow.

### Affordances
- Provides a clear, step-by-step example of how to approach a task, making it easier for learners to understand and replicate the process. Supports metacognition by making thinking visible. Allows learners to revisit the model as needed.

### Personalization
- Models can be tailored to different skill levels and tasks. Teachers can create models with varying complexity. Edtech tools can allow learners to create and share their own models, fostering personalized learning.
