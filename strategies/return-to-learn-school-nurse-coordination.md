# Implement an early return-to-learn protocol with the school nurse as point-person coordinating health care teams and classroom accommodations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that schools implement early return-to-learn protocols after pediatric mTBI, with the school nurse acting as in-school coordinator and mediator between the community medical provider and teachers. It argues "accommodations are best delivered if feasible and practical", citing extra time for assignments and increased breaks as examples that let students recover without disturbing classroom workflow. Risk factors identified in the study (c-mTBI, incomplete recovery) should guide which children receive the protocol.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Written communication from health care providers to guide return-to-learn recommendations, which school professionals request
#### Constraints
- Benefits of early return-to-learn are established for uncomplicated concussion; unclear whether the same benefits extend to c-mTBI, though the authors do not disagree with a similar approach assuming proper support

### Target Learners
- school-aged children (ages 5-18) returning to school after mild traumatic brain injury

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining academic performance during post-injury recovery
