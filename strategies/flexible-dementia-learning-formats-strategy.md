# Use innovative, flexible dementia-learning formats (short app-based modules, interactive e-learning, monthly workshops, tele-mentoring) instead of conventional formal training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend reevaluating traditional training in favor of flexible formats: "Innovative training can include short app-based modules and interactive e-learning sessions that fit into daily routines, allowing for continuous learning without disrupting workflows." Monthly workshops or tele-mentoring sessions with case-based discussions and expert feedback, plus professional-organization partnerships and continuing-education-credit incentives, are proposed to raise engagement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formats must fit into daily routines without disrupting workflows; incentives such as continuing education credits are suggested to encourage participation.
#### Constraints
- The article reports this as a proposal informed by its finding that formal training was not significantly associated with knowledge; its impact on knowledge improvement is left to future research.

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals (medicine, nursing, pharmacy, allied health) in hospital and primary care settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Alzheimer's disease knowledge: life impact, risk factors, symptoms, treatment, assessment, caregiving, disease course
