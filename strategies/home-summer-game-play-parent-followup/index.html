Home-based summer game play with parent notification and periodic follow-up¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The study implemented game-based practice outside school hours: participants "played the video games with their personal PC at home" during the summer between pretest and posttest. Parents were informed of the research objectives face-to-face or by phone, and the researchers followed up "at specified intervals during the summer" giving advice to students and parents, which the article credits with producing no participant dropout.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Parental awareness and cooperation, secured by face-to-face or phone contact before the research
  • A follow-up schedule at specified intervals during the summer

Constraints¶

  • School computer-site capacity was insufficient, so home PCs were the primary delivery channel

Target Learners¶

  • High school English learners in a summer break period

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Retention of English language concepts over the summer break

Claims¶

  • English recall posttest scores differed significantly across three groups (video games with English language concepts, games without them, and no games) after controlling for pretest scores; the English-concept games group scored highest, but no pairwise comparisons were reported [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Implement TGT game-based cooperative learning in after-school programs with adequately trained teachers and extended treatment duration
