# Website Domain Analysis

## Description
Learners are taught the differences between .com, .gov, .org, and .edu domains to assess the reliability of websites. This can be taught as a mini-lesson where students learn the typical purpose and potential biases of each domain type.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a variety of websites with different domain types and a framework for guiding the analysis (e.g., a checklist or rubric).
#### Constraints
- Domain analysis is just one factor to consider when evaluating the reliability of a website. Learners should also be taught to consider other factors, such as the author's credentials, the currency of the information, and the presence of bias.

### Affordances
- Provides learners with a simple and practical framework for assessing the trustworthiness of websites, supports the development of critical literacy skills, and promotes informed decision-making in online environments.

### Personalization
- The depth of the analysis can be adjusted to meet the needs of diverse learners. Scaffolding can be provided to learners who are less familiar with website domains and online source evaluation.
