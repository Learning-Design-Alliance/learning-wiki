# Introduce global health through e-learning in conjunction with face-to-face small group teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 3 studies (2 causal, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends using e-learning modules as a supplement and primer rather than a replacement for in-person teaching. Students "were also in favour of a “combination of teaching methods”", valuing small group discussions, and the authors conclude the program "will likely be best introduced and utilized in conjunction with other face to face teaching, such as small group sessions with a global health expert". Where no global health expert is available, it could provide a primer for the learner.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- E-learning content integrated with opportunities for face-to-face interaction, ideally small group sessions with a global health expert
- A resource person students could contact with questions was suggested to overcome lack of interactivity
#### Constraints
- Standalone e-learning was perceived as structured and linear, limiting opportunities to ask for clarification

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students before global health field work

### Target Learning Goals
- introductory global health conceptual knowledge
- preparation for cross-cultural clinical encounters
