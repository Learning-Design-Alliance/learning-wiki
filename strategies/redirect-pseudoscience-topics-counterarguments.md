# Redirect pseudo-scientific or already-covered project topics toward new material or scientific counterarguments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
When students propose topics already covered in class or promoting pseudo-science (astrology, the Maya Doomsday hype), the instructor works with them to find uncovered angles or requires scientific counterarguments alongside the topic discussion. The author reports that for astrology "that usually works well," but for the Maya Doomsday scenario "the students did not heed my advice at all," and the video scored low on science and scientific accuracy while still winning the People's Choice Award.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor approval of topics before production
- Willingness to negotiate topic scope with students
#### Constraints
- The redirect does not always work: one doomsday video was full of propaganda and myth and scored low on science and scientific accuracy

### Target Learners
- undergraduate non-science majors in introductory Astronomy

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguishing science from pseudo-science
- investigating new material rather than repeating class content
