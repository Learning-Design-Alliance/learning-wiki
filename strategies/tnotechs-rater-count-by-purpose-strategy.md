# Use one T-NOTECHS rater for routine quality assessment and three raters for research or quality improvement analyses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors translate their reliability findings into a practical rater-count recommendation: for routine quality audits a single rater is sufficient because the overall T-NOTECHS score is reliable even from one assessor (aICC 0.84), while analyses that depend on domain-level scores benefit from averaging three raters, which raised all domain ICCs to excellent. "For daily practice, one rater to assess non-technical skills using the T-NOTECHS seems legitimate as part of quality assessment, while the overall score is a reliable value." For research or quality improvement, they suggest three-rater assessment when low overall scores are correlated with factors such as trauma mechanism, injury severity, or team experience.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessors should be trained in T-NOTECHS use; the study's assessors had 1 year of video-analysis experience plus a 2 h training session
#### Constraints
- Routine assessment during resuscitation is time consuming and, in the authors' opinion, not feasible for experienced clinicians, making assessment by other personnel more practical

### Target Learners
- trauma teams at trauma centers

### Target Learning Goals
- reliable assessment of trauma-team non-technical skills for quality audits and training evaluation
