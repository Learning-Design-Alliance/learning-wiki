# Calibrate identity salience to instructional goals rather than maximizing self-similarity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (2 for, 2 mixed, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the digital self boosted experiential engagement while lowering immediate retention, the authors conclude that "identity salience should be calibrated to instructional goals rather than maximized". They propose three design strategies: an Adaptation Phase that gradually introduces the digital self to reduce novelty, Selective Presence that emphasizes the agent at key moments while minimizing its presence during instruction, and Stylized Abstraction using non-photorealistic representations to reduce uncanniness while retaining identifiable features.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A design goal of helping learners enter a narrative and form emotional or imaginal connections with historically distant content.
#### Constraints
- The authors note the approach may extend to other high self-distance contexts such as cross-cultural learning or social-issue documentaries, though further research is needed; immediate learning performance may be undermined.

### Target Learners
- Learners of history or other temporally or culturally distant content

### Target Learning Goals
- Balancing immersive self-representation with pedagogical effectiveness in personalized learning environments
