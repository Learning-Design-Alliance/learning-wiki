# Use varied face-to-face teaching strategies matched to learning objectives to sustain engagement in a full-day course

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The course was delivered face-to-face rather than online or through recorded videos to allow personalized attention and enable instructors to modify teaching style based on learners' needs. The authors selected teaching methods based on objectives, content, and available resources, using visual/multimedia aids, interactive questions between slides, discussion, game-based learning, case scenarios, and role play. They report that "The most effective teaching strategy in the course was the use of case scenarios and facilitated discussions, which appeared to resonate with nurses by allowing them to relate to real clinical experiences while reducing the cognitive load associated with abstract concepts."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pre-course reading materials shared 2 weeks before the scheduled date
- Carefully selected course instructors with relevant specialty expertise
#### Constraints
- The course lasted 8 hours, and many nurses reported the content was dense and suggested delivering it over 2 days

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses at a tertiary healthcare institution

### Target Learning Goals
- Antimicrobial stewardship knowledge and clinical practice
