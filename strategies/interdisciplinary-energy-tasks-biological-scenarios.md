# Use interdisciplinary problem-solving tasks that apply physical energy models to biological scenarios

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (6 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (3 qualitative, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The thread's tasks place physical energy reasoning in biological contexts: a protein-folding task using optical tweezers, Energy Skate Park homework on bound states, a Gauss gun as a mechanical analog of an exothermic reaction, an ATP hydrolysis/myosin homework problem, a temperature-regulation task, a kinesin energy-bar-chart task, and a culminating ATP 'high-energy bond' reconciliation essay. As the authors put it, "The protein folding task asks students to reason about the relationship between force, work, and energy in the context of using optical tweezers to unfold proteins." Tasks are open-ended, ask students to define their own systems, and prompt reconciliation of disciplinary ideas.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks must be sequenced to build connections across the course rather than appear as one-off activities
- Students need prior familiarity with reaction coordinate diagrams from chemistry and biology
#### Constraints
- The multiple-well toy model has limitations the authors recognize and encourage students to explore; its independent variable does not correspond to a single physical parameter

### Target Learners
- life sciences majors in introductory physics

### Target Learning Goals
- coordinate energy-as-stability with force/work/energy frameworks; reconcile 'high-energy bond' language with potential-energy bond models
