# Withhold species identities until activities are complete to reduce intellectual resistance to human evolution

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The exercise presents species as anonymous codes (%1–%5) and reveals—only after all analyses are done—that one species is Homo sapiens. The authors write: "In an effort to reduce intellectual resistance to the topic, we elect not to reveal the identity of the species until all activities are complete." This lets students who resist human evolution work freely on the data before the reveal, which then connects the fused chromosome finding to human origins.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Datasets coded anonymously in student materials
- A postactivity reveal moment (e.g., breaking the code that species %4 is Homo sapiens)
#### Constraints
- The authors state this aspect of the exercise is by no means required

### Target Learners
- students resistant to evolutionary theory, including those with religious objections to human evolution

### Target Learning Goals
- acceptance of human evolution from primate ancestors
- engagement with phylogenetic analysis
