# Use multilevel modeling rather than difference scores to analyze switch costs with continuous predictors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article advocates multilevel modeling over traditional subtraction-based difference scores for switch-cost analysis, noting it does not require "homoscedasticity (homogeneity of variances), compound symmetry, and sphericity" and permits "testing of interactions between discrete and continuous predictors." The authors modeled cross-level interactions between trial-type contrasts (within-subjects) and standardized inhibition or working-memory scores (between-subjects), trimming non-significant three-way interactions. This approach allowed direct tests of whether individual differences in executive skills moderated cost magnitudes while controlling perseveration.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Repeated-measures experimental data with trial-level observations nested within children
- Standardized predictor scores so effect magnitudes are directly comparable
#### Constraints
- As a class of the linear model, multilevel modeling retains some distributional assumptions

### Target Learners
- preschool children in repeated-measures executive-task batteries

### Target Learning Goals
- quantifying how individual executive skills relate to flexibility components
