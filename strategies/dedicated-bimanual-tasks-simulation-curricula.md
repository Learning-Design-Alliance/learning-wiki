# Incorporate dedicated bimanual control tasks in simulated surgical curricula to reduce laterality in trainees

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that simulation-based orthopaedic training include tasks specifically requiring bimanual control — camera operation with one hand and grasping with the other — so that trainees reduce hand laterality early. It states that "Dedicated bimanual control tasks to reduce laterality in trainees should be incorporated in simulated surgical curricula", and that future curricula require "integrated bimanual-dedicated modules to mirror real-world practice".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulator task requiring bimanual control, such as the remove-the-stars module with camera control and grasper operation per hand
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on observed laterality in novice and intermediate groups; laterality in experts may not be detectable by current simulator software
- Disproportionate gender representation limits generalisability of the underlying findings

### Target Learners
- Orthopaedic residents and trainees
- Medical students on surgical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- Bimanual psychomotor control in arthroscopic surgery
- Reduction of hand laterality in surgical performance
