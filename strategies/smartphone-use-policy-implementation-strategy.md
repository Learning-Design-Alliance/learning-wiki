# University administrators and policymakers should implement a smartphone use policy for learning, teaching, and campus residence

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding of high nomophobia prevalence and its significant association with academic achievement, the authors recommend that academic administrators and policymakers lead the implementation of a smartphone use policy covering learning and teaching and students' stay on campus. They further suggest awareness campaigns and therapeutic strategies for at-risk subpopulations, and follow-up studies in Ghanaian universities to monitor nomophobia and its associates. The article states policymakers "should be the front liners to ensure effective implementation of smartphone use policy for learning, teaching, and during students’ stay on campus" — wait, quoting exactly: "Academic administrators and policymakers in the university should be the front liners to ensure effective implementation of smartphone use policy for learning and teaching, and during students’ stay on campus."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional policymakers and administrators positioned to implement and enforce the policy
#### Constraints
- The study was conducted at a single public university in Ghana using convenience sampling, which the authors state limits generalisation to private universities and other socio-economic communities

### Target Learners
- higher education (university) students in Ghana

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing adverse effects of habitual smartphone use on academic success
