# Use sequential adaptive e-learning plus simulator training to establish novel minimally invasive surgical techniques

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a blended format in which an adaptive e-learning module delivers the theoretical background before a short hands-on simulator course, so course time can "focus explicitly on practical exercises within the course timespan". The authors state blended courses "might be utilized not only for the implementation of UBE but also for many other MIS techniques in the educational and clinical setting", and highlight access benefits for trainees from low and lower-middle-income countries.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adaptive e-learning platform and learning-engineering support
- Realistic simulator models and instructor capacity
#### Constraints
- The authors state a further comparison analysis with conventional courses, including long-lasting training effects, might be necessary to justify permanent implementation

### Target Learners
- residents and junior attendings learning novel MIS techniques
- trainees from low and lower-middle-income countries

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition of novel minimally invasive surgical techniques
- shortening the learning curve for MIS procedures
