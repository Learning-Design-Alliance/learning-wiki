# Invite students to feel physics in their bodies through guided everyday-movement activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A set of classroom activities in which students experience physics concepts somatically — the article says such activities 'can engage students to experience the physics they are learning in their bodies in ways that can also help them reduce unnecessary stress'. Activities cover Newton's third law, pendulum-like arm swinging, centre of mass, and air pressure, and are 'flexibly modified so that everyone can participate', including people with mobility differences.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators guide students through physical activities such as pressing hands against a wall and shifting weight, then invite explanation
#### Constraints
- The article presents them as adaptable examples, stating 'These are but a few examples'

### Target Learners
- high-school physics students
- first-year university physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- Newton's third law and mutual interaction
- centre of mass
- air pressure
