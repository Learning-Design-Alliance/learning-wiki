# Give specific, timely, supportive feedback and use it to promote reflection on attitude barriers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For attitude barriers, the article recommends feedback as "a useful strategy for addressing inappropriate stereotyping or bias." Feedback "should be specific, focused, objective, and offered in a timely manner," delivered in a direct yet supportive manner, and teachers may benefit from a brief time out to organize their own thoughts before giving it. Feedback is especially powerful for promoting reflection when it is "corrective and discordant with the learner's self-perception," and probes such as "tell me more" encourage deeper reflection.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A diagnosed attitude barrier; a teacher able to deliver feedback calmly and supportively in an emotionally charged situation
#### Constraints
- The article cautions it is often neither possible nor necessarily desirable to establish the role of bias in a learner's remark; feedback targets the cognitive shortcut rather than assigning blame

### Target Learners
- Medical students
- Residents

### Target Learning Goals
- Recognizing cognitive shortcuts and stereotypes in clinical reasoning and reflecting on their impact on patient care
