# Use old hardware projects as learning tools: have students retrofit and test past designs to pinpoint open-source barriers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy, drawn from student exit-survey feedback, has learners work retroactively on an existing documented design to expose exactly where open-source usability breaks down (obsolete parts, legacy software, ambiguous documentation). One student is quoted: "I would consider using old projects because I see how looking retroactively allowed us to pinpoint exactly what problems to target when it comes to making the project open source." The article's conclusion encourages educators and creators to keep assessing past designs in contemporary settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to extensive, publicly available documentation of the original design and test procedures
- Mentor support, such as a physics teacher with relevant experience or as-needed access to an original engineer
#### Constraints
- The exercise prioritized qualitative feedback because each team comprised only six students
- Students did not manufacture hardware; they obtained quotations only

### Target Learners
- high school students
- undergraduate students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding open-source hardware practices
- evaluating documentation longevity and reproducibility

## Claims

- [Manufacturing quotes for the decade-old Leaf board came in significantly higher than the historical 2017 price for most vendors](../claims/leaf-board-quotes-higher-than-historical.md) [+W]
