# Reduce cheating holistically by enhancing perceived assessment purpose, fostering self-efficacy, and increasing perceived costs of cheating

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that "efforts to reduce cheating should focus on enhancing the perceived purpose of assessments, fostering self-efficacy in a broad sense, and increasing the perceived costs of cheating". It insists all three areas are needed together: any approach focusing solely on one or two will likely fail. The strategy is grounded in the planned-behaviour framing and is linked to Dawson et al.'s (2024) validity framework, which aligns assessments with intrinsic motivation and self-efficacy through authentic, real-world tasks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- All three focus areas must be addressed together, since partial approaches are expected to fail
- A holistic and adaptive model considering the dynamic nature of planned behaviour
#### Constraints
- The article notes promoting self-efficacy is difficult in practice because the hidden curriculum of individualism may inadvertently promote cheating as a shortcut

### Target Learners
- university students

### Target Learning Goals
- academic integrity
- intrinsic motivation for learning
- ethical engagement with assessment
