# Withhold peer-comparison feedback until a significant percentage of the group has achieved high performance, to accommodate social pressure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes that when social pressure is a salient barrier, peer-comparison feedback can be counterproductive: when group performance is low, an upper-percentile benchmark "may be undermined by individuals' awareness of the performance of the group." It recommends that "social pressure could be accommodated by withholding comparative feedback until a significant percentage of the group had achieved a high level of performance." This is a theory-informed design recommendation for feedback timing, not a tested manipulation in this article.
