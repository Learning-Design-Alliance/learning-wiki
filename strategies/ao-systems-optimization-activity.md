# Systems optimization activity varying aberrations, speed and software parameters

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
For deeper investigation, students introduce various aberrations and time dependencies and vary software parameters such as gain and aggressiveness, which apply corrections to the DM from 10 to 60 times per second. In the new UHMC engineering technology BAS courses, a rotating aberrator with variable speed control was introduced, and "A detector has been placed at a re-imaged science camera focus to allow students to analyze digital data to quantify the quality of the image (Strehl ratio) and to measure system performance as a function of phase screen rotational velocity (in analogy to the wind speed)." This larger activity introduces data analysis, image calibration and processing, and systems optimization.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Software and hardware control of optical components; a detector at a re-imaged science camera focus; a rotating aberrator with variable speed control
#### Constraints
- The two DM types strongly influence which activities are possible: drum-head A-Optics DMs correct defocus well but cannot take complicated shapes easily, while segmented IrisAO DMs can take complicated shapes rapidly

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in engineering technology courses
- summer-school participants

### Target Learning Goals
- system performance characterization
- data analysis and image calibration
- relationship between correction quality and aberration dynamics
