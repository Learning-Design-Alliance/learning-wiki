# Communicate course structure and expectations early to support students' self-efficacy at course start

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size

## Description
The article recommends that, because initial self-efficacy predicts early task interest and first-test performance but standard socio-cognitive supports (mastery or vicarious experiences) take time, instructors can instead support perceived control through clear communication. As the article puts it, "This communication about the course structure and expectations helps students understand the demands of the course and how to work towards them, potentially providing a path to enhancing their self-efficacy for the course of study right away." This is offered for introductory courses where students arrive with low prior knowledge and varied interest.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructor communication of expectations, fundamental goals and course organisation at the beginning of the course
#### Constraints
- Suggested for compulsory introductory courses where instructors do not yet know students and standard self-efficacy supports are impractical

### Target Learners
- university students in introductory courses with low prior knowledge and self-efficacy

### Target Learning Goals
- early engagement and self-efficacy for course content
