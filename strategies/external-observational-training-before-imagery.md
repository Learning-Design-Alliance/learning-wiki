# Use external observational training before mental practice of an unfamiliar procedure

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that when a surgeon is not familiar with a procedure and its flow, external observational training should be performed first, through "observation of recordings from trusted sources (i.e., VuMedi, clips from industrial educational websites, YouTube with well-known surgeons performing, etc.), surgical technique guides, and textbooks." A progression is suggested: after watching a recording, the surgeon listens to its audio component while visualizing the procedure rather than watching it.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mental practice is only useful for procedures the subject is already familiar with; observation must come first for unfamiliar procedures
#### Constraints
- Intended for periods when the surgeon cannot perform procedures or access simulators

### Target Learners
- novice surgeons learning unfamiliar procedures

### Target Learning Goals
- building a procedural mental model sufficient to support imagery-based practice
