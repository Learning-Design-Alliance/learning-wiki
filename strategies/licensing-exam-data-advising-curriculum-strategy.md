# Use licensing-exam performance data to advise students and inform curriculum design for matching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that institutional match-outcome data be used to guide student advising and curricular planning. The authors state that "These results may help faculty prepare students and inform curriculum design to improve matching" and that results "can help faculty better advise students, inform curricular design to optimize matching, as well as understand student risk factors for not matching." The strategy rests on identifying which exams and scores drive first-choice and competitive-specialty matching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional collection of pre- and post-admission academic performance data and self-reported match outcomes (as in the study's mandatory graduation survey with 100% response rate)
#### Constraints
- Findings generalize only to institutions in the United States, due to differences in postgraduate medical education entry processes in other countries

### Target Learners
- osteopathic medical students preparing for residency applications

### Target Learning Goals
- successful matching into first-choice residency and desired specialty
