# Convert the bed-of-nails pressure demonstration into a magic trick, then extend it with a deceptive walking-on-nails follow-up that teaches critical thinking

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The instructor stages an occult-levitation performance around lying on a bed of nails. "Dropping the apple makes explicit the preconception that anything in contact with the nails will be perforated", building tension before the instructor lies down unharmed and the inquiry cycle into pressure as force over area begins. A second act has the instructor appear to walk barefoot on the nails using hidden cardboard insoles, ending "with a suggestion that critical thinking requires one to go beyond appearances".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The preconception that anything contacting the nails will be perforated must be made explicit, e.g. by dropping an apple onto the bed before the trick
#### Constraints
- The walking follow-up relies on deliberate deception (hidden insoles), which the instructor reveals to confirm the impossibility of the feat

### Target Learners
- high school and college physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding pressure as the ratio of force over surface area; critical thinking
