Use a three-circle action research sequence — KWL, then SQ3R, then Collaboration-Discussion-Query — for primary school reading intervention¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article proposes a follow-up action research plan containing three circles for primary school reading work. The first circle uses the relatively simple KWL strategy ("What I Know?" (K), "What I Want To Know?" (W), "What I Leaned?" (L)), the second uses the SQ3R reading comprehension strategy, and the third uses Collaboration-Discussion-Query (CDQ). According to the authors, the first circle provides more working time on texts, the second improves surveying, researching, reading and note-taking skills, and the third improves group working, discussion and query skills.

Claims¶

  • During the SQ3R intervention, students' words read per minute and word recognition percentages improved, with only four students reaching the 120-words-per-minute benchmark [+W]
  • A 10-week SQ3R-based reading program increased reading comprehension test scores of all seven participating 4th graders with comprehension difficulties [+W]
  • Students showed uneven success across SQ3R stages: Survey succeeded for five of seven, the Question stage fell below expectations, and the Recite stage was hardest [~W]
  • Students expressed positive opinions about the SQ3R intervention, while reporting that summarizing was difficult [+W]
