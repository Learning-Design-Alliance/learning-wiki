# Improve SBL rubric reliability through rater calibration, detailed domain-level checklists, and formative assessment with a single rater

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends concrete tactics for raising the reliability of simulation-based rubric assessment: rater training with "regular calibration exercises and providing feedback"; integrating "a checklist within each evaluated domain" because detailed scoring checklists have shown good reliability; adding formative evaluation, for which "a Phi-coefficient of 0.70 is considered acceptable", so "only one rater is needed"; and considering self- and peer assessment through multisource feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Raters must be adequately trained on how to utilize the assessment form and apply the criteria consistently
#### Constraints
- Shortening scenarios and making content more generic, suggested by prior anesthesiology SBL work, should be considered with caution for pre-clinical students who are relatively new to the ACLS context

### Target Learners
- Pre-clinical medical students assessed in simulation-based ACLS courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Reliable and valid evaluation of ACLS skills across cognitive, psychomotor, and affective domains
