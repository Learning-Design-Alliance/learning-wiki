# Use an advocacy-inquiry approach when debriefing unfamiliar simulation content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend an advocacy-inquiry approach to debriefing this simulation because the material is unfamiliar to most learners. They state that "The advocacy-inquiry approach assists educators in determining the learner's reasoning for his or her actions, which can help identify any knowledge gaps that need to be addressed." Debriefing was led jointly by a simulation specialist with expertise in debriefing techniques and psychological safety and a content expert in cardiac devices.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitators familiar with advocacy-inquiry debriefing; the authors recommend pairing a debriefing-expert facilitator with a content expert
#### Constraints
- The authors note a number of debriefing techniques could be used successfully for this scenario; advocacy-inquiry is a recommendation, not a requirement

### Target Learners
- Emergency medicine residents of all levels of training

### Target Learning Goals
- Reasoning about defibrillator malfunction management; surfacing and addressing knowledge gaps
