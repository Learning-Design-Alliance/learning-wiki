# Graduate the AI policy across the curriculum so that permitted AI use tracks each course's pedagogical purpose

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The strategy sets AI permissions course by course rather than adopting a single program-wide policy, because "the pedagogical purpose of a course determines what AI use serves or subverts it." In the introductory programming course AI use is barred outright; in the intermediate data-structures course it is encouraged on projects but barred from in-class exercises and pen-and-paper exams; in the upper-division systems course it is a study aid but not a substitute for working problems; and in the AI course itself it is required. One boundary holds wherever AI is allowed: the model may write code, but reflective writing must be the student's own voice.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The instructor must teach across the sequence and be able to key the policy to each course's pedagogical purpose
- A boundary on reflective writing must hold at every level where AI is allowed
#### Constraints
- The author states a single AI policy across a degree program is the wrong instrument; no outcome evidence is offered for the graduated policy itself

### Target Learners
- undergraduate computer science students across a degree sequence

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational programming skills built by hand
- individual competence certification
- AI-supported production work
