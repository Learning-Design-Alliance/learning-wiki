# Tiered ECE implementation: virtual and case-based learning first, then simulated patient interaction, then supervised real patient contact

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 quant-synthesis), `q3`–`q4` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend a sequenced implementation model: "It is recommended to actively utilise digital and AI technologies early on, building foundational knowledge and decision-making skills through virtual simulations and case-based learning; then enhancing procedural proficiency through structured simulated patient interactions; and finally progressing to supervised, incremental patient contact in real clinical settings." They state this tiered approach manages patient safety, alleviates student anxiety and addresses resource constraints, and suggest combining modalities to reduce costs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- ECE content should align with students' accumulation of theoretical knowledge
- real clinical settings demand carefully structured supervision balancing educational objectives, student stress and patient safety
#### Constraints
- Simulation-based approaches frequently require costly equipment or well-trained standardised volunteers; high costs, logistical complexity and uncertain short-term outcomes limit widespread adoption

### Target Learners
- preclinical dental students across academic years

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical skills, clinical reasoning, professional identity and self-efficacy development

### Affordances
- Ece Three Type Taxonomy
