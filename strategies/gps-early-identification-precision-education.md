# Use genetic prediction early to identify children likely to struggle and deliver early, individualized prevention and learning programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that polygenic scores could serve as an early-warning tool in education, moving toward what they call precision education by analogy with precision medicine. They argue that because "a great strength of GPS is that they can predict at birth just as well as later in life", scores could identify children at genetic risk for reading problems at birth and enable early intervention and individualized programs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Genome-wide polygenic score data for the individual child
- GWA summary statistics from large discovery samples to weight the score
#### Constraints
- Current EduYears GPS predicts only a modest share of variance (about 10% of GCSE variance in this sample)
- DNA-based methods rely on additive effects of common SNPs and do not capture gene-gene or gene-environment interplay

### Target Learners
- school-age children
- children at risk of educational problems

### Target Learning Goals
- early identification of educational difficulties
- individualized prevention and learning programs
