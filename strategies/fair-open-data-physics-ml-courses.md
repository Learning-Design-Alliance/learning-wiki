# Use FAIR-preserved open data and models in DS/ML physics courses so students do publication-quality work with reproducible benchmarks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A strategy for course design at the physics/DS/ML intersection: preserve and use data and AI models according to the FAIR principles (findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable) so they "can be reliably reused by researchers and educators to reproduce benchmark results for both research and pedagogical purposes". The paper reports that the FAIR readiness of the CMS H(bb) dataset supported ML exercises in the UCSD course, and that using real datasets communicates to students that they are doing publication-quality work; course-based undergraduate research experiences can foster a sense of identity and belonging in the field.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Data and models must be preserved in accordance with the FAIR principles to enable reliable reuse, transparent provenance and reproducibility.
#### Constraints
- The authors note the present software landscape may change on the 5–10 year timescale, and that infrastructure should work without detracting from the learning environment.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate and graduate physics and data science students

### Target Learning Goals
- reproducing benchmark ML results on real scientific data
- model interpretability and uncertainty quantification
- developing research identity and belonging in the field
