# Train staff in cognitive-aid use, placement, cueing and content before implementing bedside resuscitation cognitive aids in live clinical settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors argue that implementing cognitive aids in practice requires deliberate implementation strategies and training. They state that "training in the use of the handbook will be crucial for widespread adoption", including the need for a 'Resuscitation Guide Manager' role, training on where the handbook is placed and how clinicians are cued to use it, and training on its contents so staff know which protocols it covers. They also predict that additional group training and practice with the handbook could lower error rates further.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training prior to implementation in live clinical settings
- A designated role such as a 'Resuscitation Guide Manager'
- Prominent placement of the handbook in the resuscitation room for ease of access
#### Constraints
- The article notes no team naturally designated someone to read the handbook aloud during the trial

### Target Learners
- emergency department clinical teams
- nursing staff or human-factors specialists taking coordination roles

### Target Learning Goals
- effective use of cognitive aids during real resuscitation events
- sustained adoption of standardised crisis management practice
