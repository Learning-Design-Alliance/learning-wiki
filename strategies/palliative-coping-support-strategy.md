# Combine active and experiential learning with coping-skill training, reflective practices, and peer support to address the emotional burden of palliative care education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that palliative care curricula complement active and experiential learning with strategies addressing emotional challenges. It specifically names "reflective practices, mindfulness-based stress reduction, and peer support groups" to help students manage emotional demands, alongside mentorship and interdisciplinary collaboration opportunities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A supportive educational environment with facilitated small-group discussion forums for debriefing reactions in a peer setting
#### Constraints
- The article states short-term courses may be insufficient to build true self-efficacy, so support should be longitudinal with repeated structured debriefing sessions

### Target Learners
- fifth-year medical students in clinical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- palliative care knowledge and attitudes
- emotional resilience and self-awareness for end-of-life care
