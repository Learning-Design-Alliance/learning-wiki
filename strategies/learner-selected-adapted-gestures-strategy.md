# Involve learners in selecting or adapting gestures during instruction to ensure cultural meaningfulness

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy addresses the cultural familiarity dimension of gesture-supported vocabulary learning. The article recommends that, where culturally familiar gestures are unavailable or risky, "Another possibility is to involve learners in selecting or adapting gestures during instruction, which may help ensure that the resulting gestures are interpretable and culturally meaningful to them." Learner participation in gesture choice is presented as a way to align instructional gestures with learners' sociocultural repertoires.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors or researchers must allow learners to choose or modify gestures rather than imposing a fixed gesture inventory
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as one possibility for addressing cultural familiarity; it does not report a test of this strategy's effectiveness

### Target Learners
- L2 vocabulary learners from diverse cultural backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- Interpretable, culturally meaningful gesture-word pairings supporting vocabulary retention
