# Emergency medicine and primary care stakeholders should collaborate to share PoCUS knowledge and skills

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that "emergency medicine and primary care stakeholders implement strategies to collaborate and share PoCUS knowledge and skills, as opposed to our traditional siloed approach to healthcare". They argue this could empower clinicians in primary care settings other than EDs to use PoCUS for diagnostic and therapeutic purposes, giving communities easier and more equitable access to care and decentralising aspects of care away from regional and tertiary facilities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Collaboration mechanisms between emergency medicine and primary care stakeholders would need to be established
#### Constraints
- The article presents this as a recommendation; it did not test collaborative knowledge-sharing

### Target Learners
- primary care practitioners
- generalists
- emergency department clinicians

### Target Learning Goals
- diagnostic and therapeutic use of point-of-care ultrasound in primary care settings
