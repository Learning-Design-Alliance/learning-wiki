Future studies of executive function and academic achievement in depressed adolescents should combine performance-based executive measures with questionnaires and interviews on coping, motivation, self-efficacy and perfectionism, and include educational attainment data¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors propose a design recipe for future research: studies should measure well-defined executive skills with performance-based tests while also gathering questionnaire and interview data on psychosocial variables likely related to educational success, and must include data on educational attainment. They write that "a comprehensive study on the relationship between executive function and educational achievement should encompass not only the performance-based measures of very well-defined executive skills but also questionnaires and structured/semi-structured interviews focussing on coping strategies, motivation and self-efficacy and perfectionism".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Studies must include data on educational attainment, which none of the reviewed studies reported

Constraints¶

  • The recommendation targets adolescents with MDD; the authors note long-term engagement in exam preparation is a context where psychosocial variables are especially likely to matter

Target Learners¶

  • adolescents diagnosed with major depressive disorder

Target Learning Goals¶

  • academic performance and educational attainment in the context of executive function

Claims¶

  • Decision-making deficits were not clearly identified in adolescents with MDD, though poor risk adjustment appeared in one task but not another [+W]
  • The relationship between educational attainment and problem-solving and inhibitory control in adolescents with MDD cannot be established from existing literature [+W]
  • Inhibitory control results in adolescents with MDD are inconsistent across studies, with only one of three tests showing worse performance in one study [+W]
  • No planning deficits were found in adolescents with MDD in either of the two studies assessing planning [+W]
