Organise probability instruction in small supportive groups of two to four students working toward shared goals¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Student-based learning is implemented as small groups of two to four members who work together, being supportive, encouraging, helpful and assisting each other, exchanging information and strategies among themselves and with the teacher. The article states "Student based strategy used groups of two to four me mbers. Students then worked together being supportive, encouraging and exchanging information and strategies among themselves and with the teacher." Both groups received two hours of instruction over six weeks, so the strategy replaces individual receptive work without changing instructional time.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Group tasks must be structured so members experience shared outcomes and positive interdependence, with individual and group accountability

Constraints¶

  • The study did not consider changes in incentives and task structure brought about by certain forms of student based learning, and teacher quality was not taken into consideration

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school mathematics students (Form three)

Target Learning Goals¶

  • probability achievement
  • positive attitudes toward probability
  • social and communication skills

Affordances¶

  • Constructivist Learning Theory Papert Piaget

Claims¶

  • Student-based instruction enhanced students' attitudes toward probability relative to teacher-based instruction [+W]
  • Students viewed probability as important but about half lacked perseverance to work long on probability problems [~W]
  • Most students found probability difficult, mainly because of difficulty grasping its fundamental concepts [~W]
