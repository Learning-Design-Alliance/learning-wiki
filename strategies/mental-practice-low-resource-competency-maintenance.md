# Use mental practice as a low-resource adjunct for maintaining procedural competency when clinical opportunities are scarce

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that faculty deliberately use mental practice — mental rehearsal of procedures through imagery without physical movement — to maintain procedural competency when bedside opportunities decline. MP requires no equipment, "can be used at the patient's bedside or in the classroom", and its "low-resource nature of MP could make it a useful adjunct when considering how to maintain procedural competency". These processes can also be made explicit and shared with junior learners, who could benefit from being shown their usefulness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- no additional ancillary equipment; relies on volitionally controlled mental imagery of the procedure
#### Constraints
- based on a single-center qualitative study of academic PCCM faculty; the article describes MP as a useful adjunct rather than a replacement for direct practice or simulation

### Target Learners
- faculty physicians in procedural specialties
- supervising physicians with supervisory roles
- junior learners introduced to MP

### Target Learning Goals
- maintenance of procedural competency
- readiness and decision-making during procedures

### Affordances
- Motor Imagery Integrative Model
