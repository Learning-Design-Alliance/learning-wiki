# Integrate GTA teaching as part of a broader multi-modality strategy, with hybrid GTA–manikin models, co-produced standards, and longitudinal, patient-inclusive evaluation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends treating GTA programmes as part of a broader educational strategy rather than a replacement for simulation or workplace-based learning, since pelvic examination training appears most effective when multiple modalities are integrated across curricula. It further recommends hybrid models combining GTA and manikin-based teaching, co-production with GTAs to address ethical concerns, and nationally agreed standards covering ethical guidance, GTA safety frameworks and pedagogical rather than anatomical standardisation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programme design guidance including minimum standards for GTA training, support and safeguarding.
#### Constraints
- Evidence on sequencing of hybrid models is mixed: early GTA exposure can enhance confidence, while prior manikin practice may better prepare students for GTA sessions.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students
- medical schools implementing GTA programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- pelvic examination competence
- communication skills
- sustainable and equitable programme implementation

### Affordances
- Gta Four Domain Framework
