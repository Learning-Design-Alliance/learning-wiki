# Bundle recurrent hands-on hypertension training, simplified point-of-care guidelines, and facility chronic care systems (registers, recall, referral protocols) for PHC workers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends a multicomponent capacity-building strategy for PHC workers in security-challenged, low-resource settings: recurrent competency-based in-service training targeting identified gaps, simplified pictorial or wall-chart guidelines in every consultation room, and chronic care supports such as patient registers and recall systems. The authors state that "The implementation of simple quality improvement tools—such as patient registers and recall systems—could help address the critically low follow-up rates." They further recommend future implementation research test bundled interventions combining training, job aids, and supervision.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- State and national policies mandating guideline availability at every PHC facility and regular competency-based training for all cadres
- Structured supportive supervision emphasising practical application of guidelines, follow-up systems, and referral protocols
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness of the bundled interventions in security-constrained settings has not been tested; the authors call for future implementation research to evaluate it

### Target Learners
- Community Health Extension Workers (CHEWs)
- Community Health Officers (CHOs)
- Nurses, midwives, and medical officers in PHC centres

### Target Learning Goals
- Knowledge of hypertension diagnostic thresholds and complications
- Consistent guideline-adherent management practice, follow-up scheduling, and referral
