# Broaden meaningful participation in AIEd system design to equitably serve diverse populations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The chapter recommends involving diverse educational stakeholders — students, teachers, and parents from a range of socio-economic, cultural, and language backgrounds — in co-design processes or hiring more diverse design teams. A constant effort must be made to seek out unrepresented voices and ensure they have "a meaningful impact on AIEd system designs," rather than token participation. The authors argue this acknowledges that stakeholders know their lived experiences well while designers hold scientific knowledge.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Participation must be meaningful, not tokenistic; teams should not hire "token" members of particular demographic groups simply to cross off a checklist
#### Constraints
- The authors note critiques: students and teachers are not scientific experts and broad participation could lower quality (e.g., learning-styles-based design), and designing different technologies for different groups risks stigmatization; they argue disparate treatment is already ubiquitous in education and often necessary

### Target Learners
- Students and teachers from diverse socio-economic, cultural, and linguistic backgrounds

### Target Learning Goals
- Equitable design of AIEd systems that serve diverse populations
