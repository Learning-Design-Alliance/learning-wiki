# Combine needs-based and pre-taught approaches when introducing social skills to cooperative groups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The digest describes two basic approaches to introducing social skills: the teacher observes cooperative groups, determines the specific skills needed, and introduces them on a needs basis; or specific, predetermined social skills are infused into each academic lesson so groups practice them while completing tasks. The authors' position is that both methods are well worth considering and a mixture may suit teachers best: basic skills (calling members by first names, listening, quiet voices) are introduced and illustrated when teachers first start using cooperative groups, and higher-level skills (praising members, respecting opinions, ensuring everyone participates in decisions) are added as groups improve.
