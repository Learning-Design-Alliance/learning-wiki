# Use the RASE framework and NetLogo tool in experiential learning activities for STEM faculty to demonstrate cumulative (dis)advantage

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A facilitation strategy in which STEM faculty work directly with the RASE framework and its NetLogo simulation during diversity and inclusion sessions. Facilitators manipulate the tool to demonstrate long-term impacts of disparities, and participants are assigned to a framework element and asked to generate real-world instances of it. The article reports: "faculty participants are assigned to a specific framework element (R, A, S, or E) and tasked with brainstorming real-world examples of the element." The authors ground this in intervention research suggesting experiential learning interventions with simulations of accumulating advantage and disadvantage promote gender-equity knowledge and reduce sexist beliefs compared with information alone.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements

#### Constraints

### Target Learners

### Target Learning Goals
