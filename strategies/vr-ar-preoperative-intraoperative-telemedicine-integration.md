# Integrate radiological data into VR/AR for preoperative planning and intraoperative guidance, and extend VR/AR-supported anatomical knowledge to telemedicine

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In its discussion the review recommends integrating "radiological data ... into VR or AR applications to visualize anatomical structures," noting VR/AR-based preoperative planning helps users become familiar with complex surgical procedures and intraoperative guidance "reduces the likelihood of serious complications and enhances trainees' surgical skills." It also proposes VR/AR-supported anatomy knowledge for telemedicine, from remote diagnosis to complex tele-interventions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration of radiological data such as CT and MRI images into the VR/AR applications
#### Constraints
- The FDA approves these technologies based on their continuous evolution; the review notes there is still room for improvement

### Target Learners
- medical trainees
- surgical trainees
- practicing physicians

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical skill acquisition
- familiarity with complex surgical procedures
- remote diagnosis and management capability

## Claims

- [Review by Baashar et al. reported AR significantly increases performance speed, satisfaction, and confidence, though it is less effective in improving knowledge and skill](../claims/ar-increases-speed-satisfaction-confidence-less-effective-knowledge-skill.md) [+W]
- [Meta-analysis by Yeung et al. found AR improved performance time, confidence, and satisfaction but had no significant effect on knowledge or skill versus controls](../claims/ar-meta-analysis-improves-time-confidence-satisfaction-not-knowledge-skill.md) [+W]
- [Systematic review by Barteit et al. found AR and VR head-mounted devices achieved results at least non-inferior to conventional teaching](../claims/ar-vr-head-mounted-non-inferior-conventional-teaching.md) [+W]
- [Umbrella review by Tene et al. found no significant association between immersive technologies and improvements in medical education and training versus traditional methods](../claims/immersive-technologies-no-significant-association-umbrella-review.md) [-W]
- [Review by Kyaw et al. found VR improved post-intervention knowledge and skill outcomes among health professionals compared to traditional or other digital education](../claims/vr-improves-post-intervention-knowledge-skill-health-professionals.md) [+W]
