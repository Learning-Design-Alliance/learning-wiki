# Prepare teachers and infrastructure before implementing VR-based learning on sensitive topics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 6 studies (4 review, 2 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 6 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators using VR resources on sensitive topics prepare for emotional reactions, ensure accessible technical assistance, and secure institutional anchoring and training before implementation. Students in the focus groups "reported the need for technical support and guidance when using VR," and the authors stress that integrating VR into programs requires both infrastructural and pedagogical adjustments, including management anchoring and equipment logistics such as charging and software updates.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- teachers should have a plan for handling emotional reactions and provide support, guidance, and supervision throughout
- accessible and effective technical assistance must be available during use
- institutions must invest in technology, organize training, and manage equipment logistics
#### Constraints
- costs of VR equipment, software, and updates can still be a barrier for many educational institutions
- the article recommends future iterations consider accessibility features such as subtitles or alternative formats

### Target Learners
- health care students using VR-based learning resources

### Target Learning Goals
- safe and effective implementation of immersive learning on sensitive therapeutic topics
