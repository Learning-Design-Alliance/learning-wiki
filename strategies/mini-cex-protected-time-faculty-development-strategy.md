# Institutionalise Mini-CEX with protected time, faculty development, and a feedback-rich culture

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that to maximise Mini-CEX's impact, "institutions must address systemic barriers by providing protected time for assessments and investing in faculty development initiatives focused on Mini-CEX methodology and effective feedback delivery." They further recommend integrating Mini-CEX sessions into clinical rotations with scheduled protected time, adopting modified formats with written narrative feedback and structured reflection, and building a culture that embraces frequent, low-stakes assessments to normalise observation and reduce student anxiety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated faculty development workshops on Mini-CEX methodology and feedback delivery; scheduled protected time within clinical rotations.
#### Constraints
- Resource-constrained environments with high student-to-faculty ratios and demanding clinical schedules limit faculty time for assessments; hierarchical teacher-student relationships may inhibit open feedback exchanges.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in clinical rotations

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical competence, clinical reasoning, self-directed learning, reduced performance anxiety

### Affordances
- Lorwald Four Theme Mini Cex Impact Model
