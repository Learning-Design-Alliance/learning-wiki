# Balloon-and-bottle demonstration of Charles' law using hot and room-temperature water baths

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
A one-hour demonstration in which balloons are sealed over the mouths of dry plastic bottles and the bottles are submerged in hot (approximately 80°C) and room-temperature water. The set-up maintains a fixed amount of gas at fixed (atmospheric) pressure, so heating makes the gas expand and fill the balloon, illustrating that volume is directly proportional to temperature as Charles' law states. The process is reversible: returning the bottle to room-temperature water makes the balloon slowly shrink. A variant inflates a balloon and places it in a freezer for about an hour.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Balloons, hot (approximately 80°C) and room-temperature water, two dry empty plastic bottles, two water tubs large enough to fit the bottles
- Care when using hot water (safety note)
#### Constraints
- Suitable for students from around 14 years old

### Target Learners
- students from around 14 years old

### Target Learning Goals
- Charles' law: the direct proportionality of gas volume and temperature at constant pressure
