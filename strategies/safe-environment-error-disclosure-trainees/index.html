Create a non-judgmental, safe environment for trainees to disclose and discuss medical errors¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The review recommends that training programs create a non-judgmental, safe environment, which would contribute to the trainees' willingness to disclose medical errors. The most commonly reported impediment to error identification was environmental, defined as the culture of the organization or institution, reflected by concerns about perceived legal repercussions and the impact of error identification on career development.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A forum where trainees can openly discuss experiences without fear of legal repercussions

Constraints¶

  • Barriers stem from resident attitudes or personal factors, as well as environmental and system-related issues

Target Learners¶

  • Medical residents
  • Medical students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Error identification and reporting
  • Patient safety culture

Claims¶

  • Creating a safe and supportive learning environment is crucial for clinical reasoning development [+W]
  • The AI's nonjudgmental conversational tone and adaptive prompting fostered risk-taking and productive struggle in a psychologically safe environment [+W]
  • Learners valued safe, repeatable, nonjudgmental AI practice environments that reduced anxiety and bridged the theory-practice gap [+W]
