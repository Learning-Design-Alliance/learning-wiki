# Calming Movement Breaks

## Description
Integrating repetitive and rhythmic vestibular input, such as rocking, swaying, or gentle swinging, into the learning environment to help learners reset when they are overstimulated, overwhelmed, or dealing with tantrums. This can be achieved by adding rocking chairs or exercise balls to the room.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A designated space for movement activities. Rocking chairs, exercise balls, or other equipment for vestibular input. Clear guidelines for appropriate movement.
#### Constraints
- Some movement activities may be disruptive to other learners. May require additional supervision to ensure safety. Requires careful planning to integrate movement breaks into the learning schedule.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Reduce overstimulation, promote self-regulation, provide vestibular input

### Affordances
- Provides a way for learners to regulate their emotions and attention through movement. Can be integrated into various learning activities and settings. Is a low-cost and easily accessible strategy.

### Personalization
- Offer a variety of movement activities to meet the diverse needs and preferences of learners. Allow learners to choose the type and intensity of movement that works best for them. Provide options for individual or group movement activities.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe learners' engagement and participation in movement activities. Collect feedback from learners on the effectiveness of different movement activities. Track changes in learners' behavior, attention, and emotional regulation.

## Impact
- Research indicates that repetitive and rhythmic movement can reduce anxiety and promote self-regulation. Studies have shown that vestibular input can improve attention and cognitive function.
