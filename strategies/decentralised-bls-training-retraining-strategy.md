# Decentralise BLS training at scale with periodic retraining and certification tracking in performance management

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend moving BLS training from a centralised model to decentralised delivery through district health services, using family physicians' clinical governance role to enable training at scale. They argue that "the current centralised approach to BLS training limits capacity, and more decentralised training could improve coverage". Because knowledge and skills deteriorate within a year, facilities should document certification status and include it in performance management systems to maintain competency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Family physicians deployed in district health services with responsibility for clinical governance
- Documentation of nurses' BLS certification status
#### Constraints
- Knowledge and skills deteriorate within a year after training, making periodic retraining necessary

### Target Learners
- Nurses at district hospitals in South Africa

### Target Learning Goals
- Basic life support knowledge and skills
- Maintenance of resuscitation competency over time
