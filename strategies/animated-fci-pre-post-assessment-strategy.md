# Assess conceptual learning from programming exercises with an animated Force Concept Inventory administered immediately before and after each exercise

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 5 studies (3 causal, 2 design), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 5 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors re-created all 30 animated FCI questions as mp4 screen-capture videos in an online quiz, avoiding obsolete java applets, and plan to integrate them into a custom p5.js learning management system. Questions will be asked as a "pre-lab" immediately before students start the code and immediately after submission, "so as to avoid a situation where student performance could be attributed to something besides the programming exercises." Questions 21–24 on rocket motion in free space are seen as particularly relevant.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A website where students complete exercises in an in-browser editor, with animated questions served before and after each exercise
#### Constraints
- The animated FCI was re-created from supplemental files of Dancy and Beichner and physlet physics libraries; the larger definitive study is deferred to future work

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students at high school or early college level

### Target Learning Goals
- conceptual understanding of Newtonian mechanics, especially rocket motion in free space
