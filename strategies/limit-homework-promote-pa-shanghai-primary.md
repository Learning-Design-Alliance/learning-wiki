# Limit homework time within 2 hours and promote moderate- and minimal-intensity physical activity in schools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on the finding that homework beyond two hours daily was negatively associated with APS while moderate and minimal physical activity showed positive associations at lower performance quantiles, the authors recommend that schools "limit homework time within 2 hours" and state that the findings "support the promotion of physical activities in schools". They argue low-performing students could be worse off if physical activity time is replaced with more homework and extracurricular study.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Schools and parents would need to reallocate time currently spent on homework and extracurricular study toward physical activity
#### Constraints
- Rests on cross-sectional associations from Shanghai public schools, which the authors state limits the conclusions that may be drawn

### Target Learners
- fifth-grade primary school students, especially low academic performers

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance as measured by teacher-reported APS
