# Using Positive Affirmations

## Description
Replacing negative messages from the inner voice with positive affirmations. Positive affirmations can be specific to growth mindset or general statements of self-worth and capability.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Examples of positive affirmations. Time for students to reflect and choose affirmations. Encouragement and support from the educator.
#### Constraints
- Affirmations may feel insincere or ineffective if not genuinely believed. Some students may resist using affirmations. The activity's success depends on students' willingness to engage in the process.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- K-12 students, particularly those who need support in building self-confidence and resilience.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- To cultivate a positive mindset and increase self-esteem through the use of positive affirmations.

### Affordances
- Promotes self-awareness by identifying negative messages. Supports emotional regulation by replacing negative thoughts with positive ones. Fosters a growth mindset by emphasizing potential and possibilities.

### Personalization
- Adaptable to different age groups and learning styles. Students can create their own affirmations or use pre-made examples. Educators can provide varying levels of support and guidance.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe students' engagement and enthusiasm when using affirmations. Assess students' ability to articulate the impact of affirmations on their emotions. Review students' journals or reflections for evidence of positive self-talk.

## Impact
- Research shows that positive affirmations can support growth mindset. In order to realize the effects of positive affirmations, the learners need to engage with them regularly.
