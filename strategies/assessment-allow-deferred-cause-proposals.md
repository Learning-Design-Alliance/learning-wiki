# Assessments of modeling should allow students to skip the propose-cause prompt and circle back after further measurements and revisions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because students generally experience difficulty with the Propose Cause subtask, the authors recommend that a modeling assessment allow students to make measurements and revisions to reconcile unexpected results, skip the propose-cause prompt, and return to it later. The assessment should not focus solely on whether a probable cause was proposed initially, and should accommodate the process by which a student arrives at a proposed cause.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment tasks must allow students to make measurements and revisions to reconcile unexpected experimental results
#### Constraints
- Derived from a small qualitative interview study of ten students in one electronics troubleshooting context

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- proposing causes for discrepancies between measurements and model predictions
