# Implement student-support mechanisms and embed well-being in the medical curriculum to sustain strength of motivation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators use longitudinal monitoring of motivation strength to pinpoint when students struggle to adapt, and respond with structural supports. It states the findings "highlight the importance of implementing mechanisms for faculty to address the cognitive burden of information overload" and that "Incorporating well-being into the curriculum and designing preclinical and clinical schedules that optimize training while prioritizing student well-being could also significantly enhance outcomes."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty capacity to monitor motivation fluctuations and provide timely support across training phases
#### Constraints
- The article notes an innovative, student-centered curriculum alone may not be sufficient to sustain student motivation throughout medical training

### Target Learners
- Medical students in preclinical and core clinical training

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustaining strength of motivation and well-being across medical training
