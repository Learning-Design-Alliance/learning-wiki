Offer supplementary reading programs to children at substantially elevated genetic risk for dyslexia years before formal diagnosis¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

A forward-looking application the article sketches: if genetic screening can eventually predict dyslexia risk sufficiently well, parents of children at substantially elevated risk could be offered enrollment in supplementary reading programs years before a formal diagnosis. The article frames this under its statement that "knowledge of genetic risk may help parents more effectively to choose environments for their children", borrowing the example from Benjamin et al.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Genetic screening must eventually be able to predict dyslexia risk sufficiently well before formal diagnosis is possible.

Constraints¶

  • Mere knowledge of risk is of limited use if no environmental intervention is available to mitigate or compensate for the risk.

Target Learners¶

  • children with substantially elevated genetic risk for dyslexia

Target Learning Goals¶

  • early reading skills before formal dyslexia diagnosis

Claims¶

  • Genetic factors account for a substantial share of variation in educational outcomes and related behavioral traits [+M]
  • Heritability estimates are population-specific parameters, and the heritability of cognitive skills rises gradually through childhood and adolescence [+M]
  • Polygenic score predictive power for educational attainment increases with discovery sample size and is projected to approach the SNP heritability bound [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use beat synchronization assessment and early music and rhythm training as early indicators and supports of reading readiness
  • Screen all children for dyslexia early with an evidence-based screener and follow up with evidence-based interventions
