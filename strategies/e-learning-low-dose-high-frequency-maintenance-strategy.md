# Use e-learning as a low-dose, high-frequency skill maintenance tool after an initial blended learning course to counter skill decay

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because a single blended or face-to-face session did not prevent rapid decay of defibrillator skills within 2 months, the article proposes that after an initial blended learning course, the e-learning component be reused for maintenance. It states that "adding an e‐learning course to FFL seems relevant because after an initial BL course, e‐learning is designed to be used as a low‐dose, high‐frequency skill maintenance tool."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An initial blended learning course establishing the technical skills before maintenance use of the e-learning
#### Constraints
- The article states future studies will need to assess the frequency and related extent to which availability and use of e-learning enable participants to maintain the acquired skills

### Target Learners
- nurses and residents trained in manual defibrillator use in paediatric emergency and critical care settings

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term retention of defibrillation, cardioversion and transcutaneous pacing skills
