Six collective-responsibility strategies for AIED researchers facing commercial misuse¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), q1 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The author proposes that AIED researchers collectively ask how their research might be misappropriated by the commercial sector, listing six strategies: prioritising ethical research, carefully selecting topics and avoiding 'indefensible applications such as e-proctoring', empowering teachers and student agency, exercising due diligence on unintended consequences, engaging stakeholders including policymakers, and advocating for 'transparency, accountability, public scrutiny, robust guidelines and appropriate regulations'.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • AIED faculty members must 'give a lead' for these practices to become standard

Constraints¶

  • The author explicitly declines to assign direct ethical liability to individual researchers

Target Learners¶

  • AIED researchers
  • commercial AIED developers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Protecting learners' education, human rights, and agency across the AI-in-education lifecycle

Claims¶

  • AIED has reached substantial commercial scale while attracting criticism for poor pedagogy, datafication and surveillance [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Distinguish academic AIED from the commercial sector and develop ways to hold the sector to account
  • Investigate stakeholder privacy requirements and define transparent rules for learner-data use
  • Raise student and colleague awareness of AI-related privacy risks through research-ethics curricula
