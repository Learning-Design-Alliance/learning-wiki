# Use embodied modeling activities to prepare and deepen agent-based programming, with perspective-taking across agent types

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The chapter reports that all teachers in the authors' studies "saw embodied modeling as a valuable activity for teaching students how to think like an agent." Embodied modeling introduces students to the computational rules behind agent-based commands, helps them debug programs, and deepens understanding of graphs in simulations. Activities must be designed so students can take the perspectives of different types of agents, not just one, and mathematical inscriptions from embodied activity provide representational continuity with subsequent ABMs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Embodied activities must precede or accompany ABM work and generate mathematical inscriptions (e.g., bar graphs) reused across representations
#### Constraints
- For modeling interactions between different types of agents, activities must let students take on multiple agents' perspectives rather than one

### Target Learners
- Elementary students learning agent-based modeling

### Target Learning Goals
- thinking like an agent; debugging programs; interpreting simulation graphs

### Affordances
- Phenomenological Epistemology Computational Thinking
