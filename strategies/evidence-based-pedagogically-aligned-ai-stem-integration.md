# Integrate AI-enabled personalized STEM education with teacher participation, project-based collaboration, and cross-disciplinary task design, matched to students' cognitive developmental level

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q4` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article advocates an "evidence-based and pedagogically aligned integration of AI-enabled personalized STEM education". Based on its moderator findings, it recommends that educators consider students' cognitive developmental levels and self-regulation capacities, balance technical minimalism with student interest, and design tailored instructional strategies for different educational stages. For tool developers, it recommends instructional content that integrates disciplines and encourages cross-disciplinary learning activities, for example combining physics, mathematics, and IT knowledge into real-world problems through project-based or problem-driven learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- AI tools should be combined with classroom use, teacher participation, and student-centered pedagogies such as project-based and collaborative learning, which the moderators show are more effective
#### Constraints
- The kindergarten subgroup showed only a minimal effect (g = 0.118), and the authors caution that younger students' limited attention and cognitive depth may hinder effective use of AI functionalities

### Target Learners
- K-12 students, especially junior and senior high school students in STEM subjects

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved STEM knowledge and skills, engagement, and cross-disciplinary problem-solving
