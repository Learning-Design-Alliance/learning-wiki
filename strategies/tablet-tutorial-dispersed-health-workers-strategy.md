# Deliver time-limited, self-paced electronic tutorials to dispersed health workers via tablet computers during epidemics

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends exploring mHealth tutorial applications for training frontline health workers in low- and middle-income countries during disease outbreaks. The tutorial was "streamed in real time" and health workers viewed it "at their convenience over a 2-week period", with multiple views allowed and identical pre- and post-assessments to measure change. The authors state that using mHealth tutorials "as a means of bolstering the capability of health systems to manage disease conditions and improving health in LMICs should be explored further".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tablet computers already deployed in facilities, prepaid mobile network subscriptions, and an orientation programme training health workers to use the tablets and application
#### Constraints
- Knowledge and attitude changes were modest; some improvement may arise from parallel exposure to government mass-media campaigns; a 2-month industrial strike prevented some participants from completing the post-tutorial survey

### Target Learners
- frontline health workers in low- and middle-income countries

### Target Learning Goals
- disease-specific knowledge and prevention attitudes during epidemic response
