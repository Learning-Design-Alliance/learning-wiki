# Schedule individual student-faculty debriefing meetings one week after F-OSCEs, with students pre-annotating their videos, to ensure use of performance data

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes individual student-faculty debriefing meetings scheduled for two F-OSCEs per year, occurring one week after the exam. Students prepare by reviewing their videos and "annotating areas of strength and areas for improvement to discuss with the faculty member" in 20-minute meetings. The study's data show these meetings significantly increased review of all data modalities, making them an effective strategy to ensure better use of available feedback data.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to simulation recording software that releases scores, checklists, and video recordings to students after each F-OSCE
- Faculty time to conduct individual 20-minute meetings per student
#### Constraints
- The authors state the disadvantage of meetings is the substantial use of faculty time

### Target Learners
- first- and second-year medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical skills development
- self-directed use of performance feedback
- reflection on clinical performance
