# Combine mandatory and optional intervention components so organizations can adapt the intervention to their circumstances

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article defines adaptability of a bundled intervention by splitting components into mandatory core tasks and optional tasks, so that facilities can focus on their institution's specific needs. The authors state that "The possibility to adapt the intervention to the individual circumstances and needs of the institution shall support identification with the intervention and subsequently implementation." Workshop participants likewise emphasized that a manageable range of nursing tasks maintains feasibility.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A bundle of components clearly designated as core (mandatory) versus optional
- Stakeholder input to decide which tasks are resident-related priorities
#### Constraints
- Derived from stakeholder ratings and team consensus in an intervention development study, not yet tested for effect

### Target Learners
- nursing home managers and nursing teams in long-term care

### Target Learning Goals
- implementing a new nursing role profile in routine care
