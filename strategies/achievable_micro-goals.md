# Achievable Micro-goals

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Achievable micro-goals break a large learning task into small, immediately attainable sub-goals that a learner can complete in a single session or less. Each completed micro-goal produces a visible marker of progress, which builds self-efficacy and sustains motivation through the difficult middle phases of learning. The strategy is carried out by decomposing the task, sequencing the sub-goals so early ones are reliably achievable, and making completion explicit (checklists, progress bars, check-ins).

## Design Implications

Micro-goals work because perceived progress is one of the strongest drivers of motivation and persistence [Progress toward goals increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M], and because self-efficacy is built primarily through mastery experiences — repeated success at tasks perceived as challenging but attainable [+S]. Sub-goals also function as process goals, which are more effective than distant outcome goals for novices [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]. Decomposing tasks into sub-goals is closely related to [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md): both reduce the working-memory and motivational load of a large task by restructuring it into units the learner can fully process and complete.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A task analysis that identifies a coherent sequence of sub-goals, each completable within a short time frame
- Early sub-goals calibrated so learners experience success quickly, then increase in difficulty ([Fading](../elements/fading.md) logic applied to goal difficulty)
- Visible progress markers — checklists, progress bars, or [Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md) — so completion is registered, not just performed
- A link from each micro-goal back to the larger goal, so learners see the sub-goals as meaningful rather than busywork

#### Constraints
- Goals that are trivially easy produce completion without learning; goal difficulty must stay within the challenging-but-attainable band [Specific difficult goals lead to higher performance than easy or vague goals.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md) [~M] — micro-goals trade some of this difficulty effect for motivational momentum
- Excessive decomposition can fragment learning and prevent learners from developing whole-task coordination [Whole-task performance improves transfer.](../claims/whole-task-performance-improves-transfer.md) [-M] — micro-goals should be nested within a visible whole-task structure, not replace it
- Externally imposed micro-goals can undermine ownership; learners set more ambitious and better-calibrated goals when given autonomy in how sub-goals are defined [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Instructor-defined sequences (scaffolded curricula, levelled platforms) versus learner-defined sub-goals supported by [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md)
- Session-level micro-goals ("finish the first draft paragraph") versus habit-level micro-goals ("practice 10 minutes daily")
- Digital progress mechanics (Duolingo streaks, Khan Academy mastery bars) versus analog checklists and conferencing

### Target Learners
- Novices and learners with low self-efficacy, for whom early mastery experiences are the primary source of confidence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+S]
- Learners who are overwhelmed by large, ambiguous tasks; decomposition reduces avoidance and procrastination
- Less necessary for experts, who typically self-decompose tasks; imposed micro-goals may feel constraining [Worked-example guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Persistence and self-regulation: building the habit of planning, monitoring, and completing work [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]
- Procedural skill acquisition through incremental [Practice](../elements/practice.md)
- Motivational outcomes — confidence, task value, and sustained engagement — more than single-session knowledge gains

### Instructions
1. Analyze the target task and identify its natural sub-steps; chunk them so each micro-goal is completable in one sitting ([Chunking](../principles/chunking.md))
2. Sequence the sub-goals so the first is reliably achievable and difficulty rises gradually, fading support as competence grows ([Fading](../elements/fading.md))
3. State each micro-goal in specific, process-oriented terms before learners begin ([State Objectives](../elements/state-objectives.md))
4. Provide guidance and worked models for the earliest sub-goals, then withdraw it ([Provide Guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md), [Practice](../elements/practice.md))
5. Make completion visible and celebrate progress — checklists, progress displays, or brief [Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md)
6. Gradually transfer goal-setting to learners, having them plan and monitor their own sub-goals ([Action Planning](action_planning.md))

## Related Strategies
- [Action Planning](action_planning.md) — the learner-directed form of the same move; micro-goals are the units of an action plan
- [Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md) — the accountability and progress-visibility mechanism that makes micro-goals salient

## Examples
- **[Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com)** — daily goals, streaks, and unit-by-unit progression turn language learning into a sequence of small, reliably achievable sessions; progress displays register each completion.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — mastery bars and skill-level badges decompose a subject into micro-goals, with [practice](../elements/practice.md) attached to each.
- **Writing instruction** — instructors break essay assignments into thesis, outline, draft, and revision deadlines, each a micro-goal with feedback, rather than a single due date.
