Video-Based Feedback Cycle for Teacher Teams¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

A teacher (or team) articulates their own inquiry question about their practice; a lesson is filmed; a facilitator (researcher or coach) selects video clips relevant to that inquiry question rather than clips chosen to evaluate the teacher; the team and facilitator watch and discuss the clips together, with the facilitator positioning their video-analysis expertise as complementary to the teachers' instructional knowledge rather than authoritative over it; the team debriefs, often spontaneously invoking outside practices or frameworks from their own prior experience or PD as they make sense of what the video shows.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A teacher- or team-initiated inquiry question that drives which clips get selected, rather than a fixed observation rubric
  • A facilitator stance of collaborative sensemaking rather than evaluation or coaching-as-correction
  • Multi-angle video capture (whole class plus a focal small group, in the source study) so relevant moments are actually captured

Constraints¶

  • The type of support this cycle provides (reflective sensemaking on one's own video vs. surfacing external practices/frameworks) appears to be most useful at different points in a team's own change process — PD support type and salience depends on a teacher team's current phase in the adaptive learning cycle [~M]
  • Teams entering the partnership already mid-cycle (e.g., already past problematization) may not benefit as much from the reflective, gap-surfacing function of early video review

Target Learners¶

  • In-service teacher teams in an extended (multi-year) professional development partnership

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Supporting teachers in an inquiry-driven, non-evaluative examination of their own practice, timed to what they are actually ready to work on

Instructions¶

  1. Teacher or team articulates a question or inquiry focus about their own practice.
  2. A lesson is filmed (multiple camera angles; whole-class plus a focal small-group audio track, if relevant to the inquiry).
  3. The facilitator reviews the footage and selects clips relevant to the team's stated inquiry focus.
  4. The facilitator and team watch the clips together and discuss collaboratively, with the facilitator's analysis offered as a complementary perspective, not a verdict.
  5. The team debriefs, reflecting on what the clips revealed and drawing in outside practices, frameworks, or prior experience as relevant.

Examples¶

  • Project SIGMa used this cycle with school-based teacher teams over one to two years, with teams entering and progressing through different phases of the adaptive cycle of change at different rates.
