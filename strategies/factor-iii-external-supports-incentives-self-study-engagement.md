# Provide external supports and incentives (grades, learning communities, time-management modules) to drive engagement with self-study tools

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Drawing on Factor III of the SELF framework, the authors recommend embedding motivational and structural supports when implementing self-study tools: grade incentives, learning communities with follow-up group activities, buy-in framing, time-scheduling modules with electronic reminders, and explicit connections to in-class lessons. As they put it, "an instructor could incentivize participation in learning via grade incentives to ensure that students work on them as prescribed." They also suggest awarding credit per sub-problem with decreasing score for repeated guessing, and having students submit and justify answers to sub-problems as homework.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Implementation-level support beyond tool design: instructor-provided motivation, accountability structures, and scheduling help
#### Constraints
- These supports are recommended, not tested in this study; the study's implementation lacked them

### Target Learners
- students in large brick-and-mortar introductory courses needing out-of-class remediation

### Target Learning Goals
- deliberate, prescribed engagement with self-paced e-learning tutorials

### Affordances
- Self Framework Self Study Engaged Learning
