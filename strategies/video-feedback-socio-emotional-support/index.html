Use video-based feedback to provide socio-emotional support and social presence in remote learning¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 theoretical), q2–q3 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article reports that responses to Borup et al. (2015) build "a strong argument that feedback is a source of social-emotional support enabling social presence in a remote learning COVID-19 situation". Ryan's response features "the affordances of video-based feedback providing socio-emotional support for affective outcomes of online students". Kaplan-Rakowski argues for ensuring emotional support, mental wellness, and social presence sustaining connections between socially isolated teachers and students.

Claims¶

  • Borup et al. (2015) found video feedback more convenient for student support and text-based feedback more convenient for exploratory feedback [+W]
  • A large meta-analysis found that feedback channel per se is non-significant [-W]
  • Human embodiment in video: perceived social presence benefits learning ratings, but instructor-face inclusion shows no significant learning-performance difference, and learners prefer human over robot presenters with mixed recall [+W]
  • Including videos of the students' own human teacher giving motivational feedback was more effective than an animated pedagogical agent and improved homework completion rates [+W]
