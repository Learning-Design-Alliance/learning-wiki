# Structured post-screening discussion using amplification followed by moral case deliberation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article proposes a concrete classroom procedure: screen a film with existential themes, then have students discuss trigger questions in pairs ('What triggered your attention?'; 'By which scenes were you touched and moved?') to apply amplification and reconstruct narrative understanding. The tutor then takes stock of dilemmas the pairs identify, and the group selects one to explore through the steps of moral case deliberation — formulating the dilemma, analysing perspectives, values and norms, seeking alternatives, making individual choices and engaging in dialogical inquiry.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A tutor or moderator facilitating each working group
- A film screened beforehand, preferably in a cinema-like setting
- A safe educational environment for sharing personal associations
#### Constraints
- The author reports the group size of approximately 18 people as one he and his colleagues have found effective, based on his own teaching experience rather than a controlled comparison

### Target Learners
- University students in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Expressing and supporting moral intuitions with arguments
- Exploring existential questions raised by narrative film
