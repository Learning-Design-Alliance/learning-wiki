# Use validated context-specific resilience measures to identify students vulnerable to early dropout and target support

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends developing and validating instruments that enable in-depth analysis of moderating variables facilitating positive responses to stress and adversity, so educators can better understand students' coping strategies in the face of negative experiences and make them less vulnerable to the negative effects of stressful events. The validated Spanish ARS provides such a measure, since low capacity to adapt and overcome failure is an indicator of early dropout, lack of promotion, and disruptive behavior.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated, context-appropriate instrument such as the Spanish ARS with adequate psychometric properties
#### Constraints
- The nonprobability sampling method and low geographic variability limit generalization; future research should analyze other populations

### Target Learners
- university students
- students at risk of dropout

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying students' capacity to adapt to and overcome academic failure
