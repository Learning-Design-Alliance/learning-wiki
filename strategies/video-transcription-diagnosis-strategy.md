# Use video-recorded think-aloud case reports to diagnose knowledge gaps and tailor teaching strategies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators transcribe and analyse video-recorded case reports to understand individual knowledge levels and adjust instruction. It states that video-recorded case reports becoming written materials emerged as "a valuable tool for educators and students alike, facilitating a nuanced comprehension of individual knowledge levels, thereby enabling tailored teaching and learning strategies". Clinical nursing leaders are also urged to curate collaborative learning opportunities, foster psychological resilience, and prioritize metacognitive training within nursing curricula.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video recording of case reports with clear and reliable video quality
- Transcription adhering to strict norms, with cumulative silence not exceeding 10% of total test time
- Assessment of data reliability and validity by two researchers over two rounds until mutual agreement
#### Constraints
- The strategy analysis relied on a logical rather than statistical approach with 11 evaluation criteria; three videos were excluded for silence exceeding 10% of duration and five for noncompliant transcription standards

### Target Learners
- new nurses in nursing teaching rounds

### Target Learning Goals
- metacognitive aptitude
- psychological and team-oriented competencies
- tailored learning strategies
