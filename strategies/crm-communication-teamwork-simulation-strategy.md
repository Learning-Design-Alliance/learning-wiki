# Use simulation debriefing with Crisis Resource Management, effective communication methods, and teamwork improvement to reduce communication-related errors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose simulation training strategies targeting the errors identified in both classification systems. Their approach "focuses on error management and fostering a safety culture, aiming to reduce communication-related errors by introducing the principles of Crisis Resource Management, effective communication methods, and overall teamwork improvement." This strategy follows from the finding that communication breakdown and poor team performance were among the most frequently observed errors in the simulation sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- High-fidelity simulation sessions with structured post-session debriefing emphasizing technical and non-technical skills
#### Constraints
- The article notes miscommunication remains the predominant cause of medical errors in both simulation and clinical settings, so the strategy addresses a persistent problem rather than a solved one

### Target Learners
- Postgraduate healthcare professionals in Critical Care simulation courses

### Target Learning Goals
- Error management, communication, situational awareness, role clarity, and teamwork
