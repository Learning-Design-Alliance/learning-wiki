Evaluate serious game learning environments with both quantitative learning-effectiveness measures and qualitative and affective measures¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's evaluation combined quantitative and qualitative measures on a sample of 48 children, collecting data on learning effectiveness together with enjoyment, engagement, and intention to participate. The authors state that "data collection consisted of quantitative and qualitative measures," and the qualitative data offered a plausible explanation for the affective results. This dual-measure approach allowed the study to detect equal learning but higher appeal.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Collect both quantitative and qualitative data on the same sample

Constraints¶

  • Demonstrated only on a sample of 48 children in one school class comparison

Target Learners¶

  • Elementary education children

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Learning effectiveness and affective response to the learning environment

Claims¶

  • Qualitative data offer a plausible explanation for the virtual group's higher enjoyment and engagement despite equivalent learning outcomes [+W]
  • Students in the serious virtual world group reported enjoying the class more, being more engaged, and having greater intentions to participate than students in the traditional group [+M]
  • A serious virtual world class does not produce statistically significant differences in learning effectiveness compared with a traditional class of identical learning objectives and contents [+M]
