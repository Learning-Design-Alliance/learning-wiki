# Incorporate an expert human in the loop to support LLM coding of difficult adjacent coding categories

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article's discussion recommends exploring human-in-the-loop support when multi-agent refinement alone leaves adjacent coding categories confounded. It states that "Consulting a trained human rater about points of difficulty may substantially enhance the performance of the LLM," because human coders can offer deeper insight into distinctions between overall and adjacent levels than an additional LLM-driven agent. The strategy applies after automated refinement when κ–QWK gaps reveal persistent adjacent-category confusion on complex items.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a trained human rater with expertise in the coding construct
- Coding manuals and disagreement data identifying the items where the model struggles
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as a direction to explore, not a tested manipulation in this study; the items where it would apply were also challenging for human coders

### Target Learners
- U.S. middle school mathematics teachers (whose responses are being coded)

### Target Learning Goals
- Reliable automated coding of teachers' pedagogical content knowledge from open-ended responses
