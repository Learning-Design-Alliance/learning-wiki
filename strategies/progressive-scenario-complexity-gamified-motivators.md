# Progressive scenario complexity with consequences and gamified motivators to sustain simulator practice

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
A strategy drawn from Theme 2 and Theme 3 in which trainees progress through scenarios of increasing complexity, face consequences for errors that feed into subsequent scenarios, and are motivated by gamification, peer score comparison, proficiency gates and a simulation logbook. Participants suggested that serious gaming modules were "a strong method of internal motivation" and that being barred from operating-theatre progression until simulation proficiency was achieved would be a strong external motivator.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios of increasing complexity inspired by real patient files, recorded complications or malpractice reports
- Feedback tailored to the learner with clear endpoints
- Access to simulation and protected time to practice
#### Constraints
- Metrics displayed after VR simulator use were not seen as a strong motivator on their own
- Transferability of simulation gains is difficult to prove

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees from junior to senior levels

### Target Learning Goals
- technical skill in open limb trauma procedures
- decision-making under unpredictable complications
- motivation for sustained deliberate practice
