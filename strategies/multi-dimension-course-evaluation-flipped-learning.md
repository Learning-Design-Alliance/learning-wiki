# Evaluate course quality beyond student performance, assessing design alignment, outcomes, satisfaction, and the learning environment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article advises evaluating not only student performance but overall course design and instructional effectiveness to inform future iterations. Evaluation should address multiple dimensions: alignment of instructional design with learning objectives, achievement of learning outcomes, and student and faculty satisfaction — rather than relying solely on Likert-scale perceptions. Appropriate methods include comprehension quizzes, clinical reasoning worksheet assessments, group presentations, structured satisfaction surveys, faculty and student surveys or interviews, and assessment of the learning environment itself (material accessibility, tool usability, quality of in-class interactions).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Systematic collection of feedback from diverse sources (students, faculty, learning-environment indicators) to support iterative improvement
#### Constraints
- Reliance solely on Likert-scale perceptions, common in practice, is inadequate for judging course quality

### Target Learners
- Preclinical medical students and the faculty teaching them

### Target Learning Goals
- Continuous improvement of case-based flipped learning implementation
