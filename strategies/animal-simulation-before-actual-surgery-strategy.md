# Provide animal-based simulation training before performing actual endoscopic surgeries

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's tested training sequence: trainees first undergo animal-based simulation training and only then perform actual endoscopic thyroidectomies, rather than proceeding directly to live surgery. The authors argue this "has the potential to decrease reliance on trial-and-error approaches in clinical practice, thus accelerating the learning curve, and reducing the incidence of iatrogenic complications". In the trial this sequence produced higher theory, proficiency, and engagement outcomes than direct clinical training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to an animal-experiment-based simulation platform with porcine models and instructor guidance
#### Constraints
- Evaluated in a single-center preliminary study; generalizability to other practice settings may be limited

### Target Learners
- surgical trainees learning endoscopic thyroidectomy

### Target Learning Goals
- surgical proficiency, theoretical knowledge, engagement in surgical learning
