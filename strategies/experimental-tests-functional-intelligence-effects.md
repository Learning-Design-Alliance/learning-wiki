# Evaluate proposed instructional approaches with controlled experiments measuring far-transfer problem solving

## Description
The article proposes experimental designs to test its two new hypothetical approaches: functional use of intelligence and explicit instruction in search-based heuristics. It recommends comparing groups with different levels of domain training on genuinely novel problems outside their domains, and manipulating access to example-based heuristic instruction, with assessment "preferably on delayed post-tests" of genuinely novel problem-solving performance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Evaluation methods must be able to measure genuinely novel or far-transfer problem-solving performance; comparison groups should be demographically similar.
#### Constraints
- The article states better performance on such delayed post-tests would count as evidence the proposed approach works; the approaches are currently untested hypotheses.

### Target Learning Goals
