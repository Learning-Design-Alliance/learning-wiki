# Best practices for administering attitudes and beliefs surveys: pre/post timing, framing, online and in-class protocols

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 design), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size

## Description
The article recommends giving the survey as a pre-test in the first week of the term and a post-test in the last week; stressing to students that the survey is not meant to evaluate individuals, that there is no right answer, and that results will not influence grades. For online administration it recommends announcing in class and by email, a three-to-seven-day window, a timer with discarding unusually fast responses, and follow-up reminders. In-class administration yields higher completion rates, and scantrons ease tabulation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Give the survey in its entirety with original wording and question order for meaningful cross-class comparisons
- Stress that there is no right answer and results will not influence course grade
#### Constraints
- Test security is not important for these surveys because there is no right answer, unlike concept inventories

### Target Learners
- physics students completing the surveys

### Target Learning Goals
- obtaining valid measurements of shifts in students' beliefs about learning physics
