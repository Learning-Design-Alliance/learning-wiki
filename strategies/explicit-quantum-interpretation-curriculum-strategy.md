# Explicitly teach physical interpretation of quantum physics as a course topic while leveraging classical intuitions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 4 studies (3 qualitative, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors developed a modern physics curriculum whose aims are to "make the physical interpretation of quantum physics a course topic unto itself, and consistently attend to student ontologies throughout the course"; help students acquire language to articulate their beliefs about reality; and provide experimental evidence confronting intuitions. Rather than telling students what to believe, instructors provided logical arguments and experimental evidence, then let students decide for themselves, promoting a Matter-Wave interpretation for consistency.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors should be explicit with students about consciously separating classical and quantum ontologies
- Directly confronting intuitive classical ontologies in multiple contexts
#### Constraints
- A strictly particle view is not illegitimate, but its consistent application in all contexts can lead novices to paradoxical or incorrect conclusions
- No consensus exists among practicing physicists regarding the correct ontological nature of electrons

### Target Learners
- undergraduate modern physics students (engineering and physics majors)

### Target Learning Goals
- physically interpreting quantum theory
- understanding domains of applicability of interpretations
