# Disaggregate absences by excused versus unexcused type to identify at-risk elementary students early

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that research and policy treat excused and unexcused absences as distinct signals rather than a single attendance total. It argues that "conduct ing this analysis on a sample of elementary school students allows for the early identification of at-risk students in primary years of schooling," before risks intensify in later grades. Districts already record absence type, so the disaggregation can be applied to existing records.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Student records must distinguish excused from unexcused absences, as the Philadelphia School District's Attendance and Truancy Office guidelines do.
#### Constraints
- The article notes that even with some notes, an absence can still be classified as unexcused, and suspension cannot be distinguished from other unexcused absences in these data.

### Target Learners
- Elementary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- Early identification of students at academic risk from unexcused absenteeism
