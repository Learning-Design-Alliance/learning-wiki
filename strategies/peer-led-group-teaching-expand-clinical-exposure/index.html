Use peer-led small group teaching across multiple clinical sites to expand students' condition exposure without increasing educator workload¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends peer-led small group teaching as a useful adjunct to clinical teaching where sites, educators, and finances are limited. Because clinical learning was mostly peer-led, introducing weekly group sessions at all four paediatric sites expanded the health conditions students encountered "without increasing the work load of clinical educators". The strategy is supported by the finding that instructor type did not affect test scores.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Well-structured content and contextually relevant teaching, as in this study
  • A clinical educator to co-ordinate sessions and cover any content the peer presenters missed

Constraints¶

  • Students preferred educator teaching, so the strategy may face acceptance barriers despite equivalent outcomes

Target Learners¶

  • third-year physiotherapy students on clinical placements

Target Learning Goals¶

  • broadened exposure to paediatric health conditions
  • clinical knowledge applicable across conditions

Claims¶

  • Peer-led and clinical educator-led group teaching produce no significant difference in student test scores in clinical physiotherapy placements [+W]
  • Students reported significantly higher satisfaction with clinical educator teaching than with peer teaching despite equivalent outcome scores [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • School counselors set up peer-led small groups where trained students teach STAC strategies to their peers
  • Set up peer tutoring stations with audio prompting as a low-cost supplement to teacher-led instruction
