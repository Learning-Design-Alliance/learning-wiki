# Comparative visualization of a permanent geostationary satellite to interpret non-geostationary orbits

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article reports that a timer alone was insufficient for students to interpret non-geostationary orbits observationally. The authors therefore designed a checkbox that activates the display of a permanent geostationary satellite, so that "quick and clear interpretation of the meaning of non-geostationary by comparing with a geostationary orbit could be made." For example, a red satellite above Singapore completing one revolution in 24 hours is shown simultaneously with a slower orbit of radius 10.5 times Earth's radius completing half a revolution in 48 hours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A checkbox-controlled display of a permanent geostationary reference object must be built into the simulation.
#### Constraints
- The authors found the timer display alone insufficient for students to make clear meaning from observation.

### Target Learners
- GCE Advanced Level physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- interpreting orbital period differences through direct visual comparison
