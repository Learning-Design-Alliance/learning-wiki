# Train peer instructors in advance with expert faculty oversight and written facilitator guides before deploying student-led skills teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Before the SLPTS, the seven peer instructors attended a training session one week ahead, received facilitator guides and presession videos, and were oriented by an expert simulation educator on giving feedback using the Pendleton model. An emergency medicine physician with a simulation fellowship was present "to ensure proper technique was taught," and faculty also reviewed the facilitator guides and suggested revisions. This preparation addresses the concern that peer instructors may teach improper technique.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Peer instructor training session held prior to the learner session
- Expert faculty review of clinical content and facilitator guides
- Orientation of instructors on feedback delivery (Pendleton feedback model)
#### Constraints
- The article frames this as mitigation of a stated concern about improper technique, not as a tested manipulation

### Target Learners
- Medical students serving as peer instructors

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural skills teaching and feedback delivery
