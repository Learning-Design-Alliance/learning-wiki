Strategies

Use free online vocabulary games in groups of three, with a teacher-chosen first game and student choice thereafter¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's implementable classroom recipe: select games from free online game sites based on relevance to the vocabulary of the preceding lesson, seat students in groups of three with turn-taking because computers are fewer than students, have the teacher choose the first game to keep students on target vocabulary, then let each group replay, continue to a related game, or switch to a more difficult or easier one.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A computer laboratory with sufficient computers for group rotation, and games selected for relevance to the target vocabulary of the preceding lesson.

Constraints¶

  • The article notes the first game is teacher-chosen so students do not deviate from the target vocabulary and lesson learning goals are achieved.

Target Learners¶

  • primary-school EFL students working in small groups

Target Learning Goals¶

  • practising and retaining newly presented English vocabulary

Claims¶

  • Low-achievement 4th-grade students who practised English vocabulary with computer games raised their achievement by 38.7% on average over 24 weeks [+W]
  • Control-group students taught with conventional coursebook vocabulary tasks showed a 2.8% decrease in scores over the same period [+W]
  • In the final interview, 81.25% of experimental-group students said online computer games helped them learn vocabulary quicker and more effectively [+W]
  • Low-scoring experimental-group students moved from 25% scoring below 25% at pre-test to none scoring below 25% at post-test, with scores reaching as much as 56.8% [+W]
  • Among the vocabulary-teaching methods used, students most often named computer games as the most enjoyable (57%), most beneficial (40%) and preferred (61%) option [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use index-card games as follow-up activities to consolidate and retain new vocabulary
