# Teach SNAPPS to students with a single 30-minute orientation session before case presentations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article reports that students proved able to use the SNAPPS method after a single 30-minute training session, in which the technique was demonstrated, an example case presentation was shown on video, and students could ask questions, supported by a pocket-sized card listing the six steps. Teaching SNAPPS to students appears to be simple, efficient, and time effective, achieving short-term learning outcomes with no extra effort from the teacher to lead the session.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A 30-minute orientation session including a demonstration, an example video, opportunity for questions, and a pocket-sized card listing the six SNAPPS steps
#### Constraints
- Results are from written cases in a controlled setting; further studies are needed in the context of real clinical teaching and with learners of different expertise levels

### Target Learners
- medical students beginning clinical clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- taking an active role during case presentation
- expressing clinical reasoning and uncertainties
