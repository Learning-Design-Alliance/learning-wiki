# Design OSCE scenarios that measure examinees' responses to pre-specified emotional cues, using validated frameworks such as VRCoDES

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Participants proposed enriching traditional OSCE stations with specific communication challenges, "for example, by introducing specific emotional cues or concerns in the OSCE case". The Verona Coding Definitions of Emotional Sequence was proposed as a framework for developing such scenarios and for assessing the appropriateness of examinees' responses to emotional distress portrayed by standardized patients.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Emotional cues or concerns "should be related to the medical problem" or its perceived consequences.
- A measure to assess the appropriateness of the examinee's response must be developed.
#### Constraints
- Participants suggested limiting the number of stations with specific emotional stimuli and varying the types and intensity of emotional states, because examinees expecting an emotional agenda in every encounter may adopt a non-authentic, test-induced communication style.

### Target Learners
- medical school graduates taking a high-stakes licensing OSCE

### Target Learning Goals
- responding appropriately to patient emotional cues and concerns
