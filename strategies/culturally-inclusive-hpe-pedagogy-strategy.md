# Use culturally inclusive pedagogies, such as incorporating indigenous language and values authentically into HPE lessons

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article shows teachers building social cohesion and promoting marginalized groups through culturally inclusive pedagogies. In New Zealand, teachers incorporated te reo Māori and Māori values; Kendall stressed doing this "in a way that is authentic rather than" tokenism, and in Sweden a teacher adapted his teaching with body language and peer demonstration for new immigrant children.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cultural sensitivity and critical consciousness as starting points
- authentic rather than tokenistic incorporation of indigenous culture
#### Constraints
- Practices were adapted to the specific cultural context of each school and country

### Target Learners
- 13–15-year-old students in multicultural schools, including new immigrant students

### Target Learning Goals

- acceptance of diversity
- full participation
