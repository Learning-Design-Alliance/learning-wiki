Use computer-based simulations to teach chemistry topics that are difficult to demonstrate, as a gender-friendly bridge of the achievement gap¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (4 causal), q2 · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends adopting computer-based simulations for chemistry instruction where regular expository methods leave little room for thinking, and specifically for topics like electrolysis whose processes do not manifest visibly. It states "the use of CBS teaching approach is gender friend ly and is an effective instructional strategy of bridging the gender gap in chemistry achievement", and that enhanced CBS use would reduce rote learning and emphasize student-centred activities.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • CBS materials developed and implemented in an effective way
  • teacher training via a teachers' manual in experimental schools

Constraints¶

  • Findings come from a quasi-experimental study in four co-educational district schools in one Kenyan sub-county

Target Learners¶

  • secondary school chemistry students, boys and girls in co-educational schools

Target Learning Goals¶

  • understanding of chemistry concepts and principles

Claims¶

  • Secondary school students taught chemistry through computer-based simulations achieve significantly higher post-test scores than students taught through regular teaching methods [+M]
  • The CBS advantage over RTM in chemistry achievement persists after adjusting post-test scores for prior attainment using KCPE as covariate [+M]
  • The pre-tested experimental group gained more on the Chemistry Achievement Test than the pre-tested control group [+M]
  • No statistically significant gender difference in chemistry achievement among students taught through computer-based simulations [+M]
  • Combining analogy-based simulation with laboratory activities produces significantly higher corrected post-test achievement in simple electricity than either environment alone [+M]
  • Secondary students taught in an e-classroom with ICT resources significantly outperform students taught by conventional methods on post-test achievement [+W]
  • No significant difference in organic chemistry post-test achievement between male and female students irrespective of instructional method [+M]

Related Strategies¶

  • Chemistry teachers should employ varied effective pedagogies, especially inquiry-based and problem-based approaches, supplemented with visualization and real-world materials
  • Design plugged CT experiences to be accessible and appealing to girls, and address technology-related anxiety through supportive, culturally relevant designs
