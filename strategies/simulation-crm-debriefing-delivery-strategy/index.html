Strategies

Deliver NTS training through simulation-based courses with structured debriefing focused on Crisis Resource Management principles, supplemented by in-situ refreshers and visual reminders.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 8 claims (8 for) · 3 studies (2 review, 1 design), q2 · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 8 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The predominant instructional strategy across included studies was "simulation-based learning, commonly combined with didactic lectures, interactive discussions, and structured debriefing sessions focusing on Crisis Resource Management (CRM) principles". Specific approaches included script-based rehearsal, situation display systems for enhancing situational awareness, structured debriefing using the STOP5 format, and role play or procedural practice. To reinforce learning, certain interventions employed "in-situ refreshers, educational newsletters, and visual reminders".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Simulation-trained faculty and clinical educators from emergency medicine, trauma, or critical care departments to lead delivery

Constraints¶

  • Training courses were predominantly short term and delivered within a single working day, with sessions lasting from 1.5 to 7 h.

Target Learners¶

  • Healthcare professionals working in emergency departments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Teamwork, communication, situational awareness, and leadership skills within emergency resuscitation teams

Claims¶

  • Similar behavioural domains improved across diverse organisational cultures in North America, Europe, and Asia, suggesting core NTS constructs are portable and adaptable locally. [+W]
  • Evidence that NTS training improves clinical or process outcomes in EDs is limited but generally favourable in single-centre studies, including shorter resuscitation times and improved protocol adherence (low to very low certainty). [+W]
  • NTS training for emergency department professionals is associated with consistent pre- to post-training improvements in knowledge, teamwork attitudes, and self-confidence (low to moderate certainty). [+W]
  • NTS training improves observable team behavioural performance in EDs, including more frequent closed-loop communication, clearer role allocation and prioritisation, improved situational awareness, and higher teamwork ratings (moderate certainty). [+W]
  • In one included study, simulation-based team training in a trauma bay was followed by a 16 percent reduction in total resuscitation time and faster completion of primary surveys. [+W]
  • Interventions combining core simulation with in-situ refreshers, visual prompts, and structured micro-debriefs more often reported larger or more durable behavioural improvements. [+W]
  • A team-focused, video-reviewed ED CPR bundle combining mechanical compression device training, in situ simulation, and multidisciplinary video review was followed by higher ROSC [+W]
  • After implementing collaboratively designed changes (emergency trolley, grab bags, orientation rules), the second in-situ simulation showed smoother, faster emergency care delivery [+W]
