# Institutionalize a multifaceted inclusion program combining acknowledging the dynamics, articulating guiding principles, and updating the framework of policies and rewards

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a coordinated, institutionalized program in which each component is essential and ineffective without the others: acknowledging the dynamics endorses the value of both in- and out-groups and lets them confront culture change together; articulating guiding principles is itself an act of inclusion, since without being explicitly written "the unacknowledged principles remain those of the in-group"; and updating the framework means revising policies and procedures governing admission, promotions, rewards and training programs to include all groups. The strategy draws on research suggesting the effectiveness of "inclusive multi-culturism" and "perspective training" to support engagement across viewpoints.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Engagement of those who build the culture of the institution — a partnership of faculty and administration
- Institutionalization so principles can be sustained as populations entering academia continue to evolve
#### Constraints
- None of the components will succeed in isolation; the article reports isolated initiatives can be ineffective or counterproductive
