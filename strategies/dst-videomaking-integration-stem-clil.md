# Integrate collaborative videomaking (DST) into STEM-A and CLIL courses as an authentic, student-centred literacy activity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends integrating DST within STEM-A as an educational technology that shifts focus from teachers' subject expertise to active inquiry and shared knowledge construction. The authors state the DST pedagogical approach "is a student-centred approach that provides a middle ground for shared knowledge construction between students, which also could be accommodated in CLIL programmes", and that its multimodal nature can create an authentic learning context for all students regardless of background. Implementation in this study involved group video production in English about astronomical phenomena within a 20-hour inquiry course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Well-planned integration of videomaking projects so curriculum time is not displaced
- Teacher training in DST and video-editing software (iMovie was used in this intervention)
#### Constraints
- Teachers remain cautious about DST because of curriculum and time constraints, per the article's literature review; the study's comparison cases differed in several respects, limiting interpretation

### Target Learners
- EFL students in extracurricular or school STEM courses
- Students in CLIL programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- Disciplinary literacy in English within STEM topics
- Collaborative knowledge construction and science communication
