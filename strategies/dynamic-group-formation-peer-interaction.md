# Use dynamic group formation so students interact with as many peers as possible across a semester

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's forward-looking recommendation is that "dynamic group formation strategies that give students an opportunity to interact with as many peers as possible throughout a semester" benefit students. In the studied course, small group membership was randomly selected and changed several times throughout the semester, with multiple rotations of seating assignments, so students interacted with classmates within and outside their small groups. This strategy is offered as a design lever connected to decreased physics anxiety.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A classroom structure that permits students to communicate and work with peers beyond a fixed small group
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from active-learning introductory physics sections using the Modeling Instruction curriculum

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in active-learning introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing physics anxiety through broader peer interaction
