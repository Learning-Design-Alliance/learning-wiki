Use repeated reading probes several grade levels below the student's grade level, with visible goals, extrinsic rewards, and daily progress charting¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the study's failure to transfer fluency gains to harder text, the author recommends that teachers using repeated reading "should try using probes that are several grade levels below the participant’s grade level," since the treatment text was at participants' true reading level. The author also reports that students charting their own fluency were intrinsically motivated but some needed extrinsic rewards, and recommends making goals well known and adding extrinsic rewards. Daily charting and frequent pre- and post-assessment are described as practices the author adopted after the study.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Passages at the student's instructional or true reading level, timed one-minute oral reads with partner error correction, and weekly-changing probes with Monday/Friday monitoring

Constraints¶

  • The study's fluency gains did not transfer to more difficult texts, and the author states results could possibly improve with lower-level probes but this was not tested

Target Learners¶

  • adolescent readers with learning disabilities reading below grade level

Target Learning Goals¶

  • reading fluency
  • reading comprehension

Claims¶

  • Repeated peer reading did not produce transferable fluency gains on the AIMSweb posttest for most struggling adolescent readers [-W]
  • Within the same passage, treatment students' fluency increased from Monday cold reads to Friday reads each week [+W]
  • Fluency gains from rereading follow the power law: improvement occurs up to about the fourth reading and then diminishes [+W]
