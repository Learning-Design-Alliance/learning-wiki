# Select FFI type strategically based on grammatical complexity and learner proficiency rather than using a one-size-fits-all approach

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that educators diversify FFI strategies: beginner and intermediate learners may receive input-based FFI to attend to morphological form-meaning mappings in smaller phrases (e.g., verb tenses, plural nouns), while more proficient learners can be given output-based FFI tasks (speaking or writing) that induce processing of word order in complex syntax (e.g., relative clauses). As the article states, "Rather than using the same FFI strategy in all circumstances, educators may diversify FFI strategies based upon characteristics of the target feature and the learner, thereby maximizing effectiveness."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of the target feature's complexity level (intra-phrasal, inter-phrasal, or clausal) and the learner's L2 English proficiency level
#### Constraints
- The article notes methodological limitations of the underlying studies, and recommends more controlled experimental research before theory is fully applied to practice

### Target Learners
- EFL learners at beginner to intermediate English proficiency levels

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate production of English grammatical features in speech and writing
