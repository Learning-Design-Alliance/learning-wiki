# Implement faculty development programs, continuous formative assessment, and curriculum reform to support student-centred learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The study concludes that institutions in resource-constrained medical education settings should pursue three connected moves: curriculum reform, continuous formative assessment, and faculty development programs. Educators in interviews "explicitly stated that their teaching methods and assessment approaches underwent noticeable changes after participating in the course", and the authors call for environments that "value pedagogical excellence and support innovative teaching practices". The strategy is grounded in the study's conclusion that student-centred approaches are recognised but blocked by institutional pressures.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty development programs that train educators in designing and implementing appropriate assessment tools, including correct use of OSCEs
#### Constraints
- Institutional pressure to produce high pass rates on centralised annual exams promotes rote learning and can undermine formative assessment
- Change is gradual and requires ongoing workshops; senior faculty resistance and junior faculty exclusion from workshops were reported

### Target Learners
- Medical and dental faculty educators
- Undergraduate medical and dental students

### Target Learning Goals
- Student-centred teaching
- Clinical competence and practical skills
- Professionalism and communication
