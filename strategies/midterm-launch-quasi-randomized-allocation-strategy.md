# Launch the e-learning platform at midterm of a semester-long course to create quasi-randomized access groups and reduce allocation bias

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because students were assigned to course dates by the university's medical education office for scheduling purposes and "evenly distributed over a whole semester without influence by the authors", launching the online platform and new curriculum at midterm created two quasi-randomized, single-blinded groups. The article states the main purpose of this randomization was "to prevent a selection of e-Learning-attracted students in the intervention group (allocation bias)". This is an implementable enrolment design for evaluating voluntary e-learning within a mandatory course.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Course cohorts scheduled across a semester by an office independent of the researchers
- Platform launch timed at the semester midpoint
#### Constraints
- The design yields quasi-randomization only; the authors note a control group showing superiority of the flipped classroom was missing

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students in scheduled practical courses

### Target Learning Goals
- fair evaluation of e-learning interventions
- reduction of allocation bias in curriculum studies
