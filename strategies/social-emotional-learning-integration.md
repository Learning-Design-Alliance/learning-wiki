# Social Emotional Learning Integration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Social Emotional Learning (SEL) Integration embeds the development of social-emotional competencies — self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, and responsible decision-making (CASEL's five domains) — into everyday academic instruction. Rather than delivering SEL as a stand-alone curriculum, teachers weave it into content teaching: structuring collaborative work to build relationship skills, framing tasks to support emotion regulation, and using academic discourse to practice perspective-taking.

## Design Implications

Integrated SEL improves both behavioral and academic outcomes, with meta-analytic evidence showing gains in achievement alongside social-emotional skills [SEL programs improve academic achievement and social-emotional skills.](../claims/sel-programs-improve-academic-achievement.md) [+S]. Integration works because competencies are practiced in authentic contexts rather than in decontextualized lessons, which supports transfer to real academic and interpersonal situations [~M]. However, effects depend on quality of implementation: SAFE features (sequenced, active, focused, explicit practice) predict outcomes, while poorly implemented or short-duration programs show weak effects [~S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit naming of the SEL competency being practiced within the academic task, so skills are not left implicit
- Classroom routines that create safe conditions for emotional and social risk-taking ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), norms for discussion)
- Teacher modeling of emotional and social skills ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) extended to emotion regulation and interpersonal reasoning)
- Sustained implementation — brief or one-off integration shows little durable effect [~M]

#### Constraints
- Stand-alone, lecture-style SEL lessons without authentic practice opportunities produce minimal behavior change [~M]
- Integration without academic substance — using SEL as a substitute for content instruction — erodes achievement and credibility [-M]
- Effects are weaker when teachers lack training or buy-in; mandated programs implemented without support show near-zero effects [~S]
- Cultural mismatch: SEL frameworks built on one cultural model of emotion and selfhood can misfire in other communities [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Embedded framing:** academic tasks framed with purpose and relevance (e.g., "we give peer feedback because critique is how writers improve")
- **Structured collaboration:** [Cooperative Learning](../patterns/cooperative-learning.md) roles that require perspective-taking and shared accountability
- **Emotion check-ins:** brief routines ([Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), mood meters as in RULER) that build self-awareness before academic work
- **Discipline-integrated practice:** role-play and debate in humanities; productive-failure norms in mathematics

### Target Learners
- K–12 students broadly, with strongest documented effects in elementary grades [SEL programs improve academic achievement and social-emotional skills.](../claims/sel-programs-improve-academic-achievement.md) [+S]
- Students at risk of disengagement; belonging-focused integration improves outcomes for marginalized students [Belonging interventions improve academic outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Adolescents benefit from autonomy-supportive rather than compliance-oriented framing [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [+S]

### Target Learning Goals
- Social-emotional competencies: emotion regulation, empathy, collaboration
- Academic behaviors: persistence, help-seeking, engagement with feedback
- Perspective-taking and intergroup understanding [Building empathy improves intergroup attitudes.](../claims/building-empathy-improves-intergroup-attitudes.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. Open with a brief emotional/relational [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) that connects to the day's content.
2. Frame the academic task with an explicit purpose linking content mastery to a competency ("today we practice disagreeing with ideas, not people").
3. Structure collaborative work with roles and norms that require relationship skills ([Cooperative Learning](../patterns/cooperative-learning.md)).
4. Model self-management and social awareness during instruction — narrate how you handle confusion, frustration, or disagreement.
5. Close with structured [Reflection](../elements/reflection.md) (e.g., [3-2-1 Reflection](3-2-1_reflection.md)) that includes both content and social-emotional processing.
6. Maintain routines over time; integration is a sustained practice, not a unit.

## Examples
- **[RULER](https://www.rulerapproach.org)** (Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence) — integrates emotion-labeling tools (mood meter, meta-moment) into classroom routines and academic work.
- **[Facing History and Ourselves](https://www.facinghistory.org)** — humanities curriculum that builds social awareness and ethical reasoning through historical case study.
- **Responsive Classroom** — morning meeting and academic choice routines that embed SEL into daily elementary instruction.
