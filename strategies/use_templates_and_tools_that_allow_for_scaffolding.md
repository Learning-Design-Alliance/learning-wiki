# Use Templates and Tools That Allow for Scaffolding

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
This strategy provides learners with structured supports — planning templates, graphic organizers, sentence frames, and structured tools — that externalize the organization of a complex task such as writing. The supports reduce the cognitive demands of managing structure and content simultaneously, and are progressively faded as learners internalize the underlying organizational schema.

## Design Implications

Templates work by offloading the structural demands of a task so working memory can be devoted to content and reasoning [Chunking reduces the load imposed on working memory.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]. Their effectiveness depends on contingent support: scaffolds calibrated to current learner performance improve learning, while support that is too heavy or too light does not [Scaffolding that is contingent on learner performance improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+S]. Critically, scaffolds must be faded — responsibility for structuring the task must transfer to the learner, and templates that are never removed can become a crutch that blocks independent performance [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility from scaffold to learner.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Templates and organizers matched to the genre or task structure (e.g., argument frames, story maps, lab-report templates)
- Explicit instruction in how and why to use each tool, not just distribution of the tool ([Provide Guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md))
- A plan for fading: moving from full templates to partial frames to independent composition
- Ongoing feedback on how learners are using the structure ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md))

#### Constraints
- Overly rigid templates can produce formulaic writing and discourage authentic voice; learners may satisfy the template without engaging the underlying rhetorical purpose [-M]
- If scaffolds are never faded, learners remain dependent on external structure and fail to transfer organizational skills to unsupported tasks [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility from scaffold to learner.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [-M]
- Learners with strong prior knowledge of a genre may find templates redundant and demotivating [~M]
- Tools without explicit instruction in their purpose are often used mechanically or ignored [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- Genre-specific templates (persuasive essay frames, lab reports, source-analysis grids) versus generic organizers
- Digital tools with embedded prompts (e.g., sentence starters, checklist sidebars) versus paper-based organizers
- Partial templates with deliberate gaps, requiring learners to generate the structural elements themselves as an intermediate fading step
- Collaborative completion of templates before individual use, shifting from co-construction to independence

### Target Learners
- Novice writers and reluctant writers who lack internalized organizational schemas [Scaffolding that is contingent on learner performance improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+S]
- Learners with limited working memory capacity for whom managing structure and content simultaneously is overwhelming [Chunking reduces the load imposed on working memory.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Less beneficial for proficient writers in the same genre, for whom templates impose unnecessary structure [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural knowledge of task and genre structure (how a genre is organized)
- Planning and revision skills in writing
- Gradual internalization of self-regulated composing strategies

### Instructions
1. Model completing the template on a shared example, thinking aloud about each structural decision ([Provide Guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md))
2. Have learners complete the same template on a parallel task ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
3. Provide feedback specifically on structural and organizational choices, not just content ([Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md))
4. Fade the scaffold: move to partial templates, then to planning independently, then to full [Whole-Task Performance](../elements/whole-task-performance.md) without the tool
5. Ask learners to self-explain how the template's structure served the task's purpose, supporting internalization of the schema [Self-explanation improves conceptual understanding.](../claims/self-explanation-improves-conceptual-understanding.md) [+M]

## Related Strategies
- [Modeling and Think-Alouds](../strategies/modeling-and-think-alouds.md) — demonstrating template use makes the reasoning behind the structure visible
- [Gradual Release of Responsibility](../strategies/gradual-release-of-responsibility.md) — the fading sequence (I do → we do → you do) is the mechanism by which templates build independence
- [Self-Regulated Strategy Development](../strategies/self-regulated-strategy-development.md) — SRSD embeds planning templates within explicit strategy instruction and self-monitoring

## Examples
- **SRSD (Self-Regulated Strategy Development)** — Graham and Harris's extensively validated approach uses mnemonic planning templates (e.g., POW+TREE for persuasive writing) that are explicitly taught, modeled, and faded as students internalize the strategy ([https://srsdonline.ca](https://srsdonline.ca))
- **The Writing Revolution (Hochman & Wexler)** — sentence-level templates and scaffolds (because-but-so frames, appositive templates) that build from sentence construction to full compositions ([https://www.thewritingrevolution.org](https://www.thewritingrevolution.org))
- **Google Docs outline templates / graphic organizer tools such as Popplet and Inspiration** — digital organizers that let learners rearrange and restructure ideas, supporting planning before drafting
