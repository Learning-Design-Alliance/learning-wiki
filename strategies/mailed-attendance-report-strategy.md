# Use low-cost mailed attendance reports to guardians as a scalable absence-reduction intervention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article identifies an "easy-to-implement, extremely cost-effective way to reduce absenteeism": mailings built from existing administrative data, sent five times per school year, telling guardians the number of days their student missed. It reports the intervention "cost around $6 per incremental day of attendance it generated", versus back-of-the-envelope estimates of $50-$100 for social workers and truancy officers based on RCT evaluations of NYC's Success Mentors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires only district administrative data (demographics, enrollment, guardian contact information, attendance data) rather than hiring, training, and managing attendance specialists
#### Constraints
- Effects were studied on students absent more than 2 days above the modal student in their school and grade the prior year, excluding students more than two SD above the mean, those with disability or homeless flags, and pilot participants

### Target Learners
- Students in grades 1-12 in a large urban district with high chronic absenteeism

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved school attendance as a foundation for course performance and reduced dropout risk
