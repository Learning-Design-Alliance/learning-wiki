# Rapid cycle deliberate practice: repeat the same simulation three times with instructor micro-debriefs, stopping and restarting when critical actions are omitted

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 design), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Rapid cycle deliberate practice (RCDP) is a simulation technique in which "learners repeatedly perform[ing] the same simulation with micro-debriefs interjected by the instructor." In this curriculum, the scenario runs once in full, then twice more with each participant cycling through each role; the facilitator stops the second and third repetitions when a critical action point is omitted, gives feedback, and restarts the case. Four short mini-lectures are delivered between repetitions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trained facilitator who has participated in and co-facilitated at least one session
- A facilitator packet with guide, scenario with critical action points, and teaching scripts
- Low-tech CPR-compatible mannequin, training code cart, pediatric mask and self-inflating bag, and stool
#### Constraints
- The article notes it could be difficult to schedule or implement a similar curriculum at other institutions, depending on staffing structures and educational practices

### Target Learners
- Multidisciplinary acute care hospital staff: registered nurses, attending physicians, advanced practice providers, and pediatric trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- Initiating high-quality CPR as a first responder
- Calling for help and checking responsiveness
- Airway, breathing, and circulation management in the first 3 minutes of a code
