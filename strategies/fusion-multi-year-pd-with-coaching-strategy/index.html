Provide multi-year professional development with instructional coaching before and during implementation of a comprehensive reading program¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes a multi-year professional development model for teachers implementing Fusion Reading, based on validated practices for professional learning and Partnership Instructional Coaching. Before classes began, "teachers were provided three days of PD" covering attributes of struggling readers, theoretical underpinnings, classroom routines, instructional methodology, grouping strategies, and materials, with additional PD days in the spring, summer contact with curriculum leaders, a full-day Year 2 review, and four further PD days during the second year. Once instruction began, extensive instructional coaching was provided to each FR teacher. Building and district-level administrators also received PD.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers identified before instruction is delivered
  • Multiple models of experts demonstrating strategy use, with practice teaching and feedback
  • Administrators responsible for curriculum and instruction included in PD

Constraints¶

  • PD was provided by the program developers in the context of a research study in one district

Target Learners¶

  • Sixth-grade special education teachers implementing the reading program

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Fidelity of implementation of reading strategies and lesson routines

Claims¶

  • In an underpowered randomized study, Fusion Reading students gained significantly more than Second Chance Reading controls on the GRADE composite comprehension score (Hedges's d = 0.66–0.70) [+W]
  • Middle school students with reading disabilities taught Fusion Reading significantly outperformed matched comparison students on the overall GRADE reading measure after Year 1 (Hedges's g = 1.66) [+M]
  • In a large RCT (n = 871), Fusion Reading produced a small significant effect on word reading efficiency (Glass Δ = 0.11) but no significant differences on reading comprehension measures [+M]
  • In a single-group pre-/posttest study of 266 students with IEPs, Fusion Reading produced statistically significant gains on the TOSCRF (d = 0.55) and a mean grade-level gain of 0.92 after about 4 months [+W]
