# Produce microlearning videos via a three-step scripted workflow — scripting, separate audio and video recording, then editing — accepting that scripting one 15-minute video can take up to a day

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article prescribes a three-step production process for short videos covering "the essential aspects in just a few minutes": scripting, separate audio and video recording with cross-checking, and editing. Scripting is described as "the major accomplishment in improving the videos compared to recordings of a free talk", enabling avoidance of filler words and anticipation of misunderstandings, while separate audio and video scripts let the recorder focus on one task and editing allows pacing adjustment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A team of about five people, with scripting done by at least two people to discuss wording and sketch arrangement, and separate recorders for audio and video who cross-check each other
#### Constraints
- Scripting one video of about 15 minutes can take up to one day, making it the most time-consuming production step, and editing time highly depends on the video's contents

### Target Learners
- first- and second-semester engineering and STEM students in an introductory university physics course

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding of essential aspects of classical mechanics through solid-state physics topics covered in short self-learning videos
