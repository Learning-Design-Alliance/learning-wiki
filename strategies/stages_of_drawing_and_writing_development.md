# Stages of Drawing and Writing Development

## Description
Children progress through stages of drawing and writing development, from random scribbling to controlled scribbling, lines and patterns, pictures of objects or people, and letter and word practice. Art and early writing skills are one and the same. Children discover the link between their hand holding the crayon and the line they made on the page, experiencing the power of cause-and-effect. As children develop better control over the muscles in their hands and fingers, their scribbles become more controlled.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires art and writing materials such as crayons, markers, and paper. Requires time for children to experiment and explore.
#### Constraints
- The timetables for each stage are approximate, and children may master these skills faster or slower.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Understand the stages of drawing and writing development. Encourage children's artistic and writing skills through experimentation and exploration.

### Affordances
- Children learn that their movements result in lines and scribbles on the page. They develop better control over the muscles in their hands and fingers. They understand that writing is made up of lines, curves, and repeated patterns. They realize that their drawing conveys meaning.

### Personalization
- Children can progress through the stages at their own pace. Teachers can offer repeated fun experiences with a variety of art and writing materials.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe children's progress through the stages of drawing and writing development. Evaluate the complexity and control in their drawings and writings. Notice their ability to represent objects and people and use letters and words.

## Impact
- Art experiences help children develop independence within limits, and gives them the opportunity to represent their ideas on paper or in other formats. Creative expression lets children tap into the magic of their own imaginations.
