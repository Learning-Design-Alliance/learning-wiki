# Use in-situ simulation within a PDSA cycle to gather real-time user feedback and iteratively refine a new clinical protocol

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends running in-situ simulation sessions as the testing step of a Plan-Do-Study-Act cycle: after each session, facilitators solicit feedback from participants and observers in real time, plus emailed feedback from clinical use, and the authorship group uses it to produce subsequent protocol iterations. The authors state that "an airway algorithm can be developed and refined in real time based on user feedback and rapidly disseminated to ED providers." During the study period this process produced 12 versions of the algorithm, tracked in a Change Log.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A mechanism to collect feedback from participants and observers in real time and via email
- An authorship or leadership group to review feedback and issue updated versions
- A repository (Box) where updated guidelines are posted for learners
#### Constraints
- Ever-changing consensus recommendations in a new disease required maximal flexibility and adaptability
- The recommendations were best-practice recommendations, as limited evidence supported a definitive management algorithm during the pandemic

### Target Learners
- Emergency department physicians, nurses, and respiratory therapists

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe implementation of a new high-risk clinical protocol
- Continuous process improvement of clinical guidelines
