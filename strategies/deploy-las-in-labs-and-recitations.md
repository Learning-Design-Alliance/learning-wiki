# Deploy learning assistants in environments conducive to small-group collaborative work, such as labs and recitations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because LA-associated gains varied by classroom context, the authors recommend placing LAs where small-group collaboration is structurally easiest. They state their findings "lead us to recommend using LAs in environments that are more conducive to small group collaborative work, such as labs and recitation." For instructors who use LAs in lecture, they recommend transforming the lecture environment to better support small-group collaborative activities, such as changing seating arrangements so LAs and instructors can interact with student groups more easily.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An LA program must be in place with LAs assigned to specific course contexts (lab, recitation, or lecture), and instructors must be able to shape the classroom environment to support small-group work.
#### Constraints
- The study is exploratory and cannot definitively identify the cause of higher outcomes in LA courses; differences in instructors or institutions may affect student outcomes.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students in introductory physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- increase conceptual learning gains in introductory physics measured by the FCI or FMCE
