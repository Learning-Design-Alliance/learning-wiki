# Fishbowl Discussion

## Description
Fishbowl is a strategy for facilitating group discussions. In a Fishbowl discussion, students inside the 'fishbowl' actively discuss a topic. Students outside the fishbowl listen carefully to the conversation. They take turns in these roles to practice being both contributors and listeners in a group discussion. A Fishbowl activity is especially useful when you want to make sure all students participate in a discussion, when you want to help students reflect on what a good discussion looks like, and when you need a structure for discussing controversial or difficult topics. A Fishbowl discussion makes for an excellent pre-writing activity, often unearthing questions or ideas that students can explore more deeply in an independent assignment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A circle of chairs ('the fishbowl') and enough room around the circle for the remaining students to observe. Effective prompts that allow for multiple perspectives and opinions. Prior preparation for the discussion. Established norms and rules for participation.
#### Constraints
- Requires careful setup and clear explanation of rules to ensure all students understand the process. Some students may feel uncomfortable in the 'fishbowl' or struggle to actively listen when observing.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Suitable for students in grades 6–12, advisory, civics and citizenship, English and language arts, history, and social studies. Useful in diverse educational settings to ensure all students participate and reflect on effective discussion.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims: [[claims/claim-slug]] -->
- Encourage active participation, improve listening skills, promote reflection on discussion dynamics, and provide a structure for discussing controversial topics.

### Affordances
- Provides a structure for discussions that encourages both contribution and active listening. Allows students to practice being both speakers and observers. Facilitates the discussion of complex and controversial topics. Serves as an excellent pre-writing activity, stimulating ideas and questions.

### Personalization
- Variations include a Fishbowl Debate for opposing positions, allowing each group to discuss while the other observes, and a Fishbowl Exercise for multiple perspectives, assigning different viewpoints to groups for discussion.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the level of student participation and engagement. Evaluate the quality of contributions and active listening. Assess the ability to follow norms and rules. Gather feedback on the effectiveness of the discussion and individual performance.

## Impact
- Encourages active participation, careful listening, and meaningful reflection. Helps students practice being both contributors and listeners in a group conversation. Promotes deeper understanding and empathy.
