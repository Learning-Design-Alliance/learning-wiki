# Balance virtual simulation training with sufficient hands-on technical skills practice time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that virtual simulation training be combined with technical skills training while protecting hands-on practice time. After finding lower technical skills scores in the VR group, they state that "in the future training, the skills training time for the virtual simulation group will be added four more hours to the same level as that of the control group", and that "more attention should be brought to ensure the balance between virtual training and conventional training".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient technical skills training hours equivalent to conventional training, alongside virtual simulation sessions
#### Constraints
- The strategy responds to a finding where the intervention group's skills training time was 4 hours less than the control group's; the article frames it as a future adjustment, not a tested manipulation

### Target Learners
- emergency reserve nurses
- nursing trainees in simulation-based programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- technical nursing skills such as donning and doffing PPE, respiratory tract sampling and mechanical ventilation
