# Integrate AI- and technology-related communication skills systematically into medical curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that digital communication competencies receive systematic curricular attention, noting that students "reported limited confidence in digital aspects of interaction, suggesting a gap between emerging professional demands and current training." They cite a growing body of literature advocating early integration of AI-related competencies within medical education, and their pilot suggests blended learning with AI-related modules can address this gap.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Dedicated course content addressing digital health applications, genAI tools, and communication with informed patients
#### Constraints
- Evidence is exploratory and hypothesis-generating given the small pilot sample

### Target Learners
- Medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- AI-related counselling competencies
- Engagement with informed, technology-using patients
