# Sequence a galaxy-simulation project from 2D elementary examples toward 3D replication of real interacting galaxies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article recommends a project sequence for high-school settings: students first program a working two-dimensional orbit simulator, then replicate Toomre & Toomre's two-dimensional "Four Elementary Examples", optionally add the velocity Verlet algorithm with a discussion of numerical stability, and only then make "the transition from a two-dimensional to a three-dimensional simulation". The authors note the hardest aspect of the 3D step is describing the geometry in terms of standard orbital elements, for which they found Rodrigues' rotation formula a helpful short-cut.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need solid interest and commitment, high-school physics and maths concepts, and appropriate programming skills; basic linear algebra is described as a big plus for the optimization strategy.
#### Constraints
- In the form presented, the project requires a considerable amount of time; the most difficult aspect of the 3D transition is spatial thinking about orbital-element geometry.

### Target Learners
- advanced high school students
- students transitioning from high school to university

### Target Learning Goals
- numerical integration
- orbital mechanics
- programming
- understanding tidal interactions between galaxies
