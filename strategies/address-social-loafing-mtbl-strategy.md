# Address unequal contribution in peer-evaluation-free team-based learning by assessing individual contributions and establishing group rules that create psychological safety

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because M-TBL drops peer evaluation, students reported a sense of unfairness when some members did not contribute. The authors recommend a more robust evaluation system that assesses the contributions of each member and the group's overall work, possibly using ICT to limit added faculty workload, and creating psychological safety in group work—for example, teachers can announce rules before class and require students to pledge compliance, such as allowing members to express ideas freely and not divulging members' ideas outside the group.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty capacity to assess individual member contributions alongside group output
- Teacher announcement of group rules and student pledge of compliance before class begins
#### Constraints
- The individual-contribution assessment strategy may increase faculty workload, which the authors propose offsetting by integrating digital technology into the evaluation system

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate nursing students working in small groups

### Target Learning Goals
- Teamwork and equitable participation in team-based group work
