# Use HRA-derived task analyses in surgical education and simulation-based training to guide trainees toward error-prone and high-criticality steps

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that a standardised protocol assigning risk to subtasks be used in training and assessment: it "can guide trainees to improve their operative skills and can address concerns in allowing trainees to perform technically challenging steps, while enhancing patient safety by reducing errors." The framework shows trainees the sequential tasks required, highlights steps prone to error and alerts them to high-criticality steps. The article reports the framework has potential to aid skill acquisition and error reduction in simulation-based training.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An optimum technique for the procedure must be established through HTA, and internal agreement reached at each unit to prevent large variations in practice being taught to trainees
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that applying HRA to emergency cases may add further burden to an already time-sensitive procedure and that variation in clinical practice requires local refinement of the HTA

### Target Learners
- trainee surgeons in the early phase of learning an elective procedure

### Target Learning Goals
- operative skill acquisition
- error awareness and risk-informed practice of procedural steps
