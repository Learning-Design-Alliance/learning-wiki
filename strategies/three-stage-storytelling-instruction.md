# Teach a storybook vocabulary lesson in three stages: pre-storytelling, during-storytelling, and post-storytelling

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's instructional procedure organizes storybook teaching into three sequential stages. In the pre-storytelling stage children were motivated to listen and new words were introduced "by the help of some flashcards"; during-storytelling the teacher read the story with pictures and questions; post-storytelling generalized new knowledge to children's real lives through questions about their lives. Each of the first two stages ran three one-hour sessions and the last ran two sessions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A storybook chosen for the class (here Three Bears), a teacher who reads aloud, and picture supports such as flashcards, since the children could not read or write
#### Constraints
- The article tested this procedure over only six one-hour sessions with one class of 20 children, and states a longer duration may have provided different results

### Target Learners
- five-year-old kindergarten children learning English as a foreign language

### Target Learning Goals
- English vocabulary learning through story context
