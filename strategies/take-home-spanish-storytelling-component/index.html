Strengthen Spanish language promotion at home via a take-home storytelling component¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors suggest that because classroom use of Spanish vocabulary was limited, an alternative "would be to promote Spanish skills at home by providing parents/caregivers with a list of words or materials to help them emphasize the words at home." Parents already reported high engagement with the take-home storytelling sheets (average rating 4.3 on a 0–5 scale) and enjoyed the activities, suggesting a stronger take-home component is worth exploring.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Take-home activity sheets with the featured story, illustrations, and suggestions for parent engagement

Constraints¶

  • Parent engagement with take-home activities was not tracked during the study, and the classroom provided limited Spanish instruction and conversation

Target Learners¶

  • Spanish-speaking preschool dual language learners and their families

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Spanish vocabulary knowledge
  • Spanish language maintenance at home

Claims¶

  • Dual language narrative intervention produced a large English vocabulary gain (d = .98) but only a small Spanish vocabulary gain (d = .34) [+W]
  • Parents reported high engagement with and enjoyment of the take-home storytelling activities [+W]
  • Children maintained already high Spanish retelling skills during intervention, with no change in Spanish retells [~W]
