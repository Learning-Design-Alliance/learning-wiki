# Select, train and supervise peer tutors with attention to stressors and protective traits

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their findings, the authors recommend that programme staff actively manage tutor burden: "curriculum designers and staff responsible for peer teaching programmes should mind the potential burdens of tutoring and, if necessary, make efforts to mitigate stress and initiate adequate prevention strategies". Concretely, they prepare tutors in medical-technical and leadership skills, use supervision to address frequent stressors, and recruit new tutors using extraversion and general resilience as selection criteria.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of the stressors frequently encountered by peer teachers, as documented by the study's content analyses
#### Constraints
- Differences in stress levels exist depending on course type and tutor experience; other programmes may impose different demands

### Target Learners
- medical student near-peer tutors

### Target Learning Goals
- coping with teaching demands
- tutor role skills
- stress prevention
