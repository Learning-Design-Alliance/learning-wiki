Research directions: scaffold reasoning without displacing epistemic agency and rethink curricula to preserve core human capabilities¶

Strategy · All strategies Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

If the observed reversal reflects genuine cognitive reappraisal, the article recommends research directions at the intersection of human-centered AI, education, and human–AI co-learning. These include "designing interfaces that scaffold reasoning without displacing epistemic agency" and "rethinking curricula to explicitly preserve and assess core human capabilities such as judgment, synthesis, and moral reasoning under pervasive AI assistance," as well as developing hybrid systems that expose structure, rationale, and uncertainty.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Hybrid human–AI systems that expose structure, rationale, and uncertainty to support human meaning-making

Constraints¶

  • The recommendations are conditional on the observed reversal reflecting a genuine cognitive reappraisal

Target Learners¶

  • Learners in AI-assisted educational settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Judgment, synthesis, and moral reasoning under pervasive AI assistance
  • Human oversight and reliance calibration

Related Strategies¶

  • AI-assisted learning systems should scaffold cognitive development rather than replace it, and require active student contribution to preserve ownership
  • Guide learners toward the Trust Phase while actively preventing a slide into Dependency
  • Design curricula giving students opportunities to critically engage with AI and AI-generated content through validation, interpretation, and revision tasks
  • Ground AI integration in higher education on core principles of human agency, academic freedom, transparency, ethics, inclusivity, and critical thinking
  • Build educational AI with pedagogical guardrails such as withholding direct solutions and embedded reflection steps
  • Deploy AI-assisted classroom observation as a complementary first-pass screening and reflection tool, with raters retaining interpretive judgment
