# Use light propagation in a glass rod as a blown-up fiber model to teach acceptance angle, numerical aperture, and Rayleigh scattering

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article finds it "a very helpful demonstration to show a blown-up version of fiber optics: light propagation in a glass rod." By varying the incident angle of a laser entering the rod, the teacher shows which rays are confined by total internal reflection and which escape, then introduces "acceptance angle" and "numerical aperture (NA)". The same demo motivates Rayleigh scattering, which the article says "accounts for about 96% of attenuation in the optical fiber."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A glass rod and a laser light source; students should already know geometric optics concepts of reflection and refraction
#### Constraints
- The demonstration addresses light confinement and loss mechanisms conceptually; it does not reproduce the sub-millimeter scale of actual fiber

### Target Learners
- college students in a fiber optics course

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding acceptance angle and numerical aperture
- understanding Rayleigh scattering as a fiber loss mechanism
