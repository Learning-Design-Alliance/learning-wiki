# Teaching sequence: model lens, reconstruct Einstein's derivation, then consolidate by contrasting model with geometric modeling

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article's project ran in three 45-minute lessons. In the first, "students were introduced to the geometry of gravitational lensing using a model gravitational lens made from glass, which looks like the foot of a wine-glass". In the second, students used Einstein's notes to deduce the lensing equation and compute and interpret its solutions. The third lesson consolidated understanding by contrasting the model lens with the geometric modeling, for example investigating the dependency of the Einstein radius on observer–lens distance by looking at a candle through the model lens.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Familiarity with geometrical optics, Euclidean geometry and trigonometry with small-angle approximation, and quadratic-equation manipulation
#### Constraints
- The more ambitious extension to the magnification derivation requires basic knowledge of infinitesimal calculus, solid angles, and the notion of brightness and geometric light amplification

### Target Learners
- twelfth-grade high school physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- deriving and solving the lensing equation
- interpreting the Einstein ring geometrically
