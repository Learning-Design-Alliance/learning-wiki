# Use index-card games as follow-up activities to consolidate and retain new vocabulary

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends periodic follow-up games with learner-made vocabulary cards: every three sessions the researcher played a game in which groups laid their cards word-side up and held up the matching card when a definition was called out. The article states that "Taking advantage of follow up activities such as index card games is considered as a useful tool which may be beneficial in the English language learning classes and can make the lessons more interesting and enjoyable". It cites Decarrico (2001) that words should not be learnt by memorization without understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners first record selected words, definitions and sentences on index cards
- Periodic game sessions (every three sessions in the study)
#### Constraints
- Only the Vocabulary Self-Selection group received index cards and games in this study; the Input Enhancement group did not

### Target Learners
- pre-intermediate EFL learners in classroom groups

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of word meanings
