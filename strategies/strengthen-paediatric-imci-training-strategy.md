# Strengthen paediatric and IMCI training, clinical reasoning instruction, and community-level education for clinicians managing febrile children

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends building clinician capability through strengthened paediatric training and familiarity with IMCI guidelines, plus training in clinical reasoning and critical thinking, because greater training and experience correlated with comfort observing likely viral illness without antibiotics. It also endorses clinician-led community education, outreach, and mobile education modules to improve caregiver recognition of fever and timely care-seeking. As the authors state, the training-experience correlation "makes a case for strengthening paediatric training and familiarity with guidelines such as the IMCI."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clinicians must have access to training despite time constraints, distance, and understaffing, which the article identifies as current barriers to CME attendance
#### Constraints
- The article notes adherence to IMCI guidelines in low-resource settings is poor in prior studies and it is not proven that implementing these guidelines impacts mortality; further studies to prove efficacy are called for

### Target Learners
- clinical officers
- nurses
- medical officers
- paediatricians in malaria-endemic low-resource settings

### Target Learning Goals
- differentiating viral from bacterial febrile illness
- appropriate antibiotic prescribing
- recognizing paediatric danger signs
- applying IMCI guidelines
