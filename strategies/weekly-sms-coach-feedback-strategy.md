# Weekly semi-structured coach feedback via SMS to support engagement in web-based adolescent interventions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
In Youth COMPASS, each participant had a personally assigned coach — an acceptance-and-commitment-trained undergraduate psychology student with 18 hours of ACT training and weekly supervision — who checked program completion on the platform and then sent weekly SMS feedback comprising "three semi-structured questions": a well-being check, a mood rating on the familiar school 4–10 grading scale, and a module-specific prompt (e.g., "What is important to you?"). Non-replies triggered a structured reminder escalation: a reminder SMS, a re-send after one day, a fifth-day message, and finally a phone call. This low-cost support structure kept weekly contact without requiring participants to attend meetings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coaches with ACT training and access to supervision
- Access to the program platform to verify exercise completion before giving feedback
#### Constraints
- Coaches were undergraduate psychology students with limited prior experience (48% reported little prior experience using ACT programs)

### Target Learners
- Ninth-grade adolescents using web- or mobile-delivered programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Program adherence and engagement
- Self-monitoring of mood and well-being
