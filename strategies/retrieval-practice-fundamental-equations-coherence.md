# Target retrieval practice at fundamental equations and prompt coherence-seeking rather than rote recall of complex formulas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes that retrieval practice in physics instruction be aimed at recall of fundamental equations (e.g., Q=CV and V=Ed) that ground justifications for qualitative dependencies, rather than prescribing retrieval of complex expressions like the RC time constant, which "may discourage the use of the desirable problem-solving skills." It further suggests "retrieval practice that prompts looking for coherence between an equation and other ideas – including connections with other equations, physical models, or specific examples - may help students recall coherence as a skill."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students need recall of fundamental equations to serve as starting points for mathematical sensemaking
#### Constraints
- Prescribing retrieval practice of the RC time constant expression may discourage the use of the desirable problem-solving skills, like the coherence-seeking and mathematical sensemaking shown in the results

### Target Learners
- undergraduate physics students in introductory E&M

### Target Learning Goals
- recall of fundamental physics equations
- spontaneous use of mathematical sensemaking and coherence-seeking in problem solving

### Affordances
- Coherence Seeking Adaptive Physics Framework
