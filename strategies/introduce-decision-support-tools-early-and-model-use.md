# Introduce integrated decision-support tools early in clinical training and train supervisors to model their use

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend introducing PACK "earlier when students are first exposed to clinical primary care and not only in the final phase", and that "supervisors should be trained as educators to model use of the PACK in their own clinical practice". This pairs early curricular placement with supervisor role-modelling so students feel comfortable consulting evidence openly during consultations.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Supervisor training workshops so family physicians can incorporate the tool into practice and educational conversations
#### Constraints
- Initial familiarisation with the structure and content of the tool may be better using the paper version, even though PACK is now available electronically

### Target Learners
- medical students in early clinical rotations
- family physician supervisors and clinical tutors

### Target Learning Goals
- evidence-informed primary care decision-making
- lifelong learning and openness to changing practice in light of new evidence

### Affordances
- Four Stages Of Competence Model
