Strategies

Combine traditional explicit vocabulary instruction with subtitled authentic video viewing to serve both short- and long-term retention¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Because the study found traditional instruction superior for short-term retention and subtitled-video viewing superior for long-term retention, the authors recommend that "different methods f leaning vocabulary should be adopted" to achieve better retention in both periods. The strategy pairs explicit presentation techniques with incidental learning through subtitled sitcom viewing rather than treating either as a substitute.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Access to authentic video clips, e.g. through language centers using electronic equipment for displaying video clips

Constraints¶

  • The article states watching subtitled movies is not a substitute for traditional vocabulary exercises; some genres such as horror movies cause frustration in less experienced learners

Target Learners¶

  • advanced EFL learners at language institutes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • short-term and long-term retention of vocabulary

Claims¶

  • Subtitled-video viewing outperformed traditional instruction on long-term vocabulary retention at a three-week delay [+M]
  • Traditional explicit vocabulary instruction outperformed subtitled-video viewing on short-term retention in an 8-week EFL vocabulary course [+M]
  • Individual control-group learners showed sharp declines in vocabulary scores from post-test to delayed post-test [+W]
  • Experimental-group learners maintained nearly the same vocabulary scores across the two test administrations [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use pre-task subtitled videos of similar (not identical) tasks to build familiarity while preserving cognitive complexity
