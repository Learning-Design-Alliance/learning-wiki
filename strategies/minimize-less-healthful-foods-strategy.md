# Nutrition recommendations for school-age children should emphasize minimizing less healthful foods, not only encouraging healthful foods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 4 studies (3 associational, 1 review), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors propose that school food policy and nutrition messaging should target limiting sweet snacks, salty snacks, and sugar-sweetened beverages alongside promoting healthful foods, because their data showed only the less healthful group was associated with lower achievement. They write that recommendations "should focus not only on encouraging healthful food group intake but also on minimizing foods that are less healthful". This is a discussion-level recommendation from cross-sectional associations, not a tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cross-sectional association only; the recommendation would need intervention testing to establish effects on achievement.
#### Constraints
- Based on observational associations in lower-income Massachusetts schoolchildren; authors state results "may not be generalizable to broader populations".

### Target Learners
- elementary schoolchildren (grades 3–4, ages 8–10)

### Target Learning Goals
- academic achievement (standardized math and English test performance)
