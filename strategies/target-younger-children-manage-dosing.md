# Target serious game interventions at parents of younger children and manage information dosing by reducing in-game text volume

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that cognitive acceptability was significantly higher for parents of young children, the authors recommend that "the game may be better suited as a preparatory tool ahead of the imminent need for such conversations to build earlier family communication". They also recommend that "the overall length of the game be shortened by reducing the amount of in-game text alongside managing user expectations about realistic time commitment".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Game content must remain acceptable on cognitive and emotional dimensions
#### Constraints
- Attrition was higher in the game condition, potentially due to feedback text volume; reasons for lower acceptability among parents of older children are unclear

### Target Learners
- Parents of younger children (aged 5–9)

### Target Learning Goals
- Sustain engagement and support parents' sex and relationships communication
