# Future experiential learning interventions should apply self-efficacy-enhancing strategies such as self-regulation development, social support and playful motor-creativity activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the attitudinal and knowledge findings, the review recommends that "Future experiential learning interventions aiming to improve physical activity-related attitudes and knowledge amongst children may look to apply self-efficacy-enhancing strategies such as improving children's self-regulation capabilities, and social support and enhancing children's motor creativity through playful activities". The effective studies used critical thinking and problem-solving to build self-regulation, and small groups, pairs and positive feedback as social support.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Interventions must include strategies targeting self-efficacy, such as self-regulation development and social support, delivered within experiential learning activities
#### Constraints
- The recommendation rests on only two attitudinal studies showing medium effects and one knowledge study; the review notes it is difficult to disentangle the necessary components of an effective intervention

### Target Learners
- primary school-aged children (6–12 years)

### Target Learning Goals
- physical activity attitudes and self-efficacy
- physical activity knowledge
