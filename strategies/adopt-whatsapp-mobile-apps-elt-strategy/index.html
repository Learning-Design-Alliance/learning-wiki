Adopt mobile messaging applications, especially WhatsApp, across English-language teaching¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 11 claims (8 for, 3 mixed) · 7 studies (3 causal, 3 review, 1 theoretical), q1–q2 · 0 of 7 report an effect size · 11 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article closes with recommendations to integrate mobile applications into English teaching: "Encouraging students to use more mobile applications in learning English", making mobiles part of the teaching process for communication and home assignments, and "Adopting WhatsApp application in teaching other skills like listening and speakingthrough exchanging files between students and teachers".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Students must own smartphones and already be accustomed to the application, as the surveyed students were.

Constraints¶

  • The recommendations are drawn from one small quasi-experiment limited to writing skills at a single Saudi private college.

Target Learners¶

  • university-level EFL students

Target Learning Goals¶

  • English language skills including listening and speaking

Claims¶

  • EFL students taught writing through a WhatsApp group outperformed textbook-taught controls on a punctuation post-test [+W]
  • EFL students taught writing through a WhatsApp group outperformed textbook-taught controls on a sentence-structure post-test [+W]
  • The WhatsApp experimental group improved significantly from pre-test to post-test on the overall writing measure [+W]
  • The WhatsApp experimental group improved significantly from pre-test to post-test in punctuation [+W]
  • Learners showed positive attitudes toward receiving SMS vocabulary messages, and instant messaging raised intercultural awareness [+W]
  • WhatsApp-based mobile instant messaging projects enhanced communicative skills and vocabulary knowledge [+W]
  • In one 30-student study of high-school English learners (Lu 2008, reported in a review), vocabulary learned by mobile phone showed greater gains than vocabulary learned from print [+W]
  • The experimental group learning idioms via the mobile application outside classroom hours achieved significant within-group gains [+W]
  • WhatsApp's most cited benefit is enabling learning anytime and anywhere, but intrusiveness, message volume and cognitive load impede learning [~W]
  • Mobile devices make education more accessible anytime and anywhere, but this attractiveness comes with limitations for adoption and usage [~W]
  • Interviewed students reported increased motivation and learning support from mobile learning, alongside technical problems [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use micro-modules pairing short audio lessons with reflection texts and interactive quizzes for adult ELL mobile learning
  • Use WhatsApp as a supportive technology alongside traditional instruction, with careful attention to message timing
