# Explicitly research and articulate the conditions under which research-based curricula transfer to new environments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 mixed) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
A dissemination strategy in which curriculum developers test materials beyond the development site and make transfer conditions explicit for adopting faculty. The authors note that "it is not uncommon for curriculum to be produced and disseminated that has not been tested in contexts beyond the environment in which it was developed," and recommend testing in fundamentally different environments, specifying what transfers and under what conditions, and giving modification guidance for different contexts.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Test and refine curriculum in environments fundamentally different from the development site
- Make explicit what aspects of the curriculum will transfer and under what conditions transfer will be successful
- Provide recommendations for modifications in different contexts, such as different sized classes or schools with less prepared students
- Articulate why some aspects transfer better than others to guide instructor modifications
#### Constraints
- Most research-based curricula have been developed at research universities or elite liberal arts colleges and do not always transfer directly to other environments

### Target Learners
- college physics faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- successful transfer of research-based instructional innovations to new institutional contexts
