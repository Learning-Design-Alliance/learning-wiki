Educators should adopt applicable feedback delivery strategies when giving feedback in digital learning environments¶

Description¶

The article's stated purpose is to "offer applicable feedback delivery strategies for educators as an extension of Borup et al.'s work". It frames feedback delivery as something educators can act on strategically in digital learning contexts, extending prior research on instructor and learner perceptions of feedback. The available text does not print the individual strategies themselves.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Educators must apply the delivery strategies in digital or blended learning contexts where feedback is given.

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • educators giving feedback in digital learning environments

Target Learning Goals¶

  • effective and affective feedback delivery

Claims¶

  • In Borup et al.'s (2015) blended-course study, students and instructors perceived text and video feedback as equally valuable on quality and delivery, but valued text's efficiency over video's affective benefits [+W]
  • Borup et al. (2015) found video feedback more convenient for student support and text-based feedback more convenient for exploratory feedback [+W]
  • Research has shown that video feedback can help increase conversational and affective communication [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Apply Borup et al.'s video-versus-text feedback research when designing feedback in colleges shifting to digital
  • Use video-based feedback to provide socio-emotional support and social presence in remote learning
