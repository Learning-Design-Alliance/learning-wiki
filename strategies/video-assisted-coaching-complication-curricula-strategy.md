# Incorporate structured video-assisted coaching and complication-management exercises into surgical training curricula

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that surgical training move beyond the Halsted master–apprentice model by adding structured video-assisted coaching — a detailed live video instruction on hemostasis techniques given between practice sequences — and by training complication management, since "skills during stressful emergency situations are rarely trained." The authors state the findings "support the incorporation of structured video-assisted coaching and complication management exercises into surgical training curricula."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured instruction videos on hemostasis techniques; trainers previously trained in the exercises (train the trainers); recorded practice sequences for video-based assessment
#### Constraints
- One-to-one coaching is time consuming and costly; the study timeframe was too short to show autonomy gains

### Target Learners
- surgical residents and advanced trainees

### Target Learning Goals
- laparoscopic skill acquisition and emergency complication (bleeding) management
