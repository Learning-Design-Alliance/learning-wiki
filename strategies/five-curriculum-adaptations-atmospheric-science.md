# Reform curricula through five adaptations: computation, projects with real data, cross-sector fluency, communication training, and community leadership

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A set of five institutional adaptations the authors propose for training the next generation: building computation into required curriculum, embracing project-based learning with real data and models, fostering cross-disciplinary and cross-sector fluency, teaching communication and translation purposefully, and encouraging community conversations and leadership. On projects, "curricula should incorporate projects that integrate hands-on observation activities, use the comprehensive, publicly available datasets, and leverage user-friendly physical and AI models." The recommendations build on existing AMS statements and community documents.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional structural support: hiring faculty with public-private experience, tenure credit for AI-inclusive pedagogy, and funded shared curriculum infrastructure
#### Constraints
- The authors offer these as an invitation rather than a prescription, note curriculum reform is downstream of hiring and incentives, and acknowledge the education pipeline itself is under strain

### Target Learners
- Atmospheric science departments and educators
- Students who can request these changes from their departments

### Target Learning Goals
- Science communication and translation of uncertainty into risk
- Computational and statistical fluency with stated learning outcomes
- Evaluating model and forecast quality from diverse sources
