# Use simulator-based prospective studies with expert video rating to evaluate telemedical support of first responders

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article evaluates telemedical support by randomising teams by lot to supported and unsupported conditions in a validated simulated scenario, video-recording the scenario from multiple perspectives, and having two experts adjudicate a 17-item checklist and scenario duration. Inter-rater reliability was estimated with intraclass correlation coefficients. This design resolved hypothesised group differences the authors considered adequate for the purpose.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A validated scenario, a checklist operationalising ERC guidelines, and video recordings adjudicated by expert raters
#### Constraints
- The authors note generalisability of the findings may be limited and that their own choice of scenario, metric and validation efforts may be biassed

### Target Learners
- medical non-professionals in remote workplaces
- emergency-care researchers

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluating quality of emergency first response
- measuring guideline adherence and required time
