# Place strong emphasis on group work and student-student and teacher-student dialogue in early childhood science education

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that talking drawings improved mental models, the article recommends that "a great emphasis be placed on group work in early childhood science education". The technique is built on both student-student and teacher-student dialogues, and the author cites Vo et al. (2015) that small group work promotes social interaction when learning complex structures like the water cycle. The strategy is implementable through paired drawing discussions and whole-class sharing of differences between first and second drawings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A topic suitable for drawing and discussion, plus source books and visual materials
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from one semi-experimental study in a single Turkish preschool; the author calls for future research in various atmospheres and climates to define external variables

### Target Learners
- preschool and early childhood learners

### Target Learning Goals
- learning complex science constructs such as the water cycle
- developing speaking and listening skills
