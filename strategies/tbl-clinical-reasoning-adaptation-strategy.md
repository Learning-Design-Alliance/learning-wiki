# Adapt TBL units for teaching clinical reasoning by focusing readiness-assessment questions and application exercises on reasoning steps and structured approaches

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy modifies standard team-based learning for clinical reasoning instruction when students already have factual and conceptual knowledge from mandatory seminars. The authors report: "The questions of the readiness assurance tests were carefully constructed to focus on important steps in clinical reasoning" and application exercises presented "a prototypical clinical case" demonstrating "a structured clinical reasoning approach", with peer discussion and immediate instructor feedback on pitfalls and misconceptions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A single trained content-expert instructor; teams of five to seven participants permanently assigned across units
- Students already prepared with the underlying knowledge, e.g. via mandatory seminars whose slides serve as advance preparation
#### Constraints
- The authors note the five 90-min units did not allow the 40 h optimum team-development time Michaelsen noted, which might have reduced TBL's impact
- Diseases discussed in the TBL application exercises were excluded from the assessment problems

### Target Learners
- fourth- and fifth-year undergraduate medical students in a clinical neurology course

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning and clinical decision-making in neurology
