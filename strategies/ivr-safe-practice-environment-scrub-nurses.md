# Use immersive VR to give scrub nurses a safe, low-pressure environment for repetitive practice of complex operations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 4 studies (2 review, 1 causal, 1 qualitative), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends adopting immersive VR to address the lack of structured scrub-nurse training. It argues that iVR provides a "safe environment to learn and practice skills" — echoing the perioperative literature it cites — by letting nurses engage in safe, repetitive practice in a realistic operating theatre without intraoperative time pressure. In the study this took the form of a four-session curriculum combining guided teaching with unguided assessment modes, after which knowledge, confidence, technical skill and efficiency all improved and skills transferred to the physical world.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured sessions with a guided mode for initial learning followed by unguided assessment modes
- Equipment inventory and procedural steps faithfully modelled for the target operation
#### Constraints
- Generalisability is limited: the study covered one operation (rTKA) and orthopaedic scrub nurses only
- Some participants experience vestibular symptoms, which may hinder early adoption

### Target Learners
- Scrub nurses and scrub practitioners, particularly those newly qualified or unfamiliar with specialist equipment

### Target Learning Goals
- Technical preparedness and efficiency for complex infrequently performed operations
- Confidence and anxiety reduction around scrubbing for unfamiliar procedures
