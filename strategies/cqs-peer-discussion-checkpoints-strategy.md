# Use clicker question sequences with peer discussion and instructor checkpoints to scaffold difficult quantum concepts within limited class time

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 3 mixed) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing research-validated clicker question sequences alongside traditional lectures, with students answering individually, then discussing with peers, and the instructor discussing the validity of options after each poll. The authors state that "Opportunities to hold an overall class discussion about salient concepts such as these after students have voted are very important", and they plan checkpoints between questions to encourage such discussions even when time is limited. For future administrations they will add hints asking whether a change of basis is required, include specific numbers as expansion coefficients, and expand discussion of expectation values and Ehrenfest's Theorem.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Research-validated questions sequenced around specific learning goals
- Instructor-led discussion of answer validity after each poll
- Time within class sessions across three lecture sessions
#### Constraints
- In virtual settings, Peer Instruction could not be implemented in full, with discussion predominantly consisting of volunteers or the instructor speaking to the whole class
- Ensuring instructors hold recommended discussions is a challenge when time is limited

### Target Learners
- junior-/senior-level undergraduate quantum mechanics students

### Target Learning Goals
- overcoming common difficulties with time-development of two-state quantum systems
