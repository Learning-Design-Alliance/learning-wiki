Structure PBL teaching around the problem-laying, information-search, group-discussion, summary, and effectiveness-evaluation sequence¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The included trials consistently describe a recurring PBL teaching process. As the article states, "pharmacy students were asked to laying out the problem, search information, group discussion, summary, and effectiveness evaluation, which are the basic progress for PBL teaching methods but not for conventional teaching methods". This sequence operationalizes PBL in Chinese pharmacy classrooms: instructors present a problem, students independently search information, discuss in small tutorial groups, synthesize a summary, and evaluate effectiveness.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Small tutorial groups (the included studies used groups of roughly 4–22 students)
  • Teacher facilitation for discussion and summary phases

Constraints¶

  • Requires class-time allocation distinct from lecture hours; some studies ran 9–40 theoretical class hours under PBL

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate pharmacy students in Chinese higher education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Problem-solving and information-searching skills
  • Collaborative discussion and knowledge synthesis

Claims¶

  • PBL is associated with higher theoretical examination scores than traditional lecture-based teaching in Chinese pharmacy education (SMD = 1.17) [+W]
  • Heterogeneity among pooled PBL trials in Chinese pharmacy education is high (I2 = 93%) [+W]
  • Questionnaire results show PBL superior to conventional teaching for learning interest, independent analysis skills, scope of knowledge, self-study, team spirit, and oral expression [+W]
  • Sensitivity analysis excluding five studies with inadequate randomization leaves the PBL effect on theoretical scores unchanged (SMD = 1.06) [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Design PBL courses around small group size and realistic case scenarios to support student satisfaction
