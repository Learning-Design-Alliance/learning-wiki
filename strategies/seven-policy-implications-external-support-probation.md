# Seven policy recommendations for strengthening externally supported school improvement

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on their analysis, the authors suggest seven implications for policy, including: "Constrain the market choice, both with respect to direction and in terms of quality" by limiting partners to a handful of proven groups offering coherent instructional programs; clarify provider roles into a single line of authority; develop learning and sharing opportunities among providers; give the external partner authority to coordinate all external assistance; stimulate in-depth, content-based professional development; connect assistance to standards rather than only the test; and promote better use of data through multiple diagnostic tools such as running records and curriculum-based assessments.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- District willingness to constrain the partner market and consolidate provider authority
#### Constraints
- The authors note the two-week Targeted Assistance reading session had implementation problems of short notice and inconsistency with some literacy programs

### Target Learners
- Educators and students in low-performing schools under accountability policies

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved classroom instruction and student achievement in literacy
