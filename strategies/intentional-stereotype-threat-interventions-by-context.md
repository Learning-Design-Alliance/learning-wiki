# Be intentional about stereotype threat interventions, since self-reported ESC did not negatively impact URM performance and its effects varied across institutions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Given varying findings about the effects of ethnicity stigma consciousness across contexts, the authors advise that "researchers and faculty should be intentional in their implementation of stereotype threat interventions in their institutions." The study found self-reported ESC did not negatively impact academic performance for URM students and its mediating effects varied across institutions, suggesting stereotype threat did not impact URM performance across these contexts. The article recommends centering interventions that call upon instructors and institutions rather than students in predominantly White spaces.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Knowledge of the local institutional context and student population before selecting interventions
#### Constraints
- The phenomenon may not be as universal as previously thought, per prior work the article cites (Cromley et al., 2013)

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate biology students, especially URM students at selective and predominantly White institutions

### Target Learning Goals
- Reduce stereotype threat and support equitable exam performance
