Integrate interpreting training exercises (dictation with shadowing, active listening, note-taking) into the L2 listening classroom¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the finding that CI training improves L2 listening, the authors recommend that "interpreting skills training such as shadowing, short-term memory training, note-taking as well as active listening training can be integrated into L2 listening classroom" to supplement and diversify existing L2 listening methodology. Specifically, dictation can be coupled with shadowing since both target intensive listening, and note-taking training can sharpen selective listening because summary writing is "subsequent writing based on the notes".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers aiming to enhance selective listening should attach more value to active listening training covering key words, temporal and ordinal relation, and causal relation

Constraints¶

  • Shadowing is suggested only tentatively for elevating dictation accuracy when English majors cope with the TEM-4 dictation test

Target Learners¶

  • English majors preparing for dictation tests
  • L2 learners in listening classrooms

Target Learning Goals¶

  • intensive listening skill
  • selective listening skill
  • L2 listening competence

Affordances¶

  • Rost Six Types Listening Skills

Claims¶

  • 16 weeks of consecutive interpreting training significantly improves interpreting beginners' intensive listening skill (dictation), with no difference between built-in and stand-alone active-listening modes [+W]
  • 16 weeks of CI training significantly improves selective listening skill (summary writing), and the stand-alone active-listening group significantly outperforms the built-in group at post-test [+W]
  • Training active listening as a stand-alone component before other interpreting skills contributes more to selective listening development than training it as a built-in part [+W]
  • Students' questionnaire responses attribute listening improvement to the demands of interpreting tasks, corroborating the quantitative gains [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Adopt the stand-alone active-listening mode for interpreting learners with insufficient L2 proficiency, especially undergraduates seeking to boost listening skills
