# Use video-based instruction showing perfect peer demonstrations to teach the ABCDE approach in resuscitation courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends incorporating a short instructional video into resuscitation training: a 15-minute video showing a NICU nurse and senior pediatric resident consecutively performing perfect ABCDE demonstrations, interspersed with text screenshots emphasizing domain tasks. The authors conclude that "VBI should therefore be regarded as superior to conventional lecturing for teaching the ABCDE approach" and suggest incorporating VBI in resuscitation courses' educational arsenal.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The video must contain the same teaching content as the lecture it replaces; the authors note MB meticulously checked content equivalence
#### Constraints
- Evidence comes from a simulation setting with a single 15–30 min instruction; the authors note fellows/neonatologists could not be compared because the VBI group included none

### Target Learners
- neonatal healthcare providers: nurses, neonatal ward clinicians, fellows/neonatologists

### Target Learning Goals
- adherence to the ABCDE assessment algorithm during neonatal advanced life support
