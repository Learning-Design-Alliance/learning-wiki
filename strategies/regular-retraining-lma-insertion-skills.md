# Schedule regular learning sessions and retraining to maintain intubating LMA insertion speed and confidence in novices

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's recommendation for maintaining intubating LMA skills in doctors who manage airways infrequently. Because time to successful ventilation prolonged significantly at a 90-day reevaluation without practice, the authors suggest "regular learning sessions and retraining may be helpful to improve self-confidence and skill, and further speed the insertion", even though overall skill retention was judged acceptable for both devices after 3 months.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Periodic retraining or learning sessions scheduled after intervals in which clinicians have not practiced LMA insertion
#### Constraints
- Skill retention without retraining was acceptable for both devices after 3 months except for prolonged time to ventilation
- Evidence base is a manikin study that the article states may not be definitively extrapolated to clinical application

### Target Learners
- novice physicians in general wards for whom airway management is likely to be infrequent

### Target Learning Goals
- maintain LMA insertion skill, insertion speed, and self-confidence over months without clinical use
