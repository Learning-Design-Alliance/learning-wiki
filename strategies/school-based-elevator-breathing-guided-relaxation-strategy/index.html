Teach elevator breathing and guided relaxation in short school-based sessions over five weeks¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (1 for, 2 against) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

This strategy trains children in two brief relaxation techniques: elevator breathing, which "incorporates visualization for children" and uses diaphragmatic breathing to release mental and physical stress, and guided relaxation for children, which progressively tenses and relaxes major muscle groups with deep breathing. In the study, an investigator delivered training at school two days a week over a five-week period, with breathing practice lasting five minutes and guided relaxation about 8 to 10 minutes per session, with relaxing music playing in the background.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A trained investigator or adult to lead the sessions
  • A quiet space in the school building or classroom, with students lying down, closing eyes, or resting against a wall

Constraints¶

  • The intervention was delivered in a high-stakes testing period, and the authors note school environmental factors may have had differing effects on test anxiety across the two sites

Target Learners¶

  • third-grade elementary students facing high-stakes testing

Target Learning Goals¶

  • managing physiological and psychological responses to anxiety and stress

Claims¶

  • Relaxation training produced a significant pre-to-post reduction in test anxiety among third-grade students in the experimental group [+W]
  • Third-grade control students receiving no relaxation training showed no significant decrease in test anxiety [-W]
  • Post-test test-anxiety scores did not differ significantly between the relaxation-trained subgroups and the control group [-W]
