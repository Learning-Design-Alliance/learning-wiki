Future research should include long-term RCTs with adequate sample sizes, standardized outcome measures, and cost-effectiveness analyses¶

Description¶

The review recommends a research agenda for strengthening the evidence base on AR/VR training in dentistry and OMFS. As the authors state, "Future research on this topic should include long-term RCTs with adequate sample sizes, training protocols, standardized outcome measures, and cost-effectiveness analyses". They further specify that studies should focus not only on skill acquisition but also on user satisfaction. This addresses the methodological heterogeneity and lack of long-term retention and cost data identified as key limitations.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Studies must use standardized outcome measures to enable cross-trial comparison

Constraints¶

  • Current evidence is limited by heterogeneous study designs, small sample sizes, and a lack of data on long-term skill retention and cost-effectiveness

Target Learners¶

  • Undergraduate dental students
  • OMFS residents and surgeons in training

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Surgical technical skill acquisition
  • User satisfaction evaluation

Claims¶

  • AR/VR training shows equal or superior outcomes versus conventional training in dental and OMFS education [+W]
  • Haptic simulator trials more frequently show superior accuracy outcomes when force-feedback and structured guidance are included [+W]
  • Head-mounted display interventions improve anatomy recognition, spatial orientation, and learner confidence versus conventional instruction [+W]
  • Hybrid sequences combining VR with conventional practice yielded the most favorable outcomes in one trial [+W]
