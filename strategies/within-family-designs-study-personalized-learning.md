# Use within-family designs, with at least one parent and two or more siblings, to test personalized-learning hypotheses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The commentary recommends within-family studies as a tool for studying personalized learning: studies that include data from at least one parent and two or more of their children, using children's characteristics such as cognitive abilities, personality, and genetic dispositions. The authors state "studying how parents tailor family environments to their children's characteristics and needs can provide insights into personalized learning that are also applicable to classroom contexts" and conclude within-family designs are "a useful addition to the educational researcher's toolbox for studying personalized learning".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Data on children's characteristics may include cognitive abilities, personality, genetic dispositions, and other learning-related traits that can differ between children growing up in the same family
#### Constraints
- Family-level characteristics shared by siblings, such as socioeconomic background or ethnicity, cannot be tested with within-family designs

### Target Learners
- school-age children in families
- classroom learners

### Target Learning Goals
- understanding how tailored learning environments affect learning outcomes and inequality
