# Adopt a modified GESEA-based scoring system for multidirectional suturing assessment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends assessing multidirectional stitching performance with a modified GESEA-derived rubric rather than the OSATS tool, because OSATS "focuses more on the evaluation of the traditional sewing method and the assessment of the needle holding position and direction." The rubric scores accuracy of needle entry (2/1/0 by deviation from a marked dot), knot stability, tissue integrity, and tissue tightness, and separately records suture time and thread length. It was applied to video-recorded performances rated by two experienced laparoscopic surgeons.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Video recording of trainee performance and rating by two experienced laparoscopic surgeons
- A suture pad with marked needle entrance and exit targets
#### Constraints
- Slightly modified from the original GESEA standard to fit the designed model

### Target Learners
- junior surgical residents in simulation-based training

### Target Learning Goals
- objective assessment of laparoscopic suturing proficiency
