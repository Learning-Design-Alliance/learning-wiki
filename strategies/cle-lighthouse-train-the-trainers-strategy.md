# Lighthouse train-the-trainers strategy: phase-by-phase demonstration, return teaching, and a seven-step workshop sequence with follow-up seminars

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The lighthouse strategy spreads CLE literacy programs by training key personnel (in-service teachers, supervisors, administrators) who become resource trainers in their own provinces or countries. Core techniques include phase-by-phase demonstration by the trainer with students, feedback and discussion, and "'return teaching' whereby the workshop participants teach the phase that has been demonstrated under the supervision of the group trainers." Workshops follow a seven-step sequence: evocation, demonstrating the methodology, analysis of the new teaching sequence, practice teaching, planning implementation, implementation in teachers' own classrooms, and periodic follow-up seminars at least twice a year. A three-tier leadership structure (national coordinating committee, central project team, provincial project teams) sustains replication and dissemination.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Demonstration with a sampling group of students, ideally from a school where workshop participation is encouraged
- Follow-up sessions after training such as forums, displays of classroom activities, and visits to CLE classes
- Teams of local trainers to build a pool of experienced local educators
#### Constraints
- Rewards and recognition programs are listed as optional components of the train-the-trainer content

### Target Learners
- in-service teachers
- teacher supervisors
- school administrators
- provincial and national education officers

### Target Learning Goals
- implementation of CLE phases of instruction
- classroom management and group learning processes
- language testing, evaluation, and assessment of literacy teaching
