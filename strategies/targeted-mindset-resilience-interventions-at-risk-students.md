# Target grade-sensitive mindset interventions at students most at risk of adopting a fixed mindset, especially women receiving B or C grades

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (5 for, 1 against) · 3 studies (1 causal, 1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q3`–`q4` · 2 of 3 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's findings motivate instructional strategies that promote resilience among students at higher risk of adopting a fixed mindset after commonly awarded lower grades. The abstract states the results "highlight the need for targeted interventions that help promote resilience among students at higher risks of adopting a fixed mindset." Because women with B or C grades showed markedly larger declines in confidence in rejecting fixed beliefs than men with the same grades, interventions should be targeted by grade feedback and gender rather than delivered uniformly. The article also suggests instructional features such as high-stakes assessment or limited opportunities for reflection and revision can shape how students interpret feedback.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires awareness of which students received lower (B or C) grade feedback and monitoring of mindset-relevant responses to that feedback
#### Constraints
- The article identifies the need for such interventions but did not itself test an intervention; effectiveness in this context is untested

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate physical science and engineering students in introductory calculus-based physics, particularly women receiving B or C grades

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining or strengthening students' rejection of fixed mindsets about their own physics abilities across the introductory course sequence
