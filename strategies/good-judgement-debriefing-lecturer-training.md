# Train lecturers in the good-judgement debriefing model to ensure consistent, non-punitive debriefing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because lecturers applied debriefing inconsistently—with differences in opinions and models, and some punitive remarks reported by students—the panel prioritized training sessions on the good-judgement model for lecturers. The article states "the panellists highlighted the need to include debriefing training sessions based on the good‐judgement model for lecturers". Debriefing was considered a strength by both groups, but heterogeneity in its approach was seen as reducing student satisfaction and learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Academic institutions must prioritize the training of their facilitators in a consistent debriefing approach
#### Constraints
- Heterogeneity in debriefing approach reduces student satisfaction and learning, per lecturers' SWOT responses

### Target Learners
- simulation lecturers and facilitators in postgraduate health sciences education

### Target Learning Goals
- consistent good-judgement debriefing practice
- cohesiveness and adherence to the debriefing approach
