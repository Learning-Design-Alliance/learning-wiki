# Explicitly juxtaposing dc and small-signal analyses of the same circuits in scaffolded tasks to help students distinguish signal from bias behavior

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends designing tasks that require students to analyze the same circuits under both dc and small-signal conditions, one modification at a time. The revised task "students must explicitly consider both the small-signal and dc behavior of the same circuits," and performance improved significantly when the two analyses were explicitly juxtaposed. This supports instruction on amplifier and follower functionality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Tasks must explicitly prompt students to consider both dc and small-signal analyses of the same circuits
#### Constraints
- The request for a comparison of peak-to-peak amplitudes alone did not appear to be a sufficient prompt in the original three amplifier comparison task
- Results were obtained from a relatively small number of students

### Target Learners
- upper-division undergraduate physics and engineering electronics students

### Target Learning Goals
- distinguishing small-signal from dc bias behavior in transistor circuits
