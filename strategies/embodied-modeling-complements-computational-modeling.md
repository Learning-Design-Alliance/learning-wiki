# Use embodied and physical modeling activities to complement computational modeling so programming commands become experientially real

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 design, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
Throughout the year the teacher connected ViMAP modeling to out-of-computer experiences such as inking footprints on banner paper and measuring with yardsticks. The authors conclude that "the teacher’s emphasis on using physical and embodied modeling as a way to complement computational modeling and thinking played an important role in the students’ take-up of the norms", making commands like step-size experientially real rather than procedural instructions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Embodied artifacts (e.g., footprint strips) that students can measure and re-measure against a computational model
#### Constraints
- Reported for a 3rd-grade kinematics unit; the strategy is grounded in the argument that norms must be interpretable via actions on experientially real mathematical objects

### Target Learners
- elementary (3rd grade) students

### Target Learning Goals
- measurement conventions
- modeling motion
- take-up of sociomathematical norms
