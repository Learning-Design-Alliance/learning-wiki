# Increase conflict level and use new triggering topics in scenarios for experienced teachers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because in-service teachers reported fewer hindrances, presumably due to cumulative experience with conflictual situations, the authors recommend that, "in order to challenge them to leave their comfort zone–to ensure learning–it is recommended to increase the conflict level as well as to provide scenarios that engage in new triggering topics relevant for experienced teachers." This implements the comfort-zone account: experienced learners need stronger triggers to enter the stretch zone where learning occurs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Scenarios calibrated to learners' experience level so the departure from the comfort zone is challenging but controlled
#### Constraints
- The authors note the need to further examine the level of conflict and its effect on simulation participants

### Target Learners
- in-service teachers
- experienced teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- leaving the comfort zone to ensure learning
- professional identity development through conflict simulation
