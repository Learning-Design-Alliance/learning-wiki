# Allocate PD resources to high-cost but effective coaching programs for teachers most in need, rather than lower-cost programs for all teachers

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the policy discussion, the authors recommend that given the tens of billions spent annually on PD with limited results, administrators judge whether current expenditures could be used more effectively. They propose "to allocate resources to high-cost but effective PD programs for teachers most in need of support, such as coaching, rather than to lower-cost but less-effective programs for all teachers." They also recommend exploring ways to minimize costs while maintaining efficacy, including virtual coaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A skilled coaching corps whose personnel costs make on-site coaching a resource-intensive intervention
#### Constraints
- Per-teacher costs in one cost analysis ranged from $3,300 to upwards of $5,200; the existing literature lacks information for a reliable cost-benefit or cost-effectiveness analysis

### Target Learners
- in-service teachers, prioritizing those most in need of support

### Target Learning Goals
- improving instructional practice and student achievement through more effective use of existing PD expenditures
