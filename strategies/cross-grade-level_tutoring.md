# Cross-Grade-Level Tutoring

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Cross-grade-level tutoring pairs older learners with younger learners so the older student acts as tutor, reviewing previously mastered material and delving deeper into content. The younger learner receives individualized, responsive help; the older learner deepens mastery through the act of teaching, articulation, and explanation. Spiraled curriculum standards create natural opportunities for mutually beneficial partnerships across grade levels.

## Design Implications

Cross-age tutoring is one of the most consistently supported peer-learning formats: meta-analytic evidence shows tutoring raises achievement for both tutees and tutors, with tutors often gaining as much or more than those they teach [Peer tutoring improves academic achievement for both tutors and tutees.](../claims/peer-tutoring-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. The tutor's benefit comes from generative processing — preparing explanations, answering questions, and monitoring a novice's understanding forces reorganization of one's own knowledge [Learning-by-teaching effects depend on the tutor engaging in generative processing, not just explaining.](../claims/learning-by-teaching-improves-mastery.md) [+M]. Because the age gap reduces social comparison, younger learners may ask questions they would not ask a same-age peer or teacher, and tutors gain confidence and a sense of responsibility [Fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Older learners with genuine mastery of the target content — tutoring content the tutor has not consolidated produces weak or erroneous instruction
- Careful pairing by content need, temperament, and availability; explicit expectations for session structure and roles
- Training for tutors in how to explain, question, and give [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) — untrained tutors often simply give answers rather than prompting thinking
- Ongoing monitoring of tutoring dynamics and a clear escalation path when a pairing is not working

#### Constraints
- Tutors without training tend to model answer-giving rather than questioning, which can promote tutee dependence rather than understanding [~M]
- If tutor preparation time is not protected, sessions degrade into unstructured homework help with minimal learning gain [-M]
- Power imbalances or off-task behavior can emerge without clear norms and instructor check-ins [-W]
- Benefits to tutors shrink when the material is entirely new to them rather than a review-and-deepen of mastered content [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Reciprocal formats:** pairs alternate tutor/tutee roles across sessions or topics
- **Structured curricula:** scripted tutoring programs (e.g., Reading Partners, Number Rockets) specify materials and session routines, reducing dependence on tutor skill
- **Technology-mediated:** cross-grade video tutoring or discussion boards extend partnerships when schedules or distance prevent in-person meetings
- **Intensity matters:** effects scale with frequency and duration — brief, occasional sessions show much smaller gains than sustained multiple-week programs [+M]

### Target Learners
- Younger learners needing academic support who benefit from individualized attention and a low-stakes questioning environment [+S]
- Older learners who benefit from teaching others — particularly those whose mastery is solid but whose articulation and metacognition need development [Learning-by-teaching effects depend on the tutor engaging in generative processing, not just explaining.](../claims/learning-by-teaching-improves-mastery.md) [+M]
- Less effective when either party has significant attendance, behavior, or engagement issues that prevent consistent sessions [-W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Consolidation and deeper mastery of previously learned content (tutors)
- Remediation and fluency-building in foundational skills (tutees)
- Communication, empathy, and instructional reasoning for older learners
- Cross-grade community-building and learner responsibility

### Instructions
1. Identify content in the spiraled curriculum where older learners have mastery and younger learners need support; select and prepare tutors accordingly.
2. Train tutors in explanation and questioning techniques — model a tutoring session via [Demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) and [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md), then have tutors practice with [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) feedback.
3. Pair learners and set clear session expectations: goals, materials, roles, and norms.
4. Structure each session around tutee [Practice](../elements/practice.md) with tutor [Provide Guidance](../elements/provide-guidance.md) — tutors should prompt and question rather than supply answers.
5. Build in [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md) and review of tutoring logs; adjust pairings and provide [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) to tutors on their instructional moves [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
6. Gradually release responsibility to the tutoring pair, fading instructor scaffolds as the partnership stabilizes [Contingent scaffolding improves learning outcomes.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M].

## Examples
- **Reading Partners** (https://readingpartners.org) — a structured cross-age and volunteer tutoring program in which trained tutors deliver scripted, evidence-based reading sessions to elementary students; rigorous evaluations show significant gains in reading proficiency.
- **Cross-age reading buddies** — a common elementary practice where upper-grade students read with early-grade students, building fluency for the younger reader and expression/motivation for the older one.
- **Number Rockets** (IES/NCII early-numeracy intervention) — small-group tutoring with structured practice routines illustrating how scripted materials support less-experienced tutors.
