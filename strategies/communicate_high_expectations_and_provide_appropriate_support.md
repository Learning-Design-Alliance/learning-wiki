# Communicate High Expectations and Provide Appropriate Support

## Description
This strategy pairs two moves that are ineffective in isolation: setting explicitly high, demanding expectations for learner performance, and providing the structured support ([Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md), feedback, and guided practice) that makes those expectations attainable. The expectation alone functions as a motivational message — a form of [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) — while the support converts the message into achievable steps. Originating in Chickering and Gamson's (1987) "Seven Principles for Good Practice in Undergraduate Education," it draws on expectancy effects, self-efficacy theory, and goal-setting research.

## Design Implications

High expectations raise performance when learners believe the expectations are achievable and when support is contingent on their current competence [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+S]. Specific, difficult goals outperform vague "do your best" exhortations, but only when learners receive feedback on progress toward them [Specific difficult goals lead to higher performance.](../claims/specific-difficult-goals-lead-to-higher-performance.md) [+S]. Support must be responsive: help that is too heavy undermines the message of confidence, while help that is too thin turns high expectations into discouragement [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit, concrete statements of the performance standard — what "good work" looks like, with exemplars or rubrics
- A supportive relational climate; expectations read as confidence, not threat
- Task-level and process-level feedback so learners can see progress toward the standard [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- A mechanism for gradually withdrawing support as competence grows [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]

#### Constraints
- High expectations without support produce failure experiences that erode self-efficacy, particularly for learners with weak prior knowledge [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [-M]
- Support without high expectations invites minimal compliance; learners calibrate effort to the demonstrated standard [-M]
- Praise that is effort-focused but vague ("you're so smart") can undermine persistence after setbacks; process-focused feedback is more robust [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [~S]
- Expectancy effects are weaker and less reliable in real classrooms than in the original laboratory-adjacent studies; they do not substitute for instructional quality [~W]

#### Implementation Variability
- In one-to-one tutoring: state expectations at the session outset, request drafts and revisions, and periodically review progress against the standard
- In classrooms: publish high rubric standards early, normalize revision, and use [Check-ins](../principles/check-ins.md) to calibrate support
- In online environments: adaptive difficulty and mastery thresholds encode expectations into the system while hints and worked examples supply the support

### Target Learners
- Underprepared learners, who benefit most when high expectations are paired with contingent support rather than remediation alone [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Capable but disengaged learners, for whom demanding goals signal that their effort matters [Task value increases motivation and engagement.](../claims/task-value-increases-motivation-and-engagement.md) [+M]
- Advanced learners, who disengage when standards are undemanding; for them, expectations can rise while support fades [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Motivational outcomes: self-efficacy, persistence, and ownership of learning [Self-efficacy predicts academic persistence.](../claims/self-efficacy-predicts-academic-persistence.md) [+S]
- Quality standards: producing work that meets explicit criteria rather than "done" criteria
- Self-regulation: internalizing high standards as learners take over monitoring of their own performance [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+M]

### Instructions
1. State the standard explicitly at the outset — what excellent performance looks like, with an exemplar or rubric — and express confidence that the learner can reach it.
2. Break the path to the standard into achievable steps, providing [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) and modeling on the first step rather than the whole task.
3. Give feedback tied to the standard and to process ("your argument needs a counterexample here"), not to the person [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
4. Require revision: ask for drafts and treat the first attempt as intermediate, signaling that the standard is reachable through iteration.
5. Fade support as competence grows — reduce hints, increase task complexity, and transfer responsibility for self-assessment to the learner [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].
6. Check in periodically on progress toward the standard, adjusting support to current performance rather than to a fixed schedule [Contingent scaffolding improves learning.](../claims/contingent-scaffolding-improves-learning.md) [+M].

## Related Strategies
- [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md) — the support half of the pairing; expectations without it fail
- Mastery-oriented feedback — the feedback mechanism that keeps high standards motivating rather than punishing
- Growth mindset framing — the belief system that makes effort toward a high standard seem worthwhile

## Examples
- **Chickering & Gamson's Seventh Principle in practice** — Writing-intensive courses that require multiple drafts with instructor response, treating revision as the norm rather than remediation.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — Mastery-based progress bars communicate a high standard (100% mastery) while hints and videos provide the support to reach it.
- **Writing centers and tutoring programs** — Tutors open sessions by naming an ambitious goal for the draft, request specific revisions, and review progress at the session's end.
