# Emphasize fluent retrieval of verbal arithmetic facts and partially different memory processes when instructing multiplication, in contrast to addition and subtraction

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 9 claims (9 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the study's finding that arithmetic operations vary in their reliance on retrieval processes after formal skill acquisition, the article proposes that multiplication instruction should attend to fluent fact retrieval from verbal memory prompted by visuo-orthographic stimuli, more so than addition and subtraction instruction. The educational impact statement says: "It is proposed that multiplication, in relation to addition and subtraction, relies to a greater extent on fluent retrieval of verbal information prompted by visuo-ortographical stimuli as well as on partially different memory processes, which were more similar to those involved in reading fluency." The article also notes multiplication is bridged to addition through repeated addition and, in contrast with other operations, to a greater extent rote learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Formal skill acquisition phase with direct instruction and intensive practice should precede the emphasis on retrieval processes
#### Constraints
- The article notes that the extent to which operations are taught using procedural or retrieval approaches varies across cultures and curricula, which may shape these retrieval contributions

### Target Learners
- primary school children learning multiplication after addition and subtraction

### Target Learning Goals
- multiplication fact fluency and automatized fact retrieval

### Affordances
- Hierarchical Arithmetic Development Framework
