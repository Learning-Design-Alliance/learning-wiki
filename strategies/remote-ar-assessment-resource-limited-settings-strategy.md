# Use remote instructor-led AR simulation to deliver standardized team-leader assessment where in-person simulation access is limited

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy delivers scenario-based assessment of resuscitation team leadership by pairing local learners wearing AR headsets with a geographically distant instructor who facilitates the scenario in real time. The article frames it as a way to overcome geographic, logistical, and financial barriers, noting that "This portability makes AR simulation especially relevant for rural hospitals, humanitarian settings, and low- and middle-income countries with limited access to simulation centers." It was implemented cross-continentally, with instruction from Stanford and assessment in Brazil.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Four AR headsets, a compression torso, and reliable broadband internet connectivity
- A remote instructor site able to facilitate the scenario and adjust clinical parameters in real time
#### Constraints
- Excludes participants whose corrective glasses interfere with headset fitting
- Supported for team-leader cognition and nontechnical behaviors, not hands-on technical skill metrics

### Target Learners
- health care professionals (internal medicine and emergency medicine residents)

### Target Learning Goals
- team leader decision-making and nontechnical performance in cardiac arrest resuscitation
