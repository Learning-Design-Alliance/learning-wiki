# Make reflection on students' goal preferences a recurring element of the curriculum to support cooperative learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that "reﬂection on students' goal preferences should be a recurring element of the curriculum, especially in secondary vocational education where the drop-out rate is very high". Goal reflection is proposed as a means of enhancing motivation by making students aware of the goals they want to achieve and of the links between their personal goals and the goals presented by teachers, instruction methods and course books. The stimulated-recall setting used in this study is offered as a prototype approach for encouraging such reflection throughout the school program.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students must be invited to think about their own goals and about the links between goal preferences and the goals presented to them by teachers, instruction methods, course books, and other students
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is grounded in an exploratory study whose nature the authors describe as mainly descriptive and exploratory

### Target Learners
- secondary vocational education students at risk of dropping out

### Target Learning Goals
- self-regulation of learning and motivation for cooperative learning
