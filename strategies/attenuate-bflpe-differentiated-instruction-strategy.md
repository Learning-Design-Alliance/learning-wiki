# Educators and parents should be informed of the BFLPE and use measures such as differentiated instruction to attenuate its negative effect on academic self-concept

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review), `q3` · 2 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Because the BFLPE is pervasive and strongest for high school students, the article recommends that parents considering high-achieving schools or advanced classes "should be informed of the potential negative consequence on ASC," and that educators apply appropriate teaching practices to help students develop proper ASC. It notes that "differentiated instruction strategies can be used to attenuate the BFLPE ( Roy et al., 2015 )" and cites the necessity of special education classes for disadvantaged students at risk of low ASC in regular settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Educators need to understand how ASC might be influenced by the BFLPE to apply appropriate teaching practices
#### Constraints
- The differentiated-instruction evidence is attributed to Roy et al. (2015), who found significant BFLPE only for low-achieving students whose teachers used differentiated instruction less frequently

### Target Learners
- high school students at risk of the strongest BFLPE
- students in high-achieving or selective schools and classes

### Target Learning Goals
- protecting and developing positive academic self-concept

### Affordances
- Bflpe Social Comparison Theory
