Employ output-prompting strategies more frequently when correcting young learners' grammar¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (2 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on its finding that the output-prompting group achieved the highest grammar gains and uptake rates, the article recommends that language teachers "can employ output -prompting strategy more in their classes for improving grammatical accuracy of the learners". Teacher trainers should instruct teachers in focus-on-form options and encourage attention to output-prompting strategies, and material designers should prepare materials that encourage their use, calibrated to students' knowledge level and individual differences.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Teachers receive instruction on what and how to give feedback, as the study's teachers did from the researchers

Constraints¶

  • The article also states both input- and output-providing strategies "seem to be effective" and teachers should employ both judiciously; the advantage of prompts was demonstrated only for young elementary-level learners on three target structures

Target Learners¶

  • young EFL learners in primary-level language institutes

Target Learning Goals¶

  • grammatical accuracy on target forms through oral corrective feedback

Affordances¶

  • Input Output Negotiation Strategy Taxonomy

Claims¶

  • Grammar-oriented uptake rates are significantly higher in input-providing and output-prompting feedback classes than in a no-feedback class among young EFL learners [+W]
  • Uptake rates do not differ significantly between input-providing and output-prompting feedback groups [~W]
  • Output-prompting feedback outperforms input-providing and no-feedback conditions on both immediate and delayed grammar posttests in young EFL learners [+W]
