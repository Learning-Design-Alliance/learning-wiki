# Embed subject-specific web credibility modules into existing courses rather than creating standalone courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that "curriculum developers could create subject-specific modules for instructors to integrate into existing curricula" rather than designing entirely new courses. Because the modules were embedded in regular course content and delivered asynchronously, they avoid the scalability barriers of prior add-on interventions in which researchers delivered instruction themselves. They propose a national database of open educational resources to disseminate the approach and collaboration between subject-matter experts and curriculum developers for frequently taught courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Subject-matter experts collaborating with curriculum developers to create modules for frequently taught courses
- A bank of modules so asynchronous delivery requires no extensive faculty development
#### Constraints
- Further research is needed on efficacy across varied disciplines and on how controversial subject matter and motivated reasoning influence student behavior

### Target Learners
- Undergraduate students across disciplines in higher education

### Target Learning Goals
- Evaluating the credibility of online sources within disciplinary course content

### Affordances
- Lateral Reading Fact Checker Strategy
