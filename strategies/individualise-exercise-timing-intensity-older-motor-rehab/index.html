Optimise acute exercise timing and intensity to individual characteristics when supporting motor learning in older adults¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (2 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that acute exercise interventions for older learners be tailored: "supporting the importance of optimising acute exercise interventions based on individual characteristics", and calls for research to identify "the appropriate timing and intensity of exercise for this age group to maximise the beneﬁt on motor consolidation, while minimising the impact on motor performance." In rehabilitation, exercise before practice may stabilise skill over delay but should be applied cautiously given acquisition costs in the oldest learners.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Knowledge of individual age and fitness characteristics; measurement of consolidation outcomes separate from acquisition performance

Constraints¶

  • Exercise immediately before learning conferred no acquisition benefit and harmed performance for the oldest participants; timing after learning was not tested in this study

Target Learners¶

  • healthy older adults
  • older adults in motor rehabilitation settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • motor skill consolidation
  • motor rehabilitation

Affordances¶

  • Arousal Dose Response Exercise Motor Learning Account

Claims¶

  • High-intensity exercise prior to practice reduced skill acquisition in older adults, with the detriment specific to the oldest participants [~W]
  • Force error increased at retention for the rest group but not the exercise group [+W]
  • The acquisition detriment after exercise was driven by the accuracy subcomponent: older participants reduced force error after rest but not after exercise [~W]
  • Exercise eliminated the offline skill decline seen after rest: the rest group lost skill over the 6-hour delay while the exercise group did not [+W]
