# Phased implementation of AI assessment starting with root opportunities in low-stakes settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that medical schools adopt AI-driven assessment through a staged sequence following the four-layer roadmap rather than ad-hoc tool adoption. As the conclusion states, "A phased piloting approach is recommended, beginning with the foundational 'root' opportunities like automated feedback in low-stakes formative settings to build stakeholder trust and refine institutional workflows." Institutions then progress to intermediate opportunities such as simulation and longitudinal assessment before implementing highly dependent impact opportunities like personalised learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional context and ethical principles must be considered; stakeholder trust needs to be built through low-stakes piloting before higher-stakes deployment
#### Constraints
- The article cautions that particular attention must be paid to ethical, trust, and equity implications where AI is integrated into high-stakes, summative assessments

### Target Learners
- medical students; medical school assessment teams, educators, and institutions

### Target Learning Goals
- responsible, strategically sequenced adoption of AI-driven assessment
