# Pair open-enrollment advanced coursework with targeted supports and recruitment for underrepresented groups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that expanding advanced coursework through opt-in enrollment alone is insufficient for equitable participation and should be paired with additional supports. Promising approaches it names include "clearer and more personalized communication with families, universal screening or opt-out enrollment, and efforts to strengthen belonging and representation within advanced programs". It cites prior work showing universal screening and opt-out acceleration policies can substantially increase participation among low-income and minority students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Districts must be able to identify students whose predicted benefits and enrollment behavior diverge, using joint analysis of treatment effect heterogeneity and enrollment patterns.
#### Constraints
- The article states that simply removing formal enrollment restrictions may be insufficient to produce equitable participation, since participation gaps may reflect information barriers, institutional gatekeeping, differences in social and cultural capital, and uncertainty about belonging.

### Target Learners
- middle-school students, particularly historically underrepresented and lower-socioeconomic students

### Target Learning Goals
- equitable access to and participation in advanced coursework; improved ELA achievement
