Three development directions for observational self-regulation measures: broader behaviors, cross-context observability, and clear operational definitions¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The review proposes three directions for developing observational measures of children's self-regulation in naturalistic settings. As the authors state, potential directions include "incorporating a wider range of behaviors that reflect the multidimensional nature of self-regulation" and "identifying behaviors that are observable across diverse contexts, such as school, home, and community settings." The third direction is providing clear operational definitions of target behaviors to enhance inter-rater reliability and facilitate interpretation of links between observable behaviors and underlying self-regulation processes.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Observers require appropriate training before using observational measures across settings

Constraints¶

  • Current observational tools show limited capacity to provide precise conceptualization of underlying self-regulation processes, and results are often interpretable only in specific contexts

Target Learners¶

  • children aged 0-8 years in school, home, and community settings

Target Learning Goals¶

  • observation and coding of self-regulation behaviors (cognitive, emotional, behavioral)

Related Strategies¶

  • Study the development of self-regulatory skills in naturalistic settings over longer periods of time
  • Combine standardized tests and naturalistic classroom observations (mixed methods) when assessing oral communicative competence
  • Combine cognitive tasks and ecological teacher questionnaires when measuring children's inhibition for academic research
