Move beyond explorative expert-novice comparisons to hypothesis-driven designs investigating application of evidence-based pedagogical principles¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that research on professional vision shift from explorative expert-novice comparisons to controlled, hypothesis-driven designs examining how teachers learn to apply evidence-based pedagogical knowledge. It argues explorative comparisons are of limited value because teacher education programs offer sparse opportunities for deep learning of scientific pedagogical knowledge, so experienced teachers may not master principles novices should learn. The author also challenges the reacting-teacher conception, arguing careful lesson planning makes classroom complexity predictable and enables flexible reaction.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Evidence-based pedagogical principles from psychology on instructional design and classroom management as the content of hypothesis-driven studies

Constraints¶

  • The author notes teachers' conscious representation of their teaching may fall apart from what they actually do, which eye-tracking may help detect

Target Learners¶

  • teacher students in teacher education
  • researchers of teacher expertise

Target Learning Goals¶

  • applying evidence-based pedagogical knowledge
  • lesson planning
  • professional vision

Claims¶

  • Experienced teachers diagnose underestimating, overestimating, and uninterested student profiles more accurately than novices, with partial eye-tracking support [~W]
  • Only a minority of teacher educators interpreted a critical classroom-management incident as theoretically expected, despite marked eye-tracking differences [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Advance AI-motivation research through theory-driven, pedagogically explicit, longitudinal designs covering broader motivational constructs
  • Complement lesson study with a theoretical perspective on learning so teachers can plan individually with the theory
