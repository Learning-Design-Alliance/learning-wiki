# Infuse evolutionary content into existing courses and rotations rather than creating new courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy responds to deans' reported preferences in the 2013 survey: rather than adding standalone evolutionary medicine courses, schools should embed evolution content into existing courses, rotations, and assessments. The article reports that "Deans reported little interest in new courses, but considerable interest in strategies for infusing evolution into existing curricula," and the most-wanted resources were model test questions, model curricula for existing courses and rotations, and case studies with facilitator guides.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Ready-made integration resources such as model test questions with explanations, model curricula for existing courses and rotations, and case studies with facilitator guides
#### Constraints
- Few deans were interested in having an evolutionary biologist join clinical rounds, despite positive reported feedback at three schools

### Target Learners
- medical students in North American MD and DO programs

### Target Learning Goals
- applying evolutionary principles to medical problems
- understanding core evolutionary medicine topics such as antibiotic resistance and environmental mismatch
