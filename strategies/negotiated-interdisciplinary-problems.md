# Co-design interdisciplinary problems with biologists, negotiating realism, modeling, and multiple representations, and explicitly naming what biology is simplified away

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 qualitative, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors created problems in conversation with biologist colleagues, matching biological relevance to what introductory students can do. Their worm-growth scaling problem, negotiated with Todd Cooke, "has been successfully used in our IPLS class both as a homework assignment and as a group problem-solving activity." A cellular gradient problem seeds the Nernst potential while deliberately omitting multiple mobile ions and active pumping — omissions that are both made and explained to students as simplifications.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Negotiate problems with biologists so they combine realism, explicit discussion of modeling, mathematical abstraction, multiple representations, and functional implications
#### Constraints
- Leave out biology to make problems manageable, but tell students what was left out and why, as a first step to understanding

### Target Learners
- introductory IPLS students and students in the partner biology course

### Target Learning Goals
- scaling and dimensional reasoning; understanding how gradients cause and stop motion; judging what physics matters in a biological system
