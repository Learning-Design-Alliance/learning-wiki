# Dual moderator roles: PCP moderators lead interaction and engagement while specialist moderators prepare and consult on clinical content

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The CFC program assigned distinct complementary moderator roles. Specialist moderators (6 clinical endocrinologists) focused on "content preparation and clinical consultation aligned with up-to-date clinical practice," while 2 PCPs with special interest in bone health curated specialist content for primary care relevance, introduced topics, and led group discussion with a primary focus on direct interaction to encourage involvement. At any one time both a PCP and a specialist were active as moderators, combining clinical authority with peer-level facilitation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Recruitment based on clinical expertise, prior experience with similar projects, availability, and suitability with respect to project aims and team dynamics
#### Constraints
- Selected specialists were all clinical endocrinologists with a clinical and research interest in bone and mineral disorders

### Target Learners
- Primary care physicians in small private groups of 12 to 16 per cycle

### Target Learning Goals
- Case-based discussion of screening, treatment protocols, and evidence translation in osteoporosis care
