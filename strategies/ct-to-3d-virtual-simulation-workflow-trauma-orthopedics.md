# Use a structured virtual simulation workflow converting hospital CT imaging data into interactive three-dimensional fracture models for student training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a specific workflow for virtual simulation-based training in trauma orthopedics. High-resolution CT imaging data of fracture cases were retrieved from the hospital's picture archiving and communication system (PACS) in DICOM format, imported into Mimics software for threshold-based segmentation and region-growing, converted into three-dimensional surface models, exported in STL format, and imported into 3D Slicer. In this environment, "students could perform multi-angle rotation, zooming, slicing, transparency adjustment, model clipping, and simulated fracture reduction".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires access to high-resolution CT imaging data in DICOM format from a hospital PACS
- Requires Mimics software (Materialise, Belgium) for bone tissue segmentation and 3D Slicer for interactive model manipulation
- Requires a computer-based three-dimensional virtual simulation platform with appropriate hardware
#### Constraints
- The implementation of virtual simulation depends on technical support and financial resources, which may limit wider adoption

### Target Learners
- clinical medical students undertaking internships

### Target Learning Goals
- observing fracture morphology and spatial anatomical relationships
- understanding reduction strategies
- practicing fracture reduction and internal fixation placement
