# Demonstration-Based Flipped Classroom

## Description
In a demonstration-based flipped classroom, the instructor records narrated demonstrations — worked problems, software walkthroughs, lab techniques, geometric constructions — and assigns them as pre-class viewing. Learners can pause, rewind, and rewatch at their own pace, then attend class for [practice](../elements/practice.md), feedback, and resolution of difficulties. The approach suits procedural, precision-dependent subjects (mathematics, chemistry, physics, engineering, software) where observing exact expert steps matters.

## Design Implications

The strategy combines two evidence-backed mechanisms: observing a modeled performance before attempting it, and relocating first-pass instruction outside class so contact time is spent applying rather than receiving. Pre-class demonstrations function as [worked examples](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M], reducing unguided search for novices, while in-class [practice](../elements/practice.md) converts observation into transferable skill [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [+S]. Video's pause-and-rewind affordance lets learners self-pace through high-precision steps that a live demonstration would expose only once.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Screen-recording and narration tools (e.g., OBS, Camtasia, Loom) and skill in producing clear, concise demonstrations
- Narration that makes reasoning explicit, not just the actions ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) style commentary)
- An accountability mechanism — embedded questions or notes — so viewing is active rather than passive
- In-class activities that require applying what the demonstration showed, with instructor circulation for [feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md)

#### Constraints
- Watching a demonstration without an application task creates illusions of understanding; learners overestimate what they learned from viewing alone [Pairing worked examples with practice or fading supports transfer better than examples alone.](../claims/worked-examples-with-practice-improve-transfer.md) [-S]
- Producing high-quality demonstrations is time-intensive; poorly paced or overloaded videos degrade learning [Cognitive overload impairs learning outcomes.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-M]
- Requires reliable access to devices and connectivity outside class; inequitable access undermines the model
- Less effective for open-ended, discussion-based, or ill-structured goals where there is no single correct procedure to model

#### Implementation Variability
- Partial flip: only the most precision-critical topics are demonstrated on video; the rest stays live
- Interactive video (e.g., Edpuzzle, H5P) embeds questions to enforce active viewing
- Demonstration–problem pairs: each video is followed immediately by an isomorphic problem, mirroring [example–problem sequencing](../claims/example-problem-sequences-reduce-cognitive-load.md) [+M]
- Student-generated demonstrations as an assessment form, deepening processing through teaching

### Target Learners
- Novices in procedural domains who benefit from a complete expert model before attempting tasks [Worked examples reduce unnecessary search for novices.](../claims/worked-examples-reduce-novice-search.md) [+M]
- Learners who need to revisit steps at their own pace (second-language learners, students with processing-speed differences)
- Less beneficial for learners with strong prior knowledge, for whom explicit modeling becomes redundant [Guidance that helps novices can hinder more knowledgeable learners.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency: executing multi-step methods accurately (algebraic manipulation, titration, CAD operations)
- Schema formation: building a mental model of the task's structure before independent problem solving
- Self-paced review and remediation of precise techniques

### Instructions
1. Identify the procedures or problem types where observing exact expert steps adds the most value; script the reasoning, not just the clicks.
2. Record short (under ~10 minute) narrated demonstrations, applying [cognitive load management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md): segment steps, highlight relevant screen regions, and remove extraneous content.
3. Assign the video with an embedded question or brief written summary so viewing is active.
4. Open class with a quick [check-in](../elements/check-in.md) on what was confusing, then run in-class [practice](../elements/practice.md) on isomorphic problems.
5. Circulate and provide [feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) targeted at the task and process level [Feedback is most effective when directed at the task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
6. Fade support across the unit: move from full demonstrations to partial examples to unscaffolded problems as competence grows.

## Related Strategies
- [Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) — shares the model-then-practice sequence; the flip relocates the modeling phase outside class
- [Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md) — the pre-class video serves as the modeling phase before in-class coaching
- [Four-Component Instructional Design](../patterns/4cid-four-component-instructional-design.md) — demonstrations supply the supportive/procedural information for learning tasks

## Examples
- Engineering courses using screencasts to demonstrate CAD software techniques, then dedicating lab sessions to instructor-supervised modeling tasks.
- Chemistry lab safety and technique videos (e.g., pre-lab demonstrations of titration) viewed before practical sessions, freeing lab time for coaching.
- [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org) — narrated step-by-step problem demonstrations paired with practice exercises, widely used in flipped math classrooms.
