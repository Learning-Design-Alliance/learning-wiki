Measure proximal, body-movement-based and affective outcomes in future physical activity intervention studies¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that future intervention studies broaden their outcome measures to capture near-transfer effects and possible mediating pathways. It states: "Future intervention studies should consider measuring outcomes requiring body movements, which may reflect near-transfer effect, and/or instruments measuring affect or stress, which may reflect mediating pathways of interest". It gives the example of a Simon Says task to measure inhibitory control, and suggests exploring how positive affect and reduced stress may mediate effects of physical activity on creativity.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Outcome instruments should be relevant to the activity or real-world test, in this case using physically active instruments

Constraints¶

  • Testing psychosocial variables such as affect in younger participants is described as challenging

Target Learners¶

  • primary school children aged 5–12

Target Learning Goals¶

  • executive functions
  • creativity
  • academic achievement

Claims¶

  • Outcome proximity moderates the dose–response relation: distal outcomes outperform proximal outcomes at dosages at or below 5.43 h, while proximal outcomes exceed distal outcomes at or above 22.6 h [~W]
