# Select early-phase laparoscopic radical prostatectomy patients for smaller prostates and no anticipated lymph node dissection to shorten initial operative times

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that surgeons starting an LRP program in low-volume, non-mentored settings deliberately stage case difficulty. It states: "early-stage patient selection—especially choosing patients with smaller prostates and no need for lymph node dissection—may help reduce operative times and improve initial results." This follows directly from its finding that prostate volume, blood loss, and lymph node dissection independently prolonged operative time in regression analysis.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Surgeons beginning an LRP program with limited open experience; baseline training in open surgery and general laparoscopic skills as the article describes for its surgeon
#### Constraints
- Findings are exploratory due to the retrospective design and limited sample size; the 61-case threshold is cohort-specific and should not be extrapolated to other surgeons or settings

### Target Learners
- Surgeons initiating laparoscopic radical prostatectomy programs in low-volume or resource-limited settings

### Target Learning Goals
- Acquiring procedural competency and technical efficiency in laparoscopic radical prostatectomy through self-directed practice
