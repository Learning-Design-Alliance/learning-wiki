# Use collective debriefing during and after gameplay, and stress that efficiency is not the only goal in learning games

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that instructors counter students' performance-driven interpretation of game-based teamwork by emphasizing collaborative values at introduction and, crucially, by debriefing collectively. They state: "We emphasize the importance of collective debriefing both during and after the game", suggesting students might have shifted from competition mode had communication and collective learning been reinforced mid-game. They also advise discussing learning outcomes in more detail after the game and closer collaboration between game developers and pedagogical instructors.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lecturers trained as facilitators who set up the game and act as passive instructors and observers
- Pre-game reading material on teamwork, team roles, and team leadership
#### Constraints
- Instructions given at the beginning of gameplay have little impact on students' perceptions of the learning environment, per Vandercruysse et al. as cited by the authors

### Target Learners
- undergraduate university students

### Target Learning Goals
- teamwork skills
- collaborative learning
- team leadership
