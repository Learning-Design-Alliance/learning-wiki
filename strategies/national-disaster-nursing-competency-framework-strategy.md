# Build disaster nursing preparedness through a national competency framework, mandatory disaster modules, scenario-based simulation and leadership development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a package of policy and educational actions to strengthen nurses' disaster preparedness in Slovenia: creating a national competency framework, adding mandatory disaster nursing modules to all nursing programmes, embedding scenario-based simulation in clinical placements, providing structured training for practising nurses, and prioritising investment in "regular disaster drills, scenario‐based simulations and leadership development". It argues these measures would standardise training and clarify roles across healthcare facilities.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Institutional investment in training infrastructure and simulation resources
- Workplace culture supporting continuous professional development and interdisciplinary collaboration
#### Constraints
- The article's supporting evidence is cross-sectional and cannot establish that these interventions causally improve competencies
- Barriers include limited training opportunities, scarce resources and insufficient organisational support

### Target Learners
- Registered nurses in clinical practice
- Nursing students in formal education programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- Disaster preparedness and response competencies
- Disaster-related planning, leadership and decision-making skills
