# Combine PBL and lectures, matching method to subject: PBL for physiology, pathology and pharmacology; lectures for biochemistry and microbiology; both for anatomy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends tailoring the teaching method to the subject rather than adopting PBL wholesale: PBL for physiology, pathology and pharmacology-related concepts, conventional didactic lectures for biochemistry and microbiology, and a combination of lecture and PBL sessions for anatomy during the basic science years. This mirrors the authors' citation of Nandi et al.'s meta-analysis concluding that "a combination of both the conventional lecture-based and newer PBL curricula would provide the most effective training". Community health is better served by community postings and clinical medicine by bedside teaching.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A curriculum that can schedule both PBL sessions and didactic lectures for the same cohort, as PAHS's hybrid PBL method does
#### Constraints
- The preference findings come from one Nepalese medical school's first two batches; a reviewer judged direct per-subject PBL-versus-lecture preference comparison inappropriate given the questionnaire design

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- basic science subject learning (physiology, pathology, pharmacology, anatomy, biochemistry, microbiology)
