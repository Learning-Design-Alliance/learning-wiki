# Integrate psychosocial support strategies—mentoring, mindfulness-based stress reduction, peer-support groups, and academic skills training—into medical education programs

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the review's findings that distress and burnout hinder performance while motivation, emotional intelligence, and social support appear protective, the authors recommend fostering learning environments that support psychological well-being, promote intrinsic motivation, and strengthen social support systems. They state that "Interventions such as mentoring, mindfulness-based stress reduction, peer-support groups, and academic skills training may mitigate risk factors and enhance protective traits." At the institutional level they recommend addressing structural barriers including financial hardship, workload intensity, and access to academic resources.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Early identification of elevated stress, burnout, or low social support to inform targeted academic counseling, mentoring initiatives, and mental health resources.
#### Constraints
- The review notes these associations cannot be interpreted as causal given cross-sectional designs; intervention effectiveness is not established.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- academic performance
- student well-being
