# Hire and train Indigenous educators and deliver programs in the relevant Indigenous language

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The paper recommends that planetariums without Indigenous presenters make significant efforts to correct this, citing Sydney Observatory's Indigenous program that brought in Aboriginal guides. The guides "were able to draw from their lived experiences, which non-Indigenous educators cannot do in this context", and the paper urges delivering programs in the relevant Indigenous language where possible. It also suggests moving away from the 'add on consultant' model toward full-time positions such as 'Curator of Indigenous Scientific Knowledge Systems'.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Pathways for Indigenous people to be hired and trained as astronomers, educators, and science communicators
#### Constraints
- If an Indigenous staff member is unavailable or non-existent, an appropriate person may be nominated to deliver content, or elders' recorded voices or videos can be used

### Target Learners
- Indigenous youth, adults, and communities
- planetarium and museum staff

### Target Learning Goals
- Increasing STEM and informal science learning opportunities for Indigenous communities
- cultural pride and engagement in science
