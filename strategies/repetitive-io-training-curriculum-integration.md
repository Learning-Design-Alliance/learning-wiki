# Integrate repetitive IO-access training into the medical school clinical skills curriculum to ensure retention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
Because the OSCE was performed immediately after the workshop and retention of the skill was not tested, the authors recommend that repetitive training in achieving IO access be integrated as part of medical school's clinical skills curriculum. They argue this would ensure retention of the gained knowledge, increase competence by allowing time for spaced repetition of the skill, and potentially improve initial resuscitation attempts and patient outcome. They also suggest teaching students the procedure during medical school may result in more frequent clinical use of IO access in the future.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Repetitive training sessions scheduled over time rather than a single workshop
#### Constraints
- Retention was not tested in this pilot study, so the recommendation rests on the untested assumption that repetition is needed to secure it

### Target Learners
- Medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Retention of intraosseous access insertion skill for emergency situations
