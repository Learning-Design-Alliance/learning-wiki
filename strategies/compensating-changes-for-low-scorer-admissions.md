# Pair admissions of lower-scoring students with compensating changes to other admissions criteria or program structure to maintain graduation rates

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The comment's forward-looking recommendation for programs adopting barrier-reducing admissions criteria. It argues that because prior literature indicates test scores and grades do predict graduation, "admitting more students with low scores will require compensating changes either to other admissions criteria or to program structure to maintain or improve graduation rates." It suggests such changes may already be embedded in the new rubrics and calls for follow-up outcome studies.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs adopting rubric-based admissions that admit more low-scoring applicants must monitor graduation outcomes, since the comment holds the de-emphasized predictors are not demonstrably non-predictive.
#### Constraints
- The comment notes it is unknown whether compensating changes are already integrated into the new rubrics, and that effects on demographics were not directly evaluated due to lack of demographic data.

### Target Learners
- applicants to physics PhD programs

### Target Learning Goals
- maintaining or improving PhD completion rates while broadening access
