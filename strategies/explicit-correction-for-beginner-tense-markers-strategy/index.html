Use explicit correction rather than or-choice feedback with beginner EFL learners working on tense markers¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 4 studies (2 causal, 2 quant-synthesis), q2–q3 · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

Based on the study's results, the article recommends explicit correction — directly supplying the correct form, as in 'My father works' not 'my father work' — over or-choice feedback for beginner learners producing English tense markers in communicatively oriented classrooms. The author states the study "contributes to the arguments about the benefit of different types of corrective feedback by confirming that explicit correction is more beneficial compared to or-choice feedback to the beginner Iranian EFL learners".

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Feedback delivered immediately during communicative classroom interaction on targeted tense-marker errors

Constraints¶

  • The author states these findings "may not be generalizable to other instructional contexts"; evidence comes from one small two-class study of beginners

Target Learners¶

  • Beginner Iranian EFL learners

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Accurate production of English tense markers

Claims¶

  • Both explicit correction and or-choice feedback improved beginner EFL learners' accurate use of English tense markers over a ten-session instructional period [+W]
  • Explicit correction was more effective than implicit or-choice feedback for beginner Iranian EFL learners' production of English tense markers [+W]
  • Planned focus on form via corrective feedback on simple past tense errors improves Iranian EFL learners' oral accuracy by the final treatment session [+W]
  • Corrective feedback effects on partially acquired structures may emerge only on delayed measures, not immediately [~W]
  • Corrective feedback on second-language errors has a significant effect on L2 development that is maintained on delayed tests [+S]

Related Strategies¶

  • Use intensive recasts repeatedly focused on a single structure as an implementable alternative to explicit feedback
  • Provide delayed explicit and metalinguistic feedback after communicative tasks, using board presentation and whole-class explanation
