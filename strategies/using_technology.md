# Using Technology

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Using technology as a strategy means deliberately selecting and integrating digital tools — multimedia, simulations, learning platforms, collaboration tools, adaptive systems — to serve specific instructional purposes. The strategy treats technology as a delivery and interaction medium whose effectiveness depends entirely on the pedagogy it carries, not on the presence of the technology itself.

## Design Implications

Meta-analyses consistently find that technology-mediated instruction produces learning gains comparable to or modestly better than traditional instruction, but the effect is driven by pedagogy and content, not the medium [Technology's effect on learning is small and depends on pedagogy, not the medium.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654311417738) [~M]. Multimedia design matters: presenting words with relevant images, managing extraneous load, and segmenting content improves learning relative to poorly designed presentations [Multimedia learning is improved by coherence, signaling, redundancy, and segmentation principles.](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511811678) [+S]. Technology is most valuable when it enables something otherwise difficult — visualization of dynamic processes, immediate feedback at scale, adaptive pacing, or collaboration across distance.

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear instructional goal the technology serves better than a non-technological alternative
- Alignment of media design with multimedia learning principles (coherence, signaling, segmenting)
- Reliable access and basic digital fluency among learners and instructors
- A mechanism for learners to act on content — [Practice](../elements/practice.md), [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md), or production tasks — rather than passive consumption

#### Constraints
- Technology used as a content-delivery channel alone (e.g., lecture capture without interaction) yields minimal gains [Technology's effect on learning is small and depends on pedagogy, not the medium.](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654311417738) [~M]
- Decorative media, gratuitous animation, and seductive details depress learning by adding extraneous load [Decorative illustrations do not improve learning.](../claims/decorative-illustrations-do-not-improve-learning.md) [-S]
- Redundant on-screen text read aloud verbatim impairs learning compared with narration alone [Multimedia learning is improved by coherence, signaling, redundancy, and segmentation principles.](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511811678) [-S]
- High learner autonomy in open-ended digital environments can overload novices; guidance matters more as prior knowledge decreases [Guidance benefits reverse as learner expertise grows.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Digital distraction — off-task multitasking on devices — reduces performance for users and nearby peers [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Blended designs** combine online and face-to-face components; blended learning with structured online interaction outperforms either mode alone [~M] — see [Blended Learning](../patterns/blended-learning.md)
- **Flipped designs** move exposition online to free class time for active work — see [Flipped Classroom](../patterns/flipped-classroom.md)
- **Adaptive systems** adjust task difficulty and pacing to individual performance — see [Adaptive Learning](../principles/adaptive-learning.md)
- **Simulation and visualization tools** (PhET, Labster) make dynamic or invisible processes observable

### Target Learners
- Novices benefit from well-segmented, signalled multimedia that manages working memory load [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Advanced learners benefit from learner-controlled pacing and richer environments, but can be harmed by redundant guidance [Guidance benefits reverse as learner expertise grows.](../claims/expertise-reversal-effect.md) [~M]
- Learners with limited access or digital fluency need scaffolding for the tool itself before it can support content learning

### Target Learning Goals
- Conceptual understanding through dynamic visualization and simulation
- Procedural fluency through adaptive practice with immediate feedback [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S]
- Collaborative and communicative skills through structured online interaction

### Instructions
1. Define the learning goal and identify where technology adds capability beyond non-digital alternatives.
2. Select or design media following multimedia principles: coherent, signalled, segmented, narration-plus-graphics rather than narration-plus-redundant-text [Multimedia learning is improved by coherence, signaling, redundancy, and segmentation principles.](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511811678) [+S].
3. Embed interaction — questions, [Practice](../elements/practice.md), simulations, or discussion — rather than passive viewing [Active learning improves exam performance.](../claims/active-learning-improves-exam-performance.md) [+S].
4. Use platform analytics or [Assessment](../elements/assessment.md) data to monitor progress and adjust pacing or difficulty.
5. Teach the tool explicitly; ensure the interface itself does not consume working memory needed for learning [Managing cognitive load is essential when novel interfaces add extraneous demand.](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md) [+M].

## Examples
- **[PhET Interactive Simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu)** — research-validated science simulations; studies show guided inquiry use outperforms unstructured exploration.
- **[Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org)** — segmented video exposition paired with adaptive practice and immediate feedback.
- **[Labster](https://www.labster.com)** — virtual laboratory simulations used to prepare for or substitute for physical labs.
- **Perusall** — social annotation platform that converts assigned reading into interactive, assessable engagement.
