# Study longitudinal, quality inclusion across the full schooling period, since no cohort study of children in quality inclusive settings throughout schooling exists

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The author argues that inclusion has yet to be truly tried, stating he knows of no cohort study of children who have been in quality inclusive settings throughout their schooling. He recommends studying this administrative arrangement with a longitudinal lens, noting the follow-up data show the necessity of such a design to evaluate the arrangement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Longitudinal follow-up spanning multiple years of schooling with quality-defined inclusive placements
#### Constraints
- The author quotes an instructor's caveat that only after a perfectly designed intervention is implemented by perfectly trained personnel can one speculate about the capabilities of people with disabilities

### Target Learners
- children with autism spectrum disorder across K–12 schooling

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term academic, social, cognitive, and adaptive outcomes of sustained inclusive placement

## Claims

- [Fully inclusive classrooms differed from less inclusive settings on two observed quality dimensions: peer membership (means 4.7 vs. 1.9) and classroom climate (means 4.3 vs. 3.1), both favoring inclusive settings](../claims/inclusive-classrooms-higher-membership-and-climate.md) [+W]
- [Among CARS-matched pairs, autism symptom scores 4 years post favored the included member of each pair (d = .68), and prosocial skills favored included children (d = .48)](../claims/inclusive-placement-autism-symptoms-prosocial.md) [+W]
- [When kindergarten placement is treated as an independent variable, CARS-matched pairs show large, statistically significant outcome differences favoring children in inclusive settings (d = .66–.81)](../claims/inclusive-placement-matched-pair-large-effects.md) [+W]
- [Four years after the LEAP RCT, LEAP graduates outperformed comparison children significantly on cognitive (Leiter, d = .42) and social skills (SSRS prosocial, d = .52), with non-significant differences on achievement, language, and adaptive behavior](../claims/leap-followup-significant-cognitive-social-gains.md) [+W]
- [Qualitative interviews with data collectors attributed outcome differences between placements to curriculum content and dosage, paraeducator support styles, and teacher expectations](../claims/placement-outcome-differences-qualitative-themes.md) [~W]
