# Self-Regulated Strategy Development (SRSD)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 3 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 review, 1 theoretical), `q1`–`q4` · 1 of 3 report an effect size

## Description
SRSD is an instructional approach designed to enhance students' writing skills by addressing their affective, behavioral, and cognitive characteristics. It integrates findings from cognitive development, behavior, and affect research. Teachers explicitly teach a genre-specific writing strategy (e.g., planning or revising), then scaffold students toward independent, self-regulated use of that strategy — including goal setting, self-monitoring, self-instruction, and self-reinforcement — while fostering positive attitudes about writing and about themselves as writers. The core goals are to develop knowledge about writing, support students' ability to monitor and manage their own writing, and promote positive attitudes toward writing.

## Design Implications

SRSD is one of the most strongly supported writing interventions available: meta-analytic evidence shows large effects on writing quality, structure, and length, with gains maintained and generalized over time [Graham & Perin's meta-analysis found strategy instruction among the most effective writing practices.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.99.3.445) [+S]. Its power comes from combining explicit strategy teaching with gradual release of self-regulatory control to the learner, consistent with [Self-Regulated Learning](../theories/self-regulated-learning.md) theory.

### Instructions
1. Develop background knowledge — activate prerequisite skills and vocabulary for the genre ([Activation](../elements/activation.md))
2. Discuss the strategy — establish what it is, why it helps, and when to use it ([Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md))
3. Model the strategy — teacher performs a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) while using the strategy, including self-regulation statements ("I'll check that my reasons support my topic sentence")
4. Memorize it — students learn the strategy steps until they can recite them ([Practice](../elements/practice.md))
5. Support it — students use the strategy with scaffolds, self-monitoring charts, and collaborative writing, with supports gradually faded ([Practice](../elements/practice.md); [Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md))

## Examples
- **TREE for opinion essays** — Students learn to state a Topic sentence, list Reasons, Examine each reason, and End with a conclusion, using a graphic organizer and self-monitoring checklist before writing independently.

- **SRSD for persuasive writing in middle school** — De La Paz and Graham combined planning strategy instruction with genre knowledge lessons, producing essays with more elements and higher quality scores than comparison instruction.
- **POW+TREE in elementary persuasive writing** — students learn to Pick an idea, Organize notes, Write and say more, then plan essays with Topic sentence, Reasons, Explanations, and Ending; extensively validated by Harris, Graham, and Mason with students in grades 2–6.

# Self Regulated Strategy Development

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
SRSD (Graham & Harris) teaches learners a concrete academic strategy—most commonly for writing, but also math and reading comprehension—through six stages: develop background knowledge, discuss it, model it, memorize it, support it, and independent performance. Crucially, strategy instruction is paired with explicit training in self-regulation: learners set goals, monitor their own performance, use self-talk to manage effort and frustration, and evaluate their outcomes. The goal is not just that learners *can* use the strategy, but that they *do* use it on their own, maintaining and generalizing it beyond the instructional setting.

## Design Implications

SRSD rests on the premise that struggling learners often fail not because they lack ability but because they lack both a strategy and the metacognitive machinery to deploy it persistently. Meta-analyses show large effects on writing quality and strategy maintenance, particularly when self-regulation components are included rather than strategy steps alone [Strategy instruction with self-regulation components improves writing outcomes.](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2018.1481404) [+S]. The modeling stage is where self-regulation becomes visible: instructors use [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) to verbalize not only the strategy steps but also the self-statements ("Okay, this part is hard—slow down and check my plan") that regulate effort and emotion.

- A well-specified, teachable strategy with memorable steps (e.g., POW + TREE: Pick my idea, Organize notes, Write and say more; Topic sentence, Reasons, Explanations, Ending)

- Scaffolded practice with collaborative and supported performance before independent use ([Scaffolding](../principles/scaffolding.md), [Practice](../elements/practice.md))
- Explicit development of background knowledge so the strategy connects to what learners already know
- Time: SRSD is typically delivered across multiple sessions, not a single lesson

### Instructions
1. **Develop background knowledge** — ensure learners have the prerequisite skills and vocabulary the strategy assumes; activate relevant experience ([Activation](../principles/activation.md)).
2. **Discuss the strategy** — examine current performance, introduce the strategy, and build commitment: learners compare their unaided attempts with what the strategy could offer.
3. **Model it** — the instructor performs the task using [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md), verbalizing both strategy steps and self-regulatory statements (goal setting, self-instruction, coping with difficulty).
4. **Memorize it** — learners learn the strategy steps (often via mnemonic charts) until retrieval is automatic, freeing working memory for content ([Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md)).
5. **Support it** — practice with collaborative modeling, graphic organizers, and prompts, then [Fading](../elements/fading.md) of supports as learners take over regulation of their own performance [Support fading promotes transfer of responsibility to the learner.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M].
6. **Independent performance** — learners use the strategy without supports; instructors provide [feedback](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) focused on strategy use and self-regulation rather than only the final product [Feedback is most effective at task and process levels.](../claims/feedback-most-effective-at-task-and-process-levels.md) [+S].
