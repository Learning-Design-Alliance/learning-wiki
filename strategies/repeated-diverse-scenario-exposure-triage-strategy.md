# Embed repeated exposure to diverse disaster triage scenarios in training to build adaptive decision-making

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
In the discussion, the authors recommend that, to reduce triage time in high-uncertainty disaster situations, "training should incorporate repetitive exposure to diverse scenarios, enabling individuals to develop adaptive decision-making skills under complex and unpredictable conditions." This strategy treats VR simulation as a repeatable, resource-light environment where learners repeatedly apply triage protocols across varied casualty cases rather than a single scenario pass. The study's own program embedded repeated training sessions and six varied casualty cases in line with this approach.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A simulation environment that can present varied casualty scenarios repeatedly at low cost
#### Constraints
- The article states meaningful triage-time differences may be difficult to observe after a single-session intervention

### Target Learners
- Nursing students and prospective healthcare professionals training for disaster response

### Target Learning Goals
- Rapid disaster triage decision-making
- Adaptive decision-making under uncertainty
