# A simulation-based teaching sequence that elicits, analyses and scaffolds student discernment using the ADD and MD hierarchies

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
An implementable teaching recipe in two phases. Phase one: choose a semiotic resource that realistically represents the target disciplinary knowledge, evaluate its disciplinary affordances, envision what students will discern, "ask for their discernment using, for example, an 'exit ticket'" or web questionnaire, analyse answers with the ADD and MD hierarchies, and plan teaching from this. Phase two is content-specific, e.g. gravity and orbits or phases of the Moon, with students revisiting the resource to discern new affordances.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A suitable semiotic resource that clearly and realistically represents the disciplinary knowledge to be taught
- Analysis of student answers using the ADD and MD hierarchies to locate students' levels
#### Constraints
- For the phases-of-the-Moon example, the exercises should be supported by real naked-eye observations of the Moon

### Target Learners
- university astronomy students in planetary astronomy and introductory courses

### Target Learning Goals
- discerning disciplinary affordances of simulations
- building a conceptual model of moon phases
- understanding gravity and planetary orbits

### Affordances
- Reading The Sky Competency Model
- Anatomy Of Disciplinary Discernment Hierarchy
- Multidimensionality Hierarchy E3D
