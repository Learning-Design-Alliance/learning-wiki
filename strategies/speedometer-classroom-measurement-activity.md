# Engage students in measuring instantaneous speed with the speedometer, timing the motion by video analysis or by the sound of bells

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
A concrete classroom activity: students release a sphere from chosen points on the incline, measure the time T for distance D on the horizontal track, and compute the instantaneous speed as D/T, assembling tables of t, x and v. The article notes "Galileo's device provides an oppor- tunity to engage students in a practical activ- ity, reifying an abstract concept that many of them ﬁnd diﬃcult to grasp".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A speedometer track with a smooth incline-to-horizontal transition.
- A timing method: video analysis with Tracker, photogates, or recording the sound of little bells hung over the sphere's path.
#### Constraints
- Time resolution of the reported video setup is about 10⁻² s (cellphone camera at 120 frames/second).

### Target Learners
- introductory physics students
- high-school students

### Target Learning Goals
- measure instantaneous speed operationally
- obtain the laws of uniformly accelerated motion from measurements
