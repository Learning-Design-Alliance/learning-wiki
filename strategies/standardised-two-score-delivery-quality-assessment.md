# Develop standardised delivery-quality assessment using a maximum of two scores (one communication, one content-delivery)

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
To overcome the lack of clarity in healthcare professional delivery-quality outcomes, the review recommends that future studies assess delivery quality with at most two scores: one for communication skills and one for delivery of intended intervention content. The review states "it is suggested that future studies develop a standardised method that can assess delivery quality via a maximum of two scores (one for communication and one for delivery of intended intervention content)", which would decrease the potential for bias due to multiple outcome measurement.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assessment must cover both communication skills and delivery of intended intervention content
- Future studies must comply with recognised reporting guidelines to ensure clarity of methodology and results
#### Constraints
- Validated and reliable methods exist for patient-centred communication skills, but there is a lack of validated measures for content-delivery in health behaviour change interventions

### Target Learners
- Researchers evaluating healthcare professional training interventions

### Target Learning Goals
- Measuring delivery quality of health behaviour change interventions
