# Private-public partnership to deliver free simulation training to public-sector health professionals in low- and middle-income settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a partnership in which a private simulation center offers high-quality obstetric emergency training free of charge to public maternity staff, aligned with the WHO reproductive health strategy advocating "collaboration and complementary action among the private, non-governmental and public sectors". In this implementation the state health department selected hospital order by regional maternal mortality ratio, released staff for two days, and covered lodging and food, while the private partner supplied the center, instructors and course. The authors state the model "could be replicated in similar settings" and suggest governments create non-material incentives.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Strong commitment and determination from both partners
- Strategic organization, including flexibility in scheduling modules
- Legal agreements drafted between both partners' legal departments
#### Constraints
- Formalizing the partnership took several weeks of legal negotiation because the model was new
- Participants were selected by hospital directors, limiting generalizability of results

### Target Learners
- Healthcare professionals working in public maternity hospitals

### Target Learning Goals
- Improved management of obstetric emergencies to reduce maternal mortality

## Claims

- [In one uncontrolled pre-post study, test scores rose from pre-course to immediate post-course in all four modules of a 16-hour simulation-based obstetric emergency course (p < 0.001)](../claims/simulation-course-significant-score-increase-all-modules.md) [+W]
- [Average proportional score change was similar across hemorrhage, sepsis and resuscitation modules and significantly higher than in the hypertension module (p = 0.019)](../claims/hemorrhage-sepsis-resuscitation-gains-exceed-hypertension.md) [+W]
- [Participants rated the course modules very highly, with mean overall satisfaction 9.6–9.8 on a 0–10 scale](../claims/high-satisfaction-obstetric-simulation-modules.md) [+W]
