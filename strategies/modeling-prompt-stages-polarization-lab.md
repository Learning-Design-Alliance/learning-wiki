# Use guided-inquiry prompts organized by modeling-framework stages (construction, prediction, measurement modeling, comparison, refinement) in a polarization-of-light lab

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The paper implements modeling learning goals through example prompts aligned with the framework stages of Fig. 1: construction prompts for the physical system, prediction prompts (e.g., "Using the Jones formalism, derive Malus' law"), prompts constructing models of measurement tools and using them to interpret data, comparison prompts (e.g., producing circularly polarized light and measuring its elliptical polarization parameters), and refinement prompts that "extend their ex isting model to include non-ideal features that could account for the deviation and use them t o make new predictions."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Prompted activities require prior conditions: model sophistication appropriate to available time and students' prior knowledge of the physics ideas and apparatus
#### Constraints
- Some groups found the mathematical and computational modeling challenging, per the paper's implementation discussion

### Target Learners
- junior-year-or-beyond physics students in the CU advanced lab

### Target Learning Goals
- constructing and refining quantitative models of optical systems and measurement tools
- quantitatively modeling systematic error sources
