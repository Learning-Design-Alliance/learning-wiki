# Improve audiovisual quality, connectivity, and standardized recordings before deploying live-streamed procedural teaching

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The authors recommend concrete improvements before using first-person–view live streaming for procedural skills teaching: "We recommend improvements in the audiovisual quality of the camera and a stable internet connection before performing the LS teaching method." They also suggest higher-quality recording devices, requiring all students to connect to university Wi-Fi, and providing a standardized recording of the procedure so students can review and gain confidence before performing independently.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Higher-quality recording devices, stringent monitoring of connectivity with mandatory university Wi-Fi, and minimized audio delay
#### Constraints
- Using prerecorded video to standardize teaching material would remove the interactive quality of the live-streamed, first-person–view method

### Target Learners
- medical students in surgical clerkships learning procedural skills

### Target Learning Goals
- acquisition and self-directed practice of manual procedural skills such as simple wound suturing
