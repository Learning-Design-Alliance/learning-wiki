# Reduce stakes, align question difficulty with exams, and add game-like feedback when implementing TBL to lower student stress

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
When extending TBL to a new program, the authors made three changes: reduced the TBL grade contribution from 29% to 2%, cognitively aligned TBL questions with unit exams, and used scratch-off IF-AT cards for instantaneous, less stressful feedback. They conclude "the qualitative experience of students may be an important consideration that should be carefully evaluated during implementation of a new teaching technique."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Peer review of questions by faculty to verify content level and a single best answer
- Heterogeneous team assignment balancing performance levels
#### Constraints
- The comparison involved two different student populations, a confound the authors state could skew results
- The classic TBL format's advocates argue divergence may diminish effectiveness

### Target Learners
- first-year professional (medical and dental) students

### Target Learning Goals
- physiology concept mastery
- reduced assessment-related stress
- team collaboration skills
