# Use brief student-driven meta-moments before each inquiry phase to prompt reflection on how to perform that phase

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Meta-moments are short, whole-group reflective pauses inserted before each phase of an inquiry activity. Noting that students struggled at each phase, the instructors "decided to implement a series of short meta-cognitive moments where we gave the students a chance to reﬂect upon each phase" — e.g., asking "What makes a good questioner?" before question generation and listing student answers publicly. The moments were "entirely student-driven so as to give them ownership and a sense of responsibility for fulﬁlling the phase", and the authors report they can stimulate progress toward attitudinal goals and make process and content goals easier to achieve.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A facilitator prepared with prompts and a backup list of items to avoid lulls
- Time slots in the activity schedule (5-10 minutes each)
