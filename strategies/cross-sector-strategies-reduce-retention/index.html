Use cross-sector data-driven strategies — housing affordability, green space, restorative discipline, and civic engagement curricula — to reduce retention and raise graduation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · no claims cited

Description¶

The brief's policy and practice takeaways direct educators, city leaders, policymakers, and advocates to act across education, neighborhood, and housing policy. Recommendations include "Using data-driven discussions to understand what additional supports students need to avoid being retained in grade," increasing housing affordability and green space, building teams of social workers and counselors, supporting high-poverty schools, and building supportive climates via restorative practices and civic engagement curricula.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Coordination across education, neighborhood, and housing policy areas at federal, state, and district levels

Constraints¶

  • Grounded in observational associations from one district (Chicago Public Schools, 2012-15 cohorts), not tested interventions

Target Learners¶

  • K-12 students in urban public school districts, particularly students at risk of grade retention

Target Learning Goals¶

  • High school graduation; preventing grade retention and overage-for-grade status

Related Strategies¶

  • Apply the neighborhood-centered grouping approach in other cities for research, policy decisions, and service allocation
