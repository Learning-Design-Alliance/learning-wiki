# Provide real-time multimodal feedback during early training, then withdraw guidance for unassisted assessment to prevent over-reliance

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The IMR-SS provided "concurrent real-time multimodal feedback (visual warnings, auditory prompts, and haptic cues) during training, allowing participants to immediately recognize and correct mistakes," which the authors note is especially beneficial for novices. Because some studies caution that excessive navigational cues may cause over-reliance, the design deliberately removed the headset for the assessment session; the IMR group retained significantly better freehand accuracy than controls even without guidance. The authors describe a phased reduction of visual aids through hierarchical instructional design so skills transfer from virtual to real-world application.
