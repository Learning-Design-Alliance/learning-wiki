# Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS)

## Description
PBIS is a multi-tiered behavioral framework in which schools explicitly define, teach, and reinforce a small set of positive behavioral expectations (e.g., "be safe, be respectful, be responsible") across all settings. Tier 1 applies universal expectations and acknowledgment systems to all students; Tier 2 adds targeted group supports (e.g., check-in/check-out); Tier 3 provides individualized, function-based behavior plans. The approach is carried out through direct teaching of routines, frequent positive acknowledgment, consistent consequences, and continuous data-based decision-making.

## Design Implications

PBIS treats behavior like an academic skill: it is taught, practiced, and reinforced rather than assumed or punished into shape. This aligns with [Behaviorism](../principles/behaviorism.md) — reinforcement contingencies shape behavior — but extends it with explicit instruction and systems-level consistency. Meta-analytic evidence shows school-wide PBIS reduces office discipline referrals and suspensions and improves school climate [School-wide positive behavior support improves school climate and reduces discipline referrals.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S], with smaller but positive effects on academic achievement [+M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A leadership team with administrator support and a defined set of 3–5 positively stated expectations
- Explicit lesson plans that teach expectations in each setting, like any [Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) sequence
- A reinforcement/acknowledgment system (tokens, praise, privileges) delivered consistently by all staff
- Behavioral data collection (referrals, attendance) reviewed regularly for tier placement decisions
- Family and staff buy-in; the framework fails when only some classrooms participate

#### Constraints
- Implementation without fidelity — expectations posted but never taught or reinforced — produces no measurable benefit [-S]; effects depend on sustained (3+ years) high-fidelity implementation
- Punitive or inconsistent staff responses undermine the reinforcement system; mixed messaging reduces effectiveness [~M]
- Tier 1 alone is insufficient for students with intensive needs; withholding Tier 2/3 supports while claiming "PBIS doesn't work" is a common misattribution [-M]
- Critiques note that acknowledgment systems can feel controlling or inequitable if rewards favor already-compliant students; culturally responsive adaptation is required [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Check-In/Check-Out (CICO)** — a Tier 2 strategy pairing students with a mentor for daily goal-setting and feedback; widely replicated
- **Classroom-level PBIS** — the same teach-and-reinforce cycle applied to routines (transitions, group work) within a single classroom
- **Integrating with SEL** — combining PBIS with social-emotional curricula to address skill deficits alongside behavior

### Target Learners
- All students benefit from predictable, positively framed expectations [+S]
- Students with externalizing behavior or a history of discipline referrals benefit most from Tier 2/3 supports [+M]
- Empathic, relationship-based delivery matters: brief empathic discipline interventions reduce suspensions, especially for marginalized students [Brief empathic discipline interventions reduce suspensions.](../claims/brief-intervention-empathic-discipline-cuts-suspensions.md) [+S]
- Belonging-supportive framing improves outcomes for at-risk students [Belonging interventions improve outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency in behavioral routines (transitions, help-seeking, collaboration)
- Self-regulation and social skill acquisition
- School climate and engagement as preconditions for academic learning

### Instructions
1. Define 3–5 positively stated school-wide expectations with staff and, where possible, student input.
2. Create a behavior matrix specifying what each expectation looks like in each setting (hallway, cafeteria, classroom).
3. Teach expectations directly using modeling and [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) — brief lessons, role-play, and practice in context, the way academic content is taught.
4. Establish an acknowledgment system that reinforces expected behavior at high rates initially, then thins toward natural reinforcement.
5. Respond to problem behavior with consistent, instructional (not purely punitive) consequences.
6. Collect referral and acknowledgment data monthly; use team review to move students into Tier 2 supports such as [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) structures or Tier 3 individualized plans with appropriate [Accommodations](../elements/accommodations.md).

## Related Strategies
- [Classroom Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) — community-building practices that reinforce respectful norms
- Restorative practices — an alternative/complement to exclusionary consequences within Tier 2/3

## Examples
- **Maryland Safe and Supportive Schools / Maryland PBIS Partnership** — statewide implementation studied in a large randomized trial (Bradshaw et al., 2010), showing reduced suspensions and improved organizational health.
- **Check-In/Check-Out (CICO)** — a Tier 2 intervention in which a mentor greets the student each morning, reviews point goals, and gives end-of-day feedback; one of the most replicated PBIS Tier 2 practices.
- **Illinois PBIS Network (now Midwest PBIS, [pbis.org](https://www.pbis.org))** — the national technical assistance center providing implementation guides, fidelity measures (e.g., TFI, BoQ), and state-level scaling.
