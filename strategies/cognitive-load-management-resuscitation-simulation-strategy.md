# Manage cognitive load in complex resuscitation simulation through scenario sequencing, role rotation, and immediate feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 2 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a three-part instructional design that optimises cognitive load during complex resuscitation training: scenario sequencing that decomposes the resuscitation process into manageable sub-tasks, role assignment and rotation so learners focus on one responsibility per session, and immediate feedback with reflection to clarify misconceptions promptly. Pre-class micro-lessons and case materials activate prior knowledge, and non-essential alarms are disabled during simulation to reduce extraneous load.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Standardized step-by-step scenario progression with defined phases
- Pre-class micro-lessons and case-based materials prepared before training
- Instructor input and collaborative discussion available for immediate feedback
#### Constraints
- Described as the authors' theoretical interpretation aligned with cognitive load theory, not separately tested in this study

### Target Learners
- Paediatric postgraduate trainees in emergency resuscitation training

### Target Learning Goals
- Effective performance within complex resuscitation scenarios
- Reduced extraneous cognitive load during simulation
