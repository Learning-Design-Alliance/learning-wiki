# Use social-belonging interventions that provide students a framework for interpreting ambiguous social cues and making attributions

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size

## Description
Social-belonging interventions, informed by attributional theory, give students a framework for interpreting ambiguous cues about whether they belong — for example, that facing challenges and questioning belonging is normal and "will pass with time". Logel explains such interventions "can, therefore, have significant impacts on people's experiences and outcomes without even changing the actual context or actual cues of belonging they are seeing". The article calls them a notable exception to the general lack of applied implications from belonging theory.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires delivery of an attributional framework that normalizes belonging worries as temporary and non-diagnostic of nonbelonging
#### Constraints
- The article notes school belonging interventions remain scarce in general; effects depend on students' social identity and context

### Target Learners
- school and higher-education students, particularly those vigilant for cues about whether people like them belong

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining students' sense of belonging and improving experiences and outcomes

### Affordances
- Integrative Framework Of Belonging School Extension
