Strategies

Sustained, rigorous, relevant technology-centered PD with school-district support, collaborative facilitation, and lesson-plan sharing to promote faithful implementation¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), q3 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article's PD design recipe: a weeklong summer institute followed by six half-day Saturday sessions during the school year, planned and implemented collaboratively by university faculty and school-based master teachers. Facilitators modeled the target instruction, discussed "How will you teach this content using DG software?", encouraged teachers to share insights and problems, and had teachers prepare and share lesson plans.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • PD must be "sustained, rigorous, and relevant to participating teachers, with substantial sup port from their school districts"
  • Teachers must first master the approach themselves before implementing it in classrooms

Constraints¶

  • Without professional development, the article notes (citing Clements et al., 2011), "teachers often fail to implement new approa ches faithfully"

Target Learners¶

  • inservice high school geometry teachers

Target Learning Goals¶

  • mastery of the DG instructional approach
  • integration of dynamic geometry software into geometry teaching

Claims¶

  • DG-group lessons were significantly better designed for conjecture-to-proof progression and used dynamic features far more than control lessons, while other observed teaching aspects did not differ significantly [+W]
  • A weeklong DG-centered summer institute improved teachers' conjecturing-proving knowledge, with a statistically significant average gain of 1.37 points (p = .003) [+W]
  • DG-group teachers felt more comfortable than effective using GSP, and teacher use of GSP exceeded student hands-on use during the school year [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Train educators through a Summer Institute plus PLC and coaching cycles on data use and instructional routines
  • Provide 70 hours of professional development in three phases: intensive training, practice, and implementation
  • Design professional development that is intensive, on-site, peer-based, responsive, and grounded in teachers' real classroom experiences
  • Brief initial training with extensive follow-up coaching and on-site facilitation for whole-school program implementation
  • Layered implementation support for teachers adopting a new science curriculum: preservice training, follow-up training, and a science coach
  • Pair new instructional practices with sustained in-class coaching after initial professional development
  • Pair Tier 2 intervention delivery with professional development workshops and in-class coaching visits
  • Provide comprehensive professional development and ongoing coaching when implementing a structured literacy program
  • Implement Reading Edge with two days of initial teacher training plus monthly in-class coaching and peer support structures
