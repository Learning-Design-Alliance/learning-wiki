# Guided systems-diagramming warm-up on a functioning AO workbench

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
In this short activity, students familiar with some AO terminology are shown the functioning system with minimal explanation and, as a team, trace light through it and draw block diagrams and ray traces. Tracing the beam with a notecard helps them identify collimated regions, focal points, beam diameter changes and which detector displays what image. Assessment follows the article's own criteria: "Students can be assessed on the correctness of their parts identification, the accuracy of their diagrams and the completeness of their ray-traces." Group discussion of observations and of good diagram properties is scaffolded by the facilitator.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A functioning workbench whose LLA spot array and science-camera image are visibly distinguishable, and a computer program tracking the LLA spots
#### Constraints
- Designed as a short, guided activity for students already familiar with some AO terminology

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students
- short-course participants

### Target Learning Goals
- systems thinking and block diagramming
- beam expansion, compression and collimation
- identifying subsystems and light pathways
