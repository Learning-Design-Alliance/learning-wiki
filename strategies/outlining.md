# Outlining

## Description
Outlining asks learners to represent content as a hierarchical structure — main ideas, sub-ideas, and supporting details — before producing a full text or while processing source material. It can be instructor-provided (a skeleton to complete), learner-generated (a plan built before writing), or co-constructed. The outline functions as an external planning device that offloads organization from working memory.

## Design Implications

Outlining reduces the attentional overload of juggling ideation, organization, and transcription simultaneously, which is why outline strategies improve writing quality and efficiency [Kellogg's work on planning strategies shows outlines reduce attentional load during composition.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.14.2.355) [+M]. As a reading or study tool, an outline acts as an [Advance Organizer](../elements/advance-organizers.md) that gives learners a structure into which new material can be assimilated [Advance organizers improve learning and retention.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]. Because outlines impose a single hierarchy, they work best when the content genuinely has a hierarchical structure; forcing linear outlines onto web-like content can distort understanding [Concept mapping improves learning.](../claims/concept-mapping-improves-learning.md) [~M].

### Context
#### Requirements
- Source material or a writing task with identifiable main-idea structure
- An explicit model of what a good outline looks like (levels, labels, granularity)
- A follow-on task that uses the outline (drafting, summarizing, presenting) — an outline made and abandoned adds little [Pairing planning with application supports transfer better than planning alone.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]
- For novice writers, instruction in how to select and order ideas, not just how to format them [Explicitly taught planning strategies improve writing quality.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.89.1.170) [+S]

#### Constraints
- Learners with weak topic knowledge cannot outline meaningfully — they lack the basis for judging what is a main idea; pre-teaching or source scaffolds are needed first
- Overly detailed outlines can consume planning time that drafting needs, and can lock writers into a structure they cannot revise [~W]
- For creative or exploratory writing, a rigid outline can suppress discovery-based composing; looser "working plans" fit better [~W]
- Copying an instructor's outline without generating the hierarchy themselves produces shallow engagement [-M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Completion outlines**: instructor provides headings; learners fill in details (lower load, good for novices)
- **Reverse outlining**: learners extract an outline from a finished draft to diagnose structure (powerful revision tool)
- **Sentence outlines vs. topic outlines**: sentence outlines force fuller commitment to claims; topic outlines are faster
- **Digital outliners** (e.g., Workflowy, Roam Research, Notion) allow restructuring, mitigating the rigidity cost
- **Collaborative outlining**: groups negotiate hierarchy, surfacing disagreements about what is central

### Target Learners
- Novice writers, whose planning is typically sparse and reactive [Explicitly taught planning strategies improve writing quality.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.89.1.170) [+S]
- Learners with high working-memory demands in the task (long texts, multi-source synthesis) [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- Less beneficial for expert writers, who often plan mentally or in fragments and may find formal outlining redundant [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Text structure and discourse organization: understanding how ideas relate hierarchically
- Reading comprehension: extracting and sequencing main ideas from expository text [Advance organizers improve learning and retention.](../claims/advance-organizers-improve-learning.md) [+M]
- Study skills: converting material into a reviewable structure for later [Distributed practice improves retention.](../claims/distributed-practice-improves-retention.md) [+M] (an outline is a natural spaced-review artifact)

### Instructions
1. Model the skill: outline a short text or plan a sample essay aloud, showing how you decide what is a main idea ([Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) if available; otherwise narrated modeling).
2. Provide a partial outline and have learners complete it, using [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) to keep each level manageable.
3. Fade support: move from completion outlines to learner-generated outlines of new material ([Fading](../elements/fading.md) if available; otherwise gradual release).
4. Require use: learners draft, summarize, or present from their outline, then compare their structure against the source or a peer's ([Annotating](../principles/annotating.md) the outline during comparison).
5. For revision, have learners reverse-outline their own drafts to expose structural gaps.

## Related Strategies
- [Advance Organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) — the instructor-provided counterpart: a structure given before learning rather than built by the learner
- [Concept Mapping](../elements/concept-mapping.md) — the non-hierarchical alternative when relationships are cross-cutting rather than tree-like
- [Summarization](summarization.md) — outlining and summarizing both force selection of main ideas; outlining preserves structure, summarizing compresses it

## Examples
- **SRSD (Self-Regulated Strategy Development) writing instruction** — explicitly teaches students to plan essays with outlining/brainstorming before drafting; strong evidence base for improving writing quality (see [De La Paz & Graham, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.89.1.170)).
- **Reverse outlining in writing centers** — University of North Carolina's Writing Center [handout](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/revising-drafts/) has students outline their own drafts to diagnose organization problems.
- **Textbook chapter outlines** — platforms like [Perusall](https://www.perusall.com) and instructor-built reading guides ask students to reconstruct a chapter's hierarchy before seminar discussion.
