# Use structured, formal learning beyond the profession (e.g., classical liberal arts content) to grow mid-career faculty reflective capacity

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Given faculty scores near the center of the epistemic scale and a gap relative to very-late-career faculty benchmarks, the authors recommend professional development built on a trajectory of growth on the King and Kitchener scale. They suggest that "structured, formal learning not necessarily related to the profession of college professor or teacher educator-perhaps more classical, liberal arts, or content in nature-would contribute to increasing the reflective capacity of their faculty." The recommendation rests on their own mid-range findings and the Glenn and Eklund comparison rather than a tested intervention.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Baseline scaling of faculty reflective capacity on the RJM to set individual growth trajectories
- Professional growth activities matched to current stage levels
#### Constraints
- The article did not test this intervention; it is proposed as fodder for future work based on the observed gap between approximately 4.0 stage scores and the high 5.0 range

### Target Learners
- mid- to late-career college faculty and teacher educators

### Target Learning Goals
- reflective judgment
- personal reflective capacity
