# Provide students with regular green educational settings and experiences, from short nature breaks to greened schoolyards, to relieve cognitive overload and support learning

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 10 claims (6 for, 4 mixed) · 4 studies (3 review, 1 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 4 report an effect size · 9 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that schools offer nature contact in varied forms, since its included interventions ranged "from a few to 60 min" short exposures to long-term exposures of "2 months to 2 years", spanning passive window views, green walls, walks, green playgrounds and school gardens. It concludes that "Schools are well placed to provide much needed 'green' educational settings and experiences" to relieve cognitive overload and stress and optimize wellbeing and learning. Short-term exposures showed the most consistent attentional benefits.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to a natural environment such as a park, greened schoolyard, garden, or a green view or plants in the classroom
#### Constraints
- Benefits were not uniform: impulse control, processing speed and sustained/selective attention showed null or mixed results, and effects differed by age and school level

### Target Learners
- school children aged 5–18
- adolescents in primary and secondary school

### Target Learning Goals
- selective and sustained attention
- working memory
- wellbeing and stress reduction supporting learning
