# Responding with "Yet"

## Description
Adding the word "yet" to responses when learners express frustration or inability (e.g., "I can't do this") introduces the idea of a growth mindset, fostering the belief that abilities can improve over time. This simple addition signals that current limitations are not permanent and encourages persistence by implying that success is still attainable with continued effort and learning.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a shift in mindset from instructors and learners to view limitations as temporary. Consistent use of "yet" in responses to learner frustrations and challenges. Provides support and encouragement to help learners overcome obstacles.
#### Constraints
- Some learners may initially dismiss the use of "yet" as empty encouragement if not accompanied by concrete support and guidance. Requires consistent reinforcement to maintain a positive effect. May be challenging to implement in situations where there are genuine limitations or barriers.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe changes in learner behavior, such as increased persistence, positive self-talk, and willingness to seek help. Track learner responses to challenges after the introduction of "yet". Analyze learner reflections on their growth and learning experiences.

## Impact
- Research supports the use of "yet" to promote a growth mindset and encourage persistence. Adding "yet" can shift learners' perspectives and increase their motivation to continue learning. Encouragement helps learners make progress more smoothly and climb the ladder faster.
