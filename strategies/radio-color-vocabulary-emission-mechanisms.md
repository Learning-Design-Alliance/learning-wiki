# Teach a color vocabulary for the invisible universe by naturalizing radio color images by emission mechanism

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
Module 4B has students split radio data into three RGB sub-bands and build calibrated, "naturalized" color images in which color encodes emission mechanism rather than temperature. The article explains: "Thermal sources have a +2 spectral index, and consequently appear 'thermal' blue, like O stars do to the eye at optical wavelengths. Bremsstrahlung sources (e.g., star-forming regions) appear blue to white. Synchrotron sources (e.g., supernova remnants and supermassive black holes at the cores of active galaxies) appear white to light red." Through this, "Students develop a deeper understanding of these continuum-emitting processes, and learn a new color vocabulary, applicable to the invisible universe."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small radio maps of bright sources, some collected in earlier modules, that can be split post facto into RGB sub-bands
#### Constraints
- Color indicates emission mechanism here, not temperature as at optical wavelengths, so students must adopt a new interpretive convention

### Target Learners
- students who have completed the Module 1 radio skills introduction

### Target Learning Goals
- identifying emission mechanisms by color
- understanding thermal, bremsstrahlung, and synchrotron continuum processes
