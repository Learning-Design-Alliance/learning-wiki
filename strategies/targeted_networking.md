# Targeted Networking

## Description
The "Targeted Networking" strategy involves focusing on specific activities, groups, or individuals who align with one's interests and goals in order to build meaningful relationships and connections. It includes doing substantive research on prospective contacts and identifying shared interests or purposes to facilitate more authentic and mutually beneficial interactions. This approach emphasizes quality over quantity, aiming to develop potent networks through high-stakes activities that connect individuals with diverse backgrounds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Conducting thorough research on potential contacts, identifying shared interests or purposes, initiating substantive conversations, and actively participating in relevant activities.
#### Constraints
- May be time-consuming, requiring dedication to research and engagement. Limited opportunities may arise if interests are too narrow or contacts are overly selective.

## Assessment Evidence
- Number of meaningful connections established, frequency of interactions, positive outcomes from collaborations, quality of shared knowledge.

## Impact
- Research has shown that potent networks are not forged through casual interactions but through relatively high-stakes activities that connect you with diverse others. Meaningful relationships are more likely to develop when driven by substantive, shared interests identified through serious research.
