# Adopt integrated 3D visualization, software-assisted annotation, and PBL for orthopedic training, with multi-institution and longitudinal validation

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (4 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 causal), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend their multimodal approach "for broader application in orthopedic training" and propose future investigations that "expand multi-institutional cohorts to enhance representativeness, validate the integrated 3D-annotation-PBL methodology across orthopedic subspecialties, and conduct longitudinal studies evaluating sustained clinical competency development". The strategy pairs technology-pedagogy integration with an evidence-building agenda rather than claiming general readiness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- 3D anatomy software, annotation tooling, and PBL-facilitated group sessions
#### Constraints
- Current evidence is from a single institution with 120 participants and short-term outcomes only; applicability beyond spinal surgery to subspecialties like arthroplasty or trauma surgery is unvalidated

### Target Learners
- orthopedic and surgical trainees in medical education programs

### Target Learning Goals
- clinical reasoning
- spatial understanding
- student engagement
