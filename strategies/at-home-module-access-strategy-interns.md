# Provide orthopaedic interns with personal low-cost module sets for year-long at-home practice as a complement to clinical training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends supplying interns with personal, inexpensive simulation module sets to use at home over the academic year, since interns "spend most of the year involved in clinical patient care or on other medical services" and may get few hands-on operative opportunities. The modules were satisfied-with and residents felt they "should be a permanent part of orthopaedic surgical education". Unlike an expensive month-long curriculum costing more than $20,000, this approach did not require time away from clinical duties.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Low-cost materials and kits distributed to each resident; faculty scoring with the OSATS tool to track improvement
#### Constraints
- Improvement was measured on the modules themselves; translation to live operating room performance was not assessed in this study

### Target Learners
- junior orthopaedic surgery residents (PGY1)

### Target Learning Goals
- instrument handling, drilling accuracy and efficiency, depth gauge use, screw placement, fluoroscopic triangulation
