# Checklists

## Description
A checklist is an ordered list of criteria, steps, or expectations used to track whether learners are accomplishing specific tasks or meeting defined standards. In learning contexts, checklists serve three functions simultaneously: as an assessment tool (judging whether criteria are met), as a self-regulation support (guiding learners to monitor their own work against explicit standards), and as a communication device (making expectations shared and transparent between educator and learner).

## Design Implications

Checklists convert fuzzy expectations into discrete, observable criteria, which supports learner self-monitoring — a core driver of self-regulated learning [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+S]. By externalizing evaluation criteria, they offload the need to hold standards in mind while working, and they frame performance as a set of process goals — completing defined steps — which for novices produces better results than focusing on outcome goals [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]. Their assessment value depends entirely on criterion quality: specific, observable, standards-aligned criteria yield actionable feedback, while generic criteria yield box-ticking.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clear learning objectives translated into specific, observable criteria, each checkable in isolation
- Criteria aligned to standards or learning outcomes, so that checking maps onto actual competency
- A response format: binary (done/not done) or a rating scale (e.g., 4-point, 7-point) with descriptors — adding quality levels moves the checklist toward a rubric [Jonsson & Svingby, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2007.05.002) [+S]
- Training or modeling in how to use the checklist for self-assessment; untrained self-assessment is inaccurate [Andrade, Du, & Wang, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-3992.2008.00118.x) [+M]
- A feedback loop in which checklist results feed into [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) and revision, not just recording

#### Constraints
- Generic or vague criteria produce feedback too thin to act on — the most common failure mode; each criterion must name an observable behavior or product feature
- Checklists of low-level tasks invite surface compliance: learners satisfy the letter of the criterion without the underlying understanding, and externally imposed tracking can crowd out mastery motivation [Autonomy supports intrinsic motivation.](../claims/autonomy-supports-intrinsic-motivation.md) [~M]
- Long lists of procedural steps can become redundant for learners with strong prior knowledge, adding overhead without benefit [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]
- In high-stakes domains, checklists catch omissions but do not by themselves build the judgment needed for novel situations [Pronovost et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa054115) [+S]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Binary completion checklists** — done/not-done criteria for procedures and project steps; lowest cost, best for task management
- **Scaled checklists** — criteria rated on a 4- or 7-point scale with descriptors, blending checklist simplicity with rubric-level feedback
- **Co-constructed checklists** — learners help generate criteria from exemplars, which deepens understanding of quality standards [Andrade, Du, & Wang, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-3992.2008.00118.x) [+M]
- **Faded checklists** — the checklist is progressively removed or internalized as competence grows, transferring monitoring responsibility to the learner [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]

### Target Learners
- Novices who lack internalized standards and benefit from explicit criteria and process framing [Process goals outperform outcome goals for novices.](../claims/process-goals-outperform-outcome-goals-for-novices.md) [+M]
- K–12 through adult learners; the format scales by adjusting criterion specificity and abstraction
- Learners developing self-regulation, for whom structured self-monitoring builds the monitoring habit [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+S]
- Less valuable for experts, who already hold internal criteria and may find explicit lists redundant [Guidance becomes less effective as learner expertise increases.](../claims/worked-examples-less-effective-with-expertise.md) [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Procedural fluency: completing multi-step processes reliably and completely
- Self-regulation: building monitoring and self-assessment habits [Self-monitoring improves self-regulation.](../claims/self-monitoring-improves-self-regulation.md) [+S]
- Quality understanding: internalizing what "meets the standard" means in a domain
- Progress documentation: making growth visible across tasks and time

### Affordances
- [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md) — checklists enact this principle by making criteria visible *before* performance, so assessment information shapes the work rather than only judging it afterward
- [Chunking](../principles/chunking.md) — a checklist decomposes a complex task into discrete, checkable units, reducing the working-memory burden of tracking many requirements at once [Chunking reduces working memory load.](../claims/chunking-reduces-working-memory-load.md) [+M]
- [Clear Structure](../principles/clear-structure.md) — explicit criteria create shared expectations between educator and learner, removing ambiguity about what counts as done and done well
- [Cognitive Load Reduction](../principles/cognitive-load-reduction.md) — externalizing steps and standards offloads working memory, freeing capacity for the substantive work itself
- [Competency-Based Assessment](../principles/competency-based-assessment.md) — criterion-by-criterion tracking maps directly onto competency attainment, making checklists a natural progress-documentation tool

### Personalization
Checklists can be customized with different scales (4-point, 7-point), criteria, and descriptors to align with specific objectives and standards. Learners can also personalize use: some check off items during work, others after drafting; co-constructed variants let learners phrase criteria in their own words, which improves both comprehension and buy-in.

### Instructions
1. Derive criteria from learning objectives — each criterion must be specific, observable, and independently checkable.
2. Choose the response format: binary for procedures, a described scale (4- or 7-point) where quality levels matter.
3. Introduce the checklist with a model: demonstrate evaluating a sample work item against it, ideally with a [Think-Aloud](../elements/think-aloud.md) so learners see how criteria are applied, not just that they exist.
4. Have learners apply the checklist to their own work ([Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md)), then compare self-ratings with educator ratings and discuss discrepancies.
5. Feed results into [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) and a revision cycle — a checklist that terminates in a grade rather than revision is a recording device, not a learning tool.
6. Fade use as competence grows: move from full checklists to partial lists to learner-generated criteria, transferring monitoring responsibility [Fading support promotes transfer of responsibility.](../claims/fading-support-promotes-transfer-of-responsibility.md) [+M]

## Related Strategies
- Rubrics — scaled checklists with quality-level descriptors; rubrics add graded performance levels where checklists mark presence or absence
- Self-assessment protocols — checklists are the most common artifact through which structured self-assessment is operationalized
- Progress portfolios — checklists supply the item-level evidence that portfolios aggregate into a growth narrative

## Related Elements
- [Assess Performance](../elements/assess-performance.md) — the checklist is the instrument; performance assessment is the act it supports
- [Provide Feedback](../elements/provide-feedback.md) — checklist results become useful only when converted into specific, actionable feedback
- [Coaching](../elements/coaching.md) — educators use checklist discrepancies (self- vs. expert-rating gaps) as coaching entry points
- [Fading](../elements/fading.md) — the mechanism for withdrawing checklist support as expertise develops
- [Collaboration](../elements/collaboration.md) — peer checking against shared criteria turns the checklist into a tool for structured peer review

## Patterns That Use This Strategy
- [Formative Assessment](../patterns/formative-assessment.md) — checklists are a primary tool for eliciting and documenting evidence of learning during instruction
- [Competency-Based Learning](../patterns/competency-based-learning.md) — criterion tracking operationalizes mastery decisions and progress visibility
- [Explicit Teaching](../patterns/explicit-teaching.md) — publishing success criteria in checklist form is a direct enactment of making expectations explicit

## Examples
- **Surgical safety checklists** — the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist, modeled on ICU interventions that dramatically reduced catheter-related infections [Pronovost et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa054115) [+S]; the canonical demonstration that simple criterion lists prevent omission errors in complex procedures (Gawande, 2009).
- **Writing self-assessment in elementary classrooms** — Andrade, Du, & Wang (2008) had students self-assess drafts against a checklist-derived rubric before revision, improving writing quality and aligning self- and teacher-ratings [+M].
- **Project-based learning task boards** — platforms such as [Trello](https://trello.com) and Google Classroom rubrics/checklists let learners track multi-step project criteria and share progress with educators.
- **Flight and medical training** — trainees work from procedure checklists until responses are automatic, then are assessed on checklist-free performance to confirm internalization.
