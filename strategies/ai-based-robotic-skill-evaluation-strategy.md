# Pursue AI-based and console-data evaluation platforms for assessing novice robotic surgical skill

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future evaluation of robotic surgical skill move beyond crowd-sourced GEARS review. They state "the most promising solutions lie within the field of artificial intelligence for evaluation of surgical performance" and suggest "additional objective metrics, such as robotic console data (e.g., path length, error rate)" may better capture proficiency in early learners, alongside validation on alternative platforms and longitudinal designs.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Further development of artificial intelligence methods for surgical performance evaluation before large-scale deployment
#### Constraints
- C-SATS-derived GEARS scoring failed to differentiate novice from expert performers for RAC and RIHR in this study

### Target Learners
- Senior general surgery residents (PGY-4/5)
- Fellows
- Novice robotic surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- Accurate assessment of robotic surgical technical skill for training, credentialing, and proficiency benchmarking
