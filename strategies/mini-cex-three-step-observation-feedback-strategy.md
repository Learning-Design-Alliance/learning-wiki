# Implement the Mini-CEX as a three-step direct-observation assessment with feedback at one-week intervals during surgery clerkships

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing the Mini-CEX tool as an assessment and teaching method for clinical skills among operating room students, citing its effectiveness and cost efficiency. The procedure it tested: an evaluator "employed the Mini-CEX evaluation method to directly observe and assess participants' abilities in three steps with a one-week interval", observing skills during surgery, giving feedback, then repeating observation and final scoring. It also recommends future studies extend the approach to other specialized surgery clerkships and to circulating nursing students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A trained assessor (here, a 4-hour workshop led by a PhD in medical education) using the checklist and scoring guide, with feedback delivered promptly after each observation.
#### Constraints
- Blinding was not possible in this design, and the authors warn the halo effect from assessor-student relationships could bias scores; they suggest multiple blinded assessors or anonymous assessments for future studies.

### Target Learners
- undergraduate operating room technology students in surgery clerkships

### Target Learning Goals
- improvement of clinical scrub-nurse skills through repeated direct observation and structured feedback
