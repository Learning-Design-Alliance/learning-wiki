# Peer-led mock OSCE as a cost-effective strategy to reduce anxiety and support well-being before high-stakes clinical assessments

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (1 for, 4 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy engages students in designing and administering a mock OSCE one week before the summative exam, taking roles as station designers, examiners, and examinees, with immediate feedback after each six-minute station. The authors state "a peer-led mock OSCE appears to be a cost-effective strategy for helping students manage stress and anxiety" and that it "may represent a valuable strategy to enhance both academic outcomes and student well-being in high-stakes medical assessments."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Students willing to volunteer as station designers, examiners, and examinees
- Faculty supervision to correct conceptual errors without altering station structure
- An online course covering OSCE design and organization for participants
#### Constraints
- The study lacked sufficient power to confirm a performance benefit
- Single-center study with a small, predominantly female sample; generalizability should be interpreted with caution

### Target Learners
- Medical students taking a summative OSCE for the first time

### Target Learning Goals
- Clinical skills assessment preparedness
- Management of test anxiety and student well-being
