# Improve VR simulation realism by adding dynamic elements, animated avatars, and improved rendering

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 4 studies (2 causal, 1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 4 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on user feedback and the literature, the authors recommend enhancing realism and immersiveness of VR training scenes by adding sounds and movements, working on visualization techniques, and adding avatars that move and react to users' actions, "as this has been reported to enhance realism". They plan to modify lighting for better shadows, use more accurate rendering materials, introduce animated avatars, and add realistic hospital elements such as date and time information, posters and fliers.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Virtual environment should include dynamic objects and people, since subjects reported the static world reduced their sense of presence
#### Constraints
- The main goal of the VR is to let trainees practice, rather than explore the virtual world

### Target Learners
- healthcare providers training in immersive simulation

### Target Learning Goals
- realistic, engaging simulation for procedural skills training
