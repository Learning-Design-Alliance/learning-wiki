# Apply Sewell's five skills of ecological perception as eco-somatic listening activities

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article translates Sewell's five skills of ecological perception—"(1) paying attention; (2) perceiving relationships, contexts, and interfaces; (3) perceptual flexibility; (4) re-perceiving depth; and (5) using the imagination intentionally"—into dance-learning activities, often metaphorically. One activity pairs students: one improvises with a specific image and dance element while the other focuses attention on a single aspect (quality, tempo, or pathway), then responds with an improvised phrase reinventing that component. Another has duet partners improvise studies exemplifying a relational view, such as how gravity stabilizes or destabilizes, or giving and receiving weight.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Willingness to work metaphorically, since the skills "may seem a bit abstruse when their application to a dance context is considered."
#### Constraints
- The article states the phrases produced need not look alike; both participants should see evidence of the selected component, reinvented.

### Target Learners
- dance students at any level

### Target Learning Goals
- narrow and refine focus of attention; re-perceive the familiar to discover new movement possibilities and interdependent working
