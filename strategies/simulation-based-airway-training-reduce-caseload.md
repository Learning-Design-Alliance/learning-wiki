# Use continuous, comprehensive simulation-based airway management training alongside clinical practice to potentially reduce the clinical caseload needed for intubation proficiency

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The strategy is to embed simulation-based training covering recognition and preparation for difficult cases and procedural skills, in addition to technical ETI practice, within trainee curricula. The article suggests this "may be beneficial in reducing the number of ETI required to achieve proficiency", citing a program that raised FAS from 74 to 82%. It recommends simulation include preparation and patient evaluation, not only technical skills.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training must cover other components of airway management, such as preparation and patient evaluation, in addition to technical ETI skills, per the article's recommendation
#### Constraints
- The study itself did not assess the effect of simulation-based training; cited evidence comes from another program with varying success across methodologies

### Target Learners
- novice emergency medicine trainees/residents

### Target Learning Goals
- endotracheal intubation proficiency (first attempt success)
