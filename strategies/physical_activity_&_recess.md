# Physical Activity & Recess

## Description
Integrating physical activity, such as games, walking, or jogging, with learning activities can improve students' health, focus, and social skills. For example, students can discuss assignments while walking or engage in active games before returning to class for writing tasks. Developers can create activities and games that encourage movement and play through videos, prompts, or augmented reality.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Integration of movement activities into lesson plans. Access to space for physical activity. Development of prompts, videos, or augmented reality for active learning.
#### Constraints
- Requires careful planning to integrate physical activity seamlessly with academic content. May be challenging for students with certain physical limitations or disabilities. Requires additional resources and creativity from the instructor.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track improvements in student focus and attention. Measure increases in physical activity during the school day. Assess gains in social skills and teamwork through observation and feedback.

## Impact
- Studies show integrating physical activity can enhance cognitive function, improve student engagement, and reduce disciplinary issues. Playworks’ TeamUp program demonstrates improved recess organization, increased student activity and cooperation, and declines in bullying and disciplinary referrals.
