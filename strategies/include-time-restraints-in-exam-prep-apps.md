# Include time-restrained practice in future exam-preparation applications despite low voluntary uptake

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that future interventions aimed at improving performance in timed examinations such as EUNACOM should include time-limited practice despite observing that only 30% of students voluntarily used the training mode. They reasoned that the observed 8.5-minute time reduction is likely the result of practice answering multiple-choice questions, and that EUNACOM itself applies a 60-second-per-question limit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The timed mode should be accompanied by strategies addressing low voluntary uptake, such as exploring motivations to use these applications in future qualitative research
#### Constraints
- Timed exercises were in this trial accessible only once, which may have suppressed use
- Timed mode lacked key-concept feedback, possibly making performance pressure more tangible

### Target Learners
- Medical interns preparing for timed national licensing examinations

### Target Learning Goals
- Exam performance including time management when answering multiple-choice clinical vignettes
