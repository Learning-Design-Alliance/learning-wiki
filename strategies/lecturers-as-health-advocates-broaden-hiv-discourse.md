# Reform HIV classroom discourse: lecturers as health advocates who broaden discussion beyond clinical aspects and create student contact opportunities with PLHIV

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends reforming HIV discussion in the classroom so that "lecturers need to discuss HIV from a wider perspective and open discourse to sensitive aspects of HIV such as ethics, morals, stigma to address the students’ fear or anxiety toward PLHIV." Lecturers should act as health advocates promoting acceptance, recognize their own ingrained personal beliefs before teaching the topic, and create more opportunities for health care students to work with PLHIV to improve awareness and acceptance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Lecturers must first recognize their own ingrained personal beliefs and values about HIV before teaching the topic
- Instructor training to deliver HIV information with sensitivity, as the article cites from prior education-program reviews
#### Constraints
- The article notes cultural taboos around sexuality in Indonesia create discomfort in class discussion, and conformity pressures may make acceptance-promoting teaching feel like disconformity to social norms

### Target Learners
- dietetic students
- health professions students

### Target Learning Goals
- reducing stigma toward PLHIV
- professional behavior and integrity in HIV care
- accurate HIV knowledge beyond clinical aspects
