# Use teacher-student interactive map construction so the teacher can correct misinformation and introduce new ideas

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends involving both teacher and students in constructing semantic maps. The teacher functions as a participant, which per the cited literature encourages students to share in their own learning and supports in-depth processing and motivation. The article states the interactive strategy "can provide the teacher with an opportunity to correct misinformation, introduce new ideas, or change interpretations." The experiment's interactive condition operationalized this through question-elicited background knowledge and joint map completion.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher participation in map construction; opportunities for students to ask the teacher about unclear passages
#### Constraints
- Grounded in the study's EFL college freshmen context with expository passages

### Target Learners
- EFL students at secondary or college level

### Target Learning Goals
- Reading comprehension; activation and expansion of prior knowledge
