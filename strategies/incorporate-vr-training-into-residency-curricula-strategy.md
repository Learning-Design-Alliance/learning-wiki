# Incorporate VR-based simulation training into spine surgery residency curricula as an adjunct to traditional methods

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the pooled findings, the review recommends that training programs adopt VR simulation as a complement to cadaveric and operative training: "Incorporating VR-based training into residency curricula may facilitate earlier skill acquisition, objective performance assessment, and safer progression along the learning curve." The authors note it is particularly valuable "in settings with limited access to cadaveric models or operative exposure".

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to VR simulator platforms and standardized training protocols
#### Constraints
- Evidence derives from simulated or cadaveric outcomes; improvements should not be interpreted as evidence of improved patient outcomes

### Target Learners
- surgical residents
- early-career surgeons

### Target Learning Goals
- pedicle screw placement technique
- earlier skill acquisition
- objective performance assessment
