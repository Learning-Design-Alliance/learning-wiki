# Adopt literacy-based teaching methods as an alternative to grammar lecture and vocabulary rote memorization in communicative second language classrooms

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that teachers designing second language curricula focused on vocabulary and grammar acquisition, and teachers wanting to keep a communicative atmosphere while developing literacy, look to the study's design to guide activity choices. It states that "Teachers searching for an alternative to presenting grammar structures through a lecture or traditional format, and vocabulary through rote memorization, may find that the literacy-based methods can provide effective, alternative instructional methods." Teachers preparing learners for proficiency assessments are also directed to the methods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher training in the literacy-based methods before implementation
- An established curriculum whose vocabulary and grammar topics the reading and writing activities reinforce
#### Constraints
- The article recommends continued monitoring of the effectiveness of both instructional models rather than wholesale replacement
- Findings come from one teacher, one school, and one beginning-level Spanish unit

### Target Learners
- beginning-level secondary second language learners

### Target Learning Goals
- second language proficiency assessment performance
- vocabulary and grammar acquisition
- literacy development alongside communicative competence
