# "Headings and Highlight" Strategy

## Description
The "Headings and Highlight" Strategy is a digital reading technique designed to slow down reading and encourage dialogue. In Google Docs, students highlight unfamiliar vocabulary, then pairs read paragraphs, highlighting key ideas. They create four-word headings for sections, compare with partners, and justify their choices, using text evidence. Groups then consolidate headings. This promotes critical thinking and textual engagement. At the end, the teacher might have groups present or even have the whole class vote on their favorite heading. And individually students can reflect on the process they went through, how the heading helps them understand the reading, and how it helps them answer the driving question of the unit.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to Google Docs or similar collaborative document platform. Excerpts of difficult articles. Teacher facilitation to guide discussion.
#### Constraints
- Relies on Google Docs or similar technology. May be time-consuming. Requires effective facilitation of group discussions.

### Affordances
- Slows down the reading process, promoting deeper engagement. Encourages dialogue and discussion about the text. Reinforces the skill of identifying main ideas and supporting evidence.

### Personalization
- Teachers can modify text complexity or group sizes. Can be adapted for individual or whole-class activities.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe the quality of group discussions and justifications. Review the accuracy of the headings and highlights. Assess student reflections on the process.

## Impact
- The only real affordance of the technology is the ability to change the heading multiple times in a clean way. But it does help teachers see how to bring dialogue and discussion into the classroom, even when students are reading on the computer, and it has achieved the goal of slowing down reading.
