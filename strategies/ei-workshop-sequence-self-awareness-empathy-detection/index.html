Deliver EI workshops through a sequence covering self-awareness, empathy, and emotional detection¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (1 for, 3 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article describes a three-workshop sequence for teaching EI: Workshop 1 uses a Myers-Briggs video scenario to teach self-awareness; Workshop 2 uses a TED talk and augmented reality design activity to build empathy; Workshop 3 teaches emotional detection of facial expressions based on Ekman's six basic emotions. Each workshop includes a manipulation check survey. The sequence was delivered in both face-to-face and online formats with minimal instructor interaction to reduce confounding.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • A certified Myers-Briggs trainer presentation before the first workshop
  • Team discussion of workshop content in asynchronous or synchronous format
  • A manipulation check survey after each workshop

Constraints¶

  • Workshops were designed to require very little instructor interaction to minimize impact of instructor differences between online and face-to-face groups

Target Learners¶

  • undergraduate students in management information systems courses

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-awareness, empathy and awareness of others, and emotional detection skills

Claims¶

  • Students in intervention groups reported greater EI skill improvements than control students in interviews [+W]
  • No significant difference in EI growth between online and face-to-face intervention groups [~W]
  • Online EI workshops combined with mindfulness training significantly predicted peer-assessed EI growth, while neither intervention alone did [~W]
  • Pre-test scores were the only statistically significant predictor of Trait EI post-test scores [~W]
