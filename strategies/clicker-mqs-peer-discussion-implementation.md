# Implement multiple-choice question sequences as clicker questions with individual commitment, peer discussion, and instructor-led whole-class discussion at checkpoints

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 3 studies (2 design, 1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 3 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends implementing an MQS the way Mazur's Peer Instruction does: students first answer individually with clickers, which requires them to "take a stance regarding their understanding", then discuss with peers, followed by a whole-class discussion. Anonymous clicker responses avoid embarrassment and give the instructor immediate feedback on the extent of student difficulties. In this study the SGE MQS was implemented this way by two instructors in consecutive years, completed in one class period, with a general class discussion after each question except the first.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Clickers or a personal response system to record individual answers anonymously
- Class time for peer discussion and whole-class discussion after each question
#### Constraints
- Empirical data cited suggest performance on multiple-choice questions improved after peer discussion, but the article notes instructor implementation differences between years may affect outcomes

### Target Learners
- upper-level undergraduate physics students

### Target Learning Goals
- foundational quantum mechanics concepts addressed through sequenced clicker questions

## Claims

- [Average posttest scores exceeded 70% on all questions (roughly 90% on questions 1-3) after CQS implementation](../claims/cqs-larmor-posttest-above-70.md) [+W]
- [The SGE MQS produced reasonable pretest-to-posttest gains on state-preparation questions 4 and 6, with posttest scores around 80%, though smaller than QuILT gains](../claims/sge-mqs-gains-state-preparation-questions.md) [+W]
- [After the SGE MQS, upper-level QM students scored over 90% on the two Hilbert-space/physical-space and basis-choice questions, up from 61% and 40% on the pretest](../claims/sge-mqs-large-gains-hilbert-space-questions.md) [+W]
- [SGE MQS improvement was limited on basis-transformation (question 3) and superposition-vs-mixture (question 5) items, with smaller gains than the QuILT produced](../claims/sge-mqs-limited-gains-basis-superposition-mixture.md) [~W]
- [In the authors' prior work, performance on a quantum measurement quiz was 26% after lecture only, 68% after lecture plus multiple-choice questions, and 91% after a QuILT](../claims/quilt-concept-tests-improve-quantum-measurement-scores.md) [~W]
