# WWYD Critical Incidents

## Description
Learners are presented with mini case studies that feature a conflict or misunderstanding and are asked, 'What would you do if you were person X/person Y?' This activity encourages learners to consider different perspectives and offer their own interpretations and resolutions.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Mini case studies featuring conflicts or misunderstandings, a space for group discussion, and facilitation by an instructor to guide analysis and reflection.
#### Constraints
- Learners may lack the knowledge or experience to fully understand the critical incidents, or they may offer simplistic solutions. It requires careful selection of incidents and guidance from the instructor to ensure meaningful analysis.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- All learners, particularly those who need to improve their interpersonal skills and conflict resolution abilities

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Develop problem-solving skills, promote perspective-taking, encourage critical thinking, improve communication skills

### Affordances
- Provides real-world scenarios that require problem-solving and perspective-taking. Encourages learners to consider different viewpoints and offer their own solutions. Improves communication skills through discussion and role-playing.

### Personalization
- The critical incidents can be tailored to the learners' interests and the context of their learning or work environment. The level of analysis and discussion can be adjusted to their abilities.

## Assessment Evidence
- Assess the quality of problem-solving skills, the ability to consider different perspectives, and the level of critical thinking demonstrated in the analysis. Monitor changes in learners' interpersonal skills and conflict resolution abilities.

## Impact
- Case-based learning has been shown to improve problem-solving, critical thinking, and communication skills. Exposure to diverse perspectives also promotes empathy and understanding.

## Examples
- A case study describes a miscommunication between a manager and an employee. Students are asked what they would do if they were the manager, and what they would do if they were the employee.
