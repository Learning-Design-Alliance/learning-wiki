# Performance Task Design

## Description
Performance task design is the deliberate construction of tasks that require learners to *do* something with their knowledge — produce an artifact, solve an ill-structured problem, conduct an investigation, or execute a procedure — under conditions that resemble how the knowledge is used outside school. Assessment is built into the task rather than appended to it: the criteria for quality are typically made explicit in a rubric, and the task itself yields evidence of both learning and transfer.

## Design Implications

Well-designed performance tasks sit at the intersection of [Authentic Assessment](../patterns/authentic-assessment.md) and [Assessment for Learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md): they elicit the target performance directly rather than a proxy for it, and they generate evidence learners can act on [Assessment designed to inform instruction improves achievement.](../claims/assessment-for-learning-improves-achievement.md) [+S]. Authenticity matters for motivation and quality of work — tasks with real audiences and purposes elicit more effortful, higher-quality products [Authentic audiences and purposes improve student work.](../claims/authentic-audiences-improve-student-work.md) [+M]. Because performance tasks impose high intrinsic and extraneous demands, they must be designed against working-memory limits; overloaded tasks produce measurement noise rather than learning evidence [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [+S].

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clear specification of the target performance: what competent work looks like, observable and assessable
- A rubric or scoring guide shared with learners *before* they begin, so criteria function as [advance organizers](../elements/advance-organizers.md) for self-monitoring
- Sufficient scaffolding at entry — models, [worked examples](../principles/worked-examples.md), or partial tasks — that fades as competence grows
- A meaningful product or performance, ideally with a real or simulated audience ([Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md))

#### Constraints
- Scoring reliability drops sharply when rubrics are vague or when raters lack training; holistic judgments of complex work are notoriously inconsistent [-M]
- Tasks that are authentic but poorly aligned to learning goals measure general ability or effort rather than the intended constructs [~M]
- High cognitive load from unfamiliar formats, open-endedness, or extraneous demands can depress performance for novices, making the task a poor assessment [Cognitive overload degrades learning.](../claims/cognitive-overload-degrades-learning.md) [-S]
- Time-intensive to design, administer, and score; scaling beyond small classes requires trade-offs (e.g., checklists, peer scoring) that can undermine validity [Checklist-based evaluation is ineffective in online settings.](../claims/checklist-evaluation-ineffective-online.md) [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Structured → open continuum:** highly scaffolded tasks (scripted labs, guided case analyses) for novices; open-ended design briefs or capstone projects for advanced learners
- **Individual vs. collaborative:** group performance tasks add coordination demands; individual accountability mechanisms (role assignments, individual write-ups) are needed
- **Embedded vs. summative:** tasks can function as ongoing [practice](../elements/practice.md) with feedback, or as summative demonstrations in [Competency-Based Assessment](../principles/competency-based-assessment.md) systems

### Target Learners
- Intermediate learners who have foundational knowledge and are ready to integrate it; pure novices often lack the schema to navigate open-ended demands [~M]
- Learners preparing for transfer to professional or civic contexts where knowledge is applied under realistic conditions
- Less suitable as a first exposure for learners with minimal prior knowledge, who benefit from [worked examples](../principles/worked-examples.md) and [demonstration](../elements/demonstration.md) before independent performance

### Target Learning Goals
- Application and transfer: using knowledge in novel, realistic situations
- Integration: coordinating concepts, procedures, and judgment within one task
- Self-regulation: planning, monitoring, and revising extended work against explicit criteria
- Disciplinary practices: argumentation, investigation, design, critique

### Instructions
1. **Define the target performance.** Specify what learners should be able to *do*, not just know; align the task so the performance is directly observable (constructive alignment).
2. **Choose an authentic scenario.** Anchor the task in a realistic context with a genuine purpose and audience; consider [case-based learning](../elements/case-based-learning.md) or [anchored instruction](../elements/anchored-instruction.md) formats.
3. **Build the rubric first.** Draft scoring criteria before finalizing the task; if a criterion cannot be assessed, the task does not elicit it.
4. **Design entry scaffolds.** Provide models, exemplars of strong and weak work ([non-examples](../elements/non-examples.md)), and partial task structures that will be faded.
5. **Plan feedback cycles.** Build in at least one draft-and-revise cycle with [feedback](../elements/feedback.md) against the rubric, so the task functions as [assessment for learning](../principles/assessment-for-learning.md), not just measurement.
6. **Pilot and calibrate scoring.** Test the task, have multiple raters score sample work, and revise the rubric where judgments diverge.

## Related Strategies
- [Case-Based Learning](case-based-learning.md) — a common performance-task format built around realistic cases
- [Project-Based Learning](project-based-learning.md) — extended performance tasks organized around a driving question and product
- [Portfolio Assessment](portfolio-assessment.md) — aggregates performance evidence across tasks over time

## Examples
- **[4C/ID](../patterns/4cid-four-component-instructional-design.md)** — whole learning tasks are the organizing unit; performance task design governs task-class sequencing from simple to complex with fading support.
- **[Cognitive Apprenticeship](../patterns/cognitive-apprenticeship.md)** — the articulation and exploration phases rely on performance tasks that push learners to produce and defend work.
- **[PBLWorks (Buck Institute)](https://www.pblworks.org)** — publishes gold-standard project designs with authentic products, public audiences, and rubrics.
- **AP Performance Tasks (College Board)** — e.g., the AP Computer Science Principles Create task, where students design and program an artifact scored against a published rubric.
