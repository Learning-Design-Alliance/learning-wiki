# Introduce the Lorentz force as a postulated experimental equation to define E and B before Maxwell's equations

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article describes the usual introductory treatment: "in an introductory course in the electromagnetic theory on e introduces, as a ﬁrst step, the basic variables E, B of the electromagnetic ﬁeld by postulating the expression of the Lorentz force as an experimental equation". This route introduces electric charge, the electric field via the force on a particle at rest, and the magnetic field via the motion term, with charge conservation postulated through the continuity equation. The author contrasts it with introducing E and B as basic variables verifying Maxwell's equations directly.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A proportional constant (gamma-tilde) must be introduced to account for different dimensions and units of electrodynamic quantities
#### Constraints
- The article notes the alternative general-field-theory treatment, in which the role of the fields in interactions and general theorems must then be clarified separately

### Target Learners
- students in an introductory course in electromagnetic theory

### Target Learning Goals
- introduce the electric and magnetic field variables and electric charge conservation
