# PE teachers should support autonomy (offering choices, justifying decisions, minimizing pressure) and avoid controlling strategies to help reduce bullying

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the model's findings, the article recommends that PE teachers promote BPN satisfaction by "expressing closeness, concern about their problems, providing them with the possibility to choose some tasks to perform, task order, the classmates with whom to work", and avoid punishments, public reprimands, or withdrawal of attention, which the authors argue may only suppress undesirable behavior when students feel supervised.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teacher training in autonomy-supportive styles; clear bullying rules with assertive teacher responses so potential victims feel safe
#### Constraints
- Recommendations derive from correlational, cross-sectional data; the authors call for experimental and longitudinal replication

### Target Learners
- Students aged 11–15 in primary and secondary physical education

### Target Learning Goals
- Bullying prevention; fostering self-determined motivation and prosocial behavior in PE
