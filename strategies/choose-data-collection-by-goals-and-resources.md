# Researchers should weigh goals and resources when choosing between GAIQ and individual interviews

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (3 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that researchers deliberately match data-collection method to study goals and available resources, since GAIQ and interviews carry different trade-offs. The authors state: "Researchers should carefully consider their goals and resources before choosing either of these data collection sources." Interviews offer rich verbal data and on-the-spot clarification; GAIQ offers efficiency, standardization, and respondent self-categorization at the cost of written conciseness.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- An analysis of the study's goals (depth vs. breadth of data) and resources (time for collection and analysis) before selecting a method
#### Constraints
- GAIQ cannot respond on the spot to clarify respondents' ideas, and written responses are more concise than spoken ones

### Target Learners
- Education researchers studying instructor beliefs

### Target Learning Goals
- Selecting an appropriate method for uncovering instructors' instructional considerations
