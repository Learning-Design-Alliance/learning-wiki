# Routinely signpost academically struggling students to mental health support, building on academic staff depression literacy

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that academic counsellors "should consider routinely signposting academically failing students to mental health support". It argues academic staff are often first to observe worsening mental health, and cites survey evidence that staff with higher depression literacy engage more with students about mental health. Because students with low mood did not show poorer attendance, the authors warn identification of at-risk students may be difficult.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Academic staff and counsellors with sufficient mental health literacy to recognise and signpost struggling students
#### Constraints
- Students with possible MDD did not have reliably poorer attendance, making academically struggling students with low mood harder to identify

### Target Learners
- University students with depression or anxiety who are struggling academically

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintaining academic performance (GPA) and course engagement among students experiencing emotional difficulties
