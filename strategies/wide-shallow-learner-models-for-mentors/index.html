Design intelligent mentors around wide-and-shallow learner models rather than narrow-and-deep ones¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 design), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The authors propose that intelligent mentoring systems should build broad yet shallow understanding of the learner and current situation to select pedagogical strategies and respond in a motivational, emotionally-aware way. They state: "we hypothesis that for intelligent mentors‘wide and shallow’ learner models would be preferred to‘narrow and deep’", and believe the wide approach is likely to have significantly higher payoff than adding a single tutor-like competence at a time. This is a forward-looking design direction, not a tested result.

Requirements¶

  • Validation when exemplars of intelligent mentoring are implemented and evaluated
  • A sufficient spread of mentor-like skills with adequate depth to handle a wide range of situations and personal contexts

  • The authors state the hypothesis "needs to be validated when exemplars of intelligent mentoring are implemented and evaluated"

Target Learners¶

  • adult learners
  • trainees in professional development

Target Learning Goals¶

  • self-actualisation
  • self-regulation and metacognition
  • connecting learning with real-world experience

Claims¶

  • STyLE-OLM benefited both more knowledgeable learners (reflective domain interactions) and less knowledgeable learners (inspecting and challenging their models) [+W]
  • Interactive open learner modelling dialogue produced resultant learner models with more beliefs than initial models, most approved by both learners and a teacher [+W]
  • The interactive open learner modelling approach was suitable for dealing with cold start in user-adaptive systems (OWL-OLM trial) [+W]
  • No empirical evidence has been examined that an interactive open learner model leads to better learning than a non-negotiable or absent open learner model [+W]
