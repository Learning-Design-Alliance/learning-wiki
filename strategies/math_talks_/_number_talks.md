# Math Talks / Number Talks

## Description
Math Talks, also known as Number Talks, are short, daily classroom routines where students solve math problems mentally and share their strategies. The instructor presents a problem, and students work individually to find a solution without using paper or pencil. Then, students share their answers and explain their thinking, while the instructor encourages discussion and clarifies strategies. The goal is to build students' mental math skills, flexibility in thinking about numbers, and communication skills. Math Talks can be adapted for various grade levels, from early elementary to upper grades, and can be used to explore different mathematical concepts, such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, fractions, and decimals.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A clean desktop, a problem to present, and a safe and respectful classroom environment.  Instructor should be prepared with questions to clarify and extend student thinking, and be familiar with different math strategies to suggest them if they do not come up in conversation.
#### Constraints
- May require some preparation and familiarity with different math strategies on the part of the instructor. Some students may be hesitant to share their thinking or may struggle to articulate their strategies.

- Encourages flexible thinking and problem-solving skills. Promotes communication and discussion about math strategies. Supports mental math skills and numeracy. Provides opportunities for students to learn from each other's approaches.

### Personalization
- Can be adapted for various grade levels and mathematical concepts. The instructor can choose problems and visuals appropriate for the students' skill levels. Students can use different strategies to solve the problems, allowing for individual approaches.

## Assessment Evidence
- Track student participation in discussions and the variety of strategies they use. Observe student explanations and reasoning to assess their understanding of mathematical concepts. Collect student reflections on their learning and progress.

## Impact
- Research shows that Number Talks help students develop mathematical reasoning, critical thinking, language, and communication skills. They also build a mental storehouse of strategic tools and promote a community of learning in the classroom.
