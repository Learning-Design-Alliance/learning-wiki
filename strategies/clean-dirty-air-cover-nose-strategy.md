# Play-based classroom exercises in which children identify clean versus dirty air and physically cover nose and mouth when exposed to smoke

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
CHANT's sessions include concrete, play-based activities through which children practice protective behaviors. "Classroom exercises will instruct about identifying clean and dirty air," and "Participants will be instructed to cover their noses and mouths with their hands if exposed to SHS." The abstract states that "Activities are designed to allow children to play and discover 'clean and dirty air.'" These strategies turn an abstract hazard into a discriminable and actionable classroom task for 4- to 6-year-olds.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Delivered within the five 20-minute CHANT sessions
- Supported by storybook and workbook materials
#### Constraints
- Designed for pre-kindergarten and kindergarten children who speak and understand English

### Target Learners
- children aged 4 to 6 in preschool classrooms

### Target Learning Goals
- discriminate clean from dirty air and perform a self-protective response to smoke exposure
