Strategies

Use validated homogeneous outcome measures, preferably in a patient setting, in future FB SBT studies¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 quant-synthesis), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

To overcome heterogeneity and enable comparisons between studies, the review advocates that "future studies use more homogeneous validated outcome measures, preferably in a patient setting." It suggests assessing trainees with a previously validated qualitative assessment such as a validated version of the BSTAT, combined with procedure time as a secondary outcome measure, and possibly structured progress. This strategy targets the sparse and conflicting evidence at Kirkpatrick levels 3 and 4.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Assessment tools with proven validity, such as a validated BSTAT version, must be available and used consistently across studies

Constraints¶

  • The review notes designing a study of SBT effects on patient outcomes is difficult from ethical and practical points of view

Target Learners¶

  • novice bronchoscopy trainees

Target Learning Goals¶

  • basic flexible bronchoscopy skills

Claims¶

  • Evidence that FB SBT improves bronchoscopy performance in a patient setting (Kirkpatrick level 3) is sparse and conflicting [+W]
  • Flexible bronchoscopy simulation-based training improves novice trainees' skills when evaluated in a simulation setting [+W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Standardise outcome measurements and isolate individual instructional design features in future research
  • Report complete statistical data and use standardized success tests to enable future meta-analysis
  • Direct future self-regulation research toward standard methods of reporting SR outcomes to address substantial heterogeneity in outcome measures.
  • Develop and validate a reusable, cost-effective, high-fidelity training platform and build homogeneous assessment tools for aneurysm clipping simulation
