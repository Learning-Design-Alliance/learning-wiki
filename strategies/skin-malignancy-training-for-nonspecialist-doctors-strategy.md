# Educate doctors and medical students on common skin malignancies and full body skin examination

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend that dermatologists and medical educators act to bridge the melanoma knowledge and skills gap by educating doctors and medical students on common skin malignancies and full body skin examination. They note "Several studies in other countries have shown significant improvements in knowledge, confidence and clinical practice of doctors following such training programmes". The strategy targets non-specialist doctors whose undergraduate dermatology exposure is limited to 2–4 weeks.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Training should be embedded in undergraduate and postgraduate/continuing medical education, since post-training opportunities for non-dermatology careers are minimal or non-existent as the article states.
#### Constraints
- The article notes dermoscopy mastery takes time even for trainee dermatologists and false-positive rates can be high in non-specialist hands, so recommendations should come from specialist colleges and CME panels.

### Target Learners
- Non-specialist doctors (interns, resident medical officers, senior house officers, postgraduate trainees)
- Medical students

### Target Learning Goals
- Recognition of suspicious skin lesions and melanoma types
- Performing total body skin examination
- Timely referral of suspicious lesions and patient education on skin self-examination
