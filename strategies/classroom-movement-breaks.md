# Classroom Movement Breaks

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)

## Description
Classroom movement breaks are brief (typically 3–10 minute) episodes of moderate physical activity — stretching, aerobic movement, coordinated games, or dance — embedded within instructional time. They are carried out in or near the classroom, require no equipment or clothing change, and are followed by a rapid return to academic work.

## Design Implications

Movement breaks leverage the link between acute physical activity and subsequent attention and executive function: single bouts of moderate exercise reliably improve on-task behavior and attention in the minutes that follow [Classroom physical activity improves attention and on-task behavior.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+S]. They function as attention management rather than instruction — the goal is to restore the cognitive conditions for learning, not to teach content through movement (though content-integrated variants exist).

### Context
#### Requirements
- A predictable routine with clear start/stop signals so transitions cost less time than the break delivers
- Activities that elevate heart rate moderately without over-arousing students or requiring setup
- A structured return: a brief settling cue or [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md) before resuming instruction

#### Constraints
- Poorly bounded breaks can increase off-task behavior and cost more time than they recover; unstructured "free play" mid-lesson is harder to exit than a scripted routine [~M]
- High-arousal activities (loud games, competition) can leave younger students dysregulated and slower to refocus [~W]
- Benefits are acute and transient — a single break improves the next segment of work, not long-term achievement by itself; effects on standardized outcomes are inconsistent [~M]
- Breaks inserted during cognitively demanding segments (e.g., mid-explanation) fragment instruction; timing matters more than frequency [Acute exercise benefits depend on timing relative to the learning task.](../claims/acute-exercise-timing-memory.md) [~M]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Energizing breaks** (aerobic movement) before tasks requiring sustained attention
- **Calming breaks** (stretching, breathing) after high-arousal periods or before assessments
- **Content-integrated breaks** — movement that rehearses academic content (e.g., [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md), jumping to answer mental-math prompts), trading some arousal benefit for instructional time
- **Scheduled vs. responsive** — fixed intervals (e.g., every 25–30 minutes) versus teacher judgment of waning attention

### Target Learners
- Elementary students, who show the largest and most consistent attention gains [Classroom physical activity improves attention and on-task behavior.](../claims/classroom-physical-activity-improves-attention.md) [+S]
- Students with ADHD or high impulsivity, for whom acute activity produces disproportionate improvements in on-task behavior [~M]
- Adolescents in block schedules with long sedentary stretches, though effects are smaller than in younger learners [~W]

### Target Learning Goals
- Attention and self-regulation: restoring on-task behavior for subsequent instruction
- Executive function support: working memory and inhibitory control in the minutes following activity
- Not a primary vehicle for content acquisition — pair with [Practice](../elements/practice.md) or [Application](../elements/application.md) if content goals are intended

### Instructions
1. Establish a signal and routine so students can start and stop within 30 seconds.
2. Choose an activity matched to the desired state: energizing before focused work, calming before assessment.
3. Run the break for 3–10 minutes of moderate movement; avoid competition or loud stimuli that prolong settling.
4. Close with a settling cue or brief [Check-In](../elements/check-in.md), then resume instruction immediately at a natural entry point.
5. For content goals, substitute a movement variant such as [Act It Out](../elements/act-it-out.md) so the break doubles as rehearsal.

## Examples
- **GoNoodle** (https://www.gonoodle.com) — short guided movement and mindfulness videos widely used for K–5 classroom breaks.
- **TAKE 10!®** (https://www.take10.net) — a published curriculum that integrates 10-minute physical activity breaks with academic content in elementary classrooms.
- **The Daily Mile** (https://thedailymile.org) — a daily 15-minute run/walk adopted by thousands of schools, functioning as a scheduled whole-class movement break.
