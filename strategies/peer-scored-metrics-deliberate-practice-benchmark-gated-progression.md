# Peer-scored deliberate practice against predefined metrics with benchmark-gated progression in simulation training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 3 studies (3 causal), `q3`–`q4` · 0 of 3 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The E+PBP training recipe kept the same structure, duration (3.5 hours), content and facilitator:student ratio as standard simulation, but partners scored each other's phone calls against predefined metrics and shared the results as feedback at the end of each call. If proficiency was not achieved, the case was repeated before progressing, and participants were required to reach proficiency on all four cases with their partner before performing a case with the facilitator. The article attributes the benefit to practice being 'deliberate', focused on pre-defined metrics, and to benchmark-gated progression.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Predefined metrics and a proficiency benchmark for each case
- Peer scoring sheets and feedback exchange after each simulated call
- Facilitator reassessment until proficiency is demonstrated
#### Constraints
- The article states the training time was restricted by the existing undergraduate curriculum rather than what would ideally be required to train a fundamental skill

### Target Learners
- undergraduate nursing and medical students training in interdisciplinary teams

### Target Learning Goals
- ISBAR clinical communication for the acutely deteriorating patient
