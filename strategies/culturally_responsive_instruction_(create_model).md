# Culturally Responsive Instruction (CREATE Model)

## Description
Culturally responsive instruction uses students' cultural knowledge, prior experiences, language practices, and frames of reference as conduits for learning academic content rather than as obstacles to it. The CREATE model operationalizes this by making standards-based content accessible through relatable aspects of students' daily lives — student-centered stories, vocabulary, and language — while infusing interests such as hip hop, sports, and community experiences in ways that feel authentic rather than performative. The goal is empowerment across intellectual, social, emotional, and political dimensions, not merely engagement.

## Design Implications

Culturally responsive instruction works by connecting new content to existing knowledge structures, which reduces the extraneous processing learners otherwise spend bridging unfamiliar contexts [Activating prior knowledge improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]. It also functions as a belonging intervention: when students see their identities reflected as intellectually valuable, threat and disengagement decrease [Belonging interventions improve outcomes for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]. Meta-analytic evidence links culturally responsive teaching practices to improved academic outcomes across content areas [Aronson & Laughter's review of culturally relevant pedagogy outcomes.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M], though effect sizes depend on authenticity of implementation.

### Context
#### Requirements
- Genuine knowledge of students' communities, interests, and language practices — gathered through [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md), home-community engagement, and sustained relationships, not assumptions
- Willingness to adapt curriculum, examples, and discourse patterns; ongoing reflection on whose knowledge counts in the classroom
- Academic content that remains rigorous — cultural relevance is a bridge to standards, not a substitute for them
- Authenticity: cultural referents must be ones students actually use, introduced with credibility rather than tokenism

#### Constraints
- Superficial or stereotyped use of cultural referents ("surface culture" only — food, holidays, dress) can alienate students and reinforce stereotypes [-M]
- Performed or borrowed cultural references that the teacher does not authentically command read as inauthentic and damage credibility [-W]
- Relevance alone does not produce learning; culturally familiar contexts still require [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md), practice, and feedback to yield durable knowledge [~S]
- Developing culturally responsive materials takes significant time; one-off substitutions without relationship-building show little effect [-W]

#### Implementation Variability
- **Content-level:** swapping examples, texts, and case studies for culturally familiar ones (e.g., analyzing lyrics alongside canonical poetry)
- **Discourse-level:** incorporating students' home language practices and communication norms into [Class Discussion](../elements/class-discussion.md) and participation structures
- **Structural-level:** sharing instructional power through [Choice Boards](../elements/choice-boards.md), student-generated questions, and [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md)
- **Asset-framing:** explicitly teaching students to treat their cultural knowledge as intellectual capital (Gay's "cultural scaffolding")

### Target Learners
- Students from marginalized or non-dominant cultural backgrounds whose knowledge is typically excluded from school curricula [Belonging interventions improve outcomes for marginalized students.](../claims/belonging-interventions-improve-outcomes.md) [+M]
- Learners whose home discourse differs from school discourse, who otherwise pay a comprehension tax on every task
- All students benefit from diverse examples, but the strategy is most consequential where identity threat and disengagement are present [~M]

### Target Learning Goals
- Academic content mastery in any subject, accessed through familiar frames [Activating prior knowledge improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
- Identity development and academic self-concept
- Critical literacy: analyzing power, representation, and voice in texts and institutions
- Transfer between community knowledge and school knowledge

### Instructions
1. Learn who your students are — through surveys, [Check-Ins](../elements/check-in.md), community engagement, and observation of their language and interests
2. [Activate](../elements/activation.md) prior knowledge by opening lessons with questions, stories, or problems drawn from students' lived experience [Activating prior knowledge improves learning.](../claims/activation-improves-learning.md) [+M]
3. Deliver rigorous content through [Direct Instruction](../patterns/direct-instruction.md) or [Application](../elements/application.md) tasks that use culturally familiar contexts, vocabulary, and analogies
4. Provide [Feedback](../elements/feedback.md) that is high-expectation and asset-framed — holding students to standards while treating their cultural knowledge as a resource
5. Share authority: let students select texts, pose questions, and present to [Authentic Audiences & Purposes](../principles/authentic-audiences-purposes.md)
6. Reflect and adjust: audit materials and participation patterns regularly for whose culture is present and whose is missing

## Examples
- **Hip-hop pedagogy in ELA:** analyzing Kendrick Lamar or Lauryn Hill lyrics for poetic devices alongside canonical poems, then having students write and perform their own verses applying the same devices
- **[Zaretta Hammond's Culturally Responsive Teaching and the Brain](https://crtandthebrain.com)** — a widely used framework pairing cultural responsiveness with cognitive science (information processing, [Cognitive Load Management](../principles/cognitive-load-management.md))
- **Sports statistics in math:** teaching proportional reasoning and regression through NBA or soccer data students already follow
- **Community ethnography projects:** students interview family or community members as primary sources in history or science, presenting findings to real audiences
