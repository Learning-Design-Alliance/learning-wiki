# Charter school leaders should provide high-quality professional development, collaboration time, and administrative support to improve working conditions and retention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's discussion recommends that charter school leaders address the unmet needs their matched teachers reported by securing resources for professional development and structuring time for colleagues to work together. It states that "substantial administrative support and collaboration time with colleagues need to be provided to improve working conditions in charter schools," and that leaders may need to "release teachers from the classroom, extend the work day, or provide tuition reimbursement."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Charter school leaders must garner additional resources, such as releasing teachers from the classroom, extending the work day, or providing tuition reimbursement, to deliver high-quality professional development.
#### Constraints
- The recommendation is grounded in one matched comparison in a poor, rural Texas setting serving largely Hispanic, limited-English-proficient, economically disadvantaged students.

### Target Learners
- teachers in charter schools

### Target Learning Goals
- teacher retention
- improved working conditions
- student achievement
