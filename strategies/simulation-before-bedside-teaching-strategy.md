# Teach with simulated patients before bedside teaching in pain-medicine training

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends sequencing simulation with simulated patients before clinical bedside teaching in pain medicine. The authors conclude "it is beneficial to teach the simulation first before the practical training, which is also preferred by the students", and students propose including simulation in curricular pain-medicine teaching. The strategy was tested in a randomized course with small groups of three students per simulated patient.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Small groups on the ward so students take an active rather than observing role
- Experienced, train-the-trainer-prepared instructors in pain medicine
- Different simulated patients for training and examinations so simulation-group students gain no actor-familiarity advantage
#### Constraints
- High costs due to the requirement of multiple instructors
- Course capacity limited to a maximum of 12 students per week
- If organizational reasons require a change in order, this is possible because a learning effect still exists

### Target Learners
- medical students in an elective pain-medicine course

### Target Learning Goals
- communication with pain patients
- acute pain therapy skills
- interdisciplinary teamwork
