# School-based and media-based campaigns plus nutritional school programs to maintain sufficient vitamin D levels in students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (1 quant-synthesis, 1 associational), `q2`–`q3` · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends interventions to reduce vitamin D deficiency among schoolchildren, on the grounds that deficiency was associated with poor academic performance. It states that "school-based and media-based campaigns can be a good approach to raising awareness about the importance of vitamin D and its sources (diet and sun exposure) and risks", and that local authorities should check schools' implementation of nutritional school programs and implement or improve them. It also notes low awareness of the impact of vitamin D deficiency on academic performance even among medical students.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Awareness campaigns addressing vitamin D sources (diet and sun exposure) and risks; local authority oversight of schools' nutritional program implementation
#### Constraints
- The underlying association is cross-sectional; the authors state a longitudinal study is needed and causality is difficult to establish

### Target Learners
- Adolescent schoolchildren aged 10–19 years

### Target Learning Goals
- Improving academic performance by maintaining sufficient vitamin D levels during childhood and adolescence
