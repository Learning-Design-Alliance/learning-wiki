# Student-Generated Questions

## Description
Projects driven by student-generated questions allow learners to investigate topics they are curious about or think are important. This approach engages and invests students in the inquiry process, promoting ownership of learning. Instead of being spoon-fed information, learners identify the questions they need or want to answer, fostering a sense of empowerment and relevance.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Facilitation by instructors to guide inquiry and reflection, access to resources for research and investigation, a classroom culture that values student voice and questions.
#### Constraints
- Requires flexibility and adaptability on the part of the instructor, may be challenging to manage diverse interests and questions, demands thoughtful curation of resources to support student inquiry

### Affordances
- Increases motivation by aligning learning with student interests, promotes deeper engagement with content, develops inquiry and critical thinking skills, fosters a sense of ownership and agency

### Personalization
- Allows students to explore topics relevant to their lives and interests, provides opportunities for students to pursue individual lines of inquiry, can be tailored to different learning styles and preferences

- Quality of student-generated questions, depth of inquiry and research, evidence of critical thinking and problem-solving, engagement and participation in project activities

## Impact
- Student-generated questions lead to higher engagement and deeper learning, creating a sense of ownership and investment in the inquiry process. This strategy empowers students to take control of their learning and fosters a curiosity-driven approach to education. (Becton-Consuegra, 2019)
