# Assess incoming students' empathy to predict specialty preferences and tailor targeted courses

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 review), `q2`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The review recommends that medical educators use empathy research conclusions for curriculum planning: freshmen can be assessed for empathy levels to predict their specialty preferences, enabling educators to tailor targeted courses to enhance competence in preferred specialties. Educators can also identify students with relatively low empathy scores who prefer technology-oriented specialties and provide additional courses aimed at enhancing and maintaining empathy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Empathy assessment of freshmen at medical school entry
- Courses targeted to students' preferred specialties and empathy profiles
#### Constraints
- The review notes research on the impact of affective and cognitive empathy on specialty preference is scarce and further studies are needed

### Target Learners
- Medical students, especially first-year students

### Target Learning Goals
- Aligning empathy development with specialty-specific competence
