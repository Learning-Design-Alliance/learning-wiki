# Teachers displaying enthusiasm during instruction to foster student enjoyment

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article suggests that expressing enthusiasm—gestures, varied intonation, eye contact, humor, and lively examples—may foster students' enjoyment of class. The authors state that teachers report showing enthusiasm when the topic is dry or with a challenging group, and conclude "Our data suggest that this may indeed be a useful strategy because enthusiasm seems to have positive effects on students' enjoyment." This applies even when teachers must up-regulate expressiveness without fully experiencing enjoyment, though the emotional labor literature warns this may take effort and lead to stress and burnout.
