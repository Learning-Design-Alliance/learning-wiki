# Design profession-level governance arrangements, modeled on Ostrom's commons mechanisms, to sustain expertise regeneration

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The article recommends that HRD expand beyond organizational optimization to profession-level stewardship, arguing that commons depletion "need not inevitably lead to tragedy if governance mechanisms enable coordinated stewardship." It specifies Ostrom-derived mechanisms—boundary definition, monitoring, graduated sanctions, and collective choice arrangements—as the governance toolkit, notes these remain largely absent in the most vulnerable sectors, and suggests governance arrangements may form across organizational, professional-association, and policy levels, targeting high-vulnerability occupations first.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Coordinated multi-level institutions (organizational, professional-association, policy) since no single actor owns the expertise pool
#### Constraints
- The goal is not to resist AI adoption broadly but to preserve developmental infrastructure where commons dynamics are most urgent; the framework does not prescribe mechanisms for professions facing wholesale disintermediation

### Target Learners
- Professional associations
- HRD practitioners and policymakers
- Organizations in AI-exposed sectors

### Target Learning Goals
- Preserving entry-level developmental pathways
- Maintaining profession-wide substantive validation capacity
