# Letter Strategy

## Description
The letter strategy is a mnemonic technique that involves using acronyms and acrostics to aid memory. Acronyms are words whose individual letters represent elements in lists of information (e.g., HOMES for the Great Lakes). Acrostics are sentences whose first letters represent to-be-remembered information (e.g., 'My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Nine Pizzas' for the planets).

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Understanding of the information to be memorized, ability to create relevant acronyms or sentences, teaching students the association process, and regular review.
#### Constraints
- Effectiveness depends on the relevance and memorability of the acronyms or sentences created. May not be suitable for all types of information or learning styles.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- K-12, higher education, and adult learners, particularly useful for subjects involving lists or sequences of information. Beneficial for students with learning disabilities or memory challenges.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Improve memory of lists and sequences, enhance encoding of information through verbal cues, facilitate recall of facts and concepts, reinforce association of letters with specific elements

### Affordances
- Enhances memory by creating memorable acronyms or sentences. Simplifies complex lists of information into manageable forms. Can be applied across various subjects to memorize lists, steps, or key concepts.

### Personalization
- Teachers can involve students in creating their own acronyms and acrostics to match their learning styles and interests. Students can create personalized mnemonics that are more meaningful and memorable to them.

## Assessment Evidence
- Improved test scores on list-based questions, increased ability to recall sequences, enhanced retention of factual information, and positive student feedback on the strategy.

## Impact
- Letter strategies have been shown to be effective in helping students remember lists of information. The mnemonic 'IT FITS' provides steps to create mnemonics for vocabulary words, while 'LINCS' aids in remembering medieval history vocabulary.

## Examples
- The mnemonic 'IT FITS' (Identify the term, Tell the definition, Find a keyword, Think about the definition, Study) provides steps for creating mnemonics for vocabulary words. LINCS (List the parts, Imagine the picture, Note a reminding word, Construct a LINCing story, Self-Test) aids in remembering vocabulary like 'catapult'.
