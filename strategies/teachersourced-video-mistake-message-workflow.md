# Teachersourcing tailored video mistake messages through a common-wrong-answer authoring interface

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article proposes an authoring interface (Fig. 7 mockup) where content authors see a problem's common wrong answers and record YouTube videos or text tailored to each misconception, plus a general explanation delivered as an on-demand hint. "When students tackle this problem in the future, those that make common wrong answers will receive tailored feedback, while those that request a hint will receive the general explanation." The authors recommend the AIED community consider teachersourcing independently from learnersourcing.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A platform that logs common wrong answers per problem and links uploaded video or text feedback to those answers and to hints
#### Constraints
- This approach suits teachers and content authors, who provide the domain expertise required to tease out misconceptions

### Target Learners
- K-12 mathematics students receiving feedback

### Target Learning Goals
- Correcting common misconceptions through tailored elaborated feedback

## Claims

- [Teacher-created video mistake messages were not harmful but produced no significant learning differences compared with answer-only feedback (n = 649)](../claims/teacher-video-mistake-messages-null.md) [+W]
- [Video feedback improved performance on the next question in an ASSISTments randomized experiment, though not statistically significantly (d = 0.32, n = 89)](../claims/video-feedback-next-question-effect.md) [+W]
