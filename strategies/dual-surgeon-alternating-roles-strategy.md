# Establish formalised two-consultant co-operation with strictly alternating operator and assistant roles to maintain complex-procedure skills in low-volume settings

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (3 for, 2 mixed) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that surgeons in low case volume environments "establish and formalise co‐operations to facilitate dual surgeon operating in their hospitals". Both consultants operate together on every case with strictly alternating roles — one primary operator, one first assistant — and the assistant takes over defined procedure segments such as left flexure mobilisation. The authors state this doubles exposure to complex surgery and helps mitigate skill fade and loss of confidence.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Local expertise, adequate technical support as well as hospital infrastructure
- Support of the surgical department for the extra surgical resource
- Patients informed and consented about two consultant operations, meeting both surgeons before surgery
#### Constraints
- Pelvic exenterations are not performed at the described centre; physiologically poor patients are referred to metropolitan centres (approximately 5% of diagnoses)

### Target Learners
- Rural and non-metropolitan consultant surgeons in low/medium-volume centres

### Target Learning Goals
- Maintenance of technical and non-technical skills for complex colorectal surgery
- Prevention of skill fade and loss of confidence during low-volume practice
