# Organize a two-day accelerator workshop in which student groups take over distinct parts of an ongoing beam experiment with researcher and undergraduate mentors

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 2 studies (1 causal, 1 design), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Accel Kitchen divided twelve student participants into three groups of four, each containing researchers and undergraduate students, with each group responsible for one part of the ongoing Fr experiment: beam transportation, alpha-decay measurement, or MOT optimization. The article states "Participants were separated into three groups" and that day 1 involved visiting the experimental area and offline test experiments while day 2 involved experiments with the accelerated beam. Undergraduate mentors practiced the beam transportation and alpha measurement in offline experiments beforehand, using a rubidium ion gun and a 241Am source.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Access to an accelerator facility and an ongoing beam experiment that students can contribute to; offline test apparatus (rubidium ion gun, 241Am source) for pre-workshop practice
#### Constraints
- Participants were not registered as radiation workers, so they could not enter the radiation area after beam acceleration

### Target Learners
- junior and senior high school students

### Target Learning Goals
- research experience in particle and atomic physics
- radiation education
