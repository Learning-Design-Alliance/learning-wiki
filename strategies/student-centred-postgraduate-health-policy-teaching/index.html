Shift post-graduate health policy education from teacher-centred approaches toward student-centred methods with contemporary teaching training¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 1 claim (1 mixed) · 1 study (1 review), q2 · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

Description¶

Drawing on interview participants' suggestions, the article recommends transitioning to methods that focus more on students, such as actively engaging them in learning tasks and training on contemporary teaching methods, to move away from the traditional teacher-centred approach in post-graduate education. Participants labelled the classroom an ideal representation of the "real world" and emphasized equipping students with class-management abilities. This underpins the educational competencies category of the final framework.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Training on contemporary teaching methods to enhance interactions with students and other learners

Constraints¶

Target Learners¶

  • health policy doctoral students
  • postgraduate faculty teaching health policy

Target Learning Goals¶

  • educational and teaching competencies
  • classroom management as preparation for public matters

Claims¶

  • Student-centred learning environments show mixed effects on students' approaches to learning, with several studies reporting increased surface or decreased deep approaches [~W]

Related Strategies¶

  • Engage trainees in focused observation tasks, collaborative learning tasks, and peer communities of practice to develop reflective practitioners
  • Integrate collaborative videomaking (DST) into STEM-A and CLIL courses as an authentic, student-centred literacy activity
  • Train teachers and develop university courses on VTM as a student-centred methodology
