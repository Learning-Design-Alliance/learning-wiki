# Explicit Teacher Modeling

## Description
Explicit teacher modeling involves the teacher both describing and modeling a skill or concept. The teacher clearly describes the features of the concept or the steps in performing a skill, breaking it down into learnable parts and using multi-sensory techniques to engage students. The teacher demonstrates enthusiasm, maintains a lively pace, questions students periodically, and checks for student understanding.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires a teacher who is knowledgeable and skilled in the concept being taught. Requires a well-structured lesson plan that breaks down the skill into learnable parts. Multi-sensory materials and techniques.
#### Constraints
- May not be effective if students lack prerequisite skills. Requires significant teacher preparation and planning. Can be time-consuming.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Students of all ages, particularly those who have learning problems, attention deficits, processing problems, memory retrieval problems, and metacognitive difficulties.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- To provide students with a clear, multi-sensory model of a skill or concept; to enhance student understanding and ability to perform the skill independently.

### Affordances
- Makes the concept/skill clear and learnable. Provides high levels of teacher support and direction, enabling students to make meaningful cognitive connections. Offers multiple modes to process information through multi-sensory cueing.

### Personalization
- The strategy can be adapted to different skill levels by adjusting the complexity of the concept/skill. It can also be tailored to meet individual student needs by providing varied multi-sensory experiences.

## Assessment Evidence
- Observe student performance during guided practice and independent work. Check for student understanding through questioning and discussion. Monitor student progress on related tasks and assessments.

## Impact
- Studies show that explicit instruction is highly effective for students with learning disabilities and those who struggle with attention, processing, memory retrieval, and metacognition. It provides a clear learning map and reduces confusion and misunderstanding.
