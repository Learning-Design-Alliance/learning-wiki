# Incorporate explicit anti-Indigenous bias questions into medical school admissions files or interviews, but exclude implicit association measures

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (1 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend a differentiated use of bias measurement in selection: explicit measures may be added to applicant files or interviews, while implicit measures should stay out. They write that "it may be reasonable to incorporate these questions in the applicant file or interview to ensure that applicants with explicit biases are not admitted to medical school," while "implicit bias measures should not be included in medical school admissions processes" because of the limitations of IATs and contradicting evidence linking scores to behaviours.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Explicit bias questions would need to be added to the applicant file or interview stage of admissions.
#### Constraints
- The article states implicit bias measures should not be included in admissions due to the limitations of implicit association tests and contradicting evidence linking scores to behaviours.
- The study's explicit bias data were not used to inform admissions decisions, so the recommendation is prospective, not tested.

### Target Learners
- medical school applicants

### Target Learning Goals
- selecting applicants without discriminatory attitudes incompatible with working as a physician
