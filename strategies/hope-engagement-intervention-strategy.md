# Target hope- and engagement-building interventions, with particular attention to female students

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
Based on the finding that hope relates to adjustment partly through engagement, the authors recommend that "educational professionals develop and implement targeted strategies to enhance academic hope and adjustment, particularly among female students." They suggest interventions such as motivational workshops, mentorship programs, and goal-setting activities, noting that increasing engagement fosters hope and in turn improved adjustment. Gender differences in the hope-adjustment path should inform program design.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Programs should account for the gender moderation of the hope-to-adjustment path when designing supports
#### Constraints
- Evidence is cross-sectional and correlational; the authors caution against causal inferences and limited generalization beyond Tehran public universities

### Target Learners
- undergraduate students, particularly female students showing lower hope scores

### Target Learning Goals
- academic adjustment
- academic hope
- academic engagement
