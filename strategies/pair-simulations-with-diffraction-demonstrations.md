# Pair each numeric simulation with a demonstration experiment: two-slit to 5-6-slit slide sequences, reciprocal-lattice slides with hundreds of slits, and random-aperture slides

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 theoretical), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The discussion recommends using the simulations "together with the demonstration experiments," with their role being "to provide a framework that allows students to understand the observed patterns without performing careful and time-consuming measurements." Grating simulations for small N are compared with slide sequences from two slits up to 5-6 slits included in most demonstration kits; slides with hundreds of slits are treated as essentially reciprocal lattices; random-aperture slides introduce coherent light beyond periodic structures.

## Design Implications

#### Requirements
- Requires demonstration kits containing two-slit through 5-6-slit slide sequences and slides with randomly positioned circular apertures, plus the provided Mathematica notebook implementation

### Target Learning Goals
