# Use frequent, low-stakes, cumulative tests in varied formats, including pretests, and encourage student self-testing

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (5 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 theoretical), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The commentary recommends that instructors "use frequent, low-stakes, cumulative exams and a variety of test formats (e.g., cued recall, multiple-choice, and true/false) and give students exams both prior to learning and following the presentation of the to-be-learned material." Its Table 1 recommendations add providing feedback, incorporating collaborative retrieval, and encouraging students to self-test. Implementation tools include clickers, polling applications, review games, and collaborative quizzes (e.g., via Google Forms for instant feedback), with quiz generators and textbook test banks reducing preparation burden.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Multiple-choice questions need plausible, competitive alternatives to require retrieval rather than recognition
- Feedback should be provided so learners can identify and correct misconceptions
#### Constraints
- Short-answer questions are time-consuming to grade, and competitive multiple-choice questions are time-consuming to write
- Low-stakes assessments may require less per-semester updating than high-stakes exams

### Target Learners
- high school and college students

### Target Learning Goals
- long-term retention, transfer, and reduced test anxiety

### Affordances
- Desirable Difficulties Testing Framework
