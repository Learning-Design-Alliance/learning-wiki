# Use LLMs as faculty-supervised adjuncts for automated MCQ pretesting and formative feedback

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends guarded, faculty-supervised use of LLMs as adjuncts to MCQ assessment rather than as replacements for human scoring. Because the models showed "high accuracy under negative marking and universal answering", they can be deployed before exam administration to screen items and afterwards to support learners. The authors state that "Automated pretesting can rapidly identify flawed items, misleading stems, overlapping options, weak or non-functioning distractors, and questions solvable via superficial cues, thereby improving validity before administration", and that supervised use could also provide formative feedback and support curriculum development.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Faculty supervision of any LLM use in assessment workflows
- Confirmation across institutions, languages, and image-rich formats before broader adoption, as the authors state
#### Constraints
- Evidence base is single-institution, Spanish-language, text-focused MCQ exams; image-based items were excluded for Gemini and Copilot in Cardiovascular Medicine
- MCQs may not fully capture LLM reasoning ability compared with free-response or multi-turn formats

### Target Learners
- medical students (third and fourth year)
- medical course faculty

### Target Learning Goals
- validity of multiple-choice assessment items
- formative feedback on medical knowledge
