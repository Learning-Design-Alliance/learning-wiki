# Improve fully online PBL by clarifying instructions, strengthening the group process and adding synchronous tutor contact

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (3 for, 2 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 associational), `q3` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article's forward-looking recommendations for fully online PBL focus on support and group-process design. Students asked for clearer instruction, for example "a video explanation would be better than explaining how the PBLs work on Blackboard", and for live synchronous sessions to discuss triggers. Staff and the discussion identify exploring group size, time-zone impact and how to "fully embrace technology to allow those students with different learning styles to feel supported" as next steps.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements

#### Constraints
- The article states these adjustments were not always successful even after five refinements; success depends on interactions, equal participation and inclusion of all group members

### Target Learners
- First-year undergraduate health promotion students enrolled online

### Target Learning Goals
- Engagement in PBL discussion, peer collaboration and development of health promotion competencies
