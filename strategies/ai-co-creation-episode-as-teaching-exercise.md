# Adapting a sanitized development episode into an evaluative exercise on AI-proposed changes

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The article proposes that an instructor could adapt a small, sanitized development episode into an exercise where students "write acceptance criteria, inspect an AI-proposed change, test a counterexample, and justify acceptance, revision, or rejection." Students could contribute through a specification, interface translation, or reproducible defect report without writing code. This extends the evaluative-judgement approach to software creation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- The episode must be sanitized and small enough for classroom use
#### Constraints
- Its educational effectiveness remains untested
- Students are not required to write code or use a paid assistant

### Target Learners
- translation students

### Target Learning Goals
- evaluative judgement applied to software changes
- understanding human-AI co-creation
