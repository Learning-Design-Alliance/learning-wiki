# Teach sourcing across all online inquiry phases with explicit instruction, controversial tasks, prompts, and collaborative group work

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (4 for, 1 mixed, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The intervention combined several instructional methods: an online inquiry task "structured into manageable sequences" following the phases of online inquiry; task scenarios on controversial topics requiring students to compare and contrast stakeholder views; explicit teaching of effective sourcing practices followed by practice; a working document with analysis and reflection prompts designed to elevate sourcing; and collaborative small-group work in a shared digital workspace. Teachers demonstrated why and how to source in each phase before students practiced with authentic online information.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Teachers need instructional materials and a manual describing effective online inquiry strategies, and students need a shared digital working document with task prompts
#### Constraints
- For practical reasons the first two inquiry phases were combined into one lesson, so specifying the information need was taught only implicitly, and no intervention effect was found for that skill

### Target Learners
- upper secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- sourcing in search queries
- credibility evaluation of online texts
- synthesizing and communicating source-based information
