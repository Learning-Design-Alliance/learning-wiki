# Incorporate modifiable lifestyle strategies (NEAT, regular breaks, sleep-protective shift scheduling) into residents' work routines

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 4 claims (4 for) · 1 study (1 associational), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that policymakers and hospital administrators embed practical, modifiable lifestyle supports into residency work routines. Suggested measures include "incorporating non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT) into work routines—ensuring adequate hydration, taking regular breaks, performing basic exercises, and monitoring caloric intake—and scheduling night shifts to allow for sufficient sleep," alongside systemic/organizational interventions such as human factors and ergonomics approaches.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Organizational buy-in from policymakers and hospital administrators to modify work routines and shift schedules
#### Constraints
- The article notes residents may turn to increased alcohol consumption and smoking as coping mechanisms for stress and fatigue, and that the cross-sectional design does not establish causal benefits of these strategies

### Target Learners
- radiology residents in standardized residency training

### Target Learning Goals
- sustaining perceived-competency and professional performance
- reducing occupational burnout
