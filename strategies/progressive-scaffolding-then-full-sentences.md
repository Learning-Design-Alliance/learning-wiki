# Use progressive presentation as scaffolding for novice learners, transitioning to full-sentence presentation once familiarity develops

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends deploying progressive presentation as a scaffolding technique for learners encountering unfamiliar vocabulary, enabling gradual exposure while maintaining sentence context. The authors state that "Once learners develop familiarity, full-sentence presentation may be more efficient, allowing them to infer meanings without stepwise support." This makes progressive disclosure a transitional aid rather than a permanent presentation style.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Learners should be novices encountering unfamiliar vocabulary for the scaffolding phase to be useful
#### Constraints
- Evidence is from lab studies with small samples and L2 languages grammatically similar to L1

### Target Learners

### Target Learning Goals
- Word-level and sentence-level comprehension in a second language
