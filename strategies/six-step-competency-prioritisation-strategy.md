# Six-step structured prioritisation strategy: exclusion criteria, Delphi filtering, significance scoring, usefulness survey, AHP combination, final selection

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends sharing practical steps for integrating simulation into national curricula and describes its own multistep recipe: (1) develop exclusion criteria; (2) review competencies for inclusion/exclusion; (3) assess clinical significance; (4) evaluate perceived usefulness of training; (5) conduct the analytical hierarchy process; and (6) select final competencies for inclusion. The authors state that "Examples of practical steps taken on how to approach this may be helpful to others trying to develop integrated curriculum, especially at a national level."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Stakeholder groups across roles and locations to build a national picture of needs
- Unambiguous exclusion criteria developed by experienced clinical educators
#### Constraints
- Resource and logistical limits dictated the programme comprise no more than two half-day exercises
- Stakeholder ability to self-assess needs is variable and challenging to account for

### Target Learners
- junior doctors in national postgraduate training programmes

### Target Learning Goals
- curriculum prioritisation for simulation-based education
