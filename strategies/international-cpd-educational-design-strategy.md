# Address common cross-border CPD gaps through international educational designs adapted to each country's healthcare system

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (6 for) · 1 study (1 review), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends that challenges common across countries be addressed through international educational designs, since "Common issues might be addressed more efficiently through international educational designs, adapted to each country's healthcare system". International deployment would let scarce resources be shared and programs developed more efficiently, with potential economies of scale for education providers. Designers should verify comparability of educational activities and look for similarities for internationally deployed programs, while tailoring to cultural differences and health-system contexts. The collaborative is developing an international educational program targeting five of the identified gaps: diagnostic criteria, suboptimal integration of diabetic teams, assessment of treatment targets, modes of action of drugs and treatment guidelines.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Cultural differences and different health system contexts require tailoring to ensure international applicability
#### Constraints
- The approach was limited to four countries/regions in its first phase; generalisability to other areas should be investigated before designing local educational interventions

### Target Learners
- General practitioners and family physicians in international CPD programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Develop and maintain physicians' competencies in type 2 diabetes screening, diagnosis, treatment and management
