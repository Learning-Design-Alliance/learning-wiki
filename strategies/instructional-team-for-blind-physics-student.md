# Assemble and train an instructional team with an in-class assistant and an out-of-class tutor, held together by regular meetings and careful records

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 1 claim (1 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q1` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 1 claim rests on one study

## Description
The strategy is to give a mainstreamed blind student an in-class assistant who makes materials accessible in real time (clarifying notation, describing figures and demonstrations) plus a tutor outside class, and to run the whole effort as a team. Neither role can be filled by students currently taking the course, since both require advanced familiarity with the material; the authors "had success hiring either students who had already studied the course material, or part-time instructors with a degree in physics or a related field". The team held regular meetings on progress, exams, methods and accessibility, with weekly check-ins to the disabilities coordinator, and kept careful records to train new staff when the student transitions between courses.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Job descriptions shared well in advance with the disabilities coordinator, specifying required physics and mathematics background
- Training of all instructional staff in accessible lecture and material practices as early as possible
- Continuity of instructor and teaching assistants, supported by careful records throughout each course
#### Constraints
- Financing support positions is institution and location specific; in the US it depends first on the student's state and then the disability

### Target Learners
- blind and visually impaired students in mainstreamed college physics courses

### Target Learning Goals
- equal access to course instruction, tutoring, and assessment
