# Integrate visual slide materials with hands-on activities to support concept retention

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 design), `q1`–`q3` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
During the workshop, visual curriculum materials were deliberately connected to hands-on building tasks. The author reports that "the integration of visual materials appeared to enhance concept retention, as many students made direct connections between the slide content and hands-on activities." The strategy is to present each concept visually and immediately let students apply it in assembly, wiring, or programming.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Slide content that maps directly onto the physical tasks students perform
- Structured sequencing so each visual concept is followed by a corresponding hands-on activity
#### Constraints
- The retention benefit is an observational impression reported by the author, not a tested comparison

### Target Learners
- middle school students

### Target Learning Goals
- retention of robotics, physics, and engineering concepts
