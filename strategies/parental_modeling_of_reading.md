# Parental Modeling of Reading

## Description
Parents demonstrate the value of reading by being reading role models themselves, reading books, magazines, or newspapers regularly at home. This normalizes reading as a daily activity and conveys its importance to children. Seeing adults engage with various forms of text encourages children to view reading as relevant and enjoyable, thus influencing their own literacy habits and motivation.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Requires parents to be visible readers, dedicating time to reading books, magazines, or newspapers in the presence of their children.
#### Constraints
- May be less effective if parents do not genuinely enjoy reading or if they are inconsistent in their modeling. Children need to witness regular reading habits for the modeling to have a significant impact.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Parents and caregivers of children of all ages, especially preschool and early elementary.

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Cultivate positive attitudes towards reading, encourage children to view reading as a valuable activity, and promote a home environment that supports literacy development.

### Affordances
- Creates a home environment that naturally supports literacy. It enhances motivation by showing children the real-world relevance of reading and positively influences their attitudes towards learning. Demonstrating enjoyment in reading also encourages children to replicate this behavior.

### Personalization
- Parents can select reading materials based on their own interests, which allows for authentic modeling. It can be adapted to fit into any family's lifestyle and routine.

## Impact
- Research emphasizes that children need parents to be reading role models with daily practice in order to navigate successfully through beginning literacy skills. (Evans, Shaw, Bell, 2000).
