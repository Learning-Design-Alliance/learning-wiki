# Use cloud-accessible quantum computing platforms so students run circuits on real quantum hardware

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
At a symbolic, language-based level, the article describes platforms offering free cloud access to quantum computing power, such as Quantum Inspire and IBM Quantum with its drag-and-drop graphical interface and the qiskit Python SDK. Students "can build their qubit circuits and run them on a real quantum computer, experiencing quantum computing and its limitations", comparing simulation with the real computer to understand error mitigation and correction. The IBM textbook is aimed at university students and would need simplification for school use.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Adapt and simplify existing materials (e.g. the IBM qiskit textbook) according to students' knowledge, needs and available time
#### Constraints
- The IBM textbook is aimed at university students with high motivation and interest in mathematics; platforms require registration

### Target Learners
- upper secondary school students

### Target Learning Goals
- quantum computing systems, quantum algorithms, error mitigation and correction
