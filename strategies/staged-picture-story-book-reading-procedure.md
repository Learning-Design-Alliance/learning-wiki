# Use a staged picture-story-book procedure: whole-picture stimulus, picture-text matching in groups, ordering, then individual retelling

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 3 claims (2 for, 1 against) · 1 study (1 causal), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 3 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article describes a concrete classroom procedure for using picture story books in reading lessons. The tutor first posts a big picture to focus attention, then has groups match pictures to text with role division, then arrange pictures in order, and finally retell the story individually. The article reports that "picture story books can create a learning community in the classroom" and that the sequence stimulated imagination, motivation, confidence and autonomy.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A tutor to post a big picture, distribute picture story books, group students, and lead the matching, ordering and retelling stages
#### Constraints
- Student enthusiasm decreased by the fifth meeting when the same material type was used repeatedly in a short time

### Target Learners
- first-year Paket B students at a non-formal community learning center (PKBM)

### Target Learning Goals
- English reading comprehension, word recognition, vocabulary, confidence and autonomy in reading
