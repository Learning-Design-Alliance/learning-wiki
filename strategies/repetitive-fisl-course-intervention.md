# Repetitive facial imitation-based social learning course as an intervention for facial-expression recognition difficulties

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 7 claims (7 for) · 1 study (1 causal), `q3` · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 7 claims rest on one study

## Description
The article recommends a structured FISL course in which participants repeatedly imitate validated facial expressions (positive and negative) presented on a monitor, imitating "as accurately as possible" for 6 s per trial, across 16 sessions over about one month at 3–4 sessions per week. The authors state that "a repetitive FISL course may be particularly beneﬁcial in interventions aimed at individuals with difﬁculties in recognizing and responding to facial expressions" and suggest it as a potential therapeutic approach for mood disorders or neurodevelopmental conditions where reduced sensitivity to facial emotions is prevalent.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Repeated sessions distributed over roughly one month (3–4 per week)
- Validated facial-expression stimuli, matched and not repeated across sessions
- Automated or expert scoring of imitation performance to track learning
#### Constraints
- Transfer was demonstrated for emotional faces but not emotional scenes
- Positive-emotion benefits were stronger than negative-emotion benefits
- Evidence is from healthy adult males; clinical efficacy is proposed, not tested in this study

### Target Learners
- Adults with difficulties recognizing facial expressions
- Proposed clinical populations: individuals with ASD, schizophrenia, or depression

### Target Learning Goals
- Non-verbal emotional expression via imitation
- Recognition and decoding of others' facial emotions
