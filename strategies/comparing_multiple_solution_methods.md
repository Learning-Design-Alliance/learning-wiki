# Comparing Multiple Solution Methods

## Description
Learners compare and contrast multiple solution methods for the same problem, as opposed to studying one method at a time. This approach involves presenting learners with worked examples of different solution methods side by side and encouraging them to identify similarities and differences between the methods.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Instructors need to provide worked examples of different solution methods and encourage learners to discuss and reflect on the similarities and differences between the methods.
#### Constraints
- Requires careful selection of worked examples and structured questions to guide learners' comparisons. May not be suitable for learners who struggle with abstract concepts.

### Target Learners
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Middle school students, high school students

### Target Learning Goals
<!-- Link to sub-claims -->
- Promote procedural knowledge, procedural flexibility, and conceptual knowledge. Enhance the ability to generate, recognize, and evaluate multiple solution methods for the same problem.

### Affordances
- Leads to greater gains in procedural knowledge and flexibility compared to studying multiple methods sequentially. Helps learners become better at solving problems and more likely to use shortcuts.

### Personalization
- Learners can compare and contrast solution methods at their own pace and focus on the methods that best suit their learning style.

## Assessment Evidence
- Assess learners' ability to solve problems using multiple methods. Evaluate their understanding of the concepts underlying the solution methods.

## Impact
- Leads to greater gains in procedural knowledge and flexibility compared to studying multiple methods sequentially. Helps learners become better at solving problems and more likely to use shortcuts. Research shows comparison helps students become better at solving linear equations. This effect was found on problems similar to those in the study packets, and on transfer problems which differed from those the students had already seen.
