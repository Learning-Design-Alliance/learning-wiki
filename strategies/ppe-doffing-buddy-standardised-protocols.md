# Use a doffing buddy and standardised written protocols to reduce PPE self-contamination

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 2 claims (2 for) · 2 studies (1 review, 1 qualitative), `q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

## Description
This strategy addresses the article's point that self-contamination is most common during PPE doffing: evidence it cites suggests contamination can be reduced by using a buddy to identify and rectify errors in technique and by using standardised written protocols. The article also stresses that junior doctors must resist the temptation to rush to emergencies without suitable protection, following guidance that adequate PPE be donned before commencing assessment and treatment.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- A buddy must be available to observe and rectify doffing technique errors; standardised written protocols must be in place.
#### Constraints
- Wearing PPE for prolonged periods is hot and uncomfortable and causes difficulties in performing even basic procedures such as blood sampling and cannulation.

### Target Learners
- Junior doctors and other healthcare workers wearing PPE in ICU

### Target Learning Goals
- Safe PPE donning and doffing technique
- Infection prevention during clinical care
