# Integrate the DNH model into structured ESD curricula via simulation workshops with progressive difficulty and bleeding scenarios

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 5 claims (5 for) · 1 study (1 design), `q2` · 0 of 1 report an effect size · 5 claims rest on one study

## Description
The authors recommend integrating the DNH model into structured ESD training curricula as a bridge between classroom learning and live clinical practice: "We recommend its use in simulation workshops with progressive difficulty, inclusion of vascular bleeding scenarios, and repeated sessions to maximize cost efficiency." They argue its reusability, low cost, and strong discriminatory capability suit institutional adoption, particularly for novice training programs and resource-limited settings.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Structured ESD training curriculum with sessions arranged in progressive difficulty; repeated sessions to maximize cost efficiency; inclusion of vascular bleeding scenarios
#### Constraints
- Findings are preliminary given the exploratory design, modest sample size, and single-center setting; multi-center validation is recommended before full integration

### Target Learners
- Novice endoscopists and physicians in resource-limited training programs

### Target Learning Goals
- Development of ESD procedural competency and complication-management skills without patient risk
