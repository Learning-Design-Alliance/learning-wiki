# Run the brachistochrone activity in groups of four with learning assistants, allow fifty minutes, give hints, and provide the correct graphs at the end

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · no claims cited

## Description
The author's recommendations after the groups' disappointing performance: introductory students should work in groups of four with sufficient learning assistants, fifty minutes should be allotted, hints from the instructor supplement should be given, free body diagrams may be drawn before the kinematics graphs, and the correct graphs should be provided at the end. He writes, "We strongly recommend the introductory students work in groups of four and that there are sufficient learning assistants to help them."

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Sufficient learning assistants to help introductory student groups
- The activity's last page is an instructor supplement including hints which could/should be given to improve performance and learning
#### Constraints
- Most students clearly need significant guidance
- Fifty minutes is needed to work through this

### Target Learners
- introductory calculus-based mechanics students

### Target Learning Goals
- qualitative kinematics graphing and dynamics reasoning
