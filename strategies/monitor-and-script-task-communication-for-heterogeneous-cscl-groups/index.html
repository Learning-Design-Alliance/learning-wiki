Monitor early group processes and provide group-adapted task-related communication scripting for linguistically heterogeneous CSCL groups at risk¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 4 claims (3 for, 1 mixed) · 2 studies (2 associational), q2–q3 · 1 of 2 report an effect size · 4 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends a group-specific intervention approach for its identified risk constellation of "a combination of high first-language heterogeneity and low task-related communication". Instructors should first monitor group processes in the early collaboration phase and provide feedback on risks; if needed, heterogeneous groups can be assisted in increasing task-related communication "through adapted scripting or scaffolding procedures designed to structure task-related communication". The approach is selective so low-risk groups are not disrupted.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • systematic monitoring of group processes during the early phase of collaboration with feedback on potential risks
  • scripting prompts that focus members on task-related questions, plan formulation, and progress monitoring

Constraints¶

  • interventions may be perceived as disruptive interference (overscripting) by low-risk groups, so they should be applied group-specifically

Target Learners¶

  • linguistically heterogeneous higher-education distance-education CSCL groups

Target Learning Goals¶

  • online interaction self-efficacy for academic purposes

Claims¶

  • Initial-phase task-related communication moderates the negative relationship between first-language heterogeneity and self-efficacy gains, while getting-to-know-each-other time does not [+W]
  • First-language heterogeneity in CSCL groups lowered online interaction self-efficacy gains, moderated by early task-related communication time [+W]
  • Groups spent far more time getting to know each other than on task-related communication during the initial collaboration phase [+W]
  • Time spent getting to know each other paradoxically amplifies perceived group heterogeneity in first-language heterogeneous CSCL groups [~W]
