# Use open-ended group laboratory assignments with Socratic TA support to build strongly connected student groups

> **Strategy** · [All strategies](index.md)
> **Evidence** · 6 claims (1 for, 5 mixed) · 2 studies (1 associational, 1 design), `q1`–`q2` · 0 of 2 report an effect size · 6 claims rest on one study

## Description
The studied course uses open-ended assignments (e.g. measuring the mass of a clump of clay without a scale) that small groups of 3-5 must complete together, with TAs instructed "to not give solutions, but to use e.g. Socrative questioning to help students along". The authors report the course is "equally successful in building strongly connected groups regardless of student demographics", making this format a candidate design for courses where social integration matters.

## Design Implications

### Context
#### Requirements
- Assignments must be difficult or impossible to perform without group work; groups that end up too small are encouraged by the TA to merge and too large groups to split.
#### Constraints
- Attendance is not compulsory, so absence can signal either low commitment or efficient groups finishing early; the authors note improving social dynamics is unlikely to change the drop-out rate on this course.

### Target Learners
- first-year university physics students, including majors, minors and pre-service teachers

### Target Learning Goals
- experiment design, presentation and rudimentary error analysis; building social contacts
