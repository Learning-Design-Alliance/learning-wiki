Teachers and parents should give particular support to young people bullied in both traditional and cyber contexts, and handle impact information for bullies and victims with care.¶

Strategy · All strategies
Evidence · 2 claims (2 for) · 1 study (1 associational), q3 · 1 of 1 report an effect size · 2 claims rest on one study

Description¶

The article recommends that adults recognize dual-context victims as particularly distressed and in need of additional help, while treating information about cyberbullies' overestimated impact as a double-edged intervention. It states "teachers and parents should be aware that young people being bullied in both traditional and cyber contexts will be particularly distressed". Relaying impact information to bullies risks intensifying aggression, and telling victims risks belittling their experiences, though it may support adaptive coping such as positive reappraisal.

Design Implications¶

Context¶

Requirements¶

  • Adults (teachers, parents, school psychologists) involved in monitoring and responding to bullying among adolescents

Constraints¶

  • Relaying impact information to cyberbullies carries the risk that the bully intensifies actions or switches to more direct traditional aggression; telling cybervictims may belittle their lived experiences

Target Learners¶

  • Adolescents aged 12-20 in secondary education

Target Learning Goals¶

  • Social-emotional wellbeing; coping with peer victimization; prevention of cyberbullying

Claims¶

  • Adolescents victimized in both traditional and cyber contexts report greater negative emotional impact from cyberbullying than cyber-only victims. [+W]
  • Cyberbullies believe their victims would experience more negative emotional impact than cybervictims actually report experiencing. [+W]
